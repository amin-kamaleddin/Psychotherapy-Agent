# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established a consistently nonjudgmental, validating tone and avoided labeling, repeatedly expressing unconditional positive regard. This supportive stance was evident from the opening lines and sustained throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking made Tyler feel understood and reduced adversarial stance. A few missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration (e.g., feelings behind 'she freaked out' or peer pressure) prevented a maximal rating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on substance-related behavior and interpersonal consequences, moving from exploration to a concrete, time-limited plan. The clinician kept the discussion goal-oriented and avoided drifting into unrelated areas.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as joint problem-solving with frequent invitations for Tyler to choose next steps, and interventions were responsive rather than prescriptive. Tyler's input shaped the final plan and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly supported through offering choices, asking permission, and avoiding pressure to give up friendships. Options and harm-reduction steps respected Tyler's agency throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivational elicitation occurred (questions about goals and willingness), producing pragmatic change statements tied to reducing mom's anger. The clinician did not sufficiently draw out Tyler's intrinsic motives, values, or confidence about change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently framed the process as collaborative, asking permission and co-constructing the plan with Tyler's preferences front and center. Power-sharing was evident in choice of strategies and plan details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited concrete, pragmatic change talk (e.g., not bringing substances into the house) but did not systematically deepen desire, reasons, confidence, or commitment. There was limited use of scaling or evoking intrinsic motivation for longer-term change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided argumentation and accepted Tyler's resistance while pivoting to solutions that addressed his concerns, effectively preventing reinforcement of sustain talk. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and deeper ambivalence work kept this from being maximal.
