# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the client's experience (e.g., 'I'm glad you reached out'). They avoided labeling or moralizing and used affirmations throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, empathic listening with timely reflections that matched the client's affect and somatic descriptions (e.g., reflecting the 'tight, aching exhaustion'). The clinician stayed present and validated the client's feelings while checking in on safety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on self-harm risk and immediate safety, moving from assessment to a concrete five-part safety plan. Balanced exploration of triggers with actionable steps like removing the razor, holding ice, and setting a delay.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the work as an equal, cooperative effort by offering editable text options and soliciting the client's preferences at each step. The clinician co-worked through grounding and adapted interventions to the client's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently sought permission and offered multiple options, emphasizing the client's control over decisions and timing. Language avoided pressure and repeatedly returned agency to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitments and change-oriented behaviors (hiding the razor, sending texts, using coping strategies) but leaned more on practical safety than drawing out intrinsic reasons or values. More systematic elicitation and amplification of internal motivators would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as collaborative, inviting choice about which safety-plan element to begin and offering to stay while the client sent texts. The clinician let the client shape wording and timing, reinforcing shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced several instances of change talk, resulting in concrete commitments (e.g., hiding the razor, 15-minute delay). Could further deepen and amplify desire, reasons, ability, and commitment language to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and redirected toward safety planning and alternatives, which reduced reinforcement of self-harm rationales. Incorporating more double-sided reflections would better highlight discrepancy and soften sustain talk further.
