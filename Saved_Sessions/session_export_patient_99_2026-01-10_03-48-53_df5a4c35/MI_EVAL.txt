# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone (e.g., appreciation, normalization). Warmth and respect were evident throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and summaries that captured Matteo’s drinking patterns and effects, eliciting some self-reflection. Could use deeper affective/complex reflections to explore underlying feelings and the personal meaning of social drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and its relation to sleep, ankle healing, and functioning, moving logically from assessment to planning. Avoided drift and guided the session toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a true partner—co-created implementation plans, checked preferences, and rehearsed language together. Clinician actively sought and integrated Matteo’s input at every step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and framing goals as Matteo’s decisions. Avoided controlling language and reinforced self-directed, small goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical motivations and reasons via open questions and scaling, producing a self-generated plan. Could probe more for intrinsic values or long-term goals and amplify change statements to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong power-sharing—offered a menu of options, tailored a text and exit line together, and checked whether to polish/send the message. The client’s ideas clearly shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, used scaling, and helped set specific, doable tactics with rehearsal. Would benefit from more systematic amplification of change statements and exploration of imagined benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and neutrally reflected sustain talk, offering small-change options to reduce reactance. Missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections or reframes to explicitly pivot ambivalence toward change.
