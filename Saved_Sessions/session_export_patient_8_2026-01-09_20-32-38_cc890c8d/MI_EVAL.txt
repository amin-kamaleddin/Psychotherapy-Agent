# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating from the start, creating a safe atmosphere for ambivalence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and normalizing that facilitated disclosure, but could deepen empathy with more complex reflections about underlying feelings and tensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on social media use and its links to sleep/grades, moving logically from assessment to a concrete, time-bound experiment and monitoring plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally a shared problem-solving process with permission-seeking and input requests, though supplying many ready-made scripts and templates risked shifting toward clinician-driven solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering options, asking which is more doable, inviting patient choices for limits and start day, and affirming the ability to change course if needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk via ruler and choice points, but relies heavily on clinician-provided strategies rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and negotiates the plan with the patient, but could increase co-creation by eliciting the patient's ideas before offering many specific strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited desire/reasons and a specific one-week commitment with concrete limits, though there were missed opportunities to amplify and probe the patient's change statements for greater motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk (FOMO, relaxation) but does not use strategic double-sided reflections or framing to actively tip the balance toward change before moving to solutions.
