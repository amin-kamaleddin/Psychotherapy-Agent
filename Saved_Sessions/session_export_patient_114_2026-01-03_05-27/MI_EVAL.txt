**Comments:** Accurate, sustained reflective listening capturing content and feeling (e.g., “That sounds upsetting and confusing,” “I hear hope…”). Reflections match patient affect and ambivalence throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5  
**Comments:** Interaction treated as joint enterprise—options offered, patient preferences shape plan, clinician invites co‑creation (drafting the one‑liner together).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms patient control and choice (“I believe you can do this at your own pace,” “What would make you feel confident…”). Scaffolds options without coercion; patient decides specifics.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5  
**Comments:** Elicits patient motivations and past successes (alternating drinks, roommate checks) rather than imposing reasons. Uses patient values (scholarship, future) to draw out change talk.

### Partnership

**Comments:** Fosters genuine partnership—decisions emerge from patient input (selecting roommate check, time, backup); clinician offers help when invited.

### Cultivating Change Talk

**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies multiple examples of change talk (“I don’t want this to mess up my future,” concrete wins). Builds toward a specific short‑term experiment and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects toward practical supports rather than reinforcing justifications. Missed some opportunities for double‑sided reflections and stronger reframing of social‑bonding sustain talk; could more explicitly name ambivalence and elicit alternatives.
