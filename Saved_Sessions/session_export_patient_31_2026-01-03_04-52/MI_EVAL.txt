# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and non‑judgmental language; repeatedly normalizes and honors the patient’s wish to keep an after‑shift smoke, creating a safe, shaming‑free atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Accurate, frequent reflective listening that captures content and emotional tone (frustration, need for relief); checks understanding and shapes planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on target behavior with logical progression from goals to concrete, testable plan (patch trial, dose/timing, monitoring).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Repeated invitations to choose options and co‑construct the plan; patient preferences drive decisions (shift, timing, reminders).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms patient choice, avoids coercion, offers options and checks what feels “doable,” reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Frequently elicits patient reasons, desired outcomes, and success criteria, converting ambivalence into change talk and a specific behavioral experiment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and shared problem solving, with a few instances of offering information without an explicit elicit‑provide‑elicit—minor departure from ideal MI practice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Reflects and amplifies change talk, asks about concrete signs of success, and translates patient statements into a clear commitment to test the patch.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Avoids arguing or confronting liking of smoking; uses double‑sided reflections and frames the patch as an experiment that preserves valued rituals, reducing resistance.
