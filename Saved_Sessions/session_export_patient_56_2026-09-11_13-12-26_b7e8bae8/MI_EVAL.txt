# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and positive regard through affirmations and restatements, avoiding labeling or minimizing the client's values or concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate paraphrase and reflective listening that captures content and intent, though could deepen by naming underlying emotions and exploring the emotional meaning of 'family happiness.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on protecting family time with concrete steps (boundaries, one‑week trial), and keeps questions and reflections tied to that target without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models true collaboration by inviting the client's wording, sequencing preferences, and practice, co‑creating the plan rather than lecturing or prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and elicits preferences, offering options and framing the one‑week trial as optional to reinforce client ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical ideas and steps from the client but leans toward planning; could further draw out deeper personal reasons, importance/confidence, and past successes to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Defers to the client's judgment and shares power in decision‑making, asking which options feel doable and inviting the client's contributions to wording and steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change‑related language, scaffolding from values to specific commitments and practice steps that strengthen preparatory and mobilizing talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Validates concerns and reframes sustain talk with collaborative, low‑risk strategies (partner check‑in, one‑week test, scripting), effectively reducing threat and redirecting toward experimentation.
