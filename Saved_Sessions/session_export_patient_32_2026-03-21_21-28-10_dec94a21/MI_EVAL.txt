### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Derrick's perspective and constraints, normalizing ambivalence. No stigmatizing or confrontational language was used, supporting a top-tier acceptance rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with useful summaries and reflections on practical concerns. Missed opportunities to deepen emotional resonance around loss of the relationship and guarded humor that would raise empathy further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on preventing future DUIs and protecting Derrick's license and employment with each intervention tied to concrete goals. The clinician kept the session goal-directed and avoided drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—clinician invited input at each step, co-created messages, and tailored suggestions to Derrick's social reality. Preferences were solicited and incorporated, making the session a shared effort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Derrick's choice and control by offering options, framing suggestions as supports, and respecting his boundaries. These moves reinforced ownership of the plan throughout the session.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Effectively drew out concrete motivations and plans (e.g., cab money, app, sister check-in) and used confidence scaling with follow-ups. Could more deeply explore intrinsic values and amplify change talk into stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co-constructed with shared decision-making; the clinician deferred to Derrick's lived experience and asked him to choose actions and message tone. The plan reflected Derrick's preferences and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk, producing concrete, doable steps and commitments. To reach a top score, the clinician could more consistently probe desire, reasons, need, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized Derrick's ambivalence while pivoting to protective strategies. More use of double-sided reflections or explicit reframes would further soften sustain talk.
