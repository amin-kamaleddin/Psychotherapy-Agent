# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple explicit affirmations and praise (“You’re taking a thoughtful, values‑driven step — well done,” “Nice plan”). Slips normalized (“slips are learning, not failure”). Respectful tone throughout, but affirmations were somewhat formulaic and often immediately followed by instruction rather than individualized validation of emotions (fear of snapping, enjoyment of smoking).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflective statement acknowledged both enjoyment and concern (“I hear you — you enjoy smoking... but you worry about modeling”). Several acknowledgements of skepticism and night cravings. Reflections were generally brief/shallow and not used to probe deeper feelings or meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing children’s exposure (no smoking indoors/car) and on a time‑limited experiment. Triggers named and concrete tactics offered (gum, delay, tea, walk); plan reiterated and follow‑up promised. Direction achieved largely via clinician instruction rather than extended patient-driven planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician invited a small experiment and negotiated some details (decaf tea, gum, partner backup). After initial invitation, interaction shifted to clinician-led strategy lists and repeated directives, limiting patient-generated problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged Linda’s choice (no current plans to quit) and framed the approach as an experiment; offered options and resources (quitline). However, repeated prescriptive language and stepwise instructions reduced opportunities for Linda to choose among alternatives or rate confidence/readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk elicited (concern about modeling for kids, willingness to try specific swaps). Clinician did not consistently amplify or explore these motivations (no scaling questions, few open invitations to elaborate), limiting momentum building.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moments of co‑construction (patient agreed to specific swaps; partner role discussed). Overall dynamic became more expert‑driven with clinician repeating and prescribing strategies, reducing equal partnership in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient expressed values-driven reasons (don’t want kids to see smoking as normal). Clinician acknowledged and praised but rarely used reflections/summaries to amplify desire/ability/reasons/need or asked readiness/confidence (no rulers or targeted elicitation).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged sustain talk (skepticism about nights, cravings) and normalized it. Missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing that would explicitly hold ambivalence (“On one hand... on the other hand...”) and more actively soften sustain talk.
