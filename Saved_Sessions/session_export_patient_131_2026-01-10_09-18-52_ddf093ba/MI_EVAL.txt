# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with normalizing statements and strengths‑based affirmations. Tone remained respectful and nonjudgmental throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective‑taking captured ambivalence and key fears, showing good understanding of what matters to Wiremu. Could deepen emotional meaning with more varied, complex reflections and probe feelings behind statements like “I’ll do anything…”.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing alcohol, engaging in Stopping Violence, and arranging supportive living, repeatedly returning to actionable steps. Questions were tightly linked to target behavior and concrete planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative, co‑constructing trial stay features and scripts with Wiremu and frequently inviting his perspective. Clinician positioned themselves as an ally, sharing power in decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were explicitly framed as Wiremu’s and the clinician avoided pressure, asking him to craft wording and preferences for plans. Autonomy was reinforced throughout, preserving ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out intrinsic motivations (kids as the ‘engine’), strengths, and concrete steps, linking values to behavior change. Prompts elicited substantial change language and practical intentions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently solicited Wiremu’s preferences and invited him to shape the plan, fostering a strong partnership. Power was shared through repeated invitations to choose priorities and wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and prompted concrete actions, producing explicit change language and immediate steps. Could better amplify and summarize strong change statements to consolidate commitment and elicit confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk nonjudgmentally and used double‑sided questions to redirect toward change. Would benefit from more strategic moves (selective summaries, immediate pivots from sustain to change, and exploring costs of staying the same).
