# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses nonjudgmental, respectful language and normalizes ambivalence, conveying unconditional positive regard most of the time. Some affirmations feel generic rather than naming specific strengths or courage, so more individualized affirmations would strengthen this area.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate emotion naming and sensitivity to nuance. Reflections are sometimes long summaries rather than succinct complex reflections that could deepen emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on reducing risky drinking and improving contraception reliability and moves from values to specific, time‑bound actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as joint problem‑solving, invites client input at every step, and co-constructs concrete solutions like drafting a text together. The clinician consistently centers the client’s perspective and choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by asking which options feel doable and avoiding pressuring language, reinforcing client ownership of decisions. Multiple moves invite the client to set priorities and request the type of help they want.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the client's own motivations, values, and past successes, focusing on internal reasons for change. Questions effectively mobilized the client's resources and elicited concrete, self-generated plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative with shared power; the clinician asks permission before offering help and lets the client choose next steps. The client’s ideas visibly shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with evocative questions and planning prompts that produced client change statements. Missed opportunities remain to selectively reinforce the strongest change language and to elicit explicit commitment or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward practical strategies and supports without confrontation. Could further soften sustain talk by using double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing to highlight reasons for change while acknowledging the pull of sustain talk.
