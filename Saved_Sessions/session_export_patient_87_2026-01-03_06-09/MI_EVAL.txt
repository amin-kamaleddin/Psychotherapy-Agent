#### **Score (1–5):** 5

**Comments:** Strong evocation: clinician repeatedly prompts Marco to generate and refine strategies (portioning, veggies bowl, text nudge, Sunday prep), producing a co-created action plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Collaborative tone and shared planning
- [x] Clinician facilitates rather than dictates
- [ ] Patient fully controls pacing/agenda without subtle clinician steering

**Comments:** Very collaborative overall. Occasionally the clinician narrows options (e.g., insistence on “single” steps) which, while effective for focus, slightly shifts balance toward clinician direction. Invite patient summary to enhance shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Elicits actionable ideas and some commitment language
- [ ] Actively amplifies/reflects and strengthens change talk (DARN-C)
- [ ] Uses scaling follow-ups to deepen commitment

**Comments:** Marco produced multiple change statements (“I’ll stick with Tuesdays and Thursdays,” “I’ll try portioning…”), but clinician missed chances to amplify and deepen that talk (no reflective amplification or “what would make it a 7?” style follow-ups; limited elicitation of a clear commitment statement).

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk
- [ ] Uses double-sided reflections or links to past successes to reduce sustain talk
- [ ] Strategically redirects sustain talk into amplified change talk

**Comments:** Clinician appropriately acknowledges repeated sustain concerns (“it’s gonna be really hard”), but tends to mirror sustain language rather than using double-sided reflections or immediately linking to past successes to diminish its strength. This allowed sustain talk to persist.
