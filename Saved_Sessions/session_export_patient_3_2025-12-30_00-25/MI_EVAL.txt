# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently expresses respect and support (validates struggle, repeated affirmations, closing supportive statements). Some affirmations feel repetitive and occasionally evaluative/paternalistic (“I’m proud of you” used frequently) rather than individualized normalization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and accurate reflections of mixed emotion (hopeful/nervous, “feeling gross”). Missed opportunities for deeper, affect-laden reflections probing shame/self-judgment and the personal meaning of exercise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, sustained focus on restarting exercise and implementation (specific days/times, implementation intentions, reminders, 10‑minute restarts, scheduling follow-up). Consistently closes with concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—invites choices, negotiates check‑in time, co-constructs plan. At times offers concrete strategies directly without explicitly eliciting the patient’s ideas first or asking permission before advising.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and asks preferences, but leans toward directive language and solution-offering without explicit permission (“make an implementation intention,” “lay out shoes,” “I’ll send a reminder”). Could strengthen by more explicitly emphasizing patient control and eliciting her ideas first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits patient-generated solutions and motivations (choice of plan, confidence ruler, calendar check idea). Could deepen evocation by linking reasons/values more directly to personal life and sequencing to amplify motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functions mostly as an equal partner—negotiates, invites input, uses collaborative language. Occasionally slips into an expert stance with directive suggestions and repetitive evaluative praise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces clear change talk (multiple commitment statements and concrete plans). Would benefit from more strategic amplification and summarizing of change talk to build stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustains minimal sustain talk; clinician reframes lapses constructively and normalizes difficulty (“Be gentle with slips,” 10‑minute restarts). Could further use MI strategies (double-sided reflections, selective emphasis) when ambivalence appears to more actively soften sustain talk.
