# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly communicated unconditional positive regard multiple times (“I accept both the effort and the difficulty…”) and validated both success and struggle across the session (at least four distinct acceptance/validation statements). Suggestion: occasionally name specific emotions to deepen warmth.
- [x] Unconditional positive regard/verbal acceptance (≥4 instances)
- [x] Acknowledged successes and struggles without judgment
- [ ] Named specific emotions (limited; suggested improvement)

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections and paraphrases (“I hear you…”, “I hear pride and strain…”) demonstrating perspective-taking (≥8 reflective statements). Reflections sometimes formulaic and focused on facts rather than affect; deeper affective reflections were limited.
- [x] Frequent accurate reflections/paraphrases
- [x] Demonstrated understanding of ambivalence
- [ ] Complex/affective reflections naming underlying emotions (limited)

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking reduction and moved toward a concrete, time-limited plan (10‑minute delay, gum backup, 12:30 text, 12:25 alarm). Repeatedly narrowed options and asked for a first actionable step.
- [x] Kept session on target behavior
- [x] Elicited and finalized an actionable plan
- [x] Summarized/closed decision points

#### **Score (1–5):** 5

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (patient noted cutting two cigarettes, goal of 5/day, willingness to try delay and set supports). Missed opportunity to more strongly amplify/consolidate commitment (no 0–10 importance/confidence scaling or explicit commitment probe).
- [x] Reflected and reinforced change talk (progress & goals)
- [x] Encouraged actionable commitment (plan selection)
- [ ] Explicit amplification/consolidation of commitment (scaling or stronger commitment language)

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing (double‑sided reflections: “proud but worried”), and reframed ambivalence repeatedly. Could more actively explore discrepancy or probe the costs of continuing smoking to further weaken sustain talk.
- [x] Recognized and reflected sustain talk
- [x] Used double‑sided reflections to contain ambivalence
- [ ] Deeper exploration of discrepancy / costs of continuing (recommended)
