# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized the patient's constraints with repeated validations and nonjudgmental reflections. The clinician avoided criticism and affirmed the patient's efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, specific empathic reflections of both content and feeling and frequently invited elaboration. Reflections captured ambivalence, practical constraints, and emotional concerns, supporting strong empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained focused direction on smoking/exposure reduction and progressed logically to a concrete, time-limited one-week plan. Questions narrowed appropriately to supports and measurable signs of success without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in true co-construction of the plan, repeatedly soliciting the patient's ideas and preferences. The patient's contributions shaped the strategy, reflecting a consistently collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced the patient's choice and control by offering options and treating her as the expert. The clinician avoided pressure and checked what the patient was willing to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out internal reasons for change (protecting her son, reducing guilt) and elicited concrete strategies. Could further deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring values, past successes, and using scaling or commitment questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly adopted a partnership stance and shared power, using language that delegated ownership to the patient. Repeated invitations to choose among options reinforced the collaborative decision-making process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple pieces of change talk and helped the patient identify a concrete, small-step plan. Missed opportunities included amplifying/summarizing strong change statements and eliciting a clear commitment or confidence rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double-sided reflections and harm-reduction framing to reduce defensiveness. Could more explicitly contrast values and current behavior to more actively resolve ambivalence.
