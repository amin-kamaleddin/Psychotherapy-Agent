# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validation and normalization, avoiding blame or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used several empathic statements and reflections that built rapport, but reflections were mostly surface-level and missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings like resignation or loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on daily blood glucose checking and meds, moving from problem identification to a narrowly defined, actionable plan with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was consistently collaborative—invited input, offered to troubleshoot, and set a joint check-back—treating the patient as a partner with useful context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice and control by offering options, framing the trial as optional/time-limited, asking permission before instructions, and normalizing misses.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical behavioral commitments but did little to draw out the patient's personal motivations, values, or confidence to sustain long-term change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and invited preferences, but frequently shifted toward advising with specific instructions rather than eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encouraged concrete commitments and secured a three-day trial that produced change talk, yet did not sufficiently elicit the patient's personal reasons or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized skepticism and offered low-pressure choices to reduce sustain talk, though double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence were underused.
