# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect through validating reflections and honoring Mateo’s limits (e.g., accepting stopping if pain increases). Language is nonjudgmental and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate understanding via timely reflections and summaries that capture both feelings and content (pain, discouragement, desire to preserve mobility). Reflections validate Mateo’s experience and integrate his concerns throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior (one short set at home) and consistently ties questions and reflections back to safety, timing, supports, and a specific actionable plan. Moves session from ambivalence to a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is consistently collaborative with options offered and decisions co-constructed (timing, pacing, supports), and frequent checks to ensure the plan fits Mateo’s preferences. Uses inclusive language and solicits his input at each step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by framing the plan as Mateo’s choice, normalizing stopping, and emphasizing his control over pace and safety. Repeatedly asks permission and respects his limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits internal motivations (e.g., preserving mobility, not being a burden) but could more consistently evoke and deepen desire, reasons, and confidence with techniques like scaling or targeted questions. More frequent explicit evocation would strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly treats Mateo as the expert on his experience and co-creates the plan, letting him choose supports and pacing. The clinician refrains from prescribing and consistently seeks Mateo’s preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits tentative change talk and helps shape a specific plan, but misses opportunities to amplify and summarize desire/ability/reasons and to ask for a confidence/commitment rating. Stronger selective reflections and summary of change language would consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without confronting it and pivots to practical problem-solving and safety, reducing resistance. At times reflections repeat sustain talk verbatim; more double-sided reflections would better soften sustain talk.
