# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed respect and positive regard, affirming strengths (e.g., quitting smoking) without judgment. Patient concerns were normalized and acknowledged throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate reflections and summaries that matched the patient's language and affect, deepening disclosure. This listening fostered safety and helped the patient articulate specifics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on limiting drinking in social situations with each exchange tied to concrete strategies. Progressed logically from options to a specific rehearsed plan and reminder cue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician co-constructed the plan and repeatedly solicited the patient's preferences, adjusting suggestions to fit his comfort. Resources and supports were offered nonprescriptively with the patient driving decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered choices and emphasized the patient's control over decisions, avoiding pressuring language. Supports (practice, cues) were presented as optional tools the patient could accept or decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited practical commitment (selected script, rehearsals, cue) but relied mainly on logistical motivation. Opportunities remained to deepen personal motives, values, and confidence ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with shared power and expertise; the clinician proposed options and followed the patient's selections. The patient's choices meaningfully shaped each next step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician evoked concrete action-oriented change talk (script selection, rehearsal plans, cue) that supported behavior planning. More probing of deeper desire, importance, and confidence could have strengthened internalized motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician validated sustain-related worries (peer pressure, sounding rude) and avoided arguing, then pivoted to planning to reduce sustain talk. Using more double-sided reflections or exploring what is appealing about giving in might further weaken sustain language.
