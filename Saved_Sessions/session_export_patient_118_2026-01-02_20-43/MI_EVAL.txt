# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
Checklist (checked = behaviors explicitly observed)
- [x] Communicates respect and avoids judgment (e.g., "I hear you — you want the dental work finished...")
- [x] Permission-giving / honors patient choices ("Yes — you can go"; "If you change your mind... reach out")
- [ ] Deep normalization of feelings (not observed)
  
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently respectful and supportive, repeatedly granting permission and honoring boundaries about not discussing smoking. Reflections were warm but repetitive; missed opportunity to normalize frustration more richly (e.g., “It makes sense you’d be fed up…”).

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3  
Checklist
- [x] Basic reflective listening and paraphrase present ("I hear you — you want the dental work finished...")
- [ ] Exploration of emotional tone (irritation, fear, discouragement) — minimal/not developed
- [ ] Varied/deeper reflective complexity — limited (reflections were repetitive)

**Comments:** Adequate but uneven empathy. Accurate surface reflections and recaps occurred, but the clinician did not probe underlying feelings about medication side effects or sustain talk; reflections became rote rather than deepening understanding.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3  
Checklist
- [x] Practical, goal-oriented steps set (call dentist today; ask about procedures/timeline/healing; set alarm for 1‑minute breathing)
- [ ] Strategic evocation toward smoking change (missed/indirect)
- [ ] Consistent guided movement toward broader behavior change (limited)

**Comments:** Clinician kept the visit focused on a concrete goal (dental treatment) and produced actionable steps. However, guidance toward smoking behavior change was indirect (only via asking dentist) and there were missed opportunities to gently elicit ambivalence or change talk about smoking.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
Checklist
- [x] Offers choices and asks patient preference ("Which feels most helpful to start with?")
- [x] Frames suggestions as options (rehearse call optional; alarm optional)
- [ ] Explicit co-creation of meaning/values (limited)

**Comments:** Overall collaborative stance — clinician invited the patient to choose actions and offered options rather than prescribing. Some recaps felt procedural, but the partnership orientation was clear.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
Checklist
- [x] Clearly honors patient autonomy and decisions ("I hear you... don't want to discuss smoking right now"; "Yes — you can go")
- [x] Offers optional supports rather than mandates (rehearsal optional; brief coping step suggested)
  
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout. Clinician consistently reinforced the patient's right to decline discussion and framed interventions as suggestions.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2  
Checklist
- [ ] Elicits patient's own motivations for change (mostly absent)
- [x] Brief link to change via practical question to dentist (indirect evocation)
  
**Comments:** Evocation of the patient's reasons for change was minimal. The only tie to behavior change was an indirect task (ask dentist whether smoking affects healing) rather than eliciting the patient's own change talk.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
Checklist
- [x] Shared decision-making behaviors observed (choice of step, optional rehearsal)
- [ ] Deep joint agenda-setting or values exploration (not demonstrated)
  
**Comments:** Clinician partnered well around immediate tasks and respected the patient's agenda. Would strengthen partnership by co-creating links between dental goals and longer-term health values.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2  
Checklist
- [ ] Elicits desire/ability/reasons/need for change about smoking (not observed)
- [x] Reinforced practical steps that could indirectly support change (call dentist)
  
**Comments:** Little to no direct elicitation or reinforcement of change talk regarding smoking. Missed opportunity to ask about what matters to the patient related to dental outcomes and how smoking fits with those priorities.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2  
Checklist
- [x] Respected sustain talk (honored patient's wish not to discuss smoking)
- [ ] Used double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance to gently soften sustain talk (not observed)
  
**Comments:** Clinician appropriately accepted the patient's sustain talk and did not push, but did not use techniques (double-sided reflections, complex reflections) to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence and gently tilt toward consideration of change.
