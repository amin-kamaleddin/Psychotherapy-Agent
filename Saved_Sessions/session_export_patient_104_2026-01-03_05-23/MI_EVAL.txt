# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Communicates unconditional positive regard (e.g., “That shows care and resilience.”)
- [x] Normalizes feelings (e.g., “That makes total sense and it’s okay to feel torn.”)
- [x] Refrains from judgment / validates effort
- [ ] Confronts discord

**Comments:** Consistent warm, nonjudgmental stance across the session (4 clear acceptance statements). Validates parenting effort and normalizes distress, supporting rapport and safety.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Uses accurate reflective listening (“I hear you—‘up all night,’ ‘exhausted’…”)
- [x] Paraphrases emotional content and tone (captures “zombie,” “scared to hope”)
- [x] Makes implicit feelings explicit (clarifies worries about failing)
- [ ] Minimal empathic responses

**Comments:** Multiple precise, complex reflections that capture both content and affect, demonstrating strong perspective-taking and helping client feel understood.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Maintains clear focus on target behavior (sleep/rest → 90‑minute nap)
- [x] Elicits goal and narrows to actionable target (“What’s one small sign…?”, 90‑minute nap)
- [x] Moves toward a concrete, doable plan (specific text, backup options)
- [ ] Loses focus / wanders

**Comments:** Sustained and purposeful focus on rest/napping with stepwise movement from goal-setting to a specific plan and contingencies.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Invites client input and choice (“Which of those options feels most doable…?”)
- [x] Co‑constructs the plan (rehearses wording, offers options)
- [x] Shares power / respects client preference (accepts one‑line text choice)
- [ ] Clinician directs without client input

**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance; clinician offers options and follows the client’s preferences, rehearsing language together.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Supports client agency (“Which feels most natural to you…?”)
- [x] Offers options rather than directives (partner vs home visitor vs swap)
- [x] Avoids pressure; emphasizes choice
- [ ] Uses controlling language

**Comments:** Explicit support for Nadine’s choices and agency; clinician facilitates decision-making without pressuring or prescribing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Elicits client’s own motives and ideas (asks for “one small sign” and “one very small, doable step”)
- [x] Encourages client-generated solutions (client crafts exact text)
- [x] Uses evocative questions to draw out change talk
- [ ] Relies on clinician suggestions only

**Comments:** Repeated evocative questioning prompted client to produce change-oriented responses and a concrete plan; clinician amplified client-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Treats client as expert on her life
- [x] Works alongside client to problem-solve (brainstorming backups, rehearsing text)
- [x] Reflects client choices in the plan (one‑line text, backup home visitor)
- [ ] Expert/authority stance

**Comments:** Clinician adopted a collaborative partner stance throughout, co-creating a plan that reflected Nadine’s preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Elicits desire and reasons for change (wanting to feel “more like myself,” less snapping)
- [x] Elicits ability/plan (identifies asking partner, specific text)
- [x] Amplifies and reinforces client’s change statements
- [ ] Explicitly elicits commitment statement (opportunity not fully used)

**Comments:** Multiple instances of elicited and amplified change talk leading to a specific, doable action; could have closed with an explicit commitment question.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Acknowledges sustain/concern talk (fears: “scared to hope,” worried partner will say no)
- [x] Normalizes ambivalence (“it’s okay to feel torn”)
- [x] Gently redirects toward solutions and contingency planning
- [ ] Uses double‑sided reflections explicitly to contrast ambivalence

**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and gently redirected; opportunity to more explicitly use double‑sided reflections or a summary to further mitigate sustain talk and strengthen resolution.
