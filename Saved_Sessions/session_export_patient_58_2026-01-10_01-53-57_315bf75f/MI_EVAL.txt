# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive—validates feelings, affirms efforts, and normalizes setbacks throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening with tailored suggestions, but missed deeper complex reflections on ambivalence between 'for me' and 'so people will get off my back.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on school punctuality with tightly linked interventions and measurable goals (e.g., out by 8:00).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-produced—solicited preferences, practiced scripts, role-played, and incorporated Jamal's ideas into the checklist and reward plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and emphasizes Jamal's ownership, but occasionally provides prescriptive plans without explicitly checking which options Jamal prefers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment language but does not systematically draw out Jamal's internal reasons, values, or use scaling to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively—asks permission and invites feedback and role-play—but sometimes leads with clinician-generated strategies rather than co-discovering them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforces some change talk and commitment but quickly shifts to offering solutions instead of using open questions to explore personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively redirects sustain talk with scripts and boundary strategies, though could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
