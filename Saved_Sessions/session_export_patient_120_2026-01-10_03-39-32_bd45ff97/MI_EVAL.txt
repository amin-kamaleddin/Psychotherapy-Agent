# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect, reassurance, and normalization repeatedly, which communicates supportive, nonjudgmental regard. Some responses are formulaic and quickly followed by directives, making acceptance feel partly procedural rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses basic empathic statements and reassurances that validate the patient's fear and effort. Lacks deeper reflective listening and complex/double-sided reflections to capture ambivalence and emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use during pregnancy with concrete harm-reduction and safety strategies. Repeatedly returns to practical weekend planning, OB contact, and coping skills to keep the session targeted.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites patient input at points (choice of focus, offering to draft messages) which shows some collaboration. However, the clinician frequently supplies ready-made scripts and directives instead of co-creating wording and plans with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices and frames some actions as the patient's decision, supporting autonomy at times. Several strong directives and prescriptive language reduce perceived ownership, and the clinician seldom asks permission before giving advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Seldom elicits the patient's own motives, values, or strengths and uses few open, evocative questions. The session relies mainly on advice and scripts rather than drawing out intrinsic motivation or commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of shared agenda-setting and offers to collaborate on plans, indicating some partnership. Overall the clinician often assumes an expert stance, delivering tools and scripts instead of jointly generating them with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and lightly reinforces the patient's change talk when it appears. Tends to move quickly to solutions and scripts rather than systematically eliciting, amplifying, and exploring the patient's own reasons, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and provides many practical strategies to reduce drinking opportunities and peer pressure. Does not routinely use reflective, double-sided techniques to soften sustain talk or strategically elicit the patient's resolution away from it.
