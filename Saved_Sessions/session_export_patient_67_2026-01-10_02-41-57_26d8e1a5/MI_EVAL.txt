# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used warm, nonjudgmental language, affirmed choices, and normalized ambivalence, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and acknowledgement of uncertainty and ambivalence, but missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections to surface underlying emotions like shame or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on reducing alcohol use with specific baseline data, readiness assessment, and a concrete, measurable plan tied to follow-up steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as shared problem-solving with co-created plans, frequent checks of preferences, and avoidance of a hierarchical stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently offered choices, asked permission before questions, and emphasized that decisions were the patient's, reinforcing the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit motivation (readiness ruler, consequences) produced change-language, but the clinician relied heavily on providing strategies rather than systematically drawing out the patient's own reasons or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and inviting of patient input with co-created plans, though the clinician sometimes led with specific suggestions rather than eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (tracking commitment, choosing an alcohol-free day) but did not consistently reflect, amplify, or follow up on DARN statements to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without confrontation, but the clinician used few double-sided reflections or reframes and often shifted quickly to practical advice instead of softening sustain talk strategically.
