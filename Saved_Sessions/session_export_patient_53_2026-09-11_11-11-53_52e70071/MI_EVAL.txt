# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates clear, unconditional positive regard with frequent normalization and explicit acceptance statements; avoids judgmental language throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking capture both desire and worry, consistently validating Isabella's experience and naming specific fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on increasing community involvement through progressively narrowed, concrete micro‑steps that move toward an actionable plan without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language and repeatedly invites Isabella's preferences, offering to co‑support actions (open page, stay while she reads) and positioning the clinician as a partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently preserves choice by offering multiple options, validating pace, and asking which tiny step Isabella prefers, reinforcing her ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks evocative, choice‑oriented questions that elicit change language and micro‑commitments, but could more actively draw out reasons, importance, and confidence to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as collaborative and invites shared decision‑making, though offering several options at once occasionally risks steering rather than purely following Isabella's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete change talk and small commitments (e.g., previewing group, setting a timer) but could better amplify and reflect those statements and assess confidence/importance to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and uses double‑sided reflections without confrontation, but tends to move quickly to planning instead of more deeply exploring or reframing the functions of staying home.
