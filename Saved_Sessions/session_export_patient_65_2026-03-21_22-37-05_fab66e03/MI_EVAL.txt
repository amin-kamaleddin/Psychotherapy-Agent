# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm with frequent validations that normalize the client’s experience and affirm effort. Tone models unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately names emotions and practical concerns, showing good perspective‑taking. Reflections are somewhat repetitive and could probe deeper into underlying feelings or meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on completing court‑mandated community service with concrete problem‑solving steps. Each turn reliably links back to the central task without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Presented as shared problem‑solving with frequent solicitation of the patient’s preferences and approval. Occasional clinician-led offers (drafting, tracking) create some asymmetry, but patient input is continually sought and respected.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirms choice and asks permission before acting, offering options rather than directives. Emphasizes the client’s control and fosters ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits pragmatic, logistics‑based motivation but does not sufficiently draw out internal reasons, values, or confidence. Few evocative questions or scaling prompts were used to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly collaborative with co‑creation of next steps and repeated requests for assent. The clinician sometimes takes the lead (offering to draft or track), which reduces pure partnership at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Encourages small, practical change talk and commitment to a concrete task (drafting/sending a message) but misses opportunities to amplify desire, reasons, or confidence. Lacks strategic questions to strengthen or consolidate commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses reflections that validate while gently redirecting toward action. Could more often use double‑sided reflections to juxtapose doubts with the client’s reasons for change.
