# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Linda’s concerns with validating statements and offers of support (grounding, role‑play, drafting). No stigmatizing language or minimization; clinician repeatedly affirmed feelings and offered options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate empathic listening and perspective‑taking (reflections, checking goals), but missed some opportunities for deeper, complex reflections after Linda voiced repeated fear about side effects and dependence. The clinician often shifted to factual education rather than exploring emotional meaning more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on improving asthma management and medication decisions from start to finish. Provided specific, actionable steps (confirm dose, technique/spacer, time‑limited step‑down, written plan) with no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as true collaboration: the clinician solicited input, co‑edited the patient’s email, responded to micro‑preferences, and checked comfort throughout. Mutual problem‑solving was evident rather than clinician dominance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and endorsing shared decision‑making while honoring Linda’s pace and hesitancy. Avoided pressuring language and framed interventions as choices rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some intentions and concrete plans (email, appointment, step‑down trial), but relied substantially on providing advice and information. Could increase open questions to draw out Linda’s personal reasons, values, and confidence to generate more internal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and power‑sharing: clinician invited Linda’s agenda, asked permission before interventions, and co‑constructed the email while checking for her preferences. Behaviors conveyed that expertise about her life and choices resided with Linda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk by inviting concrete actions and co‑creating a plan/email that led to stated intentions. Missed opportunities to deepen desire, reasons, confidence, and commitment through more open questions and targeted elicitors.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated hesitancy and offered corrective information and safer alternatives rather than reinforcing sustain talk. Tended toward informational rebuttal rather than using double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to tip the balance toward change.
