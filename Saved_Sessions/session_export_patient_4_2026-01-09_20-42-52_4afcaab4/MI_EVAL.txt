# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with multiple validating statements and explicit acknowledgement of the impact on her life; avoided blaming or minimizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid empathic listening and accurate reflections of symptom burden and fear, but some reflections remained surface-level and did not consistently explore deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the presenting problem with concrete actions (drafting the letter, ordering tests, arranging follow-up) and kept the session moving toward specific next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with joint problem-solving and invitations for input on the letter and plan, though the clinician occasionally led content strongly which reduced parity somewhat.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking preferences, seeking permission for drafting and role‑play, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions and brief intrinsic content emerged (eased at wedding, worse when husband away), but the clinician did not follow up to draw out values, reasons, or personal goals for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited priorities and co-constructed materials at times, but frequently adopted an expert role by specifying tests and exact wording without first eliciting the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited limited change-oriented language (offered coping options), yet did not systematically amplify or probe for change talk, confidence, or feasibility despite clear opportunities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and accommodated requests for tests, but did not use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to reduce sustain talk and gently shift toward psychosocial explanations.
