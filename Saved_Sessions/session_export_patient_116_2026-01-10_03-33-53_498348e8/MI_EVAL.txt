# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed unconditional positive regard, honored Samir's guarded tone, and offered options rather than pressure. No shaming or moralizing language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with accurate reflections and summaries that made Samir feel heard. The clinician could deepen emotion-focused reflections and probe meanings (e.g., what 'dependence' means) to elicit more disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on medication management with concrete behavioral goals (scripts, a 2‑week trial, follow‑up, taper plan). The clinician kept returning to practical next steps rather than digressing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High collaboration—scripts and plans were co‑created and tailored to Samir's requests, and his input meaningfully shaped the session outcomes. Role‑play and iterative edits reinforced shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported Samir's sense of choice, asked permission, and affirmed that decisions were his to make. Samir's conditions (written goals, time limits) were accepted and operationalized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited concrete motivations and commitments (goals, scripts, conditional 2‑week trial) but supplied substantial clinician language that reduced Samir-generated change talk. More targeted evocative questions (rulers, past successes) would strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician adopted a genuine partnership stance, repeatedly inviting Samir's input and co-authoring the plan. Power was shared through choices, tailored wording, and joint goal setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons and actionable change language (desire for less pain/fatigue, selection of scripts, agreement to a trial). The clinician could more deliberately evoke and amplify DARN (desire/ability/reasons/need) statements and use scaling to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist rolled with resistance, validated skepticism, and avoided arguing, which prevented amplification of sustain talk. Using double‑sided reflections or explicitly naming ambivalence could further help resolve sustain talk into change talk.
