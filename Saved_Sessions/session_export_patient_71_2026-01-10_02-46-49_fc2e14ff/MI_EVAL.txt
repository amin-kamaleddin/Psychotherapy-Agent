# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, validating, and nonjudgmental; clinician models unconditional positive regard and normalizes lapses throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic understanding and occasional reflections of struggle, but offers few deep, complex reflections of emotion or underlying meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on establishing a small yoga habit and repeatedly brings the conversation back to actionable planning and review.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with co‑constructed plans and choice offering, though ready‑made scripts and detailed settings somewhat reduce parity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering options, asking permission before drafting content, and framing slips as data, consistently reinforcing patient choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and links the patient's values to concrete steps and commitment language, but could deepen evocation with more questions about personal meaning and confidence/importance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and choices but frequently shifts to an expert role with numerous templates and scripts before eliciting the patient's own ideas, reducing shared control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws and reinforces change talk by prompting a micro habit and eliciting commitments, but could more consistently amplify the patient's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectfully normalizes and redirects sustain talk with backups and reframes, yet relies more on solution‑giving than MI‑specific softening techniques like double‑sided reflections.
