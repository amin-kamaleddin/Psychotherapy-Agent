# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Mai’s confusion, validating her concerns and offering options rather than judging. Language throughout respects and affirms the patient's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking with accurate, timely reflections and follow-up support. Empathic language is steady and used to clarify Mai's uncertainty and need for clear information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on the decision about atorvastatin and concrete monitoring/adherence tasks throughout the session. The clinician consistently returns to actionable steps and timelines without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The encounter is clearly joint: the clinician co-drafts messages, agrees on monitoring plans, and adapts wording to the patient's preferences. Decision-making is shared and Mai’s input drives the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly supported from the outset and reinforced by offering options, asking permission, and affirming the patient's right to choose. The clinician tailors recommendations to Mai's preferences and invites her leadership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal motivation (worries, confidence rating, conditions for success) but the session remains information-rich and clinician-driven. Could deepen evocation by asking more about personal values and reflecting patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction with co-creation of materials, role-play, and solicitation of the patient's priorities. The clinician repeatedly asks permission and incorporates Mai's choices into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits change talk (specific plans, confidence rating) but often moves quickly to information-giving and planning. Would benefit from more deliberate reflections and amplification of the patient's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and validates uncertainty, offering options rather than confronting doubt. Missed opportunities include more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence and guide toward change.
