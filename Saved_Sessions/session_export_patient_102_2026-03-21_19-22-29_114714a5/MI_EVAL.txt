# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through nonjudgmental language, permission-seeking, and repeated reassurance. Avoided moralizing or shaming and normalized a low-pressure approach throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with validating summaries. Missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections of the teen's underlying feelings (e.g., regret, defensiveness, social pressure).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related harm reduction from start to finish. Consistently linked suggestions back to the goal of avoiding 'dumb mistakes' and avoided sidetracks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner by co-creating the plan, offering options, and checking the patient's preferences (including drafting and tweaking the friend text). Treated the patient as an active contributor rather than a passive recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice with repeated 'no pressure' language, respect for boundaries, and offering options rather than mandates. Affirmed the patient's right to decline quitting or formal follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited commitment to specific harm-reduction behaviors but relied more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient's own values and reasons. Missed openings to ask about what matters to the patient or to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as collaborative, asked permission for steps, and invited the patient's choices in planning. Co-constructed concrete strategies (friend cue, phone, ride) with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced chosen practical steps which produced some commitment, but did not systematically elicit desire, reasons, importance, or confidence for change. Underused open, evocative questions to amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing and did not reinforce minimizations, shifting gently to harm-reduction strategies. Could strengthen by using double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change.
