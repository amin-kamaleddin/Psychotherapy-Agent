# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth through validating language and respectful tone, normalizing ambivalence and treating the client as capable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and helpful summaries that capture the core conflict and motivations; could deepen empathic work with more feeling‑laden or complex reflections (shame, fear, identity) at key moments.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing heavy drinking, attending the programme, and steps toward reunification, repeatedly moving the session toward concrete, behavior‑linked actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative throughout—co‑drafts scripts, invites role‑play, and asks for the client's preferences so decisions are jointly produced rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports autonomy with choice, invitational language, and repeated emphasis that the client owns the plan without pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's own reasons and commitment for change using open questions and scaling, then builds on those intrinsic motives to develop plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters an equal, collaborative stance by asking permission, inviting input, and positioning the client as the expert in co‑constructing actionable steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk through targeted questions and summaries, prompting specific desire, reasons, ability, and commitment language that the client then articulates.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes ambivalence toward planning, but could more explicitly use double‑sided or strategic reflections to name and pivot away from sustain talk.
