# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes concerns, using validating language and a nonjudgmental tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and mirrors the patient's language well, though reflections are sometimes repetitive and could probe deeper affective meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on starting cholesterol medication and scaffolds the conversation from values to concrete steps, ending with a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine joint problem-solving, inviting David's input at every step and co-creating the plan rather than delivering top-down advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and control, offering options and framing actions as voluntary without applying pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits intrinsic motivations and practical reasons for change and secures concrete commitments, but could use scaling and eliciting the patient's own arguments to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly shares power and fosters collaboration by asking permission implicitly and letting the patient shape decisions and supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and shapes change talk toward specific actions (calling the clinic, selecting supports) but could more explicitly link change to the patient's values and past successes to amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validates and normalizes sustain talk appropriately but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or selectively amplify change talk to pivot away from sustain talk.
