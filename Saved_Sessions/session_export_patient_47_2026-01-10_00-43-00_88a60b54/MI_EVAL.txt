# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Evelyn’s experience using warm, nonjudgmental language. Reassurances and validating statements supported a safe, accepting atmosphere throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated frequent, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured both content and ambivalence. Reflections and summaries allowed Evelyn to expand on feelings and plans.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use with concrete behavioral goals (one less glass, swapping to sparkling water, skipping weeknights). Repeatedly steered the conversation toward actionable planning and realistic steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed with inclusive language and solicitation of Evelyn’s ideas at each step. The clinician adapted the plan to Evelyn’s preferences rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized Evelyn’s choice and offered multiple options while checking fit and normalizing her decisions. This fostered ownership and avoided directive or coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation by exploring what matters to Evelyn and generated concrete change talk and a plan. Could more deeply evoke intrinsic motives, long-term values, and use importance/confidence scaling to strengthen internal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly framed the interaction as collaborative and invited Evelyn to co-create the plan, sharing power and deferring to her expertise. Options were offered and the client’s preferences shaped the chosen strategy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and asked targeted questions to invite specific change language. Could more actively amplify, summarize, and elicit stronger commitment or confidence statements to consolidate momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double-sided reflections to validate ambivalence without arguing. A more strategic emphasis on selectively reflecting change-preferring statements could further shift momentum away from minimization.
