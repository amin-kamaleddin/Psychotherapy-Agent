# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful stance (e.g., “It sounds like it’s important to you as self‑expression,” “That’s a practical, balanced plan that honors your choice”). No shaming or minimizing; repeated affirmations supported unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate paraphrasing and reflective listening captured Marisol’s stance and eased anxiety (“the dentist’s warning gave you some pause without changing your mind”; “I hear you — you want to keep expressing yourself while protecting your health”). Fewer deeper/complex reflections exploring emotional meaning or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on oral‑health risk reduction and monitoring (0–10 readiness scale used twice, elicited specific protective steps, recommended a 3‑month check, listed red‑flag signs). Negligible topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co‑constructed: counselor asked permission to practice questions, solicited Marisol’s ideas (rinses, alarms), and used inclusive language (“we’ll run them together”). Patient contributions clearly shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor repeatedly affirmed choice and emphasized ownership (“honors your choice,” “You sound prepared and in control”), offered options rather than directives, and avoided pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation of patient reasons and plans (asked what would help, elicited rinses/alarms/check‑up; readiness shift ≈3–4 → 2). Could have probed motivations and values more to deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Power‑sharing evident (invited input, offered to rehearse). Occasional clinician‑led elements (providing exact phrasing, specific checklist) useful but slightly more directive than fully patient‑led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some patient change language and readiness movement; counselor asked good eliciting questions but missed opportunities to amplify and convert change talk into explicit commitment (e.g., ask for confidence, elicit specific commitment statements).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor avoided arguing and validated sustain talk (“I’m not planning to take it out — I like it”), then redirected to harm reduction and monitoring. Double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides were used implicitly but could be stronger.
