# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validates the patient's plan, and offers choices and options. Could add more individualized verbal affirmation or normalizing statements to reach a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some perspective-taking (e.g., 'That sounds reasonable') but offers few reflective or affective statements. The clinician moves quickly to information and instructions rather than exploring or reflecting the patient's skepticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, clinically appropriate focus on blood pressure and lifestyle factors with concrete targets and a 2-week trial. Spending substantial time on procedural detail limited opportunities to explore the patient's motivations or barriers further.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative by offering to review the log and asking format preferences, treating the patient as a partner. The interaction is somewhat clinician-driven in plan content; more shared problem-solving would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, endorsing tracking first, and avoiding pressure, repeatedly leaving decisions to the patient. This clear reinforcement of autonomy justifies the top score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicitation of the patient's intrinsic motivations and values is minimal, relying mainly on information and instructions. The clinician missed opportunities to ask evocative questions or draw out discrepancy between goals and current drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicits preferences and invites the patient's input (e.g., format, whether to receive help) and offers to review results, conveying partnership. However, the clinician occasionally shifts into teaching mode rather than co-creating specific steps with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Creates structural opportunities for change talk via tracking and a trial but rarely asks evocative questions or reflects/amplifies tentative change language. The clinician missed chances to elicit the patient's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not confront sustain talk and redirects neutrally (e.g., offering a reversible trial), which prevents escalation. More active techniques—such as double-sided reflections or reframing—would better soften sustain talk.
