# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with validating language, safety checks, and normalization of slips throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and reflective questions that acknowledge mixed feelings, though several reflections remained surface-level and missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking and alcohol goals and moves logically from concerns to concrete, behaviorally-focused plans and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong co‑construction of plans and invitations for patient input, but the clinician sometimes leaned toward providing scripted tactics, creating occasional asymmetry in the interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offers choices, asks permission, and accepts the patient's limits, reinforcing the patient's sense of ownership and pace for change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation of values, motives, and barriers that produced patient-generated plans, though the clinician often moved quickly to offering solutions instead of deepening intrinsic motivation or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with shared decision-making and option-offering, but frequent prescriptive suggestions occasionally shifted the stance toward expert-led guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and reinforces change language and practical commitments (e.g., smoke-free home), but could do more to amplify and summarize the patient's own reasons and assess confidence/ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids amplifying sustain talk and accepts limits, but did not systematically use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided or evocative reflections) to diminish sustain talk or explore ambivalence.
