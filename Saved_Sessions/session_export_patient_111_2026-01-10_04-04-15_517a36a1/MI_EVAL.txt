# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence with validating, nonjudgmental language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and timely reflective listening that mirrors the patient's values and feelings, making the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear behavioral focus on smoking with a progressively concrete target (moving smoking to the porch) and actionable planning without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructs the porch plan with the patient, repeatedly solicits preferences, and adopts the patient's practical suggestions, showing equal partnership in planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently foregrounds the patient's choice and control, avoids pressure, and invites the patient to tailor and accept or reject plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation anchored to protecting the marriage and draws out practical change ideas, but does not sufficiently deepen intrinsic reasons, confidence, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative and defers to the patient's expertise, asking for ideas and choices that shape the agreed plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits specific, concrete change talk about porch smoking and logistics, but focuses mainly on externally motivated reasons and misses chances to amplify internal motivations and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and validates the compromise, but does not deploy advanced strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections or eliciting costs) to shift sustain talk more effectively.
