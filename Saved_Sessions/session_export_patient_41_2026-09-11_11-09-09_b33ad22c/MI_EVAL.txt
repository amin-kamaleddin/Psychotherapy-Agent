# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys strong unconditional positive regard through validation, normalizing ambivalence, and honoring the patient’s perspective. Statements consistently avoid blame and communicate respect for Elira’s experience and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that mirror content and hesitancy, supporting rapport. Reflections tended to stay at the content/strategy level; deeper affect-focused reflections (fear, shame, meaning) would increase empathic resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the specific behavioral target and steadily moves the conversation toward an achievable, measurable action. Questions and reflections consistently return to concrete planning and iteration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction with the clinician offering options and integrating Elira’s choices and tweaks into the plan. The clinician co-constructs solutions rather than prescribing them, showing genuine two-way teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong support for autonomy through explicit statements that Elira is the expert and frequent invitations to choose among options. The clinician avoids pressuring language and accepts hesitations while offering choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Elira’s own motivations and concrete solutions via tiny-experiment framing and choices. Could deepen evocation by probing personal meaning/values behind the desired change and more frequently amplifying change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Partnership is a clear strength: the psychiatrist repeatedly positions Elira as the expert and invites her to select and refine options. Decision-making is client-led and collaborative throughout the exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits specific change statements with small options, scaling, and implementation tweaks that produced concrete commitments. To strengthen change talk further, the clinician could more consistently reflect and amplify change language and elicit clearer commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk, avoiding confrontation, but often repeats sustain content without reframing it. More use of double-sided reflections, strategic summaries, and pivots toward change talk would better reduce the pull of avoidance.
