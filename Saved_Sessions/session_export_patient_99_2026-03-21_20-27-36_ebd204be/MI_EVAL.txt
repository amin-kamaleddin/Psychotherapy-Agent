# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (warm tone, validating opening statements). No shaming or moralizing language and the client’s perspective was respected throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate summaries of Matteo’s plan and barriers. Missed a few opportunities to deepen emotional content and link concerns to longer‑term values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on reducing risky alcohol use and injury across the session. Progressed logically from exploration to a concrete, actionable plan for the upcoming Wednesday.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was largely co‑constructed with the client invited to choose strategies and rehearse lines. Collaboration was slightly weakened when the clinician offered a long list of solutions that felt more clinician‑driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Matteo’s choice and asked permission before offering ideas, avoiding pressuring language. Framed suggestions as options and respected the client’s control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic motives tied to the ankle/injury and used scaling questions to probe readiness. Could have probed deeper into personal values and amplified spontaneous change language to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed shared decision‑making and invited the client to craft messages and rehearse responses. At times the clinician led with solution lists rather than first eliciting the client’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced specific change talk, using scaling and detailed planning to generate commitment language. Could increase reflective amplification and ask more values‑linked questions to convert change talk into firmer commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk about social pressures but largely accepted it neutrally rather than using double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing. More explicit softening techniques would help reduce 'missing out' language.
