# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician treated Oliver respectfully and normalized his guilt ("Remind yourself guilt is normal"), used affirmations and supportive closures. Tone was steady and nonjudgmental, though several interventions were somewhat formulaic/instructional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening (e.g., "I hear how torn you feel — that push for independence and the guilt") and affect validation were present. Reflections were generally high‑level; missed some opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on the target behavior (getting the flatmate to move out). Provided concrete timeline, scripts, modeled→practice→feedback structure, pushback responses, and contingency/safety planning (Jan 17, backup plans).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited input and choices (permission to role‑play, offered alternatives, accepted texting). Interaction leaned toward expert/coach stance with prescriptive scripts; could have elicited more of Oliver’s own wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected agency by offering options (tweaks, texting, role‑play) and safety choices. Frequent directive phrasing and modeled scripts risked reducing perceived ownership at times.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some indirect evocation through rehearsal and acknowledgement of ambivalence, but few open questions to draw out Oliver’s intrinsic motives, values, or consequences. Session was primarily skills‑coaching.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively as a coach (modeling, then having Oliver try, brief feedback). Clinician more often led content and phrasing rather than co‑creating language from Oliver’s voice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver produced meaningful change talk and clinician reinforced some (praise, practice structure). Missed opportunities to elicit and amplify additional desire/reason/need/commitment language and confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and guilt normalized. Some initial formulations and pushback scripts were more confrontational before being softened; could use more double‑sided reflections and consistent softening strategies.
