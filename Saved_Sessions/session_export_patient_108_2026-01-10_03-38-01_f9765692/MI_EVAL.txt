# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and offering practical support; could add more individualized affirmations of strengths to reach top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening that tracks content and affect, but reflections are sometimes brief and quickly followed by solutions rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains strong focus on boundary-setting and regaining the flat with clear, actionable steps, rehearsal, and safety/legal guidance throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Approaches the session as joint problem-solving with offers to rehearse and draft, and adapts to patient concerns; could more consistently elicit the patient's ideas first and co-create wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms choice at points and offers options for safety, but often presents directive, specific recommendations without asking permission or fully eliciting the patient's preferred path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation (goal, move-out date) but relies heavily on clinician-provided solutions and scripts rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons, values, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites collaboration at moments and offers support, but the interaction is largely clinician-driven with ready-made scripts rather than co-created phrasing and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Highlights the patient's stated goal and supports concrete planning, yet misses opportunities to ask open, evocative questions and reflect spontaneous change language to amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids arguing, but uses few MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance) and moves quickly to prescriptive solutions.
