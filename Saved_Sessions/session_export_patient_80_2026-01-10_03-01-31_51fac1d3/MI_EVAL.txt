# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard, validating Luka's efforts and strengths without judgment. The warm, supportive tone and normalization of difficulties reinforced acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate empathic reflections and concise summaries captured both content and affect, matching Luka's concerns about risk and freezing. The clinician's calm, steady delivery invited elaboration and conveyed understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing violent outbursts and alcohol‑related urges, translating goals into concrete steps like a pause plan and practice day. Role‑play and safety planning reinforced actionable direction tied to the presenting problem.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions were co‑constructed (phrase, spot, practice day) and the clinician invited Luka's input, fostering teamwork. At times the clinician supplied detailed scripts and counts, which was helpful but slightly clinician‑led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were offered and permission sought, consistently supporting Luka's choice and control over strategies. The clinician emphasized Luka's discretion to try things and framed safety steps non‑coercively.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out existing change statements and elicited client‑centered goals, leading to concrete commitments. There were missed opportunities to more deeply elicit values, reasons for change, and confidence or readiness ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fostered collaboration and invited Luka to choose options and direct practice, demonstrating good power sharing. Occasionally the interaction tilted toward expert coaching with specific phrasing and instructions rather than exclusively client‑generated wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited and amplified, with the clinician translating positives into specific actions and securing a concrete plan. More frequent reflections of values, long‑term benefits, and explicit confidence/readiness queries could have strengthened commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not argued with, but the response was largely pragmatic and solution‑focused rather than strategically softening ambivalence. There was limited use of double‑sided reflections or explicit validation of both sides of ambivalence.
