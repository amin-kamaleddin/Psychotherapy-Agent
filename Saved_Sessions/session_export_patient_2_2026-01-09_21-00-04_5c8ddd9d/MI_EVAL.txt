# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—normalizing tensions, avoiding judgment, and affirming the patient's choices. The clinician thanked and recognized the patient's efforts while highlighting what she already named and planned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured priorities and ambivalence. Could occasionally deepen emotional reflections (e.g., worry about cancer or fear of letting her son down) to reach the top score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on concrete target behaviors and kept every question and reflection tied to the agreed Tuesday experiment. The clinician avoided drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in genuine partnership by inviting input at every step and co-constructing the plan with inclusive language. The patient's preferred tactics drove the plan, indicating equal-power interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced the patient's choice and control, offering multiple options rather than directives. Phrasing supported ownership and reduced pressure, respecting autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited intrinsic motivation and values (presence with her son) and explored past successes. Could more directly probe confidence and commitment (e.g., readiness/confidence ruler) and amplify emergent commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—asking which options felt most doable and framing suggestions as joint problem-solving. The patient’s ideas (alarm, sticky note, wind‑down) shaped the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk, labeled client statements, and drew out concrete commitment-oriented steps. The clinician invited small experiments and reinforced client-generated actions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and did not confront resistance (notably about drinking), which was appropriate here. Could use double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to juxtapose minimizations with concerns and further resolve ambivalence.
