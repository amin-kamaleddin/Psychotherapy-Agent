# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, supportive stance, normalizing the screening result and affirming the patient's choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic engagement and basic reflections that validated Laura's perspective, though there were missed opportunities for deeper affective or complex reflections to fully capture emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use and harm-reduction, with assessment of frequency/consequences and repeated redirection to concrete skill practice and safety planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with co‑constructed agenda and adjustments to the patient's preferences, offering brief invitational feedback rather than directive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice and control by offering options, respecting the patient's decision not to quit, and framing advice as selectable strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some material was elicited (frequency, willingness to practice) but the clinician largely provided skills and information instead of drawing out intrinsic reasons, values, confidence, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited input and role‑played collaboratively, but at times shifted into an expert information stance by offering blocks of advice without first asking permission.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some elicitation of change-relevant language and agreement to practice refusal lines, yet the clinician did not consistently amplify or deepen change talk with targeted open questions about desire, ability, reasons, or need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided confronting sustain talk and used normalization and reframing to reduce threat, though more double-sided reflections explicitly acknowledging ambivalence would strengthen this further.
