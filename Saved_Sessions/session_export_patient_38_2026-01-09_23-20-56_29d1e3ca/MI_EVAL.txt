# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician established a clearly nonjudgmental, validating tone and safety check, using reassuring language and normalizing ambivalence throughout. Labels and criticism were avoided, conveying unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were several empathic reflections that acknowledged emotions and practical concerns, showing perspective-taking. Reflections were sometimes brief and the clinician often moved quickly to information or solutions rather than deepening emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a focused agenda on contraception and immediate practical steps, repeatedly steering toward actionable items the patient requested. Priority checks and concrete next-step planning kept the interaction goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited the patient's input, offered options, and co-created practical steps like scripts and clinic lists. At times the clinician shifted into an expert/informational role without always checking permission, which slightly reduced parity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reinforced the patient's control over decisions, offered multiple options, and avoided pressuring the patient to choose immediately. Tools and scripts were provided to support the patient's independent follow-through rather than dictate choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some actionable motivation was elicited (choice of small steps), but the clinician mainly supplied information and tools instead of systematically drawing out intrinsic motives, values, or confidence. Opportunities for scaling questions and amplifying commitment language were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician worked with the patient to identify helpful resources and next steps, asking what would be most useful and offering to assist. However, providing detailed scripts and lists sometimes steered the agenda more clinician-led than fully patient-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change-oriented responses and supported small-step planning, but rarely invited the patient to articulate her own reasons, importance, or confidence. Strategic questions and reflections to amplify change talk were limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and used normalizing, permissive language that reduced pressure and did not strengthen sustain talk. More use of double-sided reflections and selective reflection of sustain talk could have more actively resolved ambivalence.
