# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect through normalization, validation, and supportive framing while avoiding blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and links feelings to values, but reflections are sometimes formulaic and miss deeper emotional exploration around identity loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking behavior and purposefully moves the conversation toward a specific, time‑limited, actionable plan and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is a joint exploration with the clinician inviting input and operationalizing Rosa’s ideas into a mutually agreed plan rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by asking preferences, avoiding pressure, and framing options as Rosa’s to choose, reinforcing her ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and ties them to concrete steps, but could deepen intrinsic motivation and bolster confidence by using scaling and probing past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions the work as collaborative, asking Rosa’s opinion and co‑creating the plan that reflects her priorities and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and moves to a doable plan, but misses opportunities to probe confidence/importance and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without confrontation but often repeats sustain phrases verbatim and rarely uses double‑sided reflections or strategic reframes to reduce sustain language.
