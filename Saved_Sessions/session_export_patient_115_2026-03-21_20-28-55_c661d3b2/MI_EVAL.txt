# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalizing statements, avoided judgment, and repeatedly affirmed the patient's efforts and progress throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that named concrete concerns and feelings, though could use deeper, affect-focused reflections to further deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on specific target behaviors tied to asthma control and used tools (confidence ruler, action planning) to guide toward a concrete, noncoercive trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly worked as a partner, co-designing plans, asking permission before offering strategies, and inviting the patient's choices and role‑plays to shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's choice and ownership, presented options rather than directives, and validated harm‑reduction (cutting down) over enforced cessation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations, resources, and concrete change language, but could do more to amplify and link those reasons to explicit commitment statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine partnership through co‑creation of plans, soliciting preferences, and jointly rehearsing scripts and implementation intentions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited meaningful change talk with targeted questions and a confidence ruler, though opportunities to reflect, amplify, and consolidate desire/need/commitment language were sometimes missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and moved toward pragmatic harm‑reduction, but used few strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or selective reframing) to actively soften sustain talk and increase discrepancy.
