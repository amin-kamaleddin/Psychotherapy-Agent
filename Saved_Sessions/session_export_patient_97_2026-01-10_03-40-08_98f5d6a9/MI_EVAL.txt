# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, using validation and praise throughout the session. The clinician normalizes Mateo's feelings and conveys warmth and positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and recognition of Mateo's ambivalence and exhaustion, with several empathic reflections. At times the clinician shifts quickly to problem-solving, limiting deeper exploration of emotions and underlying concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on finding and keeping a job with behaviorally specific goals. The session progresses logically from goal selection to skill rehearsal and an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conducted as a true joint effort with in-session practice, targeted feedback, and Mateo shaping content of the plan. The mock interview format and shared problem-solving modeled equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices and accepts Mateo's reservations without pressure, reinforcing client agency. The clinician repeatedly asks which goals and steps Mateo prefers to work on.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits Mateo's reasons and some change language (job importance, rehearsal of lines) and uses practice to draw out his voice. However, clinician often provides ready-made phrasing and solutions, reducing opportunities for Mateo to generate more of his own motivations and confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly collaborative, with frequent check-ins and joint planning, but sometimes becomes somewhat directive through extensive clinician-provided lists. Overall the clinician invites input but occasionally leads with expert solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and strengthens change talk by asking goal-focused questions and having Mateo rehearse statements, building commitment through practice. Could further amplify and elicit explicit ability and commitment language (e.g., confidence rulers or direct ask about likelihood).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and normalizes ambivalence, but provides few strategic reflections or double-sided summaries to reduce sustain talk. The session moves quickly into planning rather than exploring reasons for maintaining current behavior.
