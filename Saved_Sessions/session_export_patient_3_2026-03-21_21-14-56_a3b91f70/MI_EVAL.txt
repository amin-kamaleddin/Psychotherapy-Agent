# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with validating summaries and affirmations that normalized struggle and praised effort. No evidence of labeling, blame, or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated content and feeling. Could deepen by occasionally offering more complex or amplified reflections to explore underlying emotions and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on starting brief exercise with concrete planning and measurable indicators. Repeatedly steered conversation toward implementable steps like times, fallbacks, and confidence rulers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner, asking for the client's ideas and co-creating the plan with mutual problem-solving. Offers and templates were presented only when invited, reinforcing a shared approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized client choice and framed suggestions as optional, offering multiple options and inviting decisions. Avoided pressuring language and reinforced the client's role as decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited motivation from the client through open questions, confidence rulers, and prompts for signs of progress, drawing out client-generated reasons and commitments. Encouraged the client to articulate supports and strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with invitations to choose specifics and co-construction of the plan. Slight drift toward recommending a concrete structure without explicit permission before proposing it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk using value-focused questions, specific action planning, and confidence scaling. Could amplify and selectively reflect the client's own motivational language more to increase momentum and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing the few expressions of worry and redirected to planning and supports, acknowledging concerns without arguing. Could further use double-sided reflections and deeper barrier exploration to normalize ambivalence while steering toward change.
