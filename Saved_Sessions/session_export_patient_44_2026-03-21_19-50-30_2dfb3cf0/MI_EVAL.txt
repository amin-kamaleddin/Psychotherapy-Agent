# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently displayed unconditional positive regard and normalized struggles, validated effort, and avoided shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflections and validations of progress and mixed feelings, but reflections often stayed summary/educational rather than probing deeper shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on exercise and diet targets with concrete, measurable goals and actionable steps, and avoided drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner by inviting input, tailoring plans to constraints, and negotiating follow-up, keeping the plan mutually agreed-upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by offering options, framing suggestions as experiments, asking permission, and validating the client's right to modify or decline strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited preferences and experiments and amplified progress, but missed some opportunities to probe deeper personal motives and to elicit more self-generated reasons and commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and responsive to the client's preferences, though occasional directive prescriptions (exact portions, rigid rules) shifted some control back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced several instances of change talk and negotiated concrete steps, but could do more to amplify the client's own motivators and elicit stronger commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframed sustain-talk (e.g., the 'earn' rule) and proposed incremental experiments, but could strengthen use of double-sided reflections and reduce occasional prescriptive language.
