# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, validated coping, and affirmed the patient's preferences. Slips were treated as information rather than failure, reinforcing an accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate empathic reflections and attended to the patient's emotional conflict and practical constraints. Questions about context and safety show careful, tailored assessment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on reducing nightly drinking with a straightforward, concrete plan and safety checks. Next steps and escalation options were clearly outlined.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician solicited the patient's input at multiple decision points and integrated her ideas into the plan. Suggestions were offered as options and tools were co-constructed with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and letting the patient set the pace. Resources and next steps were offered only when the patient expressed readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some internal motivation was elicited (readiness ruler, one-week experiment), but the clinician relied more on practical strategies than exploring core values or following up on readiness. Missed opportunities to deepen self-motivating statements limit stronger commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative and power-sharing, with choices offered and the checklist co-created to fit the patient's requests. The clinician tailored the plan to the patient's context and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reinforced tentative change talk and elicited a concrete experiment, but rarely used open, evocative questions or amplified change statements to build momentum. Additional summarizing and follow-up could have deepened commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized alcohol's coping role, and offered substitutions and delay strategies. More use of double-sided reflections and strategic summaries could further soften sustain talk and highlight discrepancy.
