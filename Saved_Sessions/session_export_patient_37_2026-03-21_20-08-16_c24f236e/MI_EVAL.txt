# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, honored the patient’s preferences, normalized worry, and avoided shaming or minimizing at all times.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summarizing captured concerns and desire for autonomy, though a few deeper affective reflections naming underlying emotions would strengthen empathy to a top score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the vaccination decision and planning, repeatedly returning to specifics (which vaccines, timing, risks, and a concrete plan) without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician and patient functioned as equal partners—options and decisions were co-constructed, the clinician checked agreement, and shared problem-solving was evident throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported via frequent permission-seeking, offering options, affirming choices, and avoiding coercion, clearly leaving decisions in the patient’s control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited motivation and planning language, but could have probed more for personal values or stronger commitment statements to deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician largely treated the exchange as a partnership with joint decision-making and shared scripting, though some logistics were framed more as clinic actions than negotiated co-decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced clear change language and helped form a concrete plan, but could have amplified and highlighted change talk more and drawn out stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled non-confrontationally with reflections and permission to provide information, though more consistent use of double-sided reflections and pivots could better resolve ambivalence.
