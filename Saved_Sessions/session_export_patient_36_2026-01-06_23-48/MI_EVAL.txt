# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, affirming Elena's values and praising progress without shaming. Repeatedly reinforced self-respect language and normalized the work as values-driven.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking with accurate reflections of logistics and motivation (work balance, confidence rating, barriers). Reflections rarely probed deeper emotions behind guilt or stress, so more feeling‑laden labeling would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on protecting evenings/ME TIME throughout the session. Questions and reflections steadily moved from values to concrete action, implementation strategies, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative stance—solicited input at every step, co-created if‑then plans, and mirrored Elena's exact phrasing when requested. Offered supports only with permission and honored the client's expertise about her life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly preserved Elena's choice by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing her control over the plan. Avoided pressuring language and reinforced client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation and solutions from Elena using open questions, scaling, and linking ME TIME to values; integrated client-generated cues like 'I earned this.' The clinician amplified client reasons rather than imposing ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with repeated invitations for choice and co-construction of the plan. At times the clinician moved quickly to offer scripted wording; more elicitation of the client's own phrasing before proposing drafts would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk using a confidence scale, concrete if‑then planning, and values linkage, which increased commitment language. Could more often summarize and explicitly label accumulated change talk to elicit stronger commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anticipated and addressed the main sustain voice (guilt) with practical if‑then strategies and a cognitive cue, redirecting potential sustain talk toward action. A higher score would come from more explicit double-sided reflections when sustain talk appears.
