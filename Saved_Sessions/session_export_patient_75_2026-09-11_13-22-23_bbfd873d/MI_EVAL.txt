# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing difficulties and offering practical help rather than directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective‑taking and reflective listening with content summaries, but reflections seldom probe deeper affect (anxiety/fear) or explicitly validate emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on inhaler adherence and technique and consistently moves the conversation toward concrete, implementable next steps and scheduling.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co‑created with inclusive language, regular solicitation of preferences, and offers to take tasks only if the patient wants.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and defers decisions to the patient, framing supports rather than pressuring and reinforcing her control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal motives and practical reasons for change and invites decision‑making, but could more systematically deepen intrinsic motives and draw out explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong power‑sharing evident—clinician asks what will fit the patient's life and frames offers as supports, keeping sequencing and choices in the patient's hands.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with specific steps and commitments, though could amplify and summarize those statements to strengthen desire and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects toward problem‑solving, but would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and explicit reframing to lessen the pull of sustain talk.
