# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys unconditional positive regard from the start, affirms the patient's efforts, and avoids blame, creating a safe, nonjudgmental space for disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflective listening and perspective‑taking capture ambivalence and practical barriers well; a minor opportunity remains to probe underlying emotions (guilt, fear, exhaustion) more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session keeps a clear focus on smoking and protecting the child, translating discussion into concrete, measurable steps (reminder, craving ratings, specific rituals) and an implementation plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is clearly collaborative: the clinician invites the patient's input, co‑designs the experiment, and offers help only as requested, respecting the patient's expertise about her life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently asks permission, offers multiple options, and emphasizes choice, affirming the patient's control to accept, decline, or modify suggestions without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits the patient's concerns and concrete motivations and asks about confidence, but could deepen evocation by exploring core parenting values, identity, and past successes to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as a joint effort, invites decision‑making from the patient, and co‑creates a realistic, patient‑chosen experiment with shared responsibility for follow‑up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited and amplified through concrete planning and reinforcement of protective intentions, though the clinician could probe longer‑term reasons for change and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and neutrally acknowledges sustain talk while offering practical harm‑reduction strategies, but could more often use double‑sided reflections and selectively amplify brief change statements to further soften sustain talk.
