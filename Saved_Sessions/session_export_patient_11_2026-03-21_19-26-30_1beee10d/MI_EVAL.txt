# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed respect and nonjudgmental support, normalized variability, and used kind directives and affirmations. The patient’s preferences and limits were treated as legitimate and reasonable throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated accurate understanding and tailored responses to the patient’s constraints, validating energy variability and offering low‑energy options. Deeper, complex reflections and explicit exploration of emotional drivers were limited and could be increased.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity and functional strength, with plans and rules tied to the target behavior. Nutrition was acknowledged but appropriately deferred to preserve directional clarity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was a genuine joint effort, with structured options offered and repeatedly refined in response to the patient's preferences and questions. Co‑constructed rules and invitations for clarification reflected power‑sharing and patient input shaping the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasized choice and patient control over pace and progression, offering fallbacks and framing tracking as informative rather than punitive. Recommendations consistently reinforced the patient’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited and the clinician reinforced the patient’s readiness, but the approach leaned toward practical planning rather than systematically drawing out intrinsic reasons. More evocative open questions and reflections to amplify change talk would strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician asked permission, solicited preferences, and co‑created the plan and progression rules with the patient. Repeated invitations to choose fallback and accountability strategies reinforced a collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician scaffolded and reinforced the patient’s change talk, asked permission before offering a plan, and tailored options to stated goals. Opportunities were missed to amplify the patient’s reasons and deepen desire/commitment with more reflective and evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk, accepted the patient’s reluctance about nutrition, and pivoted to manageable movement-focused steps. Safety nets (fallbacks, RPE rules, logging) reduced resistance and supported engagement.
