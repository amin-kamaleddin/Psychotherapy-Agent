# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, with frequent affirmations and supportive language that maintained a nonjudgmental tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used solid reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured core ambivalence, but many reflections were content-level, somewhat repetitive, and missed deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and protecting the children with behaviorally specific goals and a concrete follow-up plan, avoiding drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session tone was collaborative and the plan reflected the patient's choices, but frequent concrete suggestions and scripts sometimes tipped toward an expert-driven approach rather than fully mutual problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced autonomy by avoiding pressure, offering options, and prefacing advice with permission, making help clearly available if and when the patient wanted it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited actionable change talk and small-step ideas, but did not consistently probe values or use scaling and deeper evocation techniques to strengthen intrinsic motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and co-constructed the plan with the patient, yet several moments of directive advice and numerous specific tips reduced opportunities for the patient to generate more of the plan herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively invited and reinforced change talk and helped elicit specific small changes, but could better amplify, sequence, and summarize change language to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized sustain talk while offering harm-reduction options, but would benefit from more targeted moves (e.g., double-sided reflections) to bracket sustain talk and highlight discrepancy.
