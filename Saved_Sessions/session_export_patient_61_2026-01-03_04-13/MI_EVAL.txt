# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalization (e.g., “You’re not failing; you’re trying to balance a lot,” multiple similar turns). Observer noted ~6–8 clear acceptance/normalizing statements across the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate mirroring of patient language (“huge amount,” “mornings are chaos”); several complex reflections (~7). Could deepen emotional nuance (e.g., probe feelings behind “overwhelmed”) and vary reflection types.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on diabetes self-management and narrowed to a concrete, measurable action (“one med with morning coffee,” alarm, pill placement) throughout the session with multiple goal-directed prompts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Framed work as joint effort and repeatedly invited Jonathan’s input and choice (“Which single task … feels most realistic?”); clinician followed patient-led choices and co-created the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed patient choice and normalized caution (“You’re not failing for being cautious”), offered options rather than directives, and respected the patient’s stated willingness to “try once.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic reasons and past supports (value: family; habit supports) with several evocative questions, producing some change talk; missed opportunities for scaling and systematic deepening of motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Actively fostered power-sharing and co-planning (multiple invitations to design the plan), consistently deferred to the patient’s preferences and expertise about his routines.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Elicited desire/plan/commitment statements (e.g., “I want to be around…”, “I’ll try it once next week”), but did not consistently amplify or summarize change talk or elicit strength (importance/confidence) to increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and non‑reinforcement of sustain talk via normalization and gentle redirection; could more actively diminish sustain talk using stronger double‑sided summaries and selective reflections linking values to the small step.
