### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful; frequent affirmations ("I’m with you on whatever you choose," "I’m proud you’re willing to try"), normalization of ambivalence, and validation of values (education, social life).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., "You’re torn…", "I hear the push–pull"). Reflections were mostly content-level; affective/deeper emotional reflections (impact of police incident, uncertainty) were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related behavior change throughout; moved to a concrete, realistic plan (no drinks Mon–Thu, Marcus as sober buddy, 10pm check‑in, backup plans) and secured follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance—offered options, solicited Tyler’s preferences, and co‑constructed the plan (Tyler chose Marcus and the weeknight rule).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and ownership ("I’m with you on whatever you choose," "It’s okay you’re not ready to quit"); avoided pressure, invited Tyler to select doable rules.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited motivations and capacities (0–10 confidence scale, "What would raise that to a 7 or 8?"); could have deepened exploration of Tyler’s own reasons and emotional drivers for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision‑making and recognized client expertise; at times offered operational options before fully eliciting Tyler’s own ideas, slightly shifting toward guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (Tyler’s willingness to try, link to protecting grades, confidence bump 5→7). Could amplify and elaborate change statements more to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk (desire to stay social) but used few explicit softening strategies (double‑sided reflections, empathic pivots); sustain statements remained present and under‑addressed.
