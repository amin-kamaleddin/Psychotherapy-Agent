# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating Marcus's experience and supports while avoiding blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing frustration and burden, but could deepen reflections to explore emotional nuance (e.g., the meaning of the dog) more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on adherence and barriers with logical progression from elicitation to planning, keeping the session goal-directed and patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt like a joint effort with tailored scripts and preference checks, though the clinician often provided ready-made solutions rather than first prompting Marcus to generate his own wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported Marcus's sense of choice by offering options, asking preferences, and refraining from pressure about the dog, reinforcing his control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation and a concrete commitment to daily inhaler use, but could further evoke personal reasons, values, and confidence to strengthen commitment and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative with co-created materials and invitations for input, though several clinician-led suggestions slightly reduced opportunities for Marcus to originate solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk well—asking for a small, doable step and operationalizing it—but could more often amplify Marcus's own reasons and probe confidence and planning for obstacles.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and acknowledged sustain talk, but did not sufficiently use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explore or reframe ambivalence before moving to solutions.
