# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive with validating language and a nonjudgmental opening; at times shifted toward practical instruction and could add more normalizing statements to reach the highest level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrated some empathic acknowledgement and suggested empathic phrasing, but relied more on praise and coaching than sustained reflective listening; missed opportunities to mirror frustration, pride, and ambivalence more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing juice with every intervention aligned to that target behavior; the session progressed logically from problem identification to specific strategies, rehearsal, and contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as shared problem-solving with role-play and joint rehearsal, and the clinician adjusted scripts based on patient input; however, frequent expert-led wording and tactical tips occasionally tilted the interaction away from pure collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed patient choice, offered options and a two-week trial, and encouraged kindness after slips; some prescriptive rules and detailed scripting could feel directive unless explicitly framed as the patient's decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited concrete plans and rehearsals that produced change talk, but did not consistently draw out personal motives, values, or deeper reasons for change; more evocative open questions would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership by inviting phrasing choices, asking permission for role-play, and practicing together; at times adopted a more directive/expert posture when providing detailed scripts, which slightly reduced patient ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively promoted and strengthened change talk through practice, refining language, and encouraging concrete trials and swaps; could deepen impact by asking more evocative questions about personal values and consequences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and offered harm-reduction alternatives and coaching to manage preferences, which reduced resistance; could further soften sustain talk using more double-sided reflections that acknowledge both sides explicitly.
