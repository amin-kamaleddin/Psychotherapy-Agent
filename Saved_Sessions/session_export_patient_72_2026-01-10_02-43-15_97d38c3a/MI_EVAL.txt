# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly affirms the patient’s efforts and plans. Could increase individualized normalizing language or explicit unconditional acceptance before moving to problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows basic understanding and occasional validation but reflections of the patient’s feelings are inconsistent and relatively shallow. Missed opportunities to paraphrase emotions and explore the emotional impact of barriers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on restarting exercise with concrete, actionable steps (trial pass, orientation, scheduled walk). The session stays tightly on the target behavior throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance with permission-seeking and co-developed steps, including role-play. At times the interaction shifts to a more directive, expert role with detailed plans rather than fully integrating the patient’s problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient’s choice and control by offering options, checking permission, and avoiding pressuring language. Provides tools that reinforce the patient’s sense of agency (scripts, role-play, explicit respect for not joining).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits readiness and translates it into action but largely focuses on logistics rather than exploring deeper values or long-term reasons for change. Would benefit from more open-ended questions and reflections to draw out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative elements like asking permission and offering help, yet much of the interaction is clinician-led with prescriptive recommendations. Could invite the patient’s ideas more often to co-construct the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforces and translates change talk into concrete actions and scheduling, praising readiness and prompting concrete steps. Could further amplify intrinsic motivations by asking about what changes would mean for the patient’s life.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to practical solutions when barriers arise, using planning and concrete options. Could use more strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly roll with ambivalence.
