# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with validating and affirming statements; could add more individualized affirmations and explicit unconditional acceptance of setbacks to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some empathic reflections and validations were present, but the session was largely solution-focused and missed deeper reflective listening of vivid emotional language like “I feel gross.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on starting and sustaining exercise with concrete next steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked what the patient wanted and tailored suggestions to constraints, but largely led with solutions and lists rather than shared decision-making and co-creation of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered multiple choices and alternatives which supported autonomy, but occasionally used directive language and strong recommendations without checking the patient's preference first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical constraints and honored the goal but relied more on supplying solutions than on drawing out the patient’s own motivations, values, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened collaboratively but shifted into an expert/problem-solver stance with prescriptive steps; would benefit from asking the patient to choose and shape options more often.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced existing change talk and helped plan concrete steps, but rarely used strategic reflections or open invitations to amplify desire, reasons, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively redirected limited sustain talk toward practical strategies and experimental framing, though some brisk reassurances risked minimizing the patient’s fears.
