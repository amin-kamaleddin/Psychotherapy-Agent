# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental, validating tone that normalized Erik’s experience and acknowledged his priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking and acknowledged barriers, but could deepen affective/complex reflections to better capture guardedness and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the target behavior (swapping sugary/energy drinks), narrowing the referral to an actionable plan and tying planning back to that target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a joint exploration with negotiated plans and inclusive language, though the clinician sometimes offered many suggestions before eliciting Erik’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by offering options, avoiding pressure, affirming slips as data, and asking permission for follow-up, which enhanced Erik’s ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Erik’s motivation using values questions, scaling, choice prompts, and eliciting concrete change language and commitment to a one‑week trial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered power-sharing by asking which options felt doable and setting a mutual check-in, but could more often elicit the patient's solutions before offering multiple expert suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk with values-based questions, options, and scaling, though there were missed chances to amplify his reasons and explicitly link change to values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk but did not consistently use strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to soften ambivalence, often moving quickly to planning instead.
