# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and respect with validating openings, affirmations, and supportive language. Could further strengthen by explicitly normalizing ambivalence (e.g., naming mixed feelings).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good reflective listening and perspective-taking with summaries and paraphrases of Mateo's situation and plans. Reflections focused more on content and planning than deeper emotions; more complex emotional labeling would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on re-entry goals and specific, actionable behavior change throughout the session. Repeated summaries tied actions to long-term goals and kept the session purposefully on track.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with co‑construction of the plan, frequent check‑ins, and integration of Mateo's supports. The clinician invited preferences and kept Mateo engaged as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Mateo's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and framing suggestions as offers to accept or decline. Even strong recommendations were presented in an autonomy‑supportive way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Mateo using evocative questions, summaries of his reasons, and a confidence ruler to elicit internal motivators. Could further strengthen by asking Mateo to articulate in his own words why the steps matter to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated Mateo as the expert in his life, offering options and deferring to his choices while co‑creating the plan. Power was explicitly shared throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, summaries, and a commitment‑focused confidence ruler. When brief change statements appeared, pausing to amplify or elicit examples could deepen the change talk further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward change, eliciting barriers and generating alternatives without judgment. Rarely used double‑sided reflections or explicit normalization of ambivalence, which would more actively diminish sustain talk.
