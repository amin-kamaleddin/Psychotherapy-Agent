# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation, highlighting strengths (22 days clean, grit) and avoiding labeling or blame. Warmth and steady respect are evident throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, empathic reflections capture both content and affect (pride, worry, identity concerns) and guide the conversation. The clinician consistently demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on the target behaviors (staying drug- and crime-free, routines, job-seeking) and advances concrete, actionable steps. Each intervention moves toward a specific behavioral goal rather than unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative, with the clinician soliciting Timur's ideas and using them to shape the plan. The clinician provides structure and suggestions but lets the client choose and confirm each element.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supports choice and control by offering options and asking which feels most helpful, framing suggestions as choices. This approach fosters client ownership and respects ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and amplifies internal motives (protecting Fiona, pride in 22 days, stubbornness) rather than imposing external reasons. Those internal values are translated into a feasible behavior plan that motivates change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as a partnership, inviting preferences and co-constructing the plan (nudge choice, exact message, backups). Power is shared and the client's expertise directs decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is actively elicited, labeled, reinforced, and shaped into specific actions (tea + 20 minutes, texting Fiona, alarm backup). Strength-based questions and reflections deepen the client's commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and ambivalence are acknowledged and normalized, but there were missed opportunities for more strategic softening (e.g., double-sided reflections and deeper exploration of identity concerns). The clinician often redirected to planning before fully summarizing and diminishing sustain arguments.
