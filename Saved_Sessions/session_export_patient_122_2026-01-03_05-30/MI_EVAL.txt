# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized the patient's experience (e.g., “you’re not failing for feeling that way”). Nonjudgmental, affirming language maintained across turns; no notable deficits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of feelings and circumstance captured both content and affect (tiredness, worry). Patient agreement (“Yeah, that’s true”) indicates strong empathic attunement. Could occasionally mirror emotional language more deeply if needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept conversation focused on target behavior and progressively guided toward concrete steps (tea→5‑minute stretch, mat, alarm, 1‑minute fallback). Questions consistently moved planning forward without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed work as joint (“Let’s figure out options together”), repeatedly asked for patient preferences and offered choices rather than directives. Power sharing evident; patient-generated solutions emphasized.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice and preserved agency ("Which feels easiest…?"), normalized setbacks and avoided pressuring language. Options were offered and final decisions left to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited reasons, strengths, and past successes (tea→skincare cue) and patient articulated expected benefits. Missed opportunity to probe values or link behavior to deeper personal goals to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction positioned as shared enterprise (“Let’s make it as foolproof as possible together”). Clinician shaped solutions collaboratively and emphasized the patient's expertise and ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited repeated change talk and a concrete plan, and reflected it into an action plan. Could strengthen by using amplifying reflections, eliciting a confidence/readiness (0–10) rating, and securing an explicit commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers nonjudgmentally, normalized derailment, and offered pragmatic fallbacks (1‑minute stretch). Could further soften sustain talk with double‑sided reflections or summaries that highlight discrepancy between values/goals and avoidance.
