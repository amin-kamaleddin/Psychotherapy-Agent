# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with explicit affirmations and normalizing of ambivalence (e.g., permission asking, praise for past success). The clinician avoids blame and conveys unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of facts and ambivalence, but many reflections stayed at the content/logistic level. There were missed opportunities for deeper, feeling‑laden or complex reflections to better capture the patient's affect and meaning of 'unwind.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on alcohol as the behavior tied to the BP goal, setting the agenda and moving from exploration to concrete planning and follow‑up. Questions and summaries consistently tie back to the health target and achievable behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is co‑constructed with the patient’s ideas driving the plan (choices of nights, tracking, NA beer), and the clinician offers support while honoring patient constraints. The dynamic demonstrates clear shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors patient choice by asking permission, offering options rather than directives, and eliciting preferences for implementation. Suggestions are framed as optional and patient ownership of decisions is reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations, reasons, and resources using open questions, scaling, and prompts to identify supports. The clinician draws out internal and external motivators (wife, NA beer, reminders) rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as an expert and invites collaboration throughout (permission asking, menu of options, letting patient choose plan). The patient’s preferences and ideas clearly shape the session and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk with evocative questions, scaling, and converting ideas into a specific, doable plan. Reflections and reinforcement strengthened the patient’s language of willingness and concrete steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and uses neutral acknowledgement and double‑sided reflections to defuse sustain talk, subtly shifting toward change. Could be more strategic by exploring consequences of status quo and amplifying patient‑voiced concerns to deepen discrepancy.
