# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys respect and positive regard, praises realistic choices, and normalizes constraints. These moves create a safe, nonjudgmental atmosphere that invites engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are clear empathic reflections and acknowledgement of the patient's feelings and constraints, such as after the breathing exercise. Empathy could deepen by exploring emotional nuance and using more complex reflections to elicit the patient's internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on reducing school-related stress with concrete, observable actions (sprints, breathing, 0–5 minute step). The clinician repeatedly scaffolds small, actionable steps tied to the stated concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative: the clinician solicits preferences, offers support options, and confirms plan elements so the patient shapes the plan. Language positions the clinician as helper and resource rather than director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are offered repeatedly and the clinician accepts the patient's limits without pressure, framing suggestions as options. The patient is given ownership, including crafting reminder wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits small commitments and uses the patient's language to form plans, but stops short of drawing out intrinsic motivations, values, confidence, or past successes to deepen internal motivation. More explicit questions about why lower stress matters would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treats the work as a joint enterprise, asking permission, offering menus of options, and co-creating the reminder and plan. Power is shared as the patient selects and refines the steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces small change talk through choices, ratings, and concrete planning, amplifying preparatory and commitment language. Missed opportunities include probing for the patient's own reasons, values, and confidence to strengthen desire and ability talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently avoids arguing and accepts reluctance, offering alternatives and no-fault fallbacks to defuse sustain talk. Reframing and optional steps redirect the conversation toward doable actions.
