# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing ambivalence and expressing unconditional positive regard. Frames the plan as an experiment and avoids blame throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, nuanced reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both affect and context. Picks up on subtle emotional shifts and checks in appropriately.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on tobacco reduction and moves logically from values to a concrete one‑week experiment. Balances openness with a strategic aim to develop a specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions as a genuine collaborator by soliciting Linda’s input and co-designing practical supports. Uses inclusive language and repeatedly checks what feels realistic to the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively reinforces choice by offering options, framing the plan as an experiment, and asking which option feels right. Avoids pressuring language and supports client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Linda’s reasons, strengths, and specific success indicators, but could probe deeper for confidence/readiness and stronger client‑phrased commitment. More systematic elicitation of internal motivation would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the session as a joint exploration, asking preferences and sharing decision-making power. Co-constructs the plan and keeps the client’s perspective central.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk and translates desire into a concrete short experiment, but misses chances to consolidate stronger commitment or assess confidence. Could have amplified 'I’ll try' into more specific commitment language and contingencies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses double‑sided reflections and reframes sustain concerns as legitimate, focusing on small experiments rather than all‑or‑nothing. Sustain talk about friends and routine remains and could be further softened with more frequent double‑sided reflections and detailed strategy elicitation.
