# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, using affirmations that avoid blame. The clinician emphasizes understanding of Richard’s values and accepts his concerns about meds and side effects.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening and links reflections to Richard’s values and ambivalence. Reflections are sometimes repetitive and could probe deeper emotional tone (e.g., anxiety about being on pills forever).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on cholesterol management and actionable steps (doctor visit, targets, recheck). Could push further to elicit a concrete, time-bound commitment (e.g., schedule the visit or set specific walking days).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative with co-constructed next steps, inclusive language, and repeated solicitation of Richard’s priorities. Decisions are shaped by Richard’s responses throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers options, avoids pressuring language, and affirms Richard’s control over decisions. The clinician repeatedly frames choices as Richard’s and respects his preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out internal motives and links past successes to current goals, tying change to what matters to Richard. Could use more strategic evocative questions to deepen intrinsic motivation and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates clearly as a partner rather than an expert, using collaborative phrasing and asking Richard to choose among options. Invites co-creation of the plan and shares power in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk by exploring past success and linking it to values, which builds motivation. Missed opportunities to amplify and summarize change talk into stronger commitment or confidence statements (e.g., scaling or specific commitment prompts).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and respectfully redirects discussion toward values and planning. More use of double-sided reflections or explicit summaries of both sides of ambivalence would further soften sustain talk.
