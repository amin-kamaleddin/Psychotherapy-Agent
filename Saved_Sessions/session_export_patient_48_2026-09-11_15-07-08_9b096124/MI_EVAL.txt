# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, avoiding judgment and creating a safe, non‑shaming space for the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate empathic listening by reflecting ambivalence, naming feelings and concerns, and paraphrasing concrete barriers and hopes throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation clearly focused on increasing brief physical activity and moves discussion from hypothetical ideas to specific, actionable steps and implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models genuine collaboration by inviting the client to co‑construct the plan, offering help (e.g., drafting a message), and treating the client as the expert on his schedule and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's sense of choice and control, offering options, asking permission, and resisting pressure so the client can select and adjust the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's motivations and past successes and asks evocative questions, but could further strengthen motivation by amplifying change talk and using brief scaling/confidence questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with repeated invitations to choose supports and co‑create steps, sharing power and decision‑making throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and develops change talk through open questions and concrete planning, but would benefit from more frequent affirmations, reflections that amplify change statements, and occasional scaling to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk with double‑sided reflections and redirects toward supports and planning, though it could more often reframe sustain talk (including self‑judgment) into productive problem‑solving.
