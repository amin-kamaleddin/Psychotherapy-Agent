# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates nonjudgmental, respectful regard and validates Marco's constraints and choices throughout the session. No stigmatizing or minimizing language; acceptance is modeled clearly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that acknowledges concerns and worry, but reflections remain somewhat solution-focused. Could deepen access to underlying emotions like discouragement or self-doubt behind past attempts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on specific target behaviors (small swaps, family supports, rehearsal) and advances toward actionable planning. Session avoids drift and repeatedly brings conversation back to implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is genuinely collaborative—clinician invites Marco's input, negotiates rehearsal scenarios, and shares power in decision-making. Offers to roleplay only at Marco's request and co-constructs the plan to fit his priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice and agency by offering options, normalizing choice, and framing the clinician's role as support rather than directive. Reinforces Marco's control over which steps to try and respects his preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete reasons, plans, and confidence ratings, drawing out practical motivation and barriers. Could more actively elicit personally meaningful values and sequence questions to build from desire to commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marco as the expert on what will fit his life and invites collaboration on choices and rehearsal. Language and actions reinforce shared decision-making and equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits multiple concrete change statements and uses scaling questions to invite specifics, producing actionable commitments to try swaps. Would benefit from stronger amplification and summarizing of Marco's own change language to elicit firmer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately acknowledges sustain talk and avoids arguing, reframing toward supports and planning. Sometimes reflects sustain talk verbatim rather than using double-sided reflections or reframes that explicitly tip toward change.
