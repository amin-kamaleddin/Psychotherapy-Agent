# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows clear, unconditional positive regard from the first turn, normalizing setbacks and offering tailored validations that remove blame and reinforce acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflections capture both factual and emotional content and mirror the patient's language, which the patient even acknowledges as relieving and validating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician keeps a steady focus on increasing manageable movement and skillfully guides the talk from ambivalence to a specific, measurable plan with contingencies for flare days.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is mutual problem-solving: the clinician solicits the patient's ideas, builds on them, and co-creates a doable experiment rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites choice, presents options, and affirms the patient's right to stop if it hurts, supporting self-determination and ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits practical reasons and strategies for change but could probe deeper into internal motives, values, and confidence to strengthen intrinsic motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions the patient as the expert on his body, shares power in planning, and collaboratively decides on strategies, demonstrating strong partnership throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is actively elicited and specified into concrete steps and commitment-like language, though the clinician could further amplify and summarize motivations and ask about importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized with validating and double-sided reflections that reduce defensiveness, but additional strategic reflections or eliciting the patient's counterarguments could further weaken sustain language.
