# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard and a consistently nonjudgmental tone, validating feelings and normalizing ambivalence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used solid reflective listening and matched the patient's terse, defensive tone to keep engagement, but reflections were generally surface-level and did not deeply explore underlying emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on smoking, narrowed to the morning cigarette, and established a specific, measurable experiment and follow-up metrics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked in an egalitarian, team-based manner by inviting the patient to choose targets and co-constructing the brief experiment and check-in logistics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's choice and control by asking permission, offering options, and framing the plan as the patient's decision while honoring limits for brevity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some attempts to draw out motivations and past attempts but did not sufficiently probe intrinsic reasons for change or use scaling and deeper evocation techniques to amplify internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making by asking permission, offering choices, and honoring the patient's preferences, resulting in a jointly agreed, realistic plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited past quit attempts and secured a concrete experiment, but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify the patient's own reasons for change or assess importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected toward manageable steps which prevented escalation, though repeated brief reminders risked feeling like the same repeating advice the patient resisted; more double-sided reflections would help.
