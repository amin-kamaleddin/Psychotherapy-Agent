# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—affirming, nonjudgmental, and validating David’s efforts and reasons for quitting. Examples include normalizing difficulty and acknowledging prior attempts and motivations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking with accurate reflections of content and some feeling, which strengthened rapport. Could deepen empathic nuance (e.g., emotional cost of lapses, ambivalence) and use more complex reflections to deepen connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete steps (medication review, titration, quit date, restaurant plan, 1-week check). The session stayed action-oriented and goal-focused throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative co-creation of the plan—solicited preferences, recorded exact support texts, negotiated backup options, and treated David as an equal partner in decisions. Decisions were negotiated rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported David’s sense of choice by asking permission, offering multiple medication options, and inviting him to choose which to review in detail. Phrases and actions reinforced that the decision rests with the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations, importance/confidence ratings, and resources, eliciting concrete change talk and supports. Could probe deeper into intrinsic, value-based reasons and elicit stronger commitment language beyond setting a date.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently adopted an equal-partner stance, asking permission and inviting David’s input on medication, quit date, texts, and supports. The plan was clearly co-created with David’s expertise solicited and acted upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (motivations, importance 9, confidence 6) and helped move toward commitment with a quit date. To reach a top score, the clinician could more systematically amplify and summarize change talk and elicit specific commitment language and past-success examples.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, normalized barriers, and promptly redirected to planning and solutions (restaurant plan, backups). Could strengthen use of double-sided reflections and explicit reframing of sustain talk into preparatory change talk.
