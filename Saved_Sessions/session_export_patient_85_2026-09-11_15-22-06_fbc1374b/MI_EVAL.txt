# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard, validated efforts, and normalized ambivalence with a warm, nonjudgmental tone. No shaming or criticism was present, supporting the highest rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries helped the patient feel understood and seen. To reach top score, the clinician could add deeper affective reflections exploring embarrassment, fear, or identity impact of the regain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on juice consumption and practical behavior changes throughout the session. Questions and interventions were purposefully aimed at planning and implementation without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as an equal partnership with the patient shaping language, requesting role‑play, and accepting edits to the script. The clinician negotiated next steps and co‑created the plan rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered multiple choices, asked permission, and repeatedly emphasized that the plan came from the patient, respecting his control over decisions. Language like 'Which of those feels most doable?' reinforced autonomy throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's ideas, preferences, and practical solutions, leading to chosen strategies like the seltzer trial. Could be strengthened by more systematically linking values to behavior and eliciting explicit commitment or SMART implementation intentions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert and actively fostered power‑sharing, repeatedly asking for his preferences and input. This high level of partnership supported collaborative problem‑solving and engagement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change language, asked about doable steps, and amplified ownership of the plan. To improve, the clinician could probe reasons/values more and explicitly elicit stronger commitment language or a concrete patient‑stated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk nonjudgmentally, used double‑sided framing, and steered toward practical solutions without arguing. For a top score, more explicit strategic moves (selective amplification of change talk or double‑sided reflections naming both sustain and change talk) would further reduce sustain talk's pull.
