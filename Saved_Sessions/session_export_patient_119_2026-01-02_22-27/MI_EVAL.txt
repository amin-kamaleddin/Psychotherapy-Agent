# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language (e.g., “That makes sense and sounds frustrating,” “I won’t judge,” “I won’t shame you”). Practical constraints were validated and slips normalized throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several accurate reflections and perspective-taking were used (“You’re managing a lot…”, “I hear the worry about snoozing”), conveying understanding. Missed some opportunities for deeper, complex affective reflections to explore fear of failure more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on medication adherence and a tiny routine. Used concrete questions (0–10 scales, specific times) and moved to a time‑bound plan with follow‑up, while redirecting sustain talk as needed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan was co‑created—clinician offered options, checked preferences repeatedly, invited client choice for backup, and asked permission for check‑in. Evidence of genuine shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy‑supportive language used (“If you’re open to it…”, “Which feels most doable?”). Clinician honored hesitancy, avoided pressure, and reinforced Selene’s ownership of steps and backups.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motives and constraints (desire to stay healthy, readiness/confidence scaling, concrete plan). Missed deeper elicitation of intrinsic values and sometimes offered solutions before fully exploring Selene’s own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician acted as an equal partner—negotiated follow‑up, accepted client choices, and collaborated on contingency plans. Invitations to celebrate small wins reinforced partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple instances of change talk (wanting to stay healthy, willingness to try alarms/pillbox, prep tonight). Reflected and summarized but did not consistently amplify change talk or elicit stronger commitment language before closing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized and reframed sustain talk (double‑sided reflections, “one slip is information, not failure”). Could have done more to draw discrepancy between values and sustain talk (highlighting costs of non‑adherence) earlier.
