# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, using normalizing language and safety checks. Validation and respectful framing were present throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking were evident, particularly around surprise at the intake numbers and training concerns. Reflections tended to be informational rather than deeper emotional exploration, so empathy was strong but not maximal.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and its link to training/health, moving logically from assessment to feedback to a concrete plan. The clinician set specific harm-reduction goals and a follow-up schedule to keep direction toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as a collaborative exploration with the patient invited to choose days, tracking methods, and check-in format, and recommendations adjusted to the patient's responses. Teamwork language and a simple template supported shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirmed the patient's control and choice by offering options, asking preferences, and emphasizing that slips are not failures. These behaviors consistently reinforced the patient's autonomy in decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some evoking efforts (eliciting a typical week, past attempts, confidence ruler), but the clinician did not consistently draw out the patient's own reasons, values, or change narratives. Missed follow-ups on importance/confidence limited deeper client-originated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was generally collaborative and invited patient input, but the clinician at times led with a fairly detailed, clinician-generated plan before fully eliciting the patient's own priorities. More initial invitation for the patient to generate ideas would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced some change talk (recognition of weekly drinking, plan choices, confidence rating) but did not consistently amplify or deepen it with evocative open questions or targeted reflections. Opportunities to explore desire, reasons, and what would increase confidence were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized slips, and offered harm-reduction strategies rather than confronting the patient. To reach excellence, the clinician could use more double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge sustain talk while highlighting change motivations.
