# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., opening with listening, normalizing slips) and avoided labeling or shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated the patient's feelings and barriers (naming surprise, frustration, and readiness levels accurately).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and alcohol, moved the session toward concrete, measurable steps (readiness ruler, micro-experiment, tracker, follow-up) and kept the plan actionable.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a joint effort with frequent invitations for choice and co-creation (choosing days, scripts, tracker format, and check-in plans).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respected the patient's control and choices (accepting a 3/10 readiness, honoring refusal of nicotine replacement, offering optional low-pressure experiments).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitments and some motivation (micro-experiment, scripts) but did not sufficiently probe values or deeper personal reasons to strengthen intrinsic motivation and discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a partnership stance with shared planning and mutual decision-making, co-writing the one-line plan and asking the patient to select specifics and supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language (readiness ruler, praise for wins) but missed opportunities to amplify change talk and draw out reasons for change when the patient minimized risks.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used normalization and harm-reduction framing to pivot toward change, though could have used more double-sided reflections to gently highlight discrepancy when the patient minimized use.
