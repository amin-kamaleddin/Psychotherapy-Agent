# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys nonjudgmental regard and respect (e.g., “I’m here to listen and support your choices,” “I’ll support whatever you pick”). Normalizes Jordan’s experience of financial/work stress; no blaming or dismissive language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong affect‑laden reflections and summaries that attend to feeling and content (e.g., “That sounds exhausting…,” “I hear how beat down you feel”). Demonstrates clear perspective‑taking and keeps patient engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps focus on a concrete, specific behavior (finding a resume template; 15‑minute/5‑minute plan; 8:50 reminder). Asks goal‑oriented follow‑ups and directs toward actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites patient preferences and shares decision‑making (“Which part feels most doable?”, choice of backups and check‑in method). Plan is co‑created with patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice and avoids coercion (“Whatever you choose, I support it,” “I’ll support whatever you pick”). Offers options and leaves final decisions to Jordan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some of Jordan’s reasons and willingness (e.g., “I might try tonight,” “I do want to see if this actually helps”), but limited deeper exploration of personal meaning or broader motivations; tends to move to planning rather than evoking values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames work as joint effort (“we’ll notice what got in the way and try a different tiny step”), defers to Jordan’s expertise about what will work, and offers support rather than direction alone.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and reflects brief change talk (desire/intent), and converts intentions into a plan, but does not sufficiently amplify or deepen change talk (missed opportunities to probe reasons, importance, or confidence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates and reflects sustain talk without arguing (“it’s understandable you doubt…,” “I hear how drained and doubtful you feel”) and uses some double‑sided language, but could more often pivot sustain talk into targeted elicitation of change talk and exceptions.
