# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized feelings, and avoided lecturing or shaming throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and validation (asked about feelings, normalized embarrassment), but could have deepened reflections of emotion and ambivalence with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior and provided practical, concrete harm‑reduction steps, though summaries tying logistics back to the patient's longer‑term goals and commitment were limited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner by soliciting preferences, co‑constructing resources and scripts, and offering role‑play, keeping the patient's needs and choices central.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission before giving info, and honoring the patient's preference not to quit now or receive a lecture.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created openings for the patient's perspective but relied heavily on giving information and logistics rather than asking evocative questions to draw out deeper motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly conveyed shared expertise and power‑sharing through permission‑seeking, collaborative planning, and offers to vet/format resources together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited some change talk and reinforced concrete protective behaviors, but did not consistently reflect or amplify the patient's tentative change language or explore importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk by honoring the patient's stance and providing harm‑reduction, though double‑sided reflections could better nudge ambivalence toward change.
