# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened with an explicit nonjudgmental statement and consistently used affirming, normalizing language; avoided labeling or blaming and validated ambivalence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and accurate reflections of ambivalence, validating worries about teasing and awkwardness; could deepen empathy by probing emotional nuance and family/identity-related feelings more directly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on marijuana use and concrete behavior-change strategies, using goal-oriented questions and practical techniques; ended with a clear, actionable plan and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a joint exploration by asking for preferences, rehearsing together, and sharing decision-making; followed the patient's lead and co-constructed doable steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's right to choose by offering multiple options and accepting tentative commitments; affirmed that small steps are acceptable and reinforced patient ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited client-generated solutions and choices (driver line, cash-only, exit plan) and reinforced those selections; could strengthen evocation by using more open, curiosity-driven questions to surface deeper values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance and invited the patient's input on plans and rehearsals; at times slipped into expert-style information delivery without asking permission, which reduced power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced modest change talk and supported actionable small steps and rehearsals; missed opportunities to amplify the patient's own change statements with reflective summaries and open questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used neutral acknowledgment to redirect sustain talk toward harm-reduction and alternatives; could more often employ double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence.
