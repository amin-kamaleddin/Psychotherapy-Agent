# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, repeatedly honoring Seamus’s boundaries about not discussing quitting. Avoided admonishment and validated his preferences while focusing on care.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with accurate reflections of Seamus’s priorities and frustrations, including summarizing his desire to get teeth fixed. Could deepen affective understanding with more complex reflections of underlying emotions like weariness or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on protecting oral health and completing dental treatment given the patient’s boundary. The clinician deliberately deprioritized active evocation of smoking cessation, which limited direction toward quitting specifically.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a true joint enterprise with frequent invitations for Seamus’s input and co-creation of a practical plan (rinses, gum, breaks, music, anesthetic). Clinician avoided an authoritarian stance and shaped the plan around patient preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, explicitly respecting Seamus’s decisions, and reassuring no pressure to quit. Language consistently reinforced that Seamus controlled whether and when to discuss quitting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made minimal attempts to draw out Seamus’s internal motivations beyond an initial open question and then shifted to logistics. Missed several brief openings to explore values or what might make change more likely.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as a collaborator throughout, asking preferences for comfort, breaks, and music and framing actions as what would be communicated to the team. Power was shared and patient expertise honored in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did little to evoke or amplify change talk and did not follow up on subtle cues toward wanting to change. Prioritized harm-reduction and comfort over targeted evocation questions that could have elicited reasons or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided escalating sustain talk by validating resistance and redirecting to practical protections rather than arguing. Could be strengthened with more strategic double-sided reflections to gently reframe ambivalence.
