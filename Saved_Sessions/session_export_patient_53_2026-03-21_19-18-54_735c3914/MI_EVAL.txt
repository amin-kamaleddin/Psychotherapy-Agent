# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, using normalization and affirmations that convey unconditional positive regard. The clinician affirms limits and choices throughout with no shaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly demonstrates perspective-taking and validates the patient's anxiety and hesitation. Reflections are present but tend to be brief and pragmatic rather than deep, complex explorations of underlying emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused target on increasing small community-engagement steps and repeatedly returns to manageable behavioral goals. Sets concrete implementation intentions and negotiates backup plans without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint effort by asking preferences, offering options, and inviting decisions about day/time and backups. Offers support and checks in rather than assuming sole expert authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes the patient's freedom and control, framing choices and steps as the patient's to make and avoiding coercion. Repeatedly offers options and affirms permission to stop or adjust plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to draw out small commitments but does not sufficiently elicit the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. The clinician relies more on offering solutions than asking evocative questions or using scaling to generate deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites patient input and offers to co-create reminders and scripts, conveying that preferences matter. However, the clinician often leads plan-building by supplying concrete plans rather than prompting the patient to generate them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits brief, transactional change-oriented responses by offering options and tasks, but does not explore the patient's own reasons, values, or personal meaning of change. Change talk is encouraged more through clinician suggestions than through eliciting intrinsic motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing hesitation and reframing small attempts as progress with graded, doable steps. Could be strengthened with more double-sided reflections that explicitly capture ambivalence.
