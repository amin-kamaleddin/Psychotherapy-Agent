# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence with sincere, repeated acceptance statements throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, using accurate reflections and paraphrases (including double‑sided reflections) that connect to Jordan's values and experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on protecting grandparents and building a behavior plan but shifts away from directing toward the explicit target of vaccination, limiting vaccine‑specific evocation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is highly collaborative—clinician solicits Jordan’s ideas, defers to him as the expert, and co‑constructs a detailed, personalized plan driven by his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors Jordan’s choice, offers multiple options, and frames planning around what feels doable, reinforcing ownership and freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits internal motivation to protect grandparents and links to past successful routines, but does not consistently evoke intrinsic motivation specifically for getting the vaccine.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly power‑sharing: the clinician invites input, asks for preferences, and lets Jordan lead decisions about concrete steps and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk about alternative protective behaviors (testing, masking) but misses sustained opportunities to deepen change talk about receiving the vaccine itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and uses neutral language and double‑sided reflections to soften resistance; could further reduce sustain talk by explicitly naming/reframing the reasons against vaccination.
