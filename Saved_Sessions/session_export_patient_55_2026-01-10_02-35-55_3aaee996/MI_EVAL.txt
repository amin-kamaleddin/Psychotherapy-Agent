# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys clear unconditional positive regard and normalizes the student’s experience with validating statements and nonjudgmental language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that capture pragmatic concerns and tone, though reflections could probe deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a clear behavioral focus on protecting grades but does not explicitly link the plan to the referral target (alcohol risk) or explore current alcohol involvement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuine joint exploration by eliciting the client's ideas, reflecting them, and inviting the client to choose the next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's sense of choice and avoids pressuring language, inviting doable options and validating caution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the client's own reasons and concrete strategies through open questions and summaries, amplifying intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the client as the expert, shares power in decision-making, and builds plans around the client's preferences and prior skills.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits and amplifies planning and preparatory change talk (planner, alarms, study buddy) but could more actively reinforce commitment and ability language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes cautious language which prevents escalation, but relies more on acceptance than strategic double-sided reflections or evocative prompts to shift sustain talk.
