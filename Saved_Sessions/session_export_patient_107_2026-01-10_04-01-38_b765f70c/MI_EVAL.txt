# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, avoiding judgmental language. Statements like “I accept where you are” created a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, specific reflections captured both content and affect and mirrored the client's language (e.g., ‘warm mug,’ ‘five‑minute pause’). This demonstrated accurate, consistent empathic understanding and attentive listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on reducing jitters while preserving ritual and moved logically from exploration to a concrete, time‑limited experiment. Elicited specific options, operationalized success criteria, and set measurable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative with co‑construction of the experiment and shared decision‑making. The clinician solicited and followed the client's preferences for strategy, metric, and supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, emphasizing reversibility, and framing the plan as the client's one‑week experiment. Honored the client's right to revert and validated their control over the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the client's own motivations, reasons, and concrete change plan through open questions and reflections. The clinician elicited and translated change‑oriented statements into behaviorally specific indicators.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the client as the expert and invited their input at every step. Power‑sharing was evident as the client selected the strategy, metrics, and supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk with strategic open questions and requests for specifics, moving ambivalence toward concrete, testable steps. Reflections amplified tentative commitments like willingness to try a week.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and gently redirected sustain talk toward change while validating the benefits the client wants to keep. Could be improved by using more explicit double‑sided reflections and systematically softening stronger sustain statements.
