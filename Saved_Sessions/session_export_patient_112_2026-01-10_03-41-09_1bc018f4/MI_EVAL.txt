# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with a nonjudgmental opening and repeated affirmations; could further personalize by naming specific strengths to increase felt warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, compassionate understanding using reflective formulations and normalization of feelings; could deepen empathic connection by reflecting identity loss/grief more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete next steps (NRT, scripts, quitline) and avoided drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a strong collaborative partner by co-creating scripts, rehearsing, and adapting to the patient's comfort; patient input shaped decisions and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Thoroughly supported choice by offering options, honoring reluctance about medications, and reinforcing pacing and control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation and concrete action (agreeing to call) but relied more on information and missed deeper elicitation of personal values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and asked permission, yet the session was somewhat clinician-led with many expert solutions; inviting the patient to generate options would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk at points but did not consistently deepen or amplify it; could pause to ask evocative follow-ups to move toward commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and offered targeted strategies (NRT, weight support, cost options) but sometimes moved quickly to problem-solving without using double-sided or reframing reflections.
