# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing ambivalence and creating a safe, respectful space for exploration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent reflective listening and accurate paraphrase kept the client understood, but reflections were often solution-focused rather than deeper emotional or evocative reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on reducing afternoon caffeine with repeated concrete planning and checks about barriers and confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was a genuine joint problem-solving process with the client iteratively selecting and refining strategies alongside the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offered choices, respected the client's preferences and fallback options, and avoided pressuring a single solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers elicited practical motivations and specific plans, but the clinician could have probed deeper intrinsic reasons and discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance was maintained with shared decision-making, though the volume of clinician-generated suggestions occasionally risked overshadowing the client's own idea generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and obtained some change language and commitments using scales and planning, but did not consistently amplify the client's own motivations or explore underlying values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk and ambivalence were acknowledged, yet strategic techniques like double-sided reflections and selectively reinforcing change talk were underused.
