# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental regard (multiple affirmations and validations); repeatedly acknowledged Wiremu’s priorities and concerns throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, useful reflections and perspective‑taking (e.g., “You care about your kids and your independence,” “I hear ambivalence…”). Missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance (shame, grief, fear) with more complex feeling+meaning reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on target behaviors (reduce drinking, engage services, rebuild trust); used scaling and moved to a concrete, time‑bound action plan with follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Wiremu as partner—used open questions, invited edits to scripts, asked permission for texts, and co‑constructed the plan (timing and sequencing of calls).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported choice and control (asked which option felt doable, offered options for coping and follow‑up, avoided coercive language).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations and plans (0–10 scaling, “what would move you one point higher?”) and produced change talk, but sometimes shifted quickly into problem‑solving rather than deepening the emotional reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shared power and negotiated details (allowed Wiremu to edit scripts, confirmed text timing and number); Wiremu’s ideas drove the plan and clinician supported rather than dictated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change language (scaling, action commitments), but missed opportunities to amplify and extend pivotal statements (e.g., “I’ll do anything to get my kids back”) to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and negotiated compromises, but rarely used double‑sided reflections or summaries to explicitly soften sustain talk; clinician often moved to solutions without first reframing sustain concerns.
