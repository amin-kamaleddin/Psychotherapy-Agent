# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained respectful, nonjudgmental tone repeatedly (e.g., “I hear the annoyance…”, “Be kind to yourself if it’s imperfect”). Kept patient engaged despite blunt sustain talk. Slightly formulaic/repetitive near the end and missed personalized affirmations or normalization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, concise reflections matched patient affect (“That makes sense — annoying and small tasks add up”); invited elaboration. Reflections were mostly surface-level and often moved quickly to problem-solving; missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on charging behavior and a concrete one-week experiment (anchor, charger placement, two alarms, 1‑minute fallback). Effectively moved from clarification to actionable plan and confidence rating.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited patient input (choosing anchor, tweak to raise confidence, setting alarms). Some clinician-driven elements (offered specific step list rather than eliciting alternatives); closing language repeated instead of a single co-authored summary.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed options as choices and experiments (“Would you like a small, low‑effort plan…”, “If you want, note quickly tomorrow…”). Asked permission before recommending and accepted “no promises” while negotiating a small trial.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited preferences and practical reasons (anchor choice, tweak to raise confidence); used confidence ruler effectively. Stayed at a practical level and did not probe deeper values or intrinsic motivations for using the device.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session framed as joint problem-solving; patient influenced plan (chose “before bed,” requested two alarms). Clinician initiated most options and repeated closing language rather than using patient’s exact words in a single summary.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient offered minimal, tentative change talk (“I’ll try,” “I’ll tell you tomorrow — probably ‘did it’ or ‘didn’t’”). Clinician used scaling and tweaks to elicit modest increases in confidence but did not systematically amplify or deepen change talk (limited follow-up on reasons/benefits).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward small experiments; validated patient’s reluctance. Could use more double-sided reflections or explicit bridging statements to reframe sustain talk and invite stronger change talk.
