# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect through affirmations and nonjudgmental language (e.g., opening praise, repeated support offers). There is clear validation of the client's competence and choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of the client’s situation (workload, scheduling pressures). Could deepen emotional naming and more complex reflections of feelings or ambivalence to reach the highest level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused target (study/practice time) and balances exploration with steering toward a measurable action plan. The session ends with a concrete, goal-directed plan and scheduled check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a genuine collaboration with frequent solicitation of the client's input and co-construction of contingency plans. The clinician repeatedly reflects choices and jointly develops practical strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice by offering options, asking permission, and respecting the client's wish to try the plan before troubleshooting. Clinician avoids pressure and emphasizes the client's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out motivations and resources using strategy choices and a confidence scale, integrating responses into the plan. Could more often probe intrinsic values or longer-term reasons that sustain change beyond logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters power-sharing through invitations to choose strategies, permission-seeking for check-ins, and repeated offers of support. The client is treated as an active collaborator in selecting times, tools, and contingencies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and shapes change talk via strategy menus, planning questions, and confidence scaling, leading to specific commitments. Would benefit from more frequent amplification and reflection of spontaneous change statements and linking them to values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not confront sustain talk; instead normalizes barriers and offers practical reframes and supports (timers, DND, if-then plans) to redirect toward action. Could strengthen use of double-sided reflections that acknowledge sustain pull while highlighting change desires.
