# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and normalized the patient's concerns without shaming. Language remained respectful and supportive while offering alternatives and validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening with repeated empathic statements acknowledging anxiety and skepticism. Some reflections stayed surface-level and could probe deeper into how side effects would affect daily life and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the decision about cholesterol medication and tied discussion to concrete topics (LDL, dosing, monitoring, alternatives). The session progressed toward actionable plans like scripts, timelines, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Created a genuinely collaborative dynamic by co-creating question lists, offering scripts, and rehearsing role-plays. The patient's input shaped the agenda and the clinician adapted recommendations to preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and honoring the wish to be convinced before starting meds. No coercive language; emphasis on written plans and follow-up respected autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation (rehearsal, prioritizing questions, eliciting intention to try lifestyle changes) but the encounter relied heavily on information and scripts. Missed opportunities to ask evocative, values-oriented questions to draw out intrinsic motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the interaction as a joint effort—offering to help formulate questions, rehearse, and co-create plans. Power-sharing language and invitations to collaborate were used consistently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some commitment-like language through options, scripts, and rehearsal and supported a plan to try lifestyle changes. However, the clinician did not consistently draw out the patient's own reasons, values, or deeper motivations for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated concerns and avoided reinforcing sustain talk by offering alternatives (lifestyle trial, ezetimibe, dosing strategies) and a clear stop-and-check rule. Could enhance by using more double-sided reflections that explicitly contrast sustain talk with change reasons.
