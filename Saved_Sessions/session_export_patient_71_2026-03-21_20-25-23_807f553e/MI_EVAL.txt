# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with warm affirmations and normalization throughout the session. No instances of labeling or dismissiveness, showing clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, with paraphrases that capture values, emotions, and barriers. Responses acknowledge internal experience rather than just facts, conveying strong understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (regular yoga/practice) and returns to practical, measurable steps. Clinician resists tangents and guides toward concrete scheduling, cues, and fallbacks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative, eliciting the patient's ideas and co-constructing specific steps and wording. The clinician confirms and refines plans rather than imposing them, reflecting shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offers multiple options and repeatedly asks the patient to choose what feels most doable, avoiding directives. Phrases and check-in options respect the patient's agency and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws out intrinsic motivation using open questions, scaling, and elicitation of past supports and values. Prompts deepen the patient's change talk and lead to concrete, internally generated plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the expert in her life and engages in genuine power-sharing through questions and summaries. Plans are confirmed and refined with the patient's input, showing strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change language with strategic questions, confidence scaling, and reinforcement of intentions. The clinician strengthens concrete commitments (e.g., 3 sessions/week, if‑then cues) and raises confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and redirects to contingency plans, reducing its influence, but leans more on neutral reflections and problem-solving. Could further benefit from double-sided reflections and selective amplification of brief commitment statements.
