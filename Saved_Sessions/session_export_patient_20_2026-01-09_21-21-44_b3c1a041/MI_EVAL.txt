# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance with validating language and normalization; no shaming or blaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking about drinking and ambivalence, but reflections are mostly surface-level and the clinician moves quickly to planning instead of exploring deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and consequences, translating discussion into a concrete SMART plan and specific tools tied to reducing drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is consistently collaborative and egalitarian, using inclusive language, seeking preferences, co-creating the plan, and planning a joint follow-up to review data.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports client choice through options, permission-seeking, and framing the plan as a time-limited test while providing noncoercive safety advice about withdrawal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation is present via weighing pros/cons and offering choices that elicited a modest commitment, but the clinician often shifts to advice and misses opportunities to draw out deeper personal motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys partnership by sharing decision-making, inviting client input on strategies and templates, and positioning clinician and client as collaborators in planning and follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-oriented language and a specific small goal, but does not consistently deepen or amplify spontaneous change talk with targeted follow-ups to elicit DARN language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and offers harm-reduction options, but does not actively use advanced softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to juxtapose sustain and change talk and reduce minimization.
