# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, validating constraints and avoiding judgment. Warm, nonpunitive language supported Daniela's dignity throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking and specific reflective listening, accurately summarizing details and concerns. Reflections showed close attention to Daniela's situation and priorities.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on securing reliable childcare and moved quickly to actionable steps. The clinician guided toward a concrete implementation plan (day, times, start date, backup).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative, with co‑creation of wording and options and continual invitations for Daniela to choose. The clinician worked as a partner rather than an expert prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered multiple options and respected Daniela's choices (text vs call, template vs draft), reinforcing her sense of control. There was no pressure to adopt any single path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited concrete reasons and commitment for the childcare plan, securing specific commitment language. However, the clinician missed opportunities to link actions to higher‑order values and to evoke broader motivation or confidence, including around smoking/drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited Daniela's input and ceded expertise about what would work, co‑creating the plan and wording. Power was shared throughout decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified practical change talk about childcare and concrete next steps. The focus remained largely logistical, with missed chances to deepen motivational language about values, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed barriers (financial worry, distrust of family) and promoted small tests and backups to avoid arguing for change. Could have more explicitly normalized ambivalence and used double‑sided reflections to explore sustain talk further.
