# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard—validated the patient’s anger, apologized, and maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections that acknowledged both emotional and practical concerns and matched the patient’s urgency with concise, understanding language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on managing the delay and minimizing workplace consequences, repeatedly steering toward concrete next steps like scripts and role-play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engaged the patient by offering options and role-play, but relied on ready-made scripts rather than co-creating wording from the outset, limiting some shared authorship.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed choice and invited the patient to select actions, respected refusals, and avoided coercive language while offering multiple options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Focused primarily on clinician-provided problem-solving and missed opportunities to elicit the patient’s underlying motivations, values, confidence, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered collaboration by soliciting preferences and inviting input, but sometimes moved quickly to deliver polished solutions instead of co-constructing phrasing with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Converted frustration into concrete actions (scripts, role-play) but largely supplied solutions rather than systematically eliciting the patient’s own reasons, desires, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing blame and redirected complaints toward problem-solving, though offering a breathing exercise briefly mismatched the patient’s urgent agenda.
