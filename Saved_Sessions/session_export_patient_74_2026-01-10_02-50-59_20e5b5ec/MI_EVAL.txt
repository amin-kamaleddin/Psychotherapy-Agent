# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalized ambivalence and offered options rather than directives. Demonstrated unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used solid reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., paraphrasing ambivalence) and validated concerns about children and enjoyment of smoking. Could deepen reflections to capture affective nuances like guilt or the emotional importance of rituals.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on smoking and reducing kids' exposure with clear, actionable goals and stepwise recommendations. Maintained momentum toward concrete harm-reduction strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient choice about focus, requested details to tailor advice, and offered to assist with scripts and resources. Could strengthen by explicitly co-creating the reduction plan from the patient's ideas rather than proposing it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's control and offered options without coercion, asking permission before providing information and leaving decisions to the patient. Reinforced sense of choice throughout the encounter.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Assumed motivation resided with the patient and offered appropriate options but did not sufficiently elicit internal motivations or reasons for change. Missed opportunities to probe values, prior successes, or use scaling questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operated in a collaborative stance, asking permission and offering help to choose products or locate prescribers. Some clinician-led moments (detailed instructions/plans) reduced opportunities for joint decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created openings for change talk and obtained some patient change language (concern for kids, willingness to reduce exposure). Did not consistently amplify or follow up on change statements to deepen or strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and offered harm-reduction options but relied heavily on informational responses rather than strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to reframe enjoyment and minimize sustain talk. Sustain talk was acknowledged but not actively softened.
