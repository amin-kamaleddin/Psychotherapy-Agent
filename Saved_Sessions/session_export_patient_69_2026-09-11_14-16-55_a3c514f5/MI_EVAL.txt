# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys clear nonjudgmental respect, validates practicality and boundaries, and normalizes ambivalence using affirmations rather than criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and accurate reflections of practical concerns, but provides less depth on emotional experience (frustration/resignation) before shifting to logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remains tightly focused on arranging a single actionable mock interview, with questions and planning consistently narrowing toward attendance conditions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is framed as joint problem-solving: clinician offers options, invites edits, and reinforces a two-way partnership where the client retains control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors the client's right to choose by offering options and deferring to his preferred process, reinforcing his control over wording and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws motivation from the client through open questions and persistent elicitation of his own criteria, amplifying client-generated reasons and specifics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters power-sharing by asking for preferences at each decision point, offering choices, and agreeing to follow the client's selected process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies change talk, converting guarded willingness into concrete, actionable commitments the client affirms.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully reframes resistance into usable preferences and practical problem-solving, but could more often double-side sustain talk or use rolling-with-resistance reflections to address objections explicitly.
