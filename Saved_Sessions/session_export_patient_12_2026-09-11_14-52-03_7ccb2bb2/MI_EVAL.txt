# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validates the client and invites choice; could add more explicit affirmations of strengths and slightly warmer language to convey deeper unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and summaries that show perspective-taking, but could deepen empathic connection with more complex reflections naming emotional nuance and intensity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on reducing impulsive use with clear, incremental planning (24‑hour experiment, bag, timer, backup steps) and targeted implementation questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative—asks for input, validates choices, and co-constructs the plan without imposing solutions, treating the client as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors client choice and control by offering options and checking what feels doable, supporting ownership of the plan without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical reasons and steps from the client, producing intrinsic motivators, but could probe more for deeper values, use scaling, and elicit a commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative and shares power effectively, consistently inviting the client's preferences and letting her set plan details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change-oriented language and concrete planning, yet could more consistently reflect and summarize change talk and elicit stronger desire/ability/reason/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to manageable steps, but seldom explores or reframes beliefs underlying sustain talk and could use more double-sided reflections highlighting past successes.
