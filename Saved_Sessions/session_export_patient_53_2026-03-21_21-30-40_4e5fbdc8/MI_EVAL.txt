# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicated warm, nonjudgmental regard and normalized Isabella’s experience, using validating reflections and affirmations. They avoided blame or pressure and consistently accepted hesitancy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening captured ambivalence and emotional tone, including safety and exhaustion concerns. Reflections moved the conversation forward and demonstrated high-fidelity empathic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on small steps toward community activities, using scaling and concrete micro‑steps throughout. The clinician kept the discussion purposefully directed toward actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was explicitly collaborative and treated as joint problem-solving, with negotiated decisions and co-created messages. The clinician offered options and accepted Isabella’s choices, reinforcing partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed Isabella’s choice and emphasized voluntary action, offering options and seeking permission rather than directing. Language and checks about pace reinforced Isabella’s ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited tentative motivation with open questions, scaling, and micro‑steps that raised confidence. However, much problem-solving was clinician-provided, limiting opportunities for Isabella to generate her own reasons and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned Isabella as the primary decision-maker, offering multiple options and asking which would feel most helpful. Permission-seeking and invitations to choose fostered genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling elicited tentative change talk and some commitment language, and the clinician amplified small steps. Missed opportunities remained to deepen autonomous reasons and elicit stronger desire/reason/need or commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged sustain talk, validated reluctance, and reframed tasks into micro‑steps to reduce threat. More use of double-sided reflections and selective reinforcement of brief change statements could further shift momentum away from sustain talk.
