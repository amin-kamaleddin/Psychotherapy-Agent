### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, validating strengths and reframing the missed interview without blame. Offers individualized, supportive help (drafts, role-play, resources).

### Empathy

**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and accurate reflections, capturing both pride and disappointment. Could deepen exploration of the emotional burden of childcare (stress, guilt, worry) beyond practical problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on attending interviews and related barriers, guiding the session logically from problem identification to specific, actionable plans.

### Collaboration

**Comments:** Engages in true two-way collaboration by co-composing texts, role-playing, and negotiating next steps while deferring to Amina's preferences. Clinician contributes expertise without overtaking decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Amina's autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before intervening, and checking which supports she wants. There is no evidence of pressure or coercion.

### Partnership

**Comments:** Operates largely as a collaborator, asking for preferences and co-creating plans like texts and role-plays. At times the clinician offers polished options quickly instead of eliciting Amina's brainstorm first, which slightly shifts control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk well, reinforcing confidence and co-constructing concrete next steps. Could more systematically deepen commitment language (e.g., ask what makes her rate a 10 or what would increase follow-through).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward problem solving while validating legitimate barriers. Could use more double-sided reflections and normalization to gently move sustain talk toward change.
