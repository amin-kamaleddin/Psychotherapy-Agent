# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through affirmations, praise, and respectful language; created a warm, nonjudgmental tone that supported the client's efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with reflective summaries and probes about feelings and bodily experience, showing strong understanding of both content and emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on the concrete target behavior (setting boundaries), using actionable tasks like scripts, role-play, and confidence scaling to drive toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner with the client by offering options, co-creating scripts, and sharing control of practice format, consistently deferring to the client's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control with permission-based language and options rather than directives, avoiding pressure while offering practical suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited the client's own reasons, plans, and confidence (e.g., asking for situations, using a 0–10 ruler, prompting for wording), drawing motivation from the client rather than imposing it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine partnership by inviting agenda-setting, jointly selecting strategies, and repeatedly checking the client's preferences throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and supported change talk with concrete practice and scaling, but could more often reflect and amplify the client's own change language and directly evoke commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well by normalizing guilt and offering strategies to prevent caving, though occasional double-sided reflections would better acknowledge ambivalence and pivot toward change.
