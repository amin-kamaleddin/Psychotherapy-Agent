# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, nonjudgmental positive regard throughout (opening normalization; “You’re still committed and worthy…”; repeated affirmations and “I hear you” reflections). Conveys unconditional positive regard across multiple turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening mirroring language and affect (e.g., “worn down,” “wiped out,” location and functional impact of pain). Numerous detailed paraphrases that deepen understanding and invite correction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on a concrete behavior (five‑minute stretches, three mornings, physio demo, stop rule). Occasional early leading/suggestion (introducing five‑minute stretch) rather than eliciting first.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership and co‑creation of plan (patient chooses timing, reminder preference, physio involvement). Frequent invitations to decide next steps and how to ask the physio.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated reinforcement of patient choice and right to stop (stop‑at‑4/10 rule, “Stopping if it worsens makes sense”). Avoids coercion and validates cautious stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out reasons and concrete signs of success (getting through breakfast, less wiped out). Could further probe personal values/meaning to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise about change resides with Mateo (asks which option he wants to try, how to bring it up). Repeated deference to patient preferences and knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits actionable change language (commitment to try five minutes, three mornings, ask physio) and reflects it, but stops short of systematically amplifying or scaling confidence/importance to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently names and normalizes sustain talk (“not sold it’ll help,” pain concerns) and uses double‑sided reflections and a stop rule to reduce barriers. Could more actively pivot to selectively amplify change reasons.
