# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with clear safety checks and calm responses, but offers limited warmth or individualized affirmations of the patient's feelings or efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges safety concerns and provides brief reassurance and self-care tips, yet rarely reflects or names the patient's emotions or time pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent, necessary focus on medication safety and washout timing, consistently steering the conversation to the core clinical agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates genuine collaborative moments (drafting a message, offering role-play, inviting follow-up) but much of the interaction remains clinician-driven rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respects boundaries and offers options and recommendations, but uses firm directive language for safety that reduces explicit expressions of patient choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is largely didactic and safety-focused and does little to elicit the patient's internal motivations, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates some partnership through message drafting and offers to help, yet the encounter is primarily expert-led with limited co-discovery of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Endorses and supports the patient's stated actions and provides clear instructions, but does not actively elicit or amplify the patient's motivations, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and offers practical alternatives, but does not explicitly explore or reframe ambivalence using double-sided reflections.
