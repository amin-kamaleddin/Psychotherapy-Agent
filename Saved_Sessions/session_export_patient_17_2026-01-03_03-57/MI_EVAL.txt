# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance and normalization (e.g., “You’re not failing for feeling torn; you’re managing a lot,” repeated across the session). Strong positive regard tied to her circumstances (single mother, tired). Suggested improvement: individualize affirmations by linking to specific behaviors (persistence, parenting examples).

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (accurate paraphrases of shame/ambivalence: “You feel ‘gross’ and like a ‘leper’,” multiple reflections). Missed occasional opportunities to probe deeper emotional meaning (e.g., explore the sting of the daughter’s disappointment). Suggested improvement: use more complex reflections to infer underlying emotion.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation; progressively narrowed to a concrete, time‑limited one‑week trial. Repeatedly steered toward options and specific steps (swap routine, NRT, breathing). Balanced focus and avoided drift.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong joint framing and co‑design (asks permission, offers choices, solicits preferences; client’s ideas directly shaped plan: tea/walk/gum). Multiple invitations to co‑create. Suggested improvement: further elicit details of her routines to deepen co‑construction.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit support for autonomy: offered choices, asked permission before info, affirmed need for a realistic plan and nonjudgmental slip plan. Frequent statements reinforcing client ownership of decisions. Suggested improvement: check explicit ownership when finalizing commitments.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations and strengths (past six‑week quit, daughter as motivator) and client-generated solutions (tea/walk/gum). Some reliance on providing solutions (detailed NRT instructions) rather than deeper elicitation of reasons/confidence. Suggested improvement: elicit and amplify her own reasons and confidence (scaling, values questions).

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally treated client as expert on her life and solicited preferences; co‑created plan. At times shifted toward directive/expert instruction (detailed chew‑and‑park dosing) which reduced shared problem‑solving tone. Suggested improvement: preface technical instructions with invitations to collaborate.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (repeated reflections of desire to quit, elicited doable options, secured a one‑week commitment). Missed opportunities to deepen and quantify commitment/confidence (no scaling or follow‑up to strengthen commitment). Suggested improvement: follow commitments with brief readiness/confidence probes.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized (e.g., “smoking still calms you when you’re exhausted”), and practical strategies were offered. However, limited use of strategic MI techniques to soften sustain talk (few double‑sided reflections, limited reframing before moving to solutions). Suggested improvement: use more double‑sided reflections and emphasize costs of status quo to shift balance toward change.
