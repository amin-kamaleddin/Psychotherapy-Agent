# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard (opening: “I’m here to listen and support you”), normalized uncertainty, avoided judgment, honored Evelyn’s choices about follow‑up and tracking, and framed the tracking as a practical step rather than a moral failing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking (e.g., “that sounds worrying… your son’s concern would nudge you”; “It’s okay to feel unsure”). Reflections were accurate and validating but somewhat surface‑level; missed opportunities to deepen emotional content (impact of the fall, relationship concerns, fears about independence).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and balance risk. Kept session goal‑oriented with a concrete, measurable action (two‑week tracking), repeated summaries and safety contingencies, and explicit next steps for follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Routinely invited Evelyn’s input (“Would you be open…?”, offering template, asking about follow‑up). Language was inclusive and co‑constructive, but clinician sometimes rapidly provided detailed instructions rather than eliciting Evelyn’s own ideas about feasible tracking methods.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly affirmed repeatedly (choice about follow‑up, “if you’d like, book a follow‑up”), clinician avoided pressure and framed decisions as Evelyn’s to make, supporting client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of willingness and factual information (permission for experiment, asking about the fall/meds). Limited elicitation of internal motivation, values, or reasons for change; missed opportunities for open questions to amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained collaborative stance, invited joint review of the log, and treated Evelyn as an active participant. Partnership could be strengthened by explicitly soliciting Evelyn’s expertise about what will work for her and co‑creating tracking approaches.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client change talk appeared (“I’ll try…”, “I’ll call for help if I fall”). Clinician elicited a commitment to tracking but did not strategically amplify or deepen change‑language (no selective reflections, scaling, or elicitation of pros/cons).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk (minimizations like “seven drinks isn’t much”), which is appropriate. However, stronger MI strategies (double‑sided reflections, reframing, selective summarizing to tip toward change) were not used to further soften sustain talk.
