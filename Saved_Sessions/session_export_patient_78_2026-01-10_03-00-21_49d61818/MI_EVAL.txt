# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and honored the patient's requests (no lectures), normalized ambivalence, and avoided shaming language to create an accepting climate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking and asked what would be most helpful, but reflections were often summary-level and could probe deeper emotional nuance (e.g., guilt about her daughter).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation and moved the conversation toward a concrete, measurable step (2 mg gum, frequency, and brief follow-up texts).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner by soliciting preferences, adapting to the patient's constraints, and co-constructing decisions such as NRT choice and text wording/timing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's freedom to choose, sought permission, offered options rather than directives, and honored boundaries like avoiding lectures or calls.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited reasons, past attempts, and some commitment language, but could more actively probe intrinsic motives and amplify brief change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated clear power-sharing by inviting the patient to shape the plan, responding to her constraints, and co-creating practical steps that fit her context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created openings for change talk and helped translate them into a small actionable step, but did not consistently follow up to amplify or deepen brief change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively rolled with resistance and validated frustration, avoiding argument and redirecting energy toward practical, patient-requested options to prevent sustain talk escalation.
