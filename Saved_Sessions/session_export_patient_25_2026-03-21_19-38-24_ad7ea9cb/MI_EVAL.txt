# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—validated progress, normalized concerns, and invited disclosure without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening, but reflections were often pragmatic; deeper emotional reflections (e.g., embarrassment, shame, pressure) would increase empathic resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on target behaviors—reinforced smoking cessation and moved the alcohol discussion from exploration to concrete action planning without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and co-constructed—used mutual role-play, solicited client input, and shaped plans around Daniel's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering multiple options, asking permission to rehearse, and framing suggestions as choices that reinforce client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation and capacity through context questions, choices, and rehearsal, but could further deepen evocation by asking about importance/confidence and eliciting the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered strong partnership—shared decision-making, invited preferences, and adapted recommendations collaboratively throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (affirmations, planning, rehearsal), though more explicit amplification of the client's own change statements and follow-up questions would strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to change-focused strategies, but missed chances for double-sided reflections and explicitly acknowledging the appeal of drinking when resolving ambivalence.
