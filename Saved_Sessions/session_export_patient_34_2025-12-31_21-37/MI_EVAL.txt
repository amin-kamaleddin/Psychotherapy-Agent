# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

- [x] Acceptance  
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; validated Dylan’s priorities (“I’m glad you came,” “I hear you feel blamed…”). Avoided labeling and respected his wish not to apologize; supported his goals to protect residence/record.

- [x] Empathy  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Used frequent reflections and accurate summaries that built rapport and captured priorities. Missed opportunities to name underlying emotions (e.g., frustration, worry, embarrassment) for deeper empathic work.

- [x] Direction  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the conduct referral and concrete next steps (draft statement, collect photos/texts, witnesses, timeline). Did not broaden discussion to the precipitating behavior (underage drinking) or future risk-reduction.

- [x] Collaboration  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Engaged Dylan as a partner—offered options, asked which felt doable, repeatedly checked for approval. Collaboration was narrow and accepted the patient’s chosen strategy without proposing alternative harm-reduction options.

- [x] Autonomy Support  
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Dylan’s choices, deferred decisions to him, and committed not to act without his approval (e.g., not contacting witnesses; will send packet for his review).

- [ ] Evocation  
#### **Score (1–5):** 2  
**Comments:** Elicited little about Dylan’s internal motivations beyond protecting his record; did not probe values, reasons, or ambivalence that might support behavior change.

- [x] Partnership  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Treated Dylan as an equal partner in decisions, followed his lead on statement content and process, and sought his authorization before actions. Did not actively elicit his perspective on drinking behavior itself.

- [ ] Cultivating Change Talk  
#### **Score (1–5):** 2  
**Comments:** Change talk was minimal (acknowledging presence at the party; desire to protect record). Clinician did not strategically elicit or amplify desire/ability/reasons/need/commitment to reduce risky drinking or prevent future incidents.

- [ ] Softening Sustain Talk  
#### **Score (1–5):** 2  
**Comments:** Patient’s minimization and denial were largely accepted and operationalized (helping craft a factual denial). No use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or exploration of consequences to soften sustain talk.
