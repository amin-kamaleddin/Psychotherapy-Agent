# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with validation, normalizing language, and reassurance throughout the session, avoiding criticism or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses good empathic statements and surface reflections that validate anxiety and avoidance, but reflections are mostly brief and do not frequently probe deeper emotional nuance or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing small steps toward attending community activities with tightly linked, concrete interventions and a time‑bound plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—asks for preferences and negotiates the plan—but repeated procedural instructions and detailed reminders make the interaction somewhat clinician‑led at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms choice and offers options repeatedly (e.g., 'you can stop anytime'), though multiple prescriptive reminders and instructions slightly reduce the sense of full autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits small, task‑focused motivations and agreement to try, but provides more external structure than questions that draw out Isabella's internal reasons, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a collaborative stance and co‑creates the plan with the patient, yet frequent specific guidance and step sequencing shift some authority back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits tentative change talk and a doable plan, but misses opportunities to amplify, reflect, and deepen DARN language to strengthen commitment and resolve ambivalence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and offers safety‑net options without pressuring, but tends to redirect to pragmatic steps rather than using double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
