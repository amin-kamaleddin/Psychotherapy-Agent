# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—checks safety, offers choice, normalizes struggle, and admires effort. Warm, nonjudgmental stance is sustained throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and perspective-taking, mirroring specifics and linking feelings to behavior. Reflections are mostly surface-level and could probe emotional nuance or use more complex reflections to deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on increasing gentle activity with a concrete 5-minute plan, monitoring, safety thresholds, and a short-term test. The stepwise approach and follow-up requests keep momentum toward the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as a collaborative partner—co-constructs the plan, offers materials, and invites check-ins while meaningfully incorporating patient preferences. Language like 'we'll tweak the plan' reinforces teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and framing the plan as a test the patient can stop at any time. Consistent reinforcement of control and consent is evident.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation via a low-risk experiment and check-ins, prompting change language and commitment to try. Could further draw out intrinsic reasons, values, and confidence/importance ratings to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership through asking permission and co-creating a tailored plan, but occasionally shifts toward directive guidance with specific safety rules. More explicit statements about the patient's expertise would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and shapes change talk by asking for a small, doable goal and securing concrete commitments (five minutes, alarm, check-in). Could amplify and summarize the patient's change statements and elicit more 'why' language to increase impact.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and validates sustain talk without reinforcing it, normalizing concerns and offering safety planning. Could use more double-sided reflections and strategic pivots to actively soften ambivalence.
