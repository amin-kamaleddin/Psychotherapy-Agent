# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and normalizing statements with a warm supportive tone. There is no shaming or blaming and strong unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates perspective-taking, validation, and asks permission before questions, showing good empathic responsiveness. Reflections tend to be brief and informational rather than deep emotional reflections, so empathy is strong but not maximal.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on underage drinking, risks, and safety throughout the encounter. Provides a concise, actionable plan and practical scripts that keep the session goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and asks what would be most helpful, co-constructing strategies with the client. However, frequent clinician-driven suggestions and scripts shift the stance slightly toward an expert/teacher role rather than fully co-created solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors the client's right to choose and does not pressure her to stop, offering multiple options and realistic harm-reduction strategies. The stance consistently reinforces that decisions belong to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits factual information and some worries but often pivots to information-giving and concrete suggestions. Limited use of evocative questions, scaling, or reflective amplification to draw out personal reasons and deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the encounter as collaborative and offers options, conveying that the client's preferences matter. Some prescriptive elements and scripted advice reduce the degree of shared power and co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some attempts to elicit change-relevant material but does not consistently reinforce or amplify client's change statements. The clinician often moves quickly to advice instead of eliciting importance, reasons, or confidence to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not confront or argue with sustain statements and focuses on harm reduction and options to avoid reinforcing sustain talk. Lacks more advanced MI techniques like double-sided reflections or strategic rolling with sustain talk to actively soften it.
