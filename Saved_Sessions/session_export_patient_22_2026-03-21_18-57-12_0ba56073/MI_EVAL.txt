# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalized slips, and repeatedly reassured the patient. No blaming or moralizing was observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good validation and reflective comments acknowledged frustration and social fears, demonstrating empathy. Could deepen with more frequent complex reflections to explore underlying emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained clearly focused on reducing alcohol use with aligned assessment and sequential movement from assessment to a concrete harm-reduction plan and rehearsal. The clinician kept the conversation goal-directed and action-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative approach with co-created strategies, asking for patient preferences, and tailoring materials (scripts, quick tally) to the patient. Role-play and options were offered only after confirming willingness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice by asking permission before advice, offering multiple options, and accepting the patient's decision not to quit entirely. Language promoted ownership and avoided pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some early elicitation produced change talk, but much of the session relied on clinician-provided options rather than systematically drawing out the patient's values, strengths, and intrinsic motives. Missed opportunities to deepen motivation with evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing demonstrated by soliciting adjustments, implementing patient wording, and inviting input at decision points. The plan was clearly co-constructed with the patient's expertise prioritized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, invited practice, and sought commitment language. Could further amplify client-generated reasons and confidence with targeted open questions and strategic reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided argument and adapted the plan to respect the patient's reluctance, preventing escalation. However, there was limited use of techniques like double-sided reflections to explicitly reflect ambivalence and soften sustain talk.
