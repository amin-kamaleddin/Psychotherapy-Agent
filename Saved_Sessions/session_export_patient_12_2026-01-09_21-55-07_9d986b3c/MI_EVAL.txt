# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates nonjudgmental positive regard and validation, honoring the client's limits and offering permission to stop. No blaming or moralizing language and warmth is maintained throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate empathic listening with reflections that capture both content and feeling, including complex and double-sided reflections. Frequently checks understanding and aligns closely with the client's themes of helplessness, shame, and isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear behavioral focus on strengthening social connection with concrete, achievable steps and indicators of success. Interventions consistently bring the conversation back to a specific, short-term experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is consistently collaborative—solicits preferences, invites co-planning (who, wording, timing, backups), and frames ideas as joint problem-solving. Clinician positions choices as flexible and shaped by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports the client's choice and agency, using language that preserves control and frames steps as experiments. Avoids pressure and normalizes the right to stop or keep plans private.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the client's hopes and personal reasons for change with questions about desired outcomes and small signs of success. Could deepen evocation by probing values, using scaling questions, and asking the client to summarize her own argument for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treats the client as the expert and invites shared decision-making, offering options and asking which feels safest. Frequently asks for the client's view before recommending actions and co-creates plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces tentative change talk and small-step language, highlighting strengths like 'I could try.' Missed opportunities include amplifying and consolidating that talk into firmer commitment (e.g., rulers or direct commitment prompts).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections to reframe it alongside change possibilities, which helps soften resistance. At times the clinician repeats sustain language and could more often summarize discrepancy or strategically amplify change language to shift momentum.
