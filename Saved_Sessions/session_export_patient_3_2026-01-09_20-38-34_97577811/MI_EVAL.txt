# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing struggles and praising choices. Tone and wording conveyed clear respect for Aisha's feelings and constraints throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, reflecting both content and affect and acknowledging ambivalence. Therapist responded to subtle emotional concerns with validating normalization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on starting to exercise and moved logically from readiness to a concrete, actionable plan. Pacing and agenda remained tightly aligned with the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative, co-designing schedule, routine, and alarm wording while soliciting Aisha's preferences. Decisions were made jointly and the clinician promptly adapted to the client's requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the client's sense of choice by offering options, asking preferences, and framing suggestions as choices rather than directives. Affirmed Aisha's ownership of decisions and backup plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited initial motivation and used the client's language, but later shifted toward providing concrete plans which reduced opportunities to deepen personal reasons and values. Could use more open, evocative questions to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine power-sharing and invited the client to lead choices, incorporating her expertise into the plan (e.g., exercise swaps, alarm phrasing). Consistently checked preferences and co-created the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated desire into a concrete plan with commitment prompts and implementation steps. Missed some opportunities to evoke deeper, more vividly expressed change talk and stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, acknowledged barriers empathically, and offered practical small-step strategies and fallbacks. Could increase use of double-sided reflections to more explicitly acknowledge both desire and reluctance.
