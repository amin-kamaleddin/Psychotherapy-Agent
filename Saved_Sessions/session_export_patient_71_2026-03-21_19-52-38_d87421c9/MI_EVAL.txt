# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized setbacks, validating the patient's concerns without blame. This nonjudgmental stance encouraged openness and reduced shame about slips.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used empathic statements and simple reflections that acknowledged worry and difficulty with mornings. Missed some opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of emotion and the personal meaning of the patient's values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on establishing a specific morning yoga/breathing habit and practical steps to support it. Every intervention tied back to the target behavior and a one-week test with tracking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicited preferences and adapted suggestions (e.g., habit app vs texting) and invited co-construction of cues and tracking. However, frequent specific prescriptions at times shifted control back to the clinician rather than fully co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice by offering options, tentative language, and emphasizing the patient's decision-making. Avoided pressuring language and reinforced ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and built on the patient's intrinsic reasons (health, yoga) and invited a trial commitment, helping activate motivation. Could have deepened evocation by exploring core motives, confidence, and asking scaling or elaborative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership by asking which example to use and incorporating patient preferences, creating a generally collaborative tone. At times the clinician offered many detailed prescriptions before eliciting the patient's ideas, reducing equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encouraged and reinforced change talk with a concrete tiny-habit plan, tracking template, and a one-week commitment. Missed chances to amplify the patient's own reasons and have them elaborate on how change aligns with their values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively redirected sustain talk by normalizing slips, reframing partials as progress, and offering low-friction fallbacks rather than arguing. These strategies reduced resistance and kept focus on manageable next steps.
