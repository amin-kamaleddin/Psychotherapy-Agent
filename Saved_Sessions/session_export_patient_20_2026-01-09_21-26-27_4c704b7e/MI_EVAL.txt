# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalizing Brandon's experience and affirming his preferences. This warm acceptance reduced shame and supported openness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was basic perspective-taking and useful clarifying questions, but limited use of reflective listening to surface deeper feelings or ambivalence. The clinician moved quickly to strategies instead of offering more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing risky drinking with concrete targets and structured plans. The clinician repeatedly returned to actionable goals despite patient minimization.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was respectful and co-constructive, checking preferences and incorporating Brandon's wording into the plan. However, the clinician often supplied ready-made lists rather than eliciting the patient's own ideas earlier.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offered choices, asked what Brandon wanted help with, and framed advice as options, supporting his sense of control. At times prescriptive language (e.g., 'non-negotiable') risked undermining autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some preparatory change language was elicited and converted into plans, but the clinician did not deeply evoke Brandon's intrinsic motives, values, or ambivalence. The focus stayed on external strategies over internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fostered a collaborative stance, offered choices, and co-created the plan with Brandon's input. Partnership was somewhat limited by clinician-led content and provision of many prescriptive options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change talk and translated it into concrete, actionable plans (alarms, scripts, relapse steps). Still, there was less exploration of Brandon's personal reasons or values underlying change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and normalized sustain talk but did not use strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or discrepancy) to soften it. Sustain talk was acknowledged then the session moved quickly to pragmatic solutions.
