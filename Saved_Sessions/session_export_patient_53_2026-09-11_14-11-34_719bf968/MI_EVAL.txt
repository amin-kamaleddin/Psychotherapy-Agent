# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm with frequent normalizing and validating statements that acknowledge the patient's experience without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained, accurate reflective listening captures both content and emotional tone with timely, specific paraphrasing and perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on attending a community activity and moves stepwise to concrete, achievable actions (who to ask, 30 minutes, exit plan, draft texts).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly an equal partnership—clinician invites patient preferences, co-creates the plan, and repeatedly checks what feels most doable to the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers multiple options, easy-outs, and emphasizes choice and pacing, avoiding pressure and preserving the patient's control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical ideas and small commitments from the patient but stops short of probing deeper values, importance, confidence, or past successes to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the patient as an expert on what will feel acceptable, inviting input on tone, who to ask, and sequencing so decisions are jointly made.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers concrete small-step options that elicit tentative change talk, but could better amplify and elaborate the patient's weak 'yes, I guess' statements to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and pivots to low-pressure plans, though could more actively elicit costs of staying home or amplify fleeting change reasons to counterbalance sustain talk.
