# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes David's struggle with affirmations and validating reflections, maintaining a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflective listening and complex reflections capture David's feelings and priorities (family, health, urgency in restaurants), demonstrating strong empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and immediate objectives (medication options, coping strategies, testing gum tonight), with questions and reflections consistently steering toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician works as an equal partner, using inclusive language, soliciting David's preferences, and integrating his choices into a co‑created plan rather than prescribing a single course.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician preserves David's sense of choice by offering experiments, using conditional phrasing around medications, and asking what he is willing to try, avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits meaningful internal motivations and concrete outcomes tied to values, but could further deepen evoked motivation by asking for readiness/confidence ratings and eliciting commitment sooner.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly invites David's expertise and preference (e.g., 'What do you think would feel acceptable to try first?'), co‑creating practical next steps and preserving shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several strands of change talk are elicited and occasionally amplified (family, health, calmer home), but the clinician misses some opportunities to more strongly summarize and evoke explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized, but the clinician uses mostly neutral reflections and few targeted strategies (double‑sided reflections, pros/cons exploration) to weaken sustain talk and tip ambivalence toward change.
