# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive, normalizes slips and encourages self‑compassion; could add more individualized warmth and reflective language of the client's emotions rather than leaning toward solutions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates understanding of constraints and adapts the plan to the client's context, but uses few deep or complex reflections and misses chances to amplify emotional content like 'I feel gross.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on starting exercise with concrete, measurable steps (4‑week plan, tracking, follow‑up) and ties every intervention back to that goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites preferences and provides requested tools, showing a collaborative stance, but the clinician supplies most content and structure instead of eliciting client-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices, flexibility, and non‑rigidity while encouraging self‑compassion, yet several unsolicited directives reduce perceived autonomy and could be preceded by permission checks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits priorities and builds to planning, but relies more on offering strategies than on drawing out the client's own motives, values, and confidence to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for preferences and invites reporting back, but interaction often becomes clinician‑driven with prescriptive steps instead of fully co‑creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforces and expands change talk by inviting planning and accountability, yet could more actively amplify desire/ability/importance with targeted reflections and questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers small, doable options and fallback plans while acknowledging barriers, though more double‑sided reflections would better acknowledge ambivalence.
