# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard (e.g., explicit acceptance statements) and normalizes Amira’s experience without judgment, reinforcing nonjudgmental support throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both content and emotion (tired, disoriented, cautious hope) and the clinician maintains attentive, empathic understanding across the conversation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays sharply focused on increasing short bodyweight squats with concrete implementation planning (frequency, cue, anchor, backups) and problem‑solving for barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician solicits Amira’s ideas, reflects and builds on them, and co‑constructs the plan (anchor rule, cues, backups) showing genuine mutual contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers choices, validates Amira’s priorities (activity before nutrition), and affirms that short, realistic actions are acceptable, supporting client control and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits meaningful reasons for change (strength, energy, confidence) and invites solutions, but misses deeper evocation opportunities like values exploration, past successes, or confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power is shared throughout the session: options are offered, Amira chooses the approach, and the clinician refines her choices rather than imposing a plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces sustained change talk and specific planning, but does not consistently amplify or consolidate it into stronger commitment language (e.g., scaling or a clear commitment question).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is respected and not reinforced—the clinician redirects to achievable steps—but could further reduce sustain talk with double‑sided reflections or reframing to weaken its influence.
