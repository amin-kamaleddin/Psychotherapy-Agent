# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized worries, and avoided judgment throughout the session. Language was respectful and supportive with no shaming or confrontational moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and timely reflections, acknowledging fears and practical constraints. Could deepen emotional reflection (e.g., explore feelings about lifelong meds or the emotional role of beer) before moving to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on diet, alcohol, and diabetes management and moved logically from goals to tailored practical steps. Maintained consistent directional guidance toward achievable behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—preferences were solicited and recommendations adapted to the patient’s shift schedule and inputs. The clinician treated the patient as an active partner and offered choice-based supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and normalizing slips while respecting the decision not to change meds. Interventions reinforced the patient's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some patient motivations and practical ideas and adapted interventions accordingly. Relied more on offering strategies than deeply evoking internal motivations, values, or confidence (scaling and discrepancy work were limited).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked which area to prioritize and sought permission before offering tools, tailoring plans to the patient. At times the clinician defaulted to providing many concrete recommendations instead of consistently co-constructing the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, invited goal choice, and praised small steps. Missed opportunities to amplify and summarize the patient's own change language or use scaling questions to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation and validated sustain statements, offering small nonthreatening steps that respected the patient's stance. Could more actively use double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to tip the balance toward change.
