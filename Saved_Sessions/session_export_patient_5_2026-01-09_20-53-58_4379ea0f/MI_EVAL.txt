# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses unconditional positive regard and respect, using affirmations and normalizing language. Tone is warm and nonjudgmental throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate understanding with reflective listening and clear summaries of the patient's goals and safety concerns. Reflections and restatements make the patient feel heard and understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear clinical focus on safe switching/starting of medication and drives toward concrete outcomes (washout timing, checklist). Repeatedly narrows the agenda to immediate, actionable tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as an equal, cooperative exchange with inclusive language and repeated invitations for patient input. The clinician iteratively refines the plan with the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice and pace by offering options and asking preferences rather than prescribing a single path. No pressuring language; patient directs the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical motivations and outcomes (safety, confidence) and asks what would be different if the timeline is followed. Could probe deeper into personal values, confidence, or use scaling/commitment questions to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and power-sharing, co-constructing the checklist and offering to pre-fill only with permission. Repeatedly centers the patient's expertise and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces planning and change-oriented language (options, timeline, checklist) and asks about expected benefits. Missed opportunities to amplify change talk into explicit commitment or use confidence/importance scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is limited and the clinician avoids reinforcing it, normalizing caution and redirecting to safety planning. Focus stays on pragmatic steps and reassurance rather than debating ambivalence.
