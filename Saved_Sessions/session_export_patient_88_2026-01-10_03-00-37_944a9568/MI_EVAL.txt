# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, explicitly validating Marcus’s honesty and normalizing his experience. There was no shaming or blaming and resistance was treated as understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking to acknowledge Marcus’s social motives and worries, which helped him open up. Could deepen emotional probing and use more complex reflections to capture teen identity and irritation more vividly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on weekend alcohol use, safety, and concrete harm-reduction steps throughout the session. The clinician moved the conversation toward specific next steps without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative with co-construction of plans, frequent checks, and building on Marcus’s contributions. Decisions were made together (e.g., refusal lines, check-in plan) rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Marcus’s sense of choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and framing actions as his decision. Avoided coercive language and reinforced his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Marcus’s motivations and practical commitments using scaling questions and problem-solving, producing concrete change language. Could amplify his own change statements more and explore personal values to strengthen internal motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that Marcus’s expertise mattered, asked permission before advising, and invited his input when choosing strategies. At times provided many recommendations rather than repeatedly eliciting Marcus’s own solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and supported change talk through scaling questions, offering options, and asking for implementation steps, yielding concrete client change language. Could further reinforce and amplify Marcus’s own change statements to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used double-sided reflections and redirection to prevent sustain talk escalation, offering low-threat harm-reduction options. Could use more deliberate reflective sequencing and selectively amplify small change statements to shift the balance toward change.
