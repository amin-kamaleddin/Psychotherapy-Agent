# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained unconditional positive regard and normalized Tyler’s worry throughout, using nonjudgmental, supportive language and avoiding shaming or blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated good understanding with reflections and validation, but reflections were often brief and missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflection of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician kept a clear, consistent behavioral focus on alcohol risk reduction and linked strategies directly to preventing repeat problems and protecting academics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt collaborative—Tyler’s input was invited and language was adapted—but numerous clinician-generated suggestions sometimes shifted the interaction toward an expert-delivery mode.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options were framed and Tyler’s choices affirmed, but a few directive prompts (e.g., 'send it now so it’s real') reduced the consistency of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited concrete commitments and plans from Tyler, but could have drawn more on his intrinsic values (e.g., importance of education) and used more open prompts for self-generated reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician generally conveyed a collaborative stance—asking permission and inviting modifications—but offering many prescriptive lists limited Tyler’s role as expert in his own situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and amplified change talk (choice of strategies, rehearsal, texting), yet could more frequently reflect and heighten Tyler’s own stated reasons and values to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and respected Tyler’s desire to keep friends while offering harm-reduction, but missed chances to use double-sided reflections to better resolve ambivalence.
