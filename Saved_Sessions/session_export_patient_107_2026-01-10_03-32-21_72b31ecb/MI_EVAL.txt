# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental with frequent validation and normalization of the patient’s experience. No signs of shaming, blaming, or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic understanding with reflective summaries and acknowledgement of worries. Reflections were often pragmatic and task‑oriented, with missed opportunities to probe emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing coffee use with actionable steps (taper, log, withdrawal management) and no topic drift. All recommendations tied back to the patient’s goals of productivity and sleep.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned as a joint problem‑solving effort, soliciting patient preferences and scheduling a follow‑up. Balance sometimes favored clinician expertise due to many concrete recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, offered multiple options, and avoided prescriptive language while encouraging the patient to decide pace and use safety valves. Reinforced patient control over decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did little to elicit the patient’s internal motivations; most momentum came from patient statements rather than clinician evocation. Missed opportunities to use scaling questions, values exploration, or amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked permission, offered options, and invited patient input on the plan and log. At times the clinician took a directive role with structured protocols that slightly reduced co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited via concrete options and a taper proposal, but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than evoking the patient’s own reasons or confidence. Limited use of open, evocative questions or scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated enjoyment of coffee and avoided arguing with sustain talk, but did not consistently use double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to gently shift sustain talk toward change. Sustain talk was accepted rather than actively redirected.
