# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and positive regard, using affirmations and nonjudgmental language. Normalizes difficulty and treats the patient's concerns as legitimate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing feelings and contextual stressors. Could use more complex or emotional reflections to deepen exploration of shame and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with concrete, behavior-specific steps and measurable signs of progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint exploration and integrates the patient's ideas into the plan, avoiding an expert-heavy lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options and asking which feels most doable, normalizing incremental change without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits personally meaningful motivations and surfaces strong change-talk like 'not today, for my girl.' Could more actively elicit confidence/ability and amplify change talk toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative process and repeatedly solicits the patient's input, though offering several specific options quickly can feel slightly directive at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and echoes the patient's language, but could more often summarize and scale to build momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids arguing, but makes limited use of double-sided reflections or reframes to process ambivalence and shift emphasis toward change.
