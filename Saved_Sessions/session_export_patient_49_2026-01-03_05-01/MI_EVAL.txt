# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Communicates respect / nonjudgmental stance
- [x] Uses normalizing language
- [x] Affirms patient's priorities and strengths
- [ ] Labels or criticism avoided (not explicitly documented)
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental language (e.g., “That tension is normal and okay.”) and repeated affirmations of patient priorities and past efforts; acceptance evident across turns.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Uses accurate reflections and paraphrase
- [x] Synthesizes patient's concerns
- [ ] Probes deeper affect or emotional meaning (not consistently present)
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and paraphrasing that show understanding (“I hear you — you value your health…you don’t want to lose the social connection.”). Slightly formulaic; missed opportunities to probe the emotional meaning of terms like “awkward” or “left out.”

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Maintains focus on target behavior (smoking reduction)
- [x] Progressively narrows to a concrete, testable plan
- [x] Moves from exploration to planning/commitment
**Comments:** Clear, consistent steering toward a specific experiment (gum/lozenge at work, skip work smoke breaks, evening check-in). Clinician effectively guides from ideas to a measurable plan.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Frames process as joint and asks patient's preferences
- [x] Invites patient input on decisions
- [ ] Allows patient to generate all plan elements (clinician offered options/drafts)
**Comments:** Frequently asks Hana’s preferences and co-constructs the plan. Slightly clinician-led at times (offering wording/options rather than eliciting them first), reducing full client-generated ownership.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Honors patient choice and control
- [x] Offers multiple options without coercion
- [x] Reinforces patient decision-making
**Comments:** Repeatedly respects choice (“Which of those goals feels most important…?”, multiple check-in options, backup plans). Autonomy is clearly supported throughout.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Elicits patient’s own motivations and reasons for change
- [x] Draws out past successful strategies
- [x] Builds plan from patient-generated ideas
**Comments:** Actively elicits motivations (less winded, better taste) and prior strategies (small steps, accountability), then builds a plan directly from those elements.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Acts as collaborator / co-planner
- [x] Frequently invites patient input
- [ ] Consistently prompts patient to originate phrasing or contingencies (clinician often models)
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance and shared planning. Could strengthen partnership by prompting Hana to draft wording or contingency plans before offering clinician-generated options.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Elicits and reinforces change talk (desire, reasons, strategies)
- [x] Converts general change-talk into a specific, testable commitment
**Comments:** Successfully amplifies change-talk (wanting less winded, better taste) and converts it into a concrete experiment (gum at work, no work breaks, friend check-in).

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Normalizes ambivalence / sustain talk
- [x] Reframes plan to preserve valued activities (social rituals)
- [x] Avoids arguing or confronting
**Comments:** When sustain talk appears (worry about social awkwardness), clinician normalizes it and reframes options to maintain social connection, reducing resistance and supporting experimentation.
