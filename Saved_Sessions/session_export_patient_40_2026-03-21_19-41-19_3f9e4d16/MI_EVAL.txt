# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows respect and nonjudgmental support, normalizes difficulty and offers concrete help; warm and validating but somewhat procedural with few individualized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens empathetically and acknowledges shame and worry, with some validating lines; reflective listening is limited and misses opportunities for deeper, complex reflections on ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on relapse prevention, withdrawal risk, cravings, and safety with interventions tightly linked to stated concerns; moves logically from assessment to practical planning and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively co-produces the checklist and incorporates Elias's edits, showing genuine collaboration; clinician still supplies much of the content and expertise rather than eliciting the patient's solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices, asks permission, and avoids coercion while reassuring the patient about contacting providers; some prescriptive language and directive steps slightly reduce the sense of full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Conveys that change is possible but does not sufficiently elicit or amplify Elias's internal motivations or reasons for change; focuses on practical planning rather than asking evoking questions to draw out values, desire, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and iteratively checks preferences while co-creating the checklist, demonstrating power-sharing; however, the clinician often leads the content and initially takes an expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides concrete relapse-prevention options and asks assessment questions that can elicit detail; does not consistently evoke, reflect, amplify, or sequence Elias's change talk, moving to solutions rather than developing motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk and ambivalence are largely bypassed in favor of directives and planning; few double-sided reflections, rolling-with-resistance strategies, or reframing attempts are used to soften sustain talk.
