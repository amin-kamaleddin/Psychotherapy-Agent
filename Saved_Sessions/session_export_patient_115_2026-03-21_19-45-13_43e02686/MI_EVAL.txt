# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful with safety checks, empathic acknowledgements, affirmations, and normalization of skepticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displays good perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening, but reflections are mainly summary/practical and could probe deeper emotions and use more complex reflections to deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking-related behavior change with a measurable one-week plan, safety contingencies, tracking, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs the plan and negotiates follow-up while inviting preferences, but at times supplies many specific recommendations without explicit invitation to teach-back or propose alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports Samir's choice and control by offering harm-reduction options, asking permission to choose steps, and framing safety advice as contingent rather than prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete behavioral commitments (gum, alarm, log) but does not systematically explore Samir's personal motivations, values, or confidence/importance ratings to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative and invites input on the plan, though there is occasional drift into expert-driven advice without always seeking permission first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and reinforces decisions, but misses opportunities to draw out Samir's own reasons, importance/confidence, and deeper change language by relying more on solution-offering.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and validates partial progress, offering pragmatic harm-reduction alternatives, though could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly name sustain talk and juxtapose change reasons.
