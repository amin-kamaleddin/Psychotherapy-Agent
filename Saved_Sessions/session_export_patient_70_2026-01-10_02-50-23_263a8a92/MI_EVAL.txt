# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Lars’s struggles while acknowledging ambivalence. Could add more explicit unconditional acceptance and individualized affirmations of his strengths to reach top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and validates both emotional and practical barriers. Could deepen affect-laden reflections and use more open reflections to invite fuller emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors and consistently steers conversation from goals to concrete actions and safety planning. Strong momentum and alignment with referral objectives.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction feels collaborative with permission‑seeking and offers to role‑play, and clinician responds to worries. However, frequent ready-made scripts shift content-generation toward the clinician rather than co-creating with Lars.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice by offering options, asking which steps feel doable, and providing noncoercive backups. Language consistently preserves Lars’s control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some doable steps and links to motivations but often supplies phrasing and solutions instead of drawing out Lars’s own values and reasons. Missed opportunities include using evocative open questions and scaling importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites Lars’s input and decision-making, conveying shared responsibility. Much of the session remains clinician-driven with scripted content rather than eliciting Lars’s own wording early on.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk and translates goals into achievable steps that support action. Could more systematically elicit, reflect, and amplify Lars’s own reasons and confidence (e.g., scaling, past successes).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, reframes barriers into manageable steps, and offers low‑judgment practical responses. Would benefit from more explicit double‑sided reflections to juxtapose sustain and change talk and tip ambivalence toward change.
