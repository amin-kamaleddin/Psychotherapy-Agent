# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed clear unconditional positive regard with validating, nonjudgmental language throughout the session. A minor improvement would be occasional explicit normalization of slips, but overall acceptance was consistently strong.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate, timely reflections and adopted Hana’s language to capture both emotional and practical elements of ambivalence. They could probe slightly deeper into the underlying affect, but reflections were empathic and well-timed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking reduction linked to the dental concern and moved logically from exploration to a concrete, measurable plan. Exploration was balanced with forward movement toward a specific implementation strategy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician operated as a teammate, soliciting Hana’s input, co-writing the friend text, and inviting rehearsal rather than dictating solutions. Hana’s preferences consistently shaped the session’s trajectory.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was emphasized repeatedly through offering choices, framing recommendations as options, and respecting Hana’s pace and ambivalence. This consistently reinforced Hana’s ownership of decisions and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician drew motivation from Hana with evocative questions, scaling, and prompting self-generated strategies, then reinforced those ideas. A next-level move would be linking the plan more explicitly to deeper personal values to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician adopted a genuinely collaborative stance, co-creating the plan and sharing power by inviting rehearsal and Hana-led wording. This approach fostered joint decision-making rather than an expert-driven prescription.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk using evocative questions, pros/cons, scaling, and converting intent into a concrete plan. They could further strengthen impact by selectively amplifying spontaneous health-related change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician skillfully reflected sustain talk, used double-sided reflections, and pivoted to practical, nonjudgmental strategies to reduce resistance. A brief explicit summary of both sides before eliciting options might further resolve ambivalence.
