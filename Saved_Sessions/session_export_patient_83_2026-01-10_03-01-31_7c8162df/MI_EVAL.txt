# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used consistently nonjudgmental, respectful language, validated Mai’s concerns, and offered accommodations (drafting messages, checking interactions). There were no critical or minimizing statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective‑taking and reflective listening with accurate summaries and tailoring to her context (night routine, living alone). Deeper exploration of emotional nuance (anxiety about starting a preventive med) would strengthen empathic work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on starting atorvastatin safely and fitting it into her routine, and consistently guided toward actionable next steps (alarms, messages, labs, emergency plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was co‑constructed with Mai—co‑authoring messages, creating an emergency plan, and inviting her preferences throughout. Responses were adjusted to her requests rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, sought permission, and explicitly deferred the final decision to Mai and her physician, emphasizing her control over starting the medication. No pressure to take the drug was evident.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited important information about routines, concerns, and intentions but shifted into information delivery and planning instead of more consistently drawing out intrinsic motivations or assessing confidence. More targeted evocative questions could amplify commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine power‑sharing by asking what would be most helpful, offering options, and co‑creating plans and messages with Mai. The clinician treated Mai as the expert on her life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced several instances of change talk (plans to start, avoiding grapefruit, using alarms) and supported implementation steps. Could amplify and summarize commitment language and use scaling to move from intention to stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated Mai’s doubts, avoided arguing, and reframed toward practical safety steps which reduced sustain talk. More deliberate double‑sided reflections could have helped resolve ambivalence more explicitly.
