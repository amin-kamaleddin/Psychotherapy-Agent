# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and normalizing language throughout (e.g., “That sounds overwhelming and imposed—totally understandable,” “No pressure — this is just an experiment”). Avoided blaming; warmth and regard are evident across multiple turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, validating reflections of burden and logistics (“Managing diabetes on top of work and home is a lot,” “forgetting and feeling like another chore”). Missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection (e.g., resentment, helplessness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent behavioral focus on a single micro‑goal (packing/tucking a snack, photo/alarm/backups). Repeatedly returned to same concrete experiment and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited choice and offered options (“Which feels more doable?”), framed plan as joint problem‑solving. Slight tilt toward clinician‑offered solutions rather than eliciting more patient‑generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized voluntariness and low stakes (“No pressure,” “this is just an experiment,” “if you’d rather…”), supporting patient choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used evocation tools (open options, 0–10 confidence scale) but did not successfully elicit patient’s personal motives, values, or reasons for change beyond “I’m supposed to try.”

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed interaction as collaborative (“we’ll problem‑solve together,” “let’s stick with the plan”), checked preferences repeatedly. Clinician sometimes maintained control by proposing multiple specific fixes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited small behavioral commitment and acknowledged willingness, but did not probe or amplify faint change talk (no questions about meaning, values, or potential benefits).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized reluctance without arguing, offered small/options to reduce barriers. Could add double‑sided reflections or strategic pivots to further soften sustain talk.
