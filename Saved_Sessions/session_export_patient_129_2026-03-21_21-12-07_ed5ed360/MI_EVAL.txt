# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized Tyler's situation while avoiding blaming language; could add more explicit unconditional positive regard and individualized affirmations of his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections, summaries, and asked permission, showing good perspective-taking; reflections remained largely at the content level and could probe deeper feelings or values (e.g., anger, fear of rejection, shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on substance use and parental conflict and steered toward practical, behaviorally relevant harm-reduction topics; could have closed with a negotiated, concrete next step or follow-up to build momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative with agenda-setting, options, and equal-role role-play, and decisions driven by Tyler's choices; continued strength is to maintain this co-construction in future sessions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and permission, offering options and respecting Tyler's decision to continue using, which supported his sense of control and autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked open questions to draw out Tyler's motivations and concerns but did not consistently follow or amplify limited change-relevant language; more active elicitation and amplification of any change talk would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited Tyler's input and co-created strategies (scripts, role-play), conveying that his perspective shaped the session; could more explicitly foreground Tyler's expertise about his peers and home life when generating plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Posed questions that produced clear statements of intent to keep using but missed chances to amplify mild concerns or elicit DARN (desire, ability, reasons, need) more deeply; follow-up on tentative change language would be beneficial.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing, rolled with resistance, and offered harm-reduction alternatives while refusing to collude with hiding use; could use more double-sided reflections to acknowledge benefits while gently highlighting discrepancies with Tyler's values/goals.
