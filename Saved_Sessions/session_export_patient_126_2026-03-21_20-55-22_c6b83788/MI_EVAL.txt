# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating (e.g., open invitation, normalizing, treating slips as information). The clinician conveys unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing content and pragmatic concerns. Reflections are mostly content-level and would benefit from deeper emotional or values-oriented exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with structured scaling and a stepwise harm-reduction plan. The clinician keeps the session goal-directed and returns to actionable steps when needed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions as an equal partner with the patient, eliciting preferences and incorporating the patient's ideas into the plan. The clinician checks feasibility and co-constructs practical strategies (cash, non-alc, if‑then).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly affirms the patient's right to choose and offers menu options rather than directives. Advice is framed as suggestions and the clinician emphasizes that the patient is in control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits self-generated strategies and uses scaling questions to draw out motivation and coping plans. Could further evoke deeper personal reasons, values, and a clearer commitment statement to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative with explicit power-sharing (e.g., 'You’re in charge here') and joint creation of the harm-reduction plan. The clinician repeatedly asks which options feel most helpful and feasible.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and builds change talk through scales, options, and prompting if‑then language, resulting in concrete change statements and commitments. There were missed opportunities to amplify the patient's own change language and to deepen elaboration on reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation, normalizes low priority and reluctance, and redirects toward small, achievable steps. Could more deliberately use techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further neutralize sustain talk and shift momentum toward change.
