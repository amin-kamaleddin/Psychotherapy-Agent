# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, repeatedly affirming Adrian’s experience and strengths. Warm, nonjudgmental language is used consistently throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained, accurate empathic listening with meaningful paraphrases and complex reflections that mirror Adrian’s language and feelings. The clinician follows up on concerns rather than shifting focus, demonstrating strong empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on reducing infection risk and progressively narrows from ambivalence to a concrete harm-reduction experiment. The clinician repeatedly returns to practical implementation details rather than allowing topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is framed as joint problem-solving using inclusive, invitational language and co-constructed solutions. Plans are shaped by Adrian’s ideas and past successful strategies, reflecting genuine collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports Adrian’s choice and avoids pressure, offering options and leaving the final decision to him. Repeatedly elicits his preferences to reinforce ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical motivations and self-generated strategies (e.g., avoiding infection, carrying straws) but focuses more on logistics than deepening values-based motivation. Missed opportunities include using rulers or exploring longer-term goals to strengthen internal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions Adrian as the source of solutions and invites his preferences, using language of experimentation and co-construction. Power-sharing is evident in repeated questions about what feels most doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies Adrian’s change language and translates it into specific, doable steps (carrying clean straws, checks, reminders). The clinician reflects and reinforces commitments, consolidating change talk into a plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting or amplifying sustain talk and uses some double-sided reflections to soften resistance. However, there were missed opportunities to more strategically name costs of continuing and explore discrepancies to further reduce sustain talk.
