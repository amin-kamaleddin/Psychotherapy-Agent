# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through warm opening, nonjudgmental language, affirmations, and normalization. Avoids labeling or shaming and supports the patient's efforts throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating motivation and parenting concerns. Missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections to explore the emotional texture of ambivalence and underlying worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing evening smoking with measurable goals, concrete strategies, and an explicit follow-up. Keeps the session goal-directed and action-oriented throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is cooperative, soliciting input like start day and brief report content, and adapting suggestions to preferences. However, the encounter leans clinician-led with structured checklists and prescriptive prompts rather than patient-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering options, asking permission before intervening, respecting boundaries (e.g., declining to focus on alcohol), and framing suggestions rather than mandates. Encourages patient choice about start day and tools.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks some open questions and elicits past attempts and a short-term commitment, but quickly shifts to clinician-delivered advice and scripts. Could deepen motivation by drawing out values, reasons, and personal meanings tied to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts partnership by asking permission and soliciting brief input, yet much of the session is clinician-driven with a checklist and directive guidance. Limited co-construction of the plan reduces shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces patient change talk by inviting a concrete goal, affirming plans, and arranging follow-up, which generates commitment language. Could more often amplify the patient's own reasons and confidence with targeted reflections and questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and does not reinforce sustain talk, redirecting focus toward smoking strategies and allowing the patient to opt out of discussing alcohol. Would benefit from more strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further weaken sustain talk.
