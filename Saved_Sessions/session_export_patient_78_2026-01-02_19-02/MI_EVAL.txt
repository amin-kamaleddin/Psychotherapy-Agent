# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., “I’m here to listen,” repeated acceptance of patient choice). Tone was somewhat procedural/formulaic; missed opportunities for personalized affirmations of strengths or resilience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate mirroring of frustration and triggers (“I hear your frustration…”, reflections of stress/coffee cues). Reflections remained surface-level and repetitive; limited probing of deeper feelings or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, sustained focus on smoking behavior. Elicited frequency/triggers, used readiness ruler, set concrete, stepwise experiments (10‑minute delay, coffee change) and a brief follow‑up format.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—offered choices, negotiated brief check‑in and honored patient preference for brevity. Occasionally offered advice without explicit permission; could invite more patient-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced patient choice and framed change as optional experiments (“you don’t have to quit cold turkey,” “useful information, not failure”), supporting ownership and agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited readiness and willingness for a small experiment but did not actively elicit or amplify change talk (missed chance to explore “common sense” comment or reasons behind the 3/10 readiness).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance evident (choices, negotiated follow‑up, honoring requests). Could strengthen partnership by asking permission before advice and more consistently inviting patient ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited cultivation—clinician acknowledged small change-talk hints but rarely amplified or explored them (e.g., no follow-up on why readiness was a 3 or what might increase confidence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized; setbacks reframed as useful data, reducing pressure. Could further soften sustain talk with more complex reflections and targeted elicitations of discrepancy.
