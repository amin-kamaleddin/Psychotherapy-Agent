# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opens nonjudgmentally, offers safety check and open options, and affirms the patient's progress and triggers throughout, demonstrating consistent unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are empathic moments and basic perspective-taking, but reflective listening is limited and the clinician moves quickly to information and strategies instead of deeper emotional reflection or summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, sustained focus on smoking cessation with concrete next steps (medication options, prescriber message, and a tentative quit date) and repeatedly returns to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction: the clinician iteratively co‑composes the prescriber message, solicits the patient's agreement, and incorporates the patient's preferences into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers multiple options and asks permission, supporting patient choice and volition, though more explicit language emphasizing patient control would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation is present through factual questions and permission-seeking, but the clinician relies largely on providing information rather than eliciting the patient's intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally treats the patient as a partner—asking permission and offering options—and collaborates on decisions, though sometimes leans into the medical-expert role when explaining side effects.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicits action-oriented statements and supports planning (medication, quit date) but does not consistently draw out or amplify the patient's intrinsic reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers pragmatic strategies for triggers, but uses few active MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to soften ambivalence.
