# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental regard, respected boundaries, and apologized when asked to stop repeating safety prompts, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used solid reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., "I’ll follow your lead") but offered few deeper, emotion-focused reflections that might have labeled feelings like mistrust or irritation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed appropriately focused on engagement and safety, though repetitive safety phrasing and offers to listen occasionally risked irritating the patient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as a joint, respectful endeavor with inclusive language and honoring of the patient's preferences, avoiding an authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed choice and control, respected requests (e.g., to be silent or stop repeating), and reinforced the patient's agency throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Beyond the initial open question, there were minimal efforts to elicit the patient's internal motivations, values, or small reasons for change, with the clinician prioritizing containment over evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician clearly positioned the interaction as collaborative, offering to follow the patient's lead and apologizing when overstepping, which supported genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was little active elicitation of change talk; after the patient's decline the clinician shifted to listening and did not pursue small, low-threat prompts about goals or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician did not confront sustain talk and effectively redirected with nonthreatening options (e.g., silence, listening), though could have used more deliberate double-sided reflections to further reduce resistance.
