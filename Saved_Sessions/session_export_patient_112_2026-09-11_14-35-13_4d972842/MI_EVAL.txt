# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys clear, unconditional positive regard throughout, avoids judgmental language, and validates the patient's identity and concerns about withdrawal, weight, and cost.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, timely perspective‑taking with reflective statements that capture ambivalence and integrate the patient's specific worries, deepening understanding rather than merely restating facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a strong, consistent focus on smoking cessation and progressively narrows to a concrete, time‑limited first step with specific supports and implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is structured as joint problem‑solving with co‑creation of texts, scripts, and backup plans, repeatedly checking and deferring to the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes choice and frames options rather than mandates, affirming the patient's control over timing and method of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's ideas, resources, and concrete experiment options well, but could more often probe deeper personal motives, importance/confidence, past successes, and values to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the patient as the expert, asks which options they prefer, and co‑creates the plan so the patient's choices shape the approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk and a manageable experiment, but missed opportunities to reflectively amplify spontaneous change statements and to elicit stronger commitment and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk calmly and avoids confrontation, pivoting to harm‑reduction, though could more actively use double‑sided reflections and explicit links to values to further reduce sustain talk's influence.
