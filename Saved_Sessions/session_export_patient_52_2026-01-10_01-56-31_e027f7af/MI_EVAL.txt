# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive with reflective language and explicit reassurance (e.g., promising a brief, nonjudgmental follow-up). These behaviors model strong acceptance throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic understanding with accurate reflections of guilt, exhaustion, and need for 'me' time and notes concrete impacts (sleep, parenting, work). Missed a few opportunities for deeper double-sided reflections to capture ambivalence and the emotional weight of being a single parent/student.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on evening alcohol use and concrete, small behavioral changes tied to the patient's goals. Every turn returns to actionable strategies that support job, school, and parenting aims.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative—offers role-play, asks for feedback, and negotiates next steps together. At times the clinician supplies many ready-made options and scripts rather than pausing to elicit the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by asking permission, offering options, inviting the patient to choose a doable change, and honoring follow-up preferences. Clinician reinforces the patient's control and choice throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from the patient's own statements and builds plans from her constraints, asking which change feels doable. Could strengthen evocation with more probing open questions and scaling/confidence prompts to draw out intrinsic reasons and intensify commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance by inviting input, role-play, and shared planning. However, frequent provision of concrete scripts and multiple structured options slightly shifts power toward provider-driven solutions instead of fully co-constructed plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces concrete change talk and secures a specific one-week commitment to delay the first drink and alternate sips. Could more strategically amplify the patient's own reasons for change and elicit stronger DARN-C language (desire/ability/reasons/need/commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and offers alternatives without confrontation, validating the need for 'mine' time. Rarely uses double-sided reflections or summaries to explicitly reframe ambivalence, and often moves quickly to practical solutions.
