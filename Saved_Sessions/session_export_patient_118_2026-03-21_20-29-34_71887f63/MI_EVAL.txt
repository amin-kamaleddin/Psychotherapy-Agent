# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and honored the patient's boundaries (e.g., explicitly agreeing not to raise smoking). Avoided labeling or shaming while validating the patient's priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and demonstrated strong listening about facts and priorities, but could have probed and reflected deeper emotional content (frustration/embarrassment) more explicitly. Overall empathic but with room for deeper affect reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kept a clear, patient-centered focus on dental needs but repeatedly redirected away from the behavioral target of smoking. Respected autonomy but missed brief, permission-seeking links between smoking and dental outcomes to maintain appropriate behavioral direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engaged in shared problem‑solving around scheduling, priorities, and practical constraints and adjusted plans per the patient's requests. Collaboration was strong for logistics but less so for jointly exploring ambivalence or behavior change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's choice and control (e.g., honoring requests not to discuss quitting and asking permission for questions). Avoided pressuring language and reinforced patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did little to draw out the patient's own motivations about smoking; evocation was limited to dental goals. Missed brief MI-consistent prompts to elicit internal reasons or values related to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the treatment plan and solicited the patient's input on timing, priorities, and accommodations. Partnership was somewhat transactional and could be deepened by inviting the patient's perspective on behavior change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicited or amplified change talk regarding smoking, focusing instead on logistics and honoring the patient's request not to discuss quitting. Missed opportunities to follow up on brief patient change utterances or ask curiosity questions to evoke motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoided arguing or reinforcing sustain talk and reframed focus to dental care, but did not use double-sided reflections or gentle reframes to move sustain talk toward change. Effective at not escalating resistance but limited in actively softening it.
