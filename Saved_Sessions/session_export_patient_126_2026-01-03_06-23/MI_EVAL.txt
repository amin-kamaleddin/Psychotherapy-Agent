# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized the patient's stance (e.g., “I accept where you are”); consistent, nonjudgmental language throughout the session — exemplary acceptance.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries (captured family priorities and limits). Missed some opportunities to explore the emotional weight of “be around” and to use deeper/complex reflections.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, persistent focus on target behaviors (smoking/alcohol) and progressive steering to a specific, actionable plan (no weeknight booze; one‑week trial). Prevented topic drift.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance with inclusive language (“let’s figure it out together”) and invitations for input. Slight tendency to move quickly through planning questions rather than pausing for more volunteered elaboration.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected choices and boundaries (honored “no checking up”), asked what felt doable, normalized uncertainty. Could add more affirmations of strengths to further bolster autonomy.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited multiple strands of change talk and practical commitments through successive elicitation. Could more actively amplify intrinsic motivations and elicit confidence/importance ratings.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good partnership language and shared planning; clinician honored patient preferences. At times the clinician’s directionality risked mild directiveness rather than purely collaborative inquiry.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change statements (e.g., reduce heavy nights, not bringing booze home, one‑week trial). Would benefit from reflecting values language and asking for a brief commitment statement or confidence ruler.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not confront minimizations and skillfully pivoted toward workable change. Could further soften sustain talk using double‑sided reflections or selective reflections that juxtapose values with minimizing beliefs.
