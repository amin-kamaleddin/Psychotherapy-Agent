# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental support through affirmations and normalizing slips, which reduced shame. Missed deeper emotional validation of Aisha's struggle and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking (e.g., motivation to be there for her son; link between drinking and smoking) which helped the patient feel heard. Relied more on brief reflections and quickly moved to advice rather than exploring affect with complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing evening cigarette use with concrete tactics and a specific follow-up plan, and linked related drinking behavior to that target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as a joint problem-solving team, refining the patient's ideas, offering choices, and respecting boundaries like not pushing the quitline. At times the clinician led with many concrete suggestions rather than first eliciting the patient's full set of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported choice by asking permission, offering alternatives, and normalizing slips to foster ownership. Some prescriptive language and checklists, however, reduced opportunities for the patient to lead decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflected and amplified intrinsic reasons (protecting her son) and invited a patient-owned plan, but most of the session focused on clinician-provided strategies rather than eliciting deeper motivations or strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance by inviting input, co-constructing the plan, and scheduling mutually agreed follow-up. The clinician sometimes assumed an expert role by prioritizing prescriptive strategies over eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete planning, affirmations, and commitment prompts (reminders, logs, follow-up). Missed opportunities to deepen change talk using open questions and scaling to strengthen desire, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation and offered harm-reduction strategies that reduced sustain talk at times. Did not consistently use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, exploring pros/cons) to strategically elicit and soften sustain talk about drinking.
