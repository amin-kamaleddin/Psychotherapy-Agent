# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful; honored patient’s wishes repeatedly (“I won’t pry… I respect you not wanting to talk more now,” “I hear you and I won’t push”). Normalized experience early and avoided labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (“I hear frustration…”, “It makes sense that parental arguing…”). Reflections accurate but somewhat surface-level; missed opportunities to probe emotional subtext of resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on anxiety, engagement, and safety; offered grounding/tools and repeatedly re-focused when patient declined. Slight limitation: did not secure a small, mutually agreed next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used collaborative language and offered choices (“If you prefer…”, “Would you like me to check in…?”). Positioned patient as decision-maker but relied more on clinician-generated options than eliciting patient-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly affirmed client control throughout (“You can reach out anytime,” “I won’t push,” “I won’t contact you”); repeatedly offered and respected choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation (asked about onset/triggers; obtained brief trigger), but did not pursue or deepen exploration of patient’s motivations, values, or goals beyond that single disclosure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed interaction as shared and respectful, offering options and invitations. Partnership limited by minimal co-creation of strategies given brief, clinician-led options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal to no change talk from patient; clinician asked open questions and offered tools but did not systematically elicit, amplify, or summarize potential change language or small commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively defused sustain talk by validating boundaries and honoring resistance (“I respect wanting space… I won’t push…”). Could increase use of double-sided reflections to gently surface ambivalence.
