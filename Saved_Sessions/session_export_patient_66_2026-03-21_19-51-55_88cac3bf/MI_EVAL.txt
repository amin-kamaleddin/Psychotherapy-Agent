# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating the patient's stance and supporting pragmatic harm-minimizing goals throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking by summarizing concerns and adapting suggestions; could deepen empathy by exploring emotional nuances and affect-laden reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on managing smoking at work with each intervention tied to that target and a measurable one-week trial to keep momentum and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a clear collaboration between equals by soliciting preferences, co-creating the plan, and offering to review and tweak notes rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by respecting the patient's choice not to quit, offering options rather than directives, and framing conditional next steps that preserve control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation for concrete experiments and self-management but could have drawn out deeper intrinsic motives, values, or used scaling questions to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the interaction as a partnership by inviting input, accepting the patient's language edits, and using inclusive language to share decision-making power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and operationalized actionable change talk (trial of gum, breaks, scripts) but missed some opportunities to amplify ambiguous statements about ability or reasons to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and strategically pivoted to manageable alternatives, though could more often use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to acknowledge both sides while shifting emphasis.
