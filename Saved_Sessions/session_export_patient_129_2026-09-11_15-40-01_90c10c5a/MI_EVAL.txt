# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, naming both sides of Tyler's ambivalence and validating his reluctance without criticism. Frames options and treats his tentative language as legitimate, conveying unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and double-sided reflections about belonging versus risk. Could deepen emotional exploration (e.g., the felt meaning of peer acceptance) and use more emotion-focused reflections at times.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on avoiding risky behaviors while preserving social ties, consistently steering toward concrete, actionable steps. Each turn builds toward specific plans like daytime hangouts, scripts, and backup strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session feels clearly collaborative—clinician offers options but repeatedly defers to Tyler’s judgments and preferences. Joint problem-solving is evident in co-creating scripts, backups, and follow-up supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and avoids pressuring language, offering options and validating limited or trial commitments. Affirms Tyler’s right to pick what he’ll try and respects hesitancy, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly draws ideas and motivations out of Tyler, eliciting concrete solutions and incremental commitments rather than supplying answers. Elicited progressively stronger change talk and personally-generated strategies (scripts, canned texts, post-texts).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions Tyler as the expert and invites his input throughout, using questions like ‘What do you think would work?’ to share power over decisions. Co-creates the plan rather than prescribing, reinforcing an equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits, reinforces, and refines Tyler’s change talk, prompting specific, proximal commitments and details about how he’ll act. Clinician asks for concrete lines, strategies, and supports which strengthen and sustain commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids direct confrontation and redirects sustain talk toward practical compromises, rolling with initial resistance. Could more explicitly reflect sustain talk (e.g., double-sided reflections or pros/cons comparisons) to further diminish its pull.
