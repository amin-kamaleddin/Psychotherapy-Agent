# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized Richard's autonomy. Avoided judgmental language and acknowledged the difficulty of long-standing routine.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections of both content and emotion that conveyed understanding. Reflections were neither mechanical nor dismissive and helped Richard soften and share more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking and consistently funneled toward concrete behavior change. The session progressed logically from ambivalence to a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner, co-creating the plan and reminder text with frequent invitations for input. The interaction was shaped by Richard's preferences rather than clinician-driven directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and ownership through validating statements and offering options rather than commands. Built the plan around Richard's boundaries to reinforce his sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation from Richard by eliciting personally meaningful reasons (e.g., his marriage) and amplifying self-generated solutions. Avoided supplying external reasons and focused on intrinsic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Richard's preferences and co-constructed the plan, demonstrating clear power-sharing. Offered choices and checked in about how to proceed together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions and reflections, leading to concrete change language. Moves elicited a specific first-step plan (porch-only, set times, gum, reminder text).

**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and frequently redirected toward change using acknowledgment and some double-sided language. Missed a few opportunities for stronger strategic pivots (explicit double-sided reflections and immediate linking to values).
