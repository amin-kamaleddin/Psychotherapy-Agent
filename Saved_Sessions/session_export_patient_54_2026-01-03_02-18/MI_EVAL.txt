# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and respect (multiple "I hear you" statements, normalizing language). Reflections preserved autonomy and avoided judgment; acceptance present across many turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking throughout (early paraphrase of ambivalence; frequent concise reflections that matched tone and content).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session kept a clear focus on alcohol use in pregnancy and on concrete next steps (brochure, partner hides wine, try non‑alc, brief check‑in). Clinician consistently steered toward a time‑limited plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent invitational language and deference to the patient’s agenda; plan was co‑constructed (questions like “what feels most acceptable…?”), showing genuine power‑sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly reinforced (“that’s your choice,” asking what would feel owned); clinician avoided directives and asked permission before offering materials.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician drew out patient's own motivations and ideas (open evocative questions elicited concrete change talk and patient-generated strategies rather than advice‑giving).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language and offers (brief factual check‑ins, asking how to support) fostered teamwork; decisions and next steps reflected joint planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple pieces of change talk were elicited and reinforced (values, specific steps, time‑limited plan). Missed opportunities to explicitly amplify and consolidate commitment/confidence (no commitment ruler or summary to strengthen a firm commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (skepticism, "my body, my choice," peers who drank) was handled tactfully with rolling with resistance and double‑sided reflections, reducing defensiveness and refocusing to workable steps.
