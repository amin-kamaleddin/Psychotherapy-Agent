**Comments:** Regularly affirmed choice and elicited willingness (e.g., “Are you willing to…?”, “Which one will you do first?”). Occasional clinician praise could shift emphasis from Marco’s ownership.

**Comments:** Strong, repeated elicitation of Marco’s reasons, ideas, and concrete steps (importance/confidence scaling, obstacle plans, if‑then contingencies, spouse request). Numerous targeted evocation prompts throughout.

### Partnership

**Comments:** Collaborative stance maintained; clinician invited Marco’s choices and framed joint planning. Occasionally offered reformulations and summaries that steered rather than purely reflected Marco’s voice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (scales, concrete steps, commitment language). Missed some opportunities to deepen and amplify change talk (e.g., linking to values or past successes to intensify commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized, but few deliberate softening strategies were used (limited double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic discrepancy to reduce repeated “pantry” sustain talk). Sustained patient sustain statements persisted.
