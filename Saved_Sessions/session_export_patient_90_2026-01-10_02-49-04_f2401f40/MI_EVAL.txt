# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Marcus's experience and praising incremental steps while avoiding blame. Created a supportive tone (confidentiality reminder, offers of billing help) throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking (acknowledging overwhelm, pacing around stressors, brief calming techniques). Missed opportunities to deepen reflections about the emotional conflict over the dog and how overwhelm feels day-to-day.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and related barriers, steering toward concrete tasks (prioritization, inhaler check, billing call). Maintained goal-oriented progress without losing the therapeutic tone.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort with co-created elements (role-plays, prioritization, drafting emails). At times the clinician supplied detailed scripts and steps before eliciting Marcus's own wording, reducing patient-driven solution generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Marcus's choice by offering multiple options, asking permission, and checking preferences for next steps. Avoided pressuring language and reinforced Marcus's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change statements and practical commitments (inhaler check, $25 plan) using open invitations and role-play. Could use more evocative questions and scaling to draw out personal reasons and strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Promoted shared decision-making and invited Marcus to choose next steps, with several co-created elements. Sometimes shifted to a more directive stance by offering clinician-generated plans before inviting Marcus's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created multiple opportunities for change talk through prioritization, scripts, and rehearsal, eliciting desire/ability/action language. Could more frequently amplify and reflect explicit change talk (importance/confidence, pros of change) to consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and offered harm-reducing compromises (keep dog out of bedroom, baths), but did not strategically reframe ambivalence with double-sided reflections or explore costs/benefits. The clinician moved quickly to practical solutions rather than resolving sustain talk.
