# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with validations and practical, nonpunitive options; could add more personalized affirmations and explicit normalization of relapse to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflections of feelings and context and tailored recommendations for safety concerns, but empathic reflections are brief and often followed quickly by advice rather than deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation with concrete next steps (medication plan, quit date, coping scripts, monitoring and check‑ins) and sustained guidance toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative problem‑solving—solicits history, negotiates quit date and check‑ins, drafts messages—though at times expert recommendations dominate and slightly shift power toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Presents multiple options and frames decisions as patient‑driven while deferring to prescribers, but some directive dosing language and occasional lack of permission‑seeking reduce perceived autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of intrinsic motivation or values—clinician affirms and operationalizes commitment but does little to draw out the patient's own reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites collaboration and tailors the plan with patient input, yet detailed dosing and monitoring recommendations sometimes reduce the sense that the patient fully shapes the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reinforcement of change talk (scheduling quit date, check‑ins) but few open questions or reflective strategies to amplify the patient's reasons, values, or confidence; interaction often shifts into advice/information.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, acknowledges barriers, and offers concrete tactics and medication alternatives; could add more double‑sided reflections to explicitly pair ambivalence with change language.
