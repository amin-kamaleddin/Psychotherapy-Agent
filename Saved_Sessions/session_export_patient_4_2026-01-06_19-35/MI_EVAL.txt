# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and respect, validates the patient’s experience, and avoids judgmental language. Reassures and normalizes distress while acknowledging real-life impacts on work and family.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening that captures both content and emotion and links patterns to the patient’s worries. Emotional attunement allowed the patient to disclose naturally and feel heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on obtaining an urgent gastroenterology referral and appropriate tests, repeatedly returning to actionable next steps. Keeps the session constrained and purpose-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine collaborative problem‑solving by co‑constructing referral language, offering options, and inviting the patient to choose the sequence of actions. Power is shared concretely (e.g., offering to send draft for approval).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering multiple options and asking permission before acting, and by honoring the patient’s request to review wording. Reinforces patient ownership of decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s external priorities (safety, being taken seriously, return to work) and practical willingness (symptom tracking). Less emphasis on drawing out intrinsic motives or linking coping options to personal values and internal change resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as a collaborator and expert, inviting input on wording, timing, and which actions to prioritize. Encourages shared decision‑making and co‑ownership of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of change‑related language (agreement to symptom tracking, choice questions), but the clinician does not systematically amplify or deepen the patient’s change statements or assess confidence/commitment. Focus remains more on medical advocacy than on strengthening self‑management talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and does not reinforce sustain talk, validating fears and honoring requests for tests. Misses opportunities for double‑sided reflections or gentle reframes that would acknowledge medical concerns while highlighting observations that could pivot toward change.
