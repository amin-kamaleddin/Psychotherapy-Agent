# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence (e.g., “You’re allowed to care about both,” “That’s hopeful and realistic”). Tone remained respectful and non‑blaming throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective‑taking with frequent reflective summaries capturing embarrassment, desire for connection, and worry about reputation (multiple “I hear you…” reflections anchoring the session).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on drinking‑related injury and moved the conversation from ambivalence to concrete change planning (alarm, two‑drink rule, NA beer, nonrefundable spin class).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as exploratory and invited Kevin’s input (“Let’s explore what feels doable,” “Which of those supports feels most doable?”), co‑constructing solutions rather than prescribing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported patient choice and avoided coercion (“no pressure, just learning,” asking what feels realistic), reinforcing ownership of decisions and pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Kevin’s own motivations (avoid injury, keep job/reputation, stay social) and used focused questions to draw out reasons for change and practical supports.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Kevin as the expert on his situation, shared power in decision‑making (choice of alarm, friend, nonrefundable commitment) and asked how he preferred to set up plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified change talk, converting desire/intent into specific implementation steps (paid booking, told boss, asked Mark to text), strengthening preparatory and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used normalization, but missed a few opportunities to more actively soften sustain talk (e.g., “it’d kill some fun,” teasing). Could have used more double‑sided reflections or cost‑of‑sustain questions to further reduce the pull of sustain talk.
