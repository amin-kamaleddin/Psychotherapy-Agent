# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Margaret’s choices, validating her values without criticism. Multiple accepting statements and affirmations created a warm, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses timely, accurate reflections that capture Margaret’s perspective and help her elaborate. Could deepen by offering more complex reflections of emotion or underlying fears rather than primarily restating content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on reducing alcohol-related risk while preserving social contact and consistently moves conversation toward practical actions. Questions and reflections guide from values to concrete behavioral steps without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative with open questions and co-constructed decisions; the clinician builds on Margaret’s ideas. Power is shared as Margaret selects realistic strategies and the plan is jointly developed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports Margaret’s choice and control, accepting her decision not to quit and asking what feels realistic. Avoids pressure and reinforces that final decisions remain hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Margaret’s own motivations (independence, avoiding a fall, not worrying her son) using evocative questions. Elicited personally meaningful reasons that shaped a self-chosen action plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as a joint effort and invites Margaret’s expertise throughout the session. Repeatedly asks which options feel doable and co-constructs the plan, demonstrating genuine partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Systematically elicits concrete change talk and practical ideas (packed drink, alarms, limits), producing clear client-generated commitments. Could further amplify, summarize, and probe for commitment/confidence (e.g., scaling or start dates) to strengthen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and accepts sustain talk without arguing, then reframes toward harm-reduction options to avoid reinforcing resistance. Would benefit from earlier double-sided reflections and gently exploring costs of sustain talk to tip ambivalence more toward change.
