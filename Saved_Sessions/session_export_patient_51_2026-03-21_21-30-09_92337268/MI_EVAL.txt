# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respect and positive regard, using nonjudgmental, validating language and acknowledging Ingrid's time pressure. Affirmations and supportive statements reinforced a safe, accepting stance throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and brief reflective listening were used, particularly around Ingrid's time constraints and responsible behavior. Reflections remained mostly functional and logistical rather than exploring deeper emotions or concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, clinically appropriate focus on medication safety and the citalopram–phenelzine interaction, providing concise, accurate information and practical next steps. The session stayed on task despite options to expand the discussion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was team-based and co-constructed, with the clinician soliciting Ingrid's preferences for format, timing, and content of follow-up materials. The psychiatrist consistently honored her input and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reinforced Ingrid's right to choose, offering options for follow-up and explicitly advising not to change meds without contacting the prescriber. Language supported patient ownership of decisions without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician prioritized information and logistics and made minimal effort to elicit Ingrid's internal motivations, values, or reasons for checking the interaction. There were no targeted questions about importance, confidence, or personal goals to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership by asking what Ingrid wanted, offering choices, and co-creating the follow-up note and checklist with her input. Power-sharing was evident as the clinician negotiated details and respected her preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some practical support for change-related planning was present (note, follow-up booking), and Ingrid voiced preparatory change talk, but the clinician did not intentionally elicit or amplify her reasons, importance, or commitment language. Efforts were pragmatic rather than strategic to evoke change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not challenged, with the clinician respecting Ingrid's stance and redirecting to safety planning and follow-up. To reach the highest rating, the clinician could have used double-sided reflections to gently highlight discrepancy while normalizing reluctance.
