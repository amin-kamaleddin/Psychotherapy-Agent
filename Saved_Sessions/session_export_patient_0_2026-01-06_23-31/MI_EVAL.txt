# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, with validating statements and practical options that normalized harm‑reduction. Avoided moralizing language and conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated empathic gestures and perspective‑taking, including explicit reflections and attention to privacy concerns. Missed deeper rolling reflections around shame, defensiveness, and values tension.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on meth use and concrete harm‑reduction (testing, sterile supplies, naloxone) throughout the encounter. At times shifted into extended instruction without checking priorities or summarizing agreed next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the visit as joint problem‑solving, asked what would be most helpful, and tailored recommendations to the patient's anonymity concerns. Much of the exchange remained clinician‑provided information rather than co‑generated steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice, offered multiple options, and sought permission before offering to locate services, supporting patient control. Advice was noncoercive and framed to preserve the patient's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation present (asking which option was most helpful, offering to locate services), but overall prioritized information over eliciting the patient's motivations or values. Lacked open questions and reflections to amplify emerging change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited the patient's preferences and offered options that distributed power, supporting a collaborative stance. Could have more explicitly named the patient's expertise and co‑constructed a first small step together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened space for change talk and the patient accepted harm‑reduction, but the clinician primarily provided information rather than eliciting or amplifying the patient's own motivations. Change talk was encouraged only sporadically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, yet did not employ strategic MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to gently reframe ambivalence. The approach was neutral‑to‑supportive information instead of actively softening sustain talk.
