# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected and nonjudgmental tone with frequent normalization of choices, which reduced pressure; comments sometimes felt formulaic and were paired with directive suggestions rather than individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid perspective-taking and reflective statements that validate fatigue and scheduling stress; could use more complex or affect-focused reflections to deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on increasing study productivity (single Pomodoro) and brief sleep habits, repeatedly returning to practical, concrete steps and a copy-ready plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone and offers framed as partnership with options and check-ins, but the clinician frequently supplied exact phrasing and stepwise instructions instead of eliciting the client's language or solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and agency, accepted refusals without pressure, and repeatedly reinforced that the client could opt out or modify the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of intrinsic motivation or values; relied more on offering logistical solutions than drawing out the client's reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used collaborative wording and offered help, but often moved into expert-driven problem-solving and concrete instructions rather than co-creating the plan with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced small behavioral commitments (micro change talk) but missed opportunities to systematically evoke reasons, importance, or confidence language to amplify motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided confrontation, normalized ambivalence, and reframed plans as low-pressure experiments which reduced sustain talk; could have used more strategic double-sided reflections to shift ambivalence toward change.
