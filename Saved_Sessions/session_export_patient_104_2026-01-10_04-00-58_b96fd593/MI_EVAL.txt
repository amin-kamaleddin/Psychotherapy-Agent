# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizes Nadine's feelings, and explicitly accepts her plan without blame. Language is warm and nonjudgmental throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and specific, picking up Nadine's words and emotional states (e.g., 'so tired,' 'a zombie') and elaborating their meaning. These responses validate her priorities and show strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear, consistent focus on the single, concrete sleep-support behavior and its measurable outcome. Interventions remain tightly tied to the brief experiment and avoid drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The style is clearly collaborative with joint language and frequent invitations for input, co-creating a small practical plan. Negotiation of concrete steps reflects shared decision-making and mutual respect.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options, asking preferences, and accepting Nadine's boundary to try only one night. There is no pressure and the clinician reinforces Nadine's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Nadine's own motivations and reasons for change, probing values and expected outcomes. Nadine articulates desired outcomes and the clinician builds on those internal motivations to support the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative and invites choice, conveying shared problem-solving and substantial client voice. Partnership could be deepened by eliciting Nadine's own strategy ideas and explicitly naming her expertise about her baby and routines.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicits and amplifies change language, asks which tiny change feels realistic, and reinforces Nadine's commitment. Questions about expected outcomes help strengthen desire, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and not amplified, with normalization and a time‑bound trial to gently redirect concerns. The clinician could more often use double-sided reflections or contrast sustain talk with change language to further reduce resistance.
