# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence with warm, nonjudgmental statements, validating the client's experience throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, sustained reflective listening and perspective-taking capture both emotion and content, repeatedly matching the patient's language and affect to help them feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on the afternoon coffee swap and tracking plan, moving logically from exploration to a concrete, time-bound experiment without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functions as genuine collaboration: the clinician solicits preferences and uses them to shape a feasible plan, keeping the patient in the driver’s seat.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes choice and frames the plan as a voluntary, low-risk experiment, avoiding pressure and reinforcing client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits intrinsic motivation by framing a curiosity-driven experiment and asking about hoped-for outcomes, but could probe values and confidence/commitment more deeply to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the session as joint exploration and treats the client as the expert, using open invitations and collaborative wording to co-construct the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out desire and concrete reasons for a small experiment and specific markers to watch, though could more often amplify and summarize change statements and elicit confidence/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk by reframing the swap as a low-risk experiment and using double-sided reflections, but sometimes restates sustain-oriented language instead of preferentially amplifying change talk.
