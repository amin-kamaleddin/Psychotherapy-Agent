# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys unconditional positive regard throughout (timely safety check, repeated thanks and validation) and normalizes slips without judgment, making the patient feel respected and heard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening by naming and mirroring the patient's experiences and emotions, which fostered understanding and rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (aligning choices with family happiness) with concrete implementation steps and a planned follow-up to support accountability.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like a true joint enterprise: the clinician and patient co-created the plan, checked preferences, and arranged shared follow-up, reflecting equal-partner teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by asking permission before offering options, presenting clear choices, and emphasizing gentle, patient-led pacing so the patient retained ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivation by linking behavior to core values, asking for patient-generated solutions, and drawing out commitment language rather than imposing reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates power-sharing and respect for the patient's expertise by inviting input at decision points and allowing the patient to select and modify the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and reinforces change language through concrete examples, asking for a small specific change, and setting short monitoring and follow-up tasks to sustain commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges barriers without judgment and provides practical contingencies that reduce sustain talk, but could more explicitly use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or summaries) to further diminish ambivalence.
