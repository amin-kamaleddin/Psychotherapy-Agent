# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation, respecting the patient's limits and offering choices; no blaming or punitive language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and contextual reflections (work breaks, coffee routine), but some reflections remained surface-level with missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation and managing repetitive advice, consistently steering to concrete next steps like a script and short plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the encounter as a joint effort through agenda choices, co-created scripts and role-plays, and adapted interventions to the patient's stated preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported autonomy with permission-seeking, offering options, and removing pressure while accepting hedged commitments without confrontation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some reasons and tentative commitments but largely supplied solutions rather than systematically drawing out the patient's own values, reasons, or confidence with evocative questions or scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership through permission, role-play, and shared tool-building, though at times the clinician offered many suggestions without first eliciting the patient's priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and small commitments, but did not consistently deepen or amplify patient change language or use focused DARN-C elicitation to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively rolled with resistance, validated frustration, and avoided arguing, though could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly balance sustain and change talk.
