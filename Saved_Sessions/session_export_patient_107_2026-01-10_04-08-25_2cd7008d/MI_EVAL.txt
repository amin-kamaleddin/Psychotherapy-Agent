# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence; the patient explicitly said they appreciated not being made to feel bad.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and the patient's language to show understanding, but could deepen emotional/affective reflections to explore feelings more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, steady focus on coffee reduction and moved the session toward a concrete, measurable one-week experiment without drifting to other topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and repeatedly invited the patient's input, co-creating the plan and integrating the client's past strategies into decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice, framed the plan as an experiment, and avoided pressure, supporting the patient's right to decide and to restart after slips.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic reasons, observable signs, and feasible strategies, though could strengthen evocation by linking the plan to deeper values or longer-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as the expert and shared power in decision-making, asking for preferences and letting the client drive specifics of the experiment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language and framed the plan as an experiment, but could more often amplify and summarize client change statements to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided reinforcing it, using low-risk framing to reduce its influence, though more double-sided reflections could further tip sustain talk toward change.
