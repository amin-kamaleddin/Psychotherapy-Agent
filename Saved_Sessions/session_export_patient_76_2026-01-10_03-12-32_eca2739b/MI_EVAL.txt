# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through normalization and reassurance, avoiding blame or moralizing. To refine mastery, briefly individualize praise by naming specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, high-quality reflective listening that labeled bodily sensations and ambivalence and normalized the struggle. Could occasionally add deeper affect-laden reflections (e.g., naming shame or exhaustion) to access underlying emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing self‑harm and building safer coping with concrete, behaviorally specific goals. Repeatedly returned to an actionable experiment rather than drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in genuine joint problem-solving, asking for input and integrating the patient's ideas (ice placement, reminders) into the plan. Language consistently shared power and honored practical constraints like roommates and uni.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported client choice by framing strategies as experiments and normalizing doubt rather than prescribing solutions. Repeated invitations to choose reinforced client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited internal motivations and personal reasons for alternatives, linking to avoiding being 'a mess' and surviving the moment. Could strengthen by more consistently tying those values to the experiment and probing confidence/commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the patient as an expert and co-created the plan, soliciting preferences at each step. The clinician's collaborative stance fostered engagement and feasible planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions and by exploring past successes, producing concrete change language. Would improve by amplifying and sequencing those statements into stronger commitment language (e.g., summaries, scaling questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and often reframed doubts in ways that preserved ambivalence while nudging toward experimentation. Could more frequently use double-sided reflections that explicitly contrast costs and benefits to tip the balance toward change.
