# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated respect and nonjudgmental regard (multiple validations of time pressure and irritation). Affirmed limits and supported choices. Slightly procedural — missed deeper, individualized expressions of unconditional positive regard and more nuanced normalization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases (e.g., readiness, frustration). Kept tuned to practical/efficiency stance. Missed opportunities to probe or reflect affective layers (impact of chronic time pressure, feeling disrespected).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (weight/BP, time‑efficient activity). Maintained goal-oriented structure from opening through to a concrete plan and follow‑up; avoided drift despite repeated patient complaints.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently invited patient input and shared decision‑making (offered options, asked which felt doable, co‑constructed alarm text). Patient’s preferences determined the plan—active, sustained collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported patient choice and pace (“I’ll support what you decide,” “your choice, your pace”). Offered options, respected limits, and accepted “not promising miracles,” reinforcing autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations and barriers using open questions and scaling (readiness, confidence, what would raise it, barriers, signs of success). Used patient responses to shape the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned patient as expert and shared power (asked permission to suggest, checked feasibility, accepted blunt wording). Plan elements were co‑created and driven by patient choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (readiness, small commitments, noticing benefits). Could have amplified and elaborated desire/reasons (linking weight goals to values or broader benefits) to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward small, achievable changes; used barrier‑reduction strategies. Could improve use of double‑sided reflections and explicit discrepancy statements to more effectively soften sustain talk.
