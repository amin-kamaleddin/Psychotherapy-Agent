# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence with a nonjudgmental tone, repeatedly affirming the patient's efforts and constraints.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and validates feelings which deepens rapport, but reflections are sometimes repetitive and miss opportunities to probe deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on smoking and protecting the child, consistently steering toward actionable, short-term implementation steps without being directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative and builds on the patient's ideas, though offering a short list of clinician-suggested options early slightly steers the choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces patient choice and self-direction, accepts the patient's stance, and asks what feels doable without applying pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical motivations and concrete strategies, but could deepen evocation by exploring more personally meaningful motives and confidence ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the interaction as a joint exploration, defers to the patient's expertise, and co-creates a realistic plan using inclusive language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with evocative questions and reflections, leading the patient to articulate specific intentions and implementation steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and validates sustain talk while redirecting to feasible alternatives, but rarely uses strategic techniques like double-sided reflections to reframe ambivalence.
