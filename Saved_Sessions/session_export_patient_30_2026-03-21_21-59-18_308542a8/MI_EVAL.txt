# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validated David’s life context and constraints, normalizing ambivalence. Avoided labels or criticism, supporting a strong accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections captured both content and underlying values (protecting work and family), helping David feel understood. Could have slightly deepened emotional exploration, but empathy was consistently strong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on cholesterol management and medication hesitancy throughout the session. Translated ambivalence into a concrete, time-limited action plan tying all conversation branches back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner by inviting David’s input, co-constructing the plan, and offering tools only after asking. The clinician positioned themselves as a helper rather than an authoritarian expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed David’s right to choose and offered multiple pathways, repeatedly leaving decisions to him. Avoided pressuring language and honored his preferences about how to involve his wife or doctor.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited values and modest change talk (e.g., swaps, walks, scheduling) and asked about confidence and barriers. Could have probed deeper into intrinsic motivations, long-term meaning, and past successes to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and invited preferences, creating a largely collaborative dynamic. However, the clinician often offered concrete solutions and drafted materials without always checking how much directive help David wanted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced specific change language and created concrete plans (swaps, walks, timeline). Missed opportunities to amplify change talk and follow up on the 0–10 confidence rating to build stronger commitment and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and neutrally reframed ambivalence while offering alternatives that honored concerns. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to pivot sustain talk toward change.
