# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used nonjudgmental language and multiple affirmations (“I appreciate your honesty,” “You’re the one in charge,” promises of no lecture). Appeared respectful across turns but could use warmer normalization of ambivalence and fewer mildly praising statements to reach top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and paraphrases (e.g., “you’re kind of torn,” restating plan/confidence) showed good perspective-taking. Reflections were sometimes surface-level; could deepen with more complex feeling/meaning reflections linking values and feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on alcohol risk-reduction and actionable planning (goal setting, confidence, backups, safety). Clinician maintained agenda and steered to a clear, realistic plan without getting sidetracked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent invitations for Marcus’s input and co-construction of plan (asks which is doable, solicits his wording). Occasionally offered solutions/examples before explicitly asking permission; more explicit shared decision language would strengthen partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice and ownership (“You’re the one in charge”), respected Marcus’s goal (weekends-only) and supported decisions while addressing safety — consistent autonomy support throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Marcus’s reasons, preferences, confidence, and concrete implementation intentions (leave by 11, text Miguel, backups). Clinician drew out Marcus’s own change language rather than imposing solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed interaction as teamwork and invited input; clinician sometimes shifted into expert/problem-solving mode (providing scripts) without always prefacing with permission. Greater “permission + offer” phrasing would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited desire, reasons, and commitment statements (avoid blackouts, “I’ll try not to drink on school nights,” plan to leave by 11). Missed opportunities to amplify and consolidate change talk into stronger commitment language (shift from “try” to “will”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and acknowledged benefits Marcus named, then redirected toward planning. Could further soften sustain talk via double-sided reflections and explicit nonjudgmental discrepancy linking values to behavior.
