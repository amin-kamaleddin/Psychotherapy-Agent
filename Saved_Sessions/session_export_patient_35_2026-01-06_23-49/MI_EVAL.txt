# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through multiple affirmations and normalization, never shaming Elena for smoking. This created a clearly accepting, nonjudgmental tone throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening captured Elena’s context and ambivalence, mirroring her language and acknowledging barriers and emotion. The clinician accurately validated stress and doubt rather than minimizing them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, sustained focus on smoking/secondhand smoke reduction and progressed logically to concrete steps (tools, start date, tracking, follow-up). Moves were goal-directed without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed the plan with Elena, soliciting her input at each step and offering practical supports shaped by her preferences and constraints. Decisions and concrete plans were clearly shared and negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered choices and validated Elena’s right not to promise quitting, reinforcing her sense of control and ownership of decisions. Suggestions were framed as optional and noncoercive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited self-generated reasons for change and a specific plan, producing commitment language, but did less to explore deeper values, intrinsic motivators, or past successes that could strengthen internal motivation. More probing of personal meaning and confidence would enhance evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction modeled an equal partnership with repeated invitations to choose, offers to tweak plans, and collaborative problem-solving. Elena’s expertise and preferences were centered in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (choices, start date, willingness to try) but could have deepened and amplified intrinsic reasons and confidence (e.g., values questions, confidence rulers, past successes). Additional probes would likely strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double-sided reflections to redirect toward change, validating doubt without arguing. There were missed opportunities to more fully reflect the stress-relief function of smoking before pivoting, which might further reduce defensiveness.
