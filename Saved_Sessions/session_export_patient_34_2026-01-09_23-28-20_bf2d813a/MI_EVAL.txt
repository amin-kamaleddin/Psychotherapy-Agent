### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalized Dylan's frustration and honored his preferences; unconditional positive regard was sustained throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflections and empathic listening that helped Dylan feel heard; missed opportunities to probe deeper emotions (e.g., shame, fear, or values).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on resolving the conduct referral with actionable steps (drafting statement, email, rehearsal); could have more deliberately linked this work to broader drinking-related goals or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a collaborative problem-solving session, co-creating the statement and integrating edits; clinician avoided an authoritarian stance and shared decision-making was evident.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered options and respected Dylan's choices, transparently stated limits (e.g., not sending emails) and preserved his control over actions and wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicited little of Dylan's internal motivations beyond pragmatic incentives to avoid sanctions and did not explore values, ambivalence, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established a clear collaborative stance, repeatedly invited Dylan's preferences, and deferred to his final decisions, showing strong power-sharing and client-led decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (agreement to complete an online course) and offered concrete options, but did not systematically deepen, reflect, or sequence emerging change language toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and remained neutral to minimizations, but did not actively employ MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to soften sustain talk.
