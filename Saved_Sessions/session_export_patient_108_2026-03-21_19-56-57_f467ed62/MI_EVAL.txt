# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent normalizing and affirming statements that convey warmth and validation throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and acknowledges guilt and ambivalence, but reflections are often brief and the clinician moves quickly to problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on asserting the move-out boundary with concrete, stepwise interventions (scripts, role-play, checklist) that avoid drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative—asks questions, elicits priorities, and invites practice—but the clinician frequently supplies scripted language instead of co-developing wording with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice by offering multiple phrasing options and inviting preferences, though a few prescriptive moments (firm commands/steps) somewhat reduce maximal autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out rehearsal and preparatory change talk using role-play and prompts, but could further elicit the patient’s own arguments and values to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and tailors coaching to the patient's concerns, yet occasionally shifts toward directive coaching with clinician-provided scripts that lessen equal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change language and helps translate intent into concrete plans, but could more often reflect and amplify the patient's own change-talk statements to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk but primarily responds with tactical rebuttals and solutions rather than exploring or reframing the emotional reasons for maintaining the status quo; limited double-sided reflections.
