### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with repeated validation and normalization of Amina’s feelings and motivations (e.g., acknowledging her protectiveness and anxiety). The clinician conveys unconditional positive regard and avoids labeling or criticism.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate empathic listening and reflects both content and emotion (e.g., noticing trembling, tears, and nervousness). Empathy could be deepened by lingering more on underlying emotions and values before shifting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on improving co-parenting and establishing consistent routines, repeatedly returning to concrete goals (bedtime, screens, two-week trial). Interventions and role-plays are consistently tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: asks for Amina’s preferences, adapts scripts based on her feedback, and uses role-play to co-construct language and plans. The clinician integrates the client’s expertise into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking permission before giving materials, and framing proposals as trials rather than mandates. Language and suggested responses preserve Amina’s agency and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation tied to Amina’s values (love for her son) but relies heavily on clinician-provided strategies rather than drawing out sustained client-generated reasons, importance, or confidence. More open, evocative questions would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively and incorporates Amina’s wording and preferences, using iterative revision and role-play. Early expert-driven advice slightly reduced the balance of co-creation, preventing a top score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some concrete change proposals and trials (scripts, two-week trial) but does not consistently elicit Amina’s own reasons, benefits, or confidence language. The clinician more often offers solutions than asking evocative questions to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges resistance and provides de-escalation lines and options, but at times moves to directive recommendations that may feel prescriptive. Greater use of double-sided reflections and rolling with resistance could better soften sustain talk.
