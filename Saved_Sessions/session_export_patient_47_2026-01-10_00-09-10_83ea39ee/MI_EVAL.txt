# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm—normalizes defensiveness, thanks the patient, and offers options without criticism, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating the patient's plan and preferences, but could use more complex reflections to name deeper emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use throughout, moving logically from assessment to neutral information to a specific, time-limited behavior change plan with practical supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicits preferences, co-constructs the plan, accepts patient edits, and offers tools the patient can choose and tailor, so the patient's ideas shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, and validating the patient's decision to try a month-long plan rather than pressuring for abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own reasons for change (fall, son's concern, sleep) and uses them to build a plan, but misses opportunities for scaling questions and deeper value exploration to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly framed as a partnership: power is shared, preferences are checked repeatedly, and the clinician implements patient-suggested tweaks, reflecting high-quality partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk and helps co-create a concrete month-long plan, but could further amplify reasons for change and assess importance/confidence to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and uses neutral information and small steps to honor sustain talk, though could more strategically use double-sided reflections and selectively emphasize change statements to shift ambivalence.
