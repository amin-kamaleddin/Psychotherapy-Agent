# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, opening with clear unconditional positive regard and maintaining a warm, accepting tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and accurate reflections that validate emotion and practical burden, though some reflections are repetitive and deeper emotional links (shame/belonging) could be explored further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on the target behavior and practical steps (automatic transfers, cardless account), with minor lapses in quickly converting planning into a firm, time-bound commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-creation of the plan—clinician invites Lukas’s preferences, leverages his examples, and avoids lecturing, maintaining an equal partnership throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and ownership by offering options and framing suggestions as choices, preserving Lukas’s control over what to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations, past successes, and concrete reasons for change, but could more actively amplify change language and press for a specific commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally positions Lukas as the expert and invites his ideas to co-construct the plan, with a few slightly steering moves and missed opportunities to explicitly set agenda or confirm next steps in his words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and past success, drawing out doable steps, though the clinician could more often amplify spontaneous change statements and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
