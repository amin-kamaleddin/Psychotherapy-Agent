# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes David's context while validating concerns, and affirms his realistic, cautious plan without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures content and life constraints, but reflections are often literal/repetitive and the clinician does not probe deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on cholesterol management and medication hesitancy, linking questions to actionable steps and keeping momentum toward a concrete, time-limited experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shows genuine teamwork by co-creating the plan with David and his wife, soliciting preferred supports and formats, and shaping follow-up based on patient preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, checks what feels doable, and frames medication as a pause while asking how to support the patient, preserving his sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical, personally meaningful indicators and short-term motivators, but does not sufficiently probe deeper values or longer-term reasons to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as collaborative, invites the patient's expertise and choices, and explicitly defers to David and his wife for implementation details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits change talk and concrete planning (steps, tracking, outcomes), but misses chances to amplify change statements and draw out stronger desire/ability/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes the approach as an experiment with safety monitoring, though it often repeats sustain-talk phrasing instead of using double-sided reflections to gently contrast concerns with reasons for change.
