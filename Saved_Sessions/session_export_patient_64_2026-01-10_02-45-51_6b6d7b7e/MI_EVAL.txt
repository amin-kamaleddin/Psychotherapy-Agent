# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing ambivalence and affirming Jordan's feelings and plan. No shaming or confrontational language was used, supporting a high level of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates clear understanding with accurate reflections that capture ambivalence and practical concerns. Could deepen by probing emotional nuance and using more feeling‑based reflections to validate the emotional weight of the decision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (getting the flu vaccine) and moves from exploration to concrete action planning. Repeatedly brings discussion back to vaccination decisions and practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative: the clinician asks Jordan's preferences, co‑creates scripts, offers role‑play, and invites follow‑up to jointly revisit decisions. The plan is clearly shaped around Jordan's choices and needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Jordan's autonomy by offering options, affirming it’s okay to stay undecided, and avoiding pressure. Clinician explicitly acknowledges Jordan's ownership of the final decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits protective motives for grandparents and translates them into actionable steps and scripts that draw on Jordan's values. Could more systematically use open questions to elicit additional self‑motivational statements and build toward commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership through shared decision‑making, asking preferences, and co‑creating plans. Partnership is slightly reduced at times by offering information without first asking permission or using Elicit‑Provide‑Elicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces some change talk (protecting grandparents, crafting scripts, planning actions) and praises helpful statements. Missed opportunities to more actively evoke and amplify desire, reasons, need, and confidence through open questions and strategic reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers neutral‑to‑helpful alternatives and information. Did not consistently use targeted MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, rolling with resistance) to strategically soften sustain talk and move toward discrepancy.
