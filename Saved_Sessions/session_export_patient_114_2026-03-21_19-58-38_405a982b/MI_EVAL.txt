# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing Ryan’s concerns and offering practical, blame-free help throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections (acknowledging embarrassment, worry, and conflicting priorities) but missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections to explore defensiveness and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on the immediate target (academic risk and hearing preparation) and consistently guided the session toward actionable steps without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a collaborative problem-solving partner by co-creating templates, asking for preferences, inviting drafts, and offering iterative feedback to share responsibility for next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Ryan’s sense of choice and control by offering options, asking permission to personalize drafts, and deferring sending decisions to him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation indirectly by linking actions to Ryan’s values (protecting grades/scholarship) but relied more on pragmatic problem-solving than directly drawing out his personal reasons, importance, confidence, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a shared, power-balanced interaction through offering choices, inviting collaboration, and positioning Ryan as the decision-maker in creating plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported and drew out change talk tied to academic priorities and offered concrete steps aligned with those values, but did not systematically elicit or amplify desire/ability/reason/need language about drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirected to harm-reduction and actionable steps, though did not use more explicit MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to actively soften sustain talk.
