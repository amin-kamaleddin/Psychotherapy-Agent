# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with repeated validation and respectful framing, avoiding blame throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and acknowledged priorities (e.g., dad's birthday), but reflections were often brief and pragmatic with missed opportunities to explore deeper vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on re-establishing a phone call and avoiding further trouble, providing concrete, stepwise plans and contingency options to move toward the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked jointly to craft and refine messages and plans and invited input, though the clinician often supplied the substantive wording rather than eliciting more of the patient's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, asked permission, and respected the patient's preferences, framing options so Tariq could choose the approach that fit his priorities.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used the patient's stated family motivation but did limited elicitation of deeper intrinsic reasons, confidence, or commitment language and relied more on external problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making by asking which option Tariq wanted and iterating drafts, but frequently took the lead in generating content rather than prompting patient-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Picked up on pro-change statements about family connection and provided supportive tools, but did not sufficiently elicit or amplify additional desire, reasons, or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and briefly acknowledged frustration, yet missed opportunities for strategic softening (e.g., double-sided reflections or gentle reframing) before shifting to problem-solving.
