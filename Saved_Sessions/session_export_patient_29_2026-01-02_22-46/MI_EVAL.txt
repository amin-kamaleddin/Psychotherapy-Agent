# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., “I’m glad you came…,” “thank you for that honesty”). Repeated affirmations of patient priorities/constraints (quit timing, work deadlines) and validation of side‑effect concerns. Multiple acceptance moves across the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective summaries and empathic statements (“I hear you…,” “that’s reasonable”) that demonstrate accurate understanding. Could deepen emotional reflections about work stress/family burden to further validate emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear steering toward a quit plan (med options, titration, Jan 17 quit date, follow‑up). Balanced direction with choice. Missed brief opportunity to consolidate direction with an explicit commitment statement after the quit date was set.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent shared decision‑making — offered options, asked permission (e.g., send boss message?), offered to join spouse call only if patient wanted. Safety planning and follow‑up arranged collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported patient agency (multiple medication options, asked preference, invited patient to choose who sends messages, honored decision to stop meds if side effects occur). Tone and language protected autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations and barriers (importance/confidence questions, “what would help raise it one point?”, med preferences). Could further deepen by asking brief evocative questions to expand reasons and imagery of benefits.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician worked as partner — offered to join spouse call, provided scripts for spouse/boss, arranged follow‑up and on‑call contact. Demonstrated collaborative problem solving and support.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient produced strong change talk (desire, reasons, plan, chosen quit date). Clinician supported and summarized these statements but could more actively amplify and elicit a concise commitment statement and first concrete step to strengthen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged and normalized concerns (concentration, nausea), offered options to manage/monitor and to stop if severe — which softened sustain talk. Could further reduce sustain talk by reflecting ambivalence more deeply on a few key turns.
