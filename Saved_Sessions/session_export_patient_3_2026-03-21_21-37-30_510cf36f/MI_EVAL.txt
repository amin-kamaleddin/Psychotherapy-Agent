# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized struggle, praised effort, and avoided judgment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and summaries that captured both practical strain and emotional motivation, helping the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on resuming exercise with questions, reflections, and planning all tied to a specific, time‑bound walking plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co‑constructed the plan with the patient, jointly drafting texts, cues, and fallbacks while adapting suggestions to her preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and ownership by offering options, asking permission before ideas, and repeatedly affirming that decisions were the patient's.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic motives and used scaling and values questions, but could further deepen motivation by drawing out the patient's own vivid descriptions and arguments for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated an inclusive, collaborative stance and checked preferences, with a slight drift toward offering multiple expert suggestions rather than eliciting the patient's solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk and planning well, though could more often amplify and elaborate strong change statements to build momentum and elicit firmer commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and redirected minimal sustain talk toward planning and fallbacks, with room to use more double‑sided reflections to explicitly name the pull of the status quo then pivot to change.
