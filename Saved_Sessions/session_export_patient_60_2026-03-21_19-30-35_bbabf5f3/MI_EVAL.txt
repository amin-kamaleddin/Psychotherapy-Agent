# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing the student’s perspective and offering practical help rather than moralizing. Language and tone reinforced unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic listening with accurate reflections and attending to the student’s concerns. Could increase use of more complex reflections to explore underlying emotions (e.g., frustration or embarrassment).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on underage drinking, safety, and academic consequences, offering two targeted directions early on. Session content consistently ties back to the immediate behavioral issue and the patient’s goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites patient preference, offers format choices, and tailors information to requests, promoting collaboration. At times the clinician slips into offering solutions rather than co-creating plans step-by-step with the student.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and framing tips as optional harm-reduction rather than mandates. Reinforces the student’s right to choose their approach throughout the interaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-relevant language and links safety to the student’s goals, but largely supplies expert information instead of systematically drawing out personal reasons or confidence. Missed opportunities for targeted evocative questions and reflections to build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership through choice and responsive information, asking what format is useful and checking preferences. Tends toward expert-driven guidance (detailed instructions) rather than jointly constructing solutions with the student.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk (patient agrees to try tips) and offers choice prompts, but does not consistently amplify or deepen the patient’s own reasons and commitment. The clinician often moves quickly to give information instead of reflecting and exploring change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids argument and rolls with resistance, reflecting and reframing toward harm reduction and goals. Could further soften sustain talk by using more double-sided reflections to gently shift ambivalence toward change.
