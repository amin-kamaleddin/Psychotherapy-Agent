# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's choices, repeatedly affirming limits and harm‑reduction goals without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows consistent, warm understanding with brief reflections and normalizing statements, but responses stay somewhat surface‑level and could probe emotional nuance more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on smoking harm reduction and NRT with clear, actionable goals (dosing, logging, check‑in timing) and avoids drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conducts collaborative problem solving and checks feasibility, but much of the detailed plan (dosing, log format) is clinician‑driven rather than co‑generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice through options, permission‑style questions, and explicit acceptance of the patient's decision not to quit, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits behavioral commitments and trial steps but offers limited open questioning to draw out the patient's intrinsic motivations, values, or perceived importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative and asks preferences for follow‑up, though the clinician supplies substantial structure that slightly shifts power toward expert direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively invites small steps and elicits trial behavior with affirmations that consolidate change talk, but misses opportunities to amplify personal reasons and benefits more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes sustain talk into adaptive harm‑reduction alternatives, though double‑sided or amplified reflections to more explicitly acknowledge both sides are used infrequently.
