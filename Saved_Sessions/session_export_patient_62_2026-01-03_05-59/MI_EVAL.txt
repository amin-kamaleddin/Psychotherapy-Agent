# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Communicates unconditional positive regard
- [x] Avoids blaming, shaming, moralizing
- [x] Uses affirmations/validation repeatedly
- [ ] Minimizes or redirects emotions in a way that risks judgment
Comments: Clinician repeatedly normalized and affirmed (“you’re doing what you can… you’re worthy where you are”) with no shaming or moralizing; multiple clear validation statements across the session.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Uses accurate reflections and summaries frequently
- [x] Mirrors patient affect and content
- [ ] Deepens emotional exploration (e.g., probes chest‑tightness, shame)
- [ ] Avoids formulaic/parroting reflections entirely
Comments: Strong, consistent empathic reflections and summaries throughout; some reflections felt formulaic and repetitive (repeated literal echoing of “I can’t get a break”), missing opportunities to deepen affect exploration.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Keeps focus on target behaviors (bills, work)
- [x] Progressively narrows to a specific, time‑bound micro‑goal
- [x] Maintains appropriate momentum toward change
Comments: Clear movement from problem identification to a concrete micro‑goal (10‑minute one‑bill task, alarm/timer), with consistent direction and goal focus.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Invites patient preferences and options
- [x] Co‑designs the plan with patient input
- [ ] Imposes clinician-driven solutions without negotiation
Comments: Multiple invitations to choose options and co‑design (e.g., “Which sounds best to you?”, incorporating patient’s timing and cue preferences), demonstrating genuine collaboration.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Explicitly supports patient choice and control
- [x] Avoids pressure or coercive language
- [x] Normalizes any outcome and respects limits
Comments: Repeated statements respecting limits and choice (“I won’t expect more than you can give,” “I’m not promising I’ll keep at it”), preserving autonomy while offering support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Elicits patient reasons for change and resources
- [x] Asks about past successes/strengths to leverage
- [x] Draws out patient’s own solutions (e.g., alarm, timing)
Comments: Effective elicitation of motives and strengths (prompting temp‑job example), using patient‑sourced ideas to shape the plan.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Frames work as a team effort
- [x] Incorporates patient suggestions into plan
- [ ] Dominates decision‑making or sidelines patient input
Comments: Clinician repeatedly framed plans as co‑constructed (“Let’s co‑design a tiny plan…”), accepted and integrated patient’s concrete ideas (alarm time, 10‑minute focus).

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Elicits instances of change talk (desire/commitment)
- [x] Highlights patient’s small commitments (10‑minute trial, alarm)
- [ ] Consistently amplifies and strengthens change language into firm commitments
- [ ] Routinely links change talk to values/confidence scaling
Comments: Several examples of change talk were elicited and noted (e.g., “I’ll try one 10‑minute chunk,” “I’ll set an alarm”), but clinician missed opportunities to amplify and convert these into stronger commitment/confidence language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Avoids arguing with or confronting sustain talk
- [x] Normalizes and redirects pessimistic statements
- [ ] Uses double‑sided reflections strategically to shift sustain talk
- [ ] Avoids repeated literal parroting of sustain language
Comments: Clinician normalized and redirected pessimism well and avoided confrontation, but repeatedly echoed literal sustain phrases (“you can’t get a break”), which may have maintained its salience rather than softened it fully.
