# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician expressed clear unconditional positive regard and validated Erik's feelings repeatedly, using nonjudgmental language that reduced threat and invited engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate empathic reflections captured Erik's mixed emotions and priorities and the clinician used open questions to explore his perspective, helping him soften and offer practical steps.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on diet/alcohol changes within diabetes management and consistently moved toward a concrete, actionable weekly plan (specific nights, steps, readiness rating).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co‑constructed the plan with Erik, invited his expertise, incorporated his preferences (timing, who to tell), and checked back to confirm what felt doable.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choice and framed changes as a small personal experiment, affirmed Erik's ownership of the plan, and avoided pressure or shaming.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Values (protecting work/family) and readiness were effectively elicited and translated into short‑term goals, though the clinician could have drawn more on past successes and deeper personal reasons to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as a true partnership with invitations to choose steps, collaborative problem solving, and no expert‑led prescriptions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited specific change language and intentions (e.g., two alcohol‑free nights, telling his wife) but could have amplified and summarized that change talk more explicitly to consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and contained through reflective listening, yet the clinician relied mainly on neutral reflections rather than strategic double‑sided reflections or reframes that might further reduce sustain talk's pull.
