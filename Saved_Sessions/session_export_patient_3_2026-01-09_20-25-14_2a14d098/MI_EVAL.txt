# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is supportive and nonjudgmental, validating Aisha's motivation and concerns. Could increase explicit unconditional positive regard and use more individualized affirmations before moving to solutions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and attention to affect (e.g., 'motivated but worried,' noting 'feel gross') show good empathic listening. The clinician often shifts quickly to problem-solving rather than exploring emotional nuance more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on starting exercise and habit formation with clear, actionable steps and relapse-proofing. Guidance is consistent and appropriate to the client's readiness to plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative touches (schedule questions, offering choices), but the clinician largely supplies ready-made solutions and directs the plan. More joint brainstorming and eliciting Aisha's ideas would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician preserves choice by offering multiple options and asking permission to plan. Some prescriptive language and clinician-driven plans reduce the extent of client-led decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some ambivalence is reflected and scheduling questions invite commitment, but much of the interaction is strategy-driven rather than drawing out Aisha's own motivations, values, and change talk. More elicitation and amplification of her reasons and confidence would improve evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input yet primarily acts as the expert, providing scripts and protocols rather than co-creating them. Strengthening partnership would involve asking more exploratory questions and building plans from Aisha's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some preparatory change talk and helps plan concrete steps, but relies heavily on solution delivery. There were missed opportunities to reflect and amplify Aisha's own statements of desire, ability, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with barriers, normalizes misses, and offers gentle alternatives and contingency plans. Could further soften sustain talk with more double-sided reflections that name the pull of staying the same then pivot to reasons for change.
