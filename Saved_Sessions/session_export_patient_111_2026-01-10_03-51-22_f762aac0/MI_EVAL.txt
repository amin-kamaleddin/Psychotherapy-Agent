# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, explicitly respecting Richard’s choices and privacy. There was no blaming, labeling, or pressure throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Richard’s frustration, routine, and relational concern, helping him feel heard. Empathy could be deeper by amplifying the emotional nuance (e.g., fear about losing his wife).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking and moved from ambivalence to a concrete behavioral experiment with a follow‑up check‑in. Questions and planning consistently returned to readiness, barriers, and a specific action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co‑constructed with repeated solicitation of preferences, tailored suggestions, and joint troubleshooting of barriers. The clinician avoided an expert stance and used shared problem‑solving throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was emphasized repeatedly and the clinician honored boundaries (private text, one‑off plan), avoiding coercive language. The clinician reframed options as voluntary and accepted Richard’s decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician used readiness rulers and values questions that elicited some change‑relevant material (concern about losing his wife). However, intrinsic motivations were not sufficiently deepened or explored for personal meaning and discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative with shared decision‑making, asking permission before offering suggestions and integrating Richard’s requests into the plan. Power was clearly shared and the clinician deferred to Richard’s preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change talk and reinforced small steps, but did not consistently amplify or deepen emerging statements like “I don't want to lose her.” There were missed opportunities to ask follow‑up evocative questions to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively rolled with resistance and avoided arguing, using reflections and normalization to prevent sustaining language from escalating. More strategic double‑sided reflections could have been used to juxtapose sustain and change talk and further soften resistance.
