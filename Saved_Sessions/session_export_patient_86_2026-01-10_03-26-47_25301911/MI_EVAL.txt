# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard, validates Marco's perspective, and honors his boundaries without judgment. Language normalizing the experiment and respecting autonomy reinforces a high level of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both content and feeling, linking relief from smoking with health concerns and long habit. Frequent paraphrasing and summarizing demonstrate strong perspective-taking throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and a concrete short-term experiment (skip after-dinner smoke, gum on the table). Clinician repeatedly returns to actionable steps and measurable success criteria.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is clearly collaborative, with the clinician inviting Marco's ideas and co-constructing the plan. Repeated checks and joint problem-solving show genuine partnership rather than directive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently emphasizes choice and frames change as an experiment rather than a promise, supporting Marco's control over decisions. Respecting his 'don't start preaching' stance and offering optional steps bolsters autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Marco's own motivations and preferences and draws out change-related reasons (pride, control). Evocation could be deepened by probing past successes, using rulers, or exploring core values to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions Marco as the expert and consistently asks for his input when designing the experiment. Power-sharing and soliciting preferences for strategies demonstrate strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits and reinforces change talk and helps define small concrete steps (gum, delay, walk). Opportunities remain to amplify and summarize change language more strategically to progress toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully rolls with resistance, validates sustain talk, and shifts to manageable experiments rather than arguing. Use of more explicit double-sided reflections or summaries acknowledging both sides could further reduce sustain talk.
