# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with normalizing reflections and validation; could offer more explicit unconditional positive regard and deeper normalization of adolescent experimentation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that links concerns to goals; could slow down to explore emotional intensity and deeper feelings more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on adolescent alcohol use and consistently ties interventions to soccer and scholarship goals while progressing logically through skills and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models an equal partnership by inviting co-design, offering to role-play, and providing supportive feedback; repeatedly solicits the patient's preferences and experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by asking permission, offering multiple options, and leaving choices to the teen, preserving ownership and avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and links them to values through questions and role-plays; could probe more for importance/confidence and evoke stronger, specific commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong power-sharing through frequent permission-seeking, offering options, and following the patient's lead in practice selection and session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk well using values-based questions and selective reinforcement; could more systematically highlight and reflect explicit change statements and use scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing minimizations and redirects to goals and concrete planning; could use more double-sided reflections and structured pros/cons exploration to resolve ambivalence.
