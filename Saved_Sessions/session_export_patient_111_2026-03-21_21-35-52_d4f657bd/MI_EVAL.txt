# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validating Richard's feelings and avoiding moralizing language. The clinician accepted the patient's stance and worked from it rather than confronting or blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate empathic reflections captured both content and feeling (anger, ritual significance, relational concern) and matched the patient's tone. This listening helped build rapport and reduced defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking/cutting back and repeatedly steered conversation toward concrete, measurable steps and a time-limited follow‑up. The clinician avoided drifting and translated discussion into an operational plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Structured the interaction as an equal partnership, soliciting Richard's experience and preferences and tailoring options to his routine. The clinician co‑created reminders and a spouse script, demonstrating genuine collaborative problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, asked permission before offering options, and respected Richard's decisions (e.g., declining role‑play). Suggestions were framed as optional, reinforcing patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used open questions, scaling, and elicited prior attempts and relational motivations, successfully drawing some client-generated reasons to change. Improvement opportunity: probe more for intrinsic motives and deepen discrepancy between values and behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the work as a shared effort, offering options rather than directives and inviting Richard to choose strategies. Power was shared in planning and setting the follow‑up preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk via open questions, scaling, and inviting a small experiment (30‑minute delay + gum). Could more actively amplify and reinforce Richard's change statements to elicit stronger desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, using neutral and double‑sided language and focusing on harm‑reduction strategies. Could strengthen impact with more explicit double‑sided reflections and reframing to foreground change reasons.
