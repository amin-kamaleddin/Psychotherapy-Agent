# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance with validations and normalization, avoiding labels or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and brief reflections that validate worries and peer pressure, but could use more complex reflections to mirror ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on early alcohol use and related risks, returning repeatedly to practical harm-reduction and concrete steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with invitations for input and role-play, though occasionally shifts toward expert-driven advising by offering many ready-made scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering multiple options, asking permission to practice, and framing suggestions as the client's decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of behaviors and doable steps, but relies heavily on providing information and scripts instead of drawing out the client's values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters client input and shared decision-making through options and practice, but sometimes supplies solutions rather than co-constructing them from the client's language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-related responses with open-ish questions, yet spends more time offering strategies than amplifying and exploring the client's own reasons and intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to harm-reduction strategies and alternatives, though could use more double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence.
