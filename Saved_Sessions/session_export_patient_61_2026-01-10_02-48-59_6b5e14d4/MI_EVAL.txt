# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and normalized Jonathan's experience, using reassuring, nonjudgmental language that reduced shame. Blaming or pressuring language was avoided throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and validation lowered the interactional tone and showed good empathic listening. The clinician, however, often moved quickly to options and missed deeper exploration of the emotional texture of Jonathan's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on diabetes self-management with behaviorally specific options and a concrete follow-up plan. The session remained goal-oriented while staying client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed the plan by offering menus, soliciting preferences, and negotiating the tiny experiment and reminder. Consent for additional supports was sought and decisions were made together rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and lack of pressure were emphasized repeatedly, honoring the client's ambivalence and framing the step as optional and experimental. Coercive language was avoided and client ownership was reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Small commitments were elicited via offered options and an experiment approach, but the clinician did not draw out Jonathan's own reasons, values, or motivations for change. Most evocation was external rather than eliciting internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician conveyed a collaborative stance by offering choices and checking preferences, respecting the client's input. At times procedural instructions were offered without first eliciting Jonathan's own implementation ideas, slightly reducing the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The menu of very small options produced minimal commitment language (choice of apple, agreement to a reminder), but the clinician did not follow up to elicit or amplify client-generated reasons, importance, or confidence. Change talk was evoked occasionally but not developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician reframed resistance nonconfrontationally, validated burden, and kept offers low-pressure, which reduced sustain talk. More strategic double-sided reflections or explicit summaries of discrepancy could have strengthened ambivalence resolution.
