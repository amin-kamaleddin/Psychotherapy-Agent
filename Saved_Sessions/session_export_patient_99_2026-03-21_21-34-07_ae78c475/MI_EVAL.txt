# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and offering options; conveys unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries, but reflections were mostly factual; could deepen emotional empathy by exploring subtle feelings and underlying values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use tied to ankle recovery and functioning, consistently guiding toward specific, achievable behavior changes and implementation plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as an equal partnership with frequent invitation for patient input, shared decision-making, and roleplay practice; clinician consistently positioned patient as co-author of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission to help, and affirming chosen approaches, thereby reinforcing the patient's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the patient using rulers and linking change to a personally meaningful outcome (ankle healing), building on patient statements to elicit concrete steps and increased confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as collaborative, soliciting preferences and co-creating supports (texts, alarms, code words) which demonstrated genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used importance/confidence rulers and planning prompts that generated change talk and increased confidence, but could more often reflect and amplify desire, ability, and reasons to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and respectfully redirected sustain talk toward low-threat strategies, though could increase use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while gently shifting momentum to change.
