# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard—normalizing, validating, and avoiding blame throughout the session. The clinician's warm, nonjudgmental language supported rapport and reduced shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with reflective listening and mirrored affect (e.g., acknowledging doubt and fatigue). Empathic statements and normalizing comments conveyed strong understanding of the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing brief physical activity with every intervention tied to that goal. Moves the client toward a specific, measurable action (one test this week).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with options and co-creation of the plan (alarm phrasing, backup choices). Collaboration is tempered by frequent clinician-driven strategies and stepwise instructions that at times position the clinician as primary planner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports client choice and avoids coercion by offering options and framing the plan as a test the client can accept or decline. Respects the client's reluctance to involve others and affirms trying without perfection.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and asks the client to notice outcomes, but relies mostly on offering strategies rather than exploring intrinsic motivation, values, or confidence. Missed opportunities for scaling questions and deeper elicitation of personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fosters partnership by inviting choices and shared follow-up, but the clinician supplies many concrete recommendations that tilt the balance toward expert direction. Overall collaborative but somewhat directive in implementation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk by requesting a specific doable step and affirming choices, and asks the client to notice outcomes. There were missed opportunities to further amplify the client's own language about why change matters and to explore importance/confidence more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reframes sustain talk without arguing, redirecting to practical small experiments and normalizing barriers. Relies more on neutral reframing and suggestions than on strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or selective summaries) that could further reduce sustain talk.
