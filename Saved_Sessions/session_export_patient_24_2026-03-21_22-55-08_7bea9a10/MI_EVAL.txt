# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient’s concerns with affirmations and validation; could occasionally name strengths more explicitly (e.g., perseverance in training).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that builds rapport, but reflections sometimes repeat surface content and could probe deeper emotional conflict (e.g., identity vs. evening rituals).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use in relation to marathon training and moves steadily from problem identification to concrete, measurable steps. 

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively invites the patient’s input, co-constructs the plan, and maintains equal-footing decision-making throughout the session. 

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options and framing the plan as an experiment the patient can accept, modify, or restart, which reinforces ownership. 

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s own reasons for change by linking to meaningful goals (marathon, sleep) and using scaling questions to draw out supports and motivation. 

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative, resists an expert-driven stance, and invites the patient to choose what feels most doable, fostering client ownership. 

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits and amplifies change talk, turning tentative statements into a concrete experiment with implementation intentions and supports. 

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reframes sustain talk (e.g., valuing downtime, fear of slipping) and normalizes tentativeness, but could more often use double-sided reflections or direct elicitation of reasons the status quo is less desirable.
