# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician communicates warm, nonjudgmental support throughout, validating feelings and honoring the patient's priorities. Labels and criticism are avoided while the patient's concerns are normalized and respected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections capture both content and affect, helping the patient feel heard and understood. The clinician consistently names feelings and links them to values like being there for the child.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session keeps a clear, appropriate focus on getting a medical evaluation and repeatedly returns to actionable steps. Conversation progresses from exploration to concrete planning (scripts, held appointment, logistics).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is clearly collaborative, with co‑construction of scripts, role‑play, and joint decision‑making. The clinician invites the patient to choose options and shapes the plan with her input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offers options and honors the patient's pacing and preferences, avoiding coercive language. Choice and control are emphasized at each decision point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the patient's own values (e.g., being there for her child) and built on through scaling and concrete steps. The clinician elicits and amplifies internal reasons for change, increasing readiness through rehearsal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treats the patient as a co‑expert, asking which options feel easier and inviting direction on next steps. Power is shared through joint planning and respectful deference to the patient's knowledge of her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits and reinforces change talk using values questions, scaling, and problem‑solving for small, doable steps. Change language is amplified and operationalized into scripts and a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully—fear is normalized and reframed into manageable steps without arguing. The clinician could strengthen practice by using more explicit double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries of ambivalence.
