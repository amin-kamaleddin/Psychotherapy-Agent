# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, validated ambivalence, and avoided judgment. Honored Richard’s preferences (low‑dose trial, pillbox + alarm) throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with frequent reflections, summaries, and targeted follow-up questions. Acknowledged specific worries and patient priorities, supporting strong empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on medication adherence and lifestyle change, moving logically from values to concrete implementation. Set measurable follow-ups (6‑week labs, 1‑week check‑in) to keep the session goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was a genuine partnership with co‑creation of the plan, offering tools and negotiating next steps together. Clinician invited the patient to shape materials and supports rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before interventions, and repeatedly emphasizing that the decision was Richard’s. No coercive language was used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations by linking treatment to retirement goals and using importance/confidence scaling. Could have probed deeper for personal meaning and elicited stronger self‑generated arguments or a clear commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt collaborative with shared decision‑making, repeated solicitation of preferences, and joint planning (days/times, medication‑support options). Power was consistently shared and the patient was positioned as the expert in his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and supported change talk through scaling questions, value linking, and specific implementation planning, producing explicit change statements. Missed opportunities to amplify those signals further and to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk with neutral normalization and addressed barriers via low‑dose trials and lab monitoring. Sustain talk persisted and the clinician could have used more double‑sided or selectively emphasis reflections to de‑emphasize sustain talk and increase discrepancy.
