# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a warm, nonjudgmental stance with frequent validations and normalization of struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures behavior and affect, but could deepen exploration of emotional nuance (stress, fear of slipping, identity).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained tightly focused on smoking reduction with clear, specific goals and actionable implementation steps that translate motivation into behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—plans and wording were co-created, the clinician rehearsed with the patient, and patient preferences guided decisions throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission before advice, and affirming the patient's choices while avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's own motivations and solutions using scaling, choice of strategies, and prompting the patient to craft reminder wording and scripts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the patient as an equal expert—inviting input, permission, and shared decision-making in every step of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete commitments and planning, though could more deliberately amplify and summarize motivational statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately redirected sustain talk toward solutions and planning, but would benefit from more strategic use of double-sided reflections and exploration of smoking's function to resolve ambivalence.
