# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized David's harm‑reduction approach, validating his priorities without criticism. No shaming or minimizing comments and the plan respected his desire to keep evenings free.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective‑taking and reflective listening, capturing ambivalence and specific concerns like skin irritation and vivid dreams. Summaries and empathic phrases showed strong attunement to both practical and emotional aspects.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking/harm reduction and moved logically from assessment to a concrete, time‑limited trial. The session stayed on topic with practical goals and actionable steps (patch timing, reminders, tracking).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with co‑constructed decisions about timing, alarm wording, partner backup, and tracking method. Clinician offered expertise while deferring to David's preferences and choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported David's choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and phrasing suggestions tentatively. Patient ownership was reinforced through choices about reminders and plan details.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation through confidence ratings and built on small commitments (willing to try a week), but did not consistently probe deeper values, long‑term goals, or personal reasons for change. More targeted questions could have strengthened internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine power‑sharing and co‑creation, repeatedly inviting David's input on NRT, reminders, and backup plans. Decisions were tailored and jointly agreed rather than imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (confidence scaling, doable options) and highlighted willingness to try, but missed opportunities to probe personal benefits and deepen desire/reasons/need language. More prompts for internal reasons would strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and accommodated enjoyment of smoking with a harm‑reduction plan, reducing resistance. Could increase use of double‑sided and strategic reflections to more actively soften sustain talk and shift emphasis toward change.
