# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard through nonjudgmental language and affirmations. Adjusted wording and tone to match the patient's preferences, reinforcing acceptance rather than blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and acknowledged the patient's emotions and values. Reflections were generally brief and pragmatic, with missed opportunities to deepen emotional nuance and explore defensiveness or cultural context.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and actionable goals, moving the session toward concrete steps and a printable plan. Regularly redirected content back to relevant goals and next actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—co‑produced the handout line by line and immediately incorporated requested wording changes. Framed the interaction as joint problem‑solving and deferred to the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized patient choice, offered multiple options, and avoided pressuring language. Respected the patient's decisions and discomfort with role‑play while providing alternatives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's values and preferred actions and used them to guide the plan with open questions. Missed opportunities to more deeply draw out intrinsic motivation and commitment using scaling or deeper exploratory prompts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership by inviting input and negotiating decisions about wording and next steps. The patient’s choices directly shaped the final product, showing shared ownership of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk through questions about priorities and by affirming concrete steps and plans. Could have further strengthened commitment and confidence by using scaling questions and prompts like 'what makes that possible?'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argument and confrontation, offering neutral facts and pragmatic supports while accommodating skepticism. Did not consistently use double‑sided reflections or explicit rolling with resistance to acknowledge sustain talk more actively.
