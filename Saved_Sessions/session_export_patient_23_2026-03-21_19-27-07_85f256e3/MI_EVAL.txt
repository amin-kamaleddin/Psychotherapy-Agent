# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used a warm, nonjudgmental tone and explicit supportive language throughout, affirmed effort and avoided blaming to convey unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and checked understanding, but offered limited deeper affective reflections (e.g., did not explicitly explore sadness/loneliness or the function of drinking).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use with a structured 4‑week plan, concrete tactics, and specific follow‑up arrangements.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort by soliciting preferences, checking summaries, and co‑producing simple tools that the patient accepted and customized.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's choice by asking permission, offering options rather than commands, and emphasizing flexible ownership of decisions and tracking methods.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivation and past attempts but moved into clinician-led solutions; more open questions to draw out personal reasons, values, or a patient‑led summary would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited input and co-created parts of the plan, but also presented a fairly detailed clinician-led plan without first eliciting the patient's full preferences or brainstorming together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, validated goals, and built on change‑oriented statements, though missed opportunities to probe importance/confidence or deepen elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided judgment and did not amplify sustain talk, but did not strategically explore or soften it with double‑sided reflections or explicit validation of the pull to drink.
