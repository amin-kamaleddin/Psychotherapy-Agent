# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation (e.g., opening support and normalization), avoiding blame throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening and accurate paraphrase (naming avoidance and past success). Could deepen by reflecting Hana's specific emotions and their intensity using more complex or amplified reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on preparing for and attending the exam and translated ambivalence into concrete, behaviorally specific steps without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and mutual: clinician offered options, adjusted based on Hana's choices, and co‑constructed the plan (text wording, date, calendar entry).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering multiple options, asking permission before suggestions, and repeatedly emphasizing Hana's choices (e.g., provisional booking, selecting text).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited elements of internal motivation (past success, small doable step, commitment to text/book) but often provided solutions rather than eliciting Hana's own language and reasons; more scaling and open evocation would help.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a shared, power‑sharing approach through repeated permission, offering choices, and inviting Hana to shape decisions and wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (highlighting success, asking which step feels doable, securing a tentative commitment) but could use more questions to draw out importance, reasons, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it and shifted to harm‑reduction strategies and if‑then plans; use of double‑sided reflections and strategic summaries would further reduce sustain talk momentum.
