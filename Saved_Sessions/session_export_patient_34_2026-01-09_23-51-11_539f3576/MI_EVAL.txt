# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, with affirmations of strengths and respectful language; no shaming or blaming was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and validates Dylan's perspective with accurate paraphrases, but reflections are somewhat repetitive and the clinician misses deeper emotional exploration of his defensiveness and feelings about unfairness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing conduct risk while preserving social life, and moves the session logically from values to concrete risk-reduction steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally an equal, cooperative problem-solving approach with open questions and invitations to choose, but the clinician sometimes steers toward particular solutions rather than fully eliciting client-generated alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors the client's choice by offering options, framing decisions, and asking which steps feel doable, avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Dylan's motivations, strengths, and concrete change language, but could deepen internal motivation by eliciting discrepancy between values and potential consequences (e.g., what a conduct mark would mean).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative with menu-style options and frequent solicitation of client choices, though the clinician at times moves quickly to a specific plan instead of explicitly co-constructing goals and rationale.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and turns vague ideas into specific steps, producing commitment language, but could further amplify reasons and probe confidence and commitment to strengthen the change pathway.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and avoids arguing, but does not employ more advanced softening strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections, strategic reframes) to pivot ambivalence toward change.
