# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Consistently nonjudgmental tone / unconditional positive regard
- [x] Validated patient perspective repeatedly
- [ ] Used confrontational or moralizing language

**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful tone (“I’m here to listen and support you”; “I respect that you don’t want your parents told”) and validated Marcus across the session.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Used accurate, frequent reflections
- [ ] Probed deeper emotional nuance (e.g., shame, belonging)
- [ ] Overly detached or invalidating

**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking with many reflections (e.g., “drinking feels like normal fun…”). Reflections were often simple/repetitive and missed opportunities for deeper emotion+meaning reflections.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Kept focus on alcohol-related risk and immediate safety
- [ ] Advanced longer-term behavior change plan beyond safety
- [ ] Lost focus or drifted off-target

**Comments:** Session consistently steered to concrete harm‑reduction and safety planning; limited push toward next-step plans for longer‑term change.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Invited input and permission (e.g., “Can I ask…”, “Would you be open…?”)
- [x] Co-created safety steps and honored patient choices
- [ ] Dominated decision-making without patient input

**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly sought Marcus’s input, offered options, and respected refusals (e.g., declined role‑play), resulting in genuinely collaborative planning.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Emphasized patient choice and respected decisions
- [x] Clearly and neutrally stated confidentiality limits
- [ ] Pressured patient to change or quit

**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced throughout (“I won’t lecture,” “I respect that you don’t want your parents told”), and confidentiality limits were communicated in a neutral way.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Made some attempts to draw out patient perspective (asked what worries him; asked which steps he'd commit to)
- [ ] Explored values, goals, or broader reasons for change
- [ ] Systematically evoked change talk

**Comments:** Small evocation attempts (e.g., “what worries you most…?”) but missed systematic exploration of values, aspirations, or longer‑term reasons that could support change.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Functioned as a partner (offered options, sought permission)
- [ ] Maintained equal power balance at all times
- [ ] Acted primarily as the expert without collaboration

**Comments:** Generally collaborative and partnership-oriented (offered wording options, asked which steps Marcus would use). Occasional directive repetitions slightly reduced equal power balance.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
- [x] Noted brief patient change statements (e.g., “I guess I could be smarter about it”)
- [ ] Reflected, amplified, or explored change talk to build motivation
- [ ] Elicited desire/ability/reasons/need/confidence language systematically

**Comments:** Marcus offered brief change talk, but clinician missed opportunities to reflect, amplify, or explore these statements before moving to planning.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Avoided confrontation and did not reinforce sustain talk
- [ ] Used double‑sided reflections to highlight ambivalence
- [x] Redirected toward safety planning rather than reframing sustain talk

**Comments:** Clinician stayed neutral and avoided arguing with sustain talk (“everyone does it”), which is positive, but did not employ double‑sided reflections or reframes to gently tip balance toward change. Redirected primarily to damage‑control safety planning.
