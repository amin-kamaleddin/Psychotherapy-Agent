# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys respect and nonjudgmental support, normalizing ambivalence and using affirming language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and reflective listening that validates feelings, though deeper affective exploration and underlying stressors are somewhat limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol-related risk reduction with concrete, measurable next steps and a specific experiment to try.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as joint problem-solving and checks preferences, but frequently supplies scripts and content instead of eliciting the patient's own phrasing first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors the patient's choices, offers options, and uses permissive language that reinforces the patient's control over decisions and actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits a plan and some buy-in but relies more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient's internal motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and consultative, but at times shifts into an expert-driven mode by providing many specific scripts and plans rather than co-creating them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language and a willingness to experiment, yet often moves quickly to solutions without reflecting and amplifying the patient's own statements about desire, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframes sustain talk and offers alternatives that preserve social benefits, though could better use double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to resolve ambivalence.
