# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating (e.g., “Wanting more tests makes sense,” “I hear how scared and disabled you feel”). At times accommodated the patient’s demand for tests rather than deeper individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective summaries and names affect (fear, disability) repeatedly. Missed opportunities for more complex reflections to surface shame, mistrust, or anxiety and to explore stress‑symptom links.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Maintained focus on the patient’s stated goal (scripts, role‑play, one‑line note) and steered session toward concrete next steps. Would have benefitted from strategic use of this focus to promote broader clinically indicated changes (symptom management, contingency plans).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and co‑constructed the script and note, honoring patient preferences. Could have elicited more of the patient’s prior experience with GPs to better tailor the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice and control (multiple options, asking preferences). Language avoids coercion and reinforces patient agency throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Began with open questions and the patient offered hints of change talk (stress link). After that, clinician moved to providing scripts and coaching instead of further eliciting the patient’s own motivations or goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Demonstrated a team stance (co‑creating wording, offering role‑play) and checked patient preferences. Could better integrate the patient’s experiential knowledge about prior encounters into planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Change talk was minimal (e.g., “it wasn’t so bad during my son’s wedding,” “worse when husband is away”), and the clinician did not capitalize on these to elicit desire, reasons, ability, or commitment for symptom‑reducing changes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (insistence on biomedical testing, “not just in my head”) was validated but not softened with MI strategies (no double‑sided reflections or reframes); clinician’s assistance in crafting stronger demands may have reinforced sustain talk.
