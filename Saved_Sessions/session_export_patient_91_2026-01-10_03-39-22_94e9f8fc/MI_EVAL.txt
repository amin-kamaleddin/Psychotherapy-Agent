# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established a clear nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly normalized Marcus's experience with respectful, warm language, demonstrating consistent unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Marcus's emotions and behaviors and were timely and validating, showing strong perspective-taking and reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on blood glucose monitoring and moved from problem identification to a specific, time-limited plan without drifting from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as a joint effort with co-created plans, options offered contingent on Marcus's choices, and frequent invitations for his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice and control by presenting options, asking which felt doable, and offering help rather than prescribing, respecting Marcus's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Some of Marcus's motivations and preferences were elicited and used to build a plan, but the clinician relied heavily on providing solutions rather than systematically drawing out his internal reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Marcus's input throughout, shared decision-making, and allowed Marcus to shape the plan, demonstrating strong power-sharing and mutual influence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and converted it into a feasible plan, but missed opportunities to more actively amplify and deepen commitment language with follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ambivalence was acknowledged and normalized, but the clinician did not consistently use MI-consistent strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploration of competing concerns) to actively roll with or reframe sustain talk.
