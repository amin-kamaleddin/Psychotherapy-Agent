# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintains respectful, nonjudgmental language (“I’m glad you reached out,” “that’s understandable”) and normalizes frustration. Acceptance is somewhat functional and tied to directing steps; limited explicit affirmations of strengths early in the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses empathic statements and clarifying questions (“I’m sorry you’re dealing with that,” “You’re right to feel frustrated”), demonstrating perspective-taking. Reflections are surface-level and focus more on logistics than underlying emotions (shame, embarrassment).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly goal-directed and pragmatic throughout — clear checklist of steps (timeline, witness list, keep paperwork, backups, role‑play/draft) and repeated closure with a specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites patient input (“Which step feels most helpful?”, “If you want, I can help draft…”), but interaction becomes clinician-led with repeated prescriptive step lists and session closings rather than co-constructed planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently acknowledges Dylan’s choice not to contact student conduct and frames options as voluntary. Occasional directive recommendations and repeated closures slightly reduce the sense of full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little elicitation of Dylan’s internal motivations, values, or broader goals; missed opportunities to ask about consequences, importance, or how this fits with his plans or identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers support and options (“we can draft,” “we’ll work through it”), but clinician frequently drives the agenda and does not fully use Dylan’s contributions to shape deeper exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is limited to logistical actions (organizing paperwork, timeline). Clinician does not amplify or reinforce these statements or elicit desire/ability/reasons/commitment language about behavior change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient’s sustain talk (minimization, denial) is accepted and normalized but not strategically softened — few double-sided reflections or pivots to highlight discrepancy between goals and current stance.
