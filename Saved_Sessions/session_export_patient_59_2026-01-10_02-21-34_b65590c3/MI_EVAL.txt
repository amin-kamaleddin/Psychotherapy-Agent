### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Jamal’s experience, reducing shame and keeping him engaged. Could add a few more individualized affirmations of strengths to reach master-level nuance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured both surface content and underlying concerns. Missed a few opportunities for deeper emotion reflections (e.g., worry about letting his mom down).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate directional focus on legal consequences and preserving sports/school, and advanced to concrete next steps (lawyer call, script). Direction was consistent without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with inclusive language and co-constructed decisions (choosing the lawyer call and drafting the script). The clinician followed Jamal’s lead and positioned themselves as a partner in planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Jamal’s autonomy by offering options, asking which felt doable, and requesting permission to draft/practice language. These behaviors supported his sense of control over decisions and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Jamal’s internal motivations and priorities, eliciting change reasons that aligned with his values and moving to a concrete plan. The clinician resisted giving prescriptive lectures and amplified Jamal’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the session as shared problem-solving and invited input, but occasionally offered solutions rather than eliciting Jamal’s own options first. Asking Jamal to generate alternatives or explicitly naming him as the expert would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, asked for specific small steps, and moved quickly to planning (stop carrying weed, short program, lawyer call). Could further strengthen commitment with scaling questions or a client summary of the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and redirected toward change, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and more strategic neutralization of minimizations. More explicit contrasts between sustain and change language would help shift momentum.
