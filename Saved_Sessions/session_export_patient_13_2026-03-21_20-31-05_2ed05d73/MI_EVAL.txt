# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opened with an explicit nonjudgmental stance and used warm, respectful language throughout, repeatedly validating the patient's feelings. Offers of help (scripts, role‑play, callbacks) were presented as support rather than pressure, reinforcing unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflections captured the patient's emotions and barriers (fear, cost, childcare) and the clinician matched tone and content to the patient's hesitancy. Empathic responses were sustained across the interaction and helped the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on the target behavior—seeking medical evaluation for the lump—with every question and intervention tied back to that goal. The clinician avoided drifting and consistently guided the interaction toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like joint problem solving: scripts and role‑plays were co‑constructed and offers were adjusted to the patient's needs. The patient's input directly shaped next steps, demonstrating true collaborative work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly supported by asking the patient to choose the focus and offering multiple options, then honoring preferences (e.g., short scripts, brief prompts). Offers of assistance were framed as available supports rather than mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician drew motivation from the patient's own words (asking about worries, barriers, and doable steps) and used scaling to tailor supports. These moves elicited specific change talk and helped convert intention into a concrete choice to call the clinic.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as a collaborator and expert on her life, routinely inviting choice and consent for interventions. Negotiated tasks and contingent offers reinforced a partnership stance throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and reinforced change talk by asking about desire, ability, and small first steps, then focused on the patient's chosen step (calling the clinic). Providing scripts, role‑play, and a callback plan strengthened emerging commitment and moved toward action.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When sustain talk arose, the clinician validated concerns without arguing and reframed barriers into addressable problems (childcare, cost, brief calls). Brainstorming practical options and supports effectively softened sustain talk while respecting the patient's ambivalence.
