# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with affirmations and normalization (e.g., opening support, validating shame and nervousness), conveying unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and validation of ambivalence and shame, but could deepen emotional exploration and use more complex reflections to name underlying values and fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on gambling and debt reduction with logical progression from exploration to concrete planning and troubleshooting (e.g., role‑play bank call).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative and tailored to patient preferences (inviting choices, role‑play, drafting texts), though at times the clinician offered many ready-made solutions instead of eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice and control by asking permission, offering multiple options, asking which steps felt doable, and using a confidence-rating to adjust the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited motivations and intentions (what feels doable, triggers, commitments) but could further prompt client-generated reasons for change and amplify the patient's change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision‑making and invited input, yet occasionally moved into clinician-led recommendations rather than maximizing patient-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced preparatory and commitment language (e.g., deleting apps, auto-transfer), but could more often reflect and amplify intrinsic motivations like the patient's travel goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and avoided reinforcing sustain talk, but used few targeted strategies (e.g., double‑sided or strategic reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk.
