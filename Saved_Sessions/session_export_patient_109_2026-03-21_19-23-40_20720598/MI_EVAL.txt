# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating Rafael's feelings and normalizing ambivalence. Avoided blaming language and offered options without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking with several reflective statements and normalizations. Reflections were often brief/functional and missed deeper mirroring of discouragement and affect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on returning to exercise with concrete scheduling, reminders, and an if‑then plan. Resisted drifting and guided the session toward an actionable two‑week experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited Rafael's input, adjusted times to his preference, and framed work as joint problem‑solving. However, the clinician frequently offered ready‑made solutions, keeping some decision‑making clinician‑led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Rafael's choices by offering multiple options and phrasing plans as experiments. Emphasized his control and avoided coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some preferences and small commitments but relied more on providing strategies than drawing out Rafael's personal motivations or past successes. Limited use of questions to evoke desire, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by asking permission and incorporating Rafael's requests, such as changing times. Still tended to lead solution generation rather than first eliciting Rafael's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change‑relevant language (e.g., 'I'll try,' agreement to reminders) but did not sufficiently amplify or shape it. The clinician moved to prescriptive plans rather than exploring reasons, hopes, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk neutrally and strategically by normalizing barriers and offering manageable backups, avoiding arguments. Could improve by using more double‑sided reflections to explicitly surface ambivalence before pivoting.
