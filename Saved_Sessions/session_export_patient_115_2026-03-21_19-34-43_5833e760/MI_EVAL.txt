# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations and validation (safety check, praising inhaler use, acknowledging financial limits). The clinician shows clear unconditional positive regard and avoids blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and accurate reflections (acknowledging fear, night symptoms, constraints). There were missed opportunities for deeper emotional exploration and more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on asthma‑related behaviors with concrete, time‑bound tasks and safety information. At times the interaction leans toward directive clinical advice rather than consistently eliciting the patient's ideas or summarizing to consolidate momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is genuinely cooperative and co‑constructed, repeatedly inviting input and negotiating next steps. The clinician adjusts recommendations to the patient's readiness and priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports patient choice by asking permission, offering options, and tailoring recommendations to budget and preferences. The clinician consistently communicates that the patient is the decision‑maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation through readiness/confidence questions and asking the patient to choose concrete steps. However, clinician‑provided solutions are frequent; more open‑ended questions could draw out internal motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly fosters partnership by inviting preferences, co‑creating the plan, and offering to carry out patient‑requested tasks. The clinician positions the patient as the expert on his life circumstances.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk with achievable steps and commitments (specific days/times, confidence rating). Could further amplify change language to strengthen ability and commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and honors partial steps, which reduces resistance and sustain talk. The clinician could more often use double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence and further soften sustain talk.
