# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and validated Mateo’s experience, using warm, strength-focused language throughout. This nonjudgmental acceptance supported rapport and disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking helped Mateo elaborate and feel understood, with accurate summaries of his situation. To reach a 5 the clinician could have probed deeper into underlying emotions like shame, fear, or hope at key moments.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on recreating daily routine and moved consistently toward actionable steps (temp agency, two 7:00 AM shifts). There was little drift and the plan was tightened into concrete, measurable actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was framed as joint problem-solving with Mateo’s input solicited at every step, and decisions were co-constructed (days, times, wording). The clinician offered concrete collaborative supports (script, role-play, calls) rather than imposing a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly reinforced Mateo’s choice and control by offering options and avoiding coercive language, while validating his uncertainty. This supported ownership of the decision and plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician effectively drew out Mateo’s intrinsic motivations and values and used past successes to build a realistic pathway. Change reasons and specific steps were elicited from Mateo rather than supplied by the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician fostered genuine power-sharing, inviting Mateo to lead planning and offering to help in collaborative ways (drafting scripts, role-playing). Language and offers demonstrated respect for Mateo as the expert on what would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently evoked and amplified change talk and guided Mateo to concrete, client-generated steps (twice next week; 7:00 AM shifts). The clinician reinforced commitment by specifying supports, days, and times.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without confrontation and reframed toward planning, but the clinician missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections or more explicitly diminish the pull of doubt. More strategic reflections or contrasts could have further softened sustain talk and elicited stronger commitment language.
