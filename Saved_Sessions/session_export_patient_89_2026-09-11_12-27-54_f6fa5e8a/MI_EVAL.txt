# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing the patient's tensions and affirming practical ideas; warm supportive tone throughout justifies the top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several accurate reflections and perspective-taking captured practical constraints and values, but could deepen with more complex emotional reflections (e.g., frustration about yo-yo dieting) to reach a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on increasing activity with concrete planning (days, cues, fallback plans) and a clear check-in, maintaining momentum toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as an equal partnership—clinician solicited and integrated the patient's ideas and co-created the plan and follow-up preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and agency by offering options, asking preferences, and checking what felt doable, which avoided pressure and fostered ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations and solutions with evocative questions and amplification of change talk, culminating in a concrete, patient-driven plan and check-in.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and offered choices, but could more explicitly foreground the patient's expertise and strengths to further strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly elicited and amplified change language, converting general desire into specific commitments and implementation cues (alarms, shoes-by-the-door, check-ins).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing and pivoted to doable options, though more strategic use of double-sided reflections or selectively emphasizing change reasons could better defuse sustain language.
