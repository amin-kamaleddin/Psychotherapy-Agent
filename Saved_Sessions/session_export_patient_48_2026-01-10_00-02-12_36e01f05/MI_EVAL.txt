# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and warmth (normalizing, explicit praise, no judgment). The clinician’s kind, supportive language maintained a steady accepting stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured emotional tone and validated ambivalence (worry, hesitation), demonstrating good perspective-taking. Some reflections were repetitive and could be deepened with more complex, specific reflections of feelings and self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on starting a brief exercise break and tied interventions to that behavioral goal. Interventions repeatedly returned to concrete logistics and a measurable experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session generally felt like team problem-solving with co-constructed practical steps and check-ins. However, the clinician frequently offered ready-made solutions rather than eliciting the client’s ideas first, slightly reducing parity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and treated the plan as an experiment, minimizing pressure and inviting the client to decide. Phrasing consistently reinforced the client’s agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited client-generated commitment and concrete planning (choice of walk, time, cues, fallback). Missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring values, asking confidence rulers, or linking the experiment to personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Promoted shared decision-making by offering options and inviting check-ins, supporting a collaborative tone. Offering many specific suggestions at times shifted control toward clinician expertise rather than foregrounding the client’s knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited concrete change talk and a firm plan (20‑min walk, time, cues, fallback). Could have amplified and summarized reasons for change, asked about benefits or confidence, and elicited stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing ambivalence, reframing as an experiment, and offering low-threat fallbacks. Could increase use of double-sided reflections and questions that elicit client counterarguments to more strategically shift sustain talk.
