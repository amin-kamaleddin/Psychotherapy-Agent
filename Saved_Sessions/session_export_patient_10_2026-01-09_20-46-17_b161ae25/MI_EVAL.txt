# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respect and nonjudgmental regard, normalized struggles, and used appreciative language without blaming the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate, timely empathic reflections and acknowledged practical and emotional aspects, but moved relatively quickly into planning without deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on attending the interview with concrete behavioral steps and specific scripts tied to the target goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited preferences, solicited choices, and incorporated the client’s priorities throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering options, seeking permission before materials, and using language that preserved the client's choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked practical motivation and concrete commitment but missed opportunities to elicit deeper internal motives, values, or personal meaning for the job.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally positioned the work as shared and asked permission, but presenting a detailed, prescriptive 6-step plan reduced opportunities for co-creation and client-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk and commitment to concrete steps, though could have deepened change talk by asking for reasons, consequences, or imagery about success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and neutrally redirected to contingency planning, but did not use double-sided reflections or explicit normalization to more actively resolve ambivalence.
