# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirms Jason's practical choices, validating his priorities. Could add more explicit unconditional positive regard and individualized strength-based affirmations to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician uses accurate reflective listening and captures Jason's perspective well throughout the session. Missed opportunities to probe or reflect deeper emotions (e.g., embarrassment, fear, peer pressure) that would deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing risk of another write-up and moves from options to a concrete action plan. Questions and reflections repeatedly return to specific risk-reduction steps, supporting behavioral specificity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is a cooperative problem-solving conversation with Jason shaping the plan and clinician offering practical help (e.g., drafting the message). Repeatedly solicits Jason's preferences and builds solutions together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supports Jason's sense of choice and agency, affirming his right to keep social life while choosing risk-reduction steps. Offers options and asks which step Jason wants to lock in, reinforcing autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicits Jason's motivations to protect his semester and turns those into actionable steps. Could deepen evocation by exploring broader values, long-term goals, and scaling importance/confidence to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance: clinician invites input, treats Jason as the expert, and co-constructs the plan (signals, timing, backups). Power is shared and decisions are made jointly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and reinforces multiple change statements and helps co-create concrete protective actions. To improve, the clinician could more consistently amplify change talk, probe importance/confidence, and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoids arguing and rolls with minimizations but does not strategically soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or selectively highlighting discrepancy. More active use of techniques to elicit costs of continued risk and contrast with goals would redirect sustain talk toward change.
