# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized struggle, using validation and praise without blame. Warm, nonjudgmental language was present throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, capturing both content and affect. Frequent empathic summaries and check-ins showed high understanding of the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on resuming short workouts with concrete planning steps. Interventions and questions were tightly linked to measurable actions and progression toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a team effort with frequent invitations for the client's input on choices and plan details. Clinician co-constructed solutions while keeping the client's priorities central.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician honored the client's choice repeatedly, offering options and phrasing suggestions as invitations rather than directives. This reinforced client ownership of the plan and reduced pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited intrinsic motivations and specific plans, using open questions and confidence scaling. Could further deepen internal motivation with more targeted evocative questions and strategic reflections to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly collaborative with frequent solicitation of preferences, though occasional structured suggestions nudged toward advising. Increasing client-generated solutions before offering clinician options would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced change talk and used scaling questions to invite more commitment. Could do more to amplify expressions of desire/reason and to deliberately elicit commitment language (e.g., have client summarize plan).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist normalized hesitancy and used supportive redirection without debating sustain talk, offering low-pressure accountability alternatives. Using more double-sided reflections and emphasizing past successes could further weaken sustain talk.
