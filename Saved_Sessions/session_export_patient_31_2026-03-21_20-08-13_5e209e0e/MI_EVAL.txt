# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental tone from the opening line through affirmations; clinician avoids labeling or criticism and validates the patient's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate, validating reflections of coping and barriers; could deepen by using more complex emotional reflections and probing underlying meaning of smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking reduction and moves toward a concrete trial plan, but provides many options and technical details that could overwhelm an ambivalent patient — a more staged, evocation-first approach would sharpen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative: clinician solicits patient input at each step, tailors recommendations to preferences, and co-constructs the plan and troubleshooting strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly emphasizes choice and ownership throughout the encounter, repeatedly offering options, asking permission, and affirming the patient's decision to test a regimen.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites the patient's own motivations and preferences and elicits concrete reasons to try a change, but could more actively draw out personal values, importance/confidence ratings, and past successes to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as an equal partner by asking permission, offering choices, and jointly selecting micro-strategies and follow-up plans that reflect the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and operationalizes a trial plan, yet could better amplify spontaneous change language and elicit stronger commitment/confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward harm-reduction strategies in a neutral way, but would benefit from more double-sided or strategic reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence and tip toward change.
