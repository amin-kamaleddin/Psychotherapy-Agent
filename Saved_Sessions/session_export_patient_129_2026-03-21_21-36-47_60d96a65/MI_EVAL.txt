# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and validated Tyler's feelings, avoiding blame or shaming. Maintain this warmth while gently exploring consequences so acceptance doesn't become permissive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections that captured both content and affect, helping Tyler feel understood. Could occasionally use deeper reflections to name underlying emotions (e.g., isolation, desire for autonomy).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on Tyler's chosen target (reducing mom conflict and safety strategies). If substance use is a clinical priority, plan to gently shift focus in later sessions to explore discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—avoided an expert-only stance and involved Tyler in choices (scripts, role-plays, planning). Regularly solicited preferences and adjusted the session accordingly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Tyler's right to decide and offered options with permission, reducing reactance. Framed interventions as tools Tyler could use if he chose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited motivation related to wanting 'less freaking out' from his mom but did not sufficiently draw out intrinsic reasons to change smoking. Would benefit from asking about values, longer-term goals, and using scaling to evoke change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly framed the session as a joint process and consistently invited Tyler to set the agenda. Power-sharing and choice fostered client ownership of plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-relevant language was elicited (worries about consequences, desired outcome) and concrete plans were developed, but change talk about the substance use itself was not systematically amplified. Use follow-up questions, reflections, and importance/confidence scaling to deepen and sustain change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to client-prioritized goals and practical strategies, which reduced arguments for the status quo. Could add double-sided reflections to more actively highlight ambivalence.
