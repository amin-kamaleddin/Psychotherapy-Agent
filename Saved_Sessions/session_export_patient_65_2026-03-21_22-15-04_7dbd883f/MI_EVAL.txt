# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized the client's situation throughout, honoring limits and avoiding blame. Language and attitude conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of logistical concerns, with frequent checks for understanding. Limited exploration of deeper emotions or affective reflections reduced the score slightly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on the target behavior (completing community service) with concrete sub-goals and actionable next steps. Questions and offers consistently aimed at clarifying logistics and moving planning forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative, with joint problem-solving, frequent checks for edits, and co-created plans. Clinician repeatedly invited the client's input and deferred action until permission was given.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission before drafting or sending messages, and honoring the client's decision to review drafts and say 'go.' This reinforced the client's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation focused largely on logistics and did not elicit the client's internal motivations, values, or personal reasons for change. Few open, evocative questions were used to draw out intrinsic motivation or strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned the client as decision-maker through choice offers and confirmation of summaries, fostering power-sharing and mutual planning. Repeated invitations to edit drafts reinforced equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicited concrete, action-oriented commitments related to logistics but did not systematically deepen intrinsic reasons for change. Missed opportunities to amplify desire, reasons, or confidence language limited stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and pragmatically redirected toward planning, accepting the client's guardedness without confrontation. More advanced MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) could further tip language toward change.
