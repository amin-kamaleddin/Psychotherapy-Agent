# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validated feelings and offered low-threat options (texts, scripts), demonstrating clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries of ambivalence and consequences; could deepen emotion-focused reflections to explore underlying feelings and anxiety about change.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Stayed tightly focused on reducing alcohol, assessed safety and history, and translated the discussion into a clear, actionable plan with referrals and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Team-oriented and responsive—invited input, rehearsed calls, and supplied resources—but sometimes adopted an expert role by delivering detailed plans without first eliciting priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and offered a menu of strategies, avoided prescribing the guideline and supported patient-set, realistic goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of past attempts and goal selection, but interaction leaned toward giving information rather than systematically drawing out intrinsic motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed a collaborative stance and co-created practical tools, though partnership could be stronger by inviting the patient to generate options before offering prescriptive suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk via strategy suggestions and past attempts, but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify the patient's own reasons, abilities, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and redirected to change with practical alternatives, but used few MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or client-led exploration of pros/cons).
