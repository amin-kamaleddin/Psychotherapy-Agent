# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard—normalizing slips, affirming efforts, and avoiding shame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic reflections and validation (e.g., normalizing FOMO) were present, but the clinician sometimes moved quickly to solutions instead of exploring deeper emotional nuances.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on the target behavior with measurable, time‑bound experiments (11pm cutoff, check‑ins, 1–2 week trial) and planned follow‑ups.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative—plans were co‑created, adjusted to the patient's concerns, and inclusive language promoted shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, normalizing choice, and accepting the patient's caveats without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (choice of change, trial agreement), but much of the session relied on clinician suggestions rather than drawing out the patient's intrinsic reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and inviting of patient input with joint planning, though at times the clinician offered many prescriptive options instead of eliciting more patient‑generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced some change talk (manageable first step, praise) but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen commitment and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and ambivalence normalized, but the clinician tended to pivot quickly to technical fixes rather than using double‑sided reflections or reframing to soften sustain talk.
