### Acceptance

**Comments:** Maintained respectful, nonjudgmental tone and frequent reassurance (“we’ll review it without judgment,” safety reminders). Minor lapse: occasional evaluative praise (“I’m proud you’re willing to try”) that risked sounding patronizing. Suggest replacing blanket praise with behavior-specific affirmations.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Accurate, concrete reflections (e.g., noting splitting from group was risky; naming worry about Mark flaking). Demonstrated perspective-taking. Missed deeper emotional exploration of ambivalence and fear of rejection; could use more emotion-focused reflections and summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related risk and protecting academics. Effectively structured the session (priorities → options → plan → confidence → barriers → rehearsal). Kept momentum toward a specific behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent invitations for Tyler’s input and co-construction of the plan (limit, buddies, lines). Some moves became prescriptive (providing lines, code word) without explicit permission; check before offering tools to preserve collaborative tone.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirmed choice and reversibility of plan; asked for commitment rather than imposing one. Occasional repeated prompts to commit and praise may have felt slightly pressuring—continue to invite modifications to enhance ownership.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited client’s values (grades, avoiding trouble) and past strategies (buddy system); used discrepancy to generate change talk and a concrete plan. Could further amplify intrinsic motives with “why” and “what would it mean…” prompts.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally worked as a partner (“let’s review,” co-created plan, set follow-up). Slightly leader-like when restating the plan and offering ready-made solutions; invite Tyler to direct more of the phrasing and choices.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk; used confidence rulers and saw confidence rise (6→7→8). Reflections amplified commitment language, though could elicit more emotionally resonant reasons and consequences to strengthen commitment.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing for continued drinking and moved to planning, which is appropriate. However, sustain talk (fear of missing out, “not promising I won’t cave”) remained underexplored. Recommend more double-sided reflections and explicit exploration of ambivalence before shifting fully to action.
