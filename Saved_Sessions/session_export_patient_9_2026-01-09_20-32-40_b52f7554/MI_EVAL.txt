# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalization convey positive regard, but acceptance is somewhat generic and often shifts to directive advice instead of deeper, individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician makes basic empathic reflections and recognizes primary emotions, but offers few complex or elaborated reflections and moves quickly to problem-solving, limiting empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on bedtime/discipline throughout the session and links every intervention to concrete actions toward that target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are occasional invitations for input and rehearsal, but the clinician largely sets the plan, scripts, and next steps, so the interaction reads more like expert coaching than co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices and options (permission, experiments, compromises) are offered and support autonomy, but many recommendations are prescriptive with specific wording that can reduce the patient's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit the patient's perspective and offer small experiments exist, yet the clinician mostly supplies language and solutions rather than systematically drawing out the patient's reasons, strengths, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician frequently adopts an expert role, prescribing scripts and step-by-step plans rather than co-creating wording and decisions, which undermines the client's sense of partnership and ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expresses change talk and the clinician sometimes reinforces it, but the clinician often supplies language and misses opportunities to elicit, reflect, and amplify the patient's own motivations and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses curiosity prompts and a low-risk two-week experiment that can soften sustain talk, but double-sided reflections and exploration of the father's perspective are limited, and sustain talk is not consistently redirected.
