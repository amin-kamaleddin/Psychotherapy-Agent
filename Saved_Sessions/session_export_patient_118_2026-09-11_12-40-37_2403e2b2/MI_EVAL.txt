# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly communicated unconditional positive regard (e.g., “Wherever you are with this, I accept it”) and consistently avoided judgment while honoring the patient's refusals to discuss smoking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening and accurate reflections mirrored the patient's language and priorities, capturing both practical concerns and emotional ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the dental treatment plan, but the clinician did not maintain direction toward the smoking‑cessation training target and repeatedly deferred that discussion instead of negotiating a brief revisit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative and framed as joint problem‑solving, with the patient’s preferences driving choices about procedures, timing, and pricing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician offered multiple explicit options, asked which felt best, and explicitly accepted the patient's choice not to discuss quitting, avoiding pressure or insistence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited motivation for dental care but made little effort to probe or elicit the patient's internal motivations, values, or ambivalence regarding smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions were consistently framed as joint (e.g., “Let’s co‑create the plan”), with frequent invitations for input and shared planning at each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicitation focused on dental preferences while the clinician made minimal attempts to draw out or amplify any desire, reasons, or commitment language about quitting smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided confrontation and redirected sustain talk to the patient's agenda, which reduced adversarial tone, though missed some opportunities for strategic double‑sided reflections.
