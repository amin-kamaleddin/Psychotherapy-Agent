# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and warmth, normalizing and validating the patient's exhaustion and embarrassment without judgment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely reflections of both content and feeling, picking up on practical constraints and emotional nuances that helped deepen planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on resuming regular physical activity and progressed logically from assessing motivation to creating a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently co‑created the plan, offered tools and options rather than directives, asked permission, and used rehearsals to support a reciprocal, joint process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's choice and offered multiple options, avoided pressure, and respected her control over the plan and who to involve.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations and solutions (importance rating, reasons, specific steps) but could more often link values to behavior and elicit explicit commitment or confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly solicited the patient's preferences, deferred to her choices, and co‑constructed specifics (days, reminders, wording), signaling genuine power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk using rulers and reflections and praised realistic plans, though could further probe confidence and draw out a declarative commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered low‑pressure alternatives and normalization to reduce barriers, but could employ more double‑sided reflections to help resolve remaining ambivalence.
