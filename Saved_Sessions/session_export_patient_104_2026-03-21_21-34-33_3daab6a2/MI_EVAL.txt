# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone with repeated normalizing statements and affirmations, validating Nadine's distress and efforts throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking tied Nadine's sleep-deprivation and feeding concerns to her emotions, with accurate summaries of facts and feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on feeding, sleep, output, and safety, translating worries into clear, behavior-linked next steps and monitoring thresholds.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created plans and scripts with Nadine, repeatedly soliciting her preferences and tailoring options to her context, demonstrating genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices and permission were offered throughout (multiple options, asking permission to suggest, checking which wording felt best), supporting Nadine's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation occurred (choice of options, confidence rating), but the clinician shifted toward giving solutions and missed opportunities to draw out Nadine's deeper motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Nadine as an expert on her situation, invited her to choose priorities, and actively collaborated on scripts and a checklist, sharing power in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced several change-oriented statements and a confidence rating, and helped form a concrete plan, though opportunities to amplify and deepen change talk were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized and then reframed toward monitoring and action, but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than on double-sided reflections or strategic summaries.
