# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental language (“We can figure this out together and move at your pace”; “That’s heavy and confusing”). Demonstrates unconditional positive regard; could show more individualized warmth and explicit normalization to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of content and some affect (e.g., “that’s heavy and confusing,” reflections about privacy and speed). Missed opportunities to probe deeper into underlying emotional pain, hopelessness, and somatic experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clear behavioral focus on safer urge-management and a concrete first step (ice-in-sock). Did not perform an explicit safety assessment (recent cutting, intent, plan, current risk) — important directional gap. Could better link short-term coping to follow-up/safety planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions work as joint problem-solving (“let’s build on that together”), elicits patient-generated options and plan. Occasionally shifts to more directive prompts; overall good shared authorship of plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly honors patient choice and pace (“Which of these would you be willing to try…”, “move at your pace”). Avoids pressure and supports patient control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits ideas and motivations (patient generated multiple concrete strategies and a plan). Could deepen evocation by eliciting broader values, pros/cons, and using more questions that amplify change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership with collaborative language and co-design of plan. Could further emphasize the patient’s expertise about her experience and negotiate agenda more explicitly to reach a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited tentative change language (agreement to try ice once; imagining differences if it worked) but did not consistently reflect, amplify, or ask confidence/importance scaling to consolidate and strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation but relied mainly on neutral reflections. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or reframes that juxtapose sustain talk with emerging change talk to reduce sustain influence.
