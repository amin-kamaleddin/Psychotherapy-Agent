# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental—normalizes feelings, checks safety, and affirms the patient's efforts without shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid perspective-taking and some reflections (e.g., normalizing nervousness, acknowledging cost/judgment worries) but uses few complex or deeper reflective paraphrases of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on reducing drinking and improving contraception, with every major intervention linked back to those target behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—offers tools and invites patient choice—but often supplies ready-made content and instructions rather than eliciting and building more from the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports choice by offering multiple options, asking which strategies the patient wants, and framing suggestions as voluntary without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits importance and confidence ratings and some reasons for change, but misses opportunities to deepen internal motivation or amplify the patient's own arguments for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys shared decision-making and invites input (e.g., drafting texts together), though at times shifts into an expert role with step-by-step instructions rather than co-constructing options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encourages and reinforces change talk, helps convert intent into concrete goals and elicits strategies, but could further probe and deepen the patient's expressed reasons and visions for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does little to acknowledge or roll with sustain talk—quickly moves to planning and solutions without using double-sided reflections or explicitly exploring the patient's ambivalence.
