# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently nonjudgmental and respectful (“I’m here to listen and support you,” “Thanks for being honest…”). Normalized ambivalence and avoided shaming or moralizing across multiple turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (summarized drinking motives, labeled ambivalence). Reflections stayed at behavioral/strategic level; missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings/identity (e.g., weekends as connection, embarrassment about “saying something dumb”).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use, progressed from assessment to options to concrete plan. Elicited confidence, addressed barriers, and set specific implementation steps (refusal lines, $20, pacing, alarms).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Collaborative stance—asked permission to explore, offered options rather than prescribing, and co-constructed an if–then plan driven by the patient’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided pressuring language, repeatedly left choices to the patient, normalized ambivalence, and provided safety resources while respecting patient agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited patient motivations and barriers (asked about downsides, which options felt doable, used 0–10 confidence scale), drawing change ideas from the patient rather than imposing them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered power-sharing through invitations for input, iterative problem-solving, and framing the work as a joint effort (e.g., rehearsal of lines, shared planning).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited clear change talk and planning (“I’ll give those a try,” tried pacing/$20), but could have more actively amplified and sequenced change talk (e.g., explore why “something to think about” matters, elicit anticipated benefits).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk with neutral feedback and harm-reduction options. Could increase use of double-sided or complex reflections to explicitly soften sustain talk and heighten discrepancy.
