# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing setbacks and praising realistic steps. The clinician validated Adrian’s concerns without labeling or shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking to capture ambivalence, encouraging elaboration. Reflections were pragmatic and summary-level, with limited exploration of deeper emotions like shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Stayed tightly focused on meth use and harm-reduction, moving efficiently from ambivalence to a concrete, specific plan. The clinician elicited concerns, offered tailored options, assessed confidence, rehearsed pushback, and scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and inclusive, with the clinician soliciting Adrian’s input at every decision point. Role-play and rehearsal invited shared work and the patient’s choices shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and respecting readiness without pressuring to quit. The clinician reinforced self-determination when co-creating scripts and backup plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out Adrian’s motivations with open questions and elicited change talk, helping the patient specify an actionable step. Could be strengthened by linking health concerns to personal values and amplifying brief change statements into stronger desire/reason/need language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with permission-seeking and co-construction of decisions, inviting the patient to choose feasible steps. Offering a long list of options in one turn risked feeling slightly expert-driven, though overall power-sharing was maintained.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with evocative questions and translated it into a specific harm-reduction plan. Missed opportunities remain to amplify health-related concerns into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double-sided summarizing to acknowledge both sides while steering toward change. Could more explicitly reflect sustain talk and then pivot to explore the costs of maintaining the behavior.
