# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance and positive regard with repeated validations and avoidance of blame. Examples include opening support statements and validating Dylan's frustration and perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking were used, including accurate reflections of anger and confusion and offering concrete emotion-regulation strategies. The clinician paraphrased Dylan's account and affirmed his feelings throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the referral concerns of underage drinking and student-conduct follow-up, with practical, stepwise guidance. All interventions were tied to concrete problem-solving (timelines, witness statements, legal resources).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was framed as a joint effort with frequent checks on Dylan's preferences and co-construction of materials (scripts, checklist). The clinician offered options and tailored plans rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and respecting Dylan's preferences. Language and offers (e.g., asking which to focus on, framing legal clinic as optional) reinforced ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician supplied solutions and procedural steps more than drawing out Dylan's internal motivations, goals, or reasons for change. There was minimal exploration of how the incident fits with his values or future plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance was evident with invitations for input and choice, but partnership could be strengthened by eliciting Dylan's own ideas before offering solutions. More co-creation of options would increase client ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was little deliberate effort to elicit desire, reasons, ability, or commitment language regarding changing drinking or accountability. The focus remained on pragmatic steps rather than evoking Dylan's motivations to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and validated Dylan's minimizing statements, but missed opportunities to actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or reframing. Redirects tended toward procedural steps rather than exploring discrepancies or values.
