# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing Lukas' feelings and avoiding blame to create a safe space for disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that helped Lukas open up, but the clinician often stayed at practical concerns and did not consistently probe deeper emotional nuance or identity-related tensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on meth-related spending, housing, and audition goals throughout, offering concrete, behaviorally anchored steps tied to Lukas' priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction of plans, texts, and role-plays with invitations for revision, though the clinician often led with pre-formed options instead of eliciting Lukas' own phrasing first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Lukas' sense of choice using permission language, honoring hesitancy and framing actions as voluntary rather than coercive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to draw out motivation and options, but the session leaned toward offering solutions and missed opportunities to systematically elicit underlying values, desires, and change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative with offers to co-create messages and plans, yet the clinician sometimes supplied multiple solutions rather than foregrounding Lukas' ownership of ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully evoked and reinforced concrete change talk and specific commitments, but missed chances to amplify spontaneous insights to strengthen desire, need, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and provided harm-reduction strategies, but did limited active softening of sustain talk (e.g., double-sided reflections or strategic summaries) to help Lukas resolve ambivalence.
