# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and validates the patient's fear and determination, using normalizing reflections. At times an authoritative medical stance (pointing out low cancer risk) risks feeling minimising rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, repeatedly acknowledging affect and frustration. Could deepen empathy by exploring emotional impact and using more complex reflections to amplify feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on investigations, referral decisions, and practical next steps with a concrete plan. Occasional emphasis on defending clinical judgment shifts the tone from guiding shared action to justification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-produces scripts, offers role-play, and repeatedly solicits the patient's preferences, keeping decision-making joint. Rarely reverts to an expert information role, but overall partnership is strong.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms patient choice by offering multiple options, asking which she prefers, and committing to document her reasons if desired. These moves clearly support the patient's agency and decision control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges that motivation resides with the patient and offers choices, but seldom elicits her own reasons, confidence, or commitment for change. Missed opportunities for evocative questions and scaling to draw out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—co-creating scripts, offering rehearsal and tailoring language for the GP. Occasionally slips into an expert stance which slightly reduces equal power in the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-relevant remarks (e.g., stress links, calmer periods) but does not consistently follow up to amplify desire, ability, reasons, or need. Much of the session centers on tests and advocacy rather than sustaining change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledges reasons for tests, but primarily counters with clinical rationale rather than MI softening techniques like double-sided reflections. Offers pragmatic alternatives but misses explicit ambivalence exploration to gently shift balance.
