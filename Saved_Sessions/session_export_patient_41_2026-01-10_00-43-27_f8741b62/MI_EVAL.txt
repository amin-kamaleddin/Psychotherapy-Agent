# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through nonjudgmental reflections, normalizing ambivalence and explicitly refusing to push. Tone and language validate the client's experience throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates accurate, consistent reflective listening and perspective-taking, reflecting both content and feeling that the patient accepts. Summaries check understanding and show strong empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear behavioral focus on a very small, specific post-practice routine and keeps questions tightly linked to implementation. The session moves logically from exploration to an actionable plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is an equal, collaborative partnership—clinician invites preferences and incorporates the client's ideas into the plan. Language and decision points show genuine co-construction rather than prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's sense of choice by offering options, acknowledging limits of promise, and reinforcing that trying small steps is the client's decision. Explicit statements (e.g., 'I'm here to troubleshoot with you, not push') uphold autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes the client's internal motivation and personal reasons for change through evocative questions and drawing out values and past strengths. The clinician helps the client articulate personally meaningful benefits that originate within her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reliably frames the work as collaborative and honors the client's expertise, repeatedly asking which option feels most doable. The plan is co-constructed with the client selecting supports and cues.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and strengthens change talk with targeted questions, reflections, and amplification of change-oriented statements. The clinician guides toward concrete, doable steps and invites the client to voice benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and not argued with, using reflections and some double-sided reflections, but there are missed opportunities for more deliberate softening (reframing or eliciting what’s less true about the fear). More strategic questions and explicit reframes could further reduce the weight of reasons for maintaining avoidance.
