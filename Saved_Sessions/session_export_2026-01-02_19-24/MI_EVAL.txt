# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple clear nonjudgmental, validating statements throughout (e.g., “I’m here to listen and support you,” “Thanks for trusting me — that sounded scary and isolating,” “Feeling embarrassed is normal”). Consistently avoided labeling or shaming; patient appeared supported.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several strong empathic reflections/validations (~4 instances) mirroring embarrassment and isolation. Missed some opportunities to deepen emotional exploration before moving quickly into information-giving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on wound care, testing, naloxone, fentanyl strips, and harm reduction; repeated concise plans and checklists maintained clinical direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician invited patient choice and prioritized his preferences (asked what to focus, offered options, asked him to pick a priority); plan was co-created and accepted by the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Respected patient’s stated stance (“I don't want to quit”), used permission language and offered options rather than pressuring; repeatedly reinforced patient choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation (asked what to focus, prompted prioritization) but clinician relied heavily on information-giving and did not sufficiently explore or draw out the patient’s own motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally partnered stance (asks, offers help, summarizes plan) but intermittently shifted into an expert/instruction role with long informational lists rather than consistently co‑authoring the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient offered brief change talk (“I don't want to get sick again,” “I’ll try to get the wound checked”); clinician missed multiple chances to amplify and elicit more commitment language before moving to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively avoided arguing with sustain talk and reframed conversation toward harm reduction and safety repeatedly; could further use double-sided reflections to surface ambivalence more explicitly.
