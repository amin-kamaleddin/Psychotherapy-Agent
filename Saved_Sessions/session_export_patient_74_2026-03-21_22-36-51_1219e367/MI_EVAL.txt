# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing and validating the patient's choices while praising concrete efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries of ambivalence were used, though reflections stayed mostly at the content/practical level rather than naming deeper emotions or the meaning of being a role model.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, sustained focus on protecting children (no smoking in the car) and moved the patient toward specific actions, supports, and a one-week experiment without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely cooperative: the clinician invited the patient's ideas, validated them, and co-designed the script, supports, and small test rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly supported choice and pace, offered options instead of directives, and repeatedly invited the patient to select what felt doable, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician drew out intrinsic reasons for change (protecting children, role-modeling) and linked strengths to action, but could have probed more to deepen and amplify change talk or elicit importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the patient as the expert on her life, consistently asked for preferences, and co-created the plan, demonstrating effective power-sharing and joint problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was actively elicited and reinforced around protective steps and doable actions, increasing commitment to no-smoking-in-car; more deliberate amplification, summaries, and a closing commitment statement would strengthen this.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and acknowledged sustain talk neutrally while pivoting to protective behaviors, but occasionally mirrored sustain language verbatim and could use more double-sided reflections or strategic reframing.
