# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, offering practical options and respecting preferences (e.g., anonymous/mail‑order). Demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured ambivalence and practical concerns. Missed deeper reflections of emotion/ashame and opportunities to probe the emotional impact of secrecy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on meth-related infection risk and immediate harm-reduction steps, progressing to concrete next actions. The session stayed on-task and moved toward feasible plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-created with frequent invitations for Adrian's input and adjustments for his anonymity preferences. Decisions and wording were shaped by the patient's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly endorsed Adrian's choices and avoided pressure to quit, offering alternative supports when he declined in-person counseling. Consistently reinforced client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change talk and specific harm-reduction steps but did not systematically draw out deeper reasons, values, or use readiness/confidence probes. More follow-up to amplify intrinsic motivation was needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Built genuine partnership through phrases like 'If you’d like…' and by offering to format/send resources per Adrian's preference. Showed clear power-sharing and joint planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and affirmed concrete change language (not sharing gear, getting test kits) and helped form a simple plan. Could have strengthened change talk by eliciting importance/confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward pragmatic harm reduction, which reduced resistance. Could be improved by using double-sided reflections to further acknowledge sustain motives while highlighting reasons for change.
