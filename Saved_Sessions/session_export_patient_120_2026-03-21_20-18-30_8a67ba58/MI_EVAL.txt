# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with permission-seeking and repeated affirmations that convey unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and good empathic listening captured core feelings, though deeper, more complex emotion-focused reflections could further explore intensity and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use during pregnancy with goal-directed techniques (scaling, concrete suggestions, role-play) that moved the session toward actionable planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like a joint effort — clinician invited choices, practiced together, and frequently handed the floor to the patient to co-develop strategies and texts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong support for autonomy through offering choices, asking permission, and affirming the patient's control and decision-making at multiple points.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally evoked the patient's motives and resources (scaling, affirmations) but could more often prompt values, future outcomes, and a client-generated commitment to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative with role-play and choice, yet could more explicitly highlight the patient's expertise and elicit their ideas before offering options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language via choices, scaling, and role-play, but would benefit from more systematic amplification and summarization of client change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and pivoted to solutions, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and deeper empathic exploration of ambivalence before moving to problem-solving.
