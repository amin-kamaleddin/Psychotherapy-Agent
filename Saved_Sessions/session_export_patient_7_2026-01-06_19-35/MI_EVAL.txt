# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes Amina’s experience, explicitly honoring her boundaries and validating her feelings. Acceptance is pervasive and specific, supporting a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections reliably convey understanding of Amina’s situation and build rapport, with accurate content- and feeling-level statements. Some reflections stayed close to paraphrase rather than using deeper, affect-focused language that might name shame, fear, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on a concrete behavioral goal (increasing privacy/calm) and repeatedly steers toward small, achievable steps tied to Amina’s values. Direction is consistent and well-maintained throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly a joint problem-solving effort: the clinician offers options, asks for preferences, and co-creates plans with Amina. Decisions and next steps come from her choices, reflecting strong collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly supports Amina’s choice and control by offering options, avoiding pressure, and checking what feels doable to her. Language and offers reinforce her autonomy and pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Amina’s motivations, reasons, and concrete ideas for change, bringing out personal goals like calm and privacy. To reach top score they could probe deeper into values and elicit stronger commitment and confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions Amina as the expert, invites her preferences, and offers to practice or plan together, demonstrating genuine power-sharing. This consistent partnership supports collaborative planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections effectively elicited desire, reasons, and an initial plan (locking door, script, charged phone) and the clinician amplified these statements. Missed opportunities to elicit stronger commitment or ask about confidence and to summarize change talk limit this to a 4.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges sustain talk without amplifying it and validates expectations of resistance, but mainly uses neutral reflections rather than strategic double-sided reflections or reframes. More selective emphasis toward change and active softening of resistance would improve this area.
