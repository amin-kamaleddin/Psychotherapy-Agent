# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Farid’s struggle using nonjudgmental language and explicit acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening captures both content and affect (e.g., workload, fatigue, worn‑out sighs) and the clinician checks understanding before moving forward.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear and appropriate focus on increasing activity is maintained, moving from ambivalence to a specific, actionable plan (10‑minute walks, scheduled reminders, accountability).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co‑constructed with Farid, the clinician frequently solicits his preferences, and decisions are made collaboratively rather than directive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician affirms Farid’s choice and avoids pressure, but repeatedly offering multiple suggestions slightly steers the client; asking permission before ideas and inviting weighing options would strengthen autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and past strengths (desire for energy, meal‑prep success), yet could more consistently probe strength/confidence, use scaling, and invite the client to articulate personal reasons/values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Farid is positioned as the expert on his life with frequent invitations to choose and shared decision‑making, demonstrating genuine power‑sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and affirmations elicit concrete change talk and link to past success, but the clinician could better amplify, summarize DARN‑C language and elicit stronger commitment statements or scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reframes and normalizes sustain talk, validates barriers without confronting, and pivots to planning which reduces the pull of reasons for inaction.
