# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used consistently nonjudgmental, respectful language and normalized Kevin’s ambivalence, praising concrete steps and conveying unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that accurately captured Kevin’s ambivalence, though there were missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflections (e.g., exploring shame or stress).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use and safety, sequencing questions purposefully and consistently moving the session toward a concrete action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort, solicited Kevin’s input, and co-constructed concrete steps using inclusive language rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly reinforced Kevin’s choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and inviting him to choose and shape the plan, supporting ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Kevin’s own motivations and resources (open questions, confidence ruler, drawing out supports) and built the plan from his ideas rather than supplying reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally treated Kevin as the expert and asked for preferences, but occasionally offered multiple practical tips rapidly instead of first eliciting more of his ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, invited small steps and confidence brainstorming, but could more consistently amplify and summarize desire/ability/reasons/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged the positive social side of drinking and redirected toward harm-reduction without arguing, though greater use of double-sided reflections could further soften sustain talk.
