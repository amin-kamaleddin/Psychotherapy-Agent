# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used nonjudgmental language, acknowledged effort, and reframed slips as data rather than failure, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good empathic listening and validation of stress and skepticism, but offered few deep or complex reflections exploring underlying emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing lunchtime smoking with every intervention tied to that behavior and a concrete follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked the patient to choose strategies and scheduled a mutual follow-up—but leaned toward clinician-driven solutions rather than eliciting more patient-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering multiple options, asking which felt doable, emphasizing choice, and avoiding pressure while inviting reporting back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation and commitments (testing strategies, journaling, follow-up) but missed deeper probes into values, reasons, and past successes to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally collaborative but often shifted into an expert, prescriptive stance by supplying many specific tactics, reducing opportunities for co‑creation and patient-led planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced and amplified change statements and asked commitment questions, yet could have used more strategic reflections and prompts to deepen desire, ability, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and redirected to change strategies while normalizing concerns, but relied more on solutions than double-sided reflections that explore the role of smoking under stress.
