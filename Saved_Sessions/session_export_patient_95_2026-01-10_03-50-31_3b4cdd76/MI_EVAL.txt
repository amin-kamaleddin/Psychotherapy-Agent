# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, repeatedly validating Maria's pain. The clinician honored her request not to be rushed into problem-solving and avoided blaming or minimizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-quality empathic listening with timely, specific reflections using the patient's own language and sensory detail. Reflections accurately captured ambivalence and emotional tone, helping Maria feel heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on immediate suicidal risk and safety, repeatedly returning to intent, access to means, and prior attempts. The clinician resisted derailing into unrelated topics and prioritized containment and assessment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reflected an equal-partner stance with frequent consent-seeking and offers to co-create steps (e.g., scripting calls, staying on the line). Interventions were adapted to Maria's stated limits, showing shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by offering options rather than directives and repeatedly deferring to Maria's preferences. Even safety-focused language was framed respectfully and alternatives were proposed rather than mandated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used open invitations that elicited protective imagery and internal material (e.g., brother memory), indicating intent to draw motivation from within. However, the clinician could have more consistently pursued and deepened change-motivating statements about values and reasons to stay.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly framed the contact as collaborative, asking what Maria wanted and offering concrete options (stay on phone, brief grounding, scripting). This consistently communicated that expertise about choices resided with Maria.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some attempts to elicit reasons for staying (notably the brother memory) but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify change talk. The clinician did not consistently follow with targeted evocation questions to expand desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double-sided reflections to acknowledge hopeless statements without colluding. There were further opportunities to more actively reframe or explore consequences of suicide to elicit resistance and weaken sustain talk.
