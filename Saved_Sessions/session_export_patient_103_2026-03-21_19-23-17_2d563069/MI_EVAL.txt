# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone and validated the patient's frustration while inviting choice; could add more explicit normalization and unconditional acceptance statements to further reduce defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with reflective listening and attention to bodily reactions; could use more complex reflections to explore ambivalence and the emotional meaning of repeated disrespect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on the target problems and moved logically from validation to grounding to concrete scripts and an email; session stayed goal-directed without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving by offering options, role‑play, and co‑editing the email; at times the clinician offered scripted language before eliciting the patient's own wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's right to choose, avoided pressure, and asked which options felt most helpful, reinforcing the patient's control over decisions and actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete commitments through choice of strategies, role‑play, and drafting an email; could deepen evocation by exploring values, long‑term goals, importance, and confidence more systematically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited the patient to set the agenda and co‑create language, fostering shared power; could more explicitly frame decisions as joint and ask permission before offering specific suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and supported concrete change actions (role‑play, sending an email) that produced commitment language; could more explicitly elicit and amplify desire, ability, reasons, need, and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Redirected away from sustain talk toward solutions and steps, which helped, but missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections and explicit exploration of the costs/benefits of staying with the status quo.
