# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validation, avoided blame, and acknowledged the patient's efforts throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were accurate reflections and recognition of practical constraints, but the clinician tended to move quickly to solutions rather than deepening feeling-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on parental smoking and reducing child exposure, with interventions tied logically to that target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was joint problem-solving with the patient's preferences prioritized and a simple, co-created plan that reflected her daily realities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician honored the patient's choice, avoided coercion, offered a menu of voluntary options, and reinforced patient ownership of any steps taken.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was drawn out (leading to a doable first step), but the clinician rarely elicited the patient's own reasons, values, or importance/confidence about quitting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited choices and follow-up, but supplying a lengthy list of expert recommendations early in the visit shifted some balance away from co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited and reinforced small change talk and used choice questions, though they could more systematically probe desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician honored the patient's reluctance to quit and avoided arguing, instead pivoting to harm-reduction and tiny-step strategies that reduced defensiveness.
