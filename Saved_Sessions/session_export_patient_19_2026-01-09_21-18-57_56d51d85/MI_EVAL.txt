# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is generally nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing Anya's anxiety and accepting her minimizing humor. Could strengthen by offering more individualized affirmations and explicitly normalizing relapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic listening and asks about feelings and risk, conveying concern. Reflections are mostly factual rather than deepening emotional content—more complex reflections of shame, fear, and ambivalence are needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent behavioral focus on gambling and financial stability with concrete, actionable steps and timelines. Repeatedly brings discussion back to practical next steps, which effectively moves toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys teamwork by inviting step selection and offering practical help (scripts, booking). However, the clinician often supplies fully formed solutions instead of co-constructing them from Anya’s own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and letting Anya pick steps. Avoids coercive language and reinforces her ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits useful information (debts, prior attempts) but does not consistently draw out or link Anya’s personal reasons and values to change. Interaction leans toward practical problem-solving rather than surfacing intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames work as collaborative and checks comfort with options, offering to help with tasks. Partnership weakens when the clinician shifts to an expert-driven mode with prescriptive lists rather than reflecting and building on Anya’s ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are prompts that elicit change-related actions and commitments, but the clinician misses chances to amplify and reflect Anya’s expressed desires and reasons for change. More reflective follow-ups to expand her change language would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimizing and doubt) is present but not actively addressed; the clinician moves quickly to practical solutions. Use of double-sided reflections, reframing, and summaries that acknowledge ambivalence would better soften sustain talk.
