# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, normalising Lars's fears and reducing shame (e.g., about the form). Language avoids blaming or labeling and conveys unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic statements and acknowledges embarrassment and fear, showing solid understanding. Missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of Lars's conflicted emotions before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, practical focus on reducing reoffending risk and completing the polytech application. Consistently steers the session to actionable steps tied to Lars's goals with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative with frequent invitations for input and co‑construction of wording. Occasionally shifts into a more directive, solution-focused mode without first eliciting Lars's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and avoiding coercive language. Reinforces that Lars retains control over disclosures and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Links actions to Lars's goals and asks preferences, but elicits limited intrinsic reasons or values. Few open questions or reflections were used to draw sustained client-produced change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership: invites choices, co-creates form language, and checks in frequently, sharing power with Lars. Clinician recognises Lars as the authority on his own facts and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective elicitation and reinforcement (routines, forward-looking lines), and offers choices that invite agency. However, there are few open questions prompting Lars to articulate his own reasons, abilities, or commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk, normalises ambivalence, and offers concrete alternatives (scripts, delay tactics). Would benefit from more double-sided reflections explicitly naming both sides of the ambivalence.
