# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validated Mateo’s experience (e.g., normalizing discouragement and affirming effort), creating a nonjudgmental tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections captured both content and feeling (linking effort and discouragement, naming emotions) which deepened Mateo’s expression and supported rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on increasing safe, manageable activity with concrete behavioral steps, timeline, and measures (heat/core, 5‑10 min walk, pain checks, try tomorrow).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created the plan with Mateo, soliciting his preferences and integrating supports (wife, alarm, log) so the plan reflected shared problem‑solving and context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were offered, permission sought for suggestions, and Mateo’s preferences guided the plan, reinforcing his control and safety (ability to stop if symptoms worsen).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Mateo’s own motivations and contingencies (what matters, readiness/confidence scalings, what would nudge him), successfully drawing out change talk and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language and approach positioned clinician and Mateo as collaborators (e.g., “Which feels most doable?”), with Mateo leading choices and the clinician supporting implementation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change language (options, scaling, affirming chosen plan) and summarized commitments, though could more frequently amplify and elaborate Mateo’s 'I’ll try' statements to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and reframed into small, testable steps and risk‑reduction strategies, but the clinician could more often use double‑sided reflections to explicitly contain ambivalence and reduce sustain talk's pull.
