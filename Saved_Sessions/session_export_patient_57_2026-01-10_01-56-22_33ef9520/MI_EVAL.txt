# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance throughout, using normalizing and validating statements and avoiding blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used empathic reflections that showed understanding of Jamal’s pressure and fears, but could have deepened and more frequently reflected emotional nuance (shame, loyalty, ambivalence).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in team contexts and actionable harm-reduction strategies, from frequency questions to rehearsal and backup planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative—clinician invited Jamal's choices, followed his lead in role-play, and co-created practical plans rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Jamal’s autonomy by offering options, asking permission to suggest or role-play, and validating his right to choose without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-related statements and doable steps but relied more on offering solutions than systematically drawing out Jamal’s own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and invited Jamal to pick tactics and rehearse, but could strengthen partnership by explicitly invoking his expertise and soliciting his ideas before proposing options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generated occasional change talk and commitments (NA drinks, refusal lines), but did not consistently amplify, explore consequences/values, or assess importance and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to practical harm-reduction strategies, though more advanced techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) were not used.
