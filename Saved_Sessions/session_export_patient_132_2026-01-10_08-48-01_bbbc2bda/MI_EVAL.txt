# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental support and unconditional positive regard, using validating language and normalizing Yasmin’s mixed feelings. Openings like “I’m here to listen and support you” and repeated normalizations reinforced acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were accurate reflections and perspective-taking (e.g., acknowledging discouragement and hope, asking about triggers), showing good understanding. The clinician sometimes moved quickly to solutions instead of offering deeper, complex reflections of shame, identity, or cultural pressures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on shifting from dieting to sustainable behaviors and concrete goals. The clinician repeatedly brought the conversation back to specific behavior targets, planning, and relapse-prevention steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt clearly collaborative, with the clinician soliciting Yasmin’s input at each step and co-creating tools like micro-scripts, reminders, and plan B options. Decisions about snacks, wording, and scheduling were jointly decided, reflecting shared power.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice by offering multiple options and using invitational language, allowing Yasmin to drive decisions. This approach reinforced Yasmin’s ownership of the plan and respected her preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Yasmin’s motivations and barriers and drew useful change talk about sustainability and enjoyment. Where evocation could improve, the clinician often supplied specific solutions rather than using more open questions to deepen Yasmin’s own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Yasmin as a collaborator, asking permission for activities and co-constructing the plan (snacks, reminders, recommit routine). Power-sharing was evident as Yasmin’s judgments guided the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, leading to Yasmin committing to a doable snack plan and micro-script. To deepen change talk, the clinician could more deliberately amplify Yasmin’s language about sustainability and probe confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing with sustain talk and provided reframing tools (naming diet-talk, micro-scripts) while validating worries about weight. Missed opportunities include using more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both the desire for weight loss and how dieting has failed her.
