# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing feelings and affirming the patient's choices without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective listening and validated concerns effectively, but missed opportunities for deeper empathic reflections about family history and the emotion behind sustain-talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a strong, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability, moving logically from assessment to concrete harm-reduction planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative throughout, inviting the patient's input, co-creating scripts and plans, and adapting interventions to the patient's preferences and context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing voluntary action while avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited motivations and encouraged planning, but could more deliberately probe and amplify deeper internal motives, values, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established a collaborative partnership, shared power in decision-making, and repeatedly sought the patient's preferences when shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked and reinforced change talk with goal-setting and affirmations, though more open questions could have deepened desire, reasons, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected toward change-oriented planning, but did not explicitly use double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to acknowledge ambivalence.
