# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, validating feelings and normalizing burden. Explicitly honored boundaries (no extra appointments) without blaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and paraphrase that matched the patient's tone, helping the patient feel understood. Reflections facilitated the patient offering concrete plans.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on improving daily preventer adherence with a specific one-week trial and concrete implementation steps. Questions and summaries consistently linked to that target behavior and safety planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with co-constructed decisions and frequent invitations for patient input. Clinician offered options and checked feasibility rather than imposing a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported patient choice and control, honoring limits like no extra appointments. Asked permission before offering tips and framed suggestions as optional supports.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited practical motivation and incremental commitments (scaling, small-step plan) but explored less about deeper personal values or long-term reasons for change. More value-elicitation could strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by soliciting preferences and co-creating the implementation plan. Deferred to the patient's choices and tailored options to their stated limits.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively evoked and amplified change talk using importance/confidence scaling, a specific small-step request, and follow-up planning questions. Reflections and prompts produced concrete commitment language and increased confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk, validated frustration, and reframed concerns while moving toward action. Offered a low-burden experiment and neutral, double-sided language to keep the patient engaged.
