# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized struggle and relapse; validated efforts. Occasional brief/part‑procedural validations rather than deeper individualized exploration of shame.

Checklist (checked = explicitly/clearly present)
- [x] Nonjudgmental language and tone
- [x] Normalized struggle/relapse ("treat it as information, not failure")
- [x] Validated patient efforts and intentions ("You’re doing the right things…")
- [ ] Extended individualized acknowledgement of stigma/shame beyond brief validations

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking; recognized feelings of shame, fear, and ambivalence and checked understanding. Missed opportunities for deeper complex reflections to explore the emotional core of "like a leper."

Checklist
- [x] Reflections of emotion and content (“That mix of hope and fear is totally understandable”)
- [x] Asked how the patient felt about plans/check‑ins
- [ ] Multiple deeper/complex reflections to name underlying values or identity loss

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on cessation with concrete, measurable plan (Saturday 9:00 AM start, 8:00 PM check‑in) and clear urge‑management strategies. At times shifted into informational/medical education without explicitly eliciting preferences first.

Checklist
- [x] Set a specific, measurable quit plan (date/time, actions)
- [x] Provided concrete coping/urge strategies (delay, breathing, lozenges)
- [x] Gave medical/behavioral options (NRT, meds, vaping) — somewhat directive
- [ ] Consistently asked permission before giving detailed medical advice

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed encounter as joint effort: invited patient input on steps and check‑in, accepted texting. Some clinical turns were more prescriptive (specific NRT dosing, vaping strategy), slightly reducing collaborative tone.

Checklist
- [x] Invited patient input and choices (“Would you like to pick one small first step now?”)
- [x] Co‑created a concrete plan (start time, actions, check‑in)
- [x] Agreed on follow‑up contact (texting, check‑in)
- [ ] Avoided prescriptive expert recommendations in all turns

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirmed choice and offered multiple options; encouraged patient control (texting, changing plans). At times recommendations (e.g., 4 mg lozenges) were given without first checking patient preference.

Checklist
- [x] Presented multiple options and alternatives (lozenges, patch, vape, meds)
- [x] Affirmed patient control and ability to change plans
- [ ] Routinely asked permission before offering specific medical recommendations

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations and history (motivation/confidence scales, past attempts) and drew out a specific commitment (start time, check‑in). Could have further amplified the patient’s own reasons and values to deepen commitment.

Checklist
- [x] Elicited motivation/confidence ratings (0–10)
- [x] Asked about past quit attempts and strategies
- [x] Elicited a concrete commitment (24‑hour trial / start time)
- [ ] Systematically amplified change talk or used follow‑ups to deepen commitment

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally treated interaction as partnership — asked permission, invited choices, offered support. Several expert‑style recommendations (dosing, med lists) tilted interaction toward clinician‑expert role.

Checklist
- [x] Used collaborative language and co‑planning
- [x] Invited patient to propose/check‑in preferences
- [ ] Fully foregrounded patient expertise when offering options

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced and praised change talk and commitment (e.g., “That’s a strong, doable first step”). Repeatedly reflected reasons for change (daughter, shame, health). Could more consistently use reflections/questions to amplify change talk.

Checklist
- [x] Reinforced and praised patient’s plan and expressed change talk
- [x] Asked for specific commitments (start time, check‑in)
- [ ] Probed further to expand and strengthen reasons/commitment statements

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk (fear of failing, cost, side effects), reframed slips as information not failure, and offered harm‑reduction alternatives. Would be strengthened by more double‑sided reflections explicitly naming both sides of ambivalence.

Checklist
- [x] Validated and normalized concerns about relapse, cost, side effects
- [x] Reframed slips as learning/information rather than moral failure
- [x] Offered alternatives (cutting down, vaping as stopgap, subsidized NRT)
- [ ] Used double‑sided reflections to explicitly contrast sustain vs change reasons
