# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating Mai’s feelings and normalizing ambivalence. The tone remained supportive with no blaming or corrective statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-quality empathic listening with accurate, often complex reflections capturing both affect and content. Open questions and paraphrases repeatedly deepened understanding of Mai’s concerns and priorities.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on safe initiation and adherence to the statin, consistently steering conversation toward concrete implementation steps. The clinician resisted drift and linked each move to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and co-creative, with the psychiatrist soliciting Mai’s priorities and inviting her wording and choices. The patient’s input was central to drafting the plan and materials.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Mai’s choice and encouraged self-direction by offering options and asking what felt most doable. The clinician affirmed preferences and avoided pressuring a particular action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively evoked Mai’s own motivations and practical reasons for change, linking them to specific behaviors (e.g., pill timing, alarm, pill box). Reflections and questions drew out internal motivation rather than supplying external reasons alone.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership was evident as decisions were placed with Mai and the clinician shared options rather than prescribing. Co-creation of the one-page and step prioritization reinforced equal collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced multiple pieces of change talk and concrete commitments (take at night, switch juice, call for muscle pain). Could improve by explicitly amplifying and consolidating change language into a clear commitment or confidence rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk neutrally and redirected toward safety planning and practical solutions without arguing. A double-sided reflection naming both doubts and reasons for change could further resolve ambivalence.
