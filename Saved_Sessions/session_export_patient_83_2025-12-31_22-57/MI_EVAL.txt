### Empathy

- [x] Reflective listening / accurate perspective-taking

- [ ] Deep/emotion-focused reflections (limited)

**Comments:** Session stayed tightly on concrete steps needed for Mai to feel safe (written sheet, pharmacist interaction, follow-up, labs); every prompt moved toward decision-making and planning.

### Collaboration

**Comments:** Clinician treated Mai as an equal partner—asked preferences at each decision point, modified plan to her priorities (pharmacy first, then printed sheet, then follow-up).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Emphasizes patient choice and pace
- [x] Offers options rather than directives
- [x] Encourages patient ownership of plan

**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Mai’s autonomy (“Which of those matters most to you?”; “I’m with you on moving at your pace”), no pressuring language, supported her decisions and self-management strategies.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicits patient’s reasons and adherence strategies
- [x] Draws out change talk (alarms, routines, start date)
- [ ] Deep exploration of intrinsic motives/values

**Comments:** Effective at eliciting concrete motivations and routines that support adherence, but could more often evoke deeper intrinsic motives and confidence (e.g., values, scaling).

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Invites patient expertise about routines/preferences
- [x] Negotiates next steps collaboratively
- [x] Integrates patient responses into plan

**Comments:** Strong shared decision-making; patient’s input directly shaped actions (pharmacist call, checklist format, timing), demonstrating robust partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicits and reinforces change talk
- [x] Reflects change-oriented statements into plan
- [ ] Consolidates commitment with scaling or explicit commitment prompts

**Comments:** Several clear instances of change talk were elicited and reflected (“I’ll try taking one tablet at night,” alarm and start-date routine), but clinician did not consistently amplify or secure commitment with scaling/questions or a final commitment check.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Avoids reinforcing sustain talk
- [x] Validates concerns and redirects toward safety planning
- [ ] Uses more active techniques (double-sided reflections, explicit pros/cons) to soften sustain talk

**Comments:** Responded nonjudgmentally to initial hesitancy and redirected to safety-focused planning rather than arguing; could further reduce sustain talk via strategic double-sided reflections and explicit elicitation of change-preference language.
