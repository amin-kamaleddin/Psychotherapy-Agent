# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized the patient's compromise, avoiding admonishment and validating practical concerns about work and partner involvement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrase of the patient's perspective around logistics, but missed deeper exploration of the emotional meaning of evening smoking (stress/ritual).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on daytime nicotine replacement throughout the session, guiding the conversation toward concrete, measurable actions and progressive planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-created with the patient generating practical details while the clinician reflected and organized those ideas into a workable plan without imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported the patient's choice and framed the plan as a controllable, doable test, avoiding pressuring language and emphasizing patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own reasons and practical motivators for trying daytime NRT, but could further probe intrinsic values, confidence, and commitment strength to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently asked for the patient's input and built the plan from his ideas, positioning the patient as the expert on his routine and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled at eliciting specific change language and a concrete commitment (patch test), though could more explicitly amplify and summarize the patient's change talk to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and neutrally reframed sustain talk to avoid defensiveness, but did not employ more advanced techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or evocative questions) to actively shift sustain talk.
