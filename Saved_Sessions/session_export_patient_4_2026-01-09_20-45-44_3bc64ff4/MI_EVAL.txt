# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and validated the patient's distress, acknowledging fears without blame. Supported the patient's request for tests rather than dismissing her concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening that integrated emotional and content-level material, reflecting symptom timeline and contextual meaning. Reflections conveyed clear perspective-taking and understanding of the patient's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, clinically appropriate focus on GI symptoms, diagnostic clarification, and practical next steps. Kept the session goal-oriented with concrete actions (diary, baseline tests, expedited referral, scripts).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a two-way partnership: the clinician invited input, role‑played, drafted notes, and negotiated the plan with consent. The patient was actively involved in choosing and modifying next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's choices and offered multiple options while avoiding coercive language. Invited preferences about wording, practice, and next steps, supporting the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some internal material (symptom patterns, willingness to keep a diary) but tended to provide solutions rather than explore the patient's own reasons, hopes, or values for change. Missed opportunities to use evocative questions to expand intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the encounter as collaborative and co‑creative, asking for the patient's priorities and offering to practice or draft materials together. Demonstrated strong power‑sharing and mutual decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language (agreement to keep a diary, do baseline tests) but did not systematically amplify or deepen change talk. Could have explored what improvements would mean for the patient and elicited confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not effectively soften sustain talk and at times reinforced it by supporting the patient's push for investigations. Used minimal reframing or double-sided reflections to gently pivot away from sustain language.
