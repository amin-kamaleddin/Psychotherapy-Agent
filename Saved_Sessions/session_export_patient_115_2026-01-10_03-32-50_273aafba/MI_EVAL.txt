# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, using affirmations and normalization. Avoided blame and explicitly validated Samir’s progress throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with practical reflections and detailed questions about symptoms. Missed opportunities to deepen affective reflections (e.g., impact of night wakings or ER visit on mood, family, or work).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on target behaviors tied to asthma control and set specific, measurable goals. Consistently guided toward concrete actions without losing patient-centeredness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used inclusive language, invited Samir to choose priorities, and scheduled a mutually agreed follow-up. Occasional clinician-driven instructions and repeated reminders reduced fully equal turn-taking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering options, inviting choice, and emphasizing Samir’s control over decisions. Encouraged safe, self-directed testing (e.g., try gum at home) and framed slips as information, not failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from Samir by eliciting symptoms, choices, and reinforcing his progress rather than imposing reasons for change. Could further evoke intrinsic motivation by exploring personal values and asking what improved asthma would mean for him or his family.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance and frequently invited Samir’s input and choices. At times provided a fairly detailed, clinician-driven plan and specific instructions that shifted some control toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, including a specific achievable change (reduce by 2 cigarettes/day) and options for NRT and dust mitigation. Could have amplified Samir’s own statements about asthma consequences to elicit stronger desire/ability/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by validating partial change and offering harm-reduction options and normalizing slips. A stronger use of double-sided reflections or reflective summaries contrasting costs/benefits could further soften sustain talk.
