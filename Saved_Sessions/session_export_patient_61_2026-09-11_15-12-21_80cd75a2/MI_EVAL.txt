# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing lapses and affirming honesty to create a safe atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used frequent, accurate reflections and perspective-taking that captured overwhelm and reluctance, but reflections were sometimes repetitive and missed opportunities to probe deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on home glucose monitoring and steadily guided the conversation toward specific, actionable steps like timing, kit placement, and a frequency goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative with joint problem‑solving; the plan emerged from patient input and the clinician frequently checked back to confirm choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported patient choice by offering options, honoring preferences (e.g., not involving his wife), and framing steps as the patient's decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical, patient-generated solutions tied to routines, but could have strengthened motivation by exploring personal reasons, values, or using scaling questions to assess commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a partnership stance throughout, inviting the patient to choose among options and co-constructing the plan rather than prescribing actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited specific change talk and low‑burden options that led to actionable intentions, but did not sufficiently amplify, summarize, or elicit strength of commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully reframed ambivalence into manageable steps and normalized missed days to reduce pressure, though more double‑sided reflections could have helped the patient hear and resolve mixed feelings.
