# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opened nonjudgmentally and maintained a consistently respectful, validating tone, normalizing concerns and offering practical support. These behaviors communicated strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of values and worries. There were missed opportunities for deeper affective reflections (e.g., when the patient said Googling ‘freaked me out’).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on asthma management and medication decisions with concrete, measurable steps (spirometry, low-dose trial, spacer, symptom log). The clinician balanced empathic exploration with forward movement toward a specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was consistently collaborative and framed as joint problem-solving, co-creating scripts, logs, and messaging. The clinician solicited and followed the patient's preferences, avoiding an expert-only lecture style.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician emphasized choice and control by offering options, a low-pressure approach, and asking which step felt most doable. The patient’s decisions were affirmed and supported throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited values and change talk (family reasons, choosing spirometry) and invited the patient to identify doable steps. To improve, they could have more systematically probed importance/confidence and amplified intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the patient as an expert in her life and co-created the plan, offering choices and checking preferences repeatedly. Power-sharing and shared decision-making were clearly evident.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and affirmed change talk and scaffolded concrete actions that promoted follow-through. They could have further amplified and summarized the patient's pro-change statements and asked for importance/confidence or a small commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged and reframed sustain talk without arguing, using double-sided reflections and nonthreatening alternatives. Stronger use of summaries and more selective reinforcement could further diminish sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
