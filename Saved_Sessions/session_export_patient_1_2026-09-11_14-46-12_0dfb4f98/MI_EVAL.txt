# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's stance and constraints, avoiding judgment and affirming practical choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with reflective listening that captures ambivalence and pragmatic concerns, checking for accuracy throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on smoking-related oral health and consistently funnels the conversation toward specific, achievable harm-reduction steps and weekly actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the encounter as a cooperative endeavor, invites input and negotiates next steps together so decisions are co-constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by offering choices, normalizing the decision not to quit, and repeatedly asking the patient which options fit their preferences and routine.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's motivations and concrete ideas (e.g., rinses, travel bottle) but could strengthen evocation by probing deeper intrinsic reasons and confidence/importance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the expert on their life, hands decision-making to them with preference questions and offers of support, demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits small steps and change-oriented language that produce concrete commitments, but could better amplify and summarize change talk to build momentum toward stronger commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections to reduce defensiveness while pivoting to harm-reduction; could further soften sustain talk by explicitly acknowledging the legitimacy of reasons for continuing.
