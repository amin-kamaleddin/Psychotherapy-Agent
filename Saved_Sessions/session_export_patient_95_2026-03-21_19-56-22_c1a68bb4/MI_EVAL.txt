# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating (e.g., thanking Maria and normalizing her pain). Could add more individualized affirmations of strengths beyond safety steps to strengthen this further.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, affect-focused reflective listening and tracks shifts in urge, respecting Maria’s request to be heard. Reflections effectively capture metaphorical content and emotional intensity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on suicidal ideation, risk assessment, and concrete means-restriction steps throughout. Session stays tightly on the immediate safety target with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs the safety plan and frequently invites Maria’s preferences (e.g., options for pills, staying on the phone). Necessary clinician-led safety checks create some asymmetry but overall the stance is collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames suggestions as options and acknowledges Maria’s choices (offers multiple options and asks preferences). At times pushes for specific commitments (e.g., moving meds), so could elicit more of Maria’s own ideas for doable steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some targeted attempts to draw out resources and reasons (asking about who she cares about and past coping), but does not consistently probe or amplify fleeting change talk or values. More systematic DARN-C questioning and elicitations would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and explicitly says 'I’m here to listen, not fix', offering options and soliciting input on next steps. The clinician appropriately becomes more directive for safety at times, slightly reducing client-led agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-relevant language (e.g., who she cares about, what helped before) and obtains commitments to delay action, but misses opportunities to amplify and deepen fleeting change talk with follow-ups. Could ask more evocative questions to build desire, reasons, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and uses empathic reflections, then redirects to safety actions. However, the clinician often moves quickly to directives rather than using MI strategies (double-sided reflections, reframing, pros/cons) to resolve ambivalence more gently.
