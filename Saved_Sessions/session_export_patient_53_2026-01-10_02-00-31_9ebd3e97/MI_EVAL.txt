# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes anxiety, avoiding blame while affirming small efforts as meaningful.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and strong reflective listening that captures the patient's affect and ambivalence and shapes supportive responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on a concrete behavioral goal with stepwise planning and contingency options, without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as shared problem-solving, invites patient choices, and negotiates options so both clinician and patient contribute to the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly emphasizes voluntariness and choice, offers multiple options and reframes smaller actions as valid outcomes without using coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some preparatory motivation through choices of time and reward but largely supplies ideas rather than drawing out the patient's personal reasons, values, or intrinsic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters power-sharing and joint decision-making, co-constructing the plan with the patient choosing specifics while clinician offers support and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generates limited change talk by prompting tiny-step choices, but misses opportunities for open questions, reflections, and amplification to strengthen desire, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids amplifying sustain talk and reframes setbacks as progress, though it seldom uses double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence.
