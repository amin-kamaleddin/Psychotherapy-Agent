# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating (e.g., normalizing frustration and honoring dislike of meds), and respects Samir’s stated preferences throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses reflective listening and adapts to Samir’s preference for brief, non-lecturing interactions, but could deepen empathy with more complex reflections probing the reasons behind his resistance to meds.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on managing family pressure and developing a brief boundary script, keeping interventions brief and pragmatic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites choices, role-play, and tailors language for specific people, but often supplies ready-made scripts rather than eliciting Samir’s own wording earlier, making the work somewhat clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Samir’s autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing his control over decisions while preserving safety about medication changes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does little to elicit intrinsic motivations or values; the interaction is largely advice- and skills-focused with few evocative questions to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance with invitations and tailoring, but sometimes shifts into an expert/directive role by providing multiple pre-made options instead of co-creating phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some concrete change language around using a script and role-playing, but does not follow up to amplify commitment, confidence, or explore pros/cons to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and validates dislike of meds, but rarely explores the reasons for that sustain talk or uses double-sided reflections to move toward ambivalence resolution.
