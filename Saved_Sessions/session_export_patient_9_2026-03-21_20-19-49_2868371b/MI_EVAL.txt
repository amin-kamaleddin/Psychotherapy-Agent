# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and warmth, normalized the client's struggle, and invited permission before intervening; little evidence of criticism or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening with accurate reflections and validation, but could probe deeper into emotional nuance (e.g., fear of escalation, guardedness) with a few more reflective questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on bedtime consistency and co‑parenting, moving logically from assessment to concrete, actionable planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑constructed texts and in‑moment lines, rehearsed together, used inclusive language, and incorporated the client's wording and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Routinely honored autonomy by asking permission, offering options, and asking the client to choose wording and strategies, supporting ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited values and used scaling questions to generate some change talk but moved fairly quickly to offering solutions; could draw out more internal motivation and past successes with open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership through shared decision‑making, negotiating tone, and deferring to the client's preferences while refining plans together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with evocative questions, scaling, and rehearsal, though could more often amplify the client's own desire/ability/commitment language before offering strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided reinforcing resistance but did not consistently use double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to process ambivalence before moving to scripted solutions.
