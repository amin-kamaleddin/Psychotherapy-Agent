# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used supportive, nonjudgmental language and validated the patient's experience throughout, offering respectful choices and no signs of blame or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both emotion and content (frustration, childcare barrier), demonstrating consistent perspective-taking and tailored responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on attending interviews by addressing childcare, moving efficiently from problem identification to concrete action steps and contingencies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interactions were highly collaborative with scripts and messages co-constructed and immediate clinician adaptation to the patient's edits and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered choices, invited patient input on wording and actions, and reinforced the patient's control without coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited actionable preferences and leveraged the patient's confidence but missed some opportunities to probe deeper intrinsic motivations and values tied to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treated the patient as the primary decision-maker, offering options and asking which felt most helpful while incorporating patient decisions into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Practically converted confidence into concrete options and next steps, though there were missed chances to explicitly elicit or amplify the patient's internal change talk and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but the clinician rapidly shifted to problem-solving and used few MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to normalize ambivalence.
