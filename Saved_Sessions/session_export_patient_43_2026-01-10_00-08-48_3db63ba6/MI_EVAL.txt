# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained an unwavering nonjudgmental tone, validated ambivalence, and reinforced small efforts as legitimate progress throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking matched the patient's affect and stance, accurately summarizing concerns and normalizing cravings and slip-ups.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, consistent focus on smoking with every intervention tied to practical steps (tracking, delays, gum) and concrete next actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a team effort: the clinician used inclusive language, solicited preferences, co‑created the plan, and invited check‑ins and feedback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and voluntariness, framed suggestions as optional experiments, and invited the patient to select what felt manageable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited pragmatic, behavior-linked motivations through experiments and questions but could have probed more for intrinsic values or deeper personal reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the patient as an equal collaborator, offering options and allowing the patient to choose steps (10‑minute delay, gum) to shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and small commitments (delays, gum) but could strengthen follow-ups to amplify desire, reasons, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing, normalized rationalizations, and redirected to low‑pressure experiments; using more double‑sided reflections and summary would better highlight discrepancy.
