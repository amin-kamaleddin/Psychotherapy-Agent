# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates safety concerns with clear precautions. More explicit normalization and individualized positive regard would raise this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some supportive touches and offers of help, but few reflective statements that name the patient’s feelings or mirror ambivalence. Increasing reflective listening and emotional labeling would improve empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused on the medication decision and safety issues, with clear, actionable next steps. Each response directly addresses the patient’s questions and moves toward concrete planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites joint problem‑solving (bring records, draft a one‑page note) and tailors advice to the patient’s concerns. To reach a 5, they could more explicitly co‑create a decision plan during the visit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician refrains from coercion, offers options, and emphasizes appropriate channels for decision‑making. Explicit affirmations of the patient’s competence and more choice‑laden language would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely informational and does little to elicit the patient’s own motivations, priorities, or readiness for change. Targeted questions about importance, confidence, or reasons for change were largely absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fosters a collaborative stance by involving the prescriber and offering practical supports like drafting notes and bringing logs. Occasional necessary directive safety language slightly shifts toward an expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician supports concrete actions (taper plan, notes, questions) that enable change but rarely elicits the patient’s own motivations or commitment language. Asking about values, importance, or readiness would amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively minimizes sustain talk by emphasizing safety and discouraging unilateral stopping while redirecting to practical solutions. More MI techniques like double‑sided reflections could further acknowledge ambivalence while steering toward safe choices.
