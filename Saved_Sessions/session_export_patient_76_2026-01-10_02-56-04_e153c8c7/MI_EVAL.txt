# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validates exhaustion and effort, and repeatedly affirms the patient's actions (e.g., locking the razor, trying ice).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, moment-to-moment reflective listening and perspective-taking, using precise reflections tied to the patient's words and bodily sensations. 

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on reducing self-harm and safety, consistently linking interventions to the target behavior and setting concrete thresholds (urge >7).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as a two-way partnership with inclusive language, co-creation of plans (drafting texts, offering to stay), and respect for the patient's boundaries and preferences. 

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Routinely offers choices and emphasizes the patient's control over decisions, avoiding coercive language while supporting and normalizing skepticism. 

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some internal motivation and problem-solving (locking drawer, choosing ice) but could further draw out values, long-term reasons, and importance/confidence to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively invites the patient's input and shares power in planning (asking preferences, co-constructing safety steps), honoring the patient's expertise about what will work. 

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits incremental change language and concrete steps, but could better amplify and summarize those statements to strengthen commitment and confidence. 

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and neutrally reflects sustain talk then pivots to harm-reduction options, though more strategic double-sided reflections could more directly resolve ambivalence.
