# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation (e.g., affirming self-awareness, normalizing slips). The warm, accepting tone was maintained throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with timely reflections of ambivalence and consequences. Reflections stayed mostly at surface/practical levels and missed deeper emotional exploration (e.g., anxiety, meaning of connection).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on phone/social media use and moved toward a concrete, time‑bound experiment with specific steps and start times. Each exchange advanced a specific action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was highly collaborative—co‑building messages, checklists, nights, and reminders while inviting edits. The clinician repeatedly followed the client's lead and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, soliciting preferences, and normalizing partial attempts and slips. Practices reinforced client control and reduced pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations and barriers (importance/confidence scales, 'what might get in the way?') and drew some change talk. The clinician often shifted quickly to solutions instead of deepening intrinsic reasons or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered partnership by asking preferences and adapting materials to the client’s input. Offering multiple specific solutions early risked tilting toward expert-driven problem solving rather than eliciting the client's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language (importance/confidence scales, commitment to a two‑night experiment). Missed opportunities to further amplify the client's own expressed reasons and strengthen desire/need statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and normalized the client's reasons for use (relaxation, FOMO) but did not consistently use strategies like double‑sided reflections or reframes before moving to behavioral prescriptions. The session moved to problem‑solving somewhat quickly.
