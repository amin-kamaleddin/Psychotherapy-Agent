# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, affirms effort and normalizes slips throughout the session; could add more empathic validations that name conflicting values and cultural/whānau context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and useful summaries with offers of support (e.g., role‑play), but reflections are mainly content-focused rather than deeper affective reflections that name shame or pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, goal-oriented focus on sobriety, violence reduction, and reunification, linking actions to the client's core value and providing practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engages the client as an active partner, soliciting preferences and incorporating tweaks, though sometimes supplies ready-made solutions before fully eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports client choice and tailors options to preferences, consistently emphasizes the client's control and offers multiple alternative approaches.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation and concrete commitments but relies heavily on clinician-structured content; would benefit from more targeted DARN questions, scaling, and eliciting the client's own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displays a collaborative stance with shared decision-making (editable checklist, permission-seeking), yet occasionally leans toward an expert role by offering stepwise plans before co‑creating them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforces and builds on existing change talk and co‑creates practical plans, but could better elicit and amplify the client's own change language and confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing for sustain talk and redirects to solutions, but does not explicitly explore ambivalence or use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk and resolve conflicting values.
