# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful tone and validated the patient's stance while honoring his request for brevity throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and wording closely matched the patient's language and affect, demonstrating accurate perspective-taking and timely reflective listening that conveyed understanding of his frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on the target behavior—making the weekly process more useful—and every offer and question advanced that actionable objective (note, backup, pushback).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked jointly with the patient to select and refine wording, invited edits and permission, and deferred to the patient's preferences, creating a genuine joint problem-solving approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were repeatedly offered and the patient's limits were respected; actions were framed as his decisions and no pressure or directives were used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician converted a terse complaint into client-authored change language and practical scripts, but did not probe more deeply into internal motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert, co-created the content, and implemented his preferences, demonstrating strong power-sharing and client-driven direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Limited change language was effectively elicited and amplified into actionable wording and plans, though the clinician did not systematically elicit desire/ability/reasons/need/confidence/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and validated resignation while redirecting toward alternatives and a plan, but could have used double-sided reflections to more softly contrast sustain and change language.
