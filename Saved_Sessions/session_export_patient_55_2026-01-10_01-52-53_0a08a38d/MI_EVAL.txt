# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing difficulty and praising effort throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate understanding and reflective listening about practical stressors, but reflections focus more on logistics than deeper emotional nuance or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on managing school stress via time‑management and Pomodoro steps, progressing to concrete action and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone is collaborative and invites input, but frequent prescriptive checklists and detailed instructions sometimes tilt the balance toward clinician‑led solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors choice and agency by offering options, asking preferences, accepting reluctance, and inviting the client to report back or tweak the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete willingness and commitments (Pomodoro plans, start times) but offers limited exploration of intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Contains collaborative elements but often adopts an expert, directive stance with many step‑by‑step suggestions instead of eliciting the client's own solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces small change language and commitments, though could more frequently reflect and amplify the client's own stated benefits and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes skepticism without confronting it and redirects to manageable steps, but misses opportunities for double‑sided reflections to deepen ambivalence work.
