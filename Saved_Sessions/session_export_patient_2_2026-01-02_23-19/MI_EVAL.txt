# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently nonjudgmental and supportive (validations like “That’s sensible…”, “If you slip, be kind…”). Accepted patient limits (did not press about drinking) and conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries (e.g., evenings are hardest; worry about cancer/son). Some reflections were surface-level and could be deeper—occasionally name affect/meaning to deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on evening smoking reduction, produced a specific measurable plan (replace two evening smokes, 15‑minute delay, 1‑minute urge tool). No drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited patient input and shared decision-making (“Which feels easiest?”, co‑writing fridge plan). Avoided an authoritarian stance; patient choices shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Emphasized choice and respected limits (asked permission to teach tool, offered options rather than directives). Language consistently supported patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons for change and concrete strategies (patient brainstormed swaps and motivations). Could further probe past successes/strengths and link values to behavior to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction framed as joint problem‑solving; clinician treated patient as expert on her life and co‑authored the plan. Partnership clear in multiple exchanges.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited desire/ability and some commitment language (“I’ll try…”, replace two smokes). Did not consistently strengthen confidence or move tentative “I’ll try” toward firmer commitment—use confidence rulers or commitment questions next time.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used double‑sided reflection acknowledging sustain talk vs change reasons. Could more strategically amplify discrepancy and employ additional targeted double‑sided or summary reflections to reduce sustain talk.
