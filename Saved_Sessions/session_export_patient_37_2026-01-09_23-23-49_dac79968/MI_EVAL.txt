# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing and affirming the patient's concerns while offering options rather than directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent empathic statements and reflections show understanding of immediate worries, but reflections are often surface-level and miss deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the vaccine decision with concrete, actionable topics and a prioritized plan for next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent joint problem-solving: co-constructs plans, invites rehearsal, and shares decision-making tasks to foster teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice by offering options, affirming the timeline to decide, and providing scripts and rehearsal to empower independent decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some preferences and brief change-language but relies heavily on providing information rather than drawing out intrinsic motivations, values, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the clinician as a helper and co-planner, though at times leans toward an informational expert role with prescriptive lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Encourages tentative movement and planning but rarely elicits the patient's own reasons, values, or uses scaling to strengthen and link change talk to commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and corrects misinformation, but often shifts to didactic corrections instead of using double-sided reflections and ambivalence-focused strategies.
