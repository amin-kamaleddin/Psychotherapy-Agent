# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., validating disclosures and affirming effort). Language avoided blame and normalized the patient's struggles, supporting a strong therapeutic stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening, and integrated somatic detail when offered. Reflections tended to be summary-level with missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional meaning and the patient's resignation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on self-harm and safety with prompt assessment and concrete steps (triggers, coping, means restriction, contacts). The clinician preserved momentum toward harm reduction and a workable safety plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly two-way: the clinician invited preferences, co-wrote the plan, and offered rehearsal/role-play. The work was framed as joint problem-solving rather than expert prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported choice by asking permission before suggestions and offering multiple options, respecting the patient's skepticism without pressure. The clinician consistently reinforced patient ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited concrete intentions and small commitments (specific strategies, asking roommate, texting a friend) and used a confidence ruler. Deeper exploration of internal motivation, values, and reasons for change was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly sought the patient's input and co-constructed the plan, including drafting texts and offering to rehearse, sharing power in decisions. This collaborative stance supported patient engagement and ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited action-oriented, procedural change talk (agreeing to breathing, delaying urges, asking roommate) but did not consistently amplify or explore desire, reasons, ability, or commitment. More targeted questions and reflections to strengthen and elaborate change talk were needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and validated the relief cutting provides while shifting to safety and alternatives. Could further soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or explicit naming of ambivalence to highlight costs and pull toward change.
