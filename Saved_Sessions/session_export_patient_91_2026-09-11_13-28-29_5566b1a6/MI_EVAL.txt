# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys nonjudgmental, respectful regard—normalizing tension and using affirmations and validations throughout. Unconditional positive regard is clear and steady.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases of Marcus's situation. Reflections stayed largely content-focused and could probe deeper emotions or underlying fears for greater empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on blood‑sugar monitoring and pushes toward a specific, time-limited experiment. Every question and reflection ties to feasible implementation steps and concrete success criteria.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session reads as collaborative with frequent invitations for Marcus to choose and prioritize strategies. At times the clinician leads by offering option lists rather than eliciting Marcus's own solutions first, which slightly shifts agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Marcus's sense of choice by offering options, asking him to pick plans, and explicitly stating they will follow his lead. No pressure or prescriptive language was used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out concrete motivations, intentions, and planning (alarms, meter placement, success markers). Could deepen intrinsic motivation by using confidence/importance rulers and summaries that amplify change talk to bolster commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintains a collaborative stance and solicits Marcus's input and priorities. Partnership is strong but could be strengthened by inviting Marcus to generate ideas before offering options and co-constructing the plan more actively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and supports change talk and helps articulate concrete implementation steps, producing sustained change statements. Missed opportunities include earlier amplification of past successes and targeted reflections to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without confrontation, but clinician seldom uses strategies to soften or redirect it (e.g., double-sided reflections, confidence rulers). More explicit exploration of doubt and targeted evocation could reduce sustain talk and build commitment.
