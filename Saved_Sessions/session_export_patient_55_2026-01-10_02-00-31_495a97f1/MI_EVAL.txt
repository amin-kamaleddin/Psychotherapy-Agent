# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance (normalizing, reassurance, safety checks). No pressuring or disapproving language; acceptance of Andrea's reluctance was clear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening with accurate reflections and summaries validating Andrea's experience. Reflections tended to stay at the surface level rather than probing deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on stress, sleep, and coping strategies that Andrea raised and kept the session practical. The initial alcohol referral was not further explored, which was reasonable given the patient's priorities but could have included a brief check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with frequent invitations for input and co-creation of a trimmed routine that respected Andrea's choices. The clinician balanced suggestions with patient preferences and offered optional follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and normalizing choice throughout the session. No coercive language; patient control over decisions was consistently emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some preferences and information about stressors but did not systematically draw out intrinsic motives or link change to values (e.g., music or IB goals). clinician offered solutions more than using open evocation strategies like importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a shared process by asking preferences and jointly adjusting plans, demonstrating respect for Andrea's expertise about what will work. Some recommendations leaned toward expert advice but permission was sought and choices guided the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional reinforcement of change language occurred (praising small steps, validating choices), but the clinician did not systematically evoke or deepen change talk toward commitment. Missed opportunities to follow up on tentative statements like 'I might try the alarm some nights.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk skillfully by normalizing reluctance and avoiding confrontation, reducing resistance. Could have further used double-sided reflections to acknowledge pros of status quo while pivoting to change.
