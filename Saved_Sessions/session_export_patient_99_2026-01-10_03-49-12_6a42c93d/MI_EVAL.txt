# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental—asks permission, affirms honesty, and normalizes ambivalence by offering options rather than prescribing a single path.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries that acknowledge worries, but reflections stayed mostly at the content/practical level with missed chances to explore deeper feelings or personal meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use and ankle recovery, moving logically from assessment to options, goal-setting, tactics, and follow-up in a focused brief-intervention sequence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine joint problem-solving—invites input at each step, co-constructs the plan, and shares power by letting the client shape choices and tactics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by asking permission, offering multiple options, avoiding pressure, and affirming the client's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's reasons and ideas (choices, confidence rating, tactics), but could probe more for intrinsic motivators and amplify brief change statements to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates a strong partnership stance—frequently asks what feels useful, invites the client to choose details, and positions the client as the expert on their life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and reinforces change language (choice of caps, confidence scale, plan) but would benefit from more strategic reflection and summarizing to strengthen commitment talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to change options with practical alternatives, though more double-sided reflections could better normalize reasons for drinking while highlighting concerns.
