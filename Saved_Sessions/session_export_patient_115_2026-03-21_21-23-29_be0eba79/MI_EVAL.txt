# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing struggles and affirming the patient’s efforts. Avoids judgment about smoking/drinking and supports incremental progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, acknowledging stressors and ambivalence. Could deepen emotional engagement with more complex reflections that name feelings and implicit concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on asthma-related target behaviors and moves conversation toward concrete, time-bound actions. Keeps the session purposeful with measurable steps and contingency plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—offers expertise while soliciting the patient’s preferences and co-creating plans and artifacts (checklist, one-line alert, text to wife). Patient’s input drives decisions and implementation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and control, respecting the decision not to quit and offering multiple options and fallbacks. Uses permission language and asks which steps feel doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical, concrete motivations and uses scaling questions to assess confidence. Could strengthen by eliciting deeper intrinsic reasons, linking change to values, and summarizing the patient’s change language more often.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the interaction as a partnership, asking for input and sharing decision-making. Co-creates a feasible plan that reflects the patient’s life context and supports follow-through.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and reinforces specific change talk around environmental steps with concrete commitments and planning. Missed opportunities to amplify and explore broader change language around smoking reduction and personal motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively softens sustain talk by honoring the patient’s readiness and avoiding confrontation, offering harm-reduction options instead. Redirects toward feasible protective actions without persuading or arguing.
