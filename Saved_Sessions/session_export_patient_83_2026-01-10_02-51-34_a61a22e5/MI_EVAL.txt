# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validation (e.g., opening support, nonjudgmental safety prompt) and treats the patient's concerns as legitimate and worthy of time.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening and accurate reflections of concerns and practical needs, though could use more complex reflections and greater normalization of emotions to deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on starting the statin safely and practical next steps, repeatedly steering the conversation back to medication safety, interactions, and how to respond to symptoms.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is highly collaborative—co‑constructs messages, invites the patient's input at each step, and shares decision-making throughout the encounter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and agency by offering options, affirming the patient's preferences, and avoiding pressure or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits specific behavioral intentions (e.g., nightly dosing, switching juice) but does little to draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence that would strengthen long‑term commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative process with invitations to participate (paste label, add RX#), though at times shifts into an expert informational stance rather than explicitly eliciting the patient's preferences for follow‑up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generates explicit change statements from the patient but misses opportunities to amplify, reflect, and explore importance or confidence to deepen and consolidate commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing with hesitancy, uses validation and clear information to redirect sustain talk, and offers concrete next steps; could further benefit from double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
