# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows respect and a nonjudgmental tone with normalizing reflections and a safety check, demonstrating unconditional positive regard. Could include more individualized affirmations of Dylan's strengths or context to reach the highest rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and reflective listening (e.g., empathic reflections and summaries). Empathy is weakened by a quick shift to problem-solving without deeper exploration of feelings or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on the conduct referral and concrete next steps throughout the session. The clinician consistently orients the conversation to procedural strategy and risk reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively by checking permission, offering choices, and providing editable templates in response to requests. Collaboration is limited when the clinician supplies detailed scripts without first eliciting Dylan’s own wording or preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Dylan's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing his decision-making. Repeatedly frames actions as Dylan's choice and clarifies boundaries (e.g., not legal advice).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Makes minimal effort to elicit Dylan’s values, goals, or intrinsic reasons for change, focusing instead on external, procedural assistance. Misses targeted questions and reflections that could generate internal motivation or ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some partnership through invitations to co-create documents and choices of services. Partnership is inconsistent because the clinician often becomes directive with long, expert-driven plans rather than pausing for Dylan’s input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicitation of change talk is limited to practical invitations (e.g., help making a plan) and does not probe for reasons, discrepancy, or commitment. More strategic open questions and reflections were needed to draw out Dylan’s motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk and reframes concerns neutrally, focusing on consequences rather than blame. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or subtle redirecting that could resolve denial and move toward change.
