# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard—warm opening, validations, and nonjudgmental language. Reassured and normalized the patient's concerns throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used basic reflective listening and validated anxiety well but reflections were often brief and informational. Could deepen rapport by using more complex reflections naming ambivalence and affect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, sustained focus on the vaccination decision with concrete, actionable aims (question list, role‑play, summaries). Regularly redirected the conversation toward preparatory steps for the appointment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as an equal partner by co‑creating the question list, offering wording options, and adapting to the patient's preferences. Frequently invited input and adjusted support to the patient's requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice with multiple offers of options and explicit permission to pause before consenting. Language consistently reinforced the patient's control over the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some concerns and preferences but relied heavily on providing information rather than systematically drawing out the patient's own motivations and values. Missed opportunities to follow up on emerging change talk with evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance through asking permission, offering choices, and co‑creating solutions like question lists and role‑play. Communicated respect for the patient's expertise about her experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively encouraged and reinforced emerging change talk and invited concrete steps (e.g., role‑play, question prep). At times moved quickly to education instead of pausing to draw out more of the patient's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated concerns while offering neutral information and permission, reducing sustain talk reinforcement. Could more often use targeted techniques (double‑sided reflections, reframing) to explicitly soften sustain statements.
