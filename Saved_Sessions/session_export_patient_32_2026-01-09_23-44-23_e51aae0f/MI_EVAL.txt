# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing and validating Derrick's conflict and avoiding labeling or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective listening and perspective-taking to paraphrase ambivalence and lower defensiveness, though many reflections stayed at surface content and were sometimes repetitive rather than probing deeper emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on preventing future DUIs and protecting the license, linking values to concrete action planning and near-term commitments without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in teamwork-based problem solving, inviting Derrick to shape the plan (choice of steps, drafting texts) and repeatedly checking preferences so decisions were co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly affirmed Derrick's choices, offered options and respected his control, and reinforced pragmatic compromises rather than pressuring or issuing ultimatums.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited Derrick's motivations and resources and produced concrete change statements, but missed chances to deepen intrinsic motivation (e.g., exploring emotional reasons, past successes, or using scaling questions and strategic summaries).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly adopted a collaborative stance and treated Derrick as the expert, inviting input, co-constructing solutions, and checking accuracy throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with targeted questions and reflections that led to concrete steps, though could have amplified commitment language and asked importance/confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and normalized sustain talk while redirecting to protective strategies, but used few explicit techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to strategically tip toward change.
