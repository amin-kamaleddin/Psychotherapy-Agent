# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with validations and normalizing language; could add more individualized affirmations of the client's specific strengths and past successes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and attunement to emotional tone demonstrated strong empathy; could use more complex reflections that name underlying emotions and values to deepen connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on co-parenting and actionable steps throughout the session; every intervention tied back to the target behavior and follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and adjusted suggestions to the client's concerns but the clinician largely led the session with pre-made scripts; more co-construction of wording and plans would increase collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered multiple options and asked permission to provide scripts, supporting choice; could more explicitly elicit which option the client prefers and explore limits to strengthen ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged the client's value-driven statements but did not consistently draw out additional intrinsic motivators, reasons, confidence, or past successes to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed some partnership by offering choices and tailoring suggestions, yet often adopted an expert role; partnering more in drafting language and plans would enhance shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provided concrete tools that supported change-oriented statements but did not consistently elicit or amplify the client's own reasons, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Neutral-to-direct redirection toward practical steps helped avoid reinforcing sustain talk, but missed opportunities for strategic softening (e.g., double-sided reflections and explicit normalizing of resistance).
