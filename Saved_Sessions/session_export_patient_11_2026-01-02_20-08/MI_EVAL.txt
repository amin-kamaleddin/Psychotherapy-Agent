### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [x]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Uses reflective, validating statements (“Feeling low energy but ready is a good place to begin”) and normalizes worry. Reflections are mostly surface-level; missed deeper affective exploration of “down about my body” and the frustration of “struggling to open a jar.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [ ]4 [x]5  
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on increasing activity throughout. Provides a specific, scalable plan (10 min, 3–4 days/week), elicits scheduling, and closes with concrete action steps and follow‑up (Sunday check‑in, fallback).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [x]3 [ ]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (offers choices, patient picks 9am and Mon/Wed/Fri). However, interaction is often clinician-driven with prescriptive instructions and ready-made scripts supplied without asking permission.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [x]3 [ ]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Offers options and affirms plan at times, but seldom uses explicit autonomy language or asks permission before giving advice. Recommendations are practical but often framed directly rather than as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [x]2 [ ]3 [ ]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of the patient’s internal motivations beyond initial statements. Clinician moves quickly to prescribing a plan instead of exploring why strength/energy matter or past facilitators of activity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [x]3 [ ]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Displays some co‑creation (choice of activity, wording suggestions), but frequently occupies expert role—provides multiple scripts and directive implementation steps that reduce the patient’s problem‑solving role.

**Comments:** Consolidates commitment (specific start time, fallback plan) which strengthens commitment language. Missed opportunities to elicit and amplify desire, reasons, need, and confidence with targeted open questions.
