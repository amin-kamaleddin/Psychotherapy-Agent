# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing the client's boundaries and distress. Reassures confidentiality while clearly stating safety/legal exceptions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and labels emotions and experiences, capturing both behavior and affect. Checks in about the client's preferences for disclosure, showing strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on engagement, rapport, confidentiality, and safety without pushing problem-solving. Could strengthen by summarizing and setting a tentative next-step for future sessions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint process by soliciting preferences, offering choices, and repeatedly deferring to the client's wishes. Positions the clinician as a collaborator rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports autonomy with explicit language about control and choice and honors requests for silence and no lectures. Transparently balances autonomy with confidentiality limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some gentle attempts to draw out the client's perspective but does not actively elicit intrinsic motivations, values, or goals. Missed opportunities for low-pressure questions about what matters or what they'd like to be different.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that control and expertise reside with the client, inviting them to choose pace and content. Repeatedly asks how the clinician should listen, operationalizing partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very limited elicitation of change talk, prioritizing listening and safety over promoting change. Offers a brief option to explore strategies but does not systematically evoke the client's reasons, values, or goals for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting resistance and affirms the client's boundary, reducing reinforcement of sustain talk. Could increase use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge both resistance and any implicit reasons for seeking help.
