# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client's priorities and choices in a warm, nonjudgmental way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking with timely, accurate reflections of both emotion and content that deepen the conversation and build rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on staying at school/reducing rumor impact, using questions and planning that advance concrete, measurable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner in problem-solving—co-designs the experiment, offers role-play, and defers to the client's choices about rehearsal and follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirms the client's control and choice, offers options, respects boundaries about involving adults, and reinforces ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits client-generated solutions and indicators but could more fully explore deeper values or long-term motives and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly invites the client's ideas and shares decision-making power, supporting her lead in designing the small experiment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently evokes change talk with open questions and options, though could better amplify, summarize, and use scaling to strengthen commitment and momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk and redirects to workable alternatives and safety plans, but could employ more double-sided reflections or reframing to contrast short- and long-term goals.
