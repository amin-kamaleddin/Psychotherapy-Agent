# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful of patient autonomy (e.g., “I’m here to listen and support you,” “wanting a change and owning the choice matters”). Multiple affirmations and nonjudgmental language, but responses felt somewhat procedural and not deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriate but limited empathic moves (one clear reflection and brief normalization). Reflections were accurate but sparse and lacked deeper, complex reflections of the patient’s tone and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the referral topic (piercing, oral care, and how to respond to the dentist). Asked an agenda-setting question and delivered targeted, actionable guidance without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient input and co-developed the wording and a single care tip. Generally collaborative, though clinician sometimes shifted into directive/scripted content and repeated advice rather than further co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient’s choice and tailored responses to her request for brief, firm lines and a single care tip. Avoided pressuring language and respected stated preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of patient motivations or values—one agenda-setting question only. Interaction largely provided information and scripts rather than drawing out change talk or exploring reasons for behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned as a partner by asking what the patient wanted and offering options; lines were co-developed and tested. Not fully client-led—clinician reverted to instruction/closing mode repeatedly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to elicit or amplify change talk. Patient made conditional commitments (e.g., “I’ll see someone if it gets messed up”) but clinician did not explore or strengthen these statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized the patient’s annoyance, which contained resistance. However, few strategic MI techniques (double-sided reflections, selective emphasis) were used to shift sustain talk toward change.
