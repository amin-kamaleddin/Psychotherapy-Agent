# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful tone and explicitly honored ambivalence (e.g., "You're trying, not perfect"; agreed to avoid lecturing). Affirmed efforts and normalized struggle. Continue naming specific strengths when giving feedback.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and validation of emotional cues (restlessness, homesickness implied). Reflections were mostly content-focused; could deepen feeling‑focused and amplified reflections to elicit more emotional material.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on phone use, sleep, and academic effects; moved to a specific, measurable plan (8:30 PM, 25/5 Pomodoro, phone zipped, granola bar) and set a follow‑up. Strong use of scaling to move toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited patient input and co‑created the plan ("Which of those sounds most doable?"); negotiated follow‑up style and respected patient limits. Power was shared throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Emphasized choice and allowed patient to set strategy and confidence level; accepted lower confidence without pressure and reframed efforts as small wins. Explicitly honored patient's preferences for follow‑up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited reasons for change and patient-generated strategies (timers, Pomodoro, rewards). Missed opportunities to probe longer‑term values/goals that would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated patient as expert in her life, adjusted plan to fit her preferences, and incorporated her requests for the check‑in wording/style. Demonstrated clear power‑sharing and mutual planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced several change statements (tired in lectures, grades slipped, agreed to try Pomodoro). Could more actively amplify and sequence DARN‑C language (e.g., link improved energy/grades to values and longer‑term goals).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and offered alternatives that respect the behavior's function (connection/relaxation). Could use more double‑sided reflections to validate sustain talk while steering toward change talk.
