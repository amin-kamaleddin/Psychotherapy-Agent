# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Dante’s experience, repeatedly acknowledging honesty and affirming small efforts throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and timely reflective listening, validating feelings and practical constraints while mirroring Dante’s concerns about energy and schedule.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing brief physical activity and effectively narrows ambivalence into specific, actionable steps (days/times, reminders, fallbacks).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session is team-oriented—clinician solicits preferences and adapts suggestions—but the clinician often drafts materials rather than inviting Dante to co-write them, limiting client-generated content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering multiple options, saying 'no pressure,' and leaving final decisions to Dante while affirming his control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation (past routines, energy patterns) and invites input on feasible plans, but relies more on structured suggestions than on evocative questions about values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language and invites co-creation of plans, yet the clinician-led drafting of reminders reduces opportunities for full power-sharing in shaping the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encourages change language and small commitments with micro-habit options and scheduling, but could better elicit Dante’s own ideas and deepen his expressed reasons and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes missed sessions as data with helpful fallbacks, though it would benefit from more intentional MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further soften sustain talk.
