# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and affirmed Marcus's strengths while normalizing his experience. No evidence of labeling or pressure; a brief explicit normalization of common emotional reactions could be added.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provided accurate, timely reflections of both logistical concerns and affect, matching Marcus's reserved, practical style and keeping him engaged. Occasionally could deepen reflections by naming underlying emotions more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on blood sugar monitoring with progressive, concrete planning (cue, storage, alarms, backups) and a scheduled follow-up. Directional control was strong without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner using inclusive language, soliciting preferences, and co-constructing the plan based on Marcus's ideas. Adapted suggestions to his privacy needs and avoided imposing a single solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice, asked permission before offering ideas, offered multiple options, and emphasized experimentation (one-week trial), respecting Marcus's boundaries. This preserved control and reduced pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation by linking prior success and using scaling questions, surfacing practical supports and reasons to try. Could be strengthened by deeper exploration of intrinsic values and personally salient reasons for monitoring.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the interaction as collaborative (permission, brainstorming, checking preferences) and shared power in decision-making. The plan was genuinely co-created and centered Marcus's expertise about his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (med adherence link, scaling, one-week commitment) but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen Marcus's own voiced reasons. More open invitations and strategic reflections would increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized ambivalence, and redirected to small, manageable steps and contingency plans. Responses validated concerns and kept the conversation change-focused without confronting hesitancy.
