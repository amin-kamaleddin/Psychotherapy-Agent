# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, using nonjudgmental language and affirming the patient's limits. The patient explicitly acknowledges and appreciates the brief, nonjudgmental approach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, picking up on constraints and guardedness. Could deepen by exploring underlying emotions or the meaning of 'unwind' and other affective nuances.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking and guides the interaction toward a concrete, measurable plan (skip Thursday mid‑afternoon, coping strategies, 7 PM check‑in). Resists drift and keeps the session goal-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner by offering options, asking the patient's preference, and adapting supports to the patient's requests (short check‑in, blunt phrasing). The plan is clearly co‑created and patient-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice throughout, asking permission before offering materials and emphasizing the patient's control over change. Reinforces that the patient is choosing a small, voluntary experiment rather than being coerced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivation and resources (confidence ruler, problem‑solving, coping options) but mostly draws pragmatic reasons rather than deep values-based motives. Could increase evocation by asking about personal values or long-term hopes to strengthen internal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites input and shares decision-making, asking which option is doable and soliciting the patient's preferred check‑in format/time. The clinician adapts recommendations to the patient's life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits specific change talk and a concrete commitment (Thursday skip, confidence=6) using choice questions and a confidence ruler. Could further amplify and summarize the patient's change language to build firmer commitment and elicit more internal reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes resistance into a manageable experiment, normalizing sustain talk and focusing on a small step. Could add more double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides and gently soften sustain talk.
