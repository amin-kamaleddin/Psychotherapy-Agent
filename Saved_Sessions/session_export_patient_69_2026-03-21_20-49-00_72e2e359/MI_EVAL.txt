# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful from the opening line, validating frustration and honoring ambivalence without labeling or confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, concise reflections that captured frustration and practical concerns, but did not consistently probe deeper emotions or personal meaning beneath being 'stuck.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on obtaining steady paid work and concrete barriers, consistently steering the session toward actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner by soliciting preferences, co-creating the checklist, and tailoring materials to the client's ZIP and document status.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, offered options, asked permission, and respected the client's control without pressuring or imposing actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked about what 'not stuck' would look like but leaned heavily on information-giving and logistical mapping rather than eliciting intrinsic motives, importance, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly fostered shared decision-making by offering choices, checking preferences, and responding to explicit requests, reinforcing collaborative momentum.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened opportunities for change talk and elicited some concrete goals, but then moved into planning instead of systematically amplifying or reinforcing the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward small, doable steps while validating qualifiers, though more double-sided reflections could further soften resistance.
