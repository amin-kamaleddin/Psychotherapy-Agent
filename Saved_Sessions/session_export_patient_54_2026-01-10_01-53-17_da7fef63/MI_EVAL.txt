# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and offering options rather than blaming. This conveyed genuine positive regard while addressing risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and validation of ambivalence with accurate reflections of values and conflict. Could deepen emotion-focused empathy by explicitly reflecting defensiveness and underlying anxiety before moving to information-giving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy with every intervention tied to reduction/abstinence. The session moves logically from elicitation to concrete planning and coping strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works with the patient as an equal partner, repeatedly checking preferences and adapting materials (checklist, scripts, prompts) to her requests. Co-constructs the plan and adjusts language based on patient input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently acknowledges choice and frames suggestions as options, asking permission for some supports. At times more directive language and repeated medical consensus statements reduce the sense of full autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivations and links values (healthy baby) to concrete steps, drawing out readiness to act. Could increase open questions to amplify Isabella’s own reasons and confidence rather than relying on information provision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and co-creates the plan, checking back and tailoring to the patient’s style. Occasionally slips into an expert stance by asserting consensus and offering directives without always asking permission.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk through specific planning and questions about amount and willingness to try a week. Would be stronger with more follow-ups to amplify desire, reasons, and confidence (e.g., asking what makes a healthy baby important or how confident she feels).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to safety and planning, but does so in a more didactic way. Missed opportunities for MI-style softening (double-sided reflections, affirming skepticism) and risked arguing against sustain talk by repeatedly stating consensus.
