# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established clear unconditional positive regard (e.g., opening statement, repeated reassurances) and avoided blame, conveying consistent warmth and respect throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the patient's ambivalence and perspective, but some reflections were brief and the clinician could have deepened emotional content with more complex, feeling‑laden reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a focused, consistent agenda on reducing children’s exposure/visibility of smoking and progressed toward concrete, practical steps and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as two‑person problem solving with strong power‑sharing (asking the patient to choose a plan), though the clinician sometimes offered many solutions rather than eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently honored the patient's choice and control (opening support statement, asking permission before advice, respecting 'not planning to quit'), supporting autonomy throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used solid evocation strategies (eliciting reasons, confidence ruler, asking for a plan), but could probe deeper into internal motives and values to generate stronger self‑directed change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance was fostered (permission before suggestions, inviting patient choice) and the plan was largely patient‑driven, yet partnership could be strengthened by eliciting the patient's brainstorm before offering options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and amplified change talk with concrete planning and a confidence ruler, but missed opportunities to use more evocative questions to deepen desire, reasons, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not argued with; clinician used double‑sided reflections and shifted to harm reduction, though strategic summaries or value‑focused prompts could further soften sustain talk.
