# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized Marcus's experience, and avoided judgment, creating safety for disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and paraphrases were accurate and timely, capturing Marcus's ambivalence and concerns and deepening his sense of being heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on asthma‑related behaviors and practical, actionable steps with no drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Marcus into planning (co‑drafting the note), checked preferences often, and integrated his choices, modeling genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice language, permission‑seeking, and offers of options reinforced Marcus's control and ownership over decisions and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician drew out Marcus's motives and values (family, dog, avoiding ER) to guide options, but could have probed deeper for values, past successes, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language like 'let's figure this out together' and invitations to choose options positioned Marcus as the primary decision‑maker and shared power effectively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation of small, doable steps and conversion into a concrete plan occurred, though the clinician could have amplified Marcus's change language and asked for commitment or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided strengthening sustain talk and redirected to feasible alternatives (dog‑free bedroom), but missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections and deeper problem‑solving around financial barriers.
