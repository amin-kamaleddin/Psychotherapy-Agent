# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with normalization, affirmations, and respect for the patient's preferences (e.g., not involving spouse). No blaming or shaming and consistent positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally good reflective listening and perspective-taking, but an early misattribution about stress reduced attunement before the clinician corrected course. Reflections thereafter matched the patient's preferences and routine.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear behavioral focus on lowering BP by reducing one evening drink, with consistent questions tied to concrete implementation and measurement. Direction was sustained without confrontation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as a joint effort with frequent solicitation of the patient's input, but the clinician often offered narrow, structured options which limited entirely spontaneous idea generation. Overall collaborative but somewhat clinician-guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed choice and honored preferences (e.g., offering options, respecting wish not to involve spouse) and avoided pressuring or prescribing. Language emphasized the patient's freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong elicitation of the patient's own motivations, strengths, and plans, converting them into specific actions (cues, tracking, substitutes). The clinician drew out intrinsic reasons and past success to support change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent prompts invited the patient's decision-making and co‑construction of the plan, but offering specific choices sometimes steered the process. Partnership was solid but intermittently clinician-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk with concrete options, reflections, links to past success, and prompting for implementation intentions and tracking. The clinician reinforced desire, ability, and commitment language effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected minimization toward a small, nonthreatening change, which softened sustain talk; however, missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or strategic reflection of sustain talk to more explicitly explore ambivalence.
