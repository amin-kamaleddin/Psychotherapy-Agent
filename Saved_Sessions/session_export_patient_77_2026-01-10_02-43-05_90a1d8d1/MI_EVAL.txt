# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating the patient's experience and checking safety respectfully.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the patient's frustration and loneliness and were used repeatedly and timely to deepen disclosure and rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on weight/fitness and eating, moving efficiently from exploration to a concrete, actionable plan and a scheduled first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed plans, asked preferences, and adapted recommendations to context, though occasional expert-driven information reduced the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices and small-step options were offered and autonomy was reinforced, but some prescriptive language lacked an explicit permission/elicitation step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some elicitation of commitments and choice, but the clinician relied more on expert solutions and missed opportunities to draw out the patient's own values, reasons, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally fostered a collaborative stance by asking preferences and co-creating supports, yet at times shifted into a more directive expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited change talk through choices and a small-goal plan and secured a commitment, but did not consistently amplify or deepen desire, ability, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced; the clinician normalized plateaus and offered nonjudgmental explanations and practical steps, though more strategic reflections (e.g., double-sided) could have further softened sustain talk.
