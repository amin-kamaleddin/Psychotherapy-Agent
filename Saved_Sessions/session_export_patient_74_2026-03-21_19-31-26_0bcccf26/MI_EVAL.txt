# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalized ambivalence and offered individualized validations and acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking and invited emotional exploration, but some reflections were brief and the clinician often moved quickly to practical suggestions rather than deepening feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking and role modeling and repeatedly returned to the core conflict, though the clinician sometimes shifted between information and values without summarizing or negotiating a single progressive goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used collaborative moves (inviting topic choice, co-creating scripts, rehearsing breathing) but frequently initiated content and supplied many strategies rather than eliciting the client's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Linda's control and choices, avoided pressuring language, and framed options as optional and client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation (e.g., asking about being a role model) but relied heavily on offering ideas and missed opportunities to draw out intrinsic reasons, past successes, confidence, and use of scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displayed elements of partnership but often took an expert role with concrete suggestions and scripts, reducing chances for co-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generated moments of client change talk around role modeling but did not consistently amplify or reflect those statements nor explore confidence or past attempts before offering solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized sustain talk while offering harm‑reduction strategies to reduce exposure; however, abundant practical tips could be seen as tacit acceptance, though appropriate for the client's stage.
