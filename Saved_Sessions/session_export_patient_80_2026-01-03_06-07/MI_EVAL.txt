# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and normalized struggle (e.g., “you’re not defined by what’s happened”; “Those small changes show real care and strength”). At least 3 clear acceptance statements. Suggestion: add occasional explicit normalization about slips (e.g., “slips are common…”) to further reduce shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking throughout (“That sounds heavy and scary”; reflections of the “heat of the moment” and practical needs). ≥4 complex reflections. Suggestion: deepen affective reflections when Luka expresses fear of being laughed at to further validate emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing drinking and managing anger, repeatedly moved session toward concrete in‑the‑moment steps and narrowing to a single actionable line. 5–6 explicit directional moves. Suggestion: close sessions with a brief summary of agreed plan and next steps (who will do what tonight; confidence rating).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited co‑design of the plan (“Let’s figure this out together”), honored Luka’s preferences, and framed the plan as his experiment. ≥5 collaborative invitations. Suggestion: continue prefacing suggestions with permission when offering options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice and control (“experiment you control”), repeatedly asked what Luka would be willing to try and what felt safe. ≥4 autonomy‑supporting moves. Suggestion: elicit a client-stated commitment in Luka’s own words to translate autonomy into a voluntary plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Luka’s solutions and wording (e.g., “I need five,” stress ball, timer, exact phrasing for partner). Multiple strong evocation attempts (≥5). Suggestion: after eliciting solutions, amplify change talk by linking actions to values and desired outcomes (e.g., “If this works, what would that mean for your relationship?”).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tone and content reflected genuine partnership—co‑authored plan, offered to craft wording together, repeatedly sought Luka’s input. ≥5 partnership instances. Suggestion: consider inviting partner into a joint discussion or role‑play (with consent) when appropriate.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited multiple change statements (cutting down, “I’ll try it tonight,” rehearsing line) and reflected them, but missed some opportunities to amplify into firm commitment and confidence ratings. Change talk present but not consistently converted to commitment. Suggestion: use a confidence/importance ruler and ask for a one‑sentence commitment (e.g., “Can you say in one sentence what you’ll do tonight?”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and effectively reframed sustain elements (e.g., “I’m not promising”), redirecting toward experimentation. Several effective redirects (≥3) but limited use of double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence. Suggestion: employ double‑sided reflections and strategic summaries to further soften sustain talk and elicit change talk.
