# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and nonjudgmental regard with validating language and normalizing statements, but some responses feel slightly formulaic and could show more individualized warmth or explicit unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that likely helped the patient feel heard, but rarely probes deeper emotional nuance or uses affective reflections to name fears or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on cholesterol management and the medication versus behavior-change decision, consistently tying discussion to concrete, measurable actions and follow-up plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner throughout, soliciting the patient's input at each decision point and co-constructing a realistic plan that reflects the patient's constraints and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice by offering options and asking what the patient prefers, avoiding pressure and framing decisions as the patient's to make while agreeing to reassess together.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's ideas and practical plans, but focuses more on logistics than exploring intrinsic motives, values, or confidence that would deepen motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the interaction as collaborative, deferring to the patient's knowledge of his life and repeatedly asking what feels most doable to co-create the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete, doable steps and short-term experiments that produced change talk, but does not consistently amplify or chain tentative commitments into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward testing alternatives and action planning, though could use more active MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections, exploring costs) to further soften sustain talk.
