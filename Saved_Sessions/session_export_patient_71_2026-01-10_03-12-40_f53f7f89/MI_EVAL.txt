# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing struggles and acknowledging strengths without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking with accurate, often complex reflections that capture both content and feeling, keeping the client understood and engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the concrete target behavior (five-minute morning practice) and progressively narrows to cues, prep, and fallback options without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed—clinician repeatedly asks for input, builds on the client's language, and integrates her ideas into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirms choice and agency with invitational, nonpressuring language and offers options drawn from the client's own preferences while respecting limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits values, reasons, strengths, and desired outcomes, but could more consistently deepen intrinsic motivation by eliciting scaling or explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly conveys that expertise resides with the client and co-creates solutions through open invitations and collaborative problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively notices and builds on change talk and elicits concrete implementation details, though could better amplify strong change statements and elicit commitment/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and validates sustain talk without confrontation but tends to mirror it rather than strategically soften it using double-sided reflections, reframes, or eliciting counter-arguments.
