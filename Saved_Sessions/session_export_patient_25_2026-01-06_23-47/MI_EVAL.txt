# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with repeated affirmations and no blame, validating the patient's efforts and agency throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening—ties drinking to the patient's values, paraphrases plans, and acknowledges likely barriers and feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use and moves the conversation toward specific, actionable steps (drink limit, alternating drinks, alarm, leaving time) without getting sidetracked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuine collaborative partnership by inviting patient choices, building on the patient's ideas, and offering to practice strategies together rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's sense of choice and control through scaling questions, offering options, and asking which strategies feel most doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivation using importance/confidence scales and by linking past success (quitting smoking) to current capability, drawing reasons for change from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative and client-centered—plans are co-created, the clinician checks preferences, and power is shared in shaping the implementation plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk with scaling and concrete planning, but could more strategically amplify and selectively reflect the patient's change language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to problem-solving, yet seldom uses double-sided reflections or explicit normalization of social belonging before pivoting to change strategies.
