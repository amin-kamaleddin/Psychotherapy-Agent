# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and multiple affirmations (“Thanks for sharing…,” “I’m proud of you”). Avoided labels/moralizing. Some praise felt formulaic and was offered immediately after ambivalent statements, which could feel premature or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked about triggers and offered brief reflection early, but missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of affect (nervous laughter, anxiety about failing) and exploration of the emotional meaning of gambling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained strong focus on gambling/debt and concrete behavior change. Offered a clear menu of options, role‑played responses, summarized a specific plan repeatedly, and set a one‑week check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient input (“Which feels most doable?”), provided options and role‑play. However, clinician frequently supplied direct recommendations and repeated the plan verbatim, which shifted some control back to the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally invited choice and avoided coercion; affirmed patient decisions. Repeated praise and directive recaps slightly undercut autonomy by steering rather than fully co‑creating pace and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change talk (goals, intentions, specific steps). Could strengthen evocation by probing importance/confidence and linking values to behavior (e.g., deeper follow‑up on Egypt goal).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned as a partner via collaborative language, options, and role‑play. At times reverted to an expert/teacher voice by listing steps without first eliciting patient’s own summary or rationale.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (cutting card, giving Mum card, taking $40). Missed opportunities to amplify change talk, elicit importance/confidence, and obtain stronger commitment language beyond immediate steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient expressed sustain talk (“just a bit of cash,” “not promising I won’t cave”). Clinician moved quickly to solutions and did not explicitly use double‑sided reflections or other strategies to normalize and work through ambivalence.
