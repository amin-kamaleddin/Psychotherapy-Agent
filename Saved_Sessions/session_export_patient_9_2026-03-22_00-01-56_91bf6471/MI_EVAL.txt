# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing Amina’s feelings and honoring her priorities and constraints. No blaming or minimizing remarks were used, supporting a strong therapeutic stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, sustained empathic listening with accurate reflections capturing both content and feeling. The clinician repeatedly mirrored the patient’s emotional language and checked understanding before moving on.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (bedtime routine) and steered toward feasible implementation steps and short-term goals. The session avoided drift and emphasized measurable outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a true partnership with iterative co‑construction of the plan and explicit invitations to brainstorm. The clinician asked, listened, and built on the patient’s contributions rather than assuming the expert role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the client’s autonomy by offering options and asking which felt most doable, framing experiments as time‑limited. Language affirmed Amina’s right to choose and preserved her agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out intrinsic motives and values (love for son, desire for safety and calm) that fueled change talk. Could strengthen by systematically exploring confidence, discrepancy, and eliciting more explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and client‑led, with the clinician soliciting Amina’s ideas and preferences and empowering her choices. The clinician acknowledged the patient’s lived wisdom and co‑created the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, produced concrete change language and labeled strengths to amplify motivation. Could further amplify and consolidate change statements, elicit confidence/commitment explicitly, and deepen DARN follow‑ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reoriented to practical, manageable steps while normalizing the tug between priorities. Would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and explicit reframing of likely objections to further reduce defensiveness.
