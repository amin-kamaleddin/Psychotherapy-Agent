# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, normalizing concerns and offering affirmations and safety checks throughout the visit. There were clear displays of positive regard and no blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used reflective prompts and summaries and showed understanding of the teen's situation, but missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflections about underlying worries or values. More complex reflections naming feelings or meanings would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol exposure and practical harm-reduction (refusal lines, exit plan, pickup text) from start to finish. The clinician consistently brought the conversation back to concrete, behavior-focused goals appropriate for a brief intervention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely collaborative—patient chose lines and co-drafted the pickup text—but the clinician sometimes supplied many scripted options instead of eliciting the teen's own phrasing first. Allowing more client-generated content before offering scripts would increase collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choices, asked which options were most helpful, and affirmed the patient's right to choose, reinforcing ownership of decisions. There was no pressuring language and options were tailored to the patient's preference.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-generated responses were elicited (choice of line, planning the text), but the approach was primarily informational and skills-based rather than drawing out the teen's own values or intrinsic reasons. More evocative questions and amplifying any spontaneous change talk would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally worked with the patient to select and refine strategies, demonstrating partnership in problem-solving. At times the clinician leaned more directive by offering many scripted options before eliciting the patient's own language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change-oriented language (refusal choice, safety planning) but did not systematically evoke the patient's own reasons, desire, ability, or commitment (DARN-C). More targeted questions about importance, confidence, and personal reasons would amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to refusal practice, but briefly voiced pushy peer lines without explicitly framing them as role-play, which could normalize sustain arguments. Clear framing and use of double-sided reflections would better soften sustain talk.
