# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician shows clear unconditional positive regard and normalizes Erik's reactions with a consistently nonjudgmental tone. Reassuring language and respectful reflections communicate strong acceptance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking with accurate, specific reflections that capture ambivalence and sustain talk. Multiple complex and concise reflections show solid understanding of Erik's viewpoint and feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on concrete behavior changes (meal swap, tracking, alcohol monitoring) and moves logically from values to small steps. Questions and reflections consistently steer the session toward measurable goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reads as genuine joint problem-solving, repeatedly inviting Erik's input on what fits his shifts and how to remember notes. Language and choices given let Erik shape the plan, fostering equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, leaving decisions to Erik, and affirming modest, self-directed goals. Clinician avoids coercion and reinforces personal agency throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Erik's own reasons and values (family, work, energy) and helps translate them into specific, meaningful measures. Questions and prompts draw motivation from Erik rather than imposing external arguments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly collaborative and respectful of Erik's expertise, asking which options fit his shifts and preferences. Occasionally offers discrete options rather than eliciting more of Erik's own strategies or past successes, so partnership could be deepened.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with open questions, manageable options, and invitations to commit to concrete steps. Clinician deepens commitment by asking what signs would show the change is working.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reflects sustain talk without arguing, and redirects toward low-effort change which is effective. Missed opportunities for more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to explicitly pair sustain and change and highlight discrepancy.
