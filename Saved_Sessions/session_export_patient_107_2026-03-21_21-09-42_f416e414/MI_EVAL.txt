# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, affirming the patient’s priorities; could soften some directive instructions by prefacing with permission statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening that accurately mirrors feelings and priorities; could deepen empathy by exploring the emotional values behind the ritual more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on reducing second‑cup caffeine with a measurable, testable experiment and concrete benchmarks; should continue balancing this direction with eliciting the patient’s own motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is co‑created with frequent invitations for patient input and incorporation of their definitions and contingencies; clinician avoids an authoritarian stance and offers suggestions tentatively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice by framing the experiment as voluntary and reversible and offering options for reminders and duration; continuing to ask permission before advice would maximize autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and barriers using scaling and preference questions, but sometimes shifts quickly into solutions rather than pausing to further draw out and amplify the patient’s own motivational language or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership and power‑sharing: clinician negotiates benchmarks, asks for acceptability, and incorporates patient preferences into the plan; maintain vigilance to keep suggestions tentative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and supports change talk (scaling, small steps, commitment), but could better amplify and reflect patient‑generated change statements before moving rapidly to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk with double‑sided reflections and avoids arguing; could further reduce sustain talk by using more strategic double‑sided pivots that explicitly name both sides then invite the change side to expand.
