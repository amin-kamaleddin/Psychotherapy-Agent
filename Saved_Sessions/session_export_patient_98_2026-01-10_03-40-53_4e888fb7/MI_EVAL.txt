# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalizing and validating Mateo's experience and checking safety in a calm, respectful way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and mirrored the patient's language about frustration and pain, but reflections stayed mostly content-level and sometimes moved quickly to solutions instead of deepening emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing safe physical activity with concrete, stepwise plans (4×30s walk, stop rules, pre/post strategies) and no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed interaction as joint problem-solving and solicited patient preferences, yet the clinician often led with multiple prescriptive suggestions, making the balance somewhat clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently asked permission, offered options, respected patient choices, and reinforced that small self-directed goals (e.g., ‘I tried’) are legitimate successes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited behavioral commitment and a concrete plan but relied more on offering solutions than exploring Mateo's internal motivations, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used collaborative language and invited input, but an expert/problem-solver stance with many directives limited opportunities for Mateo to generate his own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited specific change talk and commitments (willingness, timing, steps), but did not consistently probe deeper reasons, values, or confidence and could amplify change language more deliberately.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk and set safety boundaries, but frequently moved into problem-solving and advice rather than using MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, selectively amplifying change talk) to soften sustain talk.
