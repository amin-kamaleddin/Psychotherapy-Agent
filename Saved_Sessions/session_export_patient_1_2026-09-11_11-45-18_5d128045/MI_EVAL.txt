# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental tone and acknowledgment of Ahmed’s perspective were consistent, but explicit affirmations or normalization of ambivalence were limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking about schedule and burden, though more complex reflections to surface deeper emotions or values would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on mini-cigar use and throat/gum irritation with every question and reflection tied to concrete behavioral options and a clear short-term plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear joint problem-solving throughout: the clinician invited Ahmed to co-design the monitoring plan and repeatedly asked for his preferences to shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, asking what fits Ahmed’s life, and accepting his decision to monitor without pressure or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical ideas and manageable change options, but missed opportunities to probe deeper motivations, values, or confidence to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the conversation as a collaboration and deferred to Ahmed’s judgment about what would fit his life, demonstrating genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified tentative change language and specific first steps, yet did not sufficiently explore underlying motives or amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected focus to low-burden actions, though using double-sided reflections could more actively pivot sustain talk toward change.
