# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing Malik's feelings and conveying unconditional positive regard. These behaviors reduce shame and create a safe, supportive atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections, validation, and open prompts that show understanding and invite Malik's story. Occasionally moves quickly into problem-solving instead of exploring emotional nuance more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on substance use and the parent–teen conflict and moves toward a concrete one‑month plan. Could improve pacing by evoking motivation more before shifting into planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Often solicits Malik's preferences, adapts language, and negotiates compromises, creating a joint effort. At times the clinician supplies ready-made scripts instead of fully co-constructing content from Malik's own phrasing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges Malik's choice, asks permission before offering scripts, and offers options that respect his boundaries. Occasional detailed scripts and prescriptive instructions could feel directive and undermine autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some awareness that motivation should come from Malik and asks about desired differences, but relies heavily on advice and scripting. Elicitative strategies (scaling, elaboration) were underused and change talk remained minimal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative through role‑play offers, coaching options, and negotiation with the parent in mind. Partnership is weakened when the clinician provides expert-driven wording instead of co-creating it with Malik.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to draw out change talk (questions about differences, calm conversation, role‑play) but often supplies language and solutions instead of eliciting Malik's own DARN statements. Missed opportunities to reflect and amplify small change expressions to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and does not reinforce sustain talk, which is appropriate, but misses opportunities for double-sided reflections and selectively highlighting the costs of continued use. The clinician frequently shifts to pragmatic solutions rather than strategically softening sustain talk.
