# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys nonjudgmental regard and normalizes Jordan’s feelings, praising effort and avoiding blame to communicate warmth and acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections and perspective-taking match Jordan’s guilt, anxiety, and ambivalence, with summaries and validations that demonstrate strong empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on shoplifting and preventing recidivism, with targeted questions about triggers and a concrete, actionable plan tied to the goal of avoiding reoffending.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician and client co-construct the plan through joint problem-solving, role-play, and drafting messages, using inclusive language and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple options are offered and commitments are invited rather than imposed, with explicit permission to decline supports and framing that preserves Jordan’s choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits concrete change language and practical commitments but misses opportunities to draw out deeper intrinsic motives, values, and scaled importance/confidence questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician fosters client expertise and power-sharing by asking which steps Jordan wants, offering choices, and inviting his input on wording and supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is effectively elicited and reinforced through choices and affirmations, though the clinician could further amplify desire/ability/reason/need language and use evocative questions to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged (peer pull, desire for items) but not deeply explored or reframed; the clinician moves quickly to solutions without using double-sided reflections or examining short-term gains.
