# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys respect and positive regard, validating the patient's choices and preserving dignity. No judgmental or minimizing language used throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking with accurate reflections and paraphrases of the patient's concerns. Could deepen empathic responding by naming and exploring the emotional texture (fear, exhaustion) more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the concrete target behavior (a symptom-and-stress diary and how to present it to the GP). Questions and reflections repeatedly return to implementation details and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is strongly collaborative—clinician offers options, asks for input, and frames help as supportive tools rather than directives. The patient’s choices consistently shape the session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, checks what fits the patient's energy, and preserves her right to pursue tests if still concerned. Final decisions are left to the patient, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations, benefits of change, and practical implementation ideas (template, son support). Could further strengthen by adding confidence/importance scaling or a brief commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames decisions as the patient's and invites preferences at every step, fostering shared decision-making. Offers of sketches or tips are presented as collaborative support rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk with open questions, concrete planning, and exploring expected benefits. Would benefit from amplifying change language and eliciting stronger commitment/confidence (e.g., scaling or explicit commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and accommodates the patient's worry by offering a plan that preserves testing options. Could more actively soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections and gentle exploration of costs/benefits of continued medical searching.
