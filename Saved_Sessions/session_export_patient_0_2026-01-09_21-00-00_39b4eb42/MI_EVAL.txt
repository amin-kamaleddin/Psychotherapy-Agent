# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicated unconditional positive regard and repeatedly normalized ambivalence, reducing defensiveness; no labeling, criticism, or shaming was present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking captured Adrian's ambivalence and encouraged elaboration, but there were missed opportunities to probe deeper feelings of shame and secrecy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on safer methamphetamine use and harm-reduction, consistently returning to concrete, achievable steps and implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician solicited Adrian's ideas, built on them, and iteratively refined the plan together, demonstrating reciprocal, equal-partner problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician validated Adrian's readiness level and offered options rather than directives, supporting his sense of choice and agency in deciding next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations, strengths, and concrete strategies and linked them to past routines, but could have probed deeper values or elicited stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative stance treating Adrian as the expert; the plan (jacket check, wallet backup) was clearly co-created based on his preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (own straw, smaller lines) and linked it to values, but stopped short of consolidating stronger commitment or using scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and reframed toward safer alternatives, though more double-sided reflections or summary reflections could further reduce sustain language.
