# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly acknowledges the patient's feelings and concerns. Could more explicitly normalize ambivalence and affirm the patient's strengths/values to reach the highest level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and validates anxiety about pregnancy while asking relevant questions. Reflections stay mainly at the content level—more complex, affective reflections would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy and moves the session toward concrete, actionable steps while addressing medical safety. The interaction stays on-topic and progresses toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaves largely as a cooperative partner by asking preferences, tailoring materials, and offering role-play or scripts. Occasionally slips into an expert information-delivery mode instead of consistently eliciting the patient's solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently acknowledges choice and offers options, asking permission before plans and supporting self-direction. Some definitive, prescriptive language (e.g., 'the safest choice is to stop now') could be softened with permission statements and clearer reinforcement of the patient's control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and builds on the patient's motivations and translates values into practical actions (NA drinks, partner support). Could use more targeted, evocative questions and reflections to deepen and amplify the patient's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—offers options, tailors materials, and invites input on a plan, conveying that the patient's preferences matter. At times the clinician assumes an expert role with guideline-focused directives rather than co-creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits change-oriented statements and offers concrete strategies that moved the patient toward preparation. Could more frequently reflect, amplify, and ask targeted DARN questions to strengthen and deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue and provides neutral corrective information but often uses directive, prescriptive phrasing that may trigger resistance. Would benefit from double-sided reflections, reflective summaries validating reasons for drinking, and eliciting the patient's own arguments for change.
