# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, validating Maria's concerns and avoiding judgment; framed the trial as cautious and under her control.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and summarized ambivalence well; could have probed or reflected deeper emotional tone (irritation/fear) more often.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on inhaler use with each exchange tied to specific, achievable behaviors and steps from planning to commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—offered options, asked preferences, and co‑created the plan (alarm label, pairing with coffee, checklist), with decisions shaped by Maria.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Maria's choice and control by framing the test as hers to stop or continue and by offering options rather than pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Maria's reasons and signs of benefit (less chest tightness) and what helps her stick with behaviors, but did not deeply explore values or use scaling to assess motivation strength.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co‑constructed: clinician asked what would work, deferred to Maria's preferences, and incorporated her ideas into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk through a concrete, small experiment and specific plans; could have amplified commitment and explored pros/values further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing caution and framing a test, though more explicit double‑sided reflections could further tip toward change while honoring resistance.
