# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly and explicitly shows unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (e.g., “I accept where you are…”, “I accept all of that”). Patient responds that hearing this helps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captures content and emotion (“torn between safety/support and keeping your freedom,” “that’s a heavy, painful place to be”); reflections deepen rapport and patient validates them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on target behaviors (alcohol, violence prevention, living arrangements, reunification) and logical progression from values → options → specific plan (trial week, daily check‑ins, text schedule).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work consistently framed as joint (“Let’s figure this out together”), clinician invites patient choices and co‑creates the plan rather than prescribing it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledges conflicting values and supports patient choice (“both matter and both are true,” questions about which option feels realistic), avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent elicitation of patient motives and reasons for change (e.g., asking what matters most, what would make staying sober possible); patient repeatedly voices change talk that clinician builds on.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician emphasizes mutual expertise and shared decision‑making (invites patient to set specifics of check‑ins, trial week parameters), keeping power shared.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician elicits, reflects, and specifies change talk (turning “I’ll do anything to get my kids back” into concrete commitments like a sober trial week and timed texts/calls).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized (double‑sided reflections about fear of “signing away” freedom) and redirected into planning, but clinician occasionally repeats sustain‑language in ways that could keep the sustain side active longer than necessary.
