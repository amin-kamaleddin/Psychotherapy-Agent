# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validated limits, and thanked the patient for safety disclosure. Avoided blaming or pressuring around medication.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and affect recognition with an empathic tone, but reflections were mostly surface-level. Missed opportunities to deepen empathy by naming underlying feelings or the function of humor/deflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on sleep once the patient chose that agenda and moved efficiently to concrete behavior-change planning. Summarized the plan and provided a usable sleep log and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and co-constructed, with options offered and joint creation of a modest, achievable sleep plan. Invited patient choices for wake time, reward, and check-in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported the patient's autonomy throughout by offering choices, asking permission, and framing recommendations as options. Reinforced the patient's right to set the agenda and decide on commitment level.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete willingness to try sleep strategies and short-term commitment, but did not sufficiently explore deeper personal motives or values tied to change. Limited evocation regarding the referral issue of medication adherence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed a collaborative stance and involved the patient in decisions, frequently asking permission before advising. Could more explicitly name the patient as the expert and invite more patient-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced specific, implementable change talk around sleep (wake time, trial, reward, follow-up). Missed opportunities to deepen change talk by asking about personal reasons or values for better sleep.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with resistance about medication and avoided arguing, offering alternative topics instead of pressing the issue. Did not consistently use strategies like double-sided reflections or exploring costs of staying the same when the topic was briefly avoided.
