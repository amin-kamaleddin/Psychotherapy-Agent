# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, thanks and affirms the patient, and conducts a calm safety check; however, interaction feels somewhat formulaic with few individualized validations of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows basic empathic stance and follows the patient's lead at times, but reflections are sparse and mostly superficial, missing opportunities to mirror deeper feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and its impact on sports/grades throughout, with logical progression from disclosure to assessment to planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engages the patient in interactive role-play and choice-making, treating them as an active participant, though a clinician-driven plan somewhat reduces equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options and asks the patient's preferences which supports choice, but could strengthen autonomy by more explicitly affirming the patient's right to decide and avoiding prescriptive lists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some immediate, actionable motivations and commitment language, but leans toward offering solutions instead of systematically drawing out the patient's own values, reasons, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites collaboration at key moments (permission, choices, role-play), but overall the session is clinician-led and prescriptive, limiting co-creation of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and strengthens change-relevant language through goal specification and rehearsal, yet misses chances to probe deeper reasons, values, and confidence to amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Redirects sustain talk toward harm-reduction strategies without reinforcing it, but fails to explicitly acknowledge or normalize the patient's competing desires or use double-sided reflections to soften resistance.
