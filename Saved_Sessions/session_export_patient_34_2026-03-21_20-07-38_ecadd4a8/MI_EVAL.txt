# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated unconditional positive regard and a consistently nonjudgmental, validating tone that supported rapport with a defensive client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections and summaries showed good perspective-taking, though deeper affect-labeling and more complex reflections to capture ambivalence were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on the conduct referral and steered toward practical next steps (statement drafting, rehearsal, and deadlines).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and co-constructed—clinician drafted language with permission, iteratively revised it, and practiced with Dylan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed choice and offered options, supporting Dylan's sense of control while avoiding pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some elicitation of goals and preferences, but the clinician primarily focused on procedural problem-solving rather than drawing out Dylan's internal motivations or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invited input and shared control through choices and role-play, though could have further shifted authority by asking Dylan to propose responses first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Missed opportunities to elicit Dylan's own desires, reasons, or confidence to change drinking behavior, with emphasis instead on drafting and rehearsal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided confronting sustain talk and reframed toward solutions, but did not strategically use double-sided reflections or explicitly highlight downsides to current behavior.
