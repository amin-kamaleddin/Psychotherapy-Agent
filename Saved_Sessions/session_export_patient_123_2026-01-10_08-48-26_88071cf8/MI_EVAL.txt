# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established a warm, nonjudgmental stance and consistently validated Laura without labeling or blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective listening and perspective-taking but reflections were mostly surface-level and quickly moved to problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on school-related stress and concrete next steps with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicited preferences and adjusted wording, creating a cooperative exchange, but relied on ready-made scripts that kept the clinician more in control of content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, asked permission, and explicitly affirmed Laura's control over actions while avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of Laura's intrinsic motivation, values, or reasons for change; clinician defaulted to offering solutions rather than drawing out personal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited Laura's input and preferences, supporting shared decision-making, though the session remained somewhat clinician-led rather than fully co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supported small intentions and action planning but did not elicit Laura's own reasons, importance, or confidence for making changes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not confront sustain talk and effectively redirected toward practical supports, though missed opportunities for explicit double-sided reflections or normalization.
