# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, the clinician affirms the client's strengths and normalizes ambivalence throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, affect‑sensitive reflections capture both content and feeling, demonstrating clear perspective taking and strong empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation and moves the session toward a specific, measurable plan (patches, check‑ins, no‑inside rule).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is co‑constructed with repeated invitations for input and negotiation of details, adjusting the plan to fit the client's routine and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options and asks the client to choose what feels doable, though acknowledging the client's request to be 'convinced' risks a slightly more persuasive stance if not carefully avoided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits the client's own motivations (daughter, past success) and uses those internal reasons to build a practical plan rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and power‑sharing, asking the client to choose supports partnership, but occasional slightly directive offers could more explicitly foreground the client's authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits and amplifies change talk, drawing on past successes and translating the client's language into concrete, achievable steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and pivots to planning, but frequent verbatim reflections of fear and 'brutal' risk keeping sustain talk active rather than strategically softening it.
