# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., “Thank you — that’s a big win quitting smoking”; normalized slips). No labeling or moralizing; slips framed as normal and learning opportunities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and some reflections (acknowledged guilt, asked about triggers). Reflections were generally surface‑level and the clinician moved quickly to solutions rather than deepening emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong focus on the target behavior and concrete change plan (two‑drink limit, alternate with water, refusal line) with a specific follow‑up (Sunday 10:00 AM). Avoided drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance—offered options and invited patient choice (asked which strategy felt doable; let patient pick refusal line). At times listed many clinician‑driven strategies, which reduced opportunities for the patient to generate ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed patient choice and capacity, offered alternatives rather than directives, and scheduled follow‑up based on patient preference. Language supported self‑determination and responsibility.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of patient preferences and change talk (pride in quitting smoking, tentative commitment to two drinks), but clinician relied more on offering strategies than systematically drawing out motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated interaction as a joint effort (adapted plan to patient preferences, agreed on follow‑up). Periodic expert stance (enumerating many tactics) limited full co‑construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for and reinforced some change talk (praised smoking cessation, asked to set a limit) but did not sufficiently amplify or explore the patient’s expressed motivations or convert them into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Normalized doubt and framed slips as learning opportunities; provided practical cues and safety planning. Did not consistently use double‑sided reflections or strategic techniques to systematically shift sustain talk toward change.
