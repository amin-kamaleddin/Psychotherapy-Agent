# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Marco's experience and honoring his dislike of lectures. Tone remained warm and validating, preserving dignity throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening that matched Marco’s content and guarded, weary stance. Reflections built rapport and advanced the conversation, though deeper affective reflection (resignation/fatigue) was a minor missed opportunity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking and moved the session toward a concrete one‑week experiment with specific steps and follow-up. Each turn built toward an actionable plan while keeping it client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: invited Marco’s ideas, used his exact wording on the card, and negotiated partner phrasing and contingency plans together. Decision-making was shared and respectful of Marco’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by asking permission, offering options, and respecting limits (no lectures). Emphasized Marco’s choice and ownership of the plan throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited useful internal motivations via open questions and scaling, producing modest increases in commitment for the specific plan. Could have more deeply explored intrinsic values and summarized emerging change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership by co-creating the plan, seeking input, and deferring to Marco as the expert on his life. Power was shared and the clinician integrated Marco’s preferences throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively invited and shaped change talk with scaling, open questions, and specific planning, eliciting concrete change language and commitment for a trial. Could have amplified and summarized Marco’s change statements more to build momentum and stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argument and rolled with resistance, acknowledging sustain talk and steering toward small experiments that reduced defensiveness. Would benefit from more strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly link sustain talk to change language.
