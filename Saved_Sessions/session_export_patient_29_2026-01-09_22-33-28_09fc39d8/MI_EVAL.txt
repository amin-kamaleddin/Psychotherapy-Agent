# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated respect and positive regard (e.g., normalizing nervousness, affirming priorities) and avoided blame, creating unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections and timely paraphrases showed strong empathic listening and understanding of triggers and feelings, though deeper exploration of underlying emotional meaning could further enrich empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete steps (med choice, titration, quit date, monitoring, slip plan) and documented logistics for follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative: the clinician solicited preferences, incorporated spouse involvement and slip plans, and co‑constructed the treatment plan rather than prescribing unilaterally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician affirmed patient choice and control by offering recommendations but deferring to preferences, asking permission, and honoring conditions for treatment and monitoring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited personal reasons (importance/confidence, 'what quitting would mean') but then shifted to information and planning, missing opportunities to further draw out intrinsic motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as an equal partner in decisions (med choice, quit date, follow‑up) and negotiated the plan collaboratively, reflecting clear power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked and reinforced (desire, quit date, commitment to varenicline) but could be amplified with follow‑up questions targeting why benefits matter and what would increase confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain‑talk (stress, drinking, side‑effect worries) was acknowledged and redirected to options and monitoring, but stronger use of MI strategies (double‑sided reflections, strategic summaries) would better soften resistance.
