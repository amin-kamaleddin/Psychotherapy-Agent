# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated client's steps (e.g., “That’s a hard spot,” “You’re taking clear, concrete steps”). Occasional overt praise (“I’m proud of you”) felt slightly paternalistic rather than purely accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and affect mirroring (e.g., “I hear tired, broke, and torn,” “I hear your nerves and a bit of relief”). Reflections were generally surface-level with missed opportunities for deeper, complex empathic probes (identity/shame/fear).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently focused on target behavior and immediate goals (rent, auditions, helpline), set clear agenda and concrete steps (scripts, role‑play, call at 10am), and provided appropriate contingency instructions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used collaborative language and co‑created scripts/role‑play offers. At times shifted toward directive phrasing and repeated instructions (e.g., “Set an alarm and rest tonight”), slightly reducing shared problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly honored client choices (no forced rehab/cold‑turkey), offered decline scripts, and repeatedly reinforced Lukas’ ownership of the plan. Strong support for client pacing and preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited core values and linked options to those values, producing concrete change talk and commitments. Could have more consistently amplified the client’s own arguments for change and used targeted questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned session as shared work with offers of help and permission‑seeking (“Want to role‑play?”). Repeated reassurances and some directive language slightly tilted interaction away from full power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (client acknowledged harms, committed to steps). Missed opportunities to amplify and selectively reflect change language or use scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation and offered harm‑reduction alternatives and decline scripts. Could use more double‑sided and complex reflections to normalize ambivalence and highlight discrepancy.
