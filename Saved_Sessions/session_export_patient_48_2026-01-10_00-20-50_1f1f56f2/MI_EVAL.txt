# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and individualized validation (e.g., normalizing fatigue and offering sympathy). No blaming or labeling language; clinician remained warm and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening that matched Farid’s hesitations. Could deepen empathic impact by naming affect (frustration/discouragement) more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on increasing physical activity with concrete, actionable steps (10‑min walk, scheduling, backups, follow‑up). Balanced open exploration with steady movement toward a specific plan and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with joint problem-solving and co-creation of the plan (choice of times, alarm wording, energy rating). Clinician consistently invited Farid’s input and built the plan from his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Farid’s control by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing flexibility rather than pressuring. Clinician reinforced choice and ownership over decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Farid’s ideas for making the plan stick (reminders, backups, energy ratings) and elicited confidence scaling. Could further probe personally meaningful reasons/values and amplify intrinsic motives to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Farid as the primary agent of change and solicited his expertise repeatedly, co-creating specifics like alarm label and timing. Power-sharing was clear and consistent throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with specific planning questions, confidence ratings, and implementation details that prompted commitment language. To maximize impact, could more explicitly link changes to Farid’s values and seek firmer verbal commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confronting it and offered low-effort alternatives and normalizing statements to reduce resistance. Missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections and reframes to more actively convert sustain talk into change talk.
