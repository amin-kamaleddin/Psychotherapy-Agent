# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys clear, nonjudgmental acceptance and validates Camila's experience, normalizing ambivalence and honoring her preference for moderation. Warmth and positive regard are consistent throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking capture competing concerns (hangovers vs. social life), showing solid understanding. There is room to deepen exploration of underlying emotions like social anxiety or fear of rejection with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session keeps a steady focus on reducing problematic drinking while preserving social life using scaling questions and a concrete first step. Skills and rehearsal are consistently tied back to that clear behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites input, practice, and choice of lines, framing the work as joint problem-solving. At times directive coaching about phrasing, tone, and body language shifts power toward the clinician and reduces a fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly affirmed and choices are offered about strategies and whether to practice, respecting Camila's preference for moderation. The clinician consistently supports client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Camila's motivations and a manageable first step, drawing on her own reasons and confidence. However, the clinician frequently supplies scripts and phrasing before eliciting Camila's own language, which limits evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally works collaboratively, asks permission to practice, and lets Camila choose which lines to test. Occasional quick shifts into prescriptive coaching without first eliciting the client's ideas reduce the degree of shared co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong elicitation and reinforcement of change talk using importance/confidence scaling, asking for reasons for the rating, and requesting a small concrete first step. The clinician reflects and praises tentative change language and reinforces commitment through rehearsal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized, and the clinician offers practical strategies to preserve social connections while cutting back. There were missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or selectively emphasizing the costs of the status quo to further nudge toward change.
