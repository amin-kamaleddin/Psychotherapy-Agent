# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (apology, validation), creating clear emotional safety throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and perspective-taking (validation, normalization) but occasionally missed opportunities to deepen emotional content or explore how the experience affected trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on safely understanding and starting the statin, repeatedly returning to concrete aims and guiding the encounter toward action (scripts, med list, pharmacist steps).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by soliciting needs and co-creating tools, but often provided clinician-crafted wording rather than inviting the patient to generate language or set the agenda first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's choice and control by offering options, avoiding coercion, and encouraging the patient to decide how and when to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete preparatory steps and readiness (taking at night, calling prescriber) but relied more on information/scripts than on eliciting intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with offers to help and attention to preferences, though the interaction sometimes shifted into an instructive role rather than fully sharing decision-making power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced specific change-related intentions and actions (phone script, med-list) but could further evoke the patient's own reasons and confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed hesitancy into actionable safety steps, though greater use of double-sided reflections could better honor ambivalence while gently shifting toward change.
