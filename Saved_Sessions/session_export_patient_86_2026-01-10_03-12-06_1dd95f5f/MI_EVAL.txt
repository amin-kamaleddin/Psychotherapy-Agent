# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Marco’s stance, avoiding judgment and affirming his priorities. Repeated statements of acceptance and restraint from admonition supported a nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing content and feeling (guarded, weary, practical). Reflections sometimes felt formulaic and could be deepened with more complex emotional reflections to strengthen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on concrete tooth‑protecting behaviors rather than pushing broad smoking cessation. The clinician repeatedly narrows conversation to a specific, manageable action plan and success criteria.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is a genuine partnership with shared decision-making about strategies and implementation details. The clinician invites Marco’s input on what will make the plan easy and negotiates the plan together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through validation of Marco’s choice and explicit acceptance of his not‑ready stance. Questions offer options and elicit preference rather than prescribing a course.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits Marco’s own motivations, criteria for success, and past facilitators of change with open questions. These prompts drew out internal reasons and practical tests that deepened Marco’s motivation for harm‑reduction steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as co‑created and honors Marco’s expertise about his routines and preferences. Soliciting choices and checking what would make the plan workable reinforced equal contribution to the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces concrete change talk about rinsing, brushing, and appointments and asks for a manageable commitment. To reach a top score, selective amplification and summarizing of Marco’s change statements (and confidence scaling) could further strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently avoids amplifying sustain talk using double‑sided reflections and explicit acceptance, redirecting to achievable harm‑reduction steps. This approach softened resistance and prevented argument for quitting.
