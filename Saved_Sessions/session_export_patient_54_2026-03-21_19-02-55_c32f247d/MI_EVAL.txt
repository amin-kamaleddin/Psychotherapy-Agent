# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language and validated the patient’s perspective, honoring autonomy and offering choices. Demonstrated clear unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good empathic listening with accurate, brief reflections that acknowledged ambivalence and worries. Reflections were often short and shifted quickly to information-provision rather than deeper emotion-focused exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy and practical next steps (remove alcohol, mocktails, partner script, printable fact sheet). Offered concrete action planning and resources that moved the patient toward preparation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a collaborative exchange by inviting preferences, co-creating the partner script, and adjusting materials to the patient’s requests. Actively worked alongside the patient (e.g., offering role-play) rather than taking an authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized the patient's control and choice, offering options and asking permission for planning help. Avoided coercive language and reinforced ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some planning and the patient expressed important change language, but the clinician more often provided information than systematically drew out the patient's own reasons, confidence, or barriers. More open questions and targeted reflections would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered partnership by checking what the patient wanted and following her lead, co-creating materials and role-play. At times the clinician shifted to an expert/educational role without explicitly asking permission to share information.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and reinforced some movement toward preparation but missed repeated chances to elicit and amplify the patient’s own motivations and commitment language. Deliberate reflections and summaries of change talk would strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, responding with neutral facts and strategies. However, clinician rarely used MI techniques like double-sided reflections or reflective summaries to acknowledge sustain talk while pivoting toward change.
