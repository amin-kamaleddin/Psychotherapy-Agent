# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  
- Clinician consistently communicates respect and support (e.g., “You’re taking a careful, brave step; I believe this could help,” “You’re nervous but determined — that matters”).  
- Affirms Amina’s courage and priorities and avoids labeling.  
- Slightly instrumental at times (acceptance tied to planning); limited sustained normalization of ambivalence or sustained validation of emotional burden.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  
- Demonstrates empathic listening and checks feelings repeatedly (“I hear your worry about his anger,” “You’re anxious but determined — that matters”).  
- Reflections capture content and some affect (nervousness, fear, commitment).  
- Missed opportunities for deeper affective reflections (e.g., exploring repeated “Well, there’s a lot…” to name overwhelm/shame/helplessness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:**  
- Maintains clear focus on target behavior (bedtime routine, fewer arguments, move toward counseling).  
- Sets concrete, time‑bound plan: one‑week trial; Sunday text with specific wording; Monday 6pm follow‑up; fallback options and safety steps.  
- Strongly consistent steering toward next steps without losing client‑centeredness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  
- Treats Amina as a partner: asks what feels manageable, offers wording after asking, invites choice of fallback and scheduling.  
- Provides multiple ready‑made scripts and directives rather than co‑creating wording first; could elicit Amina’s phrasing before supplying examples.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  
- Respects choice and checks readiness (“Do you feel able to send the Sunday text and meet Monday at 6pm?”).  
- Frames options and emphasizes safety choices (stay calm, leave if threatened, call trusted person/emergency services).  
- Some directive provision of scripts/safety steps that slightly reduces purely autonomy‑led stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  
- Elicits a specific, client‑centered change: the one‑week bedtime routine trial and fallback plans.  
- Asks about preferences and commitment to testing the plan.  
- Does not consistently draw out deeper internal reasons or imagined outcomes beyond initial value statements (“I love my son… I want stability”), missing opportunities for further evocative questioning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  
- Invites shared decision‑making on scheduling, wording choices, and follow‑up; checks preferences frequently.  
- Often offers clinician‑generated solutions (multiple scripts) before eliciting Amina’s creative input, limiting client contribution to selecting among options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  
- Session elicits important change talk (e.g., “I love my son… I really want the best for him,” “I’m willing to change”).  
- Clinician focuses on planning and wording but rarely amplifies or deepens desire/reason/confidence statements (no scaling, reflective amplification, or targeted elicitors to strengthen commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  
- Clinician acknowledges barriers and sustain‑talk (father’s defensiveness, fear of escalation) and avoids reinforcing the status quo.  
- Offers pragmatic, neutral options (one‑week trial, fallback lines) that reduce confrontation and redirect toward change.  
- Could use more double‑sided reflections to explicitly normalize ambivalence and reframe sustain‑talk.
