# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes concerns while honoring the patient’s boundary about coaches/parents. Could add more explicit normalization of adolescent experimentation and stronger individualized affirmations to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and reflective listening, validating worries about consequences and parental reaction. Would benefit from more complex emotional reflections and deeper summaries to explore underlying fears and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on underage alcohol use tied to soccer/scholarship goals and keeps the session on harm-reduction and concrete behavioral strategies. Avoids drifting into unrelated topics and repeatedly returns to practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as joint problem-solving, offers role-plays and tailors the plan to patient preferences. Balance leans toward clinician-provided content—inviting the patient to generate strategies first would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by asking permission, offering options, and explicitly honoring the patient’s choice not to involve coaches/parents. These behaviors reinforce choice and control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit motivation (asking which options feel doable), but interaction relies more on providing solutions and skill coaching. Could use more open questions to draw out the patient’s intrinsic reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a partnership by offering choices and checking what feels doable, tailoring the plan to the patient. Tends to offer a clinician-driven menu rather than eliciting the patient’s ideas first, which limits power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Connects alcohol use to the patient’s goals and prompts doable steps and rehearsals that amplify change language. Could more frequently ask open questions to elicit desire, reasons, ability, and commitment and selectively reinforce the strongest change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk but responds with reassurance and risk information rather than reflective, double-sided techniques. Would improve by validating the legitimacy of experimentation while using reflections to gently pivot toward change.
