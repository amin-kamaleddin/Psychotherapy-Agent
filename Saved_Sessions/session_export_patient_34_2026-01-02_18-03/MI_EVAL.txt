# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Conveys respect / nonjudgmental stance (multiple empathic acknowledgements)
- [ ] Explicit affirmations of client's strengths or efforts
- [ ] Warm, richly relational acceptance throughout

**Comments:** Clinician consistently used neutral, nonjudgmental reflections (“I hear anger…”, “that makes sense”), protecting Dylan from blame. Acceptance felt somewhat procedural and repetitive; missed opportunities to explicitly affirm Dylan’s responsibility/concern for his scholarship.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Reflective listening of surface feelings and content (echoes anger, concern about reputation)
- [ ] Complex reflections or inference of deeper meaning
- [ ] Exploration of underlying values or ambivalence

**Comments:** Frequent surface reflections and echoes of content were present, but reflections were repetitive and did not probe deeper meanings (e.g., social meaning of “I like going out”) or infer protective motives.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Keeps clear focus on target problem (protecting record / conduct process)
- [x] Provides concrete, actionable steps (scripts, advisor, don't post, note witnesses)
- [ ] Co-creates plan with client rather than primarily advising

**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus and practical guidance appropriate for the brief session. Tended toward advice-giving/scripts rather than collaboratively eliciting Dylan’s ownership of steps.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Invites choice about immediate steps at least once (“Which of those would you like to do first?”)
- [ ] Sustained co-creation of plan with client
- [ ] Elicits client-generated wording/solutions

**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (choice about next step, offer to draft later) but the session was largely clinician-led with repeated scripts and directives rather than joint problem-solving.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Respects client declines and offers options (accepts refusal of breathing/timeline)
- [ ] Explicitly offers multiple, self-directed options and affirms right to choose
- [ ] Avoids directive language throughout

**Comments:** Clinician allowed Dylan to decline suggestions and invited follow-up, but frequent directive language and repeated closures weakened full autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
- [x] Asked about acceptable outcome once (“what outcome would feel acceptable to you right now?”)
- [ ] Explored client motivations, values, or reasons for change
- [ ] Used scaling or targeted evocation to draw out importance/willingness

**Comments:** Limited evocation—one question about acceptable outcome but little systematic elicitation of Dylan’s values, motivations, or discrepancy between values and sustain talk.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Offers to help and frames some support (offer to help plan or draft message)
- [ ] Positions clinician and client as equal partners throughout
- [ ] Elicits client expertise and integrates it into the plan

**Comments:** Occasional offers to collaborate, but overall expert/advisor stance dominated (scripts, repeated instructions) rather than an explicit equal partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
- [ ] Elicited desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment language
- [x] Acknowledged client concern about risks (reputation/scholarship) but did not amplify into change talk
- [ ] Used scaling or targeted probes to generate commitment

**Comments:** Very little client-generated change talk beyond factual admission of presence; clinician missed opportunities to follow concern about scholarship/reputation with evocation questions to amplify change language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Avoided confrontation and did not reinforce sustain talk (accepted “this wasn’t my fault” without arguing)
- [ ] Used double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sustain and change sides
- [ ] Strategically reframed sustain talk to open space for discrepancy

**Comments:** Clinician’s neutral acceptance prevented escalation, but did not actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or strategic framing to elicit the other side.
