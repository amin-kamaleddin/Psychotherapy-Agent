# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, with frequent affirmations and explicit normalization of slips. The clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking with accurate summaries of ambivalence and supportive reflections. Could deepen empathy with more complex/emotional reflections to explore frustration and meaning of smoking as 'unwinding.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and moved the session toward a concrete, measurable plan with a scheduled follow-up. Kept returning to specific triggers, strategies, and tracking measures.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like a joint enterprise—patient was invited to choose strategies and preferences shaped the plan. At times the clinician offered many concrete suggestions, but overall the stance was collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and affirming control over decisions. The clinician explicitly respected the patient's right to decline medications and emphasized nonjudgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation (scaling question, small change choice) but limited drawing out of the patient's internal reasons, values, or hopes. The clinician relied more on offering solutions than eliciting intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with invitations for choice and shared planning, and checking logistics for follow-up. However, repeated detailed suggestions sometimes shifted the dynamic toward an expert role rather than full co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk via the importance scale and a small commitment (10-minute delays) but did not probe why the patient rated a 3 or amplify personal reasons for change. The clinician moved relatively quickly to strategy rather than deepening client-generated change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided confrontation, using normalizing reflections, but employed few double-sided reflections or reframes to tip ambivalence. Offering many solutions may have left sustain talk underexplored.
