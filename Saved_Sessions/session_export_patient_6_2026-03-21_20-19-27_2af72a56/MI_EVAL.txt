# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and praise, normalizing the patient's goals. At times responses felt formulaic and the clinician moved quickly to solutions rather than explicitly naming unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking and reflective listening that matched the patient's worries and goals. Could deepen empathy by adding more complex emotional reflections (e.g., fear, embarrassment, peer pressure).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a steady, appropriate focus on adolescent alcohol use and consistently linked discussion and skills back to soccer/scholarship goals. Maintained momentum toward concrete strategies without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient preferences, let the patient choose scenarios and feedback style, and shaped the session around his choices. Occasionally shifted into a more directive coaching stance by prescribing exact wording and body posture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking what felt doable, and inviting the patient to select and lead role-plays. Feedback was provided when requested, reinforcing patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change language using the readiness ruler and follow-up probes that generated goal-oriented reasons for change. Could further draw out the patient's own solutions and elicit stronger commitment language (willingness, timing, confidence).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with shared decision-making and scenario selection driven by the patient. At times the clinician assumed an expert role with prescriptive coaching, which slightly reduced equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk and linked it to valued outcomes, praising protective choices. Could strengthen practice by more deliberately summarizing and amplifying the patient's own desire, ability, reasons, need, and steps toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and avoided shaming, but largely moved to problem-solving rather than strategically softening minimization or resolving ambivalence. Missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections, summaries, or autonomy-emphasizing responses to explore the sustain side.
