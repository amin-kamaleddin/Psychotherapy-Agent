# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with normalizing statements, supportive summaries, and encouragement of prior successes. Language is respectful and non‑shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and reflective listening, acknowledging values and barriers. Reflections are mostly pragmatic and could probe deeper emotional content and complex feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol and BP monitoring, sets concrete goals and measurement procedures, and provides actionable tools. The session consistently moves toward specific steps and a start date.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters genuine joint problem-solving by soliciting preferences, co-creating the plan, and inviting troubleshooting. Offers materials the patient requested and uses inclusive language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and asking the patient to select nights and supports. Responds nonjudgmentally to readiness and confidence ratings, reinforcing ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits change talk, uses scaling and past successes to build self-efficacy, and asks for concrete commitments. Could strengthen evocation by drawing out deeper intrinsic reasons and amplifying value statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes a collaborative stance by asking what would be useful and inviting the patient's input in planning. At times offers multiple prescriptive options which slightly reduces the purely patient-driven feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change language using scaling, specific questions, and confidence ratings, and strengthens commitment to concrete steps. There were missed opportunities to further amplify the patient's own reasons and link them to broader values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and shifts toward planning, but seldom uses MI techniques like double-sided reflections or explicit validation of minimization. A stronger approach would acknowledge the patient's minimization before offering advice to reduce sustain talk more effectively.
