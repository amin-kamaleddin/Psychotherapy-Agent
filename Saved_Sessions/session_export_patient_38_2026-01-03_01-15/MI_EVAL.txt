# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language throughout (e.g., “Hi Elena — I’m glad you came,” “You don’t need to choose today,” “You handled a scary moment well”). Respected pace/privacy (email only). No minimization or labeling.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., “You sound really relieved and understandably shaken,” “I’m glad you feel a bit calmer”). Reflections stayed at surface level; missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance (fear, shame, intensity).

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus on contraception and concrete next steps: concise options, condom tips, PDF, clinic/pharmacy info, and contingency guidance for urgent needs. Maintained goal-directed flow without pressuring a decision.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent invitations for Elena’s input (“Which would feel most helpful?”, “Want a one‑page resource or a referral?”). Incorporated her preferences into the plan (email only, hold appointment steps). Evident power‑sharing.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed freedom to choose and deferred decisions to Elena’s timeline (“You don’t need to choose today,” “I’ll hold… until you tell me you’re ‘ready’”). Avoided pressuring language and honored privacy requests.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some initial evocation (open invitation early, choice questions). Predominantly provided information rather than systematically eliciting Elena’s internal motivations, values, confidence, or using scaling to deepen change talk.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership via collaborative language and co‑creation of the PDF. At times shifted into an informative/explanatory role (detailed lists) which reduced opportunities for Elena to generate content and priorities.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient volunteered change talk (“I want to not have that happen again”), but clinician did not consistently amplify or elicit further desire/ability/reasons/confidence (no scaling, limited reflective amplification or targeted questions to build commitment).

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized ambivalence and avoided argumentation (“not ready to decide is totally okay”), offered low‑threat options (info, discreet tips, PDF). Could strengthen with more double‑sided reflections to selectively emphasize change side.
