PSYCHIATRIST: Short answer: common citalopram side effects are nausea, dry mouth, drowsiness or insomnia, headache, sweating, tremor, sexual problems, and sometimes weight change. Serious but less common risks include serotonin syndrome, abnormal heart rhythm (QT prolongation), severe allergic reaction, worsening mood or suicidal thoughts in younger people, and possible triggering of mania.

Important interaction: phenelzine (an MAOI) must NOT be taken with citalopram—together they can cause life‑threatening serotonin syndrome. You need a prescriber-directed washout (usually ~14 days) before switching.
