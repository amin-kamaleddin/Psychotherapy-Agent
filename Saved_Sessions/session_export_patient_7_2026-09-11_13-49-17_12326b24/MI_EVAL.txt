# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing Amina's experience and honoring her preferences (e.g., refusal to involve the caseworker). The clinician avoided labeling or moralizing language throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, complex reflections and perspective-taking that captured ambivalence, emotions, and motives, and repeatedly checked in with open invites. Reflections attended to both content and feeling, creating clear evidence of consistent empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on a specific target behavior (a discreet exit/landing signal) and tied questions and reflections back to that goal. The session stayed on task and narrowed options to feasible immediate steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner in co-planning (signal options, rehearsals, backups) and solicited the patient's ideas, adapting the plan to her preferences. The tone and structure were consistently team-oriented rather than expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly offered choices, checked which options 'felt doable,' and repeatedly honored the patient's decisions (including not involving the caseworker). The clinician avoided pressure and reinforced the patient's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete intentions and protective motives (avoid attention, keep control) but focused mainly on logistics and immediate steps. Could further probe deeper intrinsic reasons and strengthen confidence/commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited the patient's perspective and co-created the plan, deferring to her expertise about what feels safe. Language and actions communicated genuine power-sharing and positioned Amina as the expert in her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited actionable change talk through behaviorally specific questions and reinforced small commitments (e.g., 'I’ll try it once this week'). Would be stronger with more explicit amplification, summarizing of 'I’ll try' statements, or a confidence/commitment rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and respected sustain talk (refusal to involve caseworker), and redirected toward client priorities rather than reinforcing resistance. Missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections to more actively soften sustain talk while honoring autonomy.
