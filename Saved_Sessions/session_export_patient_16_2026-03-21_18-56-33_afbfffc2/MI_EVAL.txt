# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental support through affirmations and validation (e.g., “I’m here to listen,” praising progress). The clinician avoided moralizing language and maintained warm, unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking (validated vaping as relaxing/social and noted stress triggers). Reflections were pragmatic and could go deeper into the emotional experience of stress and its effects.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on vaping cessation with a measurable goal (quit date) and steered to concrete steps (triggers, strategies, rehearsals). Summaries and next steps maintained momentum toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a genuine partnership with the client co-designing the plan and the clinician tailoring feedback to the client's wording. Permission was asked before role-plays and decisions were made jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering a menu of options and asking which felt doable, and respected the client’s decisions (e.g., keeping the pen but leaving it at home). Permissive language around nicotine replacement and inviting role-play reinforced autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified motivation tied to basketball, suspension risk, and confidence (7/10) and asked about past attempts. However, the clinician relied mainly on extrinsic/pragmatic motivators and missed opportunities to draw out deeper values, identity, and longer-term meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and consistently deferred to the client’s expertise and preferences (offering options, asking for input on strategies and quit date). The clinician co-constructed the plan at each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk—amplified the 7/10 confidence, invited a quit date, and had the client articulate specific strategies and refusal lines. The clinician summarized and strengthened commitment language effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated reasons for maintenance (social/relaxation), then shifted quickly to planning to prevent entrenching sustain talk. Could further soften ambivalence with double-sided reflections or strategic summaries that name both sides.
