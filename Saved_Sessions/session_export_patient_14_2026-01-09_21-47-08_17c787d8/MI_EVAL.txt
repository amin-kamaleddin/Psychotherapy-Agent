# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—validated frustration about waiting and normalized his busy schedule, tailoring the plan to his constraints without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and summaries of emotion and practical concerns (e.g., 'constantly juggling tasks,' short on time), showing strong perspective-taking throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on weight-related activity and BP monitoring, moving the session steadily from problem recognition to a concrete, measurable one-month experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner—asked rather than told, incorporated the patient's schedule and preferences, and adjusted recommendations to fit his real-world constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly emphasized choice and negotiability, framing the plan as an experiment the patient could stop and honoring his boundaries about time and call quality.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited pragmatic motivators (BP change, measurable outcomes) but could have probed deeper personal values, reasons for change, or confidence to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Solicited the patient's proposals, honored his rules (no call-quality loss, quick BP checks), and co-constructed the plan, sharing power throughout the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and a realistic plan (10-minute walks, BP checks) but missed opportunities to deepen commitment with scaling or eliciting emotional reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk about time pressures without arguing, reframed barriers into manageable experiments (hands-free walks, one-month trial) and avoided persuasion.
