# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician created a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Selene repeatedly, but affirmations were somewhat brief and not deeply personalized to highlight her specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and summaries showed good perspective-taking and helped elicit barriers, though the clinician missed opportunities to name Selene’s emotional burden (e.g., overwhelm or frustration) more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on medication adherence and moved logically from barriers to concrete, time-specific solutions using classic MI tools like the confidence ruler and 'one-point higher' question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as cooperative problem-solving with frequent invitations for Selene’s input, adaptation of recommendations to her preferences, and co-creation of a practical plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offered choices, asked which options felt best, and avoided pressure, reinforcing Selene’s ownership of the plan and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling drew out Selene’s motivations and practical intentions, but the clinician could have probed deeper values and linked those to adherence to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Language and offers to rehearse or draft texts supported shared decision-making, though occasional concrete suggestions leaned slightly directive rather than purely collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited specific change talk and concrete steps (alarms, pillbox, husband text), but could have amplified Selene’s strongest change statements and probed commitment more directly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and used double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while redirecting to solutions, yet did not fully explore why 'it's not a big deal' is appealing or strategically reframe it.
