# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (opened with safety, validated frustration, and framed setbacks kindly), creating a warm, supportive atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated accurate empathic reflections and perspective-taking that tracked the patient's hesitancy and anxiety, with normalization and calming practices that reflected strong understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on improving follow-through and confidence; every intervention (if‑then plan, micro-start, alarm, accountability) was directly tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in joint problem-solving and co-constructed concrete plans (alarms, phrases, texts), rehearsed skills together, and incorporated the patient's language and choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, sought and respected the patient's choices, and used autonomy-affirming language while avoiding pressure or mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited actionable language and practical motivations (plan choice, commitments) but did not sufficiently probe deeper personal reasons, values, or the significance of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance with shared decision-making and step-by-step co-creation of the plan, demonstrating strong power-sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited specific commitment language and concrete steps (e.g., 10 minutes, prepping notes) but missed chances to deepen and amplify change talk about importance, meaning, and past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and redirected to strategies, but used few double-sided or summary reflections that would more actively reframe ambivalence toward change.
