# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician adopts a supportive, nonjudgmental stance, validates Lukas, and praises effort, conveying respect. Some responses feel formulaic and clinician-driven rather than deeply individualized, which prevents a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates adequate empathic listening with brief reflections of stress and tailored options. Reflections are relatively surface-level and do not consistently elaborate underlying feelings or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on peer-influenced drinking and protecting grades/team spot and ties interventions to Lukas’s goals. Early questioning is somewhat rapid and checklist-like, making parts feel assessment-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses roleplay, invites practice, and adapts to Lukas’s comfort, creating a collaborative tone. The clinician still largely controls content by offering many ready-made lines and tips instead of co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports autonomy by offering choices and asking permission before suggesting strategies. Frequent directive advice and scripted lines slightly reduce the sense of client-led decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some open questions but primarily engages in clinician-led teaching and rehearsal rather than eliciting Lukas’s intrinsic motivations, values, or past successes. More evocative questions could deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Lukas to choose topics, checks preferences, and involves him in roleplay, showing power-sharing. The clinician still directs much of the content and could elicit more of Lukas’s own ideas to strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for change talk and elicits some client language, but quickly shifts to giving solutions and scripts. There is limited systematic elicitation, amplification, or exploration of Lukas’s reasons, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and does not confront resistance, but fails to actively use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to soften sustain talk. The clinician tends to redirect to advice rather than explicitly acknowledging and reframing the pull to fit in.
