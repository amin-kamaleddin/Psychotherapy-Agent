# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and positive regard, normalized pragmatic steps, and offered practical supports without judgment, demonstrating unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening by summarizing ambivalence and motivating values, though deeper, affect-focused reflections would further explore underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management and concrete behavior change, repeatedly steering toward measurable actions tied to the patient's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—offered options, solicited preferences, negotiated supports, and co-constructed the plan rather than imposing decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the patient's choice by asking permission, framing options (meds as backup vs lifestyle), and using nonpressuring language that reinforced ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivation with evocative questions and links to core values, but could have probed deeper for emotion-laden reasons and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed a collaborative stance and treated the patient as the expert, asking permission and tailoring supports based on his input to foster genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk using readiness scales and planning questions and reinforced concrete commitments, yet could further amplify reasons and elicit a clearer commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and accepted the patient's minimization, but offered limited active strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly soften or resolve sustain talk.
