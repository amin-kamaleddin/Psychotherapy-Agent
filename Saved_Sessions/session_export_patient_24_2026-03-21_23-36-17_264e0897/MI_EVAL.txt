# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician expressed unconditional positive regard and normalized Daniel's perspective without judgment, affirming his choices and validating his priorities throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate, complex reflections and summaries captured Daniel's feelings and priorities and matched his language, creating a strong empathic connection and inviting further elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear focus on alcohol use in relation to marathon performance, tying questions and reflections to concrete outcomes and moving toward a specific, actionable experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was co‑operative, soliciting Daniel's preferences, leveraging his routines and supports, and jointly developing a feasible plan rather than imposing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and control were explicitly supported by framing the test as reversible and inviting Daniel to choose steps, preserving his ownership of decisions and next actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicited internally driven motivations by linking change to Daniel's values (race performance, recovery) and using evocative questions to have him generate reasons and a plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned Daniel as the expert on his life, co‑created the experiment with him, and integrated his strengths and input into the plan, demonstrating genuine partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocative questions and reflective reinforcement led Daniel to articulate specific change talk and commitment language, resulting in a concrete, self‑generated experiment to test.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with double‑sided and normalizing reflections and reframing the plan as an experiment, which reduced defensiveness and prevented sustain talk from dominating the session.
