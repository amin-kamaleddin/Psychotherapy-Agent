# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed respect and positive regard—opening warmly, validating the client's juggling of responsibilities, and normalizing awkwardness. Judgmental language was avoided and the client was thanked for openness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, frequent reflective listening captured the client's ambivalence and priorities (e.g., season readiness, social relaxation). The clinician mirrored the teen's language and offered clear summaries that supported understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on vaping and the concrete goal of staying vape-free for the season, moving from exploration to action. The clinician repeatedly returned to season-related motivation and specific planning (refusal lines, reminders, role-play).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative and co-creative—clinician and client drafted texts, refusal lines, and strategies together. The clinician positioned the work as joint problem-solving rather than expert instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice and ownership by offering options, asking which felt doable, and avoiding pressure. Slips were normalized and framed as opportunities to reset, protecting client autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicited the client's own motivations and reasons for change, using questions and confidence ratings to surface and build internal motivation. Change talk was drawn out and used to shape a client-centered plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician clearly positioned the client as the expert and invited her to choose strategies, scripts, and supports. Decisions were driven by the client's preferences and the clinician offered options to implement them together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited, reflected, and amplified change talk, asked for specific doable steps, and reinforced tentative commitments. Client statements were translated into concrete, copy-ready plans to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward goals and strategies, which reduced resistance. Improvement could come from more explicit double-sided reflections naming both sides of ambivalence to further diminish sustain talk.
