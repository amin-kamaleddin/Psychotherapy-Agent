# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validated Anya’s steps and normalized reasons for drinking without blaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and summarized concerns, but reflections were often brief and task-focused with missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on underage drinking and safety throughout and skillfully moved from assessment to an actionable party and safety plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-created the checklist and invited Anya’s input, giving meaningful choice, though parts of the session felt more clinician-driven with provided scripts and tips.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options and deferred to Anya’s preferences, reinforcing her control over decisions with questions like 'Which would help most right now?'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked about reasons for drinking and elicited some motivation, but relied heavily on clinician-supplied solutions rather than fully drawing out Anya’s intrinsic motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative with joint planning and choices, though the clinician occasionally adopted a more directive, expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk by offering choices and questions, but frequently moved quickly to options and scripts instead of reflecting and amplifying Anya’s own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged reasons for drinking and shifted to alternatives, but did not regularly use techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) specifically aimed at softening sustain talk.
