# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language with frequent affirmations and normalization, which reduced defensiveness throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing ambivalence, but could probe deeper into underlying emotions like anxiety or family-related concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on marijuana use and concrete harm-reduction targets, repeatedly tying conversation turns to specific plans and upcoming events.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a partner with the patient—brainstormed options, invited selections, and co-constructed the plan with language that reinforced teamwork and follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and affirming that the plan is the patient's decision without coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some intrinsic motivation and specific planning from the patient, but could have more fully drawn out personal reasons, values, and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered collaboration and invited the patient to shape the plan, though the clinician offered many concrete strategies rather than eliciting the patient's own solutions earlier.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and doable steps, yet relied more on offering options and advice instead of systematically drawing out the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized ambivalence, but used few MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to actively soften sustain talk and tip ambivalence toward change.
