# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence with affirmations and nonjudgmental language, creating a safe environment for this adolescent brief intervention.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries demonstrated perspective-taking and validation, though the clinician could have probed feelings more deeply to further deepen emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on THC vaping from assessment through a concrete 30-day plan, refusal lines, coping strategies, and follow-up mechanisms appropriate for a brief intervention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created the plan with the patient, repeatedly asked about preferences, used the patient's exact wording, and negotiated formats and accountability, demonstrating high-level shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician supported choice by asking permission, offering options, normalizing slips, and framing actions as the patient’s decision, which reinforced the patient’s sense of control and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited motivations (team concerns, confidence rating, triggers) and converted them into a plan, but could have used more values-focused open questions to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was clearly collaborative—decisions, wording, and materials were negotiated with the patient and her input was consistently invited and incorporated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was reflected and amplified (confidence, prior short quit), and a concrete plan was built, but the clinician could have elicited more elaborated, values-based reasons and stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged both sides of ambivalence but used limited strategic softening (e.g., few double-sided reflections) and moved quickly into planning, missing opportunities to diffuse sustain talk further.
