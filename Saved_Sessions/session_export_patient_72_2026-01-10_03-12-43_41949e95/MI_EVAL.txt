# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validation, avoiding blame. Multiple affirmations of competence and effort reinforce a respectful, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking throughout. Some reflections are surface-level or repetitive and there were a few missed opportunities to deepen emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on restarting activity with concrete steps (YMCA visit, time, reminders, backups). Questions and reflections consistently move the conversation toward actionable planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicits the client's input at each decision point and integrates her preferences into the plan. Uses inclusive language and co-constructs solutions rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors choice and frames suggestions as options (call vs. website chat, different times). Avoids coercive language and supports the client's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits internal motives (strength, energy, confidence) and links them to specific behaviors and implementation intentions. Questions draw out the client's own reasons and past strengths for following through.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as joint problem-solving and respects the client's expertise about what will work. Frequent invitations to co-plan and deference to client preferences reinforce partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively fosters and reinforces change talk, turning desire into concrete steps and commitments. Could more deliberately amplify and summarize the client's DARN-C language to consolidate stronger commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates barriers and redirects toward planning and solutions without siding with sustain talk. Occasional echoing of negative language (e.g., repeated 'tiring'/'disappointing') could be reduced to avoid keeping sustain-focused affect active.
