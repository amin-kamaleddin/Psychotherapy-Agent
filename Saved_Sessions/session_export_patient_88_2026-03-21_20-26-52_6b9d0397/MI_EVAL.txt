# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance from the start, normalizing difficulty and affirming Marcus’s agency with clear validating statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening and accurate reflections that captured ambivalence and salient feelings, though a few reflections could have probed deeper emotions or the teen’s sense of being misunderstood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on teen alcohol use and related consequences, moving logically from values to behavior to specific, action‑oriented strategies and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as an equal partner throughout—seeking Marcus’s input, co‑creating tactics, and using role‑play and joint problem‑solving rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before advising, and framing suggestions as options so Marcus retained control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Marcus’s own motivations using questions and scales to generate personal reasons for change, but could have amplified discrepancy and pursued firmer commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance with repeated invitations for Marcus to choose tactics, rehearse, and co‑construct a doable plan, treating him as the expert on his context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively solicited change talk with targeted questions and 0–10 scales and helped generate specific steps, though there were missed opportunities to amplify and convert expressed reasons into stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and acknowledged both sides, but used mostly neutral reflections and practical redirection rather than strategic double‑sided reflections or selective reinforcement to shift sustain talk toward change.
