# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with warm, affirming language and repeated encouragement throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently validated and reflected the patient's perspective and worries, but reflections were often brief and the clinician moved quickly to advice rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing workplace/lunch smoking with goal-oriented strategies, concrete plans, and explicit follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative—solicited the patient's choices and check-ins—but collaboration was reduced by offering many clinician-driven strategies before eliciting more patient-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by presenting multiple options, asking which felt doable, and framing recommendations as choices without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's commitments and concrete action steps (which two, when) but relied heavily on clinician-provided solutions and could have used more open questions to draw out intrinsic motives and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered invitations and role-play but often shifted into an expert-driven stance with long lists of instructions, leaving more power with the clinician than the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced and prompted change talk through choices, praise, and follow-up planning, yet missed opportunities to reflect, amplify, and assess confidence in the patient's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Normalized ambivalence and reframed skepticism effectively, but used few MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further soften sustain talk or explore doubts in depth.
