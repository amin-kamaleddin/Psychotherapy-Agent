# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Ahmed’s perspective with validating reflections and nonjudgmental language. Avoided labeling or moralizing about mini‑cigar use, creating a respectful, supportive tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective‑taking and accurate reflective listening with timely summaries of priorities and concerns. Could deepen emotional reflection (e.g., on defensiveness or social worries) to further strengthen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on mini‑cigar use and explicitly linked the discussion to Ahmed’s priorities (work, family). Kept the session on task with an importance ruler, brief risk feedback, and a concrete one‑week patch trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a two‑way partnership by soliciting preferences, drafting call/text scripts, and adapting recommendations to Ahmed’s needs. Co‑constructed practical next steps and respected Ahmed’s input throughout the encounter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before giving information, and affirming Ahmed’s control over decisions. Respected refusals and used low‑pressure language (e.g., 'trial' not 'quit').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic motivations (protecting work and family) and used the importance ruler to draw out reasons for change. Could have spent more time exploring and amplifying Ahmed’s own motivations before moving quickly to solution‑giving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited Ahmed’s preferences and offered options, demonstrating collaborative intent. Could increase co‑creation by eliciting Ahmed’s own ideas for handling social situations and shaping the trial plan together earlier.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk with questions (importance ruler, 'what would need to happen') and a concrete one‑week test that Ahmed tentatively endorsed. Missed opportunities to amplify change statements and more directly assess confidence and ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with minimizations and gently redirected to change‑relevant concerns using neutral information and practical options. Could use more double‑sided reflections to explicitly validate sustain talk while nudging toward change.
