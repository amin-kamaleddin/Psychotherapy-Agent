# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys clear, unconditional positive regard and normalizes Jordan’s struggle without blame, repeatedly affirming his worth. This consistent, explicit acceptance supports safety and engagement throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections reliably capture both affect and content (frustration, hope, worry) and mirror Jordan’s language, validating ambivalence. The accurate empathic listening lowers resistance and strengthens rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on stopping shoplifting and links concrete incremental steps to that goal. The dialogue moves logically from values to options to a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the interaction as teamwork, using inclusive language and co‑constructing the plan with Jordan. Jordan’s input and preferences are solicited at each step rather than imposing prescriptive advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options are offered rather than directives and Jordan is repeatedly invited to choose what feels doable, preserving his agency. The clinician emphasizes that Jordan controls the plan and how to set it up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Jordan’s motivations (freedom, not disappointing his mom, job) and ties steps to those motives, but could probe values and past successes more deeply. More targeted elicitation of confidence and explicit commitment language would strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power is explicitly shared and the clinician treats Jordan as the expert on what will work, inviting collaboration on concrete options. This fosters genuine co‑creation of doable steps and contingencies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited effectively (desire and reasons, concrete first steps) and leads to a realistic plan, but the clinician could more consistently amplify and reflect key change statements. Explicitly eliciting ability/confidence and a commitment statement would further consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and not reinforced; reflections and redirection toward planning help soften it. Opportunities to use more double‑sided reflections and to explore past instances of resisting peer pressure were missed and could further reduce sustain talk.
