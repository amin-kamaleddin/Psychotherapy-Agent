# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental respect, validating Elias's concerns and goals. Tone remained warm and supportive throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening that captured both content and emotion, including ambivalence and specific fears. Reflections were timely and updated as Elias added details.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on increasing safe activity with progressive movement from exploration to a concrete plan. Regularly steered the conversation toward specific, measurable actions and supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan using Elias's ideas and repeatedly asked for his preferences, offering tools only with consent. Decisions about timing, stop rules, and supports were shared and client‑driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Elias's right to choose and avoided coercive language, offering options and checking comfort with suggestions. Reinforced Elias's ownership by asking what would raise his confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic motivations and barriers, linking values (sharing moments, independence) to concrete steps. Used importance/confidence scaling and explored what would raise Elias's confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine shared decision‑making and treated Elias as the primary agent of change, asking permission before suggestions. Incorporated Elias's language into the plan and offered support collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated ideas into a concrete plan, but missed opportunities to amplify spontaneous positive statements and elicit stronger commitment language. More deliberate amplification of brief positive remarks and solicitation of client‑generated commitment would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and avoided reinforcing sustain talk, validating limits and offering safety strategies while redirecting toward solutions. Could more strategically use double‑sided reflections and reflect sustain talk to reduce its momentum before pivoting to change.
