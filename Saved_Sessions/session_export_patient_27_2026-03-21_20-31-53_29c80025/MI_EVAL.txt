# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing feelings and praising the client's courage. Warmth and respect were evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening that captured ambivalence and emotional nuance. Reflections and summaries validated the patient's feelings and supported understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on disclosure of diabetes and used interventions directly tied to that goal. The clinician avoided drifting and consistently returned to practical steps for disclosure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative, with the clinician inviting input, offering options, and shaping interventions in response to the patient's preferences. Role‑plays and wording tweaks showed true co‑construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the client's right to choose and avoided pressuring language, offering multiple options and asking permission before interventions. Emphasized choice and ownership of decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation and small, achievable steps that increased willingness, but relied more on providing scripts than drawing out the client's own reasons and values. Greater probing for personal meanings and scaling would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the client as an expert on her situation and routinely invited her preferences, co‑creating the plan and asking permission before offering scripts or rehearsals. Power‑sharing was consistent and clear.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and supported some change talk and small commitments, such as willingness to practice, but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify change statements with follow‑ups or scaling. More strategic prompts could have strengthened and extended change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and contained sustain talk without reinforcing it, steering toward manageable steps and rehearsal. However, the clinician used limited double‑sided reflections and did not deeply reframe sustaining arguments to resolve ambivalence more strategically.
