# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the patient's situation while offering practical supports; could add deeper individualized normalization and more explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some empathic statements and acknowledges difficulty, but reflective listening is limited and the clinician moves quickly to problem-solving rather than exploring emotions or ambivalence in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on gambling-related behaviors and immediate risk-management, with each turn tied to concrete next steps and plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session feels like a joint enterprise—invites edits, offers multiple script options, and co-produces materials, treating the patient as an expert on what will work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly respects the patient's choices and pacing, offering alternatives and asking permission before proceeding, which strongly supports client autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits concrete plans and change language but does little to draw out intrinsic motivations or link plans to the patient's values and long-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative and co-creative—asks permission and accepts wording changes—but at times moves quickly to clinician-generated lists instead of eliciting the patient's options first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and operationalizes change steps and reinforces workable choices, though it emphasizes scripts and actions more than eliciting desire, reasons, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation, normalizes nervousness, and offers acceptable alternatives that redirect sustain talk toward options, but could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
