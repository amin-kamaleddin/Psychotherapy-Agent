# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys respect and positive regard via validating statements, normalization, and nonjudgmental language throughout the session, avoiding blame or shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic understanding and some reflective listening, but reflections are often brief and quickly followed by solutions rather than deeper affect‑focused exploration or summaries of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on exercise consistency and safe progression, moving the session toward concrete, actionable planning and problem‑solving tied to the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engages in collaborative planning and adjusts recommendations to the client's constraints, but often supplies the bulk of solutions rather than eliciting the client's own brainstorming and alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, asking which options feel realistic, and framing suggestions as optional while respecting the client's preferences and need for modification.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivations, preferences, and commitments effectively, but leans toward providing clinician solutions instead of drawing more of the client's internal motives, values, and personal language about change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally adopts a collaborative stance—asking preferences and tailoring plans—but frequent expert recommendations shift some authority back to the clinician rather than fully sharing power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and reinforces change talk and specific commitments through affirmations and preference questions, yet could do more to amplify the client's own language and probe desire, ability, reason, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and redirects without arguing, but would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and eliciting the client's counterarguments to sustain talk rather than mainly supplying solutions.
