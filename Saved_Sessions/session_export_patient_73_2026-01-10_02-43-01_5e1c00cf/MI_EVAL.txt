# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing ambivalence and affirming Linda’s feelings (e.g., opening line, repeated validations). The clinician avoided blame and maintained unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections captured both content and emotion, helping Linda feel heard (e.g., paraphrases of health/cost worries and desire for non‑pressure). Empathic statements and validations strengthened rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation and moved the session toward a concrete, time‑limited 48‑hour experiment with practical stepwise guidance. Balanced exploration with actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative and co‑constructed, offering options and rehearsing strategies while soliciting Linda’s preferences. The clinician tailored recommendations to her social context and invited joint problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control through permission language, low‑pressure framing, and multiple options, emphasizing that slips are information not failure. The clinician affirmed Linda’s ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited personal reasons for change and helped formulate a short experiment, but occasionally shifted into giving solutions rather than further drawing out and amplifying Linda’s own motivations and confidence. More probing questions to deepen motivation would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered partnership by seeking permission, offering choices, and rehearsing scripts together, demonstrating genuine power‑sharing. The clinician repeatedly invited Linda to choose what felt doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and secured a concrete 48‑hour plan, but missed opportunities to deepen and sequence change talk with more strategic reflections and open questions to elicit commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and used normalizing language for slips, avoiding reinforcement of sustain statements, but could more actively use selective or double‑sided reflections that end on the change side to reduce sustain talk intensity.
