# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard, normalized the difficulty of reaching out, affirmed the client's preferences, and avoided judgmental language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening were evident via frequent, precise reflections and summaries that captured both stressors and valued activities and checked the client's priorities.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on improving study focus/time management and progressed logically from problem identification to a specific implementation plan and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as genuine two‑way collaboration with the clinician soliciting preferences, inviting the client to tailor strategies, and acting as facilitator while the client made choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, inviting modifications, and checking confidence and preferred supports for implementation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited short‑term motivation and commitment language but focused mainly on practical implementation rather than exploring deeper personal values or long‑term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work was framed as collaborative with offers of choice, permission‑seeking before suggestions, and co‑construction of the plan (e.g., timing, location, reward) throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited concrete change and commitment language (specific 40/10 plan, check‑in) but missed some opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivations and connect actions to values or long‑term goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was little sustain talk and the clinician avoided reinforcing it by focusing on supports and barriers, though using double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence would strengthen this area.
