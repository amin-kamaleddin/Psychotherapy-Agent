# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with affirmations and normalizing language; adding explicit normalization of ambivalence would be a minor enhancement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective‑taking and reflective listening throughout, accurately paraphrasing Diego's feelings and following with supportive offers; occasional deeper, more complex reflections could further deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on heroin use and harm reduction, repeatedly guiding the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a joint exploration with co‑creation of the plan, shared decision‑making, and offers to accompany or role‑play, positioning clinician and client as partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy using choice language, asking permission before advising, and affirming Diego's control over decisions, which reinforced his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivation by asking readiness, drawing out specific reasons and steps, and converting those motivations into concrete plans and commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the session as collaborative, asking permission before offering input and inviting Diego's preferences, which communicated shared expertise and respect.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (readiness ratings, concrete steps, affirmations, and role‑play); could further strengthen momentum by summarizing and reflecting Diego's change language into a brief commitment statement.

**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and steered toward harm reduction, but missed opportunities for strategic double‑sided reflections or reframing sustain talk to elicit more change language.
