# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, validating concerns and using warm, non‑stigmatizing language throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective‑taking and some reflective listening (e.g., acknowledging urgency), but reflections are brief and factual rather than exploring deeper emotions or fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on safe switching from phenelzine to citalopram, provides concise medical facts, and moves the conversation toward concrete actions with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—iteratively drafts and revises the prescriber message with the patient, offers options, and acts on the patient’s requests, demonstrating shared problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's right to choose by offering options, asking permission (e.g., to include phone number), and avoiding pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of priorities (e.g., asking about urgency) but relies heavily on providing information and task completion rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively treats the patient as a partner by asking for input, co‑constructing the message, and frequently checking preferences, which conveys shared expertise and decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports action indirectly by drafting messages and offering steps, but rarely elicits or amplifies the patient's own motivations, desires, or confidence to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and normalizes concerns with clear safety guidance, though could further soften sustain talk using double‑sided reflections to acknowledge benefits while highlighting safety.
