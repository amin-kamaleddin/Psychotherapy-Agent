# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized ambivalence, and avoided blaming language, creating a safe, nonjudgmental space for disclosures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured context and affect (worry, hesitation), but reflections were sometimes summary-focused with missed chances to deepen emotional exploration (e.g., loneliness or shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and progressively narrowed toward a concrete, achievable plan (specific nights, prep, if‑then coping) without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a true partnership: the clinician built on Kenji’s ideas, asked open questions, and checked feasibility and preferences while shaping the plan together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were explicitly offered and the clinician repeatedly asked what felt most doable, reinforcing Kenji’s control over decisions and avoiding prescriptive direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited internal motives and past successes to leverage for change, though could further amplify values, probe confidence (scaling), and elicit a clearer commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed with Kenji’s ideas treated as primary; the clinician integrated his suggestions into the plan and shared power in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and built on change language (tea swap, skipping drinks), but could more consistently amplify and summarize those statements to elicit stronger commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk and avoided confrontation while redirecting to small steps, though more use of double-sided reflections or selectively reinforcing pro-change remarks would further dilute sustain talk.
