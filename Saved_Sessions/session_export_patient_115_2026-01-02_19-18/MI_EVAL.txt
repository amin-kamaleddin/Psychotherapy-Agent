# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed partial progress several times (e.g., “good job cutting down smoking,” “You’ve done good work today”). Acceptance felt somewhat formulaic and repetitive; lacked deeper normalization of ambivalence or explicit unconditional acceptance of the patient’s readiness to reduce rather than quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic empathic statements present (“that sounds scary and frustrating”) and safety concerns acknowledged. Reflections were sparse and superficial; clinician rarely mirrored patient’s persistent worry/resignation or used complex reflections to explore emotional tension.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on asthma management and concrete behavioral targets (written asthma action plan, avoid dusty route, wear N95, cut one cigarette/day, driving safety plan). Clinician repeatedly redirected to clear, actionable goals and safety steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (asking patient to pick a small goal, offering options). However, clinician largely supplied and repeatedly restated the plan rather than co-constructing implementation strategies tailored to the patient’s shift-work and income concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician provided choices and accepted the patient’s level of readiness (e.g., offering “call Tuesday or at next visit,” accepting reduction vs quitting). Missed opportunities to further empower patient by eliciting his expertise about what will work in his schedule.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient was invited to set a goal, producing some change talk. Much of the session relied on clinician-generated solutions; little elicitation of patient’s values, reasons for change, or long-term goals to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful stance and occasional invitations to participate, but clinician frequently assumed planner/advisor role. Partnership present but not strongly enacted through shared problem-solving around barriers (e.g., shifts, fear of losing fares).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited change talk elicited (patient says “I’ll try…,” “Cutting one cigarette a day feels doable”), but clinician did not consistently reflect, amplify, or elicit elaboration to strengthen commitment or move toward firmer change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient expressed sustain talk repeatedly (“not ready to quit,” concerns about losing fares). Clinician accepted and normalized reduction but did not actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or exploration of tradeoffs; repetitive affirmation may have validated sustain talk rather than resolving ambivalence.
