# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard, honored boundaries, and avoided moralizing language, creating a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured the patient's frustration and practical concerns well, but the clinician missed some opportunities to delve deeper into underlying emotions and the sarcasm/defensiveness beneath them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a clear focus on asthma self-management and produced a specific, measurable, time-limited test (spare inhaler + alarm + one-week check‑in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-created with the patient, with the clinician soliciting preferences for follow-up and respecting the patient's boundaries, reflecting a true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered choices and framed the experiment as the patient's decision, emphasizing control and avoiding directives or pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited practical motivation and a willingness to test changes, but did not sufficiently probe the patient's personal reasons, values, or confidence to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction consistently read as equal partnership: options were generated together, the patient steered decisions, and boundaries for contact were negotiated collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited concrete, low-effort steps and a week-long commitment, but missed opportunities to amplify change talk, affirm prior efforts, and elicit importance/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged skepticism without arguing, honored requests to avoid lecturing, and redirected sustain talk toward small, low-pressure experiments effectively.
