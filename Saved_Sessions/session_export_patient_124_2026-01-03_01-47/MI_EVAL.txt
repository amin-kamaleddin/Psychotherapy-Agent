# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language (e.g., “You’re choosing this—I support whatever pace feels safe,” “I’m here to support whatever you decide”). Reassured patient and offered practical safety guidance rather than blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good use of reflections and validation ("I hear relief now vs. worries…", “Those concerns are understandable”) and elicited values. Could deepen affective reflections to capture underlying shame/fear and Sofia’s casual dismissiveness (missed opportunities for more complex emotional reflections).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on marijuana use and its link to Sofia’s goals (grades, college, anxiety). Session progressed logically from pros/cons to importance scaling and a concrete, time-bound plan (3‑sec breathing, texting friend, reminders, follow-up).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong invitational tone and shared decision-making (offered message options, asked which felt like her, co-created action plan and backup strategies). Clinician offered rehearsal and invited Sofia’s preferences throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Sofia’s agency and choices (“You’re choosing this…”, offered options about telling mom vs. waiting for doctor). Avoided coercion and honored patient pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Sofia’s reasons and resources (values, supports, coping needs) and used scaling to draw out motivation (importance 7, confidence 6). Could have amplified emerging change talk more and used more evocative questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative stance: clinician tailored language to Sofia’s voice, invited her wording for texts, and positioned Sofia as expert about her life. Offered to rehearse and jointly plan next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited multiple forms of change talk (desire to feel less anxious, ability to try breathing/texting, tentative commitment to try). Missed opportunities to convert change talk into stronger, specific commitment language and concrete if/then plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician validated concerns and avoided argumentation (acknowledged skepticism, teasing, withdrawal worries) and redirected to small steps and safety planning. Could have used more double-sided reflections and summaries to highlight discrepancy between values and current behavior.
