# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician opens supportively, checks safety, and maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone while answering concerns. To reach a 5 they could add more personalized normalization and explicit statements of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are empathic elements (supportive opening, reassurance, safety guidance) but limited reflective listening of the patient’s emotions. More reflections or summarizing the patient’s feelings would strengthen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, sustained focus on medication safety, washout timing, recognition of serotonin syndrome, and concrete next steps. Responses stayed tied to the target behavior and moved the patient toward decisions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The encounter was treated as a team effort, inviting prescriber/pharmacist involvement and offering materials the patient could bring. The psychiatrist tailored outputs to the patient’s needs and incorporated their requests directly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported patient autonomy by advising consultation with prescribers, offering options, and asking if the patient wanted help drafting questions. Decision-making was left with the patient in partnership with their care team.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session was primarily informational and clinician-driven with few attempts to elicit the patient’s motivations, values, or confidence. More open questions and reflections drawing out the patient’s goals and barriers would improve evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopted a collaborative stance—encouraging prescriber/pharmacist collaboration and offering to help draft questions. They could further foster partnership by explicitly asking how the patient prefers to proceed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reinforced safety-oriented intentions and provided alternatives and concrete instructions, which indirectly supported change talk. However, they did not actively elicit or amplify the patient’s own motivations, values, or confidence about following a washout plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk, validated safety concerns, redirected to actionable steps, and discouraged abrupt cessation. Using more double-sided reflections to acknowledge and reframe sustain statements could further soften resistance.
