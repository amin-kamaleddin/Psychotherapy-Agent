### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Jordan’s ambivalence without judgment, validating concerns about needles, side effects, and time. The clinician accepts Jordan’s position while remaining respectful and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate, timely reflections and summaries that capture Jordan’s experience and checks meaning with open questions. Reflections deepen understanding rather than merely restating facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on receiving the flu vaccine and moves the conversation toward practical steps like scheduling, doctor reassurance, and scripting. Questions are purposeful and geared to translate ambivalence into specific actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is co-created with Jordan’s input shaping the plan; the clinician asks preferences about wording and offers feasible, mutually agreed strategies. The clinician follows Jordan’s lead while offering practical options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by normalizing uncertainty, offering choices (text vs call, which script), and avoiding pressure. Emphasizes Jordan’s control over decisions and respects his pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Jordan’s core motivator (protecting his grandparents) and concrete supporting steps, but could deepen exploration of values/emotional significance and use scaling or stronger client language to build commitment. More amplified reflections of Jordan’s own words would strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions strongly as a partner by offering options, co-constructing a plan, and inviting Jordan’s input at each step. The clinician shares power and collaborates on next steps like texting or holding a late slot.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evokes and reinforces change talk about protecting grandparents and prompts initial steps, including a first move (call doctor). Missed opportunities include further amplification of change statements and probing for commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and shifts toward actionable supports (scheduling, scripts, doctor reassurance) to avoid reinforcing resistance. Could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly juxtapose sustain and change sides.
