# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing ambivalence and validating Maya’s experience in a warm, respectful tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with strong reflective listening and acknowledgment of emotional content, capturing Maya’s isolation and competing concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on underage drinking and cutting back, using readiness rulers, pros/cons, concrete strategies, and role-play to keep the session goal-directed and actionable.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as a genuine two-way partnership: the clinician solicited Maya’s preferences, offered options, and co-constructed next steps and strategies with her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Maya’s choice and control by offering menus of options, asking which felt doable, and seeking permission before offering strategies or role-play.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used open questions and the readiness ruler to draw out reasons and ideas, but could more consistently elicit deeper personal motivations and transform statements into commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making and client expertise by asking what to focus on, inviting role-play scenarios, and checking which strategies felt acceptable to Maya.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple change statements and achievable steps, but missed opportunities to amplify and chain those statements toward stronger reasons, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk with harm-reduction options and reframing, yet could more actively use double-sided or amplified reflections to shift sustain talk toward change.
