# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone and honors the patient's preference for a low‑pressure test week, showing strong unconditional positive regard; warmth is professional rather than richly individualized. 

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are basic empathic moves and occasional reflections (e.g., 'That plan makes sense'), but limited deeper reflective listening or exploration of the emotions and functions of smoking. 

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on smoking reduction with clear options, concrete dosing instructions, a measurable short‑term goal, and a specific follow‑up plan. 

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist solicits patient preferences, adapts recommendations to a low‑pressure plan, and invites check‑ins, though the encounter still leans toward an expert‑led medical consultation. 

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician reinforces patient choice and frames the plan as a test, asking permission for planning, but occasionally presents dosing choices in a somewhat prescriptive way. 

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little was done to elicit the patient's intrinsic motivations or personal reasons for change; the interaction is primarily informational rather than using evocative open questions. 

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements and joint decision‑making on logistics, but the clinician often adopts the expert role and directs specifics without consistently eliciting the patient's expertise. 

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some prompts generated change language (agreeing to test the patch, setting goals), yet the clinician did not consistently use reflections or open probes to amplify or deepen the patient's own arguments for change. 

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and offers harm‑reduction alternatives, but does not strategically use double‑sided reflections or reframing to soften sustain talk and tip the balance toward change.
