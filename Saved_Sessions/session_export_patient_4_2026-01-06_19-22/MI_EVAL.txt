# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit nonjudgmental opening and repeated validating, supportive language that respected the patient's fears and experiences, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and perspective-taking with validations and tailored responses, but the clinician missed opportunities for deeper emotional exploration of moments like relief at the wedding or the impact of job loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on obtaining appropriate investigations and symptom management, providing actionable materials (scripts, checklist, diary) and repeated summaries to keep the session on target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—co‑creating scripts and tailoring steps with the patient—but at times the clinician assumed a solution‑provider role by drafting full scripts and stepwise instructions rather than eliciting the patient's own wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by asking what she wanted to work on, offering options, asking permission before interventions, and framing recommendations as choices that reinforced ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited evocation of internal motivation beyond tests and referral; the clinician offered many tools and solutions instead of drawing out the patient's values, reasons for change, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaved collaboratively and invited patient input on drafts and escalation options, but the interaction sometimes tilted toward clinician-led solutions rather than fully equal co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of change-oriented language and practical supports, but the clinician moved quickly to solutions and did not sufficiently explore or amplify the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided direct confrontation and validated biomedical concerns, which prevented escalation, but the clinician largely accommodated sustain-talk and used few strategic MI moves (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to lessen its influence.
