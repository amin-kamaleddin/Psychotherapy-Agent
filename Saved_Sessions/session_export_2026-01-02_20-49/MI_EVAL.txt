# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful (opening: “I’m sorry you’re feeling this way — that sounds very hard”), normalizes small steps, repeatedly uses supportive closings (“Take care”), and accepts patient’s “no promises” stance about meth without moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple accurate empathic moves (opening empathy, safety check, reflecting impact of infection on functioning, validating “don’t expect big changes overnight”). Could deepen by probing emotional subtext (e.g., shame/guilt, ambivalence) with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, concrete focus on sleep/activity, follow-up booking, outreach, and harm-reduction for meth; uses repeated recaps and schedules check-in. Slight drift to checklist/instruction reduced deeper exploration of meth as single target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong co-construction: asks which steps to start, offers draft text and accepts edits, asks patient preference for check-in method, and allows patient to set specifics (sleep times, message wording, booking follow-up).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforces choice and control (asks which steps, invites edits, offers follow-up preference, “if you feel worse…”), avoids coercion. Occasional directive phrasing (“try to avoid weekend meth…”) limits to a 4.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits concrete actions (choice of steps, sending text, booking follow-up) but does not sufficiently explore intrinsic motivations, values, or ambivalence about meth use; no scaling/confidence or reasons-for-change probing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats patient as decision-maker and collaborator (accepted rewording, shared planning). Could strengthen by explicitly eliciting patient expertise about weekend patterns and inviting problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language (agreed sleep schedule, movement, “I’ll try to skip meth…no promises”) and reinforces actions, but misses opportunities to amplify substance-specific change talk or use scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and uses harm-reduction/coping strategies (grounding, planning) appropriately, but did not use double-sided reflections, strategic summaries, or other techniques to intentionally soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
