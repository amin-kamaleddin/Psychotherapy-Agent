# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, validating frustration and practical limits without judgment. The clinician normalizes constraints and works within the patient's pace.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening captures both content and tone (irritation, practicality, time pressure). Questions and responses flow logically from the patient's expressed perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on small, practical changes tied to lowering blood pressure. Questions narrow to specific behaviors and implementation details without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a partnership with the patient generating the plan and the clinician building on those ideas. Responses adapt to real-world constraints rather than prescribing clinician-led solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice and control by offering options and honoring the patient's preferences and pace. The clinician affirms the patient's right to choose the simpler tracking system.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out reasons for change, practical ideas, and past strengths that support follow-through. Could further deepen evocation by eliciting scaled importance/confidence ratings or a stronger commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly establishes a collaborative stance with power-sharing; the patient's suggestions drive the plan. Clinician questions invite the patient's expertise and refine his solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and asks for specific, doable plans that fit the patient's life. Would be strengthened by more explicit amplification and consolidation of commitment language (e.g., confidence/importance scales).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and redirects toward change but misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or reframes to reduce ambivalence. The clinician could more strategically contrast sustain versus change language to help resolve ambivalence.
