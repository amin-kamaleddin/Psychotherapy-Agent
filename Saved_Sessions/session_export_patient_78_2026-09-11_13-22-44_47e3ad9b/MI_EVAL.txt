# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes ambivalence, consistently deferring to Lucía’s expertise. It feels somewhat formulaic and could be deepened with more specific affirmations of competence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Lucía’s frustration, care for her daughter, and ambivalence, including effective double‑sided language early on. Empathy could be strengthened by reflecting finer emotional nuance and using occasional integrative summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking and moves the conversation toward a concrete, actionable plan. Guidance remains client‑centered while purposefully narrowing toward implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The exchange is joint problem‑solving with open questions and integration of Lucía’s choices into the plan. Collaboration is slightly weakened by repeated, similar prompts that risk steering toward a prefigured answer.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly supported through repeated invitations, multiple options, and acceptance of partial or tentative commitments. The clinician avoids pressure and reinforces Lucía’s sense of choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits Lucía’s ideas and practical strategies, producing concrete change statements and plans. Evocation could be deepened by more exploration of personal values and systematic elicitation of confidence and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician emphasizes Lucía’s expertise and invites her input, co‑creating practical steps like gum and reminders. Partnership is reduced at times by repetitive option‑framed questions that may subtly steer responses.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces multiple concrete change statements and practical steps, leading to tentative commitments. To improve, the clinician could more selectively reflect and amplify change talk and elicit graduated commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician rolls with resistance, avoids arguing, and uses neutral reframes and options to reduce sustain talk momentum. However, the session moves quickly into planning with missed opportunities to explore the function of sustain talk and use double‑sided reflections.
