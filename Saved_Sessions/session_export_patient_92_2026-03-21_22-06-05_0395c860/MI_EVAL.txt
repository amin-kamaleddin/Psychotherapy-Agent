### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, opening with statements of support, thanking honesty, and normalizing slips. The clinician avoided labeling or shaming and affirmed Marek’s choices throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and timely reflections that helped Marek open up. Could deepen empathic work with more complex reflections capturing emotional/identity concerns (belonging, future worry).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on weekend drinking and steadily moved to a concrete plan with tactics, implementation intentions, and a scheduled check-in. The session linked discussion directly to doable behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co-constructed with Marek, routinely soliciting his preferences and rehearsing language together. The clinician kept Marek in the lead and made the plan jointly owned.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, asked permission before giving information, and framed the experiment as low-pressure. Language reinforced Marek’s sense of choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Marek’s motivations using scaling questions and a small experiment, and leveraged his 'now that I know' language. Could further probe personal values and long-term goals to strengthen discrepancy and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power was clearly shared: the clinician asked permission, offered choices, and invited Marek’s input on which strategies felt doable. Rehearsal and joint decision-making reinforced a strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, scaling, and proposing a small experiment, then built on emerging commitment. Missed opportunities to more selectively amplify and expand change statements toward stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used double-sided reflections and low-pressure experiments to acknowledge sustain reasons while redirecting to change. Could more deliberately use targeted double-sided reflections or reflections of the cost of staying the same to further soften sustain talk.
