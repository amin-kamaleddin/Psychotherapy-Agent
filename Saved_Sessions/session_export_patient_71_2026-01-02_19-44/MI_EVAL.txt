# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician established safety and used respectful, nonjudgmental language repeatedly (“This is a safe, confidential space,” “treat the first missed day as data”). Multiple affirmations and explicit agreement to a nonjudgmental weekly review. Slightly evaluative praise (“I’m proud of you”) made tone at times feel parental rather than purely unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged logistical barriers and validated realism (mornings are tricky; pairing with shower). Missed opportunities for deeper feeling and ambivalence reflections (little complex reflection of hope vs. skepticism; few empathic statements that name emotions).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently focused on a clear behavior change with concrete steps (anchor habit, pairing, 3–7 day commitment, fallback strategies, learning slot, rest day). Repeated summaries maintained momentum and prevented drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient choice (which anchor, timing, accountability method) and adapted plan to preferences (weekly chat, flexible learning slot). Clinician often proposed the multi‑step plan and led content, limiting full co‑construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected choices and checked preferences; reinforced patient agency (“Which anchor feels doable…?”, “treat any miss as data”). Occasional directive language and evaluative pride slightly undercut pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited specific commitments and some values-linked change talk (patient: “I feel more like myself after yoga,” “I’ll start tomorrow”). Did not consistently deepen intrinsic motivation or explore the meaning of values to evoke stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used partnership language and agreed to shared review processes, but clinician supplied initial multi‑point plan rather than eliciting patient‑generated strategies first, shifting more content ownership to clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient produced clear commitment language and value statements. Clinician elicited start date and specifics but did not frequently reflect or amplify change talk, nor use rulers or evocative questions to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized lapses and offered nonjudgmental strategies (treat misses as data, 5‑minute fallback, shrink dose). Could further use double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain talk while leaning toward change.
