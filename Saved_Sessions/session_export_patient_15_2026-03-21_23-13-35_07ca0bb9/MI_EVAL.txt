# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing and validating the patient's choices throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, but reflections were often summary-level and could deepen by addressing affect and the patient's minimizing tone more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior (10-minute walks) and moved the conversation logically from options to barriers to a specific, actionable plan tied to the blood-pressure goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as genuine joint problem-solving, consistently inviting the patient's input and building on his ideas while offering supports only after preference was stated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced the patient's right to choose, offered options, and checked preferences, supporting ownership of decisions and avoiding prescriptive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's own reasons and strategies for change, eliciting intrinsic motives and linking past success to current plans to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly framed the work as collaborative and deferred to the patient's expertise, co-creating the plan and soliciting his preferences at each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk through values-based questions and concrete planning, producing specific commitments and strengthened self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and redirected toward actionable steps, but missed opportunities to gently explore or reframe the patient's minimizing language about stress and alcohol risk.
