# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validating the client's pain and affirming strengths and efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and names emotions and motivations, though reflections sometimes stay summary-oriented and shift quickly into problem-solving rather than deeper feeling exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (sobriety, safety, housing, reunification) while allowing the client to choose the immediate agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly joint and co-constructed: the clinician invites priorities, rehearses with the client, and integrates the client's preferences into the agreement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, asking permission before giving advice, and affirming the client's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some internal motivations and links actions to values, but frequently supplies solutions and scripts instead of systematically drawing out the client's own reasons, importance, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fosters a collaborative stance and checks preferences, though at times supplies detailed scripts and checklists without first eliciting the client's own wording or priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to elicit change talk and highlights steps taken, but too often moves into offering solutions rather than amplifying and deepening the client's own change language and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively redirects ambivalence into practical compromises and concrete risk-mitigation strategies (trial period, rules, exit plan), though uses fewer nuanced MI techniques like double-sided reflections.
