# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing Jordan's concerns and offering practical options without shaming. Unconditional positive regard was clearly conveyed throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate, timely reflections that captured both cognitive and emotional elements (conflict about grandparents vs. needle worry) and acknowledged physical signs like voice shake. Empathic accuracy was high and helped build rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conversation stayed focused on the target behavior (getting the flu vaccine) with attention to relevant subtopics and concrete next steps like scripts, scheduling, and pain‑management. The clinician consistently steered toward action‑oriented planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was clearly co‑crafted with inclusive language and invitations to choose scripts and routines; the clinician practiced options with Jordan and incorporated his ideas. Power and decision‑making were shared throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician honored Jordan's choice, offered alternatives if he declined, asked permission before giving information, and framed suggestions as optional. Agency was reinforced and pressure was avoided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully elicited intrinsic motivation around protecting grandparents and linked values to behaviors, but missed opportunities to deepen and probe motivation (e.g., asking what protecting them would mean or using scaling). More systematic elicitation could strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a collaborative stance, invited preferences, rehearsed scripts together, and kept Jordan involved in choosing strategies. The interaction reflected genuine teamwork.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician evoked and reinforced change talk—especially concern for grandparents—and helped craft and rehearse scripts, but did not explicitly elicit stronger commitment language or use scaling/follow‑up questions to consolidate intent. Targeted amplification of change talk would deepen this domain.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician validated sustain talk about needles and side effects, avoided arguing, and offered harm‑reduction strategies (numbing spray, scheduling, breathing routine). Asking permission before facts and redirecting gently effectively softened sustain talk.
