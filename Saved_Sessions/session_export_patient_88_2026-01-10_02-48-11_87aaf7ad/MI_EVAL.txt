# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (e.g., opening support, thanking for honesty), creating a warm, respectful stance that facilitated disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and brief reflections that validated Marcus's context, but reflections were sometimes clinical and could probe deeper into emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on teen alcohol use with concrete behavior-linked goals and an actionable harm-reduction plan, keeping the session on task throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner by offering options, inviting role-play, and adapting to Marcus's requests, creating a cooperative and co-creative tone where Marcus's choices shaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice by offering options, asking permission before role-play, and respecting Marcus's low readiness, thereby avoiding coercive language and supporting autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used open questions and a readiness ruler to elicit motivations and gained some commitment to steps, but tended to pivot quickly to advice rather than deeply evoking Marcus's own reasons and strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally positioned Marcus as the expert and invited his preferences, but at times shifted to directive teaching and offered scripted solutions before eliciting his own strategy ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some specific change language and used a readiness ruler, yet missed opportunities to amplify fleeting ambivalence and often moved quickly to offering strategies instead of developing client-generated reasons or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and accepted minimizing statements while shifting to pragmatic harm-reduction, but could more actively use double-sided and strategic reflections to further move sustain talk toward ambivalence.
