# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental regard, validating frustration about waiting and normalizing difficulty. Avoided blaming language and made space for engagement despite initial defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely reflections that captured breathlessness, weight concerns, and time pressure. Reflections de-escalated irritation and maintained engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the conversation focused on weight-related behaviors, breathlessness, and BP follow-up, moving to concrete, time-limited actions. Progressed logically from problem identification to a specific first step and medical follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort by co-creating scripts, offering to draft notes, and asking for patient preferences. Inclusive language and invitations to rehearse reinforced equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and respected the patient's control by offering multiple options and avoiding pressuring language. Accepted realistic limits and supported patient agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited a prioritized doable behavior and used a confidence ruler to explore obstacles, drawing some motivation out of the patient. However, the clinician gave substantial advice and could have used more open questions to amplify the patient's own reasons and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fostered shared decision-making by inviting input on targets, reminders, and next steps, and offering co-creation of materials. These behaviors signaled respect for the patient's knowledge and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (e.g., asking which quick change felt doable and praising the plan). Missed opportunities to deepen change talk by probing values and reasons after the patient expressed interest in weight change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain-talk themes (time pressure, food-as-fuel), then redirected toward feasible options. Could strengthen practice with more double-sided reflections to explicitly balance sustain talk and invite change talk.
