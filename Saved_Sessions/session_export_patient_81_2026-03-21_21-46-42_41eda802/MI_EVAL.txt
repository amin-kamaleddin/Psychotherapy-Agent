# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (e.g., normalizing, reflecting, honoring limits). Reassured the client and accepted his preferences throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate summaries and reflective listening that captured ambivalence and practical concerns. Could deepen empathic work by probing the emotional meaning of losing community/identity with more feeling-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on stabilizing rent, attending auditions, and reducing harm from meth use while translating ambivalence into concrete short-term goals. Routinely steered toward specific first steps and safety checks to preserve momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed with frequent invitations for input, offers of options, and negotiated follow-up. The plan (shifts, selling gear, Alex check-ins) was developed jointly with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly asked permission, offered choices, and affirmed the client's control over decisions and possible slips. Supported ownership by asking what the client preferred and confirming willingness rather than pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the client's own motivations and solutions using the readiness ruler and move-5-to-7 question, then amplified client-generated plans. Drew motivation from Lukas rather than imposing reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative, consistently soliciting preferences and centering the client's expertise. Examples include asking permission to reflect, offering options, and inviting the client to name check-in supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (readiness scale, specific 'what would move you' question) and converted statements into a time‑bound action plan. Requested a concrete first step and affirmed commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk respectfully and used double-sided reflections to balance concerns with planning, avoiding amplification. Could further weaken sustain language by explicitly summarizing costs of staying the same and using more strategic reflections.
