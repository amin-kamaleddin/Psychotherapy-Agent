# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental. Repeatedly expressed unconditional positive regard and permission language (“You’re in charge,” “I’ll support you,” “I won’t contact Ana”), normalized fears and honored Rosa’s preferences (Exchanges 1, 6, 10, later confirmations).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (accurate paraphrases, normalizing ambivalence). Missed opportunities for deeper affective/identity-focused reflections (e.g., “I just can’t imagine not going through life without smoking” could be explored more deeply).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and concrete next steps (importance/confidence scaling, options, gum selection, reduction target, two‑week check‑in, referral and scheduling logistics). Balanced guiding with collaboration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance—offered options, invited Rosa to choose preferred strategies, honored her decisions about whom to tell and scheduling preferences, and co‑constructed the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy throughout (“You’re in charge”), used permission language, avoided pressuring, and respected Rosa’s choices about Ana and clinic contact.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Rosa’s reasons and readiness (0–10 scaling, “what would make your confidence go up…”, choice of options). Could more consistently amplify and summarize change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated partnership—shared decision making, joint planning (specific reduction goal, gum use, clinic referral), and planned follow‑up support/check‑ins.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (Rosa’s statements of need and willingness, selection of gum/plan). Would benefit from more strategic summaries and targeted questions to elicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk respectfully—normalized concerns and offered coping strategies (weight, withdrawal, identity). Could increase use of double‑sided reflections and identity‑focused reframing to further reduce sustain talk.
