# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, using validation and praise throughout the exchange to normalize Nadine's feelings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking were used (timely paraphrases, normalization, and soothing suggestions) that matched Nadine's expressed fatigue and doubts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, consistent focus on rest/sleep and immediate practical supports, steering conversation toward a concrete two‑step plan and a specific follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as joint problem-solving: the clinician invited input, incorporated Nadine's choices, coordinated outside supports, and used collegial language rather than being directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered options, solicited Nadine's preferences (including follow-up mode/time), and affirmed Nadine's control over what she tries, reinforcing her sense of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some willingness and a concrete commitment to report back were elicited, but the clinician did little to draw out Nadine's intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence for making changes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with choices and negotiated plans, though the clinician sometimes shifted quickly into specific instructions without fully inviting Nadine to co‑design alternatives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were useful prompts and reinforcements for change talk (choices, praise, follow-up) but limited efforts to deepen or shape stronger commitment language, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician validated skepticism and redirected to small, manageable steps and contingency planning, though missed some opportunities for double‑sided reflections that explicitly named ambivalence.
