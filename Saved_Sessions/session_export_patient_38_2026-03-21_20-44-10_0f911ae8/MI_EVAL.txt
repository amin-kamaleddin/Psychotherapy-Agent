# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, using normalization and affirmation throughout. There was no blaming or shaming and the patient’s ambivalence was validated repeatedly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening were evident with accurate, repeated reflections of emotion and ambivalence. The clinician acknowledged the patient’s relief and shaken feelings in a way that demonstrated high-quality empathic engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on contraception and decision-relevant steps, with concrete options and actionable next steps offered. The clinician repeatedly circled back to the target behavior without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the interaction as a true partnership, asking permission before sharing information and co-constructing tools like the question list and script. Choices about format and next steps were offered to match the patient’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reinforced the patient’s control over decisions and offered nonpressured options (info, appointment, reminder tools). Decisions were left in the patient’s hands while expert information was provided when requested.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions elicited the patient’s motivations, but the clinician relied more on information-giving than systematically drawing out and amplifying intrinsic motivation. Opportunities to deepen change talk with scaling or future-oriented prompts were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly framed the work as joint and consistently sought the patient’s input and permission. Co-creating practical supports (scripts, lists, videos) and honoring the patient’s pace demonstrated strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were effective attempts to evoke change talk and the patient offered some change language, but the clinician did not consistently reflect or amplify those statements. Missed opportunities included not following up on strong statements (e.g., 'never want that scare again') to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged and neutralized sustain talk, normalizing ambivalence and offering nonpressured alternatives and small steps. More frequent use of double-sided reflections could further soften sustain talk and encourage movement.
