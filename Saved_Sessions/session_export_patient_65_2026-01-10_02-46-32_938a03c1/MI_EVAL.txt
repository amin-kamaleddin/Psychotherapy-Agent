# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Julian’s feelings, normalizing his worry. At times the clinician shifted quickly to practical instruction rather than deeper individualized acknowledgement of his guardedness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured surface feelings and provided brief normalization, but empathic responses were short and focused on logistics. The clinician missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections probing underlying emotions like shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (completing community service) and moved from problem identification to concrete next steps. The session stayed tightly tied to deadlines, scripts, schedules, and actionable tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered customization and options but produced most content (scripts, schedules) himself, leaving Julian mainly to confirm facts. The interaction felt clinician-led rather than a shared exploration where the client’s ideas shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirmed choice, respected Julian’s reluctance to call, and offered alternatives while inviting acceptance or decline of options. More explicit statements reinforcing Julian’s ownership of decisions and eliciting preferences before offering solutions would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitments and concrete details but did not probe Julian’s personal reasons or intrinsic motivations. Missed chances to use evocative questions or scaling to draw out internal motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included collaborative offers and invited input on availability, but the clinician largely directed the content and created ready-made plans. Greater power-sharing and asking Julian for his own solutions would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted some change-related statements through practical questions and offers to draft materials, resulting in concrete commitments. However, the clinician focused on problem-solving rather than intentionally eliciting or amplifying Julian’s personal reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and instead validated reluctance while offering alternatives like case worker involvement. To reach top score, the clinician could employ more double-sided reflections to acknowledge reluctance while highlighting reasons for change.
