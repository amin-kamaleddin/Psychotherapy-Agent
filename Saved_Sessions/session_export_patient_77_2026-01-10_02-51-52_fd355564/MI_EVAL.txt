# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent respect, validation, and permission (e.g., normalizing feelings, stop rules). Score not 5 because some early responses are clinical and prescriptive rather than more personalized, warm affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and reflective statements that name frustration, fear, and loneliness. Falls short of a 5 because the clinician often moves quickly to problem-solving instead of exploring the adolescent's internal emotional experience more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on weight-related behavior with progressive, concrete steps and measurable targets. The session repeatedly brings conversation back to practical actions and contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction feels largely collaborative with choices offered and adaptations for privacy/obsession concerns. Not a full 5 because the clinician sometimes leads with expert prescriptive content rather than co-constructing the plan from the client's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering options, validating refusals, and emphasizing the patient's decision-making and stop rules. Consistently reinforces choice and ownership throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some preferences and change-oriented statements, but the session is largely clinician-driven with technical guidance. Missed structured evocation (scaling, pros/cons, past successes) to draw out the client's internal motivation more fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and invites input, offering to tailor plans and check in. Partnership is weakened at times by shifting into an expert role with detailed prescriptions rather than jointly building the plan from the client's perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for change talk and affirms client choices, but relies on giving solutions rather than systematically evoking and amplifying the client's own reasons, confidence, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and offers low-obsession alternatives and clear stop rules without arguing. Not a 5 because some initial directives could unintentionally validate helplessness, and more double-sided reflections would better soften sustain talk.
