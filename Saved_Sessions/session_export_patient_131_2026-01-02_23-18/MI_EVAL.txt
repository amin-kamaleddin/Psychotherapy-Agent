# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., “Tēnā koe Wiremu — kia ora. I’m glad you came,” safety check). Repeated normalizing statements and affirmations (“That’s understandable,” “Choosing to call Mum… is a strong, self‑directed step”) and offers of support (staying on the line) demonstrate full acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., “It sounds like safety and seeing your kids matter a lot…”, “pride and fear sit together”). Reflections capture emotions and ambivalence and are followed by validating summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained focused on drinking, housing, and contact with children; clinician narrowed options to concrete actions (call whānau, set limits, book intake) and elicited specific commitments and a follow‑up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power sharing evident—clinician invites preference and permission (role‑play vs. call now), defers to Wiremu’s readiness, and frames planning as joint problem‑solving (“If she says no, we’ll plan the next step together”).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces Wiremu’s choice and agency (“Feels like I’m choosing a path, not being pushed”), avoids coercive language, and links encouragement to Wiremu’s decisions and competence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful elicitation of internal motivation (values, reasons, scaling for readiness and confidence) and building on those motivations to shape concrete steps (calls, intake, SV appointment).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership through staying on calls, co‑creating the plan (two‑week reset, intake), and offering ongoing support (scheduled check‑in), reflecting mutual engagement and joint responsibility.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple forms of change talk elicited and amplified (desire/commitment, reasons, ability/confidence). Clinician reflected and reinforced commitments (calls placed, appointments planned) and used scaling to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged and partly redirected (independence vs whānau support), but clinician could more often use double‑sided reflections and explicitly elicit/reshape arguments for the status quo to further weaken sustain talk.
