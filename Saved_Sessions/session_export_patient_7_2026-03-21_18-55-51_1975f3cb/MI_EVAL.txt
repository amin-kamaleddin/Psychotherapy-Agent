# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and respect for the client's boundaries, repeatedly acknowledging choice to remain silent and avoiding judgment or confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated consistent empathic presence and validation, tolerating silence and matching the client's need for space, but reflections stayed surface-level without exploring underlying feelings like fear or mistrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear engagement-and-safety focus throughout the visit, avoiding topic drift and keeping interventions aligned with the session goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the encounter as collaborative by offering options and explicitly following the client's lead rather than adopting an expert-driven stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the client's control and provided choices instead of directives, clearly supporting the client's autonomy and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited evocation of internal motivation—asked one preference question but did not use low-demand prompts, scaling, or curiosity invitations to draw out even minimal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed a strong partnership stance by offering to follow the client's lead, declining an expert-fix role, and consistently sharing power over the session pace and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal efforts to elicit change talk; offered options but did not invite reasons, desire, ability, or need for change or use curiosity/hypothetical prompts to evoke ambivalence or preference for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by validating autonomy and not arguing, but made few strategic moves (e.g., double-sided reflections) to actively reframe or soften sustain talk.
