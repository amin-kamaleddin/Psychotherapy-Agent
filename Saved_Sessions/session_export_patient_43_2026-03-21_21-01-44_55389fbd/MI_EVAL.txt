# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established clear unconditional positive regard, avoided judgmental language, and repeatedly validated Eric, creating a safe space for honest disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and matched Eric's language, helping him feel understood, though they were somewhat functional and could probe deeper emotional meaning or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed consistently focused on smoking and practical change options, guiding toward a specific, measurable harm‑reduction experiment without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with joint problem‑solving and co‑creation of the plan, inviting Eric’s input at each step and deferring to his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed Eric's control by offering options, honoring his choices (e.g., not involving his wife), and normalizing delay in commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician drew out Eric’s motivations and elicited change ideas and a confidence rating, but could have probed more for personally meaningful reasons, values, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician clearly positioned Eric as the decision‑maker, offering choices and asking preferences while co‑drafting language and plans when invited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provider elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and a one‑week experiment, but could have amplified and explored desire, reasons, and past successes to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double‑sided reflections to redirect toward harm reduction, though there were missed opportunities to briefly explore the costs of sustain talk in Eric’s terms.
