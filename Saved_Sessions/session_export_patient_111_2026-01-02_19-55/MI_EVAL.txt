# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (opening: “confidential, nonjudgmental space”); repeatedly acknowledged and respected patient boundaries (“You’re showing clear boundaries — I respect that”), avoided blaming or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections of frustration and routine (“I hear frustration about being pushed…”; “You care about your wife…”). Validating tone maintained throughout, though missed some opportunities for deeper emotional reflections linking ambivalence and worry about losing his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate behavioral target maintained (tobacco harm-reduction). Produced a concrete, measurable plan (15‑minute delay, 7‑day log: time, craving 0–10, mood, substitute) and closed with explicit next steps and safety instructions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered choices and invited preferences rather than prescribing (explore likes/worries vs. harm reduction). Plan was co‑constructed—the patient chose the 15‑minute delay and clinician refined it into a measurable experiment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed patient’s right to choose (“It’s okay to do it for your wife; you still get to choose”), offered noncoercive options, and respected limits (promised no lectures).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited patient perspective and change‑relevant material (asked what he liked and worried about; patient voiced concern about losing his wife). Did not sufficiently deepen intrinsic motivators or explore past change experience to strengthen internal reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered teamwork (“we’ll review what changed and decide next steps”) and used invitations. Occasional clinician‑led structure (detailed list of options and repeated log format) reduced opportunities for co‑design of tools.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Achieved concrete commitment to the 15‑minute delay and captured relational change talk (worry about wife). Missed chances to amplify or reflect change talk further (desire, ability, reasons, confidence) to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and confrontation, reframed toward harm reduction and normalized a testable step. Could have used more double‑sided reflections to explicitly link sustain and change talk and validate ambivalence.
