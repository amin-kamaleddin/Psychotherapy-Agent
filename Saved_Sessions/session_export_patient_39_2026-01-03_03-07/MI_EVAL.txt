# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance throughout (“Let’s figure this out together,” validating Elias’s limits and choices). Multiple reflections validate his concerns and preferences; no minimizing or blaming (≥8 turns).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Accurate, nuanced reflections of both content and feeling (repeated mirror of “very, very beautiful” vs. pain/fatigue). Sustained perspective-taking evident across many turns (≥8–10), capturing ambivalence and emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Session stays focused on increasing brief, safe activity and progressively narrows to a concrete action (one 5‑minute walk). Clinician advances planning and risk mitigation (cane, pill, chair) with planning-focused questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Clear collaborative stance (“Let’s figure this out together”), frequent invitations for Elias’s input, and incorporation of his preferences into the plan. Decisions are patient-shaped and shared.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Repeated affirmation of choice and avoidance of coercion; clinician respects Elias’s limits (“I won’t promise more than that”) and uses invitational questions to support autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Clinician elicits Elias’s values and reasons for change (hope, independence, not being a burden) and links them to the proposed behavior. Repeated evocative questions surface personal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Work framed as a joint enterprise with power-sharing language and repeated checks for Elias’s preferences regarding supports; patient leads practical decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Clinician elicits, reflects, and amplifies tentative change statements (e.g., “Maybe one 5‑minute walk…,” “I’ll try that”), explores benefits, and reinforces potential positive outcomes (hope, mood, independence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged and redirected toward safe, small steps (normalizing worry, focusing on supports). Fewer double-sided reflections and limited explicit confidence/commitment probes; sustain talk remains present and could be softened further with strategic summaries and confidence elicitation.
