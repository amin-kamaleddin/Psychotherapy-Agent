# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Welcoming, respectful, and nonjudgmental tone with clear safety advice and honoring patient requests; could use more explicit verbal affirmations that normalize the patient’s anxiety and warmer individualized language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides tailored, practical information addressing safety and monitoring but offers limited reflective listening or explicit acknowledgment of the patient’s emotions and unease.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a very clear, focused plan on switching meds, washout timing, tapering, BP thresholds, and emergency actions with concrete, actionable guidance for each question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames work as a team effort with prescriber and pharmacist involvement and shared documentation, but the conversation remains clinician‑led with missed opportunities to co‑construct the plan with patient input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects safety decisions and urges changes only with a signed plan, though could more often invite the patient’s preferences and explicitly acknowledge their right to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Gives comprehensive clinical information but rarely elicits the patient’s own motivations, priorities, or reasons for change, resulting in a clinician‑driven, informational approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some collaborative steps (involving pharmacist/prescriber and consent for contact) but largely assumes the expert role and rarely invites the patient to co‑author the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Misses opportunities to evoke and amplify the patient’s change language; relies on directive guidance rather than open questions, scaling, or reflections to draw out motivation and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids encouraging sustain talk and validates caution with safety advice, but uses mostly informational responses instead of MI techniques (double‑sided reflections or reframing) to gently pivot toward change.
