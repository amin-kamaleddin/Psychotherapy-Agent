# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing ambivalence and validating the client’s priorities while avoiding blame. Reinforced practical plans in a respectful, affirmative way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured concrete priorities and ambivalence, showing strong understanding of the client’s perspective. Missing deeper affective reflections that would explore emotions like worry, pride, or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on protecting academics through alternative social options and progressed logically to a specific, measurable step. Questions and reflections consistently advanced the agreed target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a joint exploration with brainstorming and co‑construction of the plan rather than clinician-led directives. Repeated invitations for the client to choose next steps demonstrated equal‑partner work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices and framed steps as client decisions using low‑pressure, trial language. Affirmed the client’s right to decide and supported ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical motivations and specific change language (resume, schedule, not 'babysitting' oneself) and strengthened commitment to a small step. Could deepen evocation by drawing out broader values, emotions, or future‑oriented meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the interaction as collaborative, asking for the client’s ideas and preferences and sharing power in decision‑making. Language supported client expertise and ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk and commitment, leading to a concrete plan (one meeting this month) with identified supports. Used reflections and follow‑ups that strengthened the client’s stated intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and neutrally acknowledged ambivalence, preventing contestation. Missed opportunities for strategic softening (e.g., double‑sided reflections or selectively privileging change language) to further shift subtle sustain arguments.
