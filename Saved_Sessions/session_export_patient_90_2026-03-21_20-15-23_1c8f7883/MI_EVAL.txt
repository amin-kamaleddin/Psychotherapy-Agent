# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized Marcus's feelings, avoided blame, and affirmed his limits, creating a warm, nonjudgmental tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician showed strong perspective-taking with accurate, timely reflections that captured Marcus's mix of fear, attachment, and overwhelm and validated his experience without interrupting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear focus on asthma management and repeatedly guided conversation toward concrete, actionable steps such as calling the doctor, bedroom boundaries, and safety instructions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician and Marcus jointly drafted messages, role‑played, and problem‑solved, sharing power and checking in frequently to co-construct practical plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasized Marcus's choice and control by offering multiple options, tailoring recommendations to his constraints, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Marcus's reasons and options and secured some client-generated commitments, but relied on clinician-provided solutions and could have drawn out more values-based reasons, confidence, and timing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Marcus as a collaborator, inviting his input on wording and next steps and co-creating scripts and plans that honored his expertise and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively evoked initial change talk (importance rating, doable options) but could have amplified and consolidated it more by asking what made it an 8 or eliciting clearer commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician often redirected sustain talk toward change-oriented alternatives and validated ambivalence but used few double-sided reflections or explicit reframing to soften sustain talk more strategically.
