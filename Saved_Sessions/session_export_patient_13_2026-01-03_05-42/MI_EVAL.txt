# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental respect and positive regard (e.g., “I accept that tension with you,” “You’re caring and cautious, not weak”). Multiple explicit validations across the session normalized Anara’s experience and supported safety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, frequent reflective listening and perspective-taking. Complex empathic summaries tied emotions to context (“scared,” “overwhelming,” childcare/cost concerns). Reflections appear in nearly every turn, helping patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behavior (seeking medical evaluation/home visit). Questions and reflections consistently steered toward concrete, achievable next steps and produced a specific action (short text).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative stance—repeated invitations for patient input (“which of those feels most doable?”), co-construction of the text, and shared problem-solving throughout the exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported patient choice and control (offered options, asked which felt doable, avoided pressure). Clinician deferred to Anara’s judgment and emphasized her agency in deciding next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations (caring for child) and barriers, and drew out feasible actions (home visit, short text). Could have more consistently reflected and amplified change-related statements to strengthen momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Anara as the expert in her life—invited her preferences, offered to co-write/practice the message, and deferred to her decisions; resulted in a jointly agreed plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and elicited concrete language (“home visit feels most doable,” “I think I can try that tomorrow”). To reach top score, clinician could have amplified change talk more and elicited firmer commitment planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, used double-sided reflections and redirected to small steps. Could further reduce sustain talk with more strategic reflections that immediately juxtapose change and sustain statements.
