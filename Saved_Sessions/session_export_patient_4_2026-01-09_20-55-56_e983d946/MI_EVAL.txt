# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with frequent validation and normalisation of the patient's distress (e.g., 'I hear you…', acknowledging exhaustion). The clinician shows steady warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately naming emotions and summarising concerns. The clinician mirrors priorities and checks understanding, demonstrating highly accurate empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on an actionable goal—obtaining urgent investigations/second opinion—and orients every intervention toward that objective. Questions and offers consistently steer the session toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed: the clinician repeatedly solicits the patient's preferences and integrates her wording into the plan. The psychiatrist functions as a facilitator, sharing power and following the patient's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options, asks permission, and avoids pressuring language, reinforcing patient choice (e.g., asking which help to prepare and whether to send the message). This consistently affirms the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit motivations (asking what matters and what would be different) yielded change‑relevant material, but the clinician focused on logistics rather than systematically drawing out values, reasons, or confidence for longer-term change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates genuine partnership through co‑drafting the GP message, checking wording, and asking about prior effective strategies. The clinician treats the patient as an equal collaborator in planning next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk about tests enabling safety and return to work, but did not amplify or deepen statements about longer‑term coping or behaviour change (limited exploration of reasons, confidence, or steps).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although empathic, the clinician largely validated and reinforced the patient's biomedical sustain talk by aligning with requests for urgent tests and strong wording. There was little use of strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to gently soften certainty about the biomedical explanation.
