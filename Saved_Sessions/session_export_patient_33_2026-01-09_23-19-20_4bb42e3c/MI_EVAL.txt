# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing difficulties and repeatedly validating disclosures. No shaming or blaming language; tone is warm and accepting throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong empathic listening and some reflections, tolerating guardedness and inviting patient choice. Could deepen empathy with more complex, affect‑focused reflections about emotions, housing fears, and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent direction toward concrete harm‑reduction and safety goals and sequences interventions efficiently. Keeps the session focused on actionable steps and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invites patient choice, co‑constructs scripts, and uses role‑play with permission. Preserves an equal‑partner feel while offering expertise as needed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by offering options, using permission language, and respecting the patient's decisions about what to practice. No coercive language; consistently affirms patient control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete motivation for immediate harm‑reduction actions and reinforces readiness and commitments. Could strengthen evocation by exploring intrinsic reasons, values, and confidence to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and engages the patient in role‑play and skill practice. At times shifts to more prescriptive scripts; inviting the patient to generate wording more often would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces specific change talk around harm reduction and readiness, with useful affirmations and concrete commitments. Would benefit from more systematic elicitation of the patient's own reasons, values, and imagined benefits to broaden change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward pragmatic safety and harm‑reduction options. Could use more double‑sided reflections and explicit normalization of ambivalence to strategically soften sustain talk.
