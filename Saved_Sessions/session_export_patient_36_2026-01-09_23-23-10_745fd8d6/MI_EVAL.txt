# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates respect and nonjudgmental support with frequent affirmations, creating an encouraging tone. At times the acceptance feels procedural rather than deeply personalized, with few extended validations of Elena's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows effort to understand by asking about specific situations and normalizing emotions, which supports connection. Reflections are often brief or absent as the clinician moves quickly to problem-solving, limiting deeper empathic resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on setting boundaries at work, with questions and planning that logically progress from identification to rehearsal and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Solicits Elena's input, adapts the script based on her tweak, and co-constructs the test plan and debrief, reflecting genuine teamwork. Offers follow-up support and keeps the client's ideas central.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, asks permission, and respects Elena's preferences (e.g., role-play vs checklist, dropping in for debrief), reinforcing her agency. The clinician lets Elena select the plan that fits her comfort level.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Links the plan to Elena's values and elicits motivation through a small, meaningful test that taps internal reasons for change. However, much of the session shifts into solution delivery, with limited reflective amplification of her change language or exploration of confidence/importance scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Overall collaborative with frequent offers of options and co-creation of a plan, including adapting wording per Elena's preference. Some prescriptive moves (providing multiple ready-made scripts and detailed steps before eliciting her own wording) slightly weaken power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits actionable change talk by focusing on a concrete situation, a small test, and a debrief plan, helping Elena articulate desire and steps. The clinician could better amplify and reflect the client's own change language and elicit stronger commitment statements or scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Proactively addresses barriers (guilt, fear) and offers normalization and coping strategies—naming guilt, breathing, values reminders, and pep texts—which reduces the influence of sustain talk. The clinician refrains from arguing for change and helps plan manageable steps.
