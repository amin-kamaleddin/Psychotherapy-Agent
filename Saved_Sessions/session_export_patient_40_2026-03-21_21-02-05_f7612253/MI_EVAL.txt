# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating Elias' shame and affirming his strengths. No shaming or labeling occurred and the clinician honored progress throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking with accurate reflections, paraphrases, and summaries that validated feelings and checked understanding. The clinician repeatedly used reflective listening to convey deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session focused and pragmatic on relapse prevention, moving purposefully from reflection to concrete planning and rehearsal. The clinician consistently returned to specific tasks like craving scripts, boundary lines, and check‑ins.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt co‑authored: the clinician presented options, incorporated Elias' wording, invited revisions, and rehearsed with him. The plan was clearly created together rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Elias' choice and agency by asking permission, offering alternatives, and inviting him to pick what to work on. Language avoided coercion and emphasized his control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited important internal motivations (kids, shame, pride) and prompted strategy generation, but moved relatively quickly to offering detailed tools. More sustained elicitation and evoking of deeper reasons/confidence before providing solutions would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and respected Elias' expertise, asking to co‑create the plan and inviting his edits. The clinician honored client influence and choices throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language and asked evocative questions about supports and plans. Missed some opportunities to amplify and deepen change talk with scaling or further exploration of desire/importance/confidence before offering solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without feeding it, normalized doubts, and shifted toward planning, which reduced resistance. Could have used more double‑sided reflections and selective amplification of change statements to further soften sustain talk.
