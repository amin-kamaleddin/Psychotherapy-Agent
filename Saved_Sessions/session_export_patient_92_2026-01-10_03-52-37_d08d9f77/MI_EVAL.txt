# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, validating Marek’s experience and avoiding labels. This respectful tone preserved rapport and kept the patient engaged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately summarized Marek’s perspective and concerns, helping him feel heard. The clinician generally stayed at content-level paraphrase and missed some opportunities for deeper affective or complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on weekend drinking and moved purposefully from feedback to concrete options and a specific plan. It ended with measurable actions and a follow-up check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a joint process with options offered and the plan co-constructed (water, text to Lukas, phone alarms). The clinician invited Marek’s input and offered supports like role-play or drafting messages.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supported choice by offering options, asking permission before advice, and avoiding pressuring language. Changes were framed as Marek’s decisions with backup options if the plan felt odd.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions and a confidence ruler elicited genuine reasons and a concrete experiment. There were missed opportunities to link small changes to broader values and to amplify tentative change statements more strongly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned Marek as the expert and fostered shared decision-making, asking what Marek wanted to explore and which options felt realistic. The resulting plan reflected Marek’s preferences and input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change talk, offered doable options, and asked a confidence ruler to support commitment. When Marek offered early change language, the clinician could have amplified and explored it further to deepen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation, reframed sustain talk, and offered risk-reduction strategies that preserved valued social aspects. More strategic use of double-sided reflections or summary reflections could have more actively shifted sustain talk toward change.
