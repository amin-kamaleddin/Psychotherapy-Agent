# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and positive regard; repeated affirmations (e.g., “You’ve made solid progress…,” “well done”). Normalized setbacks and encouraged nonjudgmental tracking. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and often followed immediately by directive advice — would benefit from one more personalized, emotionally attuned validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some brief empathic statements and acknowledgement of worry about work stress (“Cravings at work are normal,” “It’s normal your confidence sits at 6/10”). Limited use of reflective listening or complex reflections to explore emotional ambivalence; interaction moved quickly into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on smoking reduction with clear, concrete behavioral steps (scheduling, 10‑minute delay, 4‑4‑4 breathing, lunch walk/chew, phone alarm, implementation intention, support text). Clinician maintained agenda and measurable actions throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited patient input (asked which strategy to try first, solicited confidence and commitment). However, clinician often prescribed specific tactics and repeatedly presented a checklist of steps, shifting power toward clinician agenda rather than fully co‑constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Solicited commitments and confidence ratings, supporting autonomy to an extent. But repeated prescriptive statements and restating of the plan limited space for patient ownership; could more explicitly emphasize choice and invite patient-generated options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open invitation to speak and some elicitation of choices and commitment (confidence ratings). Missed deeper elicitation of personal reasons/values and did not probe what getting to five cigarettes would mean for the patient; evocation was present but superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elements of partnership present (checking patient choices, collaborative language at times). Overall dynamic tilted toward expert-driven planning with scripted instructions; patient contributions shaped parts of the plan but were not fully leveraged for joint design.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient offered multiple instances of change talk (journal, reduction, commitment to strategies). Clinician acknowledged and elicited confidence/commitment but did not consistently reflect or amplify change talk or ask targeted questions to deepen patient motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician normalized cravings and encouraged nonjudgmental tracking, which helps reduce shame. Sustain talk was acknowledged but largely redirected into planning rather than explored with double-sided or empathic reflections to integrate ambivalence.
