# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent validation and normalization; offered practical help while avoiding labeling and minimizing Naomi’s experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid understanding of Naomi’s bodily experience with useful reflections and psychoeducation, but could deepen empathic exploration of underlying emotions, fears, and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on managing anxiety during shoots with every intervention tied to that target and concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed—clinician and Naomi jointly developed and iteratively refined the checklist, practiced together, and implemented Naomi’s edits and feedback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, asked permission, and respected Naomi’s pace and preferences, framing suggestions as invitations rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited hope, small commitments, and willingness to practice, using concrete rehearsal to build motivation, but could further draw out values, reasons, and confidence to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and power-sharing with co-design of tools, though the clinician occasionally shifted into an information-heavy, expert-led mode instead of eliciting Naomi’s ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and built on Naomi’s statements about wanting improvement and willingness to try, and secured small commitments; could more deliberately amplify and reflect change talk to bolster commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
