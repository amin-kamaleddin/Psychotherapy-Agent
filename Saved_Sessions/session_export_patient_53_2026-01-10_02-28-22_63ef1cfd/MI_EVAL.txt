# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Isabella’s experience, using validating reflections that reduced judgment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-quality empathic listening with frequent, accurate reflections of affect and ambivalence, showing sustained understanding of anxiety and hesitancy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on engaging in community activities with concrete, very small steps and resisted drifting from the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—invited Isabella’s ideas, checked preferences, and co‑constructed what progress would look like rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported choice and control by offering options, emphasizing safety, and using nonpressuring language that reinforced Isabella’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client-generated ideas and small-step motives but could further deepen evocation by exploring personal values, confidence, and moving toward stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated Isabella as the expert on what felt safe and doable, repeatedly asking which options felt most manageable and seeking her preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited change talk through questions about small steps and markers of progress, but missed opportunities to amplify tentative language and consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk nonjudgmentally and redirected to tiny steps, though many reflections mirrored sustain phrases verbatim; more double‑sided reflections or reframing could better tip ambivalence toward change.
