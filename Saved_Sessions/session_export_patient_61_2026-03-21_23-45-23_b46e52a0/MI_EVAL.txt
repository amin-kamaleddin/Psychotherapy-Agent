# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses respect and normalizes Jonathan's experience, avoiding judgment (e.g., validating privacy concerns and busy mornings). These moves convey unconditional positive regard and reduce shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate, nuanced reflective listening that captures both content and feeling throughout the session. Reflections repeatedly paraphrase the patient's concerns (privacy, hassle, fatigue), demonstrating strong attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on morning blood‑glucose checks and preparatory steps, steering the conversation toward a concrete night‑before prep plan. All questions and suggestions relate back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as joint problem‑solving with repeated invitations for the patient's input and co‑creation of plans (e.g., ‘‘Let’s figure this out together’’). The clinician bases suggestions on the patient's ideas and shares power in decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors Jonathan's choices and preferences, offering options and avoiding pressure (e.g., asking which cue sounds most doable). The clinician refrains from lecturing and frames choices as the patient's to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal reasons and concrete criteria for change (lower numbers, less midday fatigue) and explores past habit formation, but stops short of deeper value linking. Could further draw out personal motives and confidence/commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative, repeatedly inviting Jonathan's ideas and deferring to his preferences (privacy, low‑hassle). The clinician co‑creates plans rather than prescribing, conveying shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some tentative change talk (e.g., ‘‘I could try checking once a morning’’) but does not systematically amplify or deepen desire, ability, reasons, or commitment. Missed opportunities include lack of scaling/confidence questions and limited strategic reflections to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk (hassle, forgetting) but often moves quickly to solutions without using double‑sided reflections to highlight ambivalence. Sustain talk remains present and occasionally dominates the conversation.
