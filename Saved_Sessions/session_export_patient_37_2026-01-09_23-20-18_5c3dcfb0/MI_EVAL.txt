# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates clear unconditional positive regard and respect, repeatedly validates concerns and honors the patient's request for time and written information without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good understanding with reflective responses and validation of worry and information needs, but leans toward didactic summaries rather than deeper or elaborated emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation tightly focused on the vaccine decision, links information and actions to that target, and guides the interaction toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as joint problem-solving—co‑develops scripts, drafts the email, and consistently invites and incorporates the patient's input and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the patient's right to decide by offering options, asking what she wants, accommodating time, and avoiding pressure or prescriptions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some information and choices but mainly uses an educational approach and misses opportunities to draw out the patient's own values, reasons, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates genuine power-sharing and partnership by asking permission, co-creating materials, and treating the patient as an equal partner throughout the visit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites questions and the patient expresses some change talk, but the clinician does not strategically elicit, amplify, or explore the patient's own motivations and benefits for protecting the child.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids argument, normalizes concerns, and provides calm, factual responses and alternatives, though could use more MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to gently redirect sustain talk.
