# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, with repeated validations and normalizing statements that created an accepting stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections of symptoms and functioning, but missed some opportunities to reflect deeper emotions (e.g., shame, fear of judgment) to reach a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior and moved the session toward concrete goals with a specific exposure hierarchy and measurable first steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative with co‑construction of the plan, frequent solicitation of preferences, and avoidance of an authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the client's choice and control by offering options, asking preferences, and using agreed stop rules that reinforced Naomi's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from the client and reinforced her statements of hope and success, but could have probed more for intrinsic motivators, values, and scaling of importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision‑making and client expertise, though at times offered somewhat clinician‑led plans rather than inviting more of Naomi's ideas before proposing options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk and translated desire into small concrete steps, but could have amplified importance, reasons, and confidence with more targeted questions and summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward small experiments and coping tools, but used few double‑sided reflections or strategic reframes to further diminish resistance.
