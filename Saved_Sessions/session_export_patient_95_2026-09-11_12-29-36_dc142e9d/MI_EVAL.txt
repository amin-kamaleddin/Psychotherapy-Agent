# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (frequent validations, permission language, stayed with patient); no blaming or punitive language, creating a safe atmosphere for a suicidal, distressed patient.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and complex reflections of ambivalence, but some reflections are repetitive and could deepen feeling-focused work to better explore the emotional color of 'unbearable' and loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, purposeful focus on the presenting concerns (safety, sleep, means restriction), moving from validation to concrete, doable steps and a concrete safety action without losing the patient's emotional experience.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-creates concrete steps, offers practical supports (drafting text, staying in chat), and consistently defers to the patient's expertise and preferences in planning and support.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, asking which feels most doable, respecting pace, and asking what feels possible rather than imposing actions, including during safety planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own reasons and resources (desire for energy for her sister) and draws out feasible, self-generated steps without supplying external reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as an equal partner—invites patient decisions, seeks permission for suggestions, and co-creates safety steps so the plan is clearly shaped by the patient's ideas and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with small, doable options and reflections that produced concrete commitments, though could better amplify and summarize change statements and more directly elicit confidence/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk and redirects with validation plus small experiments (bedtime, locking pills), but could more deliberately use double-sided reflections to juxtapose sustain and change language and help resolve ambivalence.
