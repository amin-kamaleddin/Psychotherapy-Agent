# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed respect and affirmed Richard's perspective, avoiding labeling or shaming. A few prescriptive statements and expressions of worry (e.g., firm bedroom rules) slightly narrowed the unconditional tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and perspective-taking helped Richard soften and feel heard. More consistently exploring emotional nuance and using empathic summaries would deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on smoking and its interpersonal consequences and moved to concrete behavioral steps and a two‑week plan. Direction was clear while remaining patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was framed as a joint project with co-creation of language, role-play, and frequent permission-seeking. The clinician worked side-by-side and reinforced equality throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly honored Richard's choice by offering options, resisting pressure, and letting him choose steps and phrasing. Statements like 'I won’t try to force quitting' reinforced patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician drew out relationship-based motivations and elicited Richard's own wording and a practical experiment (two weeks). However, frequent early suggestions and scripts shifted balance toward advice-giving rather than deeper elicitation of spontaneous reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was a strong collaborative stance—inviting choices, coaching wording, and role‑playing together. At times the clinician provided fairly detailed plans early instead of eliciting more of Richard’s own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk tied to relationship concerns and secured concrete commitments (e.g., smoking outside, two‑week trial). To strengthen this, more targeted open questions and probes for desire, ability, reasons, need, and confidence/commitment would help amplify change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally rolled with resistance, reframed goals to reduce conflict, and avoided confrontation. Occasional firm household directives and repeated worry risked reinforcing sustain talk; more double-sided reflections and selective reflection of sustain talk could further soften resistance.
