# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 
- Consistently nonjudgmental positive regard (e.g., “I’d like to figure this out with you,” “I respect that you want a forgiving, low-effort solution…”).  
- Normalizes ambivalence and accepts limits; no shaming.  
- Suggestion noted: occasionally personalize affirmations (name a specific effort).

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 
- Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (“I hear you — tying the meds to your morning coffee feels doable…you don’t want to rely on your husband”).  
- Missed a few opportunities to probe underlying affect (e.g., “I worry hectic mornings…” could be explored more).  
- Suggestion: add one affect-focused reflection/question to deepen emotional understanding.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 
- Clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and a week-long experiment (“what ideas have helped…, which one would you be willing to try this week?”).  
- Avoided tangents and moved to a specific action plan (pillbox on coffee maker, alarm, pre-fill Sunday).  
- Suggestion: close with an explicit agenda summary (who, when, what to report).

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 
- Genuinely collaborative: invited choices (“which feels most doable?”), co-constructed the plan.  
- Patient ownership evident in repeated selections (alarm + pillbox, pre-fill Sunday).  
- Suggestion: reflect patient’s exact language when summarizing to show plan driven by her preferences.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 
- Repeatedly supports choice and avoids coercion (“which would you be willing to try?”; respects not “making him the boss”).  
- Information (missed-dose rule) offered and choices left to patient.  
- Suggestion: consider a brief confidence/commitment scaling question occasionally.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 
- Frequently elicits patient ideas and motivations (asked what helped before; “what matters most to you…”).  
- Patient produced multiple change statements (“I do want to stay healthy,” “I’ll try that,” “pre-fill Sunday night”).  
- Suggestion: add a value-eliciting question to deepen internal motivation (e.g., “What would staying healthy allow you to do?”).

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 
- Interaction framed as joint project (“I’d like to figure this out with you,” “I’ll plan to check in next week”).  
- Questions defer to patient preferences and demonstrate power-sharing.  
- Suggestion: occasionally summarize shared responsibilities explicitly (you try X, I’ll check in Y).

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 
- Elicited multiple change statements and concrete plans (pillbox on coffee maker; pre-fill Sunday).  
- Did not consistently amplify/scale commitment or confidence (no 0–10 scaling).  
- Suggestion: use targeted probes and reflective amplification to strengthen commitment language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 
- Handled sustain talk nonconfrontationally with double-sided reflections (“sometimes I forget…yet you also say, ‘I do want to stay healthy’”).  
- Tended to reflect sustain talk rather than strategically exploring longer-term consequences to weaken it.  
- Suggestion: gently ask about potential costs or discrepancies when sustain talk repeats to soften it.
