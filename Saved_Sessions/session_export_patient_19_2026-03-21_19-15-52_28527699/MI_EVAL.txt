# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, praising small steps and normalizing difficulty to convey warmth and positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides timely empathic statements and some reflections, but empathy is sometimes brief and quickly moves to solutions instead of deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on gambling/debt and guides the session from exploration to concrete action steps and relapse planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses role‑play and rehearsals and checks in with the client, but occasionally steers content with long lists of clinician-driven recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms choice and offers options, yet frequently provides unsolicited directives without always asking permission, which mildly reduces autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and connects them to values and plans, but often supplies solutions rather than consistently drawing out the client's own ideas and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and co-creates scripts and plans, but sometimes shifts toward expert advising with specific directives instead of client-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and links goals to values, though there were missed chances to further amplify client-generated reasons and ask follow-up evocation questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers harm-reduction options, but does not consistently use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explore ambivalence and soften minimizations.
