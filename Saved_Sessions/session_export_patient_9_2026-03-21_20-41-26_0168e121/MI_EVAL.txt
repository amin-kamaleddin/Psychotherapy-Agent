# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, affirming the patient's intentions and creating a safe space for disclosure. No blaming or minimizing language observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and reflective listening with accurate summaries, but sometimes stays pragmatic and misses opportunities to deepen reflections of the patient's emotional strain. Could more explicitly name feelings like exhaustion or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior (establishing routines and co-parenting logistics) and moved toward concrete, measurable steps. Interventions consistently tied back to the goal of a calmer, more predictable home.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative: co-constructed message drafts, practiced role-play together, and negotiated compromises while soliciting the patient's preferences. Clinician tailored responses to the patient's input throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors patient choice by asking permission, offering options, and inviting edits to match the patient's voice. Avoids pressuring and reinforces the patient's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited specific actions and choice of doable steps, but relied more on offering clinician-generated strategies than drawing out the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence. Could use scaling, eliciting past successes, and asking the patient to summarize her commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership through shared decision-making and frequent invitations to shape wording and plans, but at times moves quickly to clinician-crafted scripts rather than eliciting the patient's own alternatives first. More explicit co-generation of ideas would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces proximal change talk and commitment (choice of routines, two-week trial, role-play), but misses deeper exploration of importance and confidence (e.g., scaling or asking reasons). Strengthening elicited reasons and confidence would amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, reframes potential resistance and coaches non-defensive replies while offering compromises. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections and explicit elicitation of the patient's arguments against sustain talk.
