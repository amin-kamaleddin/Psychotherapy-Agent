# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized difficulties, and validated Linda's fears while avoiding judgment. The clinician honored Linda's preference to delay daily meds and supported smaller, client-chosen steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that accurately named emotions and impacts, deepening rapport. Reflections picked up on values (wanting to feel normal, return to walks) and were timely and accurate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and getting back to walks, translating goals into concrete, achievable steps. Interventions stayed tightly linked to the target behavior without pressuring the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: co-produced scripts, rehearsed questions, and routinely solicited Linda's preferences so she shaped the agenda. Power-sharing was evident as Linda's choices determined next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Linda's autonomy by offering options, framing interventions as choices, and honoring her selected strategies. The clinician reinforced Linda's ownership of decisions and pacing throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations and values and used open questions to elicit change-aligned language. Could strengthen evocation by more systematically eliciting, reflecting, and amplifying change talk and exploring confidence/commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally conveyed partnership and invited Linda's input, co-creating plans and rehearsing together. At times the clinician offered relatively detailed scripted language before fully eliciting Linda's preferred wording, slightly reducing client control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited desire and reasons for change (e.g., returning to walks) and offered small, actionable first steps. Missed opportunities to amplify, summarize, and explore confidence or elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and gently redirected sustain talk by validating concerns and offering alternatives and a manageable sequence of steps. Could use more strategic double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence and help resolve sustain talk.
