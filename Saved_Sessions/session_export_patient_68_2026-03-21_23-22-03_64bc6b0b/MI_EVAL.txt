# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and normalized Kevin’s mixed feelings, reducing shame and encouraging engagement. Could add more explicit, personalized affirmations of Kevin’s strengths (e.g., past successful requests for help).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that accurately captured Kevin’s motivations and ambivalence. Could occasionally use deeper affect-laden reflections to explore embarrassment or worry more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol-related harm and short-term risk-reduction strategies throughout the session. Guided Kevin from problem recognition to concrete, pragmatic steps without tangents.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a joint problem-solving effort, inviting Kevin’s input at every step and co‑tweaking the plan together. Kevin’s preferences clearly shaped the plan, reinforcing shared responsibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly respected Kevin’s choice and framed strategies as optional experiments, preserving ownership of decisions. No coercive language was used and options were offered throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Kevin’s internal motivations and practical reasons for change, drawing out concrete strategies he proposed. The clinician avoided imposing external reasons and moved Kevin toward preparatory action.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and power-sharing, asking permission to co-edit the text and inviting Kevin to choose how to proceed. The clinician positioned suggestions as joint experiments rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified concrete change talk and planning (brace, limit drinks, text Mike), helping move Kevin from abstract regret to actionable steps. Missed opportunities to elicit confidence/importance ratings and to press for stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward workable harm‑reduction strategies, validating Kevin’s priorities. Could further soften sustain talk with more double-sided reflections and selective reflections of the costs of sustain talk.
