# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens respectfully and responds supportively, avoiding blame and normalizing a plan-based approach; missed a few opportunities to individualize affirmations (e.g., explicitly validating the six-month quit or normalizing relapse risk).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and attends to safety and side-effect concerns; could deepen empathic reflections of the patient's nervousness, urgency, and ambivalence to strengthen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete planning (medication options, titration, refusal lines, follow-up) and ties each major turn to the central goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs messages and refusal lines and solicits wording preferences, but occasionally shifts into an expert informational stance without permission, reducing equal partnership at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers options, honors patient choice (including stopping medication), and asks what help the patient wants next, reinforcing patient control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical commitment-oriented actions and planning but rarely uses open, curiosity-driven questions to draw out the patient's intrinsic reasons, values, or emotional drivers for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance by drafting materials together and inviting preferences, though detailed medical explanations sometimes proceed without first checking permission.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces some change-oriented statements and affirms readiness, but seldom asks open, reflective questions to amplify the patient's own motivations or elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain-talk and offers useful behavioral strategies, but few double-sided reflections, cost/benefit explorations, or reframes were used to actively soften sustain talk.
