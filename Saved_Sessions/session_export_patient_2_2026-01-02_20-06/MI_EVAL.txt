# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard across the session (opening "I'm here to listen and support you," repeated thank-you statements, normalization of slips). Tone created safety and the patient remained engaged and accepted the plan and brochure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., “It sounds like you’re worried about health and your son,” “You’re doing well to notice triggers”). Reflections were accurate but generally surface-level; missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection on fear, exhaustion, and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking throughout (concrete plan: reduce evening cigarettes, replace one with alternative, monitor triggers/urges, one‑week follow-up). Honored patient's scope (declined to address drinking) and kept session on-task.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed plan with repeated invitations for patient input (asked which cigarette to replace, checked “How does that feel?”, scheduled follow-up by patient preference). Power‑sharing evident in choices of strategies and follow‑up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice and agency (offered options, normalized slips, provided brief refusal scripts and emphasized “you don’t owe an explanation”). Patient repeatedly reaffirmed the plan, indicating autonomy was upheld.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited patient motivations and resources (confidence ruler, request to choose a cigarette to replace, asking about triggers). Generated multiple instances of change talk (health/son, willingness to try reductions); could have probed values (motherhood) more deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally partnered (asked for input, co‑created steps), but some clinician‑driven moments presented many specific instructions in a row (dosing details, multiple behavioral prescriptions) without pausing to ask which supports the patient preferred.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (praise for specific plans, asking readiness/confidence, building on health/son reasons). Missed opportunities to amplify and summarize the patient’s own reasons and to elicit stronger commitment language (move from “I could try” toward “I will try”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and did not argue; reframed slips as learning which reduces sustain talk. Did not consistently use double‑sided reflections or stronger discrepancy statements—there were additional chances to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence and tip toward change.
