# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and validation, naming the patient's experience and affirming her steps; no evidence of blame, minimization, or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and concise summaries that showed good perspective-taking and understanding of distress; could deepen empathic openings and use more complex reflections to explore fear and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on obtaining medical clarity and initiating symptom-management, steering the session toward concrete next steps and a follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a shared endeavor and co-constructed practical solutions, routinely inviting input and adjusting materials to the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the patient's choices, offered alternatives without pressure, and preserved control through options (e.g., private referral, leaving phone blank).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some goals and choices but moved quickly to practical solutions without sufficiently probing values, confidence, or amplifying the patient's own motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly conveyed shared decision-making, offering choices and checking preferences so the patient retained ownership of decisions and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities and provided tools, but did not consistently evoke, deepen, or amplify the patient's own change language or follow up on mild statements favoring change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated biomedical concerns while proposing a two-track plan that honored the patient's stance; could further use double-sided reflections and selectively amplify tentative change talk.
