# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validated Tyler's experience and normalized help-seeking, creating a warm, accepting tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking with timely reflections, but reflections were somewhat surface-level and missed deeper emotions like shame or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the alcohol incident and remediation, guiding the conversation toward concrete next steps and documentation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Felt collaborative through role-plays and invitations for input, but frequently supplied scripts and checklists that shifted problem-solving toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported Tyler's choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and respecting his preferences before intervening.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivational material and linked to academics, but relied heavily on offering solutions and missed opportunities to draw out Tyler's intrinsic motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally partnered with Tyler by offering options and asking preferences, though the clinician often provided ready-made language instead of co-creating it with him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created openings for change talk and modeled responsibility, but did not consistently evoke or amplify Tyler's own change language with open follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered harm-reduction, but did not routinely use MI-specific softening techniques like double-sided reflections or strategic summaries.
