# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validation, praise, and normalization across the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening and accurate reflections of barriers and effort, though some reflections were brief and could probe deeper into shame or somatic anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the target behavior with a specific, time‑bound experiment (5‑minute recording), reminders, backups, and a scheduled check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a joint effort by soliciting Hana's input, following her lead on prompts and timing, inviting rehearsal, and co‑creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering multiple options, asking permission, and presenting alternatives in a nonpressuring way that kept choices with Hana.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation by exploring values and past success, but could deepen evocation by amplifying change language and linking the step to broader personal values or identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and preference‑seeking, but frequent provision of concrete scripts and directives sometimes shifted control back to the clinician rather than remaining purely invitational.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively prompted and strengthened change talk with targeted questions and confidence ratings, yet missed opportunities to reflect and amplify Hana's own change statements to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation and offered low‑risk alternatives and coping strategies, though more double‑sided reflections could better pivot ambivalence toward change.
