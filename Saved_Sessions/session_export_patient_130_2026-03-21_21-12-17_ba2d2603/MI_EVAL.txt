# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clearly nonjudgmental, accepting stance throughout, validating Viktor's autonomy and normalizing ambivalence. This consistent unconditional positive regard supports Viktor's engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are multiple accurate reflections and paraphrases showing perspective-taking, but the clinician could probe deeper into underlying emotions (e.g., guardedness, shame) and relational impact. Reflective work is strong but not consistently exploring emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear focus on the target behavior (reducing risky drinking by leaving earlier) and moved logically to a specific, measurable plan (leave by 11). Questions and interventions consistently supported progress toward that small test.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as a joint exploration with Viktor invited to choose options and preferences, and the clinician co-constructed a plan. It fell short of perfect collaboration when the clinician supplied polished language and multiple structured options rather than eliciting Viktor's own phrasing more often.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly supported throughout with language like 'You're in charge' and multiple options framed as choices rather than mandates. The clinician avoided pressure and acknowledged Viktor's right to decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited reasons for change, scaling, and specific change language that produced a concrete plan. Evocation could be deepened by exploring values, comparisons, and past successes to amplify motivation and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fostered shared decision-making by asking which options felt most doable and inviting practice of language. Partnership was slightly reduced when the clinician frequently offered specific suggestions and rewritten scripts rather than having Viktor generate more phrasing himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited and strengthened concrete change talk and used tools (scaling, brainstorming) to build an actionable plan. Missed opportunities include more explicit amplification and summarization of Viktor's change statements to increase motivational salience.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing sustain talk and sometimes used double-sided reflections, but did not consistently use stronger strategies to soften sustain talk and occasionally moved quickly into solutions. More strategic reflection of sustain language and elicited discrepancy could help reduce sustain positions.
