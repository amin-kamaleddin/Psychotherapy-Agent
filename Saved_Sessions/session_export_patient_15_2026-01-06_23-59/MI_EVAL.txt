# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizing language, avoids blame, and affirms the patient's small-step approach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality empathic listening with accurate reflections capturing both content and emotion (tiredness, doubt, priorities).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on blood pressure and alcohol reduction and guides the conversation toward a concrete, actionable experiment without losing patient-centeredness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-constructs the plan by soliciting the patient's ideas and preferences and repeatedly invites shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by framing changes as a short experiment and avoiding pressure or prescriptive language, reinforcing patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits intrinsic reasons for change and a concrete plan but could probe deeper into motives, past successes, and use scaling to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters an authentic partnership through frequent invitations to plan together and by centering the patient's expertise about what fits his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk and specific steps but misses opportunities to amplify, summarize, and elicit stronger commitment or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and neutrally reframes sustain talk with double-sided reflections and normalization, though more strategic summarizing could better shift toward commitment.
