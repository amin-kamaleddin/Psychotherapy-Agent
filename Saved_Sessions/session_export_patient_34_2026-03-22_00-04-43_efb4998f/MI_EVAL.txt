# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician displays strong unconditional positive regard, normalizing Dylan's concerns and avoiding judgment throughout. Explicitly accepts Dylan's "not promising" stance which preserves dignity and reduces defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and timely reflections capture Dylan's feelings about fairness, ambivalence, and wanting control. These empathic moves deepen rapport and elicit concrete responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on managing nights out to avoid penalties, repeatedly returning to concrete steps and planning. Clinician resists drift and maintains momentum toward actionable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is consistently collaborative: clinician offers options but solicits Dylan's input and lets him choose mechanics of the plan. Decisions are co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician validates Dylan's honesty and frames strategies as his choices, reinforcing ownership of the plan. Options are offered rather than directives, supporting intrinsic motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician elicits Dylan's own solutions and past successes (midterms example) to build motivation and self-efficacy. Change-oriented statements are drawn from Dylan and used to shape the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treats Dylan as an expert on his life, asking 'what would work for you' and sharing decision-making. This genuine power-sharing supports buy-in and ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicits and amplifies several specific change-oriented steps (buddy plan, alarm, texts, booked ride) and reinforces self-efficacy. Could more intentionally consolidate and amplify these into firmer commitment language or scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids confrontation and uses neutral reflections to limit sustain talk, redirecting to planning. Greater use of early double-sided reflections or explicit summaries of ambivalence could further soften resistance.
