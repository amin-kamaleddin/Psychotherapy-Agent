# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming—validated Malik's experience, normalized ambivalence, and accepted his limits while avoiding moralizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, acknowledging frustration and practical concerns, but could use more complex reflections to name underlying emotions and probe affect more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on cannabis use with concrete goals (no use at home/before school), a one-week experiment, tracking, and planned follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created an egalitarian tone with inclusive language and invitations to choose strategies, though the clinician sometimes supplied many ready-made options instead of eliciting Malik's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported Malik's sense of choice and control by offering options, asking permission, accepting reluctance, and emphasizing that slips are information rather than failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out practical reasons and willingness (pros/cons, experiment, reward) but did not deeply elicit intrinsic values, personal motivations, or confidence to strengthen internal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the process as collaborative and sought input, but at times leaned toward expert-driven guidance instead of co-creating solutions with Malik.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively invited and reinforced change talk with concrete steps and affirmations, yet missed opportunities to strategically amplify brief change statements and explore their meaning and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used neutral language and harm-reduction framing to reduce defensiveness, but could more explicitly use double-sided reflections to name both sustain and change talk and pivot toward change.
