# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing Mateo's situation and validating his efforts. Warmth and respect were evident throughout, avoiding labeling or reproach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately reflecting ambivalence and validating low confidence. Could increase depth with more affect-focused or complex reflections to draw out underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors and steered conversation toward concrete steps (housing call, temp-app, reminder, check-in). Balanced empathy with action-oriented planning throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance—invited co-creation of scripts, asked permission, and repeatedly checked for Mateo's edits and preferences. Decisions were negotiated rather than imposed, keeping Mateo's ideas central.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Mateo's sense of choice and ownership by offering options and framing suggestions as offers. Avoided pressuring language and confirmed Mateo's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations, goals, strengths, and a specific small step, generating concrete change language. Could more deeply evoke intrinsic motivation by linking steps to personal values and amplifying change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a genuine partnership stance by asking preferences, offering options, and co-creating messages and scripts. Power-sharing was clear and consistent, treating Mateo as the expert on what would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, leading to a concrete plan and use of a confidence ruler. Missed some opportunities to deepen and amplify Mateo's own language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and validated the client's sense of obligation while redirecting to practical supports. Could use more strategic double-sided reflections to explicitly name ambivalence and pivot to reasons for change.
