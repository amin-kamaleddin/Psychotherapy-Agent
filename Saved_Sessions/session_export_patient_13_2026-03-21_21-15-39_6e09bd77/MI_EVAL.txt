# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and validated the patient's struggles (childcare, bills, fear of tests) with warm affirmations and nonjudgmental language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely reflections and tailored responses that acknowledged both emotional and practical barriers, facilitating deeper patient disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on seeking medical assessment and linked each intervention to feasible next steps (phone consult, scripts, scheduling) without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in joint problem-solving by following the patient's lead, co-creating scripts and role-plays, and repeatedly asking for the patient's preferences about format and pacing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported choice by offering multiple options, asking what felt most doable, and avoiding pressure while inviting the patient to select and modify next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations and practical barriers and translated them into small concrete steps, but could more fully probe internal reasons, values, and importance/confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with shared decision-making, deferring to the patient's choices and positioning the clinician as a supportive partner rather than an authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively invited and supported change talk and generated small-step options, though the clinician could more consistently reflect, amplify, and probe importance/confidence to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and redirected it toward manageable steps, but could increase use of double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence while inviting movement.
