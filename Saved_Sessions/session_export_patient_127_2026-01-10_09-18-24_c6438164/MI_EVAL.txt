# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance with validating statements and a warm, supportive tone that made space for ambivalence and vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries captured the patient's perspective, though reflections often repeated 'I hear you' and could include more deeper or affect‑laden reflections to surface underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained tightly focused on the target behavior (securing steady cash/employment) and moved steadily toward concrete action steps like a Wednesday call, script, role‑play, and reminders.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like equal partnership—clinician frequently solicited the patient's views, negotiated next steps, and co‑designed the plan using the patient's language and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently reinforced autonomy by offering choices, honoring the patient's preferred wording and pace, and framing decisions as the patient's to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effective use of open questions and strategic prompts elicited the patient's own reasons for change and concrete motivational statements that informed the action plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician framed the work as collaborative and deferential to the patient's expertise, offering options and following the patient's lead in co‑constructing the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively drew out and reinforced change talk, translating it into specific, achievable steps (e.g., probation call, quick certs) and asking for smallest possible actions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and reframed without reinforcement, and the clinician redirected toward change, though more double‑sided reflections or selective amplification of small change talk could further tip ambivalence.
