# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes Elias’s limitations with validating reflections and nonjudgmental language. Offers options rather than directives, creating a safe, respectful space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and gentle validation of frustration and doubt are evident, building rapport. Missed opportunities to probe the positive ‘‘very, very beautiful’’ moment and to deepen exploration of discouragement and emotional impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, sustained focus on a specific target behavior (brief chair exercises) and guides conversation toward concrete planning (what, when, supports). Converts intentions into tangible supports like alarms, notes, and confidence assessment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative session with clinician and Elias co-creating notes, sticky layouts, and plans while continually checking preferences. Clinician negotiates wording and format and invites Elias’s edits, demonstrating genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms Elias’s choice and control by offering supports only if he wants them and inviting edits and delivery preferences. Avoids coercive language and frames help as optional, respecting his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits Elias’s ideas for change, specifics on timing, supports, and confidence, drawing out motivational material. Could probe deeper into internal reasons, values, and the meaning of the positive experience to amplify intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treats Elias as the expert on his life, offering choices and co-constructing decisions (e.g., draft edits, format choices). Frequent checking of preference and incorporation of Elias’s selections demonstrates strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits specific change talk (concrete steps, days/times, confidence rating) and translates intention into actionable plans and reminders. Could do more to amplify positive language and connect actions to Elias’s values and internal motives.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and provides safety/contingency planning but relies mainly on neutral validation. Rarely uses double-sided reflections or summaries to explicitly juxtapose change and sustain talk to gently shift toward change.
