# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys nonjudgmental support, validates Lucía’s frustration, and respects her skepticism. They repeatedly frame their role as helpful rather than punitive throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking are evident (e.g., naming frustration and skepticism). Responses match the patient’s tone and help reduce defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on coping with smoking urges and specific, measurable strategies. The clinician provides concrete next steps and avoids drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as shared problem-solving by offering options and inviting feedback. The patient actively participates in choosing and shaping the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently supported through invitations to choose and asking permission before offering tools. The clinician avoids pressure and reinforces the patient’s control over testing options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits small pieces of internal motivation and behavioral commitments and links actions to valued relationships. However, deeper exploration of intrinsic reasons, values, and confidence was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear power-sharing is demonstrated by negotiating the plan and soliciting the patient’s input. The patient’s choices meaningfully shaped the session’s strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Small change talk is elicited and commitments to experiment are encouraged, with useful links to values and relationships. There was less systematic elicitation of DARN language (desire, ability, reasons, need) in the patient’s own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently rolls with resistance, validates sustain statements, and redirects to practical options rather than arguing. This approach avoids reinforcing sustain talk and shifts attention toward achievable tests.
