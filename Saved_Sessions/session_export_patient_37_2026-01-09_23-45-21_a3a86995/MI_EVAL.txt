# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes Elena's concerns, removing blame and validating protective motives. Could occasionally tie acceptance more explicitly to Elena's specific lived context for added nuance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflective listening that matches Elena's language and emotional stance, helping her articulate change talk. A brief deeper exploration of the emotional intensity behind 'protective' (fear, guilt) would further deepen connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on the vaccine decision and actionable information Elena requested (per‑million comparisons, safety plan). Could be strengthened by setting an explicit, time‑limited next step for follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently adopts a partnership stance, soliciting Elena's preferences and co‑constructing the information package and plan. The interaction avoids an expert monologue and genuinely invites Elena's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirms Elena's choice and control, offers options and respects her timeline without pressure. Could explicitly validate postponing a decision and invite Elena to set the next step on her own timeline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits Elena's reasons and preferences and frames willingness as a strength, drawing motivation out rather than imposing arguments. Would benefit from more exercises to deepen intrinsic motivation (e.g., imagining outcomes or having Elena summarize her own reasons).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently offers choices, asks what Elena wants to prioritize, and frames tasks as joint efforts, conveying genuine shared decision‑making. This strongly communicates that expertise about values resides with Elena.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces emerging change talk and asks what would need to change to feel comfortable. Could more consistently amplify, sequence, and elicit confidence/commitment language (e.g., scaling or summary reflections) to convert willingness into commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and neutralizes sustain talk with reflective statements and normalization, avoiding argumentation. Could more often use strategic softening techniques (double‑sided reflections, reframes) to highlight discrepancy and transition toward change.
