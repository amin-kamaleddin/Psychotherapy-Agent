# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed warm, nonjudgmental regard and respected Lars' values and strengths, normalizing embarrassment and avoiding labels. Acceptance was evident throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good use of reflections and validation captured Lars' perspective and embarrassment, but the clinician missed some opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance like guilt or fear. Reflective listening was solid but not consistently deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on employment with concrete tools (CV, search phrases) and momentum toward action. A stronger single next-step commitment and more integration of linked risks (alcohol, peers, boredom) would improve direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative—preferences drove decisions, materials were co-created, and the clinician positioned themselves as a teammate rather than an authority. Lars' input clearly shaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently honored Lars' choices, offered options, asked permission, and avoided pressure, supporting his sense of control. Autonomy was reinforced in each decision point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Lars' goals and used them to shape options, but did not sufficiently deepen intrinsic motivation or use scaling/confidence questions to strengthen commitment. Evocation was effective but could be intensified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership was demonstrated by joint drafting of the CV, soliciting preferences, and tailoring search plans to Lars' context. The clinician worked alongside Lars to co-construct concrete steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced meaningful change talk by linking employment to reduced offending and family goals and by asking about doable steps. More systematic DARN-C questioning and explicit commitment elicitation would further cultivate change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and redirected toward practical options, effectively reducing its influence. Using double-sided reflections or explicitly acknowledging both sides before steering to change would strengthen this skill.
