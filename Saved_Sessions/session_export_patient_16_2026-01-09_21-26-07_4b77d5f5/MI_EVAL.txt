# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (open invitation, direct safety check, multiple affirmations). Creates a warm, supportive atmosphere that normalizes ambivalence and encourages disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and reflective listening (summaries, attend to nervousness, asked about triggers). missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of emotions and sustain talk to strengthen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on vaping and the goal of no vaping during season with concrete, actionable steps (start date Feb 1, hiding vape, pre-game routine, backups). Repeatedly returns the conversation to specific behavior and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a genuine joint endeavor—solicits patient input, rehearses together, and facilitates decisions (start date, who to tell, wording). Responds to cues with practical support rather than dictating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's sense of choice by offering menus of options, asking permission, and framing suggestions as choices. Even when giving scripts, the clinician preserves ownership by seeking consent and emphasizing optionality.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations tied to valued outcomes (basketball performance, avoiding suspension) and encourages commitment language and rehearsal. Could further probe broader values and ask more evocative questions to deepen the patient's own arguments for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and co-constructs the plan by inviting choices and rehearsing together. At times provided many specific scripts and options which slightly edged toward telling rather than first eliciting the patient's language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and reinforces change talk (reasons for change, confidence rating, concrete start date, rehearsal). Missed opportunities to expand spontaneous change talk with deeper follow-ups and evocative prompts about what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward practical planning and alternatives, reducing escalation. Rarely used explicit strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) that could have more directly normalized ambivalence and softened sustain talk.
