# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating feelings and praising small steps throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking but reflections were often brief and solution-focused rather than exploring deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, persistent focus on returning to the gym with concrete behavioral goals, implementation intentions, and measurable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem-solving and invited choices, but frequently supplied strategies and content, making the interaction somewhat clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's choice and agency by offering options, respecting declines, and encouraging self-directed decisions and plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation (readiness ratings, noticing small wins) but leaned toward providing ideas instead of drawing out the patient's own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the interaction as collaborative by asking preferences and inviting check-ins, though more elicitation of the patient's own solutions would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with readiness rulers and specific action steps, but could use more open-ended questions to deepen personal motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and normalized concerns, yet tended to redirect quickly to strategies instead of using double-sided reflections or exploring ambivalence.
