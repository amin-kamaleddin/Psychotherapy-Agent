# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and respect (validation, praise, nonjudgmental language) and invited patient preferences throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with frequent reflective summaries and validations that echoed the patient's worries and experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on quitting with concrete sub-goals (medication/titration, triggers, safety) and steered the session toward a practical stepwise treatment plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan by soliciting preferences and constraints, incorporating spouse involvement and scheduling, and negotiating monitoring and follow‑up logistics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported patient choice by offering multiple medication options, asking which the patient preferred, and avoiding pressure while reinforcing the patient's role in decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations, confidence rating, and a quit date, but at times shifted into recommendations rather than pausing to draw out deeper personal reasons or solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as a collaborative partner—presented options, invited input on timing and logistics, and shared power in decision‑making about the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk with open questions and affirmations and secured a start date, though could more often amplify and summarize the patient's own change language to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed sustain talk (normalized slips, offered coping strategies) and redirected toward change, but used few double‑sided reflections and sometimes moved quickly to solutions.
