### Acceptance

**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly communicated nonjudgmental acceptance and respected boundaries (e.g., “I hear and respect that you don’t want to talk about drinking today,” acknowledging burden of juggling work/classes/parenting).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ⬜4 ☑️5  
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening capturing content and affect (“You’re juggling a lot and that’s exhausting,” reflections on fatigue/unwind), used empathic summaries to check understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ⬜4 ☑️5  
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on evening smoking reduction, time-limited scope, and kept questions/suggestions tied to the specific goal (cutting evenings from six to three).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ⬜4 ☑️5  
**Comments:** Co-created plan with the patient (choice of gum and partner help, specific weeknights, reminder timing); clinician invited patient preferences throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ⬜4 ☑️5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed patient agency, offered options, respected decision not to discuss alcohol, and asked patient to set her own early sign of success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ☑️4 ⬜5  
**Comments:** Elicited motivators (cancer worry, being a mother), used confidence ruler and asked about supports; missed opportunities to deepen and amplify change talk (could probe “being a mother” more).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ⬜4 ☑️5  
**Comments:** Interaction positioned clinician and patient as collaborators (negotiated supports, check‑in method, and concrete shared plan).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ☑️4 ⬜5  
**Comments:** Elicited several change statements (“I could try…three instead of six,” cancer concern), but did not consistently amplify or elicit stronger commitment language to move toward a firmer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ⬜1 ⬜2 ⬜3 ☑️4 ⬜5  
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and respected boundaries (redirected from drinking), but sustain talk was not always explicitly reframed or explored with double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
