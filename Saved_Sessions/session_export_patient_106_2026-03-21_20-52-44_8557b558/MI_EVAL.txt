# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and warmth with validating statements and affirmations, never judgmental. The clinician repeatedly acknowledged the difficulty of parenting and coping, creating a respectful tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, empathic understanding through reflective listening that captured practical pressures and emotional ambivalence. The clinician validated doubts about NRT and withdrawal rather than dismissing them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking change and moved conversation toward a specific, testable behavioral experiment and follow-up. The session had strong forward momentum without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed with the patient invited to choose strategies, timing, and follow-up plans. The clinician framed future check-ins as joint problem-solving and offered to tweak the plan together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission implicitly, and affirming the patient's ownership of decisions. A nonpressuring backup plan preserved autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focused on eliciting the patient's own motivations and solutions with open questions that drew out practical strategies and commitment language. The clinician reinforced self-generated plans rather than imposing reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used collaborative language and shared power in planning, allowing the patient to select the target cigarette, strategy, and follow-up. The plan was co-created and mutually agreed upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk, asking for concrete steps and confidence ratings that led to commitment to a nightly experiment. Praise and reinforcement strengthened the patient's expressed intent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward doable experiments rather than arguing, but could increase use of double-sided reflections or summaries to more explicitly validate ambivalence. More explicit rolling with sustain talk would further soften resistance.
