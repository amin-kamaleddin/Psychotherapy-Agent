# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with frequent affirmations and normalization of the patient's perspective throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and some reflective listening that matched the patient's affect, but reflections remained mostly informational and did not probe deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related risk reduction from feedback through a concrete harm-reduction plan and follow-up check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative and co‑constructed the plan by offering tools and tailoring options while letting the patient choose specific steps and wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and avoided pressure by offering options, asking permission, and acknowledging the patient's limited commitment and control over pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of willingness and planning occurred, but the clinician relied more on information and options than systematically drawing out the patient's own motives, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly partnered with the patient through joint decision-making, invitations for input, and shared planning that respected the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced small change statements and concrete commitments, but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify the patient's own reasons and desires for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and gently redirected sustain talk toward harm reduction, though could have used more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
