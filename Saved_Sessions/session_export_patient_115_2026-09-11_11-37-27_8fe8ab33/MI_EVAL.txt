# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful stance—normalizing ambivalence and validating constraints while affirming partial progress. No evidence of blame or pressure; acceptance supported the client's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening were evident, accurately naming barriers and motivations and summarizing the plan back to Samir. The clinician repeatedly checked understanding, sustaining an empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on reducing smoking in the cab/home to protect breathing and teens, moving logically from goal selection to implementation details. Questions and reflections advanced practical planning without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed with Samir’s input—clinician solicited routines and built strategies from his suggestions while offering options when asked. Power was shared and the clinician did not dominate or prescribe.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly honored Samir’s choice to cut down rather than quit, framed options, and asked which strategy felt doable, supporting personal agency. Language emphasized Samir’s control over decisions and implementation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Samir’s ideas, reasons, and practical strategies, drawing out change-oriented planning. Opportunities remained to deepen evocation by exploring personal values, using importance/confidence scaling, or eliciting a brief commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with repeated invitations for Samir’s input and joint problem-solving (e.g., keys = gum, dashboard note). The clinician built on Samir’s expertise about his routine and respected his choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited concrete change talk and specific plans (smoke-free cab/home, gum/lozenges), using open questions and choices. More frequent amplification, summarizing of motivations, and a request for a concise commitment or scaling could have strengthened commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing for quitting and reframed the client’s sustain position into actionable steps, which reduced sustain talk. However, repeatedly restating 'you’re not quitting' risked normalizing sustain talk; more double-sided reflections and gentle discrepancy work could further shift ambivalence.
