# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validates Dylan's frustration, and offers practical supports rather than criticism, conveying unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking early in the session that mirrors Dylan's concerns, but later moves become more directive with missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings and defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on the referral and next steps throughout, tying questions and interventions to how Dylan will respond to the conduct process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conducted as a cooperative effort: the clinician invites Dylan's input, offers options, and adjusts wording based on his requests, with Dylan shaping the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports Dylan's sense of choice by offering multiple options, asking preferences, and framing suggestions as choices while avoiding pressure to admit responsibility.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Fails to draw out Dylan's intrinsic motivations or ambivalence about drinking and consequences, focusing instead on tactical support for contesting the referral.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and inviting of Dylan's preferences, though the clinician sometimes shifts into an expert role by providing ready-made templates rather than co-creating language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicits or amplifies language in favor of change; interventions center on procedural defense and do not explore goals, values, or discrepancy to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** While validating Dylan's perspective reduces confrontation, the clinician often reinforces sustain talk by helping craft non-admissions and misses chances to reframe or use double-sided reflections.
