# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with repeated validations and practical offers of help (drafting questions, role‑play). Little evidence of blame or minimization, supporting unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective‑taking and timely reflections, naming the patient’s worries and checking safety. Summaries and validations helped the patient soften and engage.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on medication decisions, lifestyle targets, monitoring, and concrete next steps with no significant drift. Regular summaries and agenda‑setting moved the patient toward measurable actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a genuine partnership by soliciting the patient’s input, negotiating targets, and positioning oneself as a helper/resource. Mutual agreement to document the plan reinforced shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options (meds vs. lifestyle trial), avoided coercive language, and affirmed the patient’s control over decisions. Safety triggers and flexible plans preserved patient choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations and concrete targets, but much movement was driven by clinician suggestions and structure rather than deeply eliciting the patient’s intrinsic reasons. More open questions and values‑linked reflections would strengthen internalized motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt highly collaborative with repeated invitations for preference, co‑construction of the 3‑month trial, and offers to role‑play or draft scripts. Power was shared and decisions were negotiated rather than imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created multiple opportunities for change talk and the patient produced actionable language and agreed targets. Missed chances to more actively evoke and amplify the patient’s own reasons, values, and confidence to consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used validation, reframing, and compromises (3‑month trial, safety triggers) to reduce resistance. Some directive informational statements and a doctor‑voice in role‑play risked reinforcing sustain talk; more double‑sided reflections would help.
