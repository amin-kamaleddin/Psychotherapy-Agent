# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone (opening invitation, safety check, direct affirmations). Warmth and respect were maintained throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good empathic statements and some reflections that validated frustration and nervousness. Empathy was often brief and quickly followed by practical suggestions rather than deeper reflective exploration of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction with concrete, actionable steps (tracking, swaps, refusal lines). Questions and interventions repeatedly returned to the target behavior and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone and practice were collaborative—offered options, asked preferences, and invited tweaks to wording. At times the clinician moved quickly into offering many concrete strategies, which felt slightly directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported the client's choice by offering a menu of options, respecting her wish not to quit, and asking which felt doable. Avoided coercive language and reinforced that decisions and tracking are the client's data and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurred (asking which option felt doable, inviting role-play), but limited exploration of internal motivations, values, or personally meaningful reasons for change. The focus remained on tactics rather than eliciting why control matters to the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally cultivated a collaborative stance by offering choices and inviting input (preferences, wording tweaks, role-play). The frequent clinician-suggested tools and phrasing slightly tilted the interaction toward expert direction rather than pure co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change-oriented language and supported a short, achievable step (tracking) with operationalized tools. Missed opportunities to deepen reasons, values, or confidence (e.g., importance/confidence rulers or more probing why/how questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided arguing (noting desire for control, not abstinence) but used few double-sided reflections or summaries to reframe ambivalence. Responses leaned toward practical advice rather than strategically softening sustain talk about social fears.
