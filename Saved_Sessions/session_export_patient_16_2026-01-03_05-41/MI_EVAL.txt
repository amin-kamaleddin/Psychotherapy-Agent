# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance (e.g., “Let’s figure this out together,” repeated “I hear you,” affirmations of Anika’s plan). Avoided blaming/shaming and normalized ambivalence; no major improvements needed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking captured nuance (social motives, performance goals). Empathic tone strong throughout; could vary phrasing of “I hear you” with more complex reflections at times.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on vaping/pen-at-school and tied questions/reflections to concrete behavior change (keeping pen home, who holds it, alternatives). Minor suggestion: add brief agreement on follow-up to solidify direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned interaction as joint problem-solving, invited choices, and built on patient suggestions (sister holds pen, phone reminders). Power shared and patient-generated ideas drove the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported patient choice (asked which change felt doable, avoided coercion), fostering sense of control. Language consistently framed steps as the patient’s decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons/resources for change (plans, confidence rating, coping phrases) and drew out change talk. Could deepen evocation by probing core values/long-term motivations and asking what would increase confidence (e.g., “What would make you an 8 or 9?”).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered collaborative stance and co-created practical steps, but sometimes moved to endorsement rather than explicit co-planning. Strengthen by negotiating a shared follow-up/check-in or explicit joint monitoring plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (play better when not high, confident for a month, not bringing pen to school). Could amplify strongest change talk and elicit more explicit commitment language and implementation intentions for high-risk moments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used double-sided reflections and pivoted toward safety options to defuse sustain talk (enjoyment with friends vs. risk to team). Could further soften sustain talk with more strategic double-sided reflections that juxtapose sustain and change talk and more active redirection into specific coping plans.
