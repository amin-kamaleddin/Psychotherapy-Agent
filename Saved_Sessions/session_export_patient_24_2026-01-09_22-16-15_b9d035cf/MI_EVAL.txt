# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used nonjudgmental, respectful language and validated Daniel’s feelings (e.g., normalizing surprise and defensiveness), avoiding labeling or admonishment throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with open questions about effects on training, sleep, and mood; could use more complex reflections to probe emotional nuance and ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use with targeted questions and concrete, measurable steps (2-week tracking template, harm-reduction strategies, and a follow-up plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner by co-creating the tracking plan and scheduling a brief, client-preferred check-in while honoring Daniel’s constraints and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Daniel’s control by offering options, asking his preference, and agreeing to avoid unsolicited advice, reinforcing ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation by linking drinking to training/sleep consequences, but tended to move quickly to suggestions rather than deepening elicitation of values, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a collaboration, inviting Daniel’s input on choices (template vs. limit) and jointly agreeing the format and brevity of follow-ups.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Drew out initial change language (tracking, tentative limits) but missed opportunities to amplify or deepen commitment (e.g., scaling questions or reflecting and strengthening “I’ll try”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing skepticism and offering nonjudgmental options, though could use more double-sided reflections to gently highlight discrepancy toward change.
