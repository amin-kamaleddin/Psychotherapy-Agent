# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence (e.g., “I accept where you’re at,” “you don’t have to quit to make a change”). Validated practical constraints (studies, job) and provided repeated, nonjudgmental reassurance; no shaming or minimization observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Frequent accurate reflections and perspective‑taking that matched affect (acknowledging nervousness, worry about being left out). Reflections tended to be surface‑level; missed a few opportunities for deeper affective naming (e.g., shame, loneliness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on reducing weekday drinking. Session progressed to a specific, measurable plan (booze‑free Thursday, text Ana, coffee at 7, calendar reminder, sparkling water) with concrete implementation questions and no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Strong partnership — clinician invited co‑creation (drafting the text together), offered choices, and let the patient select strategies. Power was shared; plan emerged from patient ideas with clinician scaffolding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed client choice and control, honored boundaries (“not ready to quit”), and avoided pressuring language. Offered options rather than directives, supporting ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited patient motivations, reasons, and past successful strategies (e.g., telling someone, calendar reminders). Missed opportunities to systematically elicit stronger commitment language and confidence/importance ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clinician stance was invitational and collaborative throughout (e.g., “Want to draft the message together?”). Patient contributions shaped the plan; interaction clearly co‑constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Clinician noticed and amplified change talk (energy, study time, confidence) and asked enabling questions about differences if Thursday went well. Did not consistently translate change talk into a firm commitment statement or probe commitment strength.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and validated reasons for not quitting entirely; offered acceptable alternatives (coffee, backup plans). Could have used more double‑sided reflections and reframing to explicitly weaken sustain‑talk drivers.
