# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through normalizing statements, use of the client's name, and nonjudgmental reflections, preserving the client's dignity throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening that captures ambivalence and social concerns, though reflections can be repetitive and could probe deeper emotional nuance (e.g., shame or exclusion).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on cutting down/stopping smoking and the social obstacle, strategically advancing to a concrete, low‑pressure behavioral experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative joint problem‑solving with frequentrequests for the client's input and genuine co‑creation of the plan, following the client's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports the client's choice and normalizes uncertainty, avoiding pressure or directives and framing decisions as the client's to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the client's own ideas and practical steps effectively, but misses opportunities to deepen evocation by exploring values, asking scaling questions, or securing a commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invites the client's expertise and shares power in planning, asking rather than telling and allowing the client to select the initial strategy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits concrete change talk and action planning (e.g., choosing gum and an experiment) but could better amplify, reflect, and summarize emerging commitment and assess importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids argument and acknowledges the benefits of smoking while redirecting toward experiments, though would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and strategic reframes to tip sustain talk toward change.
