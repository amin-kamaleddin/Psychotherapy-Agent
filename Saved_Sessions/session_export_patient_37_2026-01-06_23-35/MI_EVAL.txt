# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, repeatedly validating Elena's protective intentions and creating a warm safe space throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good reflective listening and perspective-taking that matched content and softened Elena, but missed opportunities for more complex emotional reflections and explicit empathic summaries to deepen validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session clearly focused on vaccine decision-making and tied questions, education, and planning to concrete next steps (titers, scripts, timing).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a true collaborative exchange by inviting Elena to set priorities, co-creating scripts and question lists, and negotiating next steps together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Elena's right to choose, offered multiple options rather than directives, and supported informed, non-pressured decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and concrete actions, but did not consistently use classic evocative techniques (scaling, values exploration, or commitment prompts) to amplify intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered partnership and power-sharing by presenting menus of options, asking preferences, co-drafting materials, and role-playing to keep Elena central to the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided options and rehearsals that generated tentative change talk and action plans, but rarely used direct evocative questions or scaling to deepen and amplify Elena's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and redirected to safety information and neutral scripts, though strategic double-sided reflections and summaries could have more effectively softened resistance.
