# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating (e.g., “You’re not failing,” “That tension is normal”). Reframes attempts as “experimenting, not failing,” which preserves dignity and engagement. Minor: add more specific affirmations of concrete strengths/past successes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (accurate summaries of overwhelm, values, and worry). Reflections sometimes formulaic and missed deeper emotional nuance (e.g., explore worry that causes lying awake); could use more complex/feeling reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, sustained focus on medication adherence and concrete routines. Moves session progressively toward specific, measurable plans (pillbox + alarm + calendar ticks) and checks feasibility repeatedly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance—invites patient ideas, offers options, co-constructs plan. At times multiple suggestions slightly steer rather than wait for patient-generated solutions. Could pause more before offering options and ask about follow-up preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces choice and frames plan as an experiment. Frequently asks the patient to choose what feels doable and how to measure success. Minor: explicitly affirm competence when patient mentions past successes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits change talk (desire to stay healthy, willingness to try) but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen change talk or elicit stronger commitment language (e.g., scaling questions, “what makes you say X not Y?”).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language (“Let’s treat it like an experiment”), shares agenda and decisions. Could strengthen partnership by explicitly asking how the patient prefers to follow up and by naming the patient’s expertise about her routine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicits statements of desire and intention but does not consistently amplify or translate them into commitment language. Recommend selective reflection of change talk and direct elicitation of commitment (e.g., commitment scaling or a short verbal plan).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is reflected (e.g., “sometimes I forget… it’s not a big deal”) but not consistently softened with double-sided reflections or reframing. Suggest using double-sided reflections and exploring payoffs/costs to reduce sustain talk.
