# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and warmth, normalized the patient's experience, and avoided judgment or labeling throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, attending to both affect and behavior (grounding, safety check) and staying finely attuned to the patient's emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (scheduling and preparing for medical evaluation) with interventions tied to concrete, actionable tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a collaborative partner by co-constructing the plan, soliciting preferences, and responding to the patient's requests so decisions were shared and shaped by the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported choice and control by offering multiple pathways, asking permission, and honoring the patient's pace without using pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation through doable micro-steps and tools that led to action, but leaned more on clinician-provided solutions than consistently drawing out the patient's own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered power-sharing and invited input, but often provided clinician-driven solutions; more explicit invitations for the patient to propose or choose steps would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and supported change statements and offered small steps, yet missed opportunities to reflect, amplify, and deepen the patient's own desire, ability, reasons, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and redirected to manageable steps, but sometimes moved quickly to solutions instead of using double-sided reflections to explicitly resolve ambivalence.
