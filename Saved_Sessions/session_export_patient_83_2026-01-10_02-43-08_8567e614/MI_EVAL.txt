# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened with a supportive, nonjudgmental stance, invited the patient's agenda, and answered concerns respectfully while normalizing monitoring—demonstrating consistent unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged the patient's questions and safety concerns and used reassuring phrases, but relied mainly on information-giving and rarely used reflective listening to name or validate the patient's emotions; empathy was adequate but inconsistent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on starting and safely taking atorvastatin with repeated returns to medication-specific topics and concrete action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked for the medication name, responded to concerns, and offered a phone script while incorporating the patient's ideas, but often supplied information unilaterally rather than eliciting the patient's knowledge or preferences first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided coercive language and framed actions as the patient's choice (encouraging contact with the prescriber and not stopping without advice), though could more often elicit the patient's preferences and invite them to set plans or timelines.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitments (calling the doctor, timing, juice swap) but these arose mainly from informational prompts rather than from questions drawing out the patient's own motives, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and offered collaborative aids, yet the interaction largely resembled an expert-led information session with missed opportunities to co-create the agenda or ask about pros and cons.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expressed intentions to follow plans, but change talk was largely a response to clear information rather than intentionally elicited and amplified by MI strategies such as open questions or confidence/importance rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and avoided argumentation by providing facts and logistics, but missed MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, validating ambivalence) that could have further softened sustain talk and resolved ambivalence.
