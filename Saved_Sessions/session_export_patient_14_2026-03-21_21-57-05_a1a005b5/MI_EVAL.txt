# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard (apologized for wait, validated complexity) and avoided blaming language while accepting legitimate barriers like time and work.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, practical reflections and acknowledged frustration and time constraints, but missed opportunities for deeper affect-focused reflections about meaning and emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on increasing physical activity, narrowed the agenda to small doable steps, and operationalized days/times/measures with concrete follow-up planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine collaborative partner by offering drafts, inviting edits, and responding flexibly to the patient's preferences to co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering multiple options, asking preferences, accommodating privacy wishes, and confirming decisions belonged to the patient without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's reasons and used readiness rulers and change-focused questions, but could have probed values, long-term goals, and stronger commitment language more often.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative and invited the patient to shape the plan, though some clinician-initiated drafting risked feeling slightly expert-driven rather than fully patient-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk, converted intent into a concrete plan (days/times/reminders), but could have deepened intrinsic motivation and explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and did not argue with sustain talk, redirecting to small, time-efficient strategies and backups, though more double-sided reflections or normalizing language would strengthen this further.
