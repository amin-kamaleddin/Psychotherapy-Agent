# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician opened with respect and validated distress early, normalizing some feelings, but then shifted into problem‑solving without sustained, individualized acceptance or deeper normalization of the patient’s cancer fears.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were accurate empathic moments (e.g., naming the fear and invalidation), but much of the interaction moved quickly to tests and directives, missing opportunities for deeper reflective listening and exploration of emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a very clear, appropriate focus on obtaining diagnostic investigations and practical next steps, consistently returning to actionable medical targets and advocacy strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician and patient co‑produced letters and scripts and incorporated patient edits, demonstrating strong teamwork, though the clinician often led content rather than fully sharing decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were offered (role‑play, drafting, second opinion) and tools provided, which supported autonomy, but prescriptive wording and strong recommendations somewhat limited the patient’s sense of full freedom.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited pragmatic goals (answers, return to work) but did not systematically draw out the patient’s values, reasons, confidence, or prior coping to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative gestures (asking what to change, inviting edits), yet the clinician largely retained the expert role and did not sufficiently invite the patient to co‑create the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change‑relevant language was elicited and supported through concrete actions, but the clinician missed chances to amplify intrinsic motivation, explore meaning of returning to work, or elicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided direct argument and validated the patient’s concerns, which prevented escalation, but did not use MI strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to actively soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
