# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, validating constraints and normalizing setbacks. No labeling, shaming, or dismissive language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and summaries that captured the patient’s concerns and smoking pattern, which helped the patient soften and elaborate. Could deepen empathic responding by reflecting more affective nuance and using more complex reflections to amplify emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on evening smoking reduction, honored the patient’s boundary about alcohol, and progressed logically to a concrete, time‑bound plan with follow‑up. The session moved from exploration to specific implementation steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Solicited patient input at every decision point and co‑created practical supports and plans, accommodating preferences and tweaks. Interactional equality and shared decision‑making were evident throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and offered multiple options while avoiding directive language, respecting the patient’s agency. The clinician affirmed the patient’s control over what and when to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic motivations (fear of cancer, desire to be there for her son) and drew out feasible strategies and confidence levels rather than supplying reasons. The clinician focused on expanding the patient’s own change talk and tools.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the work as collaborative, asking which step to try and co‑designing messages, alarms, and backups with the patient. Power‑sharing and mutual planning were consistently demonstrated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk through scaling, questions about reasons, and planning details, strengthening commitment with specifics. Could further amplify change talk and explicitly elicit a succinct single commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, respected the patient’s boundary about drinking, normalized ambivalence, and reframed slips as information. Could more frequently use double‑sided reflections to explicitly link both sides of ambivalence and shift sustain talk toward change.
