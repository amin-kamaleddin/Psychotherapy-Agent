# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, affirming the patient's efforts and choices; only a small missed opportunity to explicitly normalize ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captures content and some affect, but reflections often moved quickly to planning instead of exploring deeper emotions like shame or longing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior and moved logically from problem identification to a concrete, actionable safety plan while staying collaborative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan at every decision point, soliciting input and positioning the clinician as a guide and teammate rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently presented options and invited the patient's choices, affirming his right to decide and strengthening ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical motivation and ability statements, but missed opportunities to elicit deeper, values-based reasons for change (e.g., family, long-term goals).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership by asking preferences, co-creating steps, and respecting the patient's wording and choices throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk using permission, confidence rulers, role-play, and affirmations, which raised and consolidated commitment and concrete actions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and redirected to change-oriented planning, though could have used double-sided reflections and explicit normalization to more artfully soften ambivalence.
