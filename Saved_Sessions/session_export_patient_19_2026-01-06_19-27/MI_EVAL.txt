# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and validating, expressing warm support and praise even when discussing difficult topics. No blaming language was used and the clinician acknowledged the client's struggles with clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the client's concerns and emotions. The clinician often moved quickly to action planning and could deepen emotional reflection (e.g., explore minimization, shame, and the ambivalence's emotional texture).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on gambling and specific risk-reduction behaviors with concrete timelines. The clinician resisted topic drift and kept exchanges goal-directed while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a true partnership with the client shaping goals and plans, co-creating scripts, and negotiating decisions. The clinician incorporated client wording and repeatedly asked what felt most helpful next.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respected the client's right to choose by offering multiple options, checking preferences, and seeking consent for plans and scripts. The clinician refrained from pressuring and supported the client's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the client's own motivations and resources using evocative questions and scaling, linking intrinsic reasons to concrete actions. The clinician helped the client generate and amplify self-directed coping strategies and plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered a collaborative stance and invited client input, but occasionally shifted into doing-for (e.g., offering to hold the cards) which reduced client ownership. Overall the plan was co-constructed but with minor drift toward clinician action.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk through scaling questions, options, and progressive confidence probes, then reinforced and summarized the client's commitments. The clinician amplified specific reasons, abilities, and plans that increased confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized ambivalence, but underused strategic techniques like double-sided reflections and exploring the function of gambling. More explicit softening of minimization and sustain statements would reduce their pull.
