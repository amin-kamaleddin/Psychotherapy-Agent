# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized slips, and acknowledged Erik’s life context without judgment. Tone remained supportive and inviting throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening with accurate paraphrases that validated Erik’s feelings. Could deepen reflections to explore underlying emotions or concerns (e.g., autonomy, family responsibilities).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on a single, specific behavior change (swapping first post‑shift drink) and guided toward a measurable plan. Questions and reflections consistently supported that targeted agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and egalitarian, with frequent invitations for Erik’s input and co‑construction of plans. The clinician treated Erik as a partner rather than an advice recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Erik’s sense of choice by offering options and avoiding directive language. Reinforced his ownership of the plan and decision to test a small change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivation, concrete measures, and used a confidence ruler to draw out commitment. Could strengthen by probing deeper into personal values or longer‑term goals to amplify intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled strong partnership by asking preferences, offering to help word lines, and jointly specifying supports and tracking. Decision‑making was clearly shared and Erik’s expertise was solicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete options and a specific test (swap first beer), and asked how he would measure success. Could more consistently amplify and summarize Erik’s own change language and elicit additional desire/reason statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed slips as normal data while pivoting to manageable options. Would benefit from more double‑sided reflections that acknowledge the draw of social drinking while underscoring Erik’s reasons to test change.
