# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician shows consistent unconditional positive regard—normalizing ambivalence, reflecting choices without judgment, and respecting boundaries (e.g., partner 'no nagging'). There is no blaming or moralizing throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections capture David’s experience and emotional tone (unwinding, practicality, concern about jitteriness) and are followed by clarifying open questions. The clinician consistently conveys understanding and perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear focus on daytime smoking/NRT and moves purposefully from feasibility to a concrete, time-limited experiment. Questions and reflections consistently tie back to measurable signs of success and implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is joint problem-solving with repeated invitations for David’s input and use of his past strategies (bringing lunch) to co-construct the plan. Decisions emerge from the patient’s suggestions rather than clinician imposition.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly reinforced by validating keeping the after-work cigarette, framing the trial as an experiment, and offering choice-oriented questions. Statements and boundaries strengthen David’s ownership of the decision and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws motivation from David by asking evocative questions that lead him to articulate indicators of success, feasible steps, and past successes. These moves build intrinsic motivation and practical self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treats David as the expert on his life—asking how NRT fits his routine and co-creating a plan shaped by his ideas (alarms, stash locations, partner phrase). Language is collaborative and invitational throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits desire, reasons, and a commitment to a one-week experiment and summarizes change language to strengthen it. A missed opportunity was probing more for confidence/commitment strength (e.g., a confidence ruler or a clearer next-step commitment) and linking to longer-term values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated and not reinforced; the clinician uses double-sided reflections and redirects toward manageable experiments (accepting the evening cigarette while testing daytime NRT). This minimizes sustain talk’s influence without confrontation.
