# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with repeated normalizing statements and individualized validation that reduced shame and maintained rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality empathic listening and accurate reflections that captured Erik's affect, values, and practical concerns while inviting correction and elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on diabetes-related behavior change and quickly narrowed to a specific, actionable target (reducing post-shift drinking) with stepwise planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative problem-solving: the clinician solicited Erik's input, built on his suggestions, and integrated his routines into a jointly developed plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly preserved choice and control by offering options, asking Erik to choose steps, and inviting him to set the pace and commit only to what fits his life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivation and concrete reasons for change (keep working, family) and some confidence, but could probe deeper into intrinsic motives and strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Erik as the expert on his life and choices and co-constructed the plan, demonstrating strong power-sharing and mutual decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited desire, reasons, ability, and some commitment (e.g., thermos plan) and used planning questions, though could more deliberately amplify change language and elicit a clearer commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and skillfully reframed sustain talk to preserve rapport, but could further weaken sustain talk using more double-sided reflections and exploring costs of staying the same.
