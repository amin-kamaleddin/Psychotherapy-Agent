# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and affirms the patient's goals, offering choices and validating concerns. Could add more explicit normalization of ambivalence and warmer, individualized affirmations to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some empathic moments (invitation to share, brief affirmations) but makes limited use of reflective listening or paraphrase. Responses often move quickly to strategies instead of exploring the patient's feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on vaping and the concrete goal (30 days/season) with structured, practical steps. Session stays goal-oriented with targeted coping skills and rehearsal tied to the patient's priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asks what the patient wants, adapts the plan, and role-plays together. At times supplies detailed content that tilts toward a teacher/coach role rather than equal co-exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, inviting wording preferences, and avoiding coercive language. Reinforces that the plan should feel doable, which strongly supports the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some preferences and choices but largely delivers strategies instead of drawing out intrinsic motivations or values. Uses few open questions to deepen change talk or explore what change would mean to the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative tone and invites the patient's input through choices and role-play. Occasionally shifts into an expert stance by prescribing multi-item plans without fully co-constructing alternatives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforces existing change talk and helps specify a plan, but does little to elicit new or deeper change language. Misses opportunities for open questions about values, reasons, and meaning that would amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and offers alternative plans, keeping focus away from defense of the behavior. Could more actively use double-sided reflections and explicitly acknowledge enjoyment while highlighting costs to soften sustain talk.
