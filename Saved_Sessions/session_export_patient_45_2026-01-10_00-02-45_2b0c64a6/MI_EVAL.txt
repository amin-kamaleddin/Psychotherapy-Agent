# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful, validating the patient’s constraints and offering pragmatic support. No blaming or shaming and clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective statements that acknowledged frustration and practical barriers. Could deepen emotional reflection and use more complex reflections to explore fears, guilt, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors with concrete, actionable steps and timelines (meal plans, testing schedule, HbA1c follow-up). Kept the session anchored to specific clinical goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with invitations for input and tailored recommendations to constraints. Some interactions felt prescriptive with clinician-delivered lists rather than eliciting the patient’s own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing individualized decision-making. No coercive language and respect for the patient’s pace and preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew out practical willingness to change and tested readiness for a one-week plan. Missed opportunities to systematically evoke personal reasons, values, and confidence with open questions or scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (asking shift times, offering to rehearse), but the interaction was often clinician-directed with early prescriptive plans. Would benefit from more co-creation and inviting the patient’s ideas before proposing multi-step solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and amplified small commitments with feasible steps. Could strengthen by explicitly probing desire, ability, reasons, and confidence (e.g., scaling) to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation and normalized sustain talk while offering balanced information about meds and practical solutions. Could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly validate sustain talk while shifting toward change.
