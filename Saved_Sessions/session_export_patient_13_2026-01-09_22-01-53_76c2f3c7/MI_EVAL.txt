# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client’s struggle with warm, nonjudgmental language. The clinician acknowledges difficulty and avoids blame, supporting a strong therapeutic stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately paraphrasing content and linking concerns to parenting. Reflections tended to focus on concrete barriers rather than naming deeper emotions or fears, so deeper empathic labeling would strengthen this area.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on seeking medical evaluation and consistently advances toward concrete next steps (phone consult, message drafting). Each intervention narrows options and moves the plan forward in an organized way.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models a collaborative stance throughout, inviting client input on wording and priorities and co-creating the plan (drafting the message together). The clinician avoids dominating decision-making and shares problem-solving responsibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms the client’s choice and control by offering options and asking preferences, avoiding pressuring language. The clinician explicitly respects pace and ownership while offering support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client’s motivations and past small successes, and elicits doable steps tied to values. Could more consistently amplify and selectively reinforce motivations to convert tentative language into firmer commitment and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly conveys that expertise resides with the client and decisions are shared, asking which option she prefers and following her lead. Power-sharing is consistent and supports joint decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk, ties steps to values, and invites small, doable actions (e.g., message or phone consult). Could more frequently amplify and summarize the client’s change statements to build stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without judgment and redirects to practical problem-solving around barriers like cost and childcare. More deliberate use of double-sided reflections or explicit validation of the logic for avoidance would further soften sustain talk.
