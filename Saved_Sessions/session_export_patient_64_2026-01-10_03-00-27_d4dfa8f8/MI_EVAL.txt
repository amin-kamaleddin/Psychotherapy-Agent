# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone and invited choice about the agenda, demonstrating clear unconditional positive regard. They normalized ambivalence and validated Jordan’s perspective throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and matched Jordan’s guarded style, advancing the conversation. The clinician paraphrased emotions and images effectively, showing high‑quality empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on receiving the flu vaccine with goal‑oriented questions and repeated steering toward concrete next steps. The clinician maintained momentum toward actionable outcomes like a script and low‑stress plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a cooperative exchange with frequent solicitation of Jordan’s input and adaptation to his preferences. Co‑creation of the script, rehearsal, and tailored planning show true partnership in problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission before giving information, and respecting Jordan’s pace. Language avoided pressure and emphasized that decisions were Jordan’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn out rather than imposed through eliciting values (protecting grandparents), imagery, and scaling questions. The clinician linked Jordan’s own reasons to concrete options and planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Jordan as an active partner, inviting choices and tailoring the plan to his preferences. They co‑constructed practical steps and rehearsed language together, reflecting genuine power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and amplified change talk using importance/confidence scales, values questions, and imagery, then summarized and used those reasons to generate steps. These moves produced clear desire/reason language supporting vaccination.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled respectfully with acknowledgment and reframing rather than confrontation, but there were missed opportunities for more frequent double‑sided reflections. More selective amplification of change statements could further roll sustain talk into commitment language.
