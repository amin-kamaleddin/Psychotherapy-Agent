# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and validating (e.g., “You did a lot of sensible things”), which reduced defensiveness; multiple instances of evaluative praise (“I’m proud…”) noted that shift toward clinician judgment and should be limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate paraphrase of concerns and frustration; reflections tended to stay at content level and missed deeper emotional nuance of ambivalence and hesitancy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and protecting scholarship; effective use of 0–10 scaling and direct change‑focused questions, concluding with a concrete harm‑reduction plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly invited Ryan’s input and choices and co‑created the plan (tweaking text/exit line); at times clinician offered ready‑made solutions before eliciting Ryan’s first ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently acknowledged choice and offered options (“If you want, I can help…”); occasional directive/evaluative language slightly reduced pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked Ryan’s values and motivators (education/scholarship), used scaling and “what would need to change…” prompts; could further elicit and amplify additional change talk and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as collaborative (“Help me understand…”, “Which would help you most?”) and co‑constructed plan; offering multiple clinician-generated options at times placed clinician in a more active role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change language (value of school, specific steps) and planning; missed opportunities to reflect/amplify change talk and to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue and acknowledged sustain talk, but limited use of double‑sided or amplified reflections to soften sustain talk and shift it toward discrepancy and change.
