# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, honoring the patient's boundaries and validating frustration throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that de-escalated tension, though could have used more complex emotional reflections or deeper summaries to reach the highest level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the patient's prioritized topic (dental care) and kept the session on track, but did not gently create a bridge toward discussing smoking or future change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-constructed practical plans, offered options, and consistently invited the patient's preferences while leaving decisions with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported the patient's sense of choice and control by affirming agenda-setting, offering options, and explicitly acknowledging the patient's right not to discuss smoking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very limited effort to elicit internal motivation for quitting; the clinician mostly avoided the topic and missed brief permission-based evocation opportunities or linking values to smoking/oral health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine partner—asked what help was wanted, offered to assist in practical ways, and framed the work as joint rather than directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal attempts to elicit or amplify the patient's change language; missed openings to ask about importance/confidence, pros/cons, or to reflect tentative change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, validating the patient's stance and redirecting to their agenda, but did not actively use strategies like double-sided reflections to transform sustain talk.
