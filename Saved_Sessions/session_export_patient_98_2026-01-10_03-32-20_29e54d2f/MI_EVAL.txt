# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and validation through empathic statements and praise for Mateo’s thoughtful, cautious plan. No minimizing or judgmental language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and present, picking up on pain and discouragement. The clinician moved fairly quickly to problem-solving rather than lingering to explore deeper emotions or using more empathic open questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing safe, gentle physical activity with a stepwise plan and safety limits. Suggestions and checks stayed tightly linked to the behavioral target and rehab coordination.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicited Mateo’s input, incorporated his tweaks, and involved spouse/PT/MD in the plan. At times the clinician led with detailed instructions and templates rather than co‑generating solutions from Mateo’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and inviting modifications while avoiding coercive language. Explicitly affirmed Mateo’s decisions and emphasized checking with PT/MD.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited a small goal and commitment but relied more on expert-generated solutions than exploring Mateo’s values, reasons, or confidence. Limited use of scaling or deeper questions to build intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan by asking for tweaks and checking willingness to follow up, demonstrating genuine partnership. Power-sharing was somewhat reduced by providing prescriptive checklists and detailed instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change language and built a micro‑goal (2‑minute walk), encouraging celebration of small wins. Could have more consistently used open questions to draw out Mateo’s personal reasons, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk and provided practical safety-based limits, but used few strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly reframe ambivalence. Responses tended toward directive problem-solving rather than eliciting Mateo’s counterarguments to sustain talk.
