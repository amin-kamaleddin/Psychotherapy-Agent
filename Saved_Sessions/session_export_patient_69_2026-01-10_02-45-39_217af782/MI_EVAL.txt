# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Kieran’s frustration with respectful language and validations. Avoids judgmental labeling and models a warm, accepting stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate, validating reflections and perspective-taking (e.g., naming frustration and skepticism) that build rapport. Misses deeper or more complex reflections that would explore underlying emotions or connections to values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on improving meetings and producing measurable job-focused outcomes, moving from problem identification to a concrete one-month plan. Maintains clear goal orientation without losing task focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructs the plan with Kieran, offers supports without imposing them, and centers his preferences in decisions (scripts, agenda, documentation). Demonstrates shared decision-making and joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices (script options, whether to rehearse or draft the email) and explicitly defers to Kieran’s preference to send the agenda himself. Language affirms ownership and reduces pressure to comply.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits a behavioral commitment to a one-month trial but does little to draw out Kieran’s intrinsic motivations, values, or personal reasons for change. Much of the motivational language and solutions are clinician-supplied rather than self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats Kieran as an equal collaborator, negotiates the plan, and respects boundaries and preferences throughout the interaction. Demonstrates clear power-sharing and invites his input on key decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates concrete opportunities for change talk and elicits specific commitments (choice of option, one-month trial, sending agenda). Could press further to elicit more self-generated reasons and personal motivations for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying resistance and normalizes skepticism, offering pragmatic compromises that redirect toward a testable change. Would benefit from more explicit MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further soften sustain talk.
