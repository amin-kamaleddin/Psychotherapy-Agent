# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized Nadine’s experience while affirming concrete steps she’s taken (e.g., joining the home visiting program). Tone remained warm and nonjudgmental throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used highly accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both facts and emotional nuance (exhaustion, panic, family comparisons). Reflections guided the conversation without redirecting prematurely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda linked to concrete behaviors (weight check, nap coverage, one-line script) and kept questions aimed at advancing a practical action plan. The session stayed centered on measurable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed with repeated solicitation of Nadine’s input and joint planning (home visitor call, nap window, script). The clinician framed the work as shared problem-solving rather than expert-driven direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options and respected Nadine’s preferences (home visitor booking vs. making the call) and reinforced her ownership of decisions. No pressure was applied—only facilitation of her chosen pathway.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified Nadine’s internal motivations with evocative questions, helping translate her reasons (calmer, less panic, confidence) into concrete commitments. Clinician drew out intrinsic motives rather than supplying them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated a collaborative stance and shared decision-making by asking preferences, offering options, and deferring to Nadine’s lived experience. The client’s choices drove the plan and actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and strengthened change language through evocative questions and reflections, and translated willingness into specific actionable steps (weight check, sleep plan, arranged coverage). Change talk was repeatedly drawn out and crystallized.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Validated expressions of doubt and fatigue and then pivoted to doable solutions (home visitor booking, arranged coverage) without arguing or reinforcing reasons for inaction. Clinician reduced ambivalence by offering small, practical steps.
