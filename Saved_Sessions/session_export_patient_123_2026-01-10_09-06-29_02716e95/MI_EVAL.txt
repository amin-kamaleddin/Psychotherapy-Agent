# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses unconditional positive regard, frequent affirmations, and normalizing language while avoiding judgment, producing a warm and supportive stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, frequent reflections and perspective-taking, but reflections remain fairly surface-level and do not probe deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on balancing school/downtime and moves the conversation toward a concrete, progressive action plan with specific implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal-partner approach with inclusive language and co-creation of the plan, though at times the clinician offers unprompted strategies and wording that lean toward an expert role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports client choice by offering options, accepting caveats, making follow-up optional, and avoiding pressure while honoring tentative commitments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits specific plans and doable steps, but does not sufficiently probe underlying reasons, values, or confidence/importance to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative relationship and invites client input, yet occasionally shifts toward advising by presenting a packaged list of strategies without first eliciting the client's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change language and concrete commitments, but misses opportunities to link the plan to values or use scaling questions to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well through normalization and acceptance without arguing, though rarely uses double-sided reflections to explicitly frame ambivalence and pivot toward change.
