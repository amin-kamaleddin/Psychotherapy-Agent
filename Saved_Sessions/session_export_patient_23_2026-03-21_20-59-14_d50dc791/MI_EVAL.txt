# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental, validating language and supportive wording throughout, avoiding labeling or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective‑taking, picking up emotional barriers, but could deepen reflections on the emotional link between drinking and low mood to further validate affect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on reducing alcohol use and moved logically from assessment to concrete, actionable planning throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was highly collaborative with co‑constructed decisions and the clinician offering concrete help only when invited, treating the patient as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and validating realistic limits rather than pushing directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client‑generated motivation and practical strategies using open questions and scales, but could probe deeper into intrinsic motives and amplify change language more strategically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered client expertise and shared decision‑making with frequent solicitations of preference, though offering many options quickly occasionally risked steering the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, praise, and concrete commitments, but missed some opportunities to amplify and convert desire/ability into firmer commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and skillfully redirected to low‑pressure alternatives, though could have used more double‑sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while pivoting to change.
