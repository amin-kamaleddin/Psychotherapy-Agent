# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with validating, nonjudgmental language and repeated affirmations that normalized effort and highlighted strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking captured practical concerns and some emotion, but reflections could probe deeper emotional nuance (e.g., exhaustion, guilt, ambivalence).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on establishing a bedtime routine and backup plans, moving each turn toward progressively concrete, implementable steps without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the encounter as joint problem-solving with frequent invitations to choose, though could further center the patient's existing expertise by asking about past successful strategies earlier.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly respected and reinforced the patient's choice by offering options, asking which felt most doable, and avoiding pressuring language to preserve ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's motivations and concrete ideas effectively, but could strengthen links between values and steps and use scaling to deepen commitment and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created real teamwork through co-constructing plans and scripts, yet occasionally led with clinician-generated options rather than eliciting more of the patient's own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and amplified specific change talk and doable steps, but missed chances to convert value statements into stronger commitment language or to assess confidence/importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged co-parenting barriers and redirected to controllable actions, though could use more double-sided reflections and explore likely partner responses to reduce sustain talk and anxiety.
