# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing Marco's position and respecting his limits. Warmth and steady acceptance are sustained throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflective listening captures Marco's history, emotional tone, and ambivalence, with repeated mirror of his language. Summaries and reflections validated his perspective and strengthened rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking reduction, repeatedly narrowing to a concrete experiment (evening smokes) and actionable steps. Questions and summaries consistently return the conversation to behavior change and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is a genuine joint exploration with inclusive language and co‑development of the plan based on Marco's ideas. The clinician solicits input at each step and adapts interventions to Marco's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly supported with validating language and avoidance of pressure. Questions are framed to elicit Marco's choices and the clinician affirms his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician assumes motivation lies within Marco and uses evocative questions to draw out reasons, strategies, and indicators of success. Past successes and personal measures of progress are elicited to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician operates as a collaborative partner, inviting Marco to lead decision‑making and co‑constructing a realistic plan. Power is genuinely shared and the patient's preferences drive the approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk, turning tentative statements into a specific, doable experiment (fewer evening smokes). The clinician highlights and strengthens Marco's 'I'll try' and explores practical supports to build confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids arguing and rolls with resistance, acknowledging sustain‑talk and honoring autonomy. There is room to more deeply explore the emotional weight and fatalism tied to past health scares and to use more targeted reflective reframing.
