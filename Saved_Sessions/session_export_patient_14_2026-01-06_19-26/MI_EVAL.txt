# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, validates frustration about the wait, and affirms practical choices without judgment. A brief, individualized affirmation naming specific strengths could deepen the sense of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Empathic listening is accurate and timely, with reflections that capture both constraints and motivators to build rapport. Occasionally using slightly deeper emotional reflections could further validate underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on concrete, time‑limited behaviors (packed lunches, two 15‑minute walks) and links plans to core motivators. The clinician resists drifting and consistently ties problem‑solving back to the goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co‑creates the plan with the patient, uses inclusive language, and seeks input before finalizing wording and reminders. Expertise is shared, with the clinician offering suggestions but deferring to the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Suggestions are framed as options and permission is requested before offering ideas, respecting the patient's choices about reminders and check‑ins. The clinician avoids pressuring language and reinforces that the plan is the patient's to accept or modify.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the patient's own reasons (less breathlessness, avoiding meds) using open questions, scaling, and eliciting supports to raise confidence. Asking for a brief client‑generated commitment statement could further consolidate evoked motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction reads as a true partnership, with joint wording of reminders, negotiated times, and frequent invitations for choice. Asking the patient to summarize the plan earlier might further strengthen shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk through open questions, reflections, and confidence scaling, then builds concrete supports to strengthen commitment. A targeted prompt for explicit commitment language could enhance follow‑through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk about time and grazing is acknowledged without confrontation and redirected into collaborative problem‑solving and backup plans. Using occasional double‑sided reflections could more explicitly highlight ambivalence and soften resistance.
