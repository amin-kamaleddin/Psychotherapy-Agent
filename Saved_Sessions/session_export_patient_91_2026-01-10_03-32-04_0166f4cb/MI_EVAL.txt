# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, using normalizing language and reassurance that reduced self-blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate, brief empathic reflections and validated worries, but reflections were often short and quickly followed by technical guidance, missing opportunities for deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on blood glucose monitoring and concrete action steps, leaving the patient with specific thresholds, scripts, and a one-week plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked permission, invited input, and offered role-play—but occasionally shifted into a teacher/expert stance with detailed instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported choice by offering options and asking for a confidence rating, though at times firm recommendations were given without first eliciting the patient’s preferred approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made attempts to draw out motivation (planning, confidence rating), but relied more on directive information than on open, evocative questions to elicit values or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed elements of partnership by co-creating a small plan and offering rehearsal, but frequently defaulted to an expert-driven approach rather than fully foregrounding the patient’s expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and elicited a willingness to try, but did not consistently reflect, reinforce, or amplify the patient’s change statements to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used normalizing language that helped reduce resistance, but mainly responded with practical solutions rather than strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to address sustain talk.
