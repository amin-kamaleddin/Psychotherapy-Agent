PSYCHIATRIST: A statin lowers LDL (“bad”) cholesterol by blocking liver cholesterol production, which reduces heart attack and stroke risk. Most are taken once daily—many work best in the evening, but some (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin) can be any time; check your prescription or ask your pharmacist. Common side effects: mild muscle aches, digestive upset, and occasional liver enzyme changes. Rare but serious: severe unexplained muscle pain, weakness, dark urine, or jaundice—seek medical care immediately. Avoid grapefruit with some statins and don’t use if pregnant/breastfeeding. Start it with a daily anchor (toothbrushing) and schedule follow-up labs. Ask your prescriber any specific concerns.
