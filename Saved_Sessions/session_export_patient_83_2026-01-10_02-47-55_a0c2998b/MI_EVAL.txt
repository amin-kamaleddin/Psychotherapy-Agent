# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the patient's concerns and affirming her plan. Language is warm and facilitating throughout, demonstrating unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking—normalizes anxiety and tailors explanations to specific worries. More explicit reflective statements naming feelings and more frequent summaries would deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on starting and safely using the statin with practical next steps (labs, timing, stopping supplements). Minimal drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites patient input, offers to co-create a checklist, and provides language for calls, making the patient an active partner. The session is often weighted toward clinician expertise, which somewhat reduces patient-led agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports the patient's right to choose, offers balanced options, and avoids pressuring language. Preserves patient ownership of the decision-making process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits immediate needs at the start but thereafter mainly supplies information and recommendations rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence. More open, strategic questions and reflections would strengthen this domain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and defers appropriately to the prescriber while inviting patient involvement. Much of the interaction is information-delivery rather than fully sharing power to let the patient shape the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforces and operationalizes the patient's change statements with affirmations and concrete steps. The clinician rarely uses strategic open questions to elicit additional spontaneous change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Addresses sustain talk nonconfrontationally with factual reassurance and pragmatic risk-reduction advice, reducing its power. Could add more reflective or double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence.
