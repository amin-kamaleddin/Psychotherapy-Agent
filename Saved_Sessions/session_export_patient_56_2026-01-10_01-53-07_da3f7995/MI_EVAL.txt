# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, using normalizing statements and affirmations throughout the session. The clinician validates difficulties and affirms the patient's efforts, demonstrating genuine warmth and respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and reflective understanding with tailored suggestions and compassionate phrasing. Could deepen affective reflections to more fully name the patient's worries and emotions rather than moving quickly to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on creating a transition ritual and ties all interventions to that specific behavior change goal. The session is organized with concrete steps, troubleshooting, and a concrete follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the process as team-based with inclusive language, choices, and invitations for input, and offers to review the tracker together. Collaboration is somewhat reduced by the clinician generating most solutions rather than co-constructing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering multiple options, asking which feels doable, and leaving follow-up scheduling to the patient. Normalizes flexibility and encourages self-directed adjustments rather than pressuring compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation by linking behavior to values and asking the patient to choose approaches, but relies mainly on clinician-provided solutions. Missed opportunities to draw out the patient's own reasons, strengths, and detailed change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and invitations to choose, but the clinician often takes an expert lead by presenting a detailed multi-step plan. Greater co-construction and soliciting the patient's ideas before offering recommendations would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language (choice of strategy, commitment to tracker) but largely moves to solution provision rather than amplifying the patient's own motivations. Could use more open questions and reflections to draw out desire, reasons, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes slips, offers self-compassion language, and proposes micro-alternatives that reduce sustain talk. Would benefit from more explicit double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while pivoting to change.
