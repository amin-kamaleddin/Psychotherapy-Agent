# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental support and positive regard (e.g., “I’m here to listen and support you,” “it’s okay to feel unsure,” “Thank you — that commitment is a strong first step”). Multiple explicit affirmations (≈6+ occurrences).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Several accurate validations and brief reflections (e.g., “That makes sense,” paraphrasing concerns about taste/skin irritation). Missed opportunities for deeper, emotion‑focused reflections (did not probe the emotional impact of smoking or explore the low confidence score further).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking behavior: asked importance/confidence, elicited triggers, negotiated a specific 1‑week tracking plan, taught coping strategies, and set a one‑week check‑in with repeated summaries.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Offered options and solicited the patient’s preference (“Which feels manageable?”, “Want to check in after the week?”). Some segments leaned toward information delivery (lengthy NRT overview) before fully eliciting patient priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and agency (multiple options, conditional offers of NRT info, leaving decisions with the patient). Tone avoided pressure and supported a manageable experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Used evocation moves (0–10 scales, triggers, “Which feels manageable?”) and elicited a concrete experiment (tracking), but did not systematically deepen or amplify the patient’s own motivations or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Generally treated the patient as expert in her life (offers of choice, invitations to decide). Some instructional/prescriptive moments (detailed NRT/technique info) reduced opportunities for fuller power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Elicited limited change talk (importance 6/10, confidence 4/10, agreement to track and try gum). Missed chances to reflect/amplify reasons and boost commitment (e.g., “Why a 6 and not a 2?”; “What would increase your confidence?”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Effectively normalized ambivalence and avoided reinforcing sustain talk (“Doubts are normal,” neutral redirections). Could have used more double‑sided reflections to explicitly balance pull of smoking with stated concerns.
