# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating; normalized the patient’s experience and affirmed realistic, incremental goals, demonstrating unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good empathic understanding and some reflective listening, normalizing ambivalence and practical concerns, but missed opportunities for deeper, emotion-focused reflections before moving into planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing alcohol use with targeted assessment, concrete goals, implementation strategies, and contingency planning tied to the behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative and joint problem-solving: asked permission, sought the patient’s input, and tailored suggestions to the patient’s preferences rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and respected readiness limits by offering options, asking permission before advice, and avoiding pressuring language to support patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some self-directed motivation through goal-setting and questions about history, but relied more on offering strategies than systematically drawing out the patient’s own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the patient as an expert and co-created plans, deferring to preferences (e.g., texting Lena) and sharing power in decision-making throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language and concrete steps, praised goals, and prompted commitments, but could further amplify change talk with scaling and questions about importance and reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged sustain concerns, but did not frequently use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or strategic summaries) to explicitly tip ambivalence toward change.
