# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (clear confidentiality statement, repeated affirmations: “I’m proud you’re trying”). Validates triggers and normalizes slips. Praise occasionally feels formulaic and clinician moves quickly to solutions without fully reflecting disappointment about past failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows moments of accurate understanding (acknowledges evenings as trigger, tiredness, family dynamics) but reflections are brief and shallow. Limited exploration of deeper emotions (discouragement, guardedness) and few complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, sustained focus on evening routines/weight behavior. Provides clear, concrete, time-limited plan (pre-cut veggies, 10‑minute walk, practice once, note one success/challenge, re-rate) and structured follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and choices (offers two scripts, asks patient to pick a plan, agrees to practice together). However, session remains clinician-led with multiple prescriptive steps and detailed instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms choices (asks patient to choose script, use confidence rating) and encourages self-monitoring. Balance leans toward directive homework language, which slightly reduces felt autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits limited change talk (patient offers desire and confidence rating). Missed opportunities to probe motivations (e.g., “why a 6?” or explore what more energy would mean), so internal reasons are not deeply developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language (“we’ll review,” “we’ll practice together”), invites preferences, and plans joint review. Clinician expertise remains central through provision of scripts and multiple directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some baseline change talk present (wanting weight loss, energy for kids) but clinician rarely amplifies or reflects to strengthen desire/ability/reasons/need; limited moves to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain-talk content (triggers, family barriers) and offers practical strategies and fallback plans, which avoids reinforcement. Does not consistently use MI-specific techniques (double-sided or strategic reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk.
