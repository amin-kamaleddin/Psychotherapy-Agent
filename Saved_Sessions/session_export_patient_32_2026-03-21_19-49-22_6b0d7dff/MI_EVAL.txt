# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an accepting, nonjudgmental tone with validating language and practical support, avoiding labeling or moralizing and conveying respect for the client's situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and immediate validation (e.g., 'that sounds scary and stressful') and checked safety, but reflections were brief and the clinician moved quickly into problem‑solving without exploring emotional ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on avoiding repeat DUIs and used concrete, actionable harm‑reduction strategies and weekly planning to steer toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was collaborative—scripts and plans were co‑created and the clinician integrated the client's language—but the clinician at times led with detailed recommendations, limiting equal control of the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported client choice by asking permission for planning and role‑play, offering options, and praising the client's wording to reinforce ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change through planning and role‑play, but rarely used open, evocative questions to draw out the client's own motivations, values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed a collaborative stance by offering options and co‑constructing plans, though sessions sometimes tilted toward clinician-led problem‑solving rather than fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change language via practical planning and scripts, but missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic reasons and boost importance/confidence with open questions and scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and steered toward harm‑reduction, but did not sufficiently use reflections, double‑sided reflections, or strategic summaries to soften and resolve ambivalence.
