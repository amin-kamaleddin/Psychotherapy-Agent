# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard—validates ambivalence, avoids judgment, and offers support without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective‑taking and frequent reflections that capture ambivalence, but many reflections are brief and the clinician sometimes moves quickly to information and solutions rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on getting the flu vaccine decision addressed and repeatedly returns to concrete next steps and planning without getting sidetracked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with invitation to input and co‑development of plans, though supplying many ready-made scripts slightly shifts toward expert provision rather than eliciting the client's own wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports the client's right to decide, offers multiple options and a short decision window, and uses tools (role‑play, scripts) to empower rather than coerce.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal motivations (concern for grandparents) and uses them in planning, but relies more on information and scripts than on open questions and reflections to draw out the client's own values and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treats the client as a partner—asks permission, offers choices, and co‑constructs the plan rather than prescribing actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for change talk by weighing options and offering planning help, but misses some chances to amplify and elicit stronger reasons, importance, and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (low perceived personal risk) but sometimes validates it by presenting non‑vaccination as an equivalent option and uses few double‑sided or redirecting reflections to shift momentum toward change.
