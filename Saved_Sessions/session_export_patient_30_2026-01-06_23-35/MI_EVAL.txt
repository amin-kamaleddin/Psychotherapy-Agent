# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized the patient's concerns with validating statements and alternatives, avoiding blame or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking that showed understanding, but many reflections stayed at the surface rather than probing deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the medication decision and steered the session toward concrete, measurable next steps (script, labs, stop criteria, follow-up) without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a true two-way partner by offering options, inviting patient input at each step, and co-constructing the plan and scripts together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored the patient's choice and control, offered multiple options, and avoided pressuring language while affirming the final decision rests with the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some efforts to draw out motivations and elicited a behavioral commitment, but relied heavily on clinician-supplied language and missed opportunities to elicit deeper intrinsic reasons or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly fostered shared decision-making by asking permission, co-creating materials, and repeatedly inviting the patient to lead or modify the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced small, actionable change talk and converted reluctance into a concrete short-trial plan, though could have more explicitly drawn out desire/reasons/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirected concerns into monitoring and alternatives, but could have used more strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further tip the balance.
