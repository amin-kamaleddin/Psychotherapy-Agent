# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Julian’s experience repeatedly; could add more explicit unconditional acceptance and brief normalizing statements about how common this anxiety is.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and noticed specific details (stumbling, pauses) with supportive tips; could deepen attunement by naming underlying feelings like embarrassment or fear of consequences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on completing court-ordered service and advanced concrete next steps (scripts, schedule, log fields) each turn; very strong actionable focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the session as problem-solving with invitations to practice and choose modes of contact, but often led solution generation rather than co-creating options with Julian first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices and respected Julian’s control (options for voicemail/text/role-play, asking preferences), avoiding pressuring language and affirming his decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied mostly on logistics and external motivators with little effort to elicit Julian’s internal reasons or values for change; missed opportunities for open evocative questions and scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited input and offered options which fostered shared decision-making, but presented many prescriptive templates without consistently eliciting Julian’s preferences first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited concrete planning and some commitments (when to call, schedule) but focused on logistics rather than exploring Julian’s personal reasons or benefits for completing hours.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and steered toward action with scripts, but offered little strategic work to acknowledge ambivalence (no double-sided reflections or explicit rolling with resistance).
