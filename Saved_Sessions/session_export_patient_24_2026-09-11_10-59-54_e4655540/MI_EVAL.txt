# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and affirming strengths throughout the session without blaming or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid perspective-taking and timely reflections that help the patient elaborate; could use more complex reflections naming emotional nuance (e.g., surprise or defensiveness) to deepen feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol in relation to marathon training and progressively narrows the conversation to concrete, measurable actions without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine teamwork is evident—clinician solicits the patient's ideas, co-constructs wording, offers variants, and repeatedly asks which approach feels most natural.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces choice by offering multiple options, asking what feels doable, and leaving follow-up timing to the patient, supporting the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws out the patient's own motivations and solutions with evocative open questions and links change to the patient's values and routines, producing concrete commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reflects equal collaboration and power-sharing—clinician checks tone/wording, invites the patient's preferences, and co-creates the plan together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk through open questions, reflections, and invitations to commit, resulting in specific plans (no alcohol before long runs, buddy accountability).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk and pivots to feasible solutions, but could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both the benefits of drinking and reasons for change.
