# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, explicitly framing the session as safe and validating the patient's choices and limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with regular, reflective listening that mirrored the patient's concerns about energy and schedule and normalized their feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity and moved the session logically toward a concrete, measurable plan (10-minute walk, alarms, swaps, follow-up).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partnership by inviting input at decision points and incorporating the patient's preferences so the plan was meaningfully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language while respecting the patient's limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out concrete motivation and used a confidence ruler, but spent more time on logistics than exploring deeper personal values or long-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative and patient-centered, though the clinician sometimes shifted toward offering concrete solutions rather than explicitly co-designing the plan and eliciting the patient's summary earlier.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with specific planning and a confidence ruler, but could more deliberately amplify and summarize desire/reasons/ability to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and offered gentler alternatives, but could use more double-sided reflections and elicit client rebuttals to further soften sustain talk.
