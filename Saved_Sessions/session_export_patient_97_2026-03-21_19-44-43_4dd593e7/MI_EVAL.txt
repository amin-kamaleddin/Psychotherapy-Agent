# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, treats Mateo respectfully and nonjudgmentally, and offers concrete assistance. Maintain this warm, normalizing tone when Mateo expresses discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic stance with invitations to share and responsive pivots to Mateo's practical preferences, but relatively few reflective statements that capture his ambivalence and emotional experience. Increase simple and complex reflections to deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remains clearly focused on target behaviors (staying out of trouble, housing, employment) with structured, actionable next steps. The clinician consistently ties tasks to concrete actions and resources.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative—resume co-creation and tailoring of materials show respect for Mateo's input—but the clinician often leads with lists and recommendations rather than jointly generating options. Invite Mateo to prioritize and choose among alternatives more explicitly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician offers choices, asks preferences, and avoids coercive language, supporting Mateo's sense of control. Could further reinforce autonomy when Mateo frames attendance as an obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (urgent needs, willingness for practical steps) but the clinician predominantly shifts to logistics and advice rather than exploring values, past successes, or confidence. Use open questions to probe reasons, values, and confidence and amplify Mateo's personal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invites preferences and solicits information, demonstrating partnership, yet frequently assumes an expert/problem-solver role with long lists of steps. Strengthen partnership by asking more before telling and explicitly co-creating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful questions that produced some change talk and operationalized action (resume, calls), but missed chances to explicitly evoke and amplify desire, reasons, and confidence. Prompt more DARN (desire/reason/need) statements and reflect or summarize those parts to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to solutions, but uses few active techniques to soften it (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring costs of status quo). Incorporate reflective reframing and elicitation of the downsides of current behavior to lessen sustain talk.
