# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and validates the client's perspective with nonjudgmental language and repeated affirmations. The clinician normalizes struggle and avoids blaming throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and paraphrases that show solid understanding, but reflections are sometimes repetitive and formulaic. Missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance behind the client's use as an escape.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on immediate harm-reduction behaviors (Narcan sharing, timing, practice) and returns to concrete, measurable steps. Resists drifting into unrelated topics and orients toward actionable outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-creates the plan with the client by soliciting preferences, checking feasibility, and mirroring the client's language. The clinician continually invites input so the plan feels like shared problem-solving rather than an expert directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports client choice by offering multiple options and asking which is most doable, avoiding directives. Reinforces ownership of the plan and the client's control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internally-held motivations for safety and concrete signs of relief, helping the client picture benefits. Could deepen by linking safety steps to broader values and eliciting confidence or a stronger verbal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative and locates expertise with the client, using language that shares power. The client's choices consistently shape the plan and implementation details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk around specific harm-reduction actions and implementation details. Could more actively deepen and amplify change talk by asking about underlying reasons, values, and confidence in follow-through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and redirects to safety without shaming, focusing on concrete harm-reduction steps. Would be stronger with more double-sided reflections or explicit acknowledgment of the legitimacy of the desire to escape before steering to safety.
