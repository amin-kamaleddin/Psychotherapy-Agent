# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance with empathic validations and repeated normalization, making the patient feel respected and safe to disclose.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and a caring tone, especially around safety, but some reflections are brief and could deepen exploration of the patient's resigned affect and hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on self-harm and immediate safety throughout the session, repeatedly prioritizing focused safety questions and concrete risk-reduction steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with co-constructed safety planning and solicitation of the patient's decisions, though at times the clinician provides scripted or firm guidance appropriate to imminent risk.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options and asks for agreement, supporting patient control, but some necessary directive language around securing the razor slightly limits perceived choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits immediate safety-oriented commitment (delay, securing razor) but does little to draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or longer-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as a team effort, stays present, and invites patient reporting, though occasional directive instructions reduce shared decision-making in service of safety.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforces small safety steps and invites short-term commitments, but misses systematic elicitation of the patient's own reasons, values, and longer-term goals for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk by validating and redirecting to alternatives and safety planning, though could use more double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence.
