# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established a clearly nonjudgmental, supportive stance and repeatedly validated the patient’s experience, avoiding blame and thanking the patient for engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were multiple empathic reflections acknowledging feelings and practical barriers, but empathy was generally brief and pragmatic rather than using deeper, complex reflections to explore emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained tightly focused on the target behavior (attending interviews despite childcare barriers) with concrete, actionable suggestions and no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician tailored materials to the patient’s preferences and invited input, but often supplied scripts and next steps rather than jointly generating options from the patient’s ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice by asking permission before offering drafts, honoring the patient’s preferences, and presenting multiple options rather than prescribing a single course.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged the patient’s confidence but did little to elicit underlying motivations, values, or reasons, relying more on solution delivery than evocative open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were invitations to collaborate and revisions based on requests, yet the interaction remained largely clinician-driven with the clinician assuming the expert role in generating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient offered strong change talk which was acknowledged, but the clinician missed opportunities to amplify and deepen commitment-language through reflective prompts and values-oriented questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized and the clinician redirected to practical solutions, though MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) were underused.
