# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing Malik's frustration and offering respectful options. Warmth and support are sustained throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and validates Malik's feelings, summarizing his social use and annoyance at being brought in. Some reflections remain at the surface level and could probe deeper emotions and relational nuances.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking and the mother–son conflict and moves the session from assessment to concrete planning and rehearsal. Conversation is consistently steered toward actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative—Malik is invited to choose focus, co-create scripts, and role‑play—fostering an equal partnership. The clinician occasionally takes the lead with structured solutions, which slightly shifts power toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports Malik's choice by asking permission, offering options, and checking willingness to practice. Some directive scripting and concrete phrases reduce opportunities for Malik to generate his own language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits factual information and some brief acknowledgment of consequences but does not sufficiently draw out Malik's internal motivations or values. The clinician more often supplies scripts and steps instead of eliciting self-motivational language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Honors Malik's expertise by inviting input and co-creating strategies while offering role‑play and choices. The provision of expert advice and scripted language occasionally lessens client-led problem formulation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for change talk with open questions and choices, producing some tentative change statements from Malik. However, the clinician does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or build on Malik's emerging change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and rolls with resistance, which prevents strengthening sustain talk, but rarely uses strategic techniques like double-sided reflections to soften it. Some harm‑reduction focus could be read as supporting the status quo rather than shifting sustain talk toward change.
