# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient’s feelings, using explicit acceptance and nonjudgmental language to create safety. These statements support the patient without shaming continued use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflections and summaries capture both content and emotion, helping the patient feel heard and open up. The clinician uses complex reflections and frequent empathic statements that are on-target.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on harm-reduction goals with clear, goal-directed questions that steer toward concrete next steps. The clinician balances exploration with momentum toward practical safety behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative and egalitarian, routinely eliciting the patient’s input before offering plans and tailoring suggestions to the patient’s preferences. Decisions and strategies are co-created throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms choice by offering options, asking permission, and validating the patient’s control over actions. Language supports the patient’s sense of agency and avoids directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws motivation and reasons for change from the patient with evocative questions and by building on patient-generated language. Change talk is elicited and translated into actionable steps and commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as a joint endeavor, asking which steps feel clearest and following the patient’s lead in planning. Repeated invitations to choose and refine options demonstrate true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is actively elicited, reinforced, and amplified, with the clinician turning desires into specific, manageable actions and rehearsals. The clinician invites concrete planning and affirms strong readiness and commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and softened through double-sided reflections and neutral acknowledgment of ambivalence, avoiding reinforcement of sustaining language. The clinician could be more strategic in briefly highlighting costs of the status quo and selectively amplifying change language to further reduce sustain talk.
