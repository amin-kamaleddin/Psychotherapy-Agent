# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and strengths-based with affirmations and respect for Marco’s efforts and values throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and reflective listening, but reflections largely stayed at a pragmatic level and did not consistently explore deeper emotions like shame or the pleasure tied to OJ.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, steady focus on reducing orange juice with concrete planning, timing, and implementation details; no drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was collaborative and co-constructed a plan with Marco, but the clinician’s frequent provision of specific scripts and tactics made the approach somewhat more directive than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Marco’s choice and control by offering options, checking preferences, and affirming ownership of the plan without pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Marco’s motivations and plans using open questions and scaling, though the clinician supplemented heavily with solutions rather than relying solely on elicited client resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally worked as an equal partner—asking preferences and inviting choices—but at times the clinician’s expertise and materials steered the session more than Marco’s ideas alone.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced multiple change statements through scaling and concrete planning, but could more selectively reflect and amplify Marco’s strongest family- and pride-based reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not actively soften or explore sustain talk about loving OJ; the clinician moved quickly to problem-solving and missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and validation of loss/pleasure.
