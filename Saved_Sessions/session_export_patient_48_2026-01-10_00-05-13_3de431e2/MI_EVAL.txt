# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing difficulty and affirming effort to create a warm, accepting stance throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening with accurate reflections of doubt and barriers, but could deepen empathy by exploring underlying emotions and using more complex reflections of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing brief physical activity with concrete steps, backups, and a specified follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-creates the plan and checks preferences, but collaboration could be strengthened by soliciting the patient's ideas earlier so the session feels more equally shaped.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms the patient's freedom of choice, frames the plan as an experiment, and avoids coercive language, supporting ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits change talk and links actions to meaningful outcomes, yet could deepen intrinsic motivation by asking more about the patient's personal reasons and values for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and invites choice, though at times becomes somewhat directive when offering specific strategies; asking the patient to propose the plan first would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and translates tentative change talk into a concrete plan, but could more consistently amplify and summarize the patient's change statements to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes doubt, reframes barriers, and avoids arguing while offering practical planning to reduce sustain talk; more use of double-sided reflections could further pivot toward change.
