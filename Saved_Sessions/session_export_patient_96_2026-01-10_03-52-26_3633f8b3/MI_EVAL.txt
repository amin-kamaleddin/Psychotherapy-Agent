# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental language and explicit admiration, normalizing Marisol’s choices. No shaming or moralizing was present throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections, summarizing motives and barriers clearly. Could have probed deeper into the emotional meaning of “more me” and explored affect/values more thoroughly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on oral risks and practical harm‑reduction steps, using a concise checklist and a planned 7‑day check‑in. The clinician steered the session toward concrete, behaviorally specific actions without getting sidetracked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like a true partnership—preferences were solicited and the plan was co‑constructed. The clinician invited Marisol’s input and positioned both as contributors to problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized autonomy, avoided directive language, and offered options rather than prescriptions. The clinician respected Marisol’s decisions (e.g., not requiring removal or quitting) while supporting choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations, identity statements, and preferred strategies, and invited Marisol to choose next steps. Missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic reasons for change or use scaling and targeted prompts to generate more client‑produced arguments for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the interaction as collaborative, asked permission before giving information, and negotiated the check‑in content. Decisions and the safety plan were co‑created and checked for fit with Marisol’s preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Made purposeful attempts to elicit change talk (asking about risks, offering choices, defining a one‑week win) and reinforced agreed steps. Could increase open questions and explicitly amplify tentative desire/ability/reasons/need language to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not amplify sustain talk, skillfully shifting to harm‑reduction and reflective summaries. Would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and gentle contrasts of costs/benefits to further diminish sustain talk.
