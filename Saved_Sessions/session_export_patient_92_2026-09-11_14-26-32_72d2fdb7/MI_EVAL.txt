# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, validated Marek's priorities, and avoided labeling or shaming, creating a safe, nonjudgmental space for change talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician offered timely, accurate reflections that built rapport and summarized Marek's tension, though they could have probed or reflected more on defensiveness and minimizations to deepen emotional resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on weekend drinking and progressed to a specific, measurable behavioral experiment (three beers, alternate with water) with a concrete follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative, with the clinician soliciting Marek's preferences, brainstorming together, and co-constructing a plan while resisting an expert-driven prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported autonomy by offering options, asking which felt doable, and inviting Marek to choose and test the strategy without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician drew motivation from Marek using evocative questions that elicited internal reasons for change and concrete signs of success rather than supplying reasons for him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned Marek as the expert on his life, shared power in decision-making, and co-created the plan in a manner that preserved Marek's ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly elicited and reinforced change talk through evocative questions, helped generate concrete strategies, and strengthened commitment with a planned check-in.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and redirected toward feasible experiments, but did not explicitly use double-sided reflections or normalizing statements to name and soften sustain talk early on.
