# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validated Maria's distress without blame. Normalized her need to be heard before moving to problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, mirroring Maria's language and affect. Offered containment (staying on the line) that likely increased safety and trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on suicidal ideation and immediate safety throughout the encounter. Conducted thorough risk assessment and moved decisively to concrete safety planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely acted as an equal partner by soliciting Maria's priorities and offering to share responsibility for steps. Could deepen collaboration by exploring past brief coping successes and aligning plans with her values.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices, asked what felt most doable, and sought permission for direct questions, supporting agency. Some necessary prescriptive safety language limited autonomy but was clinically appropriate.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some protective language about Maria's sister but focused mainly on logistics rather than internal motivations. Missed opportunities to ask evocative questions about hopes, values, and longer-term reasons for living.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with permission-seeking, options, and offers to co-create scripts and stay on the line. At times took a firmer lead due to risk management, and could invite more of Maria's ideas for change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Highlighted protective reasons (the sister) and encouraged safety behaviors but did not systematically elicit or amplify DARN-C change talk. Opportunities were missed to ask targeted open questions about values, hopes, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain talk while redirecting to safety planning, which reduced defensiveness. Could further soften sustain talk by using double-sided reflections or reframes to juxtapose change and sustain language.
