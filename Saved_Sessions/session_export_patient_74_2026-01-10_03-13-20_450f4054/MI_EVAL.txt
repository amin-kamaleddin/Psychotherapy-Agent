# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys nonjudgmental acceptance with explicit statements acknowledging Linda enjoys smoking and isn’t ready to quit; avoids scolding and affirms her choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and summaries that capture Linda’s ambivalence and values, demonstrating solid understanding; reflections are sometimes formulaic and could probe more deeply into emotional content (e.g., role-modeling feelings).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session repeatedly pivots to parental involvement and rarely explores smoking despite the referral, weakening focus on the primary target behavior; the clinician did not sufficiently link the chosen goals to tobacco cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invites Linda’s preferences, co-creates a concrete, doable plan, and checks what feels most doable; decisions are built together rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Linda’s right to choose and avoids pressuring her to quit smoking, leaving control of decisions and next steps with the client; language repeatedly supports autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively evokes Linda’s motivations for being a present parent and specifics for school involvement, but does not sufficiently elicit her own motivations to change smoking or explore importance, confidence, or past attempts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance with open questions and shared planning that centers Linda’s preferences, yet occasional directing language and repeated summaries slightly reduce client-led expertise; more explicit acknowledgement of Linda’s knowledge would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and amplifies change talk about school involvement with concrete commitments and implementation steps, but misses opportunities to cultivate change talk related to smoking and link it to role-modeling concerns.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and explicitly accepts sustain talk about smoking, which prevents escalation, but does little to strategically soften or resolve ambivalence (e.g., lacks double-sided reflections or reframing to surface discrepancy).
