# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence with explicit validations, creating a safe, nonjudgmental stance that allowed exploration of options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality reflective listening captured both content and affect (e.g., stress, craving, desire for fewer interruptions) and the clinician checked accuracy, demonstrating strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing daytime smoking interruptions via scheduled nicotine gum, linking goals to concrete steps and backup plans without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed the plan with David, soliciting his ideas about gum/lozenge choice, timing, and logistics and verifying fit with his shift routine, showing excellent shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirmed David's right to choose, framed the plan as an experiment he owns, and avoided pressuring language while inviting his preferences on dosing and logistics.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician drew out intrinsic motivators (fewer interruptions, less crankiness) and elicited a concrete trial, but could deepen evocation by exploring values and using scaling to expand internal motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated David as the expert on his life, used open questions to elicit his preferences, and built the plan from his responses, demonstrating clear power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully moved ambivalence to specific change language and a concrete trial (gum, alarm, locker), but could more consistently amplify and probe for commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and reframed sustain talk toward experimentation, though could more often use double-sided reflections to strategically acknowledge both sides and further soften sustain talk.
