# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with warm, validating language and normalized the tension between motivation and family barriers without blaming. Affirmed Amina’s choices and feelings throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening, asking about values and checking importance/confidence. Could deepen empathic reflection of the emotional impact of repeated missed opportunities with more complex reflections or follow-up questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on securing employment given childcare constraints and repeatedly steered to practical, actionable steps. The session produced a specific, time‑bound plan (supervised trial Saturday, payment terms, backup childcare strategies).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—decisions were co-constructed, materials were tailored to Amina’s needs, and the clinician positioned themselves as a helper rather than an authority. Adapted supports based on Amina’s requests and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and honoring Amina’s selection of backup childcare. Reinforced Amina’s ownership of decisions and avoided pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Amina’s motivations and confidence and surfaced client-generated steps, but offered a long list of strategies early on which leaned toward advising. Would benefit from prompting more of Amina’s own brainstorming before offering solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a partnership stance by soliciting priorities, inviting choice about next steps, and offering collaborative supports (drafting messages, role-play). Kept Amina’s expertise central in planning and decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk (importance=10, confidence=8), asked which option felt doable, and helped translate motivation into concrete actions like drafting messages and arranging trials. Reinforced commitment and offered practical supports to follow through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk and redirected to pragmatic workarounds without arguing or dismissing barriers. Could further soften sustain talk by using more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence before moving to solutions.
