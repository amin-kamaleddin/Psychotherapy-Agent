# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental respect, normalized ambivalence, and honored Samir’s boundaries (e.g., no lecturing, no copy). Minor improvement could be adding brief affirmations of strengths when he agrees to try a trial.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections showed good perspective-taking and accurate summaries that moved Samir from guardedness to planning. The clinician could deepen empathic work by reflecting underlying emotions and identity concerns (e.g., “changing who you are”).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on medication adherence and efficiently progressed from exploration to a concrete, feasible action plan. Summaries and targeted logistics kept the conversation on task and avoided tangents.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the interaction as joint problem-solving, inviting Samir’s input on wording, who does the check-in, and timing, which fostered true co-construction. Choices were offered at each step, keeping the plan patient-centered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized Samir’s control (short trial, option to stop, choice about contact), reinforcing patient ownership of decisions. Checks about which option 'feels easier' further supported autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective open prompts elicited a concrete plan and practical preferences from Samir rather than imposing solutions. Evocation could be stronger by exploring deeper personal reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Samir as the expert, seeking his preferences at every step and only offering action after asking which felt easier. This genuine power-sharing preserved partnership throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and amplified change talk, turning minimal engagement into a specific, time-limited commitment (one pill at night, start date, nurse check-in). Further work could elicit more reasons for change and confidence language to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing, accepted sustain concerns, and reframed them into harm-minimizing options (short trial, stop plan), which reduced resistance. Using occasional double-sided reflections could further attenuate sustain talk without confrontation.
