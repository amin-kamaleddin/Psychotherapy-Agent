# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician showed clear unconditional positive regard—normalizing hesitancy, validating concerns about needles and side effects, and using affirmations throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and opened Jordan up, but empathic responses remained pragmatic and somewhat surface‑level without deeper exploration of emotions like needle anxiety or underlying values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on receiving the vaccine and moved steadily toward concrete actions (readiness ruler, scripts, role‑plays, and scheduling plans).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strong joint problem‑solving—co‑creating scripts, asking Jordan’s preferences, and repeatedly checking back to shape the plan together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed Jordan’s choice, offered options, asked permission for role‑plays, and supported his ownership of decisions and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Jordan’s own values (protecting his grandparents) through open questions and targeted prompts, converting those reasons into actionable steps and language he could use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear collaborative stance was maintained—inviting Jordan’s input, co‑planning scripts and actions, and treating him as an active decision‑maker throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk using the readiness ruler and facilitator options and translated this into practical steps, though they could have more explicitly amplified change statements and elicited an in‑session commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and nonconfrontationally redirected with practical accommodations (numbing spray, convenience), but more frequent complex or double‑sided reflections could have better named ambivalence and deepened exploration of sustaining beliefs.
