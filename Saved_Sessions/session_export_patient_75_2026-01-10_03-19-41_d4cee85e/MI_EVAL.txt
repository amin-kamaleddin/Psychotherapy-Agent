# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys consistent unconditional positive regard, normalizing Linda's struggle and repeatedly reassuring her without judgment. Acceptance is explicit and linked to specific feelings, supporting a strong therapeutic stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately name Linda's emotions and life context, demonstrating solid perspective-taking. Several reflections are somewhat repetitive and there were missed opportunities to probe the intensity and impact of feelings more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on controller adherence and walking-related symptoms, moving clearly from exploration to a concrete, testable plan. The clinician consistently links questions and suggestions back to the target behavior with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is clearly structured as a joint effort with offers to draft PCP questions and a tracking sheet and repeated invitations for Linda's input. The clinician co-creates the one-week test plan and lets Linda steer next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and control are emphasized throughout, with nonpressuring language and options presented for each step. The clinician repeatedly offers to help only if Linda wants and invites her preferences for reminders and materials.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Linda's motivations and specific change ideas, bringing out concrete reasons and plans. To strengthen evocation, the clinician could more consistently amplify and summarize change talk and use rulers or values links to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician fosters shared decision-making by inviting Linda's expertise and preferences and distributing power throughout the session. Questions about what matters most and which materials to prepare emphasize authentic collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions and offers to experiment generated concrete change language and a realistic short-term plan. The clinician could improve by selectively reflecting and summarizing change talk and using importance/confidence or commitment scaling to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and balanced with double-sided reflections that validate concerns while redirecting to a short-term test. Additional use of selective reflections, reframing, or highlighting the costs of non-change would further soften sustain talk.
