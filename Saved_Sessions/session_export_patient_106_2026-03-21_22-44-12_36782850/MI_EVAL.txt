# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with frequent affirmations and normalization that convey unconditional positive regard. The clinician validates the patient's situation and role as a single mom throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic listening and accurate reflections of the patient's perspective and practical constraints. Could deepen exploration of underlying emotions (anxiety, loneliness, shame) to reach a top rating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking and creating a smoke-free home and moves the conversation toward concrete, achievable steps. Questions and reflections consistently progress toward a tangible first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative, with the clinician soliciting and building on the patient's ideas to co-construct the plan. The patient's responses repeatedly shape the next steps, reflecting shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirms the patient's choice, offers options, and avoids pressure or directives. The clinician emphasizes the patient's control over what to try and normalizes slips, supporting autonomy strongly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's motivations and past supports (protecting her son, routines that worked) and draws out intrinsic motives. Could probe deeper into values and meaning and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the expert on her life, using language that invites her ideas and preferences. The clinician repeatedly asks for the patient's input and co-creates the plan, demonstrating strong power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces change talk and asks targeted questions that produce concrete planning and commitment language. To improve, the clinician could more consistently amplify change talk and use importance/confidence scaling or explicit commitment prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes slips but underuses strategies to reduce sustain talk; missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and gentle discrepancy. More targeted reframing and exploration of minimization (e.g., 'he doesn't have asthma') would strengthen this area.
