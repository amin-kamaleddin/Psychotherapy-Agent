# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental, respectful regard and normalized Elena’s constraints, offering affirmations that acknowledged her efforts. Statements avoided blame and reinforced unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and perspective-taking captured both practical barriers and values, with repeated paraphrases and gentle follow-up questions. The clinician sustained effort to understand Elena’s viewpoint throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear focus on parental smoking and child exposure, steering the conversation toward concrete, behavior-specific goals. Momentum was maintained toward a time-bound action plan without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was co-constructed, with frequent solicitation of Elena’s input, summaries of her choices, and tailoring of plans to her constraints. Tools and suggestions were offered as options and decisions remained client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported Elena’s right to choose by offering options rather than directives and respecting her preferences (e.g., delaying pharmacotherapy). Questions about plans and confidence reinforced Elena’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used scaling and solicited self-generated solutions, orienting toward Elena’s motivations, but leaned toward pragmatic steps. More questions probing deeper values, long-term goals, or vivid reasons for change would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was framed as a collaborative exploration with shared decision-making and frequent invitations to choose among options. Elena’s preferences directly shaped the session’s next steps and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and shaped several pieces of change talk (scaling, asking what felt doable, choosing specific steps) and converted ambivalence into concrete actions. There were missed opportunities to deepen and amplify fleeting change-language about reasons, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and used redirecting and double-sided reflections that did not reinforce sustain talk. More explicit strategic reflections naming both sustain and change sides could have further softened the pull to maintain smoking.
