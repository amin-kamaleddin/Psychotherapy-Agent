# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance with validating reflections and compassion, avoiding blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking about practical barriers and fatigue, but missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection on underlying fears or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence with specific, actionable strategies and a concrete follow-up, with no drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative and co-creative, inviting choices and obstacles, but the clinician often offered multiple clinician-led solutions rather than eliciting more of the patient's own brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's autonomy by offering options, affirming choice, and avoiding prescriptive language, reinforcing the patient's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some expressions of wanting to stay healthy but did little to draw out the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence and used few open questions to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative tone and co-created an implementation intention, yet relied somewhat more on clinician suggestions than on eliciting the patient's expertise about what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Encouraged change talk and secured agreement to try a plan, but did not consistently amplify, reflect, or explore the strength, importance, or confidence behind change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk with neutral, compassionate responses, but did not actively use strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or cost exploration) to soften sustain talk.
