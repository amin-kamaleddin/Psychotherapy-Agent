# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental—validates the patient, thanks them, and treats safety concerns seriously while avoiding labeling or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid empathic validation and concise reassurance, but reflections are generally brief and do not consistently explore deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains very clear, consistent focus on the medication switch and safety issues, providing concrete monitoring parameters and next steps throughout the encounter.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites information and some shared decision elements, but interaction is mostly clinician-led education with limited co-construction of a concrete plan or explicit role negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice by offering options and emphasizing that the prescriber decides, though some recommendations are somewhat prescriptive rather than explicitly eliciting the patient's preferred path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Primarily provides information and recommendations with minimal elicitation of the patient's internal motivations, values, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partnership cues (questions about doses, coordination) but often defaults to expert-driven guidance instead of collaboratively building a written stop/start timeline or monitoring plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does little to evoke or amplify change talk—asks practical questions but misses opportunities to elicit reasons, confidence, or personal motivations for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers alternatives and risk-reduction strategies, though could better use double-sided reflections to explicitly contrast concerns with values for safety.
