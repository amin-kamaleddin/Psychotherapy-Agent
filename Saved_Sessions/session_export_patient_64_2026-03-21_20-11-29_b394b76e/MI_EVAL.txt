# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and created a safe, nonjudgmental space (opening lines, validating language) that affirmed the client's ambivalence and decision to attend.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate summaries that captured ambivalence and validated concerns; could deepen exploration of emotional nuance (worry/guilt) and ask permission before offering information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the conversation tightly focused on vaccination throughout, with each intervention tied to barriers, motivations, or concrete planning leading toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner throughout, inviting input, offering options, and co-creating practical steps (drafting texts, choosing time), with offers of ongoing support.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported the client's choice multiple times, presenting options and avoiding pressure so the client retained ownership of decisions and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic motivation and used rulers and planning to bring out change talk, but occasionally provided information without elicit-permission-elicit and could probe values more deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the session as collaborative, sharing power by weighing pros/cons together and letting the client select and shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited, reinforced, and built on change talk via open questions, affirmations, scaling, and moving toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and neutrally reflected sustain talk and redirected to problem-solving, but could more strategically use double-sided reflections and selective emphasis to reduce sustain-talk momentum.
