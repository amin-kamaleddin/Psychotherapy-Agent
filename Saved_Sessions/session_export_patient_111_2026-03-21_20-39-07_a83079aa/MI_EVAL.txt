# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating the patient's perspective and avoiding blame or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, linking routine and relationship, but could probe emotional nuance (anger, fear, relational hurt) and more explicitly normalize resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking and skillfully moved from exploration to concrete planning, securing logistical details and a seven-day commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt clearly collaborative with joint problem-solving and mutual decision-making, adjusting plans to the patient's preferences (private plan, reminder wording).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized and respected the patient's right to choose, offered options without pressure, and honored his desire to keep the plan private.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's motivations, implementation intentions, and confidence, producing client-generated commitment; could further amplify and summarize his change language, especially relational reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that the patient’s expertise mattered and invited co-creation of solutions, though at times the clinician led detailed planning without explicitly asking if the patient wanted that role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and helped produce a specific seven-day gum trial, but could more frequently amplify and summarize his reasons for change to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk using neutral and small-change strategies, but could use more explicit double-sided reflections and softening summaries to further tip the balance toward change.
