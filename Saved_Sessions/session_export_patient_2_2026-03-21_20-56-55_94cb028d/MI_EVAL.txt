# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validated constraints, and praised realistic steps; tone was warm and respectful throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and paraphrases and offered a gentle breathing exercise with check‑ins, which deepened emotional attunement and helped the patient soften and engage.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on evening smoking reduction while honoring the patient’s boundary about alcohol, and guided the session logically from concerns to a specific plan and confidence rating.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Solicited the patient’s preferences and constraints, built on her suggestions, and co‑created a feasible implementation plan with frequent check‑ins and offers of help wording reminders.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, asked permission before offering options, avoided pressuring language, and invited the patient to choose and refine her own plan, supporting ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivations (worry about cancer, desire to be present for son) and asked confidence‑building questions, but could deepen evocation by linking values more explicitly and eliciting past successes or a client summary of reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used collaborative language and genuine power‑sharing—asking permission, offering choices, and incorporating the patient’s ideas into the plan—demonstrating an equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (e.g., cutting to three, breathing, alarm) and raised readiness/confidence, though there were missed opportunities to more strongly reflect/amplify change language and ask a direct commitment question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and respected the patient’s boundary about alcohol while redirecting to change strategies, but could more often use double‑sided reflections or reframes to gently pivot ambivalence.
