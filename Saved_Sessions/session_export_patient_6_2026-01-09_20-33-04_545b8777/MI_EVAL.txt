# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, opening with support, checking safety, and validating concerns about soccer and scholarship. Avoided shaming and affirmed the patient's choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking, normalizing fears and inviting the patient's view. Missed some deeper affect-focused reflections that could have explored anxiety about parental consequences and future goals more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on adolescent alcohol use and concrete harm-reduction strategies tied to the patient's goals. Stayed targeted on assessment, refusal scripts, exit plans, and avoiding unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort by co-creating refusal lines, texts, and role-play, and incorporating the patient's wording changes. Checked in and adapted suggestions to the patient's preferences throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options and asking what felt most helpful rather than directing. Explicitly avoided forcing parental involvement and reinforced ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation by linking risky behavior to personally meaningful goals and prompting planning and practice. Could increase use of open questions, scaling, and summary reflections to elicit and amplify the patient's own reasons and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making and power-sharing by asking preferences, offering choices, and integrating the patient's suggestions. Advice was given with permission and in a collaborative frame.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change-relevant statements with planning and praise for realistic steps. Could more consistently use reflections and open questions specifically designed to amplify desire, ability, reasons, and need language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not exacerbate sustain talk and provided risk information, but normalized experimentation in ways that risked validating minimizations. Would benefit from double-sided or selective reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and tip it toward change.
