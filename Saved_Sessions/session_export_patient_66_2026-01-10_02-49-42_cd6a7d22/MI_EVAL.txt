# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the patient's feelings and reflecting rather than criticizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate understanding through clear reflections and validation, tailoring responses to the patient's concerns about taste, jitteriness, and sleep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation/harm reduction and workplace break alternatives, with concrete steps and a follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a team approach by inviting choices, framing interventions as experiments, and repeatedly checking in about preferences and reassessment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms the patient's choice and control by offering a menu of options, asking permission for trials, and emphasizing the ability to stop if needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's own motivations and willingness to experiment by asking them to choose options and shaping the plan around their concerns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fosters shared decision-making, but frequent clinician-driven technical guidance given without explicit permission somewhat reduces the sense of full patient-led choice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces language in favor of change, affirms small commitments, and uses short follow-up invitations to encourage trials and monitoring.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and offers harm-reduction alternatives, but missed opportunities for strategic double-sided reflections and more explicit exploration of ambivalence.
