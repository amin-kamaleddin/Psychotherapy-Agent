# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing and validating the patient's experience throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows accurate, timely reflections and acknowledges the patient's anxiety and commitment, but could deepen affect with more complex, emotion-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the bedtime routine and co-parenting goal, repeatedly tying strategies back to the child's needs.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—invites input, adapts wording per the patient, and co-creates practical implementation steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reliably supports choice and control by offering options, asking which feels right, and accommodating the patient's preferences without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation by asking which options feel doable and linking actions to values, but too often supplies scripts instead of drawing out the patient's own reasons, goals, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invites the patient's preferences, though the clinician sometimes leads with directive scripts rather than co-creating language first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk by offering choices and focusing on a specific behavior, but misses opportunities for evocative open questions and scaling to amplify DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk, normalizes concerns, and redirects to practical problem-solving, though could strengthen use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.
