# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, explicitly affirming and respectful throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Rosa’s ambivalence and specific fears and were followed by supportive statements; the clinician could have probed a bit deeper into emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained clearly focused on smoking cessation and related barriers, with each major turn linked to treatment decision-making or concrete quit-day planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative—soliciting preferences and tailoring suggestions—but at times the clinician offered directive recommendations before eliciting Rosa’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered options, framed recommendations as suggestions, and invited Rosa to choose, consistently supporting her sense of control and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some behavioral commitments were elicited, but the clinician relied more on information-giving and planning than on open, evocative questions to draw out Rosa’s internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as collaborative, co-constructed practical steps with Rosa, and treated her as the expert on her life and constraints.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change language (quit date, checklist) and offered concrete steps, but relied more on offering solutions than on deeper evocation to amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized with practical options offered, but the clinician mostly used informational reassurance rather than MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to actively defuse sustain talk.
