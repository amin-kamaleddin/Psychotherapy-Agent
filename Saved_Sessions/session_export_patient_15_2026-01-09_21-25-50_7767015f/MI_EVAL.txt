# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing concerns and offering practical help rather than blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening with validation and acknowledgements, but provides few deeper reflective statements to probe emotional meaning or the patient’s perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on hypertension management with a concrete 7‑day home BP plan, measurement technique, safety thresholds, and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—co‑creates supports (logs, alarms, doctor message) and repeatedly solicits the patient’s preferences to shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering multiple options, asking which feels most doable, and soliciting permission before providing tools or templates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits preferences and confidence but rarely probes the patient’s own reasons, values, or personally meaningful motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters strong partnership and shared decision‑making, deferring to the patient’s choices and treating them as central to the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk (choice to track, confidence rating) but misses opportunities to deepen or amplify reasons, benefits, and links to past successes or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk about alcohol but does not actively soften it through reflections, double‑sided summaries, or exploring the patient’s rationale.
