# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Marcus’s struggle, affirming his priorities without judgment. Warm, respectful language supported a nonjudgmental stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing goals and practical concerns. Reflections were sometimes formulaic and could deepen by naming underlying affect more often.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on a concrete, achievable behavior (a brief call about affordable meds) and avoided drifting to unrelated topics. Progressed toward a specific first step consistent with Marcus’s priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as an equal partnership, co-creating scripts and options based on Marcus’s input. Repeatedly asked for his preferences and tailored support accordingly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and offered multiple options rather than directives, honoring Marcus’s decisions (e.g., keeping the dog). Questions about which option feels doable reinforced his ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Marcus’s own reasons for change and concrete motivators (breathing, family, dog) and drew out practical contingencies. Could more systematically use evocation tools (rulers, discrepancy, 'why now') to strengthen intrinsic motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited shared decision-making and collaborated on the plan (drafting scripts, role-play options). The clinician positioned Marcus as an active partner in selecting next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk about using cheaper inhalers and reduced ER visits, and reinforced specific benefits. Missed opportunities to probe commitment and confidence or to scale importance/confidence toward a clear commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation, validated attachment to the dog and financial worries, and offered alternatives that reduced pressure. Could further soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections and selectively reframing concerns toward values.
