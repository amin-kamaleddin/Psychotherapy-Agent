# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental—affirms efforts, validates successes, endorses patient ideas, and avoids blame throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic reflections and perspective-taking, but could deepen emotional exploration (e.g., discouragement or pride) with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on reducing orange juice and moves logically to actionable steps (one-week trial, scripts, slip plan) that advance change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly team-based—offers role-play and options, repeatedly invites the patient to choose what will help, and co-creates the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's sense of choice by offering options, avoiding pressure, and asking which approach fits his comfort and preference.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies the patient's own motivation through open questions and by drawing on past successes and self-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as an equal collaborator, centers his expertise, and shares decision-making when constructing the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively draws out and strengthens change talk by asking for concrete steps, rehearsals, and commitments, leading to sustained change language and high confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward workable options (e.g., dilution, removing cartons), though could further soften ambivalence with double-sided reflections or exploring the behavior's function.
