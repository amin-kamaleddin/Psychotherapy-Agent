# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, validating small, realistic steps and avoiding moralizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with paraphrases and summaries that capture both content and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing school-night marijuana use with specific, actionable goals and concrete strategies, avoiding drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner with the client to co-create the plan, using collaborative language and following the client's lead on specifics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's sense of choice and control by offering options, asking which feels best, and respecting preferences (e.g., no parents involved).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits personally relevant reasons for change and links them to outcomes, but could deepen intrinsic motivation and strengthen commitment (e.g., scaling or 'why now').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and client-led, repeatedly asking the client to choose among options and sharing power in planning and wording decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk about clearer mornings and saving money and reflects these statements, though could more deliberately amplify and summarize to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without confrontation and pivots to experiments and alternatives, but could more explicitly use double-sided reflections or reframing to further weaken sustain language.
