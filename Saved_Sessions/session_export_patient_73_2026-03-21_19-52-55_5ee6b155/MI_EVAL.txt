# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, legitimizing slips and validating fears about lungs and finances. Warmth and acceptance were evident throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good perspective-taking, normalizing ambivalence and restating key concerns about health, money, and triggers. Deeper emotional reflections (e.g., frustration or fear of failure) were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with concrete steps, a timeline, and relapse planning. Each exchange stayed tied to the target behavior and practical action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint problem-solving, co-constructed a feasible reduction plan, and invited patient input and roleplay. The clinician’s tone supported equal partnership and mutual contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and framing slips as information rather than failure. Advice was presented as optional and patient preferences were checked repeatedly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations and past helpful strategies and drew out change language about health and money. Could have used more open questions and selective reinforcement to deepen internal motives and outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered a collaborative stance with choices and invitations, and the plan reflected the patient’s decisions. Frequent directive lists and detailed instructions sometimes shifted control back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created opportunities for and reinforced change talk, praising the plan and eliciting specific commitment language. Missed opportunities to amplify, summarize, and probe importance and confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and promptly offered trigger-specific coping strategies and plans. Relied more on informational/directive approaches rather than MI-specific double-sided reflections to gently shift sustain talk.
