# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing Isabelle's experience and acknowledging her life constraints before offering suggestions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good, generally accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing patterns and consequences; could strengthen empathy with more affect-focused reflections to explore emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on adolescent alcohol use and risk reduction, repeatedly returning to behaviorally specific elements and translating discussion into practical, time‑bound strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited Isabelle to choose among options, co-wrote texts, offered rehearsal, and accepted her limits, resulting in co-created decisions and plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by asking permission, offering options, normalizing uncertainty, and emphasizing Isabelle's choice about which strategies to use.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Isabelle's own motivations and plan using open questions, confidence rulers, and layered options, leading to patient-generated change talk and a concrete plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked in genuine partnership—asked permission, framed options collaboratively, and co-constructed texts and a stepwise plan with Isabelle as the expert on her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk with evocative questions, reflections, and commitment language, producing concrete commitment statements and a specific weekend plan.
