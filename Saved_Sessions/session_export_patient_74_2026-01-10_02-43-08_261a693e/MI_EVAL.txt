# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, affirmed feelings and choices, normalized slips and avoided blame to convey unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening, accurately summarizing ambivalence; reflections were often brief and task-focused, missing opportunities to deepen emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking behavior with practical harm-reduction strategies and a specific one-week goal, repeatedly returning to concrete implementation and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with co-created decisions, clinician solicited input at each step and adapted suggestions to the patient's context and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering multiple options, respecting the patient's decision not to quit, and framing slips as opportunities to reset rather than moral failures.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation via a week-long plan and planned check-in, but shifted quickly to solutions and did not systematically draw out or amplify the patient's own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the interaction as a partnership, using inclusive language, offering choices, and co-constructing a simple checklist and joint check-in to share power and responsibility.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-oriented statements through harm-reduction choices and the checklist, but did not consistently amplify or deepen desire/reason/need/confidence language or seek commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledged the appeal of smoking with neutral acceptance, but rarely used double-sided reflections or strategic techniques to actively soften sustain talk.
