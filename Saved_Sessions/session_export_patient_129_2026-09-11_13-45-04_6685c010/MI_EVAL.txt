# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, reflecting Tyler’s priorities without criticism. Warmth and affirmation of practical steps (alarms, stashing) avoided blaming or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with complex reflections and summaries that tied together Tyler’s goals and worries. Reflections matched Tyler’s language and affect, keeping him understood and engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing home exposure and harm-reduction planning, progressing to concrete, time-limited steps. Questions repeatedly guided toward actionable plans (alarms, stash, locker).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint exploration, repeatedly soliciting and building on Tyler’s ideas to co-construct the plan. Tyler’s input clearly shaped session content and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Tyler’s freedom to choose and avoided coercive language, accepting his stance of ‘not quitting.’ Questions emphasized Tyler’s judgment and let him pick the steps to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical solutions and past successes, eliciting self-efficacy and supports like Sam and Jake. Could deepen evocation by eliciting more intrinsic motivations and personal values beyond external drivers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated Tyler as the primary decision-maker and expert on his life, offering choices and asking which options felt doable. The clinician’s stance communicated genuine power-sharing throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited concrete, change-oriented language and small-step planning, producing actionable change talk (stashing, alarms). Could more strongly amplify and reinforce emergent commitment language (e.g., confidence rating or explicit commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and skillfully redirected sustain talk toward harm-reduction alternatives, respecting Tyler’s ‘not quitting’ stance. At times echoed sustain wording rather than using double-sided reflections to gently tip the balance toward change.
