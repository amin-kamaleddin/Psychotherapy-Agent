# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and respect, avoided blaming language, and affirmed the patient's stance repeatedly. Used empathetic statements and praise to validate efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displayed clear understanding and validation of the patient's concerns and offered tailored, supportive options. However, deeper reflective listening about the patient's minimization and underlying worry was limited as the clinician often moved quickly into instruction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a focused, behaviorally specific agenda on mini‑cigar use and throat/gum irritation throughout the session. Provided concrete, measurable steps and progressive action planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient input on goals, rehearsed scripts together, and adapted recommendations to preferences, fostering cooperation. At times the clinician supplied substantial expert advice, tilting the interaction toward clinician‑led guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the patient's right to choose by offering options, asking permission, and normalizing slips as data. Reinforced the patient's control over next steps without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some commitment language and concrete actions, but much motivation was scaffolded by clinician suggestions rather than drawn from the patient's own values. Few open questions targeted intrinsic reasons for change beyond symptom relief.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative relationship by asking permission, offering choices, and co‑creating plans and scripts. The clinician occasionally moved into expert/advice mode, slightly reducing equal power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced incremental change talk and used permission to offer concrete tracking and goals, which led to patient commitment. Less exploration was done of the patient's own deeper reasons, confidence, and importance for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and offered practical alternatives, which is positive, but used few active strategies to reframe or explicitly soften sustain talk (e.g., double‑sided reflections). The approach leaned toward instruction rather than strategically addressing ambivalence.
