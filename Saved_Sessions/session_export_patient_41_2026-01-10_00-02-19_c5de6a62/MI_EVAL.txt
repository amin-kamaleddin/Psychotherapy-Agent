# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing the patient's experience and affirming effort; language conveyed warm acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and validation that showed understanding of anxiety and avoidance, but did not probe deeply into underlying emotions or their meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on starting brief practice and moved cleanly from assessment to concrete action planning with implementation intentions and backups.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jointly developed scripts and invited role-play and edits, though the interaction included many clinician-driven solutions that slightly reduced shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, asked permission, and invited the patient's edits, preserving control and offering alternatives to support self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some action-oriented responses but relied more on logistical strategies than drawing out the patient’s intrinsic motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance by soliciting preferences and co-creating plans, though at times the clinician took a more directive, expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced and praised change-oriented statements and steps taken, but missed opportunities to elicit stronger, specific desire, reasons, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and reframed toward small doable steps without debating, but could more strategically use double-sided reflections to highlight the change side.
