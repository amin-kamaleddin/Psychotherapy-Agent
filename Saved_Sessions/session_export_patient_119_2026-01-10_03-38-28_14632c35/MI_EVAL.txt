# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language, validated Selene’s concerns, and offered multiple affirmations that conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were frequent empathic statements and practical reflections, but reflections stayed at the behavioral level and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions like guilt or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence with concrete, measurable planning (alarm time, 5/7 goal, Jan 24 check‑in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited Selene’s preferences and co‑created the plan and tracking, though at times the interaction tilted toward clinician-led solution delivery rather than purely client-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were offered and Selene was invited to pick strategies and goals, but occasional prescriptive language (e.g., 'commit to this') reduced the sense of full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some change‑oriented questions that elicited commitment (alarm time, goal), but the clinician more often offered solutions instead of deeply drawing out Selene’s motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally positioned Selene as a collaborator and repeatedly solicited her input, although frequent solution lists placed the clinician more in an expert role at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change talk and a measurable goal, but often moved quickly into problem‑solving instead of systematically eliciting, amplifying, and reinforcing Selene’s own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and lapses normalized with practical counters, but MI‑specific softening (double‑sided reflections, validating reasons while gently tipping toward change) was used inconsistently.
