# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, validates limits, and avoids blame (e.g., honoring requests for brevity and normalizing reluctance). This warm, nonjudgmental stance supports client comfort and engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflective listening captures both content and feeling, mirroring Maria’s frustration and practical constraints. Summaries and reflections are well‑attuned to her emotional and pragmatic concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on inhaler adherence and moves steadily from options to a concrete plan (checklist + home‑screen widget). Minimal topic drift and purposeful pacing advance toward a doable action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is highly collaborative, with the clinician inviting Maria to co‑write wording, choose placement, and make decisions together. The clinician positions Maria’s knowledge as central and shares control of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers choices, checks preferences, and accepts a tentative commitment without pressure. This respect for the client’s control and limits bolsters autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and reasons for change are drawn from Maria (values of control for family) and converted into specific intentions (pin checklist, try for a week). The clinician elicits and operationalizes internal motivation rather than imposing solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Partnership is explicitly fostered through joint problem‑solving language and shared drafting of the checklist. The clinician defers to Maria’s preferences and frames decisions as mutual.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions, offering small doable options, and co‑creating the checklist repeatedly elicit and amplify change language (e.g., 'try it for a week'). The clinician builds tentative commitments into a concrete implementation plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward practical solutions (checklist, demo, one‑page info), which reduces resistance. To reach top score, more double‑sided reflections or explicit normalization of ambivalence would further soften sustain talk.
