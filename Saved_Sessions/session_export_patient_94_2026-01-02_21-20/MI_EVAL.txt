# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
- [x] Nonjudgmental opening (acknowledges hurry/worry)
- [x] Avoids moralizing language
- [x] Uses polite closings (“Take care”)

#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician opens briefly and nonjudgmentally and avoids moralizing; tone is transactional and directive at times. To improve: add multiple explicit, individualized acceptance statements and 2+ affirmations of coping/strengths.

---

### Empathy
- [x] Brief reflective listening (“I hear the hurry and the worry”)
- [x] Answers patient questions in a manner that demonstrates understanding (timelines for inhaled steroids)

#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflective listening and clear answers, but reflections are shallow and infrequent. Increase number of reflections (4–6) including 2 complex reflections that capture ambivalence/emotion.

---

### Direction
- [x] Maintains clear focus on target behavior (inhaler/controller use and safety)
- [x] Provides practical, actionable steps and safety guidance

#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent clinical direction—kept scope, offered concrete steps, scripts, and monitoring thresholds. Continue to pair this clarity with more evocation/empathy.

---

### Collaboration
- [x] Offers options and checks patient preference (“Want a one‑week, time‑efficient plan to try?”)
- [x] Supplies language/scripts the patient can use with prescriber

#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some invitations and offerings present, but interaction remains clinician‑directed with ready-made solutions. To improve: invite co‑creation (ask patient priorities, brainstorm together) at least 2–3 times.

---

### Autonomy Support
- [x] Respects patient choice repeatedly (accepts “not promising I’ll start anything”)
- [x] Presents options and describes stopping/monitoring procedures

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong respect for autonomy and options given; tone sometimes closing rather than invitational. To reach excellence: add explicit autonomy affirmations in multiple places and routinely ask permission before giving advice.

---

### Evocation
- [x] Elicits small commitments (agrees to 1‑minute breathing; use of one‑sentence script)

#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal evocation—commitments were responses to offers rather than elicited motivations. Need multiple open questions to draw out DARN (desire/reason/ability/need) and follow-up reflections to amplify change talk.

---

### Partnership
- [x] Offers to draft lines and asks whether patient wants a plan (some collaborative moves)

#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership behaviors but clinician often provides ready-made solutions and closes. Increase explicit co‑creation (1–2 jointly designed steps) and check feasibility with the patient.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
- [x] Captured small change signals (agreement to try breathing exercise and use script)

#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Missed opportunities to amplify patient’s implicit change talk (researching options, attending appointment). Should reflect and elicit reasons/importance/confidence (scaling questions) and reinforce any DARN statements.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
- [x] Responds to sustain talk with balanced information and clear risk descriptions (risks of rescue-only use; rarity of systemic effects at low dose)

#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician counters sustain talk with facts effectively but relies on information rather than MI techniques (double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance). To improve: use double-sided reflections and summaries that legitimize concerns while steering toward change.
