# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating (e.g., “I’m here to listen and support you,” “Thank you — that’s a lot to hold”), treating disclosures as material to work with rather than evidence of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and accurate, concise reflections that acknowledged emotional burden, though there were limited deeper, affect‑focused or complex reflections to further deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors (reducing alcohol and improving contraceptive reliability) and moved the session logically toward concrete, actionable next steps like LARC, scripts, and role‑play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co‑constructed with repeated invitations for the patient to choose actions and the clinician adapting to the patient’s requests, demonstrating equal‑partner teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently supported the patient’s choice by offering options, asking permission, and refraining from pressure, which fostered a strong sense of ownership over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation (clarified importance/confidence and reasons) but often moved quickly to offering information and solutions instead of drawing out and amplifying the patient’s own motivations and strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as collaborative—offering to co‑draft questions, role‑play, and repeatedly asking which step the patient wanted to start with.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and supported small, concrete steps (e.g., choosing to talk to a provider), but could have spent more time eliciting personal reasons, values, and confidence language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and reoriented discussion toward harm‑reduction strategies, though explicit double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing could have further softened ambivalence.
