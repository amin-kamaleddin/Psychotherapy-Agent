# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizes Mateo's reactions, and checks safety nonjudgmentally; no stigmatizing or minimizing language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening and accurate reflections (e.g., 'That sounds exhausting'), but reflections are mostly brief and practical rather than deeper emotional or meaning‑focused explorations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on increasing manageable activity, tying questions and suggestions back to the target behavior and moving toward a concrete action (2‑minute breathing).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone is collaborative—asks permission and invites input—but frequently offers ready‑made solutions and specific directives that tilt the balance toward clinician expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering choices, framing options rather than mandates, and providing explicit stop rules so Mateo retains control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment to action (choice of small win, plan to report back) but relies more on providing strategies than drawing out Mateo's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership through permission‑seeking and soliciting preferences, yet the interaction remains somewhat clinician‑led rather than fully co‑constructed with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits actionable change talk (agreement to try breathing), but misses opportunities to deepen and amplify reasons for change or use importance/confidence scaling and reflective amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk without shaming, but rarely uses double‑sided reflections or selectively amplifies change talk to integrate ambivalence more effectively.
