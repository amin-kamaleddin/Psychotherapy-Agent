### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental regard (e.g., “I appreciate you being honest,” acknowledging priorities: soccer/college/parents). Missed opportunity to deepen acceptance with explicit normalizing statements about ambivalence or peer pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate understanding demonstrated via reflections and summaries (“It makes sense you’d hesitate…”, mirrored hesitation). Could deepen by reflecting underlying feelings (e.g., anxiety about being seen as boring, unease when alone) and explicitly naming doubt when Anya said “not totally convinced.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained tightly focused on alcohol/party safety; clinician elicited concrete strategies, used a confidence ruler, and guided toward a specific plan (practice excuse → bring drink → safety text). Clear progressive narrowing to action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed conversation as joint (asked permission, invited choices, offered rehearsal). At times slightly directive when providing exact wording; could increase collaboration by inviting Anya’s own phrasing first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong support for client choice and agency (permission, “If you want, we can practice,” accepting ambivalence). Clinician consistently respected Anya’s decisions and allowed her to choose the next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Anya’s reasons/resources for change (priorities, strategies) and confidence ratings. Could more often amplify and build on expressed change talk and probe importance/consequences (e.g., link plan to soccer/college goals).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative tone and co-constructed a plan. To strengthen partnership, invite more of the client’s language for lines/plans and prioritize her wording to enhance ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk and intention (commitment to try the practice excuse, bring a drink; confidence rose 6→7). Could better consolidate change talk by reflecting/amplifying intentions and probing for a clearer commitment (who/when/what exactly).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward solutions; acknowledged ambivalence. Could use more double-sided reflections linking sustain and change talk to subtly tip balance toward change (e.g., “On one hand… on the other hand…”).
