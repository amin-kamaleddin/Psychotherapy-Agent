# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly communicated nonjudgmental acceptance and validated the patient’s stance (e.g., “I accept that position,” paraphrases of skepticism). Normalized frustration and accepted “not promising more,” demonstrating consistent positive regard across multiple turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of content and affect (noting shrug, “not promising more,” feeling controlled). Reflections were consistent and accurate but often surface-level paraphrases rather than deeper, complex reflections that explore underlying emotion or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a tight, appropriate focus on engagement with mandated activities and moved the conversation progressively toward a concrete, testable plan (small change → specific one‑on‑one → employer outreach → who calls → trust-building detail).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient input repeatedly and co-constructed details (which employers, who should call). Occasionally shifted to promising to “push” or “raise” the plan with the caseworker without explicit negotiation of roles, a small drift toward doing-for rather than fully doing-with.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected choice and accepted limits (did not coerce; based plan on what would make the patient willing). Missed opportunities for explicit autonomy-language (e.g., “it’s your choice”) and for asking permission before taking system-level actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited the patient’s own ideas and motivations (concrete changes, past success, values, what would build trust). Questions drew out specific, client-generated solutions rather than imposing reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed work as joint and asked for input on choices; fostered problem-solving with the patient. At times took a slightly directive tone when offering to “push” the caseworker, which could modestly reduce shared ownership of next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple instances of change talk and a behavioral commitment (willingness to attend two sessions if conditions met). Tended to reflect rather than amplify or explore confidence; missed opportunities for confidence rulers or strengthening commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and accepted sustain talk without arguing (accepted “not promising more”) and reframed ambivalence into a pragmatic test. Could more actively use double-sided reflections and summaries to explicitly juxtapose sustain and change language and further weaken resistance.
