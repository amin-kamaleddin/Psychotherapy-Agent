# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician showed consistent nonjudgmental, validating language and accepted Maya's ambivalence throughout, normalizing worries and avoiding blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking were evident, but empathic work stayed at practical/consequential levels rather than probing deeper emotions like shame or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on alcohol use and protecting academic/future goals, progressing logically from exploration to a specific, measurable plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction read as a genuine partnership with frequent solicitation of Maya's preferences, co‑construction of the plan, and checking in about how supports should be used.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently upheld Maya's autonomy by offering choices, asking permission for decisions, and inviting commitment without pressuring or directing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out Maya's own motivations and strategies—asking what she noticed, using a confidence ruler, eliciting slip plans, and encouraging rehearsal of her lines.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician deferred to Maya's expertise, negotiated supports and exit lines, and repeatedly framed decisions as collaborative, reinforcing equal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was actively elicited and strengthened through specific questions, planning prompts, confidence scaling, and securing a concrete start date and trial plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirected ambivalence, but relied mostly on neutral reflections rather than more tactical moves (e.g., double‑sided reflections or selectively amplifying pro‑change statements).
