# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained respectful, nonjudgmental tone and multiple explicit validations (“That makes sense…”, “You did well calming panic…”). At times language was brisk and directive; repeated formulaic closings reduced individualization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate understanding of fear of dependence and time pressure with reflective statements. Reflections were mostly surface-level; missed opportunities for deeper affective reflections and checking understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear agenda-setting, offered concise options and asked the patient to choose, taught a brief skill, and produced a concrete safety and follow-up plan. Directional focus and safety planning were strong and consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices (a/b/c), invited patient selection, and co-created a practical script and plan. Collaboration felt somewhat procedural at the end rather than fully exploratory.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected patient skepticism, provided options and choices, and avoided pressure. Could further strengthen by eliciting patient priorities before offering solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation via offering options and asking the patient to choose; however, clinician did not systematically elicit or amplify the patient's own reasons, values, or motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked alongside the patient to produce a usable script and monitoring plan and practiced breathing together. Interaction occasionally reverted to prescriptive language rather than sustained partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation and amplification of change talk; patient supplied most change-related language (agreeing to bring log/script and try breathing), but clinician missed chances to deepen or reflect those signals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and offered alternatives, but did not routinely use double-sided reflections or bracketed summaries to soften sustain talk; sustain statements were often met with practical problem-solving instead of strategic reflection.
