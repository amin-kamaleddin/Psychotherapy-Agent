# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and used affirmations ("That sounds like a sensible, manageable step — well done."). Consistent acknowledgement of Evelyn’s perspective and choices (inviting her to pick a day, noting follow-up). Felt somewhat formulaic and occasionally overshadowed by repeated recaps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured surface content ("It sounds like you enjoy the routine..."), demonstrating perspective-taking. Reflections remained summary/educational and shifted quickly to planning; missed opportunities to explore affect (surprise, worry) or deeper emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session kept tightly focused on alcohol reduction and safety. Used readiness ruler, moved directly to specific behavioral goals (one alcohol-free day, safety checks) and set a concrete follow-up plan ("we’ll review progress in a week"). Repeated recaps reinforced the plan until the patient committed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invited Evelyn to brainstorm and choose a day, showing collaborative planning. Collaboration felt somewhat constrained once clinician proposed specific strategies and then rehearsed them repeatedly, limiting further client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged choice and encouraged a gentle approach ("If you slip up... be gentle with yourself"). However, multiple prescriptive statements and repeated safety instructions reduced opportunities to strengthen client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (readiness ruler, brainstorming) and received commitment language ("I’ll try one extra alcohol-free day"). Did not deepen or probe motivations (no questions like "What makes that a 4?") or elicit broader values/reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elements of partnership present (inviting input, planning together, follow-up). Frequently shifted to an expert/procedural role with directive recaps, reducing shared control and opportunities for Evelyn to shape the plan further.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created conditions for some change talk and affirmed patient commitments. Limited strategic reinforcement or amplification of change talk and did not follow up to strengthen commitment or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence ("that ambivalence is normal") and avoided arguing with minimizations. Did not consistently use MI techniques (double-sided reflections, strategic reflections) to actively soften sustain talk; sustain talk ("seven drinks isn’t much") was largely bypassed by rapid movement to planning.
