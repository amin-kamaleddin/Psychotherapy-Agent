# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing the client's concerns and honoring preferences (e.g., leaving contact placeholders).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate summaries of logistical concerns, but could deepen affective reflections to name anxiety, guardedness, or reluctance more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, goal-focused direction toward completing community service with concrete schedules, exact hour calculations, and actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the session as a joint enterprise by checking preferences and offering to build the Sheet, yet frequently supplies ready-made solutions that make implementation somewhat clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports client choice through options, permission-seeking, and honoring declines (e.g., not forcing mom's number), reinforcing the client's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits necessary logistical details but lacks consistent exploration of internal motivation, values, or confidence (no scaling or evocative probes to elicit personal reasons).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites input and checks which options feel doable, although promptly moving into specific solutions can reduce the sense of fully co-creating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-oriented statements and agreements around scheduling, but relies on logistics rather than asking evocative questions to amplify desire, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and offers low-commitment planning when reluctance appears, but rarely uses targeted MI techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing) to more effectively diffuse sustain talk.
