# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing small wins and offering warm praise. Missed chances to explicitly normalize adolescent shame/family dynamics and to linger on the patient’s experience before moving to solutions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and validation, noticing subtle emotional shifts after the coping exercise. Reflections are often brief and could probe deeper feelings (shame, identity, loneliness) for greater depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on target behaviors and moves from problem identification to a concrete, time‑bound plan with follow-up. The session stays tightly oriented toward actionable steps (coping sequence, alarms, logging, rewards).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Feels like collaborative problem-solving with patient input, co-created plan elements, and feasibility checks. Could reduce directive statements and preface expertise with permission to further equalize power.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice by offering options and honoring the patient’s selection while normalizing trial-and-error. Occasional unsolicited detailed advice (e.g., physiological checks, tracking methods) without permission slightly weakens autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some patient-generated plans and commitments, but does not sufficiently draw out underlying motivations, values, or future-focused reasons for change. More evocative open questions and reflections would deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asks permission, offers choices, and implements patient decisions. At times shifts into an expert/educational stance without explicit permission, reducing the sense of shared power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms small wins and asks which option feels doable, which generated commitment to the coping plan. However, the clinician does not systematically elicit or amplify DARN‑C (desire, reasons, ability, need, commitment) to multiply change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward coping and experiments, but seldom uses strategies like double-sided reflections, exploring pros/cons, or summarizing ambivalence. More targeted reflective techniques would better soften sustain talk.
