# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys respect and nonjudgmental regard (e.g., normalizing context, validating caution). Avoids labeling and repeatedly affirms the patient's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and some reflective listening (validates priorities and safety concerns). Reflections are generally brief and informational rather than deeply capturing the emotional impact of uncertainty.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on safe medication coordination and concrete next steps. Minimal topic drift and frequent actionable guidance (washout timing, monitoring, prescriber message).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint effort, soliciting preferences and leaving control (attachments, final sign-off) to the patient. Processes and language reflect equitable partnership and shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and self-direction by offering options, asking permission, and promising patient review before sending. Balances necessary safety directives with preservation of patient decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's specific concerns, priorities, and desired next steps, which supports motivation to act safely. Could deepen evocation by probing underlying values and using scaling to assess strength of commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites the patient's input and collaborates on next steps (reviewing drafts, timing), while occasionally taking the lead to provide templates. Could further emphasize the patient's expertise about values and constraints.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports language toward safe action (offers drafting help, invites choices). Missed opportunities to amplify change talk and to probe confidence or elicit a concrete commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and does not reinforce hesitation, providing corrective information to reduce fear-driven sustain talk. Could more actively use double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence and more explicitly shift toward change language.
