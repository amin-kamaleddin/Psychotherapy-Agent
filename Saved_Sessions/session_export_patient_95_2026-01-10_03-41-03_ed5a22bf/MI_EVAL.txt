# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with repeated empathic validations, responsive to the patient's pleas to be heard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and emotional presence, accurately mirroring key themes (hopelessness, fear, sister memory) to keep the patient engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained focused direction on suicidal intent and access to means, asking concrete safety questions and securing specific behavioral steps (e.g., putting pills away, staying).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as a joint effort—offered to stay, invited small shared actions, and co-constructed safety steps—while necessary safety actions limited equal power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged choice and sought consent and provided options where possible, but appropriately set firm limits due to imminent danger which constrained autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivation (asked what the patient wanted, prompted the sister memory, secured a short commitment) but could have probed values and meaning more deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance by inviting input and co-producing safety strategies, while also asserting authority when immediate risk required action.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (sister memory, 'I will wait') and obtained a concrete short-term commitment, but missed opportunities to amplify and explore that language further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated despair appropriately without arguing but rarely used double-sided reflections or reframing to pivot sustain talk toward reasons for living, and some safety-focused statements may have tightened resistance.
