# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with frequent validations and warm, non‑blaming language, and respects the patient’s limits by supporting a small, realistic plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective‑taking that captures key concerns, though many reflections are formulaic and could be deepened to explore emotional nuance and guilt/fear more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on evening smoking reduction with measurable, short‑term goals and resists drifting into other topics, keeping the conversation goal‑directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as a joint project: the clinician solicits the patient’s ideas and past strategies and co‑constructs a feasible plan rather than delivering directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms choice and agency by offering options, asking what feels realistic, and accepting a self‑selected small change without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits values and links them to concrete behavior change, but could deepen evocation by probing confidence, barriers, and longer‑term motivation more explicitly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates genuine partnership by inviting the patient’s preferences, building on her suggestions (alarms, swaps, sister check‑ins), and sharing decision‑making power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk and links actions to valued outcomes, yet stops short of explicitly eliciting stronger commitment language or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and effectively redirects to actionable change, though could more actively use double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
