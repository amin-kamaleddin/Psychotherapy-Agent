# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm across the session (opening “I’m here to listen and support,” repeated thanks/appreciation). Multiple safety‑focused, nonblaming statements and respectful tone despite patient’s dismissive humor.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective‑taking and validation (“that sounds frustrating and confusing,” reflective summaries). Reflections are mainly informational; limited affective/deeper reflections to explore underlying feelings or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on medication adherence/BP control. Logical sequence: elicitation, agenda choice, non‑med options, concrete one‑week plan, safety planning, and arranged follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks permission/choice and co‑creates plan (patient chose option 2 and the “no added salt” goal). Multiple collaborative moves and shared agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and avoids coercion; provides options and cautions to check with prescriber. Slight deduction for offering directive education/steps without explicit permission at times.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to draw out reasons (initial questions about why they skip meds, choice prompts) but largely information‑seeking. Shifts to giving options/plan rather than deeper probes to elicit values, ambivalence, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates joint problem‑solving and values patient input (co‑created plan, invitations to review barriers). Slightly expert‑led at times with more directive instruction than purely patient‑driven ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete commitment (agreed to the no‑salt week, alarms, BP checks) and operationalized it with a step‑by‑step plan and follow‑up. Could more deliberately amplify expressed desire/reasons and assess confidence/importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes skipping (“Skipping meds is common”), redirects toward a trial and safety planning. Could further use double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
