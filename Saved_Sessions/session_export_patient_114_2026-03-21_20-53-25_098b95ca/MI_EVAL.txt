# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, respectful, nonjudgmental language and multiple validations reduced shame. Could deepen acceptance by normalizing common slip‑ups and offering more explicit affirmations of Ryan’s strengths earlier and more often.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective‑taking showed understanding of ambivalence and priorities. Could use more complex emotional reflections and occasionally mirror tentative language to deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on risky drinking and moved the session from exploration to concrete, time‑based commitments. Consistently returned to core goals and used scaling and a checklist to create momentum toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions were co‑constructed (choice of ride, sober friend, checklist) with offers to role‑play and reflect rather than instruct. Language and actions reinforced equality and mutual problem‑solving throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly stated that Ryan controlled pace and focus, asked permission before offering suggestions, and repeatedly invited him to choose among options. Framing advice as options consistently reinforced ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Ryan using values questions, scaling, and asking what would raise confidence, and role‑play increased commitment. The clinician focused on eliciting Ryan’s own solutions rather than supplying reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with invitations to choose and co‑create plans and role‑plays. At times the clinician presented a menu of options early instead of first eliciting Ryan’s own ideas, which slightly reduced the emphasis on client expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk using goal questions, a confidence scale, and targeted planning that produced client commitments. Missed opportunities to explicitly explore and amplify tentative spontaneous change language and to probe values to strengthen discrepancy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used role‑play and practical solutions to sidestep resistance. Could more frequently use double‑sided reflections and strategic reframing to explicitly acknowledge and weaken sustain talk.
