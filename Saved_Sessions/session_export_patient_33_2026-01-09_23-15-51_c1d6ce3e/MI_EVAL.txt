# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmation of safety goals and harm-reduction; could add more individualized normalization and explicit unconditional positive regard to better reflect ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic empathic tone and validation of safety concerns but contains few complex, affect-laden reflections; more frequent reflective statements naming emotions and ambivalence would strengthen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on safer heroin use and overdose prevention throughout and appropriately deferred buprenorphine when the patient was not ready.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and integrates patient preferences, but often shifts to an expert/instructional role with prescriptive checklists; more continuous solicitation of Diego’s input and co-creation would enhance collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and accepting the patient's decision about buprenorphine; advice was delivered as choices rather than mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation indirectly and the patient volunteers strong readiness, but lacks systematic use of open evocative questions, reflections, and scaling to draw out and deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites the patient to choose next steps and requests location to tailor resources, yet much of the encounter is clinician-led with directive guidance; greater co-creation and elicitation of the patient's ideas would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-oriented talk and offered options, but missed opportunities to amplify and explore Diego’s strong readiness (e.g., follow-up on his '10 out of 10'); the interaction often shifted to instruction rather than deepening commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to practical harm-reduction steps, but used few MI-specific techniques (like double-sided reflections) to acknowledge sustain talk while softening its influence.
