# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing ambivalence and fostering safety for disclosure; could vary affirmations more to feel individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with frequent, high-quality reflections and summaries that captured emotions and ambivalence, facilitating deeper client exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol reduction via a pragmatic sleep-focused target and progressed toward a concrete, measurable plan without losing client-centeredness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged as an equal partner throughout, co-constructing the plan and inviting correction and input so the session felt like a genuine team effort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and voluntariness, invited the client to select goals and respected hesitancy, which promoted ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew motivation and concrete strategies from the client herself, eliciting values, change ideas, and measurable outcomes rather than supplying solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited the client's priorities and choices and positioned her as the expert on her life, supporting shared decision-making throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited and reinforced desire and reasons for change and affirmed tentative commitments, but missed opportunities to deepen ability and commitment language (e.g., scaling confidence or eliciting a commitment statement).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected toward manageable alternatives which reduced sustain talk, yet used few double-sided reflections or strategic reframing of the benefits of drinking to tip ambivalence toward change.
