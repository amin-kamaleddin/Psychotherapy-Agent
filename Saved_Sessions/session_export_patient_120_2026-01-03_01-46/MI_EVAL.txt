# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and affirmations (“That takes courage,” “I’m proud of you”); normalizes difficulty. Missed deeper, individualized normalizing of guilt/rumination (e.g., explicitly validating that replaying nights is common).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and tracking of ambivalence and affect. Reflections tended to paraphrase content rather than probe deeper emotional nuance (shame/guilt intensity); could use more affect‑focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on target behavior (alcohol use in pregnancy). Guided to specific, measurable actions (NA drink, tell friend, sister on call, call OB) and secured a follow‑up — excellent directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative: asks open questions, invites input, co‑constructs plan. Occasionally slips into more directive/advisory moves (providing scripts, repeated reassurance) rather than exclusively eliciting client solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respects choice, elicits preferences and confidence, offers options and contingency plans. Some reassurance/praise (“You’re doing the responsible thing,” “Trust the choice”) borders on directive nudging and could be reframed to reinforce client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits change ideas and concrete steps (asks for one small step, confidence rating, role‑play). Would strengthen by eliciting more of Sofia’s own language for reasons to change and amplifying her change talk before supplying solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance maintained; clinician and client co‑created checklist and plan. Partnership is strong but occasionally reduced when clinician supplied wording/scripts instead of inviting Sofia to generate them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client change talk present (“I wanna be responsible,” concrete commitments). Clinician often provided wording and reassurance rather than eliciting, reflecting, and amplifying Sofia’s own motivators — missed opportunities to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist acknowledged ambivalence and fears, used reflections and planning to reduce sustain talk and increase commitment. Could further soften sustain talk by exploring and normalizing guilt/rumination more deeply before moving to action.
