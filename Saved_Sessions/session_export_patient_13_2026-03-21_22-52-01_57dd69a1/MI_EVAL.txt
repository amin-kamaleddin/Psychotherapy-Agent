# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance through empathic statements, normalization, and explicit acceptance of fear and possible backing out.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking by naming feelings, summarizing ambivalence, and checking understanding before moving forward.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior and persistently steers toward actionable options, narrowing these into a concrete 15‑minute phone‑call plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction with step‑by‑step co‑creation of the plan, soliciting preferences and adapting details to fit the patient's needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice by offering options, checking permission for supports, acknowledging limits, and allowing the patient to decline without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and practical reasons for change (care for child, preference for a small step) but could probe more for internal values, past successes, and commitment/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a shared, client‑directed process by inviting the patient's ideas, co‑creating the plan, and explicitly asking for wording to communicate to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports change talk with open questions and reinforcement, yet misses opportunities to amplify change language and to assess or deepen commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers pragmatic small‑step options and supports; could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections and elicit more client‑generated pros/cons to resolve ambivalence.
