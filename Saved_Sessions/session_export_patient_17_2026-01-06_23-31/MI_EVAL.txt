# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing relapse and affirming the patient's efforts and struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid empathic listening with accurate reflections and tailored responses about shame and motivation, but missed opportunities for deeper, affect‑laden reflections exploring the intensity of emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout and routinely returned to concrete, actionable steps like quit date, NRT choices, and coping plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—asked preferences, co‑created plans, and checked in frequently—though substantial informational delivery at times shifted the dynamic toward clinician expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice and control by offering options, respecting hesitation about a quit date, and emphasizing self‑compassion and responsibility.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited change reasons, past attempts, and 0–10 ratings and encouraged small commitments, but leaned toward offering solutions rather than systematically drawing out intrinsic motivations and strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner by inviting input and tailoring plans, but occasionally moved quickly to recommendations instead of fully eliciting the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced multiple strands of change talk (importance, reasons, past successes) and offered choices, yet could have more actively amplified and deepened desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and normalized relapse and shame, but used few strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk before moving to advice.
