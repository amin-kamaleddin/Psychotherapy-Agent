# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, affirming efforts and normalizing struggle throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and acknowledgment of emotions and conflicts, though some reflections could probe deeper into nuanced feelings like guilt, pride, and the meaning of ‘freedom.’

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, focused agenda on safety, alcohol risk, and engagement with supports, moving efficiently from assessment to a concrete 24‑hour plan and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively—inviting preferences, tailoring messages, and incorporating edits—but occasionally leaned toward expert-driven scripting rather than fully eliciting alternatives from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported client choice by offering options, asking permission, validating dignity concerns, and framing plans as voluntary rather than prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of goals and linking actions to motivation for the children, but the session relied heavily on clinician-driven plans rather than deeper elicitation of the client's own motivations and strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the work as collaborative with joint planning and choices, yet at times shifted into a more directive expert role which limited fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforced and translated change talk into specific commitments and tasks, but could have used more open questions to elicit additional personalized reasons and confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed ambivalence into workable options (e.g., time‑limited trials), though more strategic double‑sided or selectively amplified reflections could further soften sustain talk.
