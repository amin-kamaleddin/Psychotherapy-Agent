# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, with frequent statements like “I won’t try to fix you.” The clinician avoids blame or arguing and repeatedly affirms the patient's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a warm, validating presence and acknowledges the patient's discomfort, offering to sit quietly rather than interrogate. Could deepen emotional attunement with one or two more complex reflections of underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on engagement and safety, offering grounding options and explicit safety instructions while not drifting into unrelated topics. The agenda stays appropriately narrow for a closed, resistant client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint process by offering choices and checking preferences, preserving partnership despite the patient's refusal to engage. The encounter is necessarily lopsided but language supports shared decision-making where possible.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy through repeated offers of choice and explicit respect for the patient's decision not to speak. Avoids pressure or coercive language and consistently honors patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes modest attempts to elicit input with an initial open invitation and choices, but largely moves to silence after refusal and misses low-intensity curiosity prompts. More brief, nonpressuring elicitation could have been tried to draw out minimal change-related speech.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership by deferring to the patient's preferences and offering collaborative options, positioning the patient as decision-maker. Could strengthen this by explicitly acknowledging the patient as the expert on their life or inviting very small input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to evoke change talk beyond the initial open invitation, and missed several low-intensity opportunities to elicit desire, ability, or concern. While caution was appropriate, gentle curiosity or brief prompts could have invited small seeds of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk by honoring choice and reflecting the patient's stance, and by offering presence rather than confrontation. Could enhance this further with occasional double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.
