# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opened with an explicit nonjudgmental stance and used frequent affirmations, acknowledging the client's feelings and progress while avoiding labeling or criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections often captured pride and worry accurately, but the clinician moved quickly into advice and instructions, limiting deeper emotional exploration at times.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on protecting evenings with all interventions tied to that target and concrete steps for a one-week trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as joint problem‑solving with role‑play and input requests, but the clinician supplied substantial scripted content and logistics that reduced sole client-driven planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently offered options, asked the client's preferences before acting, and reinforced that the client controlled the plan and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited values and reasons for change and set a trial, but often provided solutions rather than using open questions to draw out the client's own importance/confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—sharing planning responsibility and inviting input—but frequent ready-made plans and offers to handle tasks shifted some expertise to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language and set measurable steps, though there were missed opportunities to amplify values-based reasons and deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged worries and reframed toward actionable solutions, avoiding reinforcement of sustain talk, though at times responses were more directive than double-sided reflections.
