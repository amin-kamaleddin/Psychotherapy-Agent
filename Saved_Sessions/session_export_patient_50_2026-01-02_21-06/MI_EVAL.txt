# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently demonstrates nonjudgmental, supportive regard — opens with “This is a safe, confidential space,” offers explicit praise (“that was brave to share”), normalizes anxiety, and closes repeatedly with affirming statements (“I’m proud of you,” “Be gentle with yourself”).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate empathic reflections (names tension, sleep disruption, avoidance) and picks up on the recorded‑presentation success; reflections are generally surface‑level and clinician moves quickly to planning rather than deeper affective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong focus on the target behavior (exam preparation). Provides a clear, concrete, time‑specific plan (20‑minute study, timer, grounding breath, 5‑minute rule, schedule tonight at 6:30) and repeatedly narrows to actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (offered a menu of options; asked which felt doable), but interaction becomes directive and repetitive with clinician largely issuing instructions rather than co‑constructing the plan with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and invites selection of steps; emphasizes nonjudgmental noticing if patient stops. At times leans toward persuasive encouragement (“That 20‑minute step is perfect,” “I’m proud of you”), which modestly reduces pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits key information (exam date, worst‑case thought) and asks which option feels doable, but clinician shifts quickly to recommending solutions rather than deeply eliciting the patient’s own motivations, values, or alternative strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership is present in invitations to choose and follow‑up request to report back, but repeated directive language and reiteration of the clinician’s plan limit a sense of equal co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician identifies and reflects change‑oriented statements (patient’s recorded presentation, “I think I’m making a first step”) but does not consistently amplify or elicit further change talk (could more actively reinforce and summarize patient’s expressed desires/confidence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledges and normalizes avoidance and worry, offers nonjudgmental strategies (jot why if you stop), and provides manageable exposures and coping techniques that address sustain talk without confronting or arguing.
