# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing the client's feelings and offering help rather than blame. Acceptance was expressed throughout the session and removed pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client's experience and emotional state, and the clinician matched the patient's guarded style. A brief grounding exercise further showed attunement and support.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on completing court-ordered community service with concrete logistics and a stepwise plan. Interventions consistently tied back to the target behavior and timeline.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician proposed options but deferred to the client's choices, co-drafting messages and negotiating the plan together. Inclusive language and consent at key moments made the interaction highly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple options were offered, permission was asked before adding contact details, and the clinician respected the client's preference for texting. Decisions were framed to preserve the client's control without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited doable steps and a confidence rating and reduced barriers with templates and grounding, which supported motivation. However, deeper exploration of intrinsic reasons, values, or longer-term benefits was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as a true partnership with shared decision-making and co-creation of the plan. The clinician repeatedly sought the client's input and tailored actions to his preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some pragmatic elicitation (choice questions, confidence rating) prompted action-oriented responses but change talk was not systematically elicited or amplified. The clinician missed opportunities to probe personal importance or reflect emerging pro-change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized anxiety, and redirected avoidance into small, manageable steps. Opportunities to use double-sided reflections or more explicit acknowledgment of both sides were not fully utilized.
