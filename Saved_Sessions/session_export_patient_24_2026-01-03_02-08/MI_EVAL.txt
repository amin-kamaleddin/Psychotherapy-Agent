# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly states acceptance multiple times and consistently validates the patient's priorities (running, unwinding). Language is nonjudgmental and normalizing throughout, preserving rapport and engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections (e.g., “skeptical but curious,” “waking up ‘actually rested’ is the deal‑breaker”) and perspective‑taking. Missed opportunities to probe deeper affect (emotional function of “unwind”) for richer change talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conversation stays focused on alcohol use linked to marathon goals. Progresses logically from awareness → benefits/barriers → past success → concrete experiment and tracking plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician invites patient input at each step (“Which… feels most acceptable?”, “What’s the simplest way to track?”). Plan is co‑constructed and fits the patient’s routine and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Emphasizes reversibility and personal choice (framing plan as an experiment). Avoids pressure; repeatedly reinforces patient control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits patient’s own reasons for change (sleep, recovery, pace) and draws on past success (foam‑rolling) to build self‑efficacy. Questions and reflections pull change talk rather than supplying it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as a joint experiment, solicits preferred tracking method, and leverages the patient’s expertise about what fits his routine. Clinician’s stance is consistently “with” the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits multiple change statements and a concrete, time‑limited experiment. Missed chance to explicitly scale commitment/confidence or secure a stronger verbal commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses double‑sided reflections and validation to acknowledge sustain talk without reinforcing it. Reframes ambivalence into a pragmatic, reversible test that reduces resistance.
