# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful—validated worries, normalized slips, and honored choices (e.g., decline of NRT and not discussing drinking). These behaviors communicated unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections that helped the patient soften, but empathy could have been deeper and more frequent. The clinician moved relatively quickly to strategies rather than exploring the emotional texture of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on evening smoking with a specific, measurable short-term goal, concrete strategies, a log format, and a scheduled follow-up. The session stayed strategically directed toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a shared endeavor—solicited follow-up time, adapted the log to patient preference, and co-constructed cue phrases and strategies. Decision-making was clearly collaborative and responsive to patient input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by asking permission, offering options rather than directives, honoring treatment choices, and encouraging self-directed contact if needed. These behaviors reinforced the patient's sense of control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic motives (cancer worry, parenting) and translated them into action, but relied more on clinician-provided strategies than on open-ended elicitation of the patient's own motives, values, and confidence. More systematic exploration of personal reasons and confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engaged in collaborative behaviors such as asking permission and co-creating the one-week plan, but the clinician offered many specific strategies rather than first eliciting the patient's own solutions. Greater priority on the patient's ideas before offering options would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively amplified and reinforced concrete change talk and secured a commitment to a one-week plan, strengthening expressed motivation. To reach a 5, the clinician could have elicited more personal reasons, importance/confidence language, and open-ended exploration of benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation when the patient minimized drinking and acknowledged triggers, which helped prevent reinforcement of sustain talk. Missed opportunities for techniques like double-sided reflections to more directly soften sustain talk and shift emphasis toward change.
