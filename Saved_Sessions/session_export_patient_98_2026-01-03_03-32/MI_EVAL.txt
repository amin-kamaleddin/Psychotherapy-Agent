# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance. Validates worries and supports without minimizing or blaming; offers options rather than pressuring.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, frequent reflections capturing both content and emotion (repeats phrases like “kind of slow,” names fears and hopes). Demonstrates strong attunement throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on a specific, actionable target (five‑minute supervised walk) and repeatedly steers toward manageable planning and implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative stance—offers choices, asks patient preference, invites joint activities (role‑play, script drafting), and shares decision power.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Regularly asks rather than tells, emphasizes patient control, and respects need for reassurance before action; supports ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits reasons for change and past successes (tiny driveway win). Could further deepen by systematically amplifying patient change language (e.g., ask what made that win meaningful or use scaling).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as a shared effort—offers to role‑play, asks how to help, and follows patient’s lead in choosing methods and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete change statements and reflects them; would strengthen impact by more deliberately amplifying and reinforcing desire/ability/reasons/commitment (use selective reflections and scaling toward commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and pivots to safety and small steps (double‑sided reflections present). Could be more strategic in briefly reflecting sustain then immediately following with amplified change reflections or summary to weaken sustain talk.
