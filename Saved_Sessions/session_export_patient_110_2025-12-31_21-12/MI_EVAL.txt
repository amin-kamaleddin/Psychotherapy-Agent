# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained respectful, nonjudgmental tone ("I’m here to listen and support you"); validated concerns and offered practical supports (checklist, follow‑up). At times moved quickly to reassurance/education rather than explicitly normalizing ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected mixed feelings and prioritized specific worries (muscle pain, memory). Listened and adapted (tackled muscle monitoring first). Could have used deeper, affect‑level reflections (e.g., fear of being sidelined).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, clear focus on starting/adhering to statin and safety. Gave explicit thresholds, checklist template, scripts, and arranged 6–8 week follow‑up — clinically appropriate and actionable.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited patient choice (which concern to address, whether to use checklist/scripts) and co‑created tools. At times leaned into an expert role with extended teaching without pausing to elicit the patient’s plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirmed options (e.g., short pauses acceptable), offered shared decision steps and supports (wife, pharmacy options). Some language was more prescriptive/reassuring than explicitly emphasizing patient choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good opening question and priority‑setting prompts, but missed repeated opportunities to elicit and deepen Richard’s own motivations, importance/confidence, and concrete reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used inclusive language, sought preferences, and suggested involving the wife; felt collaborative. Occasionally defaulted to didactic explanations without summarizing or checking understanding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient produced multiple change statements (desire/reasons to be active for retirement), but clinician did not consistently reflect/amplify these statements or elicit commitment (no scaling or “why X and not 0?”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated sustain‑talk (worries about long‑term effects) and provided contingency plans, but primarily responded with factual reassurance rather than MI strategies (double‑sided reflections, strategic summaries).
