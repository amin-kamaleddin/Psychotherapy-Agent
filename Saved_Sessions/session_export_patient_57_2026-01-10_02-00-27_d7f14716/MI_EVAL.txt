# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental opening and multiple normalizing statements conveyed respect and understanding; could add more individualized affirmations or explicit unconditional acceptance to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening, validation, and perspective-taking throughout; could use more complex reflections to name mixed feelings and probe emotional valence before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in team contexts and steered toward practical strategies and concrete next steps without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative with role‑play and rehearsal and invitations for input, but frequent clinician-supplied scripts shifted some control back to the clinician instead of co-constructing language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly asked permission, offered multiple options, and invited Jamal to choose what feels doable, reinforcing his sense of choice and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicited factual information and some change talk but often moved quickly to offering solutions rather than systematically drawing out Jamal's own reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent invitations for input and rehearsal supported shared work, though earlier and more consistent co‑creation of wording with Jamal would strengthen the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change language was elicited by questions about frequency and consequences, but the clinician tended to offer strategies instead of amplifying or following up to deepen Jamal's own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without confrontation and harm-reduction options were provided, but the clinician did not use double-sided reflections or explore the costs of staying the same to gently tip ambivalence toward change.
