# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with multiple validations and explicit belief in the patient’s experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated highly accurate, ongoing perspective-taking with repeated reflections capturing hopelessness and the faint connection to the sister, and stayed emotionally present.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on immediate safety (plan, means, timeframe, location) and consistently guided the session toward concrete safety steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—co‑created and obtained approval for the text, checked preferences, and stayed with the patient while she acted, positioning her as a partner in safety planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by respecting refusal of the ER, offering multiple options, asking permission, and asking what felt okay to try first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Noticed the patient’s concern for her sister and invited small protective actions but did not sufficiently elicit or amplify deeper personal motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with shared decision-making and respect for the patient’s expertise, though occasionally leaned toward directive safety actions without always eliciting prior coping strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made modest attempts to elicit change talk (e.g., naming concern for the sister, offering options) but missed opportunities to probe, amplify, or explore values and past coping to deepen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain talk, but primarily used neutral acknowledgement rather than strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to actively soften sustain talk.
