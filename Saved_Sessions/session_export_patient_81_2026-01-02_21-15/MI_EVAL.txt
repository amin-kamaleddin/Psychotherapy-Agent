# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with repeated validation (“I hear how heavy this feels…”, “You’re taking responsible steps — well done”). Missed opportunities to normalize ambivalence about substance-related identity and shame; moved quickly into problem-solving.

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:
- [x] Maintains respectful, nonjudgmental tone  
- [x] Provides validation of patient's feelings and efforts  
- [x] Avoids labeling or condemnation  
- [ ] Normalizes ambivalence about substance-related identity  
- [ ] Explores shame/failure in depth

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, empathic reflections of distress and practical consequences; acknowledges emotional weight. Did not sufficiently explore deeper emotional content tied to meth use and belonging.

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:
- [x] Reflects content and affect (captures distress and consequences)  
- [x] Acknowledges emotional burden and effort  
- [ ] Explores deeper feelings about identity/belonging related to use

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, actionable focus on immediate stabilization (income, rent, sleep); produced concrete patient actions. Limited linkage of these tasks to substance-use behavior or long-term motivational goals.

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:
- [x] Maintains clear, goal-oriented focus on immediate problems  
- [x] Provides concrete, actionable steps and scripting  
- [ ] Connects tasks to substance-use behavior and long-term change

### Collaboration

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of Lukas’s own motivations or values regarding meth use; clinician focused on logistics. Evocation occurred only around practical problem-solving (income/rent), not inner motivation for change.

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:
- [x] Elicits practical problem-solving reasons/motivation (income/rent)  
- [ ] Elicits personal motivations, values, or ambivalence about substance use  
- [ ] Asks open questions to draw out change talk about use

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction framed as joint problem-solving; permission sought before drafting messages; clinician shared control. Could strengthen by explicitly soliciting the patient's expertise about his social scene and impact of changes.

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:
- [x] Frames work as collaborative, joint problem-solving  
- [x] Asks permission before offering drafts/advice  
- [ ] Explicitly invites patient expertise on social/identity context

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient made tentative change statements but clinician rarely reflected or amplified them; no strategic follow-ups to develop DARN-C. Missed opportunities to strengthen commitment language.

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:
- [ ] Reflects and amplifies patient change talk  
- [ ] Uses strategic follow-ups to elicit desire/ability/reasons/need/commitment  
- [ ] Translates tentative change statements into stronger commitment language

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing or confronting sustain talk, which is positive. However, there were few double-sided reflections or reframes to gently highlight discrepancy between values (career, housing) and continued use.

Checklist — criteria explicitly/clearly met:
- [x] Avoids arguing or directly confronting sustain talk  
- [ ] Uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of ambivalence  
- [ ] Strategically reframes sustain talk to highlight discrepancies
