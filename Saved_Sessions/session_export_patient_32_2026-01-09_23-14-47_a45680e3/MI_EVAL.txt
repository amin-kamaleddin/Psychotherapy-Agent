# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validated the patient’s experience, and normalized safety without moralizing despite a high BAC — clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and timely and acknowledged medical seriousness and isolation, but the clinician missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of loneliness, shame, and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on preventing repeat DUI and reducing risk, repeatedly returning to concrete, actionable next steps and implementation timing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was highly collaborative—plans, texts, and scripts were co-created and iteratively refined to match the patient's voice and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported patient choice by offering options, accepting the patient's stance about not quitting, and offering help only as requested.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical motivations and commitments tied to license and safety, but did not sufficiently explore underlying values, confidence, or amplify past success to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as a collaborator and expert, offering choices and inviting input on wording and plans, which supported shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited, reinforced, and translated the patient's change language into specific commitments and concrete plans (ride app, cash stash, code word, texts).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and shifted attention to safety and alternatives, but could have used more double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to heighten ambivalence toward sustain talk.
