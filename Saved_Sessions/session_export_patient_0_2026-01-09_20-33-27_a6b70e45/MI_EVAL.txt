# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental support and validation (safety check, repeated positive regard) and acknowledged ambivalence without shaming, offering choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of ambivalence and practical concerns, though the clinician could have deepened emotional reflection and used more complex reflections to draw out underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the infection and harm reduction, steering the conversation toward concrete, actionable next steps (medical follow-up, supplies, scripts, role‑play).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑created tools (scripts), asked permission to look up resources, invited role‑play, and followed the client’s priorities, treating the client as an equal partner in problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering choices, respecting the client's decision not to quit, and checking permission before offering help or resources.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client-generated steps and realistic commitments, but missed opportunities to draw out deeper internal motivation, values, importance/confidence, and more client-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operated largely in partnership with shared decision-making and invitations to choose focus, though a few expert-driven moments (detailed instructions without explicit permission) slightly reduced parity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited change talk leading to concrete actions (follow‑up testing, safer practices) but did not sufficiently amplify, reflect, or probe importance/confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and reframed harm‑reduction to align with reluctance to quit, but underused strategic MI techniques (double‑sided reflections, selective reflections) to more actively soften sustain talk.
