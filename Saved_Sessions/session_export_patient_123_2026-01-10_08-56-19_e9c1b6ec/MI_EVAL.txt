# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses nonjudgmental openings, thanks, and validates interests which conveys positive regard. Could add more explicit normalization and affirmations of coping strengths to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate listening with content reflections and check-ins about feelings. Reflections stay at the content level and miss deeper emotional nuances like tiredness or worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on reducing overwhelm and completing the science project, advancing concrete planning each turn. Direction is a strong feature of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites preferences and availability and uses the client's inputs to build the schedule, creating a collaborative feel. However, the clinician frequently offers ready-made solutions and templates rather than co-creating options from the client's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice by offering options, contingency plans, and asking permission before providing resources. Communication clearly preserves the client's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits behavioral commitments (choice of tasks/times) but does little to draw out personal reasons, importance, or confidence. The session favors solution delivery over exploring the client's internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently solicits input and shares planning tasks, indicating partnership. The clinician's expert-led, prescriptive suggestions sometimes reduce the sense of equal co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation of change in the form of concrete commitments and scheduling, but the clinician does not probe for desire, reasons, or confidence. Change talk remains behavioral and is not amplified or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is little sustain talk and the clinician avoids reinforcing doubts, offering pragmatic contingencies when the client expresses uncertainty. Could strengthen by using explicit MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or normalization) when mild resistance appears.
