# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, affirming effort and avoiding blame throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and accurate reflections that calmed and acknowledged ambivalence, but missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections about emotional and social functions of smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, focused target on smoking with a specific, time-bound plan (3-day tracking) and an agreed check-in, providing concrete instructions and reminders.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving, solicited preferences, tailored the plan to the patient's tools and schedule, and co-constructed the follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, asked what the patient preferred, and emphasized that change was the patient's decision without applying pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic reasons and used a readiness ruler and small-step conversion, but could have probed deeper into personal values and elicited more confidence/commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clearly collaborative stance, sharing power by offering options, inviting preferences, and co-creating the tracking plan and check-in time.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and shaped change talk (reasons, future outcomes, readiness) and converted a reason into a concrete step, yet could have amplified and reflected change statements more to deepen motivation and elicit commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized skepticism while redirecting to experimentation and small steps, but could use more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to further diminish sustain talk.
