# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing feelings and affirming choices throughout the session. Demonstrates unconditional positive regard that supports rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and accurate reflections that validate the patient's tension between work and self-care. Could deepen emotional exploration with more nuanced reflections to capture underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on a specific, time-bound self-care experiment and aligns all interventions to that goal. Avoids topic drift and moves logically from values to concrete implementation steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in joint problem-solving and co-creates the plan with the patient, offering options and respecting patient choices. Decisions are shaped by the patient's input, demonstrating a shared approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's sense of choice by offering options and avoiding pressure, while leaving responsibility for implementation to the patient. Reinforces ownership of the plan and respects autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation by linking the plan to stated values and encouraging small experiments, and asks for initial commitment. Could further probe intrinsic reasons and use confidence/importance scaling to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works in partnership by offering options, asking preferences, and acknowledging limits, which preserves shared responsibility. The clinician and patient jointly shaped the plan and follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out change-oriented statements and scaffolds a specific experiment with concrete steps and prompts for commitment. Could more explicitly amplify the patient's values language and use scaling questions to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and proactively addresses barriers with scripts and fallback plans, while normalizing likely feelings like guilt. These strategies reduce the influence of sustain talk and support movement toward action.
