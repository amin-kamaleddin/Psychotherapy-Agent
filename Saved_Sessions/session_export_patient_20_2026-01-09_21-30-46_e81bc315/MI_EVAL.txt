# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing ambivalence and avoiding moralizing language to create a safe, accepting atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and summaries that showed understanding and invited values exploration, but missed chances for deeper emotional probes and more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on cutting back drinking with targeted tools (readiness ruler, strategy menu, concrete plan) and a follow-up plan while respecting autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-created the plan by soliciting preferences, barriers, and supports, suggesting options rather than prescribing and shaping decisions with the client's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized client choice and control, offered options instead of directives, respected limits, and framed slips as information to reinforce ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations and a concrete commitment (readiness ruler, 'what would be a win'), but could probe more into intrinsic values and long-term goals to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by asking permission, inviting the client's choices, and sharing power in selecting and refining the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and shaped change talk into a specific, testable plan (e.g., Wed/Fri, seltzer, every-other-drink), though more consistent amplification and reflection of change statements would build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated reasons for drinking, but generally accepted sustain talk at face value instead of using double-sided reflections, reframing, or explicit discrepancy work.
