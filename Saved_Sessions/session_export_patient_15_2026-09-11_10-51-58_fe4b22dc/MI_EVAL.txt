# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the patient's practical choices, offering options without pressure. The rapport feels somewhat transactional and logistical rather than warmly individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate paraphrases and reflections about scheduling that show basic perspective-taking. The clinician does not explore underlying feelings or the patient's minimization of stress/alcohol, limiting empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains clear procedural direction focused on arranging a follow-up BP check. However, it is narrowly focused on logistics and does not pursue the clinical behavioral target of alcohol reduction or lifestyle/medication options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaboration around logistical planning—asking preferences, confirming choices, and negotiating backups. Collaboration remains within administrative tasks and does not extend to jointly exploring motivations or clinical priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently presents multiple choices in a noncoercive way and repeatedly asks which option feels easiest, strongly supporting patient control and ownership. Language consistently reinforces patient autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to elicit the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for change beyond scheduling. There are no evocative questions to draw out desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers options and invites the patient's input, demonstrating shared decision-making for logistics. The interaction is still clinician-led and stops short of co-creating a broader plan guided by the patient's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician affirms a manageable step (scheduling a BP check) but does not elicit the patient's own reasons, importance, or confidence for change. Opportunities to amplify even small statements toward change talk were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids confrontation but does not explicitly respond to or reframe the patient's minimizations about stress and alcohol. The conversation moves to logistics without using reflections or double-sided statements to soften sustain talk.
