# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates respect and positive regard, validating Tariq's concerns and avoiding blame. Unconditional positive regard is evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with frequent, accurate reflections that capture Tariq's feelings and motives. Reflections shape questions and planning, showing strong reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear focus on restoring phone access by demonstrating readiness, moving from elicitation to concrete planning. Questions and reflections consistently return to specific, change-relevant actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is co-created and team-oriented: clinician solicits Tariq's ideas, refines them jointly, and offers rehearsal or drafting support. The clinician stays in a coaching role rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offers options and asks Tariq to choose what feels fair and doable, reinforcing ownership of the plan. Phrases invite preference and leave decisions in Tariq's hands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician elicits motivation through open, evocative questions, leading Tariq to generate his own reasons and concrete commitments. Motivation is drawn out and then amplified and operationalized.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician frames the work as shared problem-solving and defers to Tariq's expertise about what will work. Repeatedly invites choices and collaborates on script and rehearsal, showing strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicits and reinforces multiple concrete change statements (supervised call, bed-making, programs, extra duties). Uses questions and role-play to amplify and operationalize Tariq's change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledges sustain-talk and avoids confrontation but misses some opportunities to more actively shift it (e.g., double-sided reflections or linking responsibility to consequences). More strategic reframing could further reduce the weight of externalizing blame.
