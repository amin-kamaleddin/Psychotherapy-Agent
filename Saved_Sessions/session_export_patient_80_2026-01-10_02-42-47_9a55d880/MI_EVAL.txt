# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental, supportive regard, normalized Luka's experience, and affirmed progress without blame. This created clear unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and perspective-taking—mirrored feelings, validated anxieties, and offered tailored supports. Could deepen with more complex reflections of ambivalence and affect rather than moving quickly to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing alcohol-related risk and triggers, moving efficiently from check-in to a brief relapse-prevention plan and concrete tools. Guided the session toward actionable steps while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative approach with joint problem-solving, offering options and inviting Luka to craft exact wording and choices. Luka's preferences substantially shaped the plan, reflecting true shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Luka's sense of choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and validating his preferences. Avoided directive pressure and reinforced ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Luka's own words and practical commitments (scripts, texts, practice) and drew on existing motivation to change. Less emphasis on eliciting deeper intrinsic motives, values, or confidence ratings that would strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine partnership by inviting input, co-constructing wording, and using micro-practice with tiny feedback. Power was explicitly shared and Luka's choices directed the interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (praise for cutting down, targeted questions, commitment to practice). Missed opportunities to more consistently reflect and amplify spontaneous change language into stronger desire/ability/reason/need statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and accepted partial change, building practical strategies to reduce risk. Consistently pivoted toward small, feasible steps rather than arguing for abstinence.
