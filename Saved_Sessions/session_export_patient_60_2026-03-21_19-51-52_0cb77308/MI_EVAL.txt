# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys consistent respect and a nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and acceptance of the client's choices. Could add more explicit normalization of ambivalence and unconditional acceptance to reach the top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displays good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, which clarifies priorities and scheduling needs. Would be strengthened by exploring the emotional meaning of partying and using deeper affective reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on concrete behaviors tied to the referral and provides actionable, behavior-specific goals. Could more explicitly link the alcohol incident and conduct consequences to broader long-term goals to sharpen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative, co-creating the schedule and incorporating the patient's preferences and practical supports. Could invite more of the patient's past experiences and let him propose strategies to increase co-authorship.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking permission, and affirming that slip-ups are expected, which reinforces self-efficacy. Language throughout avoids coercion and bolsters patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits change reasons tied to grades and secures concrete commitments but shifts into offering solutions rather than drawing out intrinsic motivations. Could use more open, value-based questions to deepen internal motivation and self-generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership by inviting choices, asking permission, and co-constructing the plan while respecting agency. To improve, the clinician could solicit the patient's expertise about what has or hasn't worked before shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk tied to protecting grades and operationalizes commitments into scheduled actions and accountability. Could more frequently reflect and amplify ambivalent-change statements to strengthen desire/reason/ability language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and does not escalate sustain talk, but uses few double-sided reflections or strategic reframes to acknowledge ambivalence. Would benefit from explicitly acknowledging pull toward partying while gently highlighting downsides to pivot sustain talk toward change.
