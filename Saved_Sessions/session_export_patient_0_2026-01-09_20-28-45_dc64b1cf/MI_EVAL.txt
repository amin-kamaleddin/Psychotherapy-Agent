# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and avoided judgment, normalizing feelings and offering practical, nonshaming help; meets the highest standard for acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening and reflective validation of feelings and guardedness, but missed opportunities for deeper, affect-focused or complex reflections to deepen emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on meth use, infection, and harm reduction throughout the session, tying every intervention to the target behavior and resisting drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a true joint effort—invited the patient's input, offered choices, and followed his pace, positioning clinician and patient as equal partners in planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's autonomy and offered a menu of options without pressure, supporting self-directed, small steps and honoring his decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-related statements and a patient-chosen step but did not sufficiently probe values, use scaling, or amplify tentative change language to build motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively shared power and co-constructed the plan by inviting choices, rehearsals, and resources, consistently deferring to the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced occasional change talk and a concrete harm-reduction step but failed to follow up with targeted questions or summaries to strengthen desire, ability, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk using neutral, accepting reflections and harm-reduction framing to reduce resistance; could increase explicit double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to highlight discrepancy.
