# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized concerns, avoided labeling or shaming, and accepted the patient's limits which supported rapport and engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and barrier enumeration; could use more deeper or amplified reflections to draw out underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing drinking and consistently moved the conversation toward a concrete, actionable experiment with implementation steps and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-created the plan, offered choices, checked preferences for support, and adapted suggestions to the patient's priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by framing suggestions as options, asking permission, and honoring the patient's noncommitment and choices throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivation (values, change talk, confidence scaling) and patient-generated solutions; could probe more deeply into intrinsic values and amplify motivations further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled partnership and power-sharing by asking permission, eliciting the patient's input, and letting the patient choose specifics of the plan and supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with a specific two-drink experiment and confidence work, but could more frequently amplify and cascade change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not confront sustain talk and redirected it toward small, realistic steps and practical tactics; could employ more double-sided reflections to explicitly balance ambivalence.
