# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (safety check, validating honesty) and avoided labeling the patient, maintaining a warm respectful stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used strong reflective listening and perspective-taking and asked permission for questions, but tended to stay at a behavioral/problem level rather than exploring deeper emotions or complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on reducing alcohol use and safety (driving) and matched interventions to the patient’s readiness with a low-effort experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem-solving and invited patient choices, but frequently offered specific tools and suggestions that shifted some control toward the clinician's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly acknowledged choice, asked permission, and offered options (including voluntary follow-up), consistently reinforcing the patient’s agency in decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some evocation (readiness ruler, past attempts) but prioritized practical strategies over drawing out the patient’s own motivations, values, and personally meaningful reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated a collaborative stance by co-constructing a plan and soliciting input, yet early and repeated concrete suggestions reduced opportunities for the patient to generate ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (readiness = 4, willingness to try alcohol-free nights) but did not systematically amplify or deepen reasons, desires, or commitments before shifting to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and offered alternatives when sustain talk appeared, but rarely used strategic MI techniques (double-sided or amplified reflections) to actively soften sustain talk.
