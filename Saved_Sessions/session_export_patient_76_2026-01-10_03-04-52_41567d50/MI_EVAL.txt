# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, offering multiple affirmations and normalizing Liora's distress throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and affective naming matched Liora's tone and somatic language, deepening rapport and validating her experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained clearly focused on self‑harm risk and immediate safety, with pragmatic questions and concrete actions tied to reducing harm.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician acted as a partner, co‑creating the safety plan, offering scripts and choices, and continuously checking Liora's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally supported choice by offering options and asking what felt doable, though a few mildly directive moments (e.g., instructing to set a timer) slightly reduced client-led feel.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited concrete coping ideas and small commitments but did not consistently draw out deeper internal motivation, values, or confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative and client-centered, with the clinician inviting choices, adapting plans, and sharing power in decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and supported doable steps that produced change talk, but missed opportunities to amplify desire/ability/reasons and to elicit a stronger commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected toward safety and alternatives, yet used few strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to explicitly resolve ambivalence.
