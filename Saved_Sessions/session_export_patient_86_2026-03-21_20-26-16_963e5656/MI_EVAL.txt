# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with permission‑seeking language and a nonjudgmental tone. Could occasionally add more explicit normalization of past failed attempts to further deepen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections captured both the ritual and mixed consequences of smoking and validated the patient's preferences. A few more explicit reflections of emotion (e.g., frustration or resignation) could deepen affective attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking and guided the patient to a specific, measurable experiment (30‑minute delay, alarm, substitutes, follow‑up). Direction was balanced with person‑centered choices and a planned check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative with the patient’s preferences shaping the plan and co‑planning for troubleshooting. Language and offers reflected equality and mutual problem‑solving rather than an expert stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized and respected the patient’s choice, asked permission before offering options, and avoided unsolicited advice. This consistently supported the patient's sense of control over any change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own reasons for change and used those statements to build a manageable, patient‑driven experiment. Could occasionally add scaling/confidence queries to further draw out readiness and planning language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as a joint exploration with frequent permission statements and negotiated decisions (alarm time, wording, substitutes). The patient clearly influenced the plan, demonstrating genuine shared power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change talk and guided it into a concrete experiment, producing explicit benefit statements and a willingness to test delaying cigarettes. Could strengthen by amplifying and summarizing the patient’s change statements and probing importance/confidence to consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and rolled with resistance, using double‑sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while leaving room for change. Could more proactively use strategic double‑sided reflections and explicit pivots to further soften sustain talk.
