# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Erik’s mixed feelings using supportive, nonjudgmental language, which fosters disclosure and planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, but reflections are somewhat formulaic and the clinician missed opportunities to probe deeper into emotions around social comparison and inconsistent attendance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on gym attendance and protecting reduced back pain, using purposeful questions that move from values to concrete action planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative: the clinician invites Erik’s input, offers options rather than directives, and positions themselves as a helper while leaving decisions to Erik.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy with choice-respecting language and avoids pressuring, inviting Erik to choose among realistic options and craft his own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from Erik by eliciting personally meaningful reasons for change (reduced pain, better sleep, less reliance on painkillers) and uses those motives to build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treats Erik as the expert on what will work, co-constructing plans and asking which strategies feel most doable rather than prescribing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and secures concrete commitments, but could more frequently amplify and summarize the client’s own change language and probe confidence/importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and redirects to gains and planning, though would benefit from more double-sided reflections or strategic reframes to explicitly soften sustain talk.
