# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating the patient’s distress and normalizing relapse. Language and tone avoided blame and affirmed that the patient need not handle things alone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and accurate reflections captured shame and helplessness, with supportive clarifying questions. Opportunities to deepen empathy were missed when the clinician shifted quickly to skills rather than exploring the emotional meaning behind repeated statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained clear, consistent focus on relapse risk, urges, and immediate safety throughout the session. Interventions (grounding, breathing, 3-step plan, scripting texts/calls) were directly tied to reducing immediate risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was highly collaborative, with the clinician inviting the patient’s input, accepting skepticism, and co-constructing concrete plans. The clinician regularly solicited the patient’s preferred options and wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively supported autonomy by offering multiple options, asking which the patient preferred, and using nonpressuring language. The patient was repeatedly invited to accept, decline, or tailor strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited motivation through asking about the relapse, inviting a patient-proposed plan, and praising small successes. Evocation could be strengthened by more systematic questions about the patient’s reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician fostered a strong partnership by sharing decision-making and asking which area the patient wanted to focus on. Co-creation of the 3-step plan and permission-seeking for scripting reinforced a collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some efforts to elicit and reinforce change talk (praise for grounding, setting a plan), but these were inconsistent and not fully amplified. The clinician missed opportunities to evoke richer desire/ability/reason/need language and past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk, used reflections and normalization, and redirected to coping and safety strategies. A few moments assumed supports the patient denied, and some sustain-talk themes remained dominant.
