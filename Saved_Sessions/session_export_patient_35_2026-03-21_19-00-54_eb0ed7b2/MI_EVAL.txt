# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, validating the patient's burden and affirming protective actions throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking matched the patient's experience, acknowledging stress, parenting burden, and pragmatic coping in a way that facilitated disclosure and rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking reduction and child health, moving logically from exploration to concrete low-burden options, an if–then plan, and a follow-up check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was co-constructed with the patient: the clinician solicited preferences, honored limits, and framed planning as joint problem-solving throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated use of choice language and normalization supported the patient's sense of control, offering options and emphasizing that imperfect effort is acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some intrinsic motivation by having the patient choose workable strategies and commit to a check-in, but did not sufficiently explore deeper reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership via shared decision-making language, inviting the patient to co-create the plan and honoring her choices and constraints.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change-oriented language and concrete micro-behaviors, but missed opportunities to deepen change talk by exploring personal reasons, scaling, or linking to values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and reframed sustain talk toward harm-reduction and small steps, though could have used more double-sided reflections or summaries to amplify change language.
