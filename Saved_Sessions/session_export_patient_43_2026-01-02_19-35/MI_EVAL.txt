# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and praised small efforts ("Nice work — delaying twice is progress"), but warmth felt somewhat procedural and repetitive rather than highly individualized; could use more personalized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and named key perspectives (smoking as stress relief, social/work triggers), but reflections remained surface-level and lacked deeper affective or value-based exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking; set concrete, measurable goals (delay first cigarette 10 minutes on 3–4 mornings; swap one work-break for a 5‑minute walk) and repeatedly steered conversation to strategies and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited patient input and follow-up ("Which feels doable?", "Want to try that and check back?"), but session was somewhat clinician-led with repeated recaps and many suggested options rather than co‑constructed solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on patient choice and control (offered options, framed recommendations as choices, avoided pressure); consistently reinforced ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used scaling questions and elicited discrete change talk and behavioral experiments, but missed opportunities to probe for internal motivations, values, or factors that would raise importance/confidence further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone and invitations supported partnership and follow-up, yet greater co‑construction (soliciting the patient's own ideas and integrating his language) would strengthen the collaborative alliance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk through goal-setting and positive feedback, but could do more to amplify, reflect, and summarize pro‑change statements to increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and redirected sustain talk toward small steps; could further soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections and strategic summaries that acknowledge ambivalence while highlighting discrepancy.
