# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and a nonjudgmental tone and validates Isabelle's busy life, reducing defensiveness. It feels professional and somewhat transactional rather than warmly individualized, with few normalizing statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate paraphrase, checking understanding of her drinking and safety concerns. The clinician seldom probes emotional nuance or internal conflict, so empathy is solid but not maximally deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related safety and moves purposefully from assessment to concrete risk-reduction planning and a brief check-in. The session culminates in specific actionable steps (lock-screen note, checklist, agreed follow-up).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks for Isabelle's preferences, follows her formatting requests, and co-constructs the safety plan and check-in. The clinician still proposes concrete solutions and keeps agenda control rather than eliciting more of Isabelle's own brainstormed strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by inviting pace, asking permission, offering options, and honoring her decision to try the plan once. The clinician reinforces that Isabelle is in control and respects her boundaries about brevity and follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to draw out how alcohol fits with goals and asks what would count as a win, eliciting practical motivation. However, the clinician focuses on logistics and does not explore deeper intrinsic values or strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes elements of power-sharing (permission, offering choices, formatting per request) and solicits her expertise about context. The session remains largely clinician-led and does not deeply leverage Isabelle's input to co-create broader strategies beyond logistics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes several attempts to elicit change-relevant language about safety and fitting with goals, and Isabelle expresses some willingness (e.g., trying checklist once). The clinician does not consistently amplify or explore change talk (importance/confidence, examples, consequences) to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and shifts to sensible risk-reduction options, which is appropriate given minimal sustain talk. The clinician misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or reflective evocation to gently soften resistance like 'you’d have to convince me.'
