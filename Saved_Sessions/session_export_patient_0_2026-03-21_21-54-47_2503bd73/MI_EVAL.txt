# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used nonjudgmental, supportive language and normalized ambivalence, validating Adrian's priorities while naming health concerns. No shaming or moralizing, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured ambivalence and key content. Missed some opportunities to probe and reflect underlying emotions (e.g., shame, embarrassment) to deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on weekend meth use and infection risk, steering to concrete proximal goals like one sober night and safer use. Used scaling and specific planning to guide toward actionable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—invited Adrian's choices, adapted language to his preferences, and co-created practical tools and plan. Power sharing was evident in drafting refusal lines and offering options only on request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Adrian's right to choose and avoided pressure to quit, offering options and asking permission before interventions. Consistent autonomy support reinforced client agency and reduced resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations using scaling and small-step brainstorming, drawing out concrete change ideas. Could deepen evocation by exploring personal values or longer-term goals and eliciting a stronger commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Adrian as the expert on his life, co-constructing the plan and inviting his input at each step. Refusal lines and strategies were tailored to his preferences, reflecting strong collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, scaling, and concrete options, and built on Adrian's own ideas. Could have more directly amplified and summarized his reasons for change to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed sustain talk toward harm-reduction steps, acknowledging the appeal of socializing. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections to further soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
