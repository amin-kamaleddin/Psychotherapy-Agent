# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating; clinician normalized ambivalence and honored confidentiality by offering anonymous options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate understanding and brief reflections, acknowledging stigma and practical barriers; however, deeper reflective listening to explore shame and fear was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on infection risk and harm‑reduction goals, consistently guiding the conversation toward testing, wound care, and safer practices.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicited ZIP code, co‑constructed voicemail/text scripts, and adapted wording to the patient's preferences so the patient's ideas shaped next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported the patient's autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before looking up services, and respecting anonymity and text‑only preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied mostly on providing information and resources rather than eliciting the patient's internal motivations; few open, evocative questions or strategies were used to draw out intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as an equal partner throughout, iterating language together and responding to requests without assuming expert control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created space for harm‑reduction change talk and offered resources, including one direct evoking question, but did not systematically elicit desire, reasons, need, ability, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to practical safety options, using nonconfrontational harm‑reduction responses; missed chances for double‑sided reflections or amplifying spontaneous change talk.
