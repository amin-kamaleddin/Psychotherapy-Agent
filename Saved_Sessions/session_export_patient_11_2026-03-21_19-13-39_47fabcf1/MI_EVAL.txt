# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validated the patient's choices, and used supportive, normalizing language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed some understanding (asked about limits/timing) but used limited reflective listening and rarely reflected the patient's feelings, ambivalence, or motives before moving to a plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity with concrete steps, progression, fallback plans, and monitoring throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited constraints and small adjustments and requested check-ins, but often presented fully formed recommendations rather than co-creating the plan with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and self-direction by offering options, adjustable plans, and explicit permission to postpone elements like nutrition, honoring the patient's control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some preferences and readiness but did not robustly draw out or deepen the patient's internal motivations, values, or commitment language, favoring solutions instead.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making by asking preferences and inviting tweaks, yet the clinician's expert-driven, prescriptive guidance reduced the sense of equal partnership at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created openings for change talk and received several statements of motivation, but missed opportunities to amplify, reflect, or follow up to strengthen desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not push back on sustain talk and provided flexible, low-effort options that reduced its influence, though MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) were used minimally.
