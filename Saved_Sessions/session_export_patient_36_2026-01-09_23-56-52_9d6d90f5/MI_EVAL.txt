# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly communicates unconditional positive regard with explicit acceptance statements and validating language, creating safety for exploration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries the client validates; could deepen by offering more complex reflections of emotional nuance (e.g., frustration or fear around asking for help).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on protecting self-respect via a concrete, time-limited experiment (a one-sentence morning ritual) with questions and reflections that keep returning to that goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with shared agenda-setting and joint problem-solving, though occasional clinician-as-believer statements introduce a mild expert stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options are offered rather than prescriptions and the clinician consistently affirms the client's choices, supporting ownership and avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly elicits the client's internal motivations, values, and past successes with prompts that draw out richly internalized reasons and signs of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treats the client as the expert, co-creates options, and relinquishes authority to invite the client's preferences and decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits and amplifies change talk through values-focused questions and reflections, reinforcing motivations and concrete steps the client is willing to try.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician normalizes and accepts ambivalence without amplifying sustain talk, but missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections or explore costs of the status quo to further soften resistance.
