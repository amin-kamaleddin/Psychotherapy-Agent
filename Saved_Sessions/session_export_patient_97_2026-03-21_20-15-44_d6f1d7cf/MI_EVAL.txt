# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance (e.g., normalized control, validating language, low‑key praise). No shaming or labeling, creating warmth and respect throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that keeps Mateo engaged (clear paraphrases and summaries). Could deepen empathic reflections by naming underlying emotions (fear, shame, hope) rather than staying mostly at factual/practical levels.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on moving toward steady work/training with concrete first steps and a detailed plan. Uses role-play and scheduling to keep the session goal‑oriented while remaining client‑centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑creates the stepwise plan, invites input on reminders and role‑play, and positions the clinician as a teammate rather than an authority. Mateo's ideas shape decisions and next steps throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before advising, and framing suggestions as optional tools. Repeatedly reinforces Mateo's ownership of pace, focus, and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits internal motivations and reasons (e.g., 'bring kids home') and asks what would help him stick with steps. Could strengthen evocation by using scaling, asking about past successes, and amplifying core values toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models power‑sharing and client‑centered planning, routinely asking Mateo's preferences and inviting choice of focus. The plan and supports are co‑constructed, reflecting strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete change talk and helps Mateo articulate meaningful reasons and first steps (e.g., scheduling a call, drafting a script). Would benefit from more reflection/amplification of key change statements and more direct shaping of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and asks about supports, but seldom uses MI strategies (double‑sided reflections, reframing obligation, exploring costs/benefits) to soften it. A more strategic response could better reduce sustain talk and shift toward personal reasons for change.
