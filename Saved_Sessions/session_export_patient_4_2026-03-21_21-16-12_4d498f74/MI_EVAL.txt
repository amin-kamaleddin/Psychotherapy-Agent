# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, validating distress and accommodating requests (e.g., drafting/printing the GP letter). Warm, nonjudgmental stance maintained throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shows strong, accurate empathic listening with repeated reflections of content and feeling and checks about what would be useful next. Captures both cognitive (tests) and emotional (fear, exhaustion) elements well.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the presenting GI symptoms and practical next steps, orienting the session toward tests, referral, and a brief coping step without drifting. Keeps the encounter action‑oriented and relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is co‑constructed: offers to draft materials, asks which item to prepare first, integrates patient language, and responds to preferences like translation/printing. Shares power and works with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering options (tests vs coping vs both), checking preferences, and positioning coping as optional adjuncts rather than replacements for investigations. Respects the patient's control and boundaries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some internal motivation (asks what would be different, notes desire to return to work) and secures a small commitment to breathing, but does not deeply explore reasons, confidence, or amplify existing change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as a joint endeavor, soliciting input, offering practical help (formatting/printing/translating), and following patient requests to co‑create the plan. Demonstrates strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and reinforces some change talk (goals, willingness to try breathing) but misses opportunities to follow up and deepen motivation or amplify statements about symptom improvement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids argument and validates the patient's request for tests, but largely accedes to the biomedical demand rather than using double‑sided reflections or summaries to subtly shift sustain talk toward alternatives.
