# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, validated ambivalence, and praised efforts, creating a nonjudgmental safe tone for the patient to engage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, acknowledging barriers and normalizing slips, but could probe deeper into emotional meaning and use more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on evening smoking reduction throughout the session, progressing logically from assessment to goal choice and concrete supports without getting derailed by other topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a team effort by inviting choices, co-creating alarm labels and coping lines, and incorporating the patient's preferences into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and reinforcing that the patient controls wording and decisions, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons for change and connected them to values (cancer risk, son), but could further draw out deeper intrinsic motivation and amplify change talk into commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and shared decision-making, repeatedly inviting the patient to choose and shaping the plan together rather than prescribing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with scaling questions and concrete supports, but missed opportunities to amplify motherhood/cancer motivations and elicit a one‑sentence commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided pressing on sustain talk, neutrally acknowledged resistance about drinking, and redirected the conversation to practical, small achievable steps to reduce smoking.
