# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance with clear confidentiality statements and validation. Avoided labeling or shaming when reviewing flagged items.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening, summarizing stressors and inviting the patient's reactions. Could deepen empathic work by naming affect and using more complex reflections to explore feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on the screening results and the flagged alcohol item while tying discussion to relevant stressors and offering targeted strategies. Could more actively steer toward eliciting goals and creating a concrete change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created a collaborative atmosphere by offering choices and asking how the patient wanted to proceed. Could more actively solicit the patient's own ideas and co-construct a specific plan together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy with repeated permission-seeking, multiple engagement options, and explicit confidentiality assurances. Interventions were framed as optional and the clinician respected the patient's limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some efforts to draw out the patient's perspective but the approach leaned toward information-gathering and offering solutions. Missed opportunities to elicit personal reasons, values, or concerns that could motivate change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership and power-sharing through permission requests and offering options for how to review materials. Could more explicitly invite the patient's expertise and joint problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to evoke change talk were minimal and indirect—options were offered but the clinician did not explore reasons, importance, ability, or confidence. Tentative change language was not reflected or amplified to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and maintained neutrality, which prevented escalation. However, the clinician missed strategic opportunities (e.g., double-sided reflections) to gently soften sustain talk and highlight ambivalence.
