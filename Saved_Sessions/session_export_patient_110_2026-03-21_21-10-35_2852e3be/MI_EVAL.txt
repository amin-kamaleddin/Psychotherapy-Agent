# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with multiple affirmations and normalization (e.g., validating retirement goals and offering a time‑limited trial), demonstrating steady unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and accurate understanding of the patient’s perspective, summarizing worries well; could deepen affective validation and explore underlying anxiety or ambivalence more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on starting and monitoring statin therapy with concrete, decision‑relevant elements (dose, labs, trial length, stop/switch criteria) and actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative and egalitarian—co‑creates the script/checklist, asks patient preferences, offers to role‑play, and positions the clinician as a helper rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports choice by asking permission, offering options (practice vs checklist), and repeatedly reinforcing that the patient controls the decision and can stop or switch the medication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s reasons for change (linking meds to retirement/activity goals) and uses rulers/pros‑and‑cons; could probe deeper into intrinsic values and amplify existing change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters shared decision‑making and recognizes the patient’s expertise about his life, inviting phrasing preferences and jointly developing the plan and materials to bring to the doctor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk (importance/confidence ruler, pros/cons, rehearsals) and helps craft commitment language; missed some opportunities to reflect and amplify specific change statements to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and redirects to safeguards (start‑low, monitoring, time‑limited trial), but could use more double‑sided and complex reflections and strategic amplification of change‑leaning statements.
