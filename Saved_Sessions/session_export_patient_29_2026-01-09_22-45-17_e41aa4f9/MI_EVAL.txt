# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes David’s experience, explicitly accepting occasional setbacks. Acknowledges contextual constraints (work, social triggers) rather than blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with frequent, specific reflections that capture feelings and situational details. Validates emotional impact (guilt, overwhelm), helping the patient feel understood and safe to elaborate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and practical strategies, guiding the conversation from values to options to small testable steps. Repeatedly brings discussion back to concrete next steps tied to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in an egalitarian, team-like process—solicits input before offering ideas, asks permission to rehearse, and follows the patient's lead. Session content (planning, rehearsal, backups) is mutually shaped and practical.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirms the patient's right to choose and offers multiple options, checking which feels most doable. Encourages voluntary, small experiments that reinforce the patient's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient’s own reasons for change and helps generate concrete plans, drawing on internal motivation. Could deepen evocation by probing personal meaning, using values exploration, and adding confidence/readiness scaling or evocative summaries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the process as collaborative and draws on David’s expertise about his life, avoiding an expert-prescriptive stance. Decisions and rehearsal content are led by the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies change talk and prompts specific steps (phone cue, breaths, seating). Missed opportunities to deepen and consolidate change talk—no confidence ruler, explicit commitment questions, or evocative summaries to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reflects sustain talk without reinforcing it, and shifts focus to practical mitigations and backups. Tends to neutralize rather than strategically soften sustain talk; could use more double-sided reflections and reframing of smoking's immediate benefits.
