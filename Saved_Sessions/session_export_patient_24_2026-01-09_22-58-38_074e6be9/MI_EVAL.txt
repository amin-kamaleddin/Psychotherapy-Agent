# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, using supportive, nonshaming language and affirming the client's practical ideas.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured practical concerns and ambivalence, though reflections were sometimes formulaic and did not deeply explore emotional meaning or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in relation to marathon training with goal-setting and measurable signs of success, keeping the conversation tightly on task.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative and co‑constructed, inviting the client's input at each step and shaping the plan together without taking over.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and offered options, asked the client to pick actions and success criteria, and framed the experiment as voluntary and adjustable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation tied to the client's training goals and concrete reasons for change, but did not sufficiently probe deeper values, past successes, or use scaling to strengthen intrinsic motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong power-sharing by treating the client as the expert, asking for ideas, and jointly deciding which strategies to try first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced concrete change talk and plans, yet missed opportunities to deepen commitment by exploring confidence, past successes, and using importance/confidence rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirected with normalization and small experiments, but could use more tactical approaches (double‑sided reflections, exploring social triggers) to further defuse sustain talk.
