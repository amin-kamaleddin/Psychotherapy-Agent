# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, using warm neutral language and offering practical support (co‑drafting materials, quit plan), with no labeling or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated a good empathic stance with brief reflective statements acknowledging stress and triggers, but reflections were often short and quickly shifted to information rather than deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on smoking cessation with clear movement from concerns to actionable items (medication options, safety checks, quit timeline, coping strategies).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as collaborative problem‑solving: solicited patient preferences, adapted the quit window to the patient's timeline, and co‑constructed contingency plans and tailored materials.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering choices, framing recommendations as options, and leaving decisions to the patient without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relied heavily on information and planning rather than eliciting the patient's internal motivations or values, with few open‑ended questions to draw out change talk or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the interaction as a partnership, repeatedly asking what the patient wanted help with and offering to work together on materials and timelines.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encouraged and supported change‑oriented speech through planning and options, but missed opportunities to elicit and amplify the patient's own reasons and commitment language more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and provided specific coping strategies that redirected toward change, though could use more strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk.
