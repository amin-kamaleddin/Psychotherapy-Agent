# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming (e.g., praise, normalization, gentle language); could add more individualized warmth and explicit unconditional acceptance statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows basic perspective-taking and validation but uses few reflective or emotion-focused responses; more complex reflections and paraphrasing of feelings would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on sitting and completing the exam with well-sequenced, concrete behavioral steps and follow-up planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient choices (prompt, timing, check-ins) are incorporated, creating a collaborative feel, but the clinician often defaults to structured scripts rather than co-constructing strategies with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options and elicits preferences, supporting patient control; could further emphasize the patient's ownership by inviting more patient-generated solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits past success and reinforces steps but does not systematically explore personal values, reasons, or confidence; more evocative open questions would deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites decisions and some shared planning, yet much of the interaction is clinician-directed with prescriptive checklists; greater elicitation of the patient's ideas would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces specific change steps and commitments, converting intention into action; could more deliberately deepen desire, reasons, and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids debating sustain talk and offers urge-management and normalizing language, reducing its influence; could use more strategic reflections (double-sided, selective amplification) to resolve ambivalence.
