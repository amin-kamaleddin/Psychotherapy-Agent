# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, validating Milan’s perspective and normalizing defensiveness throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, though reflections sometimes remain pragmatic rather than exploring deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol at social gatherings and moves logically from elicitation to a concrete, actionable experiment and safety plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as a true collaboration with frequent solicitation of Milan’s preferences and co-creation of the plan, including concrete tasks and contingencies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes choice, frames the plan as a small reversible experiment, asks permission before offering input, and avoids pressuring commitments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of values and use of a readiness ruler, but limited follow-up to draw out deeper intrinsic motivations or personally meaningful reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and invites Milan to choose options, though the clinician at times provides many specific tactics rather than eliciting Milan-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces tentative change talk (readiness ruler, menu, affirmations) and helps operationalize a plan, with some missed opportunities to amplify motivation further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes ambivalence, reducing resistance, though could more frequently use double-sided reflections to explicitly link sustain talk to change possibilities.
