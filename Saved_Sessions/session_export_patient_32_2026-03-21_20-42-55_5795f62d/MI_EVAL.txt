# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, supportive stance, normalized the situation, and avoided labeling or shaming while validating the patient's concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated feelings and surfaced triggers, though deeper emotional reflections earlier (e.g., isolation/fear of being an outcast) would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on preventing another DUI with concrete harm-reduction options and progressive action planning tied to that goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative problem-solving with the patient’s input driving choices, adapting language to his style, and using his proposals as the basis for the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice and agency by offering options, asking which to try, and seeking permission for rehearsals, respecting the patient's pacing and preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations and resources through targeted questions, the confidence ruler, and prompting concrete steps the patient generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction demonstrated shared power and co-creation of the plan, repeatedly inviting the patient's input and leaving decisions about wording and supports to him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicited and reinforced change language, probed barriers, used confidence scaling, and translated statements into concrete implementation intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided argument and gently redirected sustain talk toward alternatives, but used few double-sided reflections that would explicitly contrast minimizations with the patient's concerns to consolidate ambivalence.
