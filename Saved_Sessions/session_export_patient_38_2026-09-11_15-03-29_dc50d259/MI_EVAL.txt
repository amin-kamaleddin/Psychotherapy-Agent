# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing Elena's feelings and affirming her choices. No instances of shaming or directive language were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening captures affect and ambivalence (e.g., relieved and scared) and the clinician checks in about what feels doable. Reflections are appropriately complex and responsive throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician keeps a clear focus on contraception and immediate next steps related to the pregnancy scare, repeatedly summarizing and narrowing the agenda. Questions and offers remain directly tied to moving toward contraceptive planning when the patient is ready.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is consistently equal-partner: the clinician invites Elena into decisions, uses 'we' language, and offers to perform supportive tasks rather than prescribing actions. Plans are co-created with negotiable supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated statements of choice, 'no pressure' language, and offering multiple optional steps tailored to Elena's readiness. The clinician respects her not-ready stance and facilitates small, voluntary actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits internal motivations and asks what would reassure Elena, inviting her to express reasons for change. However, there is limited probing of importance, confidence, values, or translation of motives into stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the session as shared exploration, asks which options feel most doable, and offers to look up information or draft messages together. Power-sharing is genuine and evident throughout the encounter.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and reinforces practical, doable steps that generate change statements (e.g., choosing a home test first). Missed opportunities include not explicitly eliciting desire/ability/need/confidence or amplifying change talk into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk by offering low-pressure, small steps and reframing ambivalence. Improvement could come from more double-sided reflections and gently exploring underlying concerns to tie sustain talk to change talk.
