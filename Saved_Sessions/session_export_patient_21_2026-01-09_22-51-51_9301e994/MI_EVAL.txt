# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician communicates clear, nonjudgmental positive regard with explicit normalization and affirmation, validating ambivalence and small steps without blame. This supportive stance consistently reinforces the patient's worth and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is good reflective listening and perspective-taking that helps the patient feel understood, with accurate paraphrases of concerns. Empathy is more cognitive/behavioral than deeply affective, and deeper exploration of the emotional texture (fear, shame, frustration) would strengthen attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session keeps a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity and repeatedly narrows motivation into a concrete, time-bound plan (two 15-minute after-lunch walks). Questions and reflections are tied to feasibility and next steps, avoiding drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction reads as a joint enterprise with repeated invitations for the patient's views and preferences and co-construction of wording and plans. The clinician offers options and shapes the plan together rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports choice and control by offering alternatives, avoiding prescriptive language, and asking which options feel natural for the patient. This scaffolding reinforces ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicits the patient's own motivations and strategies through open questions and reflections, reinforcing internally generated reasons for change. Role-play and refinement of the patient's plans further draw out and strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasizes shared decision-making, repeatedly inviting the patient's input and asking permission to role-play or shape phrasing together. This power-sharing approach clearly locates expertise with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and reflective amplifications draw out specific change talk and commitment language, leading to concrete implementation steps (reminder, shoes, partner check-in). The clinician also offers rehearsal to support enactment of the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validates sustain-talk and frequently pivots toward change by normalizing ambivalence and focusing on small, doable steps. Opportunities for stronger strategic techniques (double-sided reflections or reframing sustain-talk into preparatory change language) were missed.
