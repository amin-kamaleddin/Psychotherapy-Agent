# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating—uses supportive language and normalizes Lars's feelings throughout the session. No shaming or critical statements were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate summaries that help Lars feel heard. Could deepen empathy with more complex reflections probing core emotions like guilt and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on targeted behaviors (employment, Friday-night risk, form help) and repeatedly steers toward concrete next steps. Avoids drifting and builds pragmatic momentum for action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—invites choice and offers to co-create scripts or plans. At times the clinician supplies extensive lists and solutions that shift the balance toward an expert role rather than eliciting Lars's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports client choice by presenting options, asking which to start with, and avoiding pressuring language. Consistently frames decisions as Lars's to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation via reflections of goals and preference questions, but the clinician relies more on offering solutions than drawing out Lars's own reasons, values, and confidence. Missed opportunities to elicit deeper change talk or use scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with offers to draft scripts and shared planning. However, frequent expert input and long lists sometimes dominate instead of co‑constructing solutions from Lars's language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and supports some change talk through concrete steps and choice, and reinforces identity-shifting statements. Does not systematically amplify change talk or use targeted open questions to draw out desires, reasons, abilities, or commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and provides practical risk-reduction strategies (Friday plan, refusal lines). Tends to move quickly to logistics rather than using MI techniques (double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance) to pivot sustain talk into change talk.
