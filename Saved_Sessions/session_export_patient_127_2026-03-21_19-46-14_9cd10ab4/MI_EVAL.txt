# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used clear nonjudgmental language and repeatedly affirmed Timur’s efforts, preserving dignity and challenging stigmatizing self-labels. Warm, respectful statements were consistent throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and normalization of feelings, with effective echoing of the client’s language and small‑thing values. Missed opportunities to probe deeper into identity and belonging before moving to action planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed sharply focused on relapse prevention and building pro‑social stability, with most interventions tied to those goals. The clinician kept the conversation goal-directed while remaining responsive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly joint: the clinician invited input, tailored materials to Timur’s blunt style, and co‑produced practical tools. The clinician followed the client’s preferences and shared problem‑solving throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently asked permission, offered options, and validated Timur’s choices, reinforcing ownership of decisions. Language and offers avoided pressure and supported tolerable, incremental steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and amplified existing motivations (little things, concern for Fiona) and drew out practical commitments. Could strengthen evocation by using more open, values‑oriented questions and scaling confidence/importance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with frequent checks for preference and co‑construction of plans. At times the clinician offered multiple specific recommendations quickly, which leaned toward expert delivery rather than eliciting ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively reinforced and amplified change talk with praise, concrete steps, and tailored scripts, helping produce actionable commitments. Further probing with open questions could have deepened the client’s own reasons, hopes, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected some ambivalence, but often moved to practical solutions without thoroughly reflecting both sides of the client’s ambivalence. More deliberate use of double‑sided reflections and eliciting the client’s own arguments against returning to crime would strengthen this area.
