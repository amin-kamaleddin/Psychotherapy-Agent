# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing difficulty and checking safety; tolerated sustain talk without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with accurate reflections and validation of social concerns, but often moved quickly to problem‑solving instead of using deeper, amplified reflections to explore emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation and its social function throughout, linking questions, role‑play, and tactics to the core behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—invited input, role‑play, and choice of strategies—but occasionally shifted into offering lengthy lists of tactics without first eliciting the patient's priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control by offering multiple options, respecting declines, using permission language, and inviting the patient to select and practice preferred strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort to draw out motivation (past attempts, scaling), but the clinician often moved to solutions rather than systematically eliciting intrinsic reasons, values, and amplifying change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with questions and joint problem‑solving and used role‑play, though at times adopted an expert/recommendation tone that reduced co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited change talk via scaling and past success and invited strategy choice, but missed opportunities to deepen, follow up, and reinforce the patient's own reasons and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively rolled with sustain talk and avoided confrontation, yet did not consistently use double‑sided or strategic reflections to reframe sustain talk toward change.
