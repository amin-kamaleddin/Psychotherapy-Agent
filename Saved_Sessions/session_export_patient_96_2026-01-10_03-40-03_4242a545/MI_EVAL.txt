# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and avoided judgment, validating Marisol’s choice and supporting safety planning rather than criticizing. The clinician accepted her decision and offered options without pressuring.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used supportive tone and brief validations that matched Marisol’s style, but reflections were generally surface-level. Missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections to explore feelings and meaning behind the piercing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on oral-health risk reduction with concrete action steps and tight role-play/scripting. The session stayed on-task and pragmatic throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a co-worker with Marisol, co-creating scripts and adapting to her preferences and choices. The clinician invited input, practiced with her, and let the patient shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Marisol’s right to choose, avoided coercive language, and framed recommendations as optional. This reinforced the patient’s ownership of decisions and supported self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited commitment to monitoring but relied mostly on information-giving and scripting, with limited attempts to draw out internal motivations or values. More open questions and scaling could have better evoked change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the work as a collaborative effort, offering help only after stating options and inviting Marisol’s preferences. Consistently shared power and respected the patient’s expertise about her choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced protective intentions and elicited watch-for commitments but did little to evoke desire, ability, reasons, or confidence for change beyond safety monitoring. More targeted open questions and amplifying reflections would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and strategically pivoted to harm-reduction and monitoring, preventing escalation. Could further soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections and brief acknowledgment of the dentist’s concerns while honoring autonomy.
