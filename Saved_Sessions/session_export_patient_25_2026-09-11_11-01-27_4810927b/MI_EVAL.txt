# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the patient’s priorities (health, family, enjoyment) without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, matching tone and validating emotions (pride, concern) through complex reflections and paraphrase.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing risky drinking while reinforcing smoking cessation and repeatedly steers toward concrete, actionable strategies (NA pick, two-drink cap, decline line).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the interaction as a shared enterprise, offering options and asking the patient to choose strategies and wording so the plan is co-constructed and natural for them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and personal agency by asking which options feel doable and avoiding prescriptive or coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from the patient’s own values and prior success (quitting smoking), drawing out concrete strategies and linking them to the patient’s competencies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites the patient’s expertise and preferences, offering options and soliciting input so decisions are driven by the patient rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and supports change talk, producing concrete plans (NA beer, cap at two, rehearsal); could further amplify and elicit firmer commitment (e.g., scaling or summarizing commitments).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk with double-sided reflections and low-drama options that acknowledge the desire to enjoy social life while neutralizing reasons to maintain current behavior.
