# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizes concerns, and validates ambivalence, creating a warm, nonshaming atmosphere. Safety check and repeated supportive language fostered a safe conversational space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician shows good perspective-taking and some reflective listening, asking about triggers and acknowledging worries. Reflections tend to be brief and functional, missing opportunities to explore deeper affect and emotions like restlessness or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing phone/social media use and ties all suggestions to that target. The one-week experiment and concrete follow-up keep momentum and measurable goals front and center.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session functions as a two-party problem-solving process with permission-seeking and client refinements to the plan. However, the clinician supplied many concrete solutions and templates instead of eliciting the client's own strategies first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly frames choices as experiments and seeks permission, offering options and contingencies that preserve client control. Language emphasizes flexibility (shorten windows, tweak plan) and treats slips as data rather than failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits actionable commitment language and has the client choose a specific experiment, drawing out some intrinsic motivation. The clinician moves relatively quickly to solutions and could spend more time exploring values, importance, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is a collaborative stance with co-creation of the plan and frequent permission-seeking. Offering a large menu of strategies early shifted expertise toward the clinician rather than foregrounding the client's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and operationalized change talk, helping the client commit to a specific, doable plan and follow-up. To strengthen this, the clinician could more systematically reflect and amplify the client's desire, reasons, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing the client's worries and offering compromises like DND exceptions and scheduled checks. More MI-specific techniques (double-sided reflections, strategic summaries) could further reduce sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
