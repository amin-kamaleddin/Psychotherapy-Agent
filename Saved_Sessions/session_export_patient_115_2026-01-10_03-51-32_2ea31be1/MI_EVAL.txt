# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—used empathic labeling, normalized slips, affirmed motives, and praised realistic, family-focused planning throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate reflections and summaries, but missed opportunities to explore underlying emotions more deeply with complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with measurable behaviors, barrier plans, and a concrete follow-up, keeping the session goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative—solicited Samir's ideas and preferences, negotiated steps around his schedule and family, and co-created the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Samir's sense of choice by offering options, honoring his decision to cut down rather than quit, and avoiding prescriptive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Samir's own motivations and values (wanting to be there for his kids), used scaling and questions to draw out change talk, and built the plan from his reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a partnership—frequently invited Samir to choose among options, checked preferences for follow-up, and shared power in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk through open questions, scaling, reflections, and reinforcing specific commitments (start date, NRT, concrete steps).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed sustain talk into achievable steps, but used few strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or selective reinforcement) to more actively diminish sustain-oriented language.
