# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance (e.g., “I’m glad you’re here; you’re not being judged,” repeated validations such as “That makes a lot of sense,” “I hear you…”). Acceptance present on nearly every clinician turn; no shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate paraphrase with emotion-labeling (frustration, embarrassment). Reflections were mostly content-focused; fewer complex or evocative reflections exploring deeper emotional nuance or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully steered session toward a practical, short-term plan that met client priorities (online course + one meeting + small fine). Limited exploration of broader values or discrepancy to deepen motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance—frequent invitations for Jason’s input (e.g., “What matters most…?”, “What ideas do you have…?”, asking for exact wording). Power and choice consistently handed to the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Preserved client autonomy (asked what Jason wanted to say, offered to advocate while not promising outcomes). Encouraged client choice and respected limits (non‑coercive language).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited concrete change language and a specific plan; asked evoking questions to narrow options. Missed opportunities to elicit broader motives, values, and stronger reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated inclusive language (“we can figure this out together,” “I can advocate for that”) and joint decision-making; clinician checked back frequently to confirm priorities and phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited commitment-level statements (willingness to complete course, meeting, pay fine; clear non‑negotiables re: parents and suspension). Could further amplify desire/reasons/need/confidence and convert statements into firmer commitment (scaling, follow‑ups).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce minimizations (“blown out of proportion,” “it wasn’t my fault”); redirected toward action and practical options. Fewer double-sided or reframing reflections explicitly softening sustain talk and creating discrepancy.
