# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized ambivalence, and affirmed feelings with a supportive tone throughout; no shaming or minimizing was observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflections and named emotions, offering brief validations, but could deepen reflective listening to explore guilt and identity nuances more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, steady focus on the target behavior with a concrete 4-step plan and repeated rehearsal, keeping the session tightly goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—routinely asked for preferences, adapted wording, and co-constructed role-plays and coaching tips, functioning as an equal partner offering tools and practice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices and encouraged authentic wording and control, but presenting an early coercive-sounding 'Direct' script risked narrowing perceived choice without further exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some preferences and behavioral commitment through rehearsal, but often supplied scripts instead of drawing out the client's own motivations, values, and intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the client as an active agent through choices and role-play, though more elicitation of the client's own solutions before offering scripts would strengthen shared expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively fostered change talk via options, rehearsal, and planning, yet missed opportunities to elicit and amplify the client's own reasons and confidence language (e.g., follow-up on 'My job is done').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing feelings and redirecting to boundaries and supports, but did not deeply co-create strategies to tolerate guilt beyond rehearsal.
