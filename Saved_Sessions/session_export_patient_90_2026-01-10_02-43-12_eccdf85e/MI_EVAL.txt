# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with validating reflections and praise, and avoided blaming or minimizing Marcus’s attachment to his dog.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and safety‑focused empathy with accurate reflections early on, but missed deeper emotional exploration (e.g., the dog’s emotional role and the felt experience of being 'crushed').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on immediate asthma management and safety, progressively narrowed to practical, actionable steps (inhaler checks, script, call/text) without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—co‑constructed scripts and texts, invited Marcus’s preferences and wife’s involvement, and framed the plan as a joint effort rather than expert directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Marcus’s choice by offering options, asking permission for role‑play, and avoiding pressuring language, reinforcing his control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out motivation by highlighting benefits and asking choice questions, but did not systematically probe Marcus’s own reasons, values, or confidence to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Marcus as a collaborator and let him choose the focus, but could more explicitly reinforce his expertise and invite him to generate solutions before offering them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated it into concrete actions, yet missed opportunities to elicit strength/confidence language, detailed reasons, and commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered neutral, practical alternatives around the dog, but could have used more double‑sided reflections and strategic redirection into change language.
