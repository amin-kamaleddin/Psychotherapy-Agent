# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental language with frequent affirmations and normalization of the patient's experience, conveying unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and validations are accurate and supportive (e.g., naming burden and normalizing responses), but some reflections are brief and the clinician shifts quickly to problem-solving—more complex, exploratory reflections would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on starting homework and building follow-through, with purposeful stepwise sequencing and every intervention tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session is largely collaborative—patient preferences are checked and patient language is used—but the clinician often leads with specific techniques and step instructions, so power-sharing is strong but not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers options, asks permission to coach, and checks which choice the patient prefers, avoiding pressure and consistently reinforcing the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation via willingness scales and small commitments occurred, but much of the interaction was clinician-driven with limited use of open, evocative questions to draw out the patient's intrinsic motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moves (asking preferences, inviting choices), but frequent clinician proposals of concrete strategies place them more in an expert role rather than jointly generating solutions with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change-oriented language through readiness ratings, experiments, and praise, though the clinician could better amplify and reflect the patient's own change statements and probe importance/confidence more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician validates and normalizes sustain talk and reframes toward very small doable steps without amplifying resistance, but could more systematically use double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence summaries to resolve sustain talk.
