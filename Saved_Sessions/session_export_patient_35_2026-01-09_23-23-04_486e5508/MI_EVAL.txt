# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing stress and validating effort throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, consistent empathic listening with reflective statements and checks after the grounding exercise that help the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing the child's smoke exposure and practical, doable steps while gently steering back from tangents and affect regulation work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner—co-constructs scripts and reminders, repeatedly checks patient preference, and shapes plans with the patient's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, accepting boundaries about quitting, and asking permission before rehearsals or role-play.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits pragmatic motivations for protecting the child and some commitments but stops short of deeper exploration of intrinsic reasons for quitting or values-based questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly adopts a collaborative stance, offering options and co-creating a patient-chosen, manageable plan with frequent shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports concrete, low-risk change talk and commitments (e.g., ENT referral, delay rule) but misses opportunities to amplify intrinsic desire and stronger change language about quitting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reinforces harm-reduction strategies rather than confronting sustain talk, though could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
