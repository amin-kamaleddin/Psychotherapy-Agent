# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, offering safety checks and validating disclosures; demonstrated strong unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with accurate reflections and validation, but reflections were mainly factual and solution-focused; missed opportunities to explore emotional nuance and mild ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in the context of the injury, linking drinking to risk and developing a concrete harm-reduction plan tied to goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session read as a joint effort with co-created goals and role-play practice, and suggestions were adapted to the client's needs; at times the clinician supplied many concrete strategies that felt more expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirmed Matteo's right to choose and offered multiple realistic options while asking which he preferred; occasionally moved into more prescriptive territory by offering many specific tactics without always asking permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation via open questions and invitations to practice, but the clinician relied largely on clinician-supplied ideas and did not sufficiently elicit Matteo's personal values or intrinsic motives for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—invited agenda-setting, offered options, and co-created the plan—but sometimes shifted into an expert/teacher role when providing medical information and numerous tactics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited tentative change talk through open questions and goal choice, but did not systematically explore or amplify reasons, importance, or confidence to deepen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and provided pragmatic alternatives, yet relied more on advice/strategies than MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) specifically aimed at softening sustain talk.
