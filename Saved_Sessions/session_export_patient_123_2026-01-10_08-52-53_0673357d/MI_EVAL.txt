# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates the client's experience with warm language and practical support. It stops short of deeper, individualized normalizing or more elaborated affirmations of the client’s struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good basic reflective listening and attention to the client's experience, with recommendations tailored to her alertness. Reflections remain fairly surface-level and the clinician moves quickly to prescriptive plans rather than exploring emotions more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on school stress and realistic study planning throughout the session. Actionable, tailored steps were established and followed through with appropriate goal orientation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits preferences and adapts the plan to the client's schedule, creating back-and-forth problem-solving. However, the clinician largely leads design of the detailed schedule so collaboration isn’t fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports client choice by offering options, inviting adjustments, and normalizing imperfect adherence. The clinician repeatedly emphasizes the client's control over choices and modifications.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician elicits practical preferences and agreement to try strategies but makes little effort to draw out the client's reasons, values, or motivations for change. Most solutions are clinician-provided rather than evoked from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are invitations for input and some shared decision points, but much of the session is clinician-driven with prescriptive planning. The client’s expertise shapes parts of the plan but not most of the concrete content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some opportunities were created for the client to express willingness and preferences, and the client made tentative commitments to try strategies. The clinician did not consistently elicit or amplify deeper change talk about reasons, benefits, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician responds supportively to expressions of uncertainty with normalizing language and pragmatic fallbacks, which reduces pressure. However, strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring pros/cons) were not used to actively tip ambivalence toward change.
