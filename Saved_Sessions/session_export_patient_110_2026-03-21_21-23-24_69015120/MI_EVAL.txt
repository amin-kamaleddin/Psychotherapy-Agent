# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect (warm greeting, recognizes values, normalizes ambivalence) and avoids judgment throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and reflective listening (mirrors concerns, uses scaling), but emotional content is not deeply explored with more complex reflections or probes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on cholesterol treatment and adherence, translating patient preferences into concrete next steps and preserving momentum toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicits preferences and co-creates the monitoring/check-in plan and materials, demonstrating genuine teamwork rather than a top-down approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice with option language, invitations to choose formats and timing, and confirms the patient's right to switch meds, reinforcing ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal motivation effectively (ties change to valued goals, uses scaling) but moves relatively quickly into education and planning instead of amplifying intrinsic reasons and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the session as a collaborative endeavor, invites the patient to shape the plan and materials, and centers the patient's expertise in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and strengthens change talk using scaling and planning, producing explicit change language, but could more often amplify change statements and elicit a stronger commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to manageable steps and monitoring, though it uses few intentional strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) specifically aimed at softening sustain talk.
