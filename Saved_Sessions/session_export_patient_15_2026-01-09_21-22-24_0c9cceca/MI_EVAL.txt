# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, with multiple explicit affirmations and normalization of the patient's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provided basic reflective and supportive statements but missed opportunities for deeper reflective listening and exploration of the patient's minimization and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on home blood pressure monitoring, thresholds, and next steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone and offers were collaborative (log template, joint review), but the clinician could have solicited more patient input on implementation and barriers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced patient choice and presented options without pressure, explicitly respecting the patient's decision about drinking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; few evocative open questions or scaling/confidence prompts were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as shared (offers to review results, ask permission), though some directive education shifted toward an expert role at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited concrete monitoring behavior and some willingness to review links to drinking, but did not systematically draw out or amplify the patient's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledged the patient's stance, though responses were more permissive than actively reframing with double-sided reflections.
