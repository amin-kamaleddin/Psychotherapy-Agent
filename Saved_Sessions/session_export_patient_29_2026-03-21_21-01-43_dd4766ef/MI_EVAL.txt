# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and respect, validated concerns without judgment, and thanked the patient for planning; warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking that matched patient concerns, though could deepen emotional validation with more complex reflections and probes about stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a very clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and related subgoals with actionable steps, timelines, safety checks, and follow-up planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively to co-create scripts, logistics, and a timeline, incorporating patient edits and preferences so planning felt shared and practical.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, alternatives, and letting the patient select the quit date, avoiding pressuring language and reinforcing control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation (past attempts, quit date) but relied heavily on information and logistics; could use more open questions to draw out values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited input and co-created plans, but relatively strong expert direction (detailed scripts/titration) slightly reduced full power-sharing with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and strengthened change language and concrete commitments (quit date, plan), yet could better amplify personal reasons and use summaries to increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain-talk without reinforcing it and shifted to problem-solving, though more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries could further resolve ambivalence.
