# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing feelings and affirming small steps throughout the encounter. The clinician avoids labeling and frames slips compassionately, showing steady unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and occasional reflections (e.g., validating loneliness and frustration). Empathy is mostly brief and functional; more complex reflections exploring deeper emotions and meanings would strengthen it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing problematic alcohol use with concrete behavioral goals and appropriate medical risk screening. Repeatedly returns to specific strategies (tracking, delay, scripts, check-in) to guide change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as joint problem-solving with frequent invitations for input and adjustments to patient preferences (role‑play, scripts, tracking). The clinician tailors plans and proceeds with the patient’s consent, reflecting strong collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, honoring non‑abstinence goals, and asking permission for interventions. Language emphasizes the patient’s control and frames slips kindly, reducing pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses tools like a readiness ruler and elicits a small goal, but much of the session is clinician-driven with skills and suggestions. Missed opportunities to probe personal motivations, values, past successes, and to draw out intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance with permission‑seeking and offering choices, and the patient’s selections shape the plan. However, the clinician provided many specific suggestions which slightly shifted power toward expert input rather than eliciting more patient‑generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk (readiness ruler, goal setting, planning) and gains explicit commitment to tracking and delay. Could further amplify the patient’s own reasons and values to deepen and multiply change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without bolstering it, normalizes worry about being left out and offers harm‑reduction alternatives. Would benefit from more strategic double‑sided reflections or eliciting the downsides of sustain talk to further weaken it.
