# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard—frequent validations and normalization of distress without judgment, explicitly acknowledging the patient's fears and impact on work.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening with accurate, timely reflections that named emotions and burdens, and responsiveness to subtle admissions about symptom variation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the patient's priority for investigations and steered toward concrete, actionable steps, though could have more deliberately balanced this with brief exploration of longer‑term contributors and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High collaboration—documents and scripts were co‑authored, the clinician invited edits and role‑played, demonstrating true two‑way engagement driven by the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and providing editable templates and choices so the patient retained control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of immediate goals and willingness to try coping tools, but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient's deeper motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent partnership—language and actions shared power, invited the patient to direct priorities, and co‑constructed materials throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional openings for change talk (options, coping ideas) but missed opportunities to amplify hints of change (e.g., symptom improvement at the wedding) and to explore motivations or confidence in behavioral strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoided arguing but largely accommodated sustain talk, strengthening biomedical requests instead of using MI techniques (double‑sided reflections, ambivalence summaries) to gently shift focus or explore costs of the status quo.
