# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized the client's experience, avoiding labels or moralizing language. Acknowledged pressures around grades and band while validating the client's values throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, empathic understanding via complex reflections and paraphrases that captured both behavior and underlying tension. The patient accepted and elaborated on these reflections, indicating strong attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent agenda focused on protecting IB standing and band role while managing social drinking. Progressed logically from values to specific, actionable steps and concrete planning questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as a cooperative effort, offering suggestions while soliciting the client's preferences and involvement. The client's ideas noticeably shaped the plan (e.g., choosing the short script and rehearsal).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and supported the client's sense of control by offering options and avoiding pressure. Affirmed the client's decisions and respected her choice to rehearse with the clinician.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation out of the client through values and strengths-based questions rather than supplying reasons. Elicited concrete, client-owned solutions and actionable plans (e.g., skipping weeknight parties, rehearsing a script).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the client as the expert and co-created the plan, repeatedly asking which options felt most realistic. Invited rehearsal and choice, reflecting clear power-sharing and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk with evocative questions and affirmations, reinforcing motivations and planning. This approach produced multiple change statements and concrete commitment to try the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk without reinforcing it, reducing defensiveness. However, the clinician relied mainly on reflections and could have used more double-sided reflections or explicit exploration of costs to more actively soften sustain talk.
