# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validates the patient's distress without judgment, normalizing the desire for testing throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses empathic statements and reflective listening and checks safety, but could deepen empathy by exploring underlying fears and ambivalence more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on obtaining further medical evaluation and consistently channels the session toward actionable steps and referrals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: co-creates the letter, checks preferences at multiple steps, and adapts language to the patient's voice while coaching rehearsal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks permission, offers options, and frames actions as the patient's decisions, consistently supporting the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits concrete actions (note, rehearsal) but does not systematically draw out the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence for seeking a second opinion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance and shared decision-making but occasionally shifts into an expert/recommendation role by proposing specific tests and wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports action-oriented steps that encourage movement toward change but rarely elicits or amplifies the patient's own change language about importance or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting or dismissing biomedical concerns and validates the need for tests, which helps soften sustain talk; could further benefit from double-sided reflections.
