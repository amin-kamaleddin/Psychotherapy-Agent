# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing ambivalence and slips while honoring the patient's preferences and avoiding judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides accurate, timely reflections that capture ambivalence and validate feelings, showing strong empathic attunement throughout the interaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on cutting back coffee, offers concrete options, elicits a specific plan, and sets a follow-up to keep momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient as an active partner by co-creating the plan, soliciting preferences for reminders and follow-up, and positioning decisions jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice and control by asking permission, offering options rather than directives, and emphasizing no-pressure, patient-led pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's reasons and implementation intentions (e.g., decaf swap, alarm) but could deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring values and longer-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters genuine power-sharing through permission-seeking, joint decision-making, and inviting the patient's input on concrete steps and follow-up timing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and builds on change talk (offers choices, uses confidence ruler) and secures a commitment, but could amplify and consolidate the patient's change statements more strongly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes slips positively while offering ritual-preserving options, though more double-sided reflections could better soften sustain talk.
