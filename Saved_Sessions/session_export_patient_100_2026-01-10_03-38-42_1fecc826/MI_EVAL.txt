# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful throughout the interaction, normalizing Maya's feelings and avoiding blame. The clinician maintained unconditional positive regard from opening to close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and accurate summaries of Maya's concerns and triggers. Missed a few opportunities for deeper, more affective reflections that would capture the emotional nuance of the blackout and embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, focused trajectory on weekend drinking and harm-reduction steps, moving logically from assessment to a concrete weekend plan. The clinician consistently steered toward specific, behaviorally actionable strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-created the trigger map, harm-reduction options, and rehearsal lines with Maya and honored her choices. The clinician invited practice and shared power in planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, offered options, and framed advice as optional and collaborative. The clinician respected Maya's caveat about not promising to stick to the plan and supported her ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivations (costs/benefits, readiness ruler, reasons for starting) and drew out some change-leaning material. However, the clinician did not consistently deepen or develop that material into stronger change talk tied to values or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated clear partnership by asking preferences, offering options, and practicing language together, with Maya shaping the plan. Interaction reflected shared decision-making and mutual engagement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-leaning statements (concern about college, embarrassment, willingness to try one night) but did not consistently amplify or selectively reflect to deepen desire, reason, or commitment. Limited exploration of values or past successes to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and accepted Maya's hesitancy, which prevented escalation. However, the clinician did not actively use strategies like double-sided reflections or ambivalence summaries to soften sustain talk and tip toward change.
