# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveyed consistent unconditional positive regard, praising effort and validating the patient’s values without judgment. Tone and affirmations normalized difficulty and highlighted strengths throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, nuanced reflections captured both surface behavior and underlying value conflicts, which facilitated elaboration. Timely empathic listening demonstrated strong understanding of the patient’s perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on managing alcohol in social situations and linked questions and reflections back to the patient’s value of being present for his child. Progressively narrowed to a concrete, actionable plan and commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician and patient co‑constructed the plan step-by-step, with the clinician offering options and reflecting the patient’s responses. Language consistently invited the patient’s preferences, reinforcing mutual problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were offered and the patient was asked to choose what felt most doable, reinforcing ownership of the plan. The clinician respected the patient’s right to decide and supported his selected strategy (Mike + emoji).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician drew motivation from the patient by linking values (being a reliable dad) to concrete strategies and asking evocative questions. The patient generated the coping strategies and commitment, indicating strong evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert and invited collaboration through open questions and preference checks. Power was shared throughout, with the patient shaping each decision point.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was systematically elicited and reinforced via solution-focused and commitment questions, plus amplification of the patient’s prior successes. The clinician asked about observable outcomes and secured a specific, patient-generated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and neutrally normalized social pressures, which reduced defensiveness and shifted toward coping strategies. However, there was limited use of double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of barriers before moving to planning.
