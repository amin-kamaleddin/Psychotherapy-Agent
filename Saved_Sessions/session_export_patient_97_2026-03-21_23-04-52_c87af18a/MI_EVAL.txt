# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently positive, nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations and normalizing statements, avoiding blame and validating Mateo’s difficulties.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking captured values and ambivalence, but many reflections stayed at content level rather than probing deeper emotions or labeling underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, sustained focus on reducing risky behavior with concrete first-step planning (text, alarm, backup) and every intervention moved the plan forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently solicited Mateo’s input, followed his lead, and co-constructed a specific, actionable plan, positioning themselves as a partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was explicitly emphasized, the plan was framed as an adjustable experiment, and the clinician offered options without pressuring commitment, supporting client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited Mateo’s own reasons and concrete motivators linking day-to-day calm to long-term goals, though deeper probing of intrinsic values and eliciting a commitment statement would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language and behavior consistently reflected co-equal decision-making, offering options and letting Mateo select supports, demonstrating strong power-sharing in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively elicited and reinforced with concrete steps and goal-linked imagery, but could be amplified by probing importance/confidence and consolidating toward stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized and the clinician shifted to planning rather than debating, though more double-sided reflections could further soften sustain language and resolve ambivalence.
