# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validated Isabella's decisions and cultural context, and honored her limits (e.g., one‑week trial) conveying unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, perspective-taking reflections that captured ambivalence, but reflections were more cognitive/behavioral than deeper affective reflections exploring feelings like anxiety or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on alcohol use in pregnancy and moved logically toward a concrete, time-limited plan with clear follow-up while balancing exploration and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated in an equal-partner style by asking preferences, offering options, and adjusting the plan to Isabella's requests, which supported shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice by seeking permission before giving information, framing suggestions as optional, and honoring Isabella's final decisions about the one‑week trial and follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Isabella's own motivations and values and drew out concrete change steps, but could have probed deeper internal motives and used scaling to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered strong partnership and power-sharing by co-creating the plan, inviting input on next steps, and positioning both parties as collaborators.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk leading to a concrete one‑week trial, yet missed opportunities to elicit importance/confidence ratings and to selectively summarize the strongest change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used permission and balanced information to not reinforce sustain talk, but could have used double-sided reflections and elicited costs of sustaining the behavior to tip the balance more.
