# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard with specific, nonjudgmental reflections tied to Margaret’s values (independence, social life), reinforcing acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely complex and reflective statements that captured both content and ambivalence, inviting further exploration rather than shutting down the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing alcohol-related risk after the fall and progressively moved toward a concrete, time-limited plan while honoring client choice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as an equal partnership with co-planning, solicitation of specific supports and follow-up, and language positioning the clinician as helper and the patient as expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported Margaret’s sense of choice and control by asking which changes felt doable and offering options rather than pressuring for abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited intrinsic reasons for change (staying independent, easing son’s worry) and amplified the patient’s own statements to foster internally generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed decisions as shared and client-led, asking for input and co-creating concrete steps (alarms, Eileen, taxi) that Margaret named and owned.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change language, asked for small-change ideas, amplified tentative commitments, and helped convert abstract desire into actionable plans.
