# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect with affirmations of competence and normalization, without judgment or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, frequent reflections capturing both affect (relief, determination) and concrete meaning, showing strong attunement to the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reinstating supportive evenings and progressively moved from values to a concrete, time‑limited plan and commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the interaction as a genuine partnership by soliciting input, reflecting ideas, and co‑creating a manageable trial and script with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and ownership by offering options, inviting the client's wording and preferences, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the client's internal motivations and linked values (family calm, rest) to specific actions and benefits rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a joint enterprise, privileging the client's expertise and inviting her to lead decisions about what feels most manageable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, helped the client elaborate concrete, achievable steps, and supported formulation of a specific request to her husband.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and generally not reinforced, but the clinician could more often use double‑sided reflections or explicit summaries of both sides to further soften residual resistance and deepen commitment.
