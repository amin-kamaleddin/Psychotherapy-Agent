# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained unconditional positive regard and a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing Jordan's concerns without criticism. This consistent supportive stance justified a top-tier rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with reflective listening and well-tuned summaries that mirrored the patient's language and ambivalence. Empathic responses validated motivations and concerns consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on influenza vaccination with logical transitions from exploration to action planning. The clinician offered concrete decision-oriented interventions (pros/cons, scripts, booking steps) without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt genuinely collaborative—preferences were solicited, materials were adapted to Jordan's style, and decisions were co-created. The clinician invited the patient to lead choices and shaped the session around his input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed Jordan's freedom to choose and presented multiple options rather than directives. Language and framing were noncoercive, reinforcing ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked internal motivation (notably concern for grandparents) and tied it to action, but could have used more open-ended elicitation to deepen intrinsic motivation and draw out commitment language. More questions about importance/confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treated Jordan as an expert on his life, offering menus of options and asking him to choose wording that fit his vibe. Power-sharing and joint problem-solving were evident throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced change talk—especially the family-protection rationale—and provided concrete next steps to build momentum. Missed opportunities include more follow-up open questions and scaling prompts to amplify and sustain commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation, acknowledged sustain talk, and reframed benefits to others to redirect toward change. To further diminish sustain language, double-sided reflections and stronger tipping toward client values could have been used.
