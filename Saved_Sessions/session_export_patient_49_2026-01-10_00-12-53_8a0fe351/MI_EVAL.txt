# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental: normalized concerns, validated feelings, and affirmed choices without shaming. The clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate content-level reflections and matched the patient's tone, showing good understanding. Could deepen affective empathy by exploring emotional aspects of stress and social pressures more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with interventions tied directly to that target behavior. Moves (options, planning, rehearsal) were purposeful and aimed at a concrete one‑week behavioral experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine partnership by soliciting Hana's input, co-creating plans, and using rehearsal rather than prescribing. The clinician shared expertise while honoring the patient's contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control by honoring the patient's preference to cut down, offering multiple options, and asking permission before offering help. Language reinforced Hana's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intentions and some self-directed reasoning (importance scale, doable options), but relied frequently on clinician-provided solutions. Could increase evocative questioning to draw out intrinsic motives, values, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative with invitations to choose and co-create wording and plans; role-play demonstrated shared decision-making. The clinician facilitated Hana's expertise rather than taking over.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, produced actionable statements and a concrete plan. Could strengthen and amplify change talk with follow-ups that probe desire, reasons, and confidence (e.g., scaling follow‑ups).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward small achievable changes without arguing for the status quo. Additional use of double-sided reflections and explicit ambivalence acknowledgment would further soften sustain talk.
