# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates respectful, nonjudgmental acceptance and validates ambivalence well. Could add more explicit unconditional positive regard and additional affirmations of the patient's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening, summarizing the patient's split between social belonging and goals. Could strengthen by naming deeper emotions (pressure, fear of exclusion) and exploring what 'feeling left out' means.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on exiting situations with alcohol and consistently narrows toward concrete, near-term actions. Moves the patient toward specific implementation steps (pickup time, text shortcut, rehearsal).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative: the plan is co-authored, the patient selects wording and labels, and preferences are solicited at each step. The clinician lets the patient's input shape the session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering multiple options and asking which feels most doable, accepting incremental plans without pressure. Language consistently preserves choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations and concrete plans, drawing out doable steps the patient is willing to try. Could probe more for intrinsic values and assess confidence to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the expert and shares decision-making through menus and choice questions. The plan is co-constructed with the patient's wording and preferences central to the outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change-oriented language, converting options into specific commitments the patient will try. Could further amplify change talk by linking the plan to broader values/goals and asking confidence/importance questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates concerns about feeling left out and redirects to feasible, relationship-preserving solutions without arguing. Could use double-sided reflections to more gently shift sustain talk toward change.
