# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect with nonjudgmental language, validation, and explicit invitations to the patient's preferences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurately understood and reflected the patient's perspective and emotions (caution, need for safety), which supported engagement and trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on safe medication management and the phenelzine→SSRI transition, repeatedly returning to washout timing, ECG indications, and concrete steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative and egalitarian, with frequent invitations for input, co‑construction of wording, and offers to role‑play at the patient's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by asking permission, presenting options, and inviting the patient to choose which steps or rehearsals to pursue.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence; relied more on education and planning than on drawing out intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly fostered partnership and shared power through offers to listen first, repeated permission‑seeking, and treating the patient as an active collaborator in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete planning and commitment‑related talk via scripts and role‑play, but did not sufficiently prompt the patient to articulate personal reasons or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed risk concerns into practical safety steps, though missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections to further resolve ambivalence.
