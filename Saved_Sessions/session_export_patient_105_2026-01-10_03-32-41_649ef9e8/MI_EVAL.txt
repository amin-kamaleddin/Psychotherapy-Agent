# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent nonjudgmental warmth and validation (e.g., normalizing reactions and offering encouragement), demonstrating clear unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective and validating responses that acknowledged Naomi's experience, though reflections were often brief and the clinician moved quickly into advice rather than probing emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing on‑set anxiety and improving functioning, with each intervention tied directly to that target and concrete behaviorally specific steps provided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt largely collaborative with invitations to rehearse and choices offered, but the clinician often delivered techniques in an expert-driven way that slightly reduced equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician supported choice by offering options and asking preferences, but frequently recommended a specific prescriptive plan and schedule rather than explicitly emphasizing Naomi's control to adapt the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change statements were elicited (hope, intent to rehearse), but the clinician tended to supply solutions instead of systematically drawing out Naomi's own motives, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance was fostered through permission‑seeking and joint rehearsal, yet much content was expert-delivered so co‑generation of the plan was limited at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician created openings for change talk and Naomi expressed tentative change language, but did not consistently amplify or explore those statements with follow‑up questions to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confronting sustain talk and normalized doubts, but did not use strategies like double‑sided reflections or reframing to systematically soften sustain talk and explore costs/benefits of the status quo.
