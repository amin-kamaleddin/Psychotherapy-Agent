# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Jamal’s experience, normalizing slips and avoiding blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several accurate content and affect reflections and empathic moves were used, but the clinician missed opportunities to deepen exploration of Jamal’s ambivalence and emotional meaning of ‘for me.’

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained tightly focused on punctuality with every intervention tied to the target behavior and clear practical next steps (3–4 day trial, two‑week plan, check‑ins).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative with invitations to choose next steps and role‑play, though the clinician sometimes led with multiple concrete suggestions rather than eliciting Jamal’s own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported Jamal’s choice, offered options, and affirmed that not promising is acceptable, reinforcing Jamal’s ownership over change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Efforts to evoke motivation were present (planning, trials, scripts) but the clinician did not sufficiently draw out Jamal’s intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invited collaboration and co‑created scripts and role‑plays, yet supplied many prescriptive options without always asking permission or fully eliciting Jamal’s ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited and reinforced through planning and concrete steps, but the clinician could have used more open questions to deepen personal reasons and explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist acknowledged sustain talk and redirected toward change with trial framing and practical steps, though could use more double‑sided reflections and reframing to reduce resistance further.
