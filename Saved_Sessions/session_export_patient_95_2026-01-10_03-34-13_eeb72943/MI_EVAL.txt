# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (e.g., repeated “I believe you”), affirming disclosures and avoiding blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-level empathic responding with accurate reflections of intense suffering and emotional attunement, including offers to stay on the line and repeated validating language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on immediate suicidal risk and safety planning; interventions (means restriction, 988 call, grounding, texting sister, alarm) were tightly linked to preventing imminent self-harm.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—co-wrote the text, offered options, and stayed engaged with the patient—though necessary safety-boundary language occasionally reduced the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently affirmed choice and used invitational language while offering multiple options, but duty-to-warn and mandated-action statements constrained autonomy despite being handled transparently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited several practical commitments (texting, grounding, alarm) but did not consistently draw out intrinsic motivations or explore the patient’s stated values (e.g., caring for her sister) to deepen internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally maintained a collaborative stance and shared decision-making (offering to co-create a safety plan), though the clinician at times shifted to a more authoritative tone when invoking required actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked concrete invitation questions that produced small change behaviors, but missed opportunities to explore and amplify emerging change language (e.g., the patient’s comment about her sister).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated despair and avoided reinforcing sustain talk, but rarely used MI strategies (double-sided reflections, reframing) to explicitly soften resistance; some conditional safety language risked amplifying sustain talk.
