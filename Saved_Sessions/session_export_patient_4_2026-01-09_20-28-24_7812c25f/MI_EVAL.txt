# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validated the patient's concerns, and honored her request for investigations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections (apologizing, naming fears), but missed opportunities to deepen affective exploration of anxiety, tears, and impact of stopping work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on obtaining timely medical investigations and written confirmation, repeatedly returning to concrete actions (drafting, scripts, escalation).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited input, co‑created and iteratively refined the letter, and treated the patient as an equal partner in planning next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and ownership by offering options, refusing to send the message for her, and providing scripts/role‑play only if she wanted them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited concrete action steps (sending the message, calling, escalation) but did not sufficiently draw out the patient's internal motivations, values, or confidence for broader change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with permission‑seeking and co‑authorship of drafts, though at times the clinician shaped content more than fully eliciting the patient's own wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supported specific action language (planning, scripts) but did not actively elicit or amplify intrinsic motivation or confidence about broader behavioral or coping changes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain talk while providing practical steps, but missed opportunities to use double‑sided reflections or reframing to gently shift toward change‑oriented language.
