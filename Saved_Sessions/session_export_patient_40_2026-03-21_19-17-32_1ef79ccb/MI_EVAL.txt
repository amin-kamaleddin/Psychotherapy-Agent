# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validated distress, and respected the patient's limits and language preferences throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, matched the patient's affect, and checked safety compassionately while validating emotions and improvements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on relapse risk, means safety, and concrete coping strategies, progressing logically from assessment to a usable safety plan and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-created the safety plan by soliciting the patient's input, incorporated his phrasing and preferences, and repeatedly checked for edits, treating him as a co-author rather than issuing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by asking permission, offering options, avoiding pressure, and emphasizing editable, patient-owned plans and wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical, actionable material for safety planning but did not consistently draw out deeper intrinsic motivators or systematically expand the patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as an expert on his life, devolved control in planning decisions, and honored his preferences (e.g., wording choices), demonstrating strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced and amplified some change talk (e.g., congratulating three months) but missed opportunities to use open, evocative questions and scaling to deepen motivation and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledged uncertainty, but did not strategically use double-sided reflections or explore ambivalence to gently tip toward change.
