# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician conveys consistent nonjudgmental acceptance and normalizes Jason's frustration, which helped him feel heard and plan a step. However, reflections sometimes feel formulaic and repetitive, reducing individualized warmth and nuance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrates accurate, empathic listening and summarizes Jason's concerns and consequences well. Reflections tend to be surface-level and repetitive rather than deepening to explore underlying emotions or nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on addressing the conduct incident and protecting housing, steering toward concrete, achievable steps. The clinician consistently converts ambivalence into a specific behavioral plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is a collaborative problem-solving exchange with options offered and decisions co-constructed, and the client's input shaping the plan. The clinician effectively balances expertise and client perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician regularly affirms Jason's right to choose, offers multiple options, and asks which step feels most doable, respecting his agency. There is no coercion and choices are left with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Jason's motivations, strengths, and readiness, producing actionable steps like a script and call. More probing into values, longer-term goals, and the meaning of housing/scholarships could have strengthened intrinsic motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician adopts a genuine partnership stance, inviting Jason's ideas and preferences and co-creating the plan (script, call, roommate backup). Power is shared and the client's expertise about his life is respected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces change-oriented language using options, strengths, and scaling, producing specific change talk and intentions. Opportunities were missed to amplify, summarize, and convert that talk into firmer commitment and a specific timeline.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully avoids arguing and rolls with defenses, shifting focus to protecting priorities which reduces sustain talk's power. More active use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing would help resolve ambivalence about socializing versus risks.
