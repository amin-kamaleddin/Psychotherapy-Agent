# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, honors boundaries and privacy, normalizes missed days, and validates the patient's nervousness throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures frustration and loneliness, but reflections tend to stay at a surface level rather than exploring deeper emotional meaning or impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on weight-management and a concrete, time-limited experiment with contingency plans, though the clinician could link loneliness/family support more directly to behavior-change strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative throughout, inviting the patient's input at each decision point and adapting the plan responsively, reflecting genuine joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, affirming control over sharing and pausing, and emphasizing that missed days are fine and the patient decides what to share.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical commitments and preferences (meal photos, summaries, cue word) but stops short of probing intrinsic motivation, values, or past successes to deepen internal drive.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shares power and co-creates decisions, asking which options feel doable and honoring the patient's choices in forming the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-related language and frames the plan as an experiment, but misses opportunities to deepen reasons for change, elicit importance/confidence, and amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently acknowledges and reframes sustain talk and offers alternatives and privacy protections to reduce defensiveness, though could use more double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
