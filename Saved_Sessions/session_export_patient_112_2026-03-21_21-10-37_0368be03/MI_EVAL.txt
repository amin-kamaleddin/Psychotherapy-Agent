# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalizing language from the outset, avoiding labeling or shame. The clinician validated the patient's struggle throughout, creating a warm, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate naming of emotions and practical concerns. Could deepen empathy by more fully exploring the patient's identity-related comment ("part of who I am") to capture meaning and loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation and linked questions/interventions to the target behavior. Successfully moved ambivalence toward a time-bound action plan with concrete next steps and barrier checks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed: the clinician elicited and built on the patient's ideas and jointly created supports and plans. Decisions and tools were developed together, reflecting equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and inviting the patient to set the start date and supports. No pressuring language; autonomy was reinforced repeatedly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operated from an evocation stance by eliciting the patient's own reasons, resources, and confidence levels. Could increase use of strategic open questions to link values and long-term goals more explicitly to the quitting plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong power-sharing through permission-seeking, collaborative language, and co-creation of messages and reminders. The clinician acted as a partner rather than imposing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, asked for small doable steps, and used scaling to assess confidence. Missed opportunities to more consistently amplify and roll with the patient's stronger change statements to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated sustain talk and often redirected toward manageable change steps, using double-sided reflections. Could further weaken sustain talk by more frequently summarizing discrepancies between values/health concerns and perceived benefits of smoking.
