# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit, repeated statements of unconditional positive regard (e.g., “I accept you exactly where you are,” “I accept that fully”) and nonjudgmental validation across multiple turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, affect-focused reflections and summaries (“pulled between wanting peace and being observed,” “you’re tired and you just want space”) used in multiple turns to match minimal patient responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on a concrete target (short trial quiet hour, fridge note, caseworker verbal agreement). Occasionally repeated reflections delayed a single concise summary of next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative language and shared decision-making (“we could,” “I want to figure this out with you,” “I’ll back whatever plan you pick”), inviting patient preferences for plan details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit support for patient choice and control (“You know your life best… I want to figure this out with you, not for you”), honoring preferences and low‑drama approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out patient’s reasons and plans (quiet hour as what “actually helps,” trial week, fridge note, caseworker statement) through targeted open questions and reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership language and joint problem-solving (co‑creating a trial week, offering to help draft the line to be read aloud) that positioned clinician and patient as collaborators.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple expressions of change talk (less on edge, better sleep, specific steps) but did not consistently convert these into an explicit commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient doubts were acknowledged and validated (“It makes total sense you’d worry they’ll ignore it”) which avoided confrontation, but sustain talk was not actively explored/neutralized or reframed into confidence-building evidence.
