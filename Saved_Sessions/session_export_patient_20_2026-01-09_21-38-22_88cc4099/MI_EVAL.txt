# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a warm, nonjudgmental tone, honoring the patient's desire not to quit while supporting harm reduction. Language normalized ambivalence and avoided labeling or confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate reflections and summaries that showed understanding of the patient's perspective and constraints. Deeper affective reflections about emotions or family tensions were limited, keeping many responses at the content/behavior level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing drinking/harm and moved logically to a specific, measurable action plan. Each turn advanced toward concrete steps, confidence ratings, and follow-up supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt like joint problem-solving with solicitation of preferences and co-construction of plans (e.g., drafting the text to Mike). The clinician could have invited the patient's ideas earlier and used a summary endorsed by the patient to strengthen shared ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly asked permission, offered multiple options, and reinforced the patient's right to choose, avoiding prescriptive language. The patient's choices (days, NA choice, who to tell) were honored throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited client-generated change language through choices, confidence ratings, and action planning that produced commitment to specific steps. However, evocation focused more on logistics than exploring personal values, past successes, or deeper motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invited collaboration and co-created the plan by offering options and asking which felt easiest, sharing power in decision-making. More explicit elicitation of the patient's expertise about his contexts and a patient-led summary would have enhanced partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and shaped change talk using low-effort options, choice, and confidence scaling, yielding concrete commitments (Tue/Thu, NA drinks). To reach a top score, the clinician could have amplified and probed the patient's own reasons and values for change more frequently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing with sustain talk, acknowledged perceived benefits of drinking, and redirected toward harm-reduction strategies using permission language. Use of more double-sided reflections naming both the pull to drink and its costs would further soften sustain talk.
