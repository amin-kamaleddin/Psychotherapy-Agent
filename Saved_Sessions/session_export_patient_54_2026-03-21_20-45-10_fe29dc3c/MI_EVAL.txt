# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, offering unconditional positive regard and normalizing the patient's autonomy and cultural values throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking were evident, though the clinician could have lingered more on affective nuances (anxiety, guilt) with a few deeper emotion-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use in pregnancy and moved purposefully from values exploration to concrete planning, culminating in a specific first step (putting wine away tonight).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: the clinician solicited the patient's preferences, co-created brochures and scripts, and tailored interventions based on the patient's input and choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly supported with options rather than directives, repeated invitations to choose, and language that reinforced the patient's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully drew out motivation by highlighting values and inviting concrete commitments, but could have used more targeted evocation (rulers, pros/cons, patient summaries) to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership was evident through shared decision-making, asking which phrasing felt natural, and offering role‑play and coaching based on the patient's requests.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was actively evoked and reinforced (planning, rehearsal, specific steps), though more explicit elicitation of desire/ability/reasons/confidence would have helped stack and amplify change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validated sustain talk and avoided argument, reflecting both sides and gently shifting to planning, but could further soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections and emphasis on costs of staying the same.
