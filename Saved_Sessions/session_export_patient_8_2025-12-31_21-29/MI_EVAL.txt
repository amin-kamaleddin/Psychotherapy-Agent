# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence (e.g., “That makes sense,” “I’ll support you without judgment,” “slips are part of change”). No shaming/blaming; warmth meets highest standard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries of mixed feelings (“You’re torn…”, “that makes a lot of sense…”). Missed opportunities to probe deeper into the emotional texture (e.g., the anxiety/restlessness could be explored with feeling‑laden reflections).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear behavioral focus from start to finish and guided toward a concrete, measurable experiment (phone across room 11:30, DND, 11:20 alarm, friend notified). Balanced pacing without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited patient input at each decision point (“Which sounds most doable to you?”), co‑created the plan, and adapted to patient preferences (text vs call; agreed response style if she slips).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and control, honored Amina’s preferences, and avoided prescriptive language — fostered patient ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons, scaling, practical supports and barriers; repeatedly drew out patient’s own motivations and strategies. Could deepen by eliciting values/consequences (e.g., impact on grades/identity) to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Amina as the expert on what will work, invited her ideas and preferences, and demonstrated genuine power‑sharing in planning and follow‑up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out and reinforced change talk (scales, concrete steps, summaries); patient’s change talk increased over the session. Missed chances to amplify change statements and to elicit explicit commitment language near the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and reflected the benefits of phone use while noting costs to sleep/focus. Could further soften sustain talk using double‑sided reflections and more strategic elicitation of patient’s own arguments for change.
