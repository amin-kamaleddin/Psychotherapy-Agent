# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Marco's struggle with frequent, specific validations that lowered defensiveness. Minor opportunity to personalize affirmations further (e.g., name a specific effort Marco has made).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with several complex reflections that helped Marco open up. Reflections were occasionally repetitive and could have probed deeper into the emotional meaning of guilt and low energy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, goal-directed focus on sustainable dietary/lifestyle changes and iteratively narrows to concrete, achievable steps. Questions consistently tied back to behavior change and confidence, producing an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine co‑construction of the plan—asking Marco to choose rules, reminder wording, and timing—while following his lead. Language and concrete co‑planning fostered an equitable therapeutic stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently preserves Marco's sense of choice with invitations to pick what feels doable and nonprescriptive phrasing. Normalizing setbacks and emphasizing decision ownership supported autonomy and reduced pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Marco's internal motivations and resources (energy for kids, past soda success) and drew out specific supports. Could more systematically build a chain of change talk and invite clearer commitment language or coping plans for high‑risk moments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Marco as the expert and solicits his input, resulting in shared decision‑making and acceptance of his proposed plan. These moves demonstrate consistent power‑sharing and genuine partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited concrete change talk (no soda, water‑before‑snack rule, walking) and drew on past success and specific rules. Could strengthen by selectively amplifying and summarizing change statements and eliciting more explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it and gently redirected to change‑focused options, validating difficulty and normalizing ambivalence. Would benefit from more strategic use of double‑sided reflections or targeted reframes to tip the balance toward change.
