# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., validating statements, asking permission to share info). Avoids labeling or shaming and normalizes the patient's perspective throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate summarizing of motives and barriers, capturing content and practical concerns. Could deepen affective reflections to capture emotional nuance (e.g., the pull of unwinding or loyalty to peers).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on tobacco use and harm‑reduction, systematically progressing toward a concrete, testable experiment. Avoids drifting and moves the conversation toward specific behavioral steps and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the interaction as a joint enterprise using inclusive language and repeatedly invites the patient's preferences and choices. Decisions and the plan are co‑constructed, with the patient selecting the gum plan, decline lines, and timing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and framing the plan as the patient's decision. Reinforces that the clinician will support whatever the patient chooses.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits the patient's own reasons, concerns, and resources, drawing out change talk and practical solutions. Uses the patient's language to build motivation and translate it into a specific plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—asks permission, offers to brainstorm together, and positions the patient as co‑author of the plan. Repeatedly invites input and shapes options with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and strengthens change talk through open questions, eliciting specific plans, and asking for a confidence rating and a step to raise it. Converts change language into actionable small experiments and commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and responds neutrally to minimization, providing brief factual info and pivoting to harm‑reduction; uses double‑sided reflection later. Could apply more immediate, strategic softening (earlier double‑sided reflections, amplifying brief change talk) to further reduce sustain talk's pull.
