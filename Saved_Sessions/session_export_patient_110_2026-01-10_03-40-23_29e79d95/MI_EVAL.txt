# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating worries and offering practical help (drafting questions, printable PDF). The warm, supportive stance was sustained throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate, reassuring reflections and tied content to values (staying active with his wife), demonstrating clear understanding. Missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration of the meaning of staying active and specific fears about losing function.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on cholesterol management and starting atorvastatin, with every intervention linked to that goal. The clinician resisted drifting and consistently guided the conversation toward actionable steps (PDF, monitoring plan, clinic questions).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was mutual and co-constructed—invited preferences for content, filename, and delivery, and suggested bringing his wife for support. The clinician positioned himself as a helper and adapted to Richard’s concerns rather than imposing decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Richard's sense of choice using permission language and offering options for the PDF and next steps. Also cautioned about not stopping medication without consulting the doctor while honoring Richard's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some of Richard's motivations (retirement/activity) and used open questions and rehearsal to draw out practical reasons for adherence. Opportunities were missed to more actively evoke intrinsic reasons, build discrepancy, and assess confidence or specific next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a strong collaborative stance by asking permission, co-creating the printout, and inviting final input on details like filename and clinic phone. Offers to rehearse questions and bring his wife demonstrated genuine power-sharing and respect for the patient's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported and amplified Richard's change talk by reinforcing his commitment and creating concrete tools (question list, PDF). Could have more consistently elicited deeper desire, ability, reasons, and confidence language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized ambivalence while focusing on pragmatic supports like monitoring and the info sheet. Could increase use of double-sided reflections and explicit acknowledgment of the pull toward the status quo to strategically move ambivalence toward change.
