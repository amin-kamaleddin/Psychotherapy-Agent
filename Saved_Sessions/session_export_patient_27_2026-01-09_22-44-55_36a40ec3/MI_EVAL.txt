# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized Daniela’s protective stance, and affirmed her goals without judgment. No instances of blaming or minimizing were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately and sensitively captured Daniela’s ambivalence, fears, and storyteller identity, tracking affect and inviting elaboration. Linking strengths to identity demonstrated nuanced understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on disclosure planning with concrete preparatory actions (script, friend choice, roleplay). Questions and reflections repeatedly brought the conversation back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician used a co‑creative approach—offering roleplay, modeling, wording tweaks, and checking preferences—so the plan was shaped jointly. Daniela’s choices guided the session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and control over timing and scope, avoided pressuring language, and invited Daniela to set the pace. Options were offered at every decision point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited reasons, resources, and past successes to draw out motivation, but could more directly probe importance/confidence and elicit explicit commitment language. Deeper elicitation of intrinsic motives would strengthen readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned Daniela as the expert, invited her preferences, and consistently asked which option she preferred, demonstrating genuine partnership. Adaptations to her pace reinforced shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change‑focused, choice‑oriented prompts and affirmations elicited practical change talk (writing a sentence, roleplaying, choosing Maya). Missed opportunities remain to amplify and consolidate these statements into stronger desire/ability/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and legitimized without arguing, which respected Daniela’s protection‑seeking. The clinician sometimes mirrored sustain talk rather than using double‑sided reflections or immediately pivoting to the change side to reduce its influence.
