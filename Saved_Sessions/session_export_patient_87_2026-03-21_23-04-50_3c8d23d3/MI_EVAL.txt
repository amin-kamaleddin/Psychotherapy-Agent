# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent validations that reduce blame, but some validations feel formulaic and could better normalize setbacks and systemic pressures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking and summarizes goals well, though reflections are sometimes surface-level and could probe deeper emotions and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on specific target behaviors and near-term action steps, effectively narrowing broad goals into concrete tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is cooperative with frequent solicitation of the patient's ideas and shared problem solving, but could more explicitly invite the patient to set the agenda and attribute ownership of plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by repeatedly asking what feels doable and avoiding pressure, reinforcing the patient's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the patient's motivations and past successes with values-based questions, but could deepen evocation by exploring underlying values and using scaling follow-ups.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and invites co-creation of plans, yet sometimes structures the agenda rather than explicitly negotiating it or summarizing shared decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces practical change talk and elicits concrete steps, but misses opportunities to amplify, summarize, and strengthen commitment or follow up on confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and avoids confronting sustain talk, but largely mirrors it rather than using double-sided reflections, reframes, or pivots that would diminish its pull and evoke reasons for change.
