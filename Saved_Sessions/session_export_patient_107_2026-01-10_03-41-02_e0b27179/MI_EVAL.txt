# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental, validating stance (warm language, normalizing slips). No shaming or minimizing of the patient's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with reflective listening and attention to symptoms and experience. Reflections are mostly simple and intermittent, with missed opportunities for more complex or amplified reflections to explore ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on coffee consumption with specific screening, concrete strategies, and a time-limited experiment. Action-oriented guidance was delivered without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed: tools were negotiated, a phone-friendly log was co-created, and plans were agreed together. Patient was treated as an equal contributor throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's choice and offered options, asking permission for formats and framing plans as experiments. This consistently reinforced the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations and preferences and invited experimentation to generate internal evidence. Missed deeper elicitation of intrinsic motives, values, and discrepancy that would strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership evident with shared decision-making, multiple options offered, and honoring of patient preferences. The clinician consistently invited collaboration and positioned the patient as the decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk (monitoring, swaps, one-week trial) and elicited some change statements. However, the clinician did not consistently evoke or reinforce the patient's reasons, desire, ability, or commitment language and missed open, evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected in a neutral, supportive way while validating the ritual and proposing small experiments. Could more strategically use double-sided reflections or reframes to gently tip ambivalence toward change.
