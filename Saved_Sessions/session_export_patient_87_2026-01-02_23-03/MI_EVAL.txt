# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates respect and validates Marco’s reasons and feelings (“You’ve named important reasons…”, “That sounds like a solid, controllable cue — nice choice”). Affirmations are supportive but somewhat formulaic; could be more personalized (e.g., naming persistence or past efforts) to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (noting role‑model goals, visible food at home, shame/need for a quick win). Reflections are accurate but largely surface‑level; more complex, affect‑focused reflections would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on late‑night snacking and progression toward a measurable plan (tonight at ~8:25–8:30: alarms, toothbrush/book by door, “teeth now,” brush then read 20 min; observable next‑day signs). No drift; pacing and sequencing are strategic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session offered choices and invited patient input (options for cues, which tweak feels doable), avoiding coercion. Interaction is collaborative overall though occasionally leans toward directive planning rather than jointly consolidating commitment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician respects Marco’s preferences (e.g., not asking wife to change), provides options, and emphasizes controllable steps. Could further bolster autonomy by eliciting explicit commitment language and asking the patient to summarize the plan in his own words.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits Marco’s own reasons and specific strategies (concrete step choice, scaling, implementation details). Effective elicitation produced multiple change statements; missed opportunities to amplify and consolidate those statements into stronger commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Use of collaborative language (“I’ll celebrate…we’ll build from there”) and joint planning supports partnership. Interaction is warm but somewhat formulaic; naming specific patient strengths would strengthen the sense of partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple instances of change talk were elicited (commitment to try tonight, specific cues, movement from 6→7). Clinician could better consolidate and amplify change talk (reflect back change statements, elicit commitment language about repeating successes).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids arguing and validates sustain‑talk (“when I go home and I see the food, it’s gonna be really hard”), then pivots to manageable strategies and compassionate self‑lines. More deliberate double‑sided reflections and strategic weakening of sustain language would improve this domain.
