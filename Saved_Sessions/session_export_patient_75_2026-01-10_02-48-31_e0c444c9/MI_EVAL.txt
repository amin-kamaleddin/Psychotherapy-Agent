# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating Linda’s experience and avoiding blame while offering practical help.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections (e.g., validating anxiety and hoarseness), but missed deeper exploration of the emotional texture of Linda’s fears before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and inhaler concerns, steering the session toward actionable steps and relevant interventions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked jointly with Linda—co‑writing the script, rehearsing, and inviting her choices—framing the interaction as shared problem solving rather than a lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Linda’s control and offered choices (practice now vs later, which interventions to try), respecting her pace and reinforcing ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some planning and willingness to try strategies, but provided many solutions and did not systematically draw out Linda’s intrinsic reasons, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited Linda’s input and co‑created the plan, though there were moments of more directive problem solving before fully eliciting her priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supported some change talk and secured concrete commitments (e.g., spacer, log, breathing), but rarely used evocative questions or systematic amplification to deepen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and provided corrective information without judgment, but often shifted to advice rather than using strategic reflections or double‑sided summaries to resolve ambivalence.
