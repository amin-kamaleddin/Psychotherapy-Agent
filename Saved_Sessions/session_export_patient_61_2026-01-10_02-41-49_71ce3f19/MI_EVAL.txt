# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses unconditional positive regard and normalizes Jonathan's experience with multiple validations and nonjudgmental language, creating a warm, accepting stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and timely reflective listening by restating context and emotions, which appears to lower defensiveness and keep the patient engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence with concrete, behaviorally specific steps and summaries, avoiding drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—elicits preferences and co-constructs a plan—but frequently offers specific clinician-driven suggestions that tilt problem-solving toward the expert rather than equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly supports autonomy by offering choices, normalizing imperfection, and inviting the patient to decide on check-ins and strategies, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some action-oriented commitments but misses opportunities to draw out Jonathan's internal motivations, values, or confidence, relying more on logistics than personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively and asks for input, but the clinician's prescriptive instructions and many suggested options could be balanced with more elicitation of the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces tentative change talk and offers manageable options, but does not sufficiently explore or amplify deeper personal reasons for change to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing and redirects toward small steps and self-compassion, though could further soften sustain talk with more double-sided reflections and explicit validation linked to change.
