# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Richard's competing wishes and offering practical, supportive options without blame. Normalizes ambivalence and treats his preferences as legitimate throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates clear understanding of Richard's perspective with accurate content reflections and recognition of ambivalence. Some reflections are formulaic and miss deeper emotional undertones (e.g., anxiety about side effects or loss of independence).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a focused, consistent agenda on cholesterol management with concrete goals and timelines (diet/exercise plan, LDL targets, 3‑month check). Every turn moves toward a specific, actionable plan and decision criteria for medication.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is highly collaborative, soliciting Richard's views and co‑designing supports like meal plans and nurse check‑ins. Clinician offers options and responds to the patient's preferences, positioning him as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Richard's choice and control by offering options, asking preferences, and avoiding pressuring language. Reinforces that decisions (and supports) are his to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Richard's own motivations and values, linking change to retirement and staying active with his wife. Uses questions that draw out self‑generated reasons and concrete criteria for confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership evident through joint decision‑making and shared planning (e.g., setting targets, choosing which supports to arrange first). Clinician shares power and invites patient input at each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly elicits and reinforces specific change talk with concrete prompts for small, realistic steps and timelines. Patient articulates multiple commitment statements and actionable plans as a result.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes minimization neutrally while focusing on incremental steps and checkpoints. Could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections and more explicit elicitation of concerns about meds to further diminish sustain talk.
