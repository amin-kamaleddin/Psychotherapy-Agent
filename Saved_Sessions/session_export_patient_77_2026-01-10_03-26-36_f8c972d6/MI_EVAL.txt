# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes ambivalence, and avoids blame throughout the session. Could slightly strengthen by explicitly normalizing plateaus for adolescents.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist demonstrates strong, accurate empathic reflections and summaries that match the client's emotional tone and validate feelings. Occasional deeper affect-labeling (e.g., naming shame) could deepen connection further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior with a concrete, incremental plan (15‑minute walk, cues, measures of success). A brief confirmation of follow-up or how progress will be checked next time would be a useful refinement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is clearly collaborative, with the clinician inviting input and co-constructing the plan using inclusive language. Could add an explicit check about whether the client wants the clinician to follow up next session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supports choice and control by offering options, honoring limits, and avoiding pressure. An explicit closing statement reinforcing that the decision is the client's could further emphasize autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist effectively draws motivation from within the client by eliciting internal goals and past successes, producing meaningful change talk. Using a confidence ruler or asking the client to summarize the plan aloud could strengthen internally generated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treats the client as the expert and repeatedly asks which options feel most doable, fostering shared decision-making. Explicitly summarizing the co-created next steps in the client's own words would further cement the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicits and amplifies change talk with open questions, choice presentation, and reflections of past success and willingness. Intentionally eliciting a brief commitment statement or confidence/importance rating near the close could strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist skillfully acknowledges sustain talk using double-sided reflections and normalization, then pivots toward change without arguing. Could further erode sustain talk by juxtaposing it more directly with prior successes.
