# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validates Camila’s experience and works within her preference not to stop drinking completely. No shaming or labeling; affirms plan and normalizes nervousness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good understanding with accurate reflections of behavior and consequences and asks about doable steps. Could increase frequency of reflections that name feelings and ambivalence rather than shifting toward solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on reducing problematic drinking and repeatedly steers toward concrete harm-reduction strategies. Little topic drift and each exchange advances planning for the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-creates and iteratively revises a concrete plan with repeated solicitation of Camila’s preferences. Patient suggestions are incorporated directly into the materials she will use.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering multiple options, asking what feels doable, and accepting hedged commitments and wording edits. Advice is framed to preserve Camila’s ownership and avoid coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivation and commitment by inviting plan formation and doable steps, which brings out change talk. Could deepen evocation by asking open questions about values, reasons, and discrepancy to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the work as co-constructed, asking permission, offering choices, and adapting wording per Camila’s requests. Demonstrates genuine power-sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and an importance statement and invites Camila to choose and refine a plan. Does not consistently amplify or deepen change talk with follow-ups about reasons, importance, confidence, or desired outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids directly reinforcing sustain talk and offers harm-reduction alternatives, but does limited explicit work to reframe or resolve ambivalence. Could use double-sided reflections and strategic links between sustain and change language.
