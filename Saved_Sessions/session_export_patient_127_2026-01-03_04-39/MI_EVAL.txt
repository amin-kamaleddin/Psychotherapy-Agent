# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Acceptance was clearly demonstrated (unconditional positive regard, nonjudgmental tone)
**Comments:** Repeated normalizing statements and affirmations (“That’s painful and makes sense…”, “You’re not defined by mistakes”, “Those 22 days … are real strengths”). Supportive, respectful tone across multiple turns (6–8 instances).

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Empathic reflections and perspective-taking present
**Comments:** Accurate paraphrases of ambivalence, fear, and pride; reflections captured content and some affect. Reflections felt somewhat formulaic (frequent repetition of “22 days …”) with missed opportunities to deepen exploration of shame/identity (“people like me are unaccepted”).

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Clear focus and purposeful guidance toward change goals
**Comments:** Consistently guided toward concrete, doable actions (calling Fiona, job‑centre appointment, PO check‑in, rehearsal). Avoided drift and linked steps to motives (protecting relationship, steady work).

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Collaborative, co‑constructed planning and shared decision making
**Comments:** Therapist invited choices (which step to prioritise), asked what would make follow‑through doable, offered help conditional on patient preference. Multiple exchanges demonstrate genuine partnership.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Supported client agency and choice; no coercion
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Timur’s choice and agency, provided options and let him prioritize (e.g., PO check‑in first). Language preserved client ownership of the plan.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicited motivations and reasons for change
**Comments:** Good questions eliciting concrete change talk and plans (“what matters most…?”, “what small, realistic step…?”, “what one line…?”). Could deepen by probing values/long‑term goals and using scaling or “what would that mean for you?” prompts.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Explicitly treated client as expert and collaborator
**Comments:** Therapist deferred to Timur’s judgment, invited his preferences, and offered supports only after eliciting his choice (rehearsal vs. PO check‑in). Consistently fostered client expertise.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Successfully elicited and reinforced some change talk
**Comments:** Reflections and affirmations reinforced “22 days,” booked appointment, intent to call Fiona. Missed opportunities to amplify/summarize sequences of change talk, use scaling (confidence/importance), or secure a firmer explicit commitment/implementation intention.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Avoided confrontation and redirected sustain talk; acknowledged fears
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain‑talk elements (“chicken out”, feeling “unaccepted”) and reframed strengths; relied mainly on neutral reflections and practical supports. Could more actively use double‑sided reflections and selective emphasis to downweight sustain talk and plan coping strategies for temptation.
