# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating the patient's fatigue and avoiding moralizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used timely reflections and perspective-taking that helped the patient feel heard, though deeper, more complex reflections exploring underlying emotions could strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on relevant target behaviors and purposefully transitioned topics (smoking → boundary scripts → harm reduction → stress skills), even when the patient resisted quitting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited role-play, asked the patient to choose strategies, and adjusted interventions to the patient's stated preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission before advice, and respecting refusals and low‑pressure experiments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some evocation techniques (asking about exhaustion, offering small experiments) but did not sufficiently draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as a collaborative effort and co‑constructed a realistic plan, emphasizing the patient's expertise and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered low‑pressure harm‑reduction options and small experiments, but did not systematically elicit or amplify the patient's own desire, reasons, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and respectfully honored the patient's reluctance while offering alternatives that softened sustain talk; could more often use double‑sided reflections or discrepancy techniques.
