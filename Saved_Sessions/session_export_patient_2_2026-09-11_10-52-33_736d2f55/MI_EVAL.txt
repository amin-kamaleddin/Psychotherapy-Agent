# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, validating the client’s priorities and choices without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and perspective-taking about practical barriers, but reflections rarely name deeper emotions (e.g., guilt, worry) or ambivalence that would deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on evening smoking reduction and consistently moves the conversation toward concrete implementation steps and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly a joint problem‑solving process: the clinician repeatedly invites the patient’s solutions and co‑constructs decisions rather than imposing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, respecting limits, and reinforcing the patient’s ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client’s internal reasons for change and readiness and translates those motives into actionable steps without supplying the motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative and client‑led, with the clinician framing questions to invite the patient’s expertise and deferring to her preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk, leading the patient to articulate concrete change language and commitment‑oriented planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and elicits fallback strategies, but uses few targeted techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk and shift ambivalence.
