# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used a warm, nonjudgmental tone and consistently affirmed the patient's perspective, praising insight and strengths. Acceptance was continuous and explicit with no shaming or minimizing remarks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good basic reflective listening, normalizing, and acknowledgement of ambivalence and anxiety. Missed opportunities for deeper emotion-focused reflections about the patient's anxiety and family history that would increase empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing risky drinking and improving contraceptive reliability. Every intervention tied back to those targets and moved the patient from risk identification to concrete planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-created the action plan, invited role-play, and followed the patient's lead on rehearsal and check-ins. At times the clinician supplied long lists of tips without prefacing them with permission, which reduced the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and reinforcing the patient's ownership of the plan. Language avoided coercion and emphasized the patient's choice about strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited patient motivations (family history, contraception concerns) and used them to build strategies. Missed opportunities to explore values, deeper meaning, and confidence to more fully evoke internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with invitations for input and shared decision-making, including role-play and mutual planning. Early directive delivery of detailed advice without asking permission sometimes shifted the dynamic away from equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced change talk through planning, praise, and rehearsal, linking reduction in drinking to contraception reliability. More strategic open questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and explicit commitment language would strengthen elicitation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and shifted focus to practical protective strategies and rehearsal to reduce its power. Did not routinely use MI techniques like double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence and tip toward change.
