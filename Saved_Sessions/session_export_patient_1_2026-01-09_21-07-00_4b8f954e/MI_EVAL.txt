# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through explicit acceptance, affirmations of strengths, and nonjudgmental language, normalizing the patient’s position rather than criticizing it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and validation of the patient’s concerns about work, family, and symptoms.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on protecting throat/gums and reducing mini-cigar use, repeatedly bringing the conversation to concrete, time-limited, actionable goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—invites the patient’s input at every turn, practices the patient’s proposed language, and co-constructs supports and the plan rather than imposing it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes patient choice and control, accepts the patient’s readiness level, and offers options while reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s reasons and values and elicits concrete steps, but misses opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation (e.g., no confidence/importance scaling or exploration of past successes).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a genuine partnership stance by treating the patient as the expert, asking for preferences, and co-creating the plan with the patient’s contributions shaping decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk by highlighting noticed benefits and asking targeted questions that lead to explicit change statements and a concrete commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without argumentation—reflects minimization, uses double-sided reflections, and pivots to small experiments and benefits to reduce focus on reasons to stay the same.
