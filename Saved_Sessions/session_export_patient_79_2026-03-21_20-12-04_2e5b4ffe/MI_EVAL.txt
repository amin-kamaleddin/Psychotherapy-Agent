# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance with explicit statements and normalization of ambivalence, reducing threat and affirming the patient's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, affect-focused reflections and perspective-taking (including responses to sarcasm/defensiveness) that validated the patient's experience and facilitated engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on simplifying asthma management and improving preventer use, keeping the session goal-directed with a concrete one-week follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan with the patient, asked permission to help, offered options, and honored the patient's agenda, demonstrating genuine joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering reversible options, emphasizing no judgment for misses, and checking how the patient wanted follow-up organized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motives, barriers, and confidence rating, but could have more systematically linked inhaler use to personally meaningful outcomes and amplified intrinsic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a clear partnership by deferring to the patient's preferences, offering choices, and engaging the patient in setting the plan and backup strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited a specific, doable step and scaled confidence while reinforcing commitment, though there were missed opportunities to amplify the patient's expressed efforts and connect the plan to valued outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation, used reflective, nonconfrontational language, and normalized misses to reduce pressure, but could have applied more strategic double-sided reflections to further shift sustain talk.
