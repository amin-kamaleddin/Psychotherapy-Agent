# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful tone with empathic labels and a calm safety check, and honored Lars’s choices (e.g., not involving Donna), communicating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed clear perspective-taking and reflective listening (e.g., empathic label, safety check) but reflections were more summary/practical and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions like guilt, shame, or pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on concrete targets (polytech forms, Friday risk, immediate safety) and produced specific, actionable plans and scripts to address those goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine collaboration by co-creating messages and plans through back-and-forth edits, inviting input, and negotiating decisions rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Lars’s sense of choice and control by asking priorities, offering multiple options, and adapting language and scripts to his preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some priorities and motivations (car course, Friday worries) but often shifted quickly to offering solutions instead of using open, evocative questions to deepen Lars’s own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered a partnership through iterative tailoring and responsiveness, though some early exchanges were more clinician-led (long lists of steps before exploring Lars’s priorities).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change-oriented content but did not consistently use evocative follow-ups or amplify desire, reasons, ability, and commitment language to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered useful practical alternatives to sustain talk (avoid mates, alarms, backups) but rarely used empathic techniques (double-sided or validating reflections) to acknowledge and gently reframe sustain talk before moving to solutions.
