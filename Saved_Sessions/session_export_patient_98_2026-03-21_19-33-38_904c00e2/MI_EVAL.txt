# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed warm, nonjudgmental acceptance with validations and normalizing language. They avoided blame and repeatedly affirmed Mateo's feelings and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were multiple accurate reflections of Mateo's feelings and concerns, acknowledging fear and anticipatory anxiety. The clinician could deepen affective connection with more complex, emotion-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on the target behavior (10-minute safe activity) with concrete planning and safety rules. Every suggestion and question tied back to the specific goal and risk management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interactions were co-constructed: the clinician proposed options and Mateo selected what felt doable, with invitations to problem-solve and check in. The tone and planning consistently treated the patient as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirmed Mateo's choice and control by offering options, endorsing shorter alternatives, and emphasizing that stopping is okay. Questions invited Mateo to choose timing and whether to have check-ins, avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Mateo's motivations and barriers through doable-option questions, timing, and confidence ratings. Missed opportunities remain to probe deeper into personal values and intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited input and emphasized shared planning and problem-solving, while setting appropriate medical limits. At times the approach leaned toward directive instruction rather than explicitly negotiating shared expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited and reinforced change talk with concrete options, commitment questions, and confidence ratings, and rewarded Mateo's commitments. They could further amplify and summarize Mateo's own change language and reasons for continuing rehab.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized without arguing, then redirected to manageable options and safety rules. Using more double-sided reflections to explicitly contrast concerns and commitment could soften sustain talk further.
