# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing struggle and gently challenging self‑criticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, validating ambivalence and attending to emotions and small pleasures like the tea ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on staying clean and relapse prevention with concrete coping strategies and specific, achievable goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative—offering options, soliciting the client's choice, and editing messages together—but frequent ready-made scripts and directive suggestions slightly reduce equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the client's sense of choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing the client's decision-making without pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation and a concrete commitment (sending the message) and highlights internal motivators, but could probe deeper into values, importance, and confidence to strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally conveys that expertise resides with the client and invites collaboration, though occasional prescriptive moments (providing scripts) limit fully co‑created wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk (celebrating 22 days, small rewards, committing to a step) but could use more open invitations to amplify desire, reasons, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids directly reinforcing sustain talk and offers alternatives, but misses opportunities for strategic MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, exploring competing values), resulting in inconsistent softening.
