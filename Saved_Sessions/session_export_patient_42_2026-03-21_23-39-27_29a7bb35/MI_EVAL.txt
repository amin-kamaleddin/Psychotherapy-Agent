### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Elise’s experience, avoiding criticism and validating ambivalence. Affirms her ideas and accepts mixed feelings without pressuring or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides accurate, nuanced reflections and summaries that capture both ambivalence and emotional concerns about belonging and teasing. Uses frequent reflective language and checks meaning, demonstrating strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with a specific, actionable first step (one-break plan). Balances exploration of values with pragmatic goal-setting and repeatedly steers the conversation toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as an equal partnership: the clinician solicits Elise’s ideas, responds, and incorporates her lived experience into the plan. Co-creates realistic steps and offers practical coaching in a collaborative way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by offering multiple options and asking which feels most doable, avoiding pressuring language. Reinforces Elise’s right to choose pacing and frames the plan as her decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations, prior strategies, and concrete supports, repeatedly drawing out reasons for change. Could deepen intrinsic motivation further by exploring core values, using rulers, and eliciting a stronger commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treats Elise as the expert and invites her input, shaping decisions together rather than directing. Co-constructs the plan (one break, gum, telling Jenna) with language that centers her preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces change talk, amplifying small wins and asking choice questions that invite commitment. Missed opportunities remain to deepen commitment via confidence/importance ratings and stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and pivots to pragmatic small steps that reduce resistance. Could more often use explicit double-sided reflections and reframing to artfully contrast sustain and change reasons.
