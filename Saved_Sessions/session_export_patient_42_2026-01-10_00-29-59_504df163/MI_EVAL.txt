# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance through frequent validation and empathic language, fostering trust and disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, mirroring the patient's language and integrating feelings and content with effective summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on smoking reduction within the social context and progressively narrows to concrete, actionable options and a first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as an equal partnership with the clinician soliciting the patient's ideas, letting those ideas guide the plan, and co-constructing next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control using invitational, noncoercive questions and validation, avoiding pressure or unsolicited directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits personally meaningful reasons and client-generated steps but could further deepen evocation by eliciting confidence, past successes, or a verbal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the work as collaborative, asking priorities and sharing power so the patient's preferences shape the plan rather than imposing expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk and concrete strategies well, though there were missed opportunities to more explicitly amplify desire/reason/need and build toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully normalizes and redirects sustain talk toward alternatives that preserve connection, but could more actively use double-sided reflections or summarize sustain vs. change to reduce pull.
