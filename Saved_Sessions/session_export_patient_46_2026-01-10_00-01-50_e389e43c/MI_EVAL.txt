# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized ambivalence, and avoided judgment, creating a safe atmosphere for a teen discussing risky behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of ambivalence and concerns, though emotional nuance (affect/fear/frustration) was explored less deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on drinking and immediate, actionable goals, repeatedly steering toward harm-reduction planning and skills practice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with joint planning and checks for permission, but the clinician sometimes supplied many solutions rather than eliciting more of the client's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently honored choice and control by offering options, asking permission before advice, and validating the patient's preference not to quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully elicited change talk and alternatives from the patient, but could have deepened intrinsic motivation by exploring values, importance, and confidence more strategically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician largely treated the patient as a collaborator and operationalized his ideas, though frequent clinician-provided tactics slightly shifted power toward expert direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and strengthened practical change talk and planning (role-play, concrete limits), but missed opportunities to amplify expressed dislike of consequences or assess importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and acknowledged both sides, steering to harm-reduction, but could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly tip the balance toward change.
