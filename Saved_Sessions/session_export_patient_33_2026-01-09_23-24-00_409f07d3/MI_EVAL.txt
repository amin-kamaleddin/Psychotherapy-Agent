# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing help‑seeking and praising concrete steps. Demonstrates clear unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and safety checking with reflections of practical concerns, but deeper affective reflection about heroin as an emotional escape is limited. More complex reflective statements about emotional pain would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on heroin use and harm reduction, guiding the session toward concrete safety steps. Consistently steers conversation to immediate, practical goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative style—co‑designs the safety plan, accepts patient edits, and integrates Diego’s preferences into planning. Frequent checking for preference and shared decision‑making is evident.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports Diego’s choice and control by offering options and not pressuring MOUD decisions. Invites Diego to choose next steps and affirms his right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some concrete commitments and willingness but relies heavily on information and planning rather than exploring internal motives or values. Missed opportunities to probe the personal meaning of change and build self‑efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a partnership, inviting input on resources and co‑constructing the plan. Offers choices and checks in about what Diego wants next.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and reinforces specific change talk and helps operationalize steps, yielding clear commitments. Could increase open, evocative questions to deepen and broaden the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and does not push abstinence, but uses few targeted MI strategies to soften sustain talk. More double‑sided reflections and strategic summaries acknowledging ambivalence would help reduce sustain talk.
