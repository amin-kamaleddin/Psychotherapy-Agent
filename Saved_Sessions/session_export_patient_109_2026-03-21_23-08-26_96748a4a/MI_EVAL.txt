# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance through validating reflections and affirmations. Maintained warmth and respect without criticism, acknowledging Rafael's feelings and efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate empathic understanding with complex reflections that captured both content and emotion and invited elaboration. Repeatedly picked up the patient's core concerns and validated his experience without minimizing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (returning to the gym) with questions and reflections tied to actionable steps. Helped move the conversation toward specific planning (fixed time, plan B, concrete options).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as a partnership and repeatedly invited Rafael's input when deciding strategies. Co-constructed the plan and shared decision-making, avoiding an expert/authoritative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Rafael's sense of choice by offering options and asking him to choose what feels doable. Affirmed his agency and avoided pressure, reinforcing that change is his decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations, past supports, and specific steps, surfacing reasons for change and workable strategies. Could more systematically consolidate motivation into commitment (e.g., scaling, summarizing plan, eliciting confidence).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited Rafael to shape the plan and checked preferences, demonstrating shared ownership of the plan. Used collaborative language (e.g., 'Let's choose one option together') to reinforce equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk with targeted open questions and generated specific commitment-oriented ideas (class, fixed time, laid out clothes). Missed opportunities to amplify and deepen change language into stronger commitment (e.g., confidence ratings, reinforced reflections).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and used double-sided reflections to normalize sustain talk, while also redirecting to change-oriented plans. At times repeated the sustain phrase in ways that kept it salient; more strategic brief reflections followed immediately by amplification of change talk would help shift momentum.
