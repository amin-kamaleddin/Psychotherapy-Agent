# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained clear unconditional positive regard with validating, nonjudgmental language and no instances of blaming or shaming. Repeated affirmations normalized ambivalence and acknowledged effort (e.g., 22 days).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and reflections that captured ambivalence and triggers, with safety checks included. Some reflections were relatively surface-level and often moved quickly to problem-solving rather than deeper emotional mirroring.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on relapse prevention and steps toward work, from safety/urge checks to concrete plans and supports. The clinician kept a clear balance between exploration and actionable planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative, routinely asking for the client's input, checking preferences, and co-constructing plans. The clinician followed the client's lead and framed tasks as joint problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered and decision ownership was reinforced with nonpressuring language. The clinician honored the client's preferences and affirmed their control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some internal motives (e.g., Fiona, quiet mornings, 22 days) and brief commitments to steps. However, the session often shifted to clinician-generated strategies instead of more systematically drawing out deeper intrinsic motives and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated genuine partnership by inviting input, offering options, and co-creating plans like message drafts and alarms. Power was shared and the client’s expertise about his situation was respected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several pieces of change talk were elicited and reinforced (e.g., 22 days, choosing supports, booking job‑centre). Missed opportunities included more targeted evocative questions to deepen DARN language and amplify commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without confrontation, but the clinician tended to move quickly to planning rather than using MI techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing) to soften sustain talk. More strategic exploration juxtaposing sustain vs. change would help resolve ambivalence.
