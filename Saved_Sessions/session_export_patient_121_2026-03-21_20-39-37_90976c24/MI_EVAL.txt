### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, validating Sofia's feelings and respecting her choices (e.g., honoring refusal to role‑play). Unconditional positive regard is maintained throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking with accurate summaries of ambivalence, and attention to safety. Missed some deeper affective reflections (hurt, shame, loneliness) that would elevate empathy to a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors (rumor responses, getting to first period, reconnecting) and moves from assessment to concrete options and an implementation plan. Session stays practical and on task.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as collaborative problem‑solving with choices and invited edits, and the clinician pastes final lines as requested. However, the clinician often offered ready‑made scripts rather than eliciting Sofia’s own wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking permission, soliciting preferences, and accepting refusals, reinforcing client ownership of decisions. Emphasizes that sending texts or role‑playing is Sofia’s choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited a concrete small goal and used a confidence ruler, but did not sufficiently draw out Sofia's internal motives, values, or longer‑term reasons for change. Evocation remained pragmatic and tactic‑focused rather than exploring intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership through choices and inviting edits, showing clear power‑sharing. To reach a 5, the clinician should have solicited Sofia’s own phrasings earlier so her voice more strongly guided the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk with concrete options, small‑step goals, and a confidence ruler, encouraging commitment and planning. Could amplify and sequence Sofia’s hopeful statements to strengthen commitment language further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirects to coping and solutions, but does not actively explore or reframe sustain talk with double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries. Missed opportunities to explicitly weigh ambivalence and soften resistance.
