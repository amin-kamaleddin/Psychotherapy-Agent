# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance and normalized hesitation throughout, offering unconditional positive regard and validating statements. There was no blaming or confrontation, supporting a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening mirrored the patient’s fear and shakiness, with paced interventions and supportive pauses. The clinician’s reflections and language conveyed deep understanding and made the patient feel heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on getting a medical evaluation and progressed logically toward concrete actions (drafting a message, specifying availability, rehearsing replies). The clinician offered concrete options that maintained forward momentum toward the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely collaborative, with the clinician offering options, inviting preferences, and scaffolding skills (scripts, role-play) while following the patient’s lead. There was consistent give-and-take shaping the session’s content and pacing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly supported with statements affirming choice and multiple options rather than directives, and the clinician respected the patient’s pace about sending messages. Even when offering prompts, choices were framed to preserve patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited the patient’s worries and used them to propose small steps, but often shifted into offering solutions rather than drawing out the patient’s own reasons, values, or confidence. More open-ended exploration of motivations and past successes would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plans and wording were co-created and the clinician repeatedly asked which option the patient preferred, demonstrating strong power-sharing. The clinician avoided expert dominance and genuinely invited the patient’s input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change talk through tiny steps and affirmations, leading to concrete patient intentions (drafting a message, choosing evenings). However, opportunities to deepen change talk (importance/confidence scaling, exploring reasons/benefits) were missed as the clinician moved relatively quickly to provide scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met nonjudgmentally with reflections and normalization, then redirected toward manageable steps like drafting and rehearsal. The clinician seldom employed double-sided reflections or reframes specifically designed to shift ambivalence more toward change.
