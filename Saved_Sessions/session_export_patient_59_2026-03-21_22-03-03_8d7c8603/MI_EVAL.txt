# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing the process and offering pragmatic accommodations (scheduling, communication preferences). There was respectful language throughout with no blaming or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, straightforward reflections and acknowledged Jamal's practical concerns and perspective. Could have deepened affective reflection and used more complex reflections to name irritation or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on completing referral logistics rather than exploring marijuana use or guiding toward behavior change. The clinician did not steer the conversation to the target behavior or long-term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner around scheduling and planning, repeatedly inviting Jamal's input and making joint decisions. The tone and process were consistently cooperative and client-centered regarding logistics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice, offered options (explain vs quick plan, mom present or not, contact method) and avoided pressure or prescriptive language. Language supported Jamal's sense of control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Made essentially no attempt to elicit Jamal's intrinsic motivation or reasons for change regarding marijuana use. No evocative questions, scaling, or exploration of values/goals were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed collaboration by checking preferences and co-creating the logistical plan, showing respect for Jamal's input. To reach top score, the clinician could have negotiated clinical goals beyond administrative tasks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not build on the minimal compliance-focused change language Jamal offered and missed opportunities to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment. Clinician focused on checklist and scheduling instead of prompting DARN-C talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk which prevented escalation, but did not actively use MI strategies (double-sided reflections, reframing, discrepancy) to gently redirect it. Sustain talk was accepted rather than explored for ambivalence.
