# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, validating Margaret’s experience and values throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with frequent paraphrases and clarifying questions that captured both feelings and priorities.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing drinking to lower fall risk and preserve independence, moving efficiently from values to concrete, time-limited planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked jointly with Margaret to co-construct practical strategies and invited her to choose which options to try, avoiding a directive or expert stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored choice and preferred goals (reduce vs. quit), asked how the clinician should support her, and avoided pressure or persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited concrete motivations and practical steps, but could have probed deeper for emotive motives, used scaling, or elicited a firmer commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly framed the interaction as a joint exploration, repeatedly soliciting Margaret’s ideas and preferences and sharing decision-making power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced substantive change talk and practical plans, though missed opportunities to amplify statements (e.g., ask for confidence/importance ratings or a specific commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk appropriately but relied mainly on reflections rather than using double-sided reflections or highlighting discrepancy to gently redirect momentum.
