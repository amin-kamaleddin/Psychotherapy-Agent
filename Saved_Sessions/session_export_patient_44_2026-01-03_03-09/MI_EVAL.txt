# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence (e.g., “It makes sense to feel torn…”, “That mix of pride and doubt is normal and okay”). No judgment, labeling, or minimization observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening capturing content and feeling (e.g., “I hear your pride… and the quiet skepticism”). Reflections occasionally felt repetitive; missed some deeper probes into emotions around comparison/shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors and progressed logically to concrete planning (sessions, snacks, warm‑ups, reminders). Could more explicitly summarize and link plan to commitment (e.g., confidence ruler, contingency planning).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Routinely treated Erik as partner and co‑creator (asked which feelings/steps, invited his choices). Clinician built on Erik’s suggestions rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Erik’s choices and doubts (“It’s okay to be skeptical”), avoided pressure or lecturing, and invited his input about what will work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons for change and specific plans (benefits, two sessions, packed snack, warm‑ups). Missed opportunities to deepen connection to broader values or amplify change statements into stronger commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered authentic partnership and consulted Erik on specifics (timing, rewards, reminders). Occasional language shifted slightly toward an expert stance (mini‑summaries/teaching) rather than pure collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited and reinforced change talk (progress, intentions, concrete steps). Could more actively amplify and consolidate commitment (ask for confidence/importance ratings, client‑stated commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated sustain talk while redirecting to change (validate skepticism, offer alternatives). More use of double‑sided reflections or reframing would further reduce sustain momentum.
