# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating (normalizing, reassurance) which conveyed acceptance; could add more individualized affirmations and explicitly normalize ambivalence to deepen unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated workload and triggers; would benefit from more complex reflections exploring underlying emotions (frustration, guilt, worry) and summarizing emotional trade‑offs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and drinking and moved to a specific, time‑bound plan with follow‑up; could strengthen motivation by more explicitly linking the plan to Thomas’s values or longer‑term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem‑solving, offered options after permission, and co‑constructed a plan; sometimes slipped into offering many directive tips instead of prompting the patient to brainstorm first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and framing strategies as options while avoiding pressure; consistently emphasized the patient's control and voluntary participation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used open questions and solicited a small plan but relied more on offering strategies than eliciting the patient’s own reasons, values, or confidence; should probe internal motives and use scaling or pros/cons to draw out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with shared decision‑making and options offered; occasional advising reduced full parity—encourage more patient‑generated solutions to strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some tentative change talk and reinforced the plan, but did not systematically evoke or amplify DARN‑C language or ask follow‑ups to deepen motivation and commitment/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and accepted sustain talk without escalating it, which prevented pushback, but lacked strategic double‑sided reflections or exploration of perceived advantages of the status quo before moving to solutions.
