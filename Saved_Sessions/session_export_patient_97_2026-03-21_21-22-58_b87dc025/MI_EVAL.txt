# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental, respectful support with validation and affirmation, praising effort and offering practical help without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent reflective statements showed understanding of Mateo's perspective and ambivalence, but reflections tended to stay at a practical level and rarely explored deeper feelings or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on obtaining steady employment with concrete action planning (specific send time, reminders, backup plan) and avoided drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as an equal partnership—resume and email were co-constructed, role-played, and decisions (timing, reminders, logistics) were made jointly with Mateo's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly invited choice, used permission language, and respected hedged commitments, reinforcing Mateo's ownership over decisions and the pace of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician drew out Mateo's reasons and linked work to family goals, but at times supplied wording and missed opportunities for deeper, curiosity-based elicitation of intrinsic values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as a joint endeavor, soliciting preferences, offering options, and co-creating materials and practice in a power-sharing manner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicited and amplified change talk using a confidence ruler, reflections, concrete commitments, and role-play to translate motivation into specific next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and redirected toward problem-solving (reminders, backup plan), but the clinician used few double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of underlying barriers.
