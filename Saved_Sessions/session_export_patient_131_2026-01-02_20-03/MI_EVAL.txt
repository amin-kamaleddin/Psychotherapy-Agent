# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates respect and nonjudgmental stance (opening with safety, multiple affirmations). Would reach 5 with more individualized warmth and explicit unconditional positive regard addressing cultural/contextual struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and paraphrase of concerns (freedom vs accountability, ambivalence about housing) and repeated safety checks. Missed opportunities for deeper affect-focused and complex reflections naming shame/fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on referral goals (safety, drinking reduction, rebuilding credibility) with concrete behavior-linked actions and repeated return to practical steps (timeout plan, coordinator, alcohol removal, written plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asks which step feels achievable and offers to draft plans. Tends toward supplying specific solutions/scripts rather than eliciting client-generated wording more often.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Honors choice and frames steps as optional/trial (e.g., “you don’t have to decide,” 4–6 week review). Could strengthen by explicitly reinforcing the client's ownership of decisions and adaptability of plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some elicitation (which step, who to call, commitment), but clinician supplies most motivational content and strategies. Would improve by eliciting client’s reasons, past successes, and strengths more deliberately.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Frames work as collaborative and offers to co‑mediate with coordinator; invites client to take scripts to family. Occasionally shifts into adviser role with ready-made scripts rather than co-constructing language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Client expresses strong change talk spontaneously, but clinician does not consistently elicit or amplify values-based motivations or confidence/commitment language. Some commitment questions are used but not systematically developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Acknowledges ambivalence (fear of losing freedom) and reframes housing as temporary/testable with concrete autonomy-preserving options, effectively reducing sustain talk. Could add more double-sided reflections to shift ambivalence further.
