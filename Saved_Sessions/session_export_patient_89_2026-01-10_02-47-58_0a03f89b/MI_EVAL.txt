# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and warmth, using validation and normalizing statements that created a nonjudgmental tone. Avoided blame and respected Marcus's experience and choices throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with reflections that captured ambivalence and shifting emotions, matching Marcus’s discouragement and cautious hope. Repeatedly acknowledged worries and partnered with understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, behavior-specific focus on increasing physical activity with concrete small actions and contingency plans. Questions and suggestions consistently tied back to the target behavior and progressed logically toward a plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely two-way, soliciting Marcus's preferences and adjusting recommendations accordingly. Invited follow-up and framed planning as a team effort to troubleshoot and refine the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Marcus's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and reinforcing ownership of decisions. Avoided coercive language and emphasized that Marcus decides how to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited practical commitments and excitement about activities but missed opportunities to draw out deeper internal motivations or values. Could have used scaling/confidence questions or explored meaning for identity/family/long-term health to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—invited Marcus's input at decision points and co-created the plan (alarms, backups, anchoring). Treated Marcus as the expert in his life and honored his choices of specific behaviors.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced concrete commitment language (set alarms, specific walk time, backups) and affirmed efforts. Missed some opportunities to deepen change talk by exploring why the behaviors matter to Marcus beyond logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed sustain talk with manageable steps and backups, reducing the power of barriers. Could more actively soften resistance with double-sided reflections or eliciting Marcus's own counterarguments to sustain talk.
