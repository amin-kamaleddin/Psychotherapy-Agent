# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth (e.g., personalized recognition, normalizing slips), which validated the client's feelings throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, consistent reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both content and emotion (e.g., summarizing ambivalence and parenting concerns).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on smoking and its impact on the child, moving logically from readiness assessment to concrete behavior-change options and a short-term plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a joint enterprise by inviting input, negotiating start dates and check-ins, and offering information after eliciting preferences, making the plan co‑constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, checking preferences, and framing slips as information rather than failure, reinforcing the client's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation with evocative questions and prompts (e.g., pros/cons, 'what would move you from a 4 to a 6?'), drawing out the client's own reasons and willingness to try.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered power‑sharing and treated the client as expert on her life by asking permission before information, offering choices, and co‑creating the week‑long plan and follow‑up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk through readiness scaling, asking what would increase motivation, and prompting specific, doable steps that produced client change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and often redirected toward change, but missed opportunities for more strategic double‑sided reflections and summaries explicitly contrasting values with rationalizations.
