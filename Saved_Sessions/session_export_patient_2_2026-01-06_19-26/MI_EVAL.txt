# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Aisha's situation, with validating language and a nonjudgmental tone. Boundaries (e.g., not probing about drinking) and explicit praise reinforced acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking—clinician notes triggers and summarizes feelings—but missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection (e.g., exploring the meaning of being a mother or feelings like guilt/pride).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on evening smoking with concrete, actionable goals and precise implementation details (start time, gum placement, if‑then plan, check‑in). The clinician repeatedly brought conversation back to the target behavior while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative, inviting Aisha's choices, preferences, and permission for follow-up, and co-creating the plan together. The clinician positioned themselves as a supportive partner throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice by offering options, accepting limits (e.g., not discussing drinking), and emphasizing that the plan is Aisha's decision. Scaling and invitation to appraise strategies respected her control and agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited motivation and generated Aisha's own plans and coping ideas, but could have used more evocative questions to deepen intrinsic reasons (e.g., exploring values, past successes, or what success would feel like).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treated Aisha as the expert, offered choices, and co-created specific steps while asking what the clinician should notice in the follow-up. Power-sharing was clear and consistent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (specific small steps, confidence rulers, implementation intentions) and supported commitment. Missed chances to amplify value-driven reasons and deepen desire/ability/reason/need language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled appropriately by avoiding confrontation and honoring boundaries, with gentle reframing and redirection to practical change. More explicit softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) could further shift ambivalence toward change.
