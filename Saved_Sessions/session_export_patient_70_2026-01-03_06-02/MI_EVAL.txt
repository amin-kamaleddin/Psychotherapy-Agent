# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 5/5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and non‑judgment (repeated “I hear you,” “I accept…”). Validated ambivalence and explicitly accepted limits (e.g., refusing Donna). Met highest standard for acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 4/5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrase of conflict and feelings. Reflections were sometimes formulaic/repetitive, limiting deeper emotional exploration (shame/fear could be probed more).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 5/5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on target behaviors and moved the session from identification to a concrete, time‑bound action plan (9am call, pack jacket, tell PO, 9:15 check‑in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 5/5
**Comments:** Framed conversation as joint work, offered options and left choices to Lars. Co‑constructed the plan (script, time, fallback) with patient preferences driving decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 5/5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced patient choice and agency (“what feels most doable,” options for support). Explicit acceptance of decisions (no Donna) and avoidance of pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 4/5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Lars’s reasons and practical strategies, producing change talk and a plan. Missed opportunities to systematically elicit values, importance, or confidence (e.g., scaling questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 5/5
**Comments:** Treated Lars as an equal collaborator and expert on his life; negotiated supports and invited selection of clinician actions (role‑play, script, check‑in).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 5/5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk (desire to avoid prison, job goals, concrete commitments). Reflections and prompts translated motivation into specific next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑️ 4/5
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk (embarrassment, “might bail,” mates) and reframed toward action with fallback plans. Relied mainly on neutral reflections; could use more double‑sided reflections and active reframing to reduce sustain arguments.
