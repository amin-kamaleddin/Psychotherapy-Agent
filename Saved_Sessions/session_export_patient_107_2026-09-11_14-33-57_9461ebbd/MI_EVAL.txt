# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, offering multiple options and validating ambivalence while honoring the client's limits. The clinician treats the patient's enjoyment of coffee as legitimate and workable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections captured the client's ambivalence and perspective, inviting elaboration rather than imposing interpretation. The clinician attends to context and constraints when asking what fits the patient's life.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on coffee use and jitteriness, guiding the conversation toward a specific, measurable experiment. Questions and planning consistently tie back to the target behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions as joint problem‑solving: the clinician repeatedly invites preferences and co‑plans the intervention so the patient's choices shape each step. Power is shared throughout the exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice and control by offering options, asking what feels doable, and respecting the patient's stated limits and pace. Language consistently reinforces that decisions are the patient's.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's motives and curiosity about change and explores possible next steps. Could deepen evocation by probing values, importance/confidence scaling, and amplifying expressed desire to experiment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and client‑centered, repeatedly soliciting the patient's input on logistics and preferences. The clinician avoids an expert stance and co‑creates the plan with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits modest change talk through small, testable steps and future‑oriented questions, prompting consideration of additional swaps. Would benefit from more explicit amplification and prompts to generate stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk, normalizes ambivalence, and redirects toward low‑threat experiments like a decaf swap. Could strengthen this by using double‑sided reflections that link sustain and change talk and amplifying curiosity about change.
