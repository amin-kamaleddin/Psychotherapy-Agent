# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing ambivalence and validating the patient’s choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking about behaviors and consequences, but missed deeper affective probes into shame, fear, or the emotional meaning of family tension.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use and harm-reduction throughout, with interventions and a concrete, time-bound plan that kept the session targeted on behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a genuine partner by soliciting input, editing the reminder text per the patient’s preferences, and letting the patient’s choices shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, normalizing slips, and framing the plan as something the patient could test and modify.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited factual information and a chosen change step but relied more on offering options and practical tips than on eliciting intrinsic motives, values, or personally meaningful reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked which strategies felt easiest and co-created the plan—but occasionally provided relatively detailed, clinician-generated suggestions that tilted toward expert direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (prior attempts, willingness to try no-drink nights) and reinforced choices, but did not systematically amplify or deepen the patient’s own reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged benefits of drinking, but did not frequently use strategic softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframes) to gently pivot sustain talk toward change.
