# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with explicit normalization and validation, avoiding judgment or pressure. Repeatedly acknowledged both the client’s priorities and limitations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent accurate reflections and summaries captured the client's two-sided experience and reduced defensiveness. Missed opportunities to probe deeper into the emotional meaning of smoking and the personal significance of being a role model.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on attending the school program but drifted from the primary target behavior of smoking cessation. Did not use scaling/confidence questions or explore discrepancy related to quitting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as genuine joint problem-solving with the client shaping practical solutions (session selection, childcare, handouts). Clinician repeatedly invited the client's choices and checked what felt doable.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly respected the client's choices, emphasized what felt realistic, and avoided pressuring change. Language consistently reinforced the client's agency in planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evoked motivation around attending sessions but did little to draw out intrinsic motivation to quit smoking. Rarely used evocative or scaling questions to elicit desire, reasons, or confidence about cessation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently promoted a collaborative stance, co-constructing plans and inviting the client's ideas. Questions placed expertise with the client and supported shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited planning and commitment language about attendance but failed to strategically amplify or elicit DARN for smoking cessation. Change talk about role-model concerns was acknowledged but not developed into commitment for quitting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and validated the client's position while redirecting to achievable steps. Seldom used targeted techniques like double-sided reflections to explicitly juxtapose sustain talk with change talk.
