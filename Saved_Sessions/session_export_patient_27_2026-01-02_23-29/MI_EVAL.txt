# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Communicates unconditional positive regard
- [x] Normalizes patient experience
- [x] Uses affirmations and supportive language
- [ ] Confronts or judges patient

**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly validates and normalizes Daniela’s feelings (“That sounds really stressful,” “That’s completely understandable”) and offers frequent affirmations (“I’m proud of you”). Tone is consistently nonjudgmental and respectful.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Uses reflective listening
- [x] Paraphrases values and concerns
- [x] Validates emotions and perspective
- [ ] Misses or dismisses affect

**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflections (e.g., “You care about spontaneity…”) and validation of fears; clinician’s reflections match affect and advance the conversation. Could occasionally add deeper emotion labeling (embarrassment/shame) but overall empathy is excellent.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Maintains clear focus on target behavior (disclosure to friends)
- [x] Uses scaling (0–10 readiness)
- [x] Moves toward actionable steps (scripts, role-plays, text)
- [ ] Loses focus or drifts from target behavior

**Comments:** Session stays sharply focused on disclosure, readiness, and small doable steps (scripts, rehearsals, sending a text). Final summary/negotiation of a single small commitment could strengthen wrap-up.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Asks permission before offering advice/scripts
- [x] Invites patient choice (text vs in-person; quick vs story-style)
- [x] Co-constructs role-plays with patient
- [ ] Dominates decision-making

**Comments:** Clinician works as a partner—asks permission, offers options, and engages in co-created role-plays. Power is shared and Daniela’s preferences drive the plan.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Asks about readiness and elicits patient choice
- [x] Frames steps as optional and under patient control
- [x] Reinforces patient’s agency (“You don’t owe extra explanations”)
- [ ] Applies pressure or insists on a plan

**Comments:** Effective emphasis on control and choice (readiness scaling, optional scripts, respect for pacing). Messages consistently reduce pressure and support adolescent agency.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Elicits patient reasons and resources for change
- [x] Draws on patient strengths (storytelling)
- [x] Uses scaling and asks for small first steps
- [ ] Imposes clinician reasons for change

**Comments:** Clinician elicits Daniela’s motives and resources (best friend, storytelling skill), uses scaling, and elicits a specific first step. Could further probe past successes to build internal motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Uses team/problem-solving language
- [x] Invites practice and checks comfort repeatedly
- [x] Shares responsibility for rehearsal and planning
- [ ] Leaves patient to figure plan alone

**Comments:** Clinician fosters genuine teamwork via role-plays, choice of text vs in-person, and repeated comfort checks. Inviting patient to teach what will work could deepen partnership further.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicits and reinforces planning/change talk (intentions, steps)
- [x] Converts change talk into concrete plans (scripts, texts)
- [ ] Consistently amplifies and summarizes change talk to elicit stronger commitment
- [ ] Elicits explicit commitment language reliably

**Comments:** Clear change talk is elicited and reinforced (planning to tell best friend, saving text). Clinician could more consistently amplify and summarize change language and use follow-ups (e.g., “what would move you from a 4 to a 6?”) to elicit stronger commitment.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Validates and normalizes sustain talk (fear, embarrassment)
- [x] Offers strategies to manage feared outcomes (scripts, role-plays)
- [ ] Uses double-sided reflections and strategic summarizing consistently
- [ ] Reinforces sustain talk

**Comments:** Clinician acknowledges and softens sustain talk—normalizes fears and provides concrete coping responses and alternatives (texts, replies to teasing). Could more frequently use MI-specific double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to reduce sustain talk’s pull and intensify change talk.
