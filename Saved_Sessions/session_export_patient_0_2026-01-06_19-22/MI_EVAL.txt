# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating from the start, explicitly accepts the patient’s stance about not wanting to quit and normalizes help-seeking throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic listening and tailored responses (reflects distress, acknowledges fears), but rarely explores deeper emotions like shame or mixed feelings underpinning the minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on meth use-related safety and practical harm-reduction goals, repeatedly steering the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asks what the patient wants, offers tools and searches, and addresses constraints—but leans toward expert-driven content rather than co-constructing the plan step-by-step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly honors choice by asking permission and offering options and printable tools, though occasional prescriptive language and a lengthy checklist introduce a mild directive tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit choices and small steps, but limited use of open questions or reflections to draw out the patient’s own motivations, values, or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites patient input and offers to help find resources, yet the session is often clinician-led with prescriptive guidance, reducing equal partnership in shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits occasional change language and offers harm-reduction options, but does not strategically amplify or explore the patient’s statements about not wanting more infections to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and shifts to harm-reduction, but lacks MI strategies (double-sided reflections, ambivalence summaries) to actively soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
