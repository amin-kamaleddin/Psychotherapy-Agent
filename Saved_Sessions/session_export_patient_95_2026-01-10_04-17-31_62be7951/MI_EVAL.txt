# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validation, explicitly saying 'I believe how awful this is' and refusing to rush the patient. Avoids judgmental language and follows Maria's lead in a warm, supportive stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking with frequent, accurate reflections and sensitive follow-ups that mirror the patient's language and affect. Summaries and explicit validation reinforce understanding of Maria's pain and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on immediate safety and a very small behavioral step (texting/typing) and asks direct safety questions, but permits extended sustain-talk before redirecting. More concise summaries and firmer, gentle guidance earlier would strengthen directional clarity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner by inviting input, offering options, and co-creating next steps like drafting a message together. Repeatedly checks preferences before taking action, promoting joint problem-solving rather than clinician-driven instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors Maria's choices and presents options rather than directives, explicitly stating limits while preserving her agency. Action is contingent on consent, which reinforces ownership of the change process even when addressing safety.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal motivation and small steps (e.g., texting or typing without sending) and draws out values such as needing to be heard. Could more deliberately amplify in-the-moment change language and probe past coping and importance/confidence to deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the interaction as collaborative, privileging the patient's expertise and offering to follow her lead. Uses options and checks consent (e.g., drafting vs. typing without sending) to share power in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces tentative change talk and converts it into concrete, doable steps like drafting or timing a text. Could further strengthen by explicitly amplifying reasons for living, using rulers, and inviting elaboration on past helpful coping.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk empathically but often reflects hopeless statements verbatim, which can maintain focus on despair. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections, gentle reframing, and strategic pivots toward hopes, values, or past effective coping.
