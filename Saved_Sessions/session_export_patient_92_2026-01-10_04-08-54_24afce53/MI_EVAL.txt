# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental language, normalization of ambivalence, and validation of Marek's priorities; no blaming or confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, frequent reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both content and underlying meaning of Marek's concerns and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on weekend drinking and specific risk‑reduction strategies, repeatedly refocusing toward concrete, behaviorally specific action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engaged in co‑construction of a plan, used joint language and checked understanding, but sometimes introduced options and steered the agenda rather than letting Marek drive from the outset.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized Marek's choice and control, framed options as his decisions, and avoided pressure, consistently reinforcing his agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited personally meaningful reasons for change (work focus, better mornings) and generated concrete change talk, but missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motives and assess confidence or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Marek as the expert and invited his input, yet occasionally offered solutions before fully eliciting his ideas, resulting in slightly more directional control than ideal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and concrete plans, but did not consistently amplify the strongest change statements or follow up with confidence/commitment scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and accepted sustain talk with double‑sided reflections to avoid argument, but relied on neutral reflections rather than more strategic moves to tip the balance toward change or address minimization.
