### Acceptance

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate understanding and useful summaries, acknowledged shame and offered anxiety strategies, but relied more on problem‑solving than deeper reflective exploration of emotions and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors with a concise plan and concrete, stepwise actions (forms, scripts, checklists) that kept the session on task.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑wrote form entries, checked preferences repeatedly, and built on the client's contributions to create shared solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported client choice by offering options, templates, and permission scripts without pressuring decisions, reinforcing Lars's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of internal motivation; the clinician largely focused on pragmatic problem solving and did not systematically draw out values, discrepancy, or scaling of importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and power‑sharing—invited choices and co‑drafted wording—but sometimes shifted into expert mode by supplying many scripted options without fully eliciting the client's own phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created some openings for change talk and reinforced forward steps, but missed opportunities to ask evocative questions or amplify Lars’s own reasons and desires for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered practical alternatives, yet used few MI‑specific strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to reframe or reduce the pull of sustain talk.
