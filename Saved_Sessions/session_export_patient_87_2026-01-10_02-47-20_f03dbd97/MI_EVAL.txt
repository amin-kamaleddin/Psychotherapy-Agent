# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizes slips and communicates unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good understanding with brief reflections and validation, but reflections are often short and quickly followed by solutions rather than deeper exploration of underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on post-dinner snacking and a specific if‑then experiment, with interventions tied directly to the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs the plan and checks Marco’s preferences repeatedly, but many clinician-driven suggestions reduce the sense that Marco’s ideas fully shaped the content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices, asks which option feels most doable, and avoids pressuring language, clearly supporting Marco’s agency and pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits modest motivation by asking for a doable option, but does not sufficiently draw out internal reasons, values, or personal motivations behind change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and choices and adapts wording with Marco, yet supplying multiple concrete steps without first eliciting his ideas creates a mildly expert-driven feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and specific commitments to try experiments, but misses opportunities to amplify, reflect, and chain change statements to strengthen desire and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk and redirects to practical strategies and normalization, though could add more double-sided reflections to gently tip ambivalence toward change.
