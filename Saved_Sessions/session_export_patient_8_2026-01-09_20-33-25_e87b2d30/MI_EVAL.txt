# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm; normalizes ambivalence, affirms effort, and frames slips as expected which reduces shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and validation of ambivalence and fatigue, but there are few deeper affect-focused reflections exploring underlying emotions like loneliness or guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on phone/social media use with concrete, measurable steps and follow-up monitoring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong cooperative problem-solving and frequent elicitation of patient preferences, though frequent unsolicited tips shift the dynamic slightly toward clinician-driven guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options, emphasizes choice, and normalizes tentative attempts, reinforcing the patient's sense of control over change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of motivation through asking what’s urgent and offering experiments, but the session relies more on pragmatic strategies than drawing out personal reasons, values, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with co-created plans and check-ins, though could invite more shared interpretation and ask permission before giving advice to equalize expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk via concrete experiments, but misses opportunities to amplify the patient's own desire, reasons, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids argument, but rarely employs double-sided reflections, reframing, or exploration to soften and resolve ambivalence before moving to action.
