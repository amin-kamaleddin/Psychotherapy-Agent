# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental from the opening line; validates the patient's concerns and normalizes monitoring while providing reassurance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and some reflective responses but many replies are information-heavy and biomedical; missed opportunities to deepen empathy about feeling dismissed or long‑term anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on understanding and initiating the statin safely, offering concrete action steps, scripts, and follow-up plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as a joint problem‑solver with scripts, role‑play, and invitations to bring meds/labs; occasionally leans into expert explanation but generally returns to collaborative language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and shared decision‑making, offering options and encouraging consultation before stopping medication without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits immediate concerns and specific intentions but does not systematically probe underlying motivations, importance/confidence, or elicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a partnership stance—asks permission, offers role‑play, and invites input—but could more explicitly co‑create decisions or ask the patient to choose among options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms and reinforces patient‑initiated plans and statements but rarely amplifies or explores change talk or asks questions to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and provides neutral information and options to defuse sustain talk; could enhance technique with double‑sided reflections and explicit validation of ambivalence.
