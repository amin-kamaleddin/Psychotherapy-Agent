# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys respectful, nonjudging acceptance (repeated validations such as “You’re here and tired of it, and that matters”). Statements maintain safety and disclosure. Occasionally feels formulaic rather than individualized (fewer concrete affirmations of specific strengths or past efforts).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and several complex reflections (links physiological cues → social pressure → behavior). Reflections often paraphrase client language rather than deepening emotional nuance (missed opportunities to explore shame, fear of rejection, meaning of “the rush”).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on target behavior (shoplifting), triggers, and concrete, testable actions (carrying cash, stepping away, naming Marcus). Session maintains momentum and rarely drifts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance—invites choices and patient input (which small step, who to involve). Solid shared decision-making but could strengthen by checking permission before offering ideas and co-constructing scripts/contingency plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice, avoids directives, and reinforces ownership of plan (asks patient to choose feasible steps; clinician refrains from pressuring language).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits spontaneous change talk and practical ideas (repeated “I can’t keep doing it,” choice of cash, naming Marcus). Would benefit from deeper elicitation of values, importance/confidence scaling, and amplifying desire/ability/need.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames session as joint problem-solving and defers to patient expertise about social context. Partnership is genuine but at times clinician-led prompts and repeated reassurance limit fuller co-creation of the plan and follow-up responsibilities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Multiple change statements present (e.g., “Shoplifting’s a problem — I can’t keep doing it,” “I’ll try carrying a bit of cash”), but clinician often reflects rather than amplifies or probes for stronger desire/ability/reasons/commitment (no scaling or commitment probes used).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and mirrored (friends normalize behavior; the rush wins) which avoids arguing, but clinician does not consistently pivot to reduce sustain talk (limited use of double-sided reflections, decisional balance, or explicit exploration of costs).
