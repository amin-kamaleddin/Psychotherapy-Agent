# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, using reflections and normalization that created a safe tone; some affirmations felt formulaic and the clinician sometimes moved quickly into problem-solving rather than offering deeper individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that helped Lukas expand on his plan; missed opportunities to name and deepen underlying emotions (e.g., anxiety or embarrassment), with some reflections remaining more descriptive than emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on early alcohol use and refusal strategies tied to grades and team goals, guiding the conversation toward actionable next steps without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session read as a team effort—clinician invited Lukas’s input, offered role-play, and co-drafted a teammate message—demonstrating clear power-sharing and joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and control by offering options, asking which felt doable, and requesting permission for role‑play, supporting Lukas’s ownership of the solution rather than prescribing actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited Lukas’s motivations and resources and drew out concrete planning language; could strengthen evocation by exploring values and longer‑term reasons for change and by eliciting more spontaneous solutions before offering lists.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance throughout—asking permission, offering supports as options, and following Lukas’s lead in selecting strategies—avoiding an expert‑authority posture.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk, reinforcing planning and commitment language which led to a concrete, client-driven plan; reflections and affirmations effectively increased Lukas’s stated commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and normalized ambivalence using double-focus language; could more explicitly use double-sided reflections and further explore how short‑term social gains fit with longer‑term goals instead of occasionally shifting to directive suggestions.
