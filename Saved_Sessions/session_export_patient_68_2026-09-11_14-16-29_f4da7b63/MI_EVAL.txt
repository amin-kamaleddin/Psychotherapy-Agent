# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes ambivalence, and affirms the patient without shaming or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, though could deepen emotional reflections (e.g., explore embarrassment/shame or stress-relief function of drinking) with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol use related to the injury and steadily moves toward concrete, actionable steps (text, exit signal, sober nights).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative problem‑solving: invites input, offers manageable options, and co‑constructs the plan rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, asking preferences, and avoiding pressuring language to reinforce ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations and solutions, building concrete commitment (drafting the text and setting a sending time) from the patient's language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the patient as the expert and repeatedly invites collaboration and choice, fostering genuine power‑sharing in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and specific plans, but could further amplify and summarize change language and probe confidence/importance to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids debating sustain talk and redirects to solutions with alternatives, though could more often use double‑sided reflections or strategic de‑emphasis to more actively soften sustain talk.
