# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and a nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the referral source, which made the patient feel heard. Occasionally moved quickly to problem‑solving instead of lingering with affective validation and explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and captures key elements of the patient's perspective and ambivalence. Missed some opportunities to deepen empathic reflection of anger/defensiveness and the emotional meaning of 'I don't want to lose her.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction/cessation and produced a concrete, goal‑linked 1‑week plan. Questions, reflections, and recommendations consistently returned to nicotine replacement and strategies tied to the patient's routine.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a partnership by asking which options felt doable and tailoring recommendations to the patient's priorities. Decisions and the plan were co‑constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, requested permission before advising, and emphasized the patient's control to try or stop interventions. These moves consistently supported the patient's sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempted to draw out motivation but much of the change talk remained externally framed (to stop arguing/please his wife). Needed deeper probing and amplification of intrinsic motives and the personal meaning of keeping the marriage.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently invited input and permission and used shared decision‑making to build the plan. At times the interaction shifted toward clinician‑led planning after permission was given, limiting maximum partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change language, explored pros/cons, and used summaries to support a trial of gum/patch. Could more strategically amplify change talk and elicit confidence and explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing, reflected sustain talk, and redirected to low‑effort options which reduced resistance and advanced planning. Could increase use of double‑sided reflections and strategic summaries to more explicitly highlight change language.
