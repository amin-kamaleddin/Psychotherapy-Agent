# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing doubt and affirming strengths without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent perspective-taking with reflections that capture both emotion and behavior, maintaining strong connection throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on small, concrete behavioral changes and ties questions to action, measurement, and sustaining change to produce a doable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functions as a true partnership: the clinician invites co-planning, uses inclusive language, and lets the adolescent drive implementation details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, validating uncertainty, and avoiding pressure so change feels self-directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits internal motivation and reasons for change with evocative questions, but could do more to deepen and consolidate that motivation into explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co-constructed—clinician repeatedly seeks the client’s input and honors her priorities, sharing power throughout the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits change talk through targeted open questions and concrete planning, though selectively amplifying and summarizing the strongest change language would strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and redirects toward change with validation and small steps, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or explicit reframing to blunt discouragement.
