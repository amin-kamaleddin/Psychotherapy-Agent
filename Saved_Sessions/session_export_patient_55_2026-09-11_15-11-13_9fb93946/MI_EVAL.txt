# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys respect and nonjudgmental regard with validating summaries and acknowledgement of constraints. Could add more individualized warmth and explicit normalization of ambivalence to reach the top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening that helps the patient feel understood. Could deepen empathic responding by exploring emotional nuances (fear of rejection, embarrassment, stress) with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related choices and concrete implementation intentions. Every clinician utterance advances toward a specific plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is strongly collaborative with co-creation of the plan and frequent checks for the patient's preferences. Power-sharing is evident as the patient shapes next steps and wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supports the patient's choice and control, offering options and avoiding pressure. The patient is invited to choose steps rather than being prescribed actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician operates from an evocation stance, drawing out the patient's own motivation and eliciting multiple change statements. Open invitations and requests for specific plans successfully mobilized patient-generated ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership is shown through invitations to generate options, co-creation of scripts, and shared decision-making. The clinician consistently defers to the patient's expertise about what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicits and rehearses change language and specific first steps, which produced multiple change statements. Could more explicitly amplify and summarize the patient's change talk to strengthen importance, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician accepts and normalizes the patient's qualifiers, avoiding confrontation but leaving sustain talk present. More active strategies (double-sided reflections, exploring barriers, recalling past successes) are needed to further soften sustain talk.
