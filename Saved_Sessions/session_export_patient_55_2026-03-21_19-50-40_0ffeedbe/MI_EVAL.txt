# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides consistent nonjudgmental support, explicit safety check, and practical validation; however, lacks a warmer, more personalized affirmation or explicit normalization beyond pragmatic validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening (acknowledging workload and skepticism), but misses deeper emotional reflections and exploration of the referral context.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on concrete target behaviors and moves the patient toward specific, actionable micro-plans throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and checks patient preferences, indicating shared problem-solving, but often supplies detailed prescriptive instructions rather than fully co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces choice and freedom by offering options, asking which feels doable, framing plans as low-pressure, and requesting permission to follow up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits small commitments and asks what feels doable, but primarily uses an instructive approach and misses opportunities to draw out intrinsic motivations or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displays some partnership through option-giving and checking in, yet the interaction remains instruction-heavy with the clinician leading rather than equally sharing decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicits minimal change talk and quickly moves to giving instructions instead of exploring the patient's desires, reasons, or commitments for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Responds nonconfrontationally and normalizes skepticism which limits sustain talk, but does not consistently use strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to shift sustain talk toward change.
