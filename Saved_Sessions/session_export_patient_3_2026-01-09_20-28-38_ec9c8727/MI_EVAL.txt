# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental, validating, and supportive throughout (e.g., normalizing statements and gentleness after setbacks). No blaming or shaming language, which fostered safety and rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening about practical constraints and low energy. Missed some deeper emotional reflections of shame/embarrassment that would deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on starting exercise with a specific measurable first-week goal and concrete scheduling, alternatives, and a backup plan. The session stayed on-task and moved logically to action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited input and tailored the plan to the client's schedule and knee issues, creating a partnership feel. Much of the content (exact workouts, scripts, checklist) was provided by the clinician, somewhat reducing equal contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, alternatives, and left decisions to the client (e.g., reminders vs printable, exercise swaps). Avoided pressure and reinforced client ownership and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited the client's initial goal but then shifted into clinician-driven planning rather than sustained exploration of internal motivations, values, or confidence. Limited use of questions to deepen change talk or elicit importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by soliciting preferences and adapting exercises, but often supplied detailed expert solutions rather than eliciting the client's own ideas earlier. Partnership was strong but somewhat clinician-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced strong client change statements and built a concrete plan, but did not systematically evoke or deepen desire, reasons, need, ability, or commitment with open questions. More targeted elicitation would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled skepticism well by normalizing doubts, offering low-threat options, and reframing partial efforts as successes. Could add double-sided reflections or further targeted elicitation to more fully resolve ambivalence.
