# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, repeatedly validated Maria's pain and honored her request to be heard before problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used frequent, accurate reflections of feelings and perspective, paced interventions to match distress, and demonstrated strong perspective-taking and attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on suicidal ideation and immediate means restriction, guiding concrete safety actions without losing empathic tone.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as cooperative problem-solving by inviting choices, offering options rather than directives, and staying with the patient through tasks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Protected the patient's sense of choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and respecting refusals while linking escalation to imminent danger.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivations (care for her brother) and doable steps, but could have probed deeper to amplify values and scaffold toward stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Maria as collaborator and expert on her experience, negotiated safety steps, and consistently invited her input on next actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created opportunities and reinforced change talk with doable steps and praise, but missed chances to follow up and deepen desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reflected despair while pivoting to safety and options, though additional strategies (double-sided reflections, explicit pros/cons) could further shift sustain talk.
