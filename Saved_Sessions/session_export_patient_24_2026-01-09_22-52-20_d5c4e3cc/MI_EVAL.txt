# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician shows strong unconditional positive regard with validating reflections and normalization of the client's values, avoiding criticism or labeling throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening capture the client's ambivalence and emotional stance, with tone and summaries that invite elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use and moves logically to a specific, measurable experiment (NA weeknights) with concrete outcome measures and logistics tied to marathon goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction of the plan—clinician solicits and tests the client's ideas, offers options rather than directives, and lets the client's input drive decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes choice and respects preferences, framing change as a low-risk experiment the client can accept or abandon without pressure toward a single path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits motivation by drawing on values and discrepancy and helps articulate success metrics, but could further elicit personal reasons, past successes, and a confidence/commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the client as the expert and invites solutions, sustaining power-sharing across the session so the client leads choices about strategies and implementation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces multiple strands of change talk and asks for specifics, though it could more directly amplify ability and commitment language (e.g., confidence, past successes).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and responds nonconfrontationally by framing change as a test and reflecting hesitations, but could strengthen use of double-sided reflections to name both sides of ambivalence.
