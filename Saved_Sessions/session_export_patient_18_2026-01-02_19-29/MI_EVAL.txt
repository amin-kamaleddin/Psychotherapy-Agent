# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicated clear nonjudgmental support and respect (opening: “I’m here to listen and support you”), checked safety (“Are you safe to talk right now?”), and normalized choices (“you don’t owe anyone an argument,” “you’re allowed to leave”). Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated attuned listening and brief accurate reflections (“It sounds like you’re curious but safety‑conscious”), used a grounding exercise and asked “How do you feel after that?” Missed opportunities for deeper, more amplified reflections on Anya’s anxiety about the future.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept session focused on party safety and related college concerns; moved logically from options to a concrete safety plan and clear next steps (text Maya, charge phone, prebook Uber, set leave time).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan by offering choices and asking preferences (“Which would you prefer…?”, “Which buddy and ride option feels most realistic?”). Checked comfort with role‑play vs grounding and incorporated Anya’s input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and agency (offered options, asked preference, statements like “you don’t need to justify”). Avoided pressure or shaming and reinforced Anya’s control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation (asked about curiosity, worries, coping strategies) but relied heavily on providing strategies and lists rather than eliciting Anya’s own reasons/values or connecting choices to future goals. Could use more open questions to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned largely as a partner (permission, collaborative questions). Slight drift to expert/problem‑solver with multi‑item directives and detailed advice that could be more jointly edited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anya voiced some change talk (safety concerns, willingness to text Maya, try grounding) but clinician did not systematically amplify or convert these into stronger desire/ability/reasons/commitment statements (no scaling or commitment checks).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and offered nonconfrontational alternatives (exit cue with Maya), but did not use many double‑sided or strategic reflections to acknowledge both pull of fun and pull of safety. More reflective techniques could better shift ambivalence.
