# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Polite, nonjudgmental tone and acceptance of the patient's preference to cut down, with recommendations tailored accordingly. Lacked deeper verbal normalization of ambivalence or explicit expressions of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged concrete concerns (sleep, side effects) and offered practical guidance. Rarely used reflective listening or validated the patient's enjoyment/conflict, limiting deeper perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on tobacco use with evidence-based NRT options, dosing, safety instructions, and a pragmatic stepwise plan. Direction was strong and appropriate for medication/behavioral planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked questions, tailored options to stated preferences, and offered a checklist, showing some collaborative elements. Much of the session remained one-way information delivery with limited genuine back-and-forth about goals or patient-generated strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported the patient's choice to cut down, offered multiple options, and asked permission before providing materials. Still leaned toward recommending specific regimens rather than fully eliciting the patient's own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Treated motivation as something to be managed with tools rather than elicited; did not ask systematic evoking questions about values, importance, or past successes. Missed opportunities to draw out intrinsic motivation and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction was expert-driven and prescriptive with the clinician largely directing the agenda and producing the plan. The patient's voice influenced some details but power-sharing and true co-creation were limited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied on information and recommendations with only brief prompts for factual details or agreement. Few efforts were made to elicit, reflect, or amplify desire, reasons, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not meaningfully acknowledge or explore sustain talk; the patient's statement 'I don't really wanna quit — I like smoking' was not validated or reflected. Clinician shifted quickly to product options instead of using double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance.
