# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows warm, nonjudgmental regard, validates barriers, and normalizes small steps, reflecting strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate understanding (naming shame and fatigue) that helps the patient feel heard, though deeper affective reflections on the emotions behind 'escape' would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on a concrete harm‑reduction goal and keeps questions and reflections directed toward specifying action, barriers, and contingencies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co‑constructed with the patient; the clinician solicits ideas, offers options, and builds a patient‑owned plan through joint problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supports choice and agency by offering options, asking which feels most doable, and avoiding pressure, thereby reinforcing patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations and concrete plans, drawing out commitment, but could deepen evocation by exploring intrinsic motives and amplifying strong change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician frames the work as collaborative and defers to the patient's expertise, repeatedly asking preferences and incorporating patient ideas into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits concrete change talk and specifics for actionable steps and backups, yet misses opportunities to amplify the strong '10/10' readiness and to further strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and the clinician redirects toward safety and doable steps, but could more actively soften sustain talk using double‑sided reflections to deepen ambivalence resolution.
