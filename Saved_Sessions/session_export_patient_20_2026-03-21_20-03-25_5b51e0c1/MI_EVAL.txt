# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Set a clear nonjudgmental tone and consistently validated Brandon’s perspective, avoiding labeling or moralizing throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and matched the client's pragmatic tone, but reflections were more content-focused and could probe deeper emotional nuance (e.g., frustration or shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use with concrete, behaviorally specific steps (safety screen, tracking plan, review) while balancing exploration and action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative: the clinician co-created plans, invited preferences, and offered tailored help while including the client in decision-making at every step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Brandon’s freedom to choose, offered multiple options, and used non-coercive language that reinforced the client as decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited client-generated ideas (booze-free nights, caps, tracking) with open questions but at times shifted quickly to offering solutions rather than deepening the client's own expressed reasons and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the work as joint and invited Brandon’s input on specifics (methods, timing, refusal lines), avoiding an expert-imperative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated it into concrete actions, though there were missed opportunities to amplify importance, consequences, and personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used neutral reflections and low-risk options to reduce defensiveness, but could more often use double-sided reflections and selective summaries to tip the balance toward change.
