# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validation (e.g., “I’m here to support you,” empathetic reflections) and avoids judgment about Marcus’s reluctance to give up the dog.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and accurate content reflections (names overwhelm and attachment to the dog) but misses deeper, complex emotional reflections about Marcus’s ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on asthma control and reducing home triggers to allow Marcus to keep the dog, moving from problem identification to concrete strategies and monitoring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely joint problem-solving with adapted recommendations for Marcus’s constraints, though the clinician frequently shifts into an expert/instructional mode with many concrete directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors Marcus’s choices, offers multiple options and trade-offs, and asks him to choose priorities, supporting his sense of control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes brief evocation (asking which option feels important, trial period) but mainly supplies solutions instead of eliciting Marcus’s internal motivations, values, or confidence about change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites Marcus’s input and co-creates a plan (7-day checklist, low-cost options) but could further elicit his own ideas rather than providing many technical solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some prompts elicit change language (concern about ER visits), but the clinician relies on information-giving and does not strategically evoke, amplify, or deepen Marcus’s own reasons and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and offers pragmatic alternatives that reduce pressure, but misses MI techniques like double-sided reflections to explicitly reframe ambivalence and shift sustain talk toward change.
