# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and legitimizes the patient's concerns with multiple validating statements, avoiding blame or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, repeated reflections capture both content and feeling and connect symptoms to life impact, reducing defensiveness and showing strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on securing an urgent gastro referral, tests, and a written management plan, repeatedly steering the conversation toward concrete action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative—invites the patient to shape wording, co-constructs the plan, and offers to act as an ally with the GP and clinic liaison, keeping the patient influential in decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, asks permission (e.g., to draft the letter), and frames decisions as the patient's, consistently affirming autonomy and avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits personal reasons and desired outcomes around testing but does not deeply explore intrinsic motivation, confidence, or longer-term engagement beyond diagnostic goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fosters shared decision-making by asking which options feel most doable and soliciting verbatim phrasing, positioning the patient as the expert on her experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits desire and reasons for change (returning to work, reduced fear) and prompts concrete actions, but could better amplify, summarize, and elicit commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and validates sustain talk while redirecting to actionable steps, though it uses few strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further tip toward change.
