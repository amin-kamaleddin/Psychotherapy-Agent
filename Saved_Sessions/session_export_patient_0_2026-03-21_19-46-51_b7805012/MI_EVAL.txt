# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with normalization and encouragement, avoiding blame and validating the patient's readiness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Set a supportive tone with safety checks and acknowledgments, but used few complex reflections to explore emotions, ambivalence, or underlying values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on meth use and its link to the infection and produced a logical, actionable stepwise plan and safety checklist.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited the patient's input and co-created a plan, but at times offered a clinician-led checklist rather than eliciting the patient's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered options, checked permission, and normalized slips to support choice, though occasional directive medical language reduced emphasis on the patient's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and for targets, leading to some patient intentions, but relied more on providing solutions than eliciting intrinsic motivations, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by asking permission and offering choices and supports, yet sometimes shifted into an expert-educational role that limited power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some concrete change language and goals, but missed opportunities to reflect, amplify, and explore the patient's change statements to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoided confrontation and offered harm-reduction, but occasional strong directive statements (e.g., 'single best way') risked reinforcing resistance instead of using more double-sided reflections.
