# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and affirming with normalizing reflections and repeated praise. Occasional cautionary language (e.g., 'I'm worried') could be experienced as mildly distancing rather than pure unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening with strong early summaries and validating comments. Could deepen emotional exploration (shame, grief, pride) with more complex reflections to fully capture underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remains tightly focused on target behaviors and safety with concrete, time-bound next steps. Clinician consistently links actions to the client's core value of being a steady dad and guides toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely team-oriented with offers to role-play and invitations for client input, avoiding an expert-only stance. Could be strengthened by more explicit shared decision-making (e.g., having the client summarize agreed steps or co-create success criteria).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice by offering options, asking preferences, and framing plans as the client's decisions. Avoids coercive language and reinforces client ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies the client's own motivations using values-based questions and targeted scaling follow-ups. Clinician draws out client-generated reasons and uses them to shape actionable plans rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance through permission-seeking, scripts, and role-play, inviting the client's choices and plans. To reach a top score, the clinician could share more explicit power‑sharing language and confirm readiness before moving into directive planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with strategic questions, scaling, and follow-ups that draw concrete commitments. Reinforces and summarizes client commitments and asks for specific first steps to strengthen motivation and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk but tends to move quickly into logistics and problem-solving rather than exploring the meaning of reluctance. Limited use of double-sided reflections or reframing—more exploration of fears about losing independence would help soften sustain talk.
