# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently nonjudgmental and respectful (offers choices about morning vs evening, asks consent for check‑in). Normalizes slips and avoids blame. Could show more individualized warmth and empathic affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some basic understanding demonstrated (acknowledges motivation, normalizes slips), but limited use of reflective or affect‑focused listening to mirror Marcus’s anxiety and ambivalence; clinician moves quickly to instruction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on target behaviors: specific morning checklist, alarms, meter/pillbox placement, and scheduled 5‑minute follow‑up. Repeatedly returns to concrete plan and safety guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician involves patient in several decisions (timing, consent, call details) but largely presents a preformed checklist and education rather than co‑creating the plan with Marcus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly offers choices (text vs call), asks consent, and emphasizes restart after slips—supports patient control. Could further encourage patient‑generated strategies to reach top score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some preferences/barriers (which part feels hardest; morning vs evening) but often supplies solutions and education instead of systematically drawing out Marcus’s own reasons or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Interaction framed cooperatively at times (scheduling based on patient availability), but clinician expertise dominates with long instructional segments; patient contributions not consistently used to shape plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient offers change language (“I want to do it,” “I’ll try”), but clinician rarely reflects or amplifies these statements or asks evocative questions to deepen desire, reasons, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and effectively softens it (normalizes slips, offers noncoercive accountability options). Could use more double‑sided reflections linking sustain talk to change reasons for a higher score.
