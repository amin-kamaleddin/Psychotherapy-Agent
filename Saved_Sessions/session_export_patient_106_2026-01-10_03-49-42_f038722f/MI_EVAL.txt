# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard—validates stress, normalizes uncertainty, and praises realistic effort without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening with accurate summaries and timely reflections that deepen rapport and allow the patient to expand.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on smoking change tied to parenting concerns and guides the session toward a single attainable behavioral target with concrete planning steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative—clinician invites the patient’s preferences, offers options, and consistently asks the patient to accept, modify, or reject suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, emphasizing no demand for perfection, and leaving control over decisions with the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from within the patient by exploring discrepancy with parenting values, using importance/confidence rulers, and having the patient generate concrete steps and commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as a joint effort, privileges the patient’s expertise, and co-constructs decisions (e.g., gum placement, alarm, check-in) demonstrating genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and strengthens change talk through rulers, options for small changes, reinforcement of change language, and elicitation of implementation intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward child-focused goals, but occasionally normalizes continuation (e.g., allowing the coping tool) so could use more selective or double-sided reflections to amplify change talk.
