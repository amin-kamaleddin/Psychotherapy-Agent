# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating—normalized slips and explicitly acknowledged shame and effort, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid empathic listening and accurate reflections that reduced distress, but a few reflections were cursory and the clinician often moved quickly to problem‑solving rather than deepening emotional content like persistent shame or family impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation with concrete, stepwise behavioral tasks (10‑minute delays, gum before coffee, alarms) and linked strategies to specific triggers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally co‑constructed plans and honored preferences, but at times the clinician supplied many specific recommendations and procedural instructions that shifted balance toward an expert‑driven approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and ownership—offered options, asked permission, honored the client's decisions, and encouraged the client to pick doable steps without pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation and linked strategies to valued reasons (e.g., daughter), using evocative questions and rehearsal, but could further draw out internal motives and confidence with scaling and deeper value exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with co‑created if‑then plans and invitations for input, though occasional directive advice and rapid lists of options reduced the sense of equal partnership at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete next steps and praise, but missed some opportunities to systematically amplify, summarize, and convert change language into stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing slips and redirecting to short experiments, but could more often use double‑sided reflections and explicitly acknowledge ambivalence to further defuse sustain talk.
