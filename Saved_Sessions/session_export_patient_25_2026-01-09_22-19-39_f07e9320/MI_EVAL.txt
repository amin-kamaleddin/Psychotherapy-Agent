# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through affirmations and normalizing language; no shaming or labeling observed. Opening and repeated supportive responses reinforced a nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening, acknowledging feelings and context and tailoring suggestions accordingly. Missed a few opportunities for deeper reflections before moving quickly to strategy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing drinking at social events and uses structured exercises to advance toward concrete behavioral steps. Every segment was tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely cooperative—invites patient to choose strategies and co‑practices role-plays rather than imposing responses. Collaboration was somewhat weakened by an early directive list that preempted eliciting the patient's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice by offering multiple options, asking which feel doable, and praising the patient's decisions. Autonomy was slightly undermined by an initial unsolicited list of advice without permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws out practical commitment and uses personal reasons (e.g., childcare) to coach concrete plans and rehearsals. Relies more on providing solutions than exploring deeper intrinsic motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some collaborative behaviors like offering options and inviting role-play, but begins with a directive, expert-style list without asking permission. The clinician felt more like the solution-provider than an equal partner overall.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk by asking which strategies feel doable and encouraging role-play, helping the patient articulate commitment. Could deepen change talk by systematically eliciting reasons, desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively redirects sustain talk by rehearsing refusals and strategies rather than debating pressure, preparing the patient for pushback. Could be strengthened by explicitly reflecting and normalizing ambivalence (double-sided reflections) before pivoting to strategy.
