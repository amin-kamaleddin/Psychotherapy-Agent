# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, with warm openings and repeated validation of the patient's efforts and choices. Avoids blaming language and conveys unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates clear empathic statements and some reflective listening, acknowledging stress and validating doubts. Misses deeper emotion-focused reflections and more complex summaries of ambivalence that would deepen empathic resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use with concrete, measurable goals and immediate next steps. Repeatedly brings the conversation back to tracking, goal-setting, and practical relapse/recovery plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works jointly with the patient to co-create plans, draft messages, and tailor suggestions to preferences. Slight imbalance as clinician often offers many concrete strategies rather than eliciting more of the patient's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and respecting pacing and optional supports. Frequently reinforces client ownership of decisions and avoids coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment-oriented behavior (goal-setting, choosing nights/support person) but relies heavily on clinician-provided solutions. Could use more open, values-eliciting questions to draw out intrinsic motivation and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative in planning and responsive to the patient's preferences, sharing power in decision-making. Would be strengthened by more explicit elicitation of the patient's own ideas before offering clinician-driven strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk, validating tentative plans and translating them into specific, measurable goals. Could further amplify reasons and values for change to strengthen desire and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and does not reinforce sustain talk, but uses few strategic softening techniques like double-sided reflections or reframing. Limited exploration of the costs of continued drinking and little active elicitation to shift sustain talk toward discrepancy.
