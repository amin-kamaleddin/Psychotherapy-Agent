# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician opens warmly and affirms Alexei’s decision, conveying respect and support, but occasionally slips into an expert, prescriptive stance (long lists of advice given without asking permission) which softens unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of basic understanding and validation of goals, but the clinician offers few deep reflections of Alexei’s fear, anxiety, or ambivalence and often moves quickly to information and strategies instead of emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol-related behavior and concrete change (refusal skills, avoidance, role‑play), with consistent redirection to actionable plans tied to Alexei’s goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician creates a team-like rehearsal atmosphere by role‑playing and inviting practice, but frequently provides scripted solutions rather than co-creating phrasing with Alexei, which reduces mutual influence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are offered and permission is sought for practice, and the clinician emphasizes Alexei’s control, but supplying many ready-made lines rather than eliciting his own options limits ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively links risks to Alexei’s values and invites planning and rehearsal to elicit motivation, though they could more often draw out and amplify Alexei’s own reasons, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally orients the work as collaborative and invites input, yet at times shifts to an expert role by presenting long lists without asking Alexei to choose or craft his own responses.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change‑relevant language is elicited and reinforced by linking alcohol risks to goals and using role‑play to consolidate plans, but the clinician could more intentionally amplify Alexei’s spoken change statements and elicit specific commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician normalizes and provides corrective information in response to sustain talk, but relies largely on information and skills rather than MI strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to explore ambivalence and soften sustain talk.
