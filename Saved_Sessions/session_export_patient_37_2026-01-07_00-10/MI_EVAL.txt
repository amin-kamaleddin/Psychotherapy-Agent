# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys clear unconditional positive regard throughout with normalizing, validating language and a warm, nonjudgmental tone. Repeated explicit acceptance statements support a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking track Elena's emotional shift, but reflections sometimes feel formulaic. The clinician could probe more deeply into the emotional core to reach maximal empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on the vaccine decision with consistent return to options, risks/benefits, and concrete next steps. Interaction progressively narrows toward a doable plan (one‑page summary → partner conversation → possible follow‑up).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed: clinician solicits preferences, integrates Elena's responses, and offers tailored materials. Language consistently frames clinician and client as partners rather than an expert directing the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasizes Elena's freedom to choose and the pace of decision-making and offers multiple options. Materials and recommendations are framed as tools to help her decide, avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits Elena's own motivations and produces meaningful preparatory change talk and a concrete plan. Opportunities remain to deepen internal motivations and to amplify change talk into stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently positions Elena as the expert on her priorities, asks which options would help most, and invites her to choose next steps. Power-sharing language supports a high partnership rating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and reinforces preparatory change talk that yields specific next steps (read summary, talk with partner, follow-up). Missed opportunities include explicitly eliciting commitment or confidence and amplifying tentative change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and the clinician redirects to planning and options, normalizing ambivalence. The clinician could more often use strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, selective emphasis of change reasons) and explore beliefs sustaining hesitancy.
