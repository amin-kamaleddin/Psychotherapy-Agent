# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Linda’s experience with a calm, nonjudgmental tone and explicit validations throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured ambivalence, though the clinician sometimes moved quickly to provide information, limiting deeper exploration of underlying guilt and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on improving controller adherence and related anxiety, with interventions logically moving from problem identification to specific, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited Linda’s input at each step and co‑constructed the plan (choice of reminders, text wording, role‑play), using inclusive language and responding to her preferences rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and control by offering multiple options, asking permission before giving information, and phrasing suggestions as invitations while affirming Linda’s pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motives, barriers, and a small commitment while drawing on patient-generated solutions, but shifted to providing clinical information relatively quickly and missed chances to elicit deeper reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the encounter as a joint exploration with frequent permission‑seeking and shared decision‑making, allowing Linda to steer which strategies to try.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced language favoring change with concrete, doable strategies and praise, but could have amplified and summarized change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk and avoided arguing while offering factual info and anxiety‑management options, though more double‑sided reflections or summaries could better resolve ambivalence.
