# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, warmth, and validation while normalizing difficulty and avoiding judgment; meets the highest standard for acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures content and affect, but reflections are sometimes repetitive and formulaic, limiting deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (evening short walks) and ties every question to making the plan realistic and actionable.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functions largely as an equal partner through joint problem-solving and co-constructed planning, though the clinician often leads sequencing and could increase client-nominated agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms choice and elicits the patient's preferred strategies rather than prescribing, but occasional expressions of belief edge toward influencing and could be balanced with more explicit options and permission statements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits Sofia's own reasons, strategies, and past successes, building on her strengths and drawing internal motivation rather than supplying solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative and respectful of Sofia as the expert, inviting choices and building the plan from her ideas, which reflects strong shared power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits, reinforces, and labels change talk with specific questions and prompts (small steps, past successes), effectively amplifying motivation and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without reinforcing it and redirects to problem-solving, but could use more strategic softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or eliciting discrepancy).
