# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard, warmth, and validation throughout, normalizing fear and allowing disclosure. No criticism or minimization was observed, fostering safety for the patient.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality empathic listening and accurate reflections were used, including mirroring language and normalizing physiological reactions. These moves helped the patient feel understood and continue sharing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained tightly focused on seeking medical assessment and communication, with concrete, actionable steps (voicemail, text, rehearsal). The clinician persistently guided toward practical goals without being forceful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was clearly co-created, with joint script development, rehearsals, and contingency planning driven by the patient's input. The clinician positioned themselves as a helper rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently upheld patient choice by offering multiple options, permission to delay action, and validating reluctance. Language emphasized the patient's control over timing and approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited from the patient's own concerns and small successes, with open questions and amplification of patient-generated reasons. The clinician drew out internal motivation and translated it into practical steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as a joint effort, offering choices and soliciting the patient's preferences frequently. Power-sharing language and invitation to decide pacing reinforced partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with barrier questions, options, and praise for small steps, producing patient commitments. Missed opportunities included more targeted elicitation (scaling, pros/cons, explicit desire/ability/commitment questions) to deepen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and reframed overwhelm into manageable options, acknowledging fear and reducing sustain talk's impact. Greater use of double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence summaries could have further softened sustain talk.
