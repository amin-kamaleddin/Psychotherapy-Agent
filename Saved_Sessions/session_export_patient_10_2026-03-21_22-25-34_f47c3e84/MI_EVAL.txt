# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, mirroring the client’s burden and explicitly normalizing difficulty. Acceptance language and contextual acknowledgment (single mother, short-term assistance) were used throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated highly accurate perspective-taking with timely, tailored reflections that labeled affect and combined emotion with content. Reflective listening deepened disclosure and helped the client feel heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on securing employment and overcoming childcare barriers, steering toward concrete problem-solving. Linked options to the employment goal and produced a time-bound plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Built an equal partnership by soliciting the client's priorities, co-constructing the plan, and sharing tasks (e.g., scripting messages). Tone and choices invited mutual problem-solving rather than expert prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering multiple options, asking which steps the client would try, and avoiding pressure. Encouraged the client to set pace and select doable actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations and concrete commitments (confidence=10; specific steps), but could further explore deeper values and emotional drivers to better anchor motivation beyond logistics. More targeted questions to amplify the client's own motivational statements would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the session as a joint effort, repeatedly deferred to the client's expertise, and invited co-creation of plans and scripts. Consistently asked for preferences and choices, reflecting strong MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited value-focused change talk and concrete commitments, asking about priorities and next steps. Missed some opportunities to amplify strong self-efficacy language (the client's '10') and deepen reasons, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and skillfully redirected to problem-solving and options, reducing focus on excuses. Could increase use of double-sided reflections to explicitly name ambivalence and further soften sustain talk.
