# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Anisa's context and normalizing relapse while affirming strengths. Could further deepen unconditional positive regard by explicitly naming and validating the client's shame at times.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate paraphrasing with occasional complex reflections of ambivalence. Could deepen affective understanding of shame and guilt and pause to cluster emotional content before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and moved logically from assessment to concrete action planning and rehearsal while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as an equal partnership: the clinician invited preferences, incorporated Anisa's selections, and co-constructed the plan. Client contributions substantially influenced the session's direction and strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and affirming that Anisa would choose which steps to try. When offering many options, a brief reminder that she can pick one or two helps preserve autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited motivation with open questions, scaling, and prompts that produced meaningful change talk about health and family. Could more systematically amplify and summarize the client's own reasons to consolidate stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the work as collaborative, repeatedly inviting Anisa to shape choices and asking which options felt doable. Occasionally offered multiple options before eliciting her ideas, but overall partnership was strong.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and encouraged specific, doable steps that built on past successes. Could further amplify the client's language about why quitting matters and explicitly elicit commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk through normalization and reframing, and redirected toward coping strategies and slip-as-information framing. Could use more double-sided reflections and avoid rapid listing of many strategies which can overwhelm and leave sustain talk unexamined.
