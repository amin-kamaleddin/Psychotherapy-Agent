# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance and normalized ambivalence (e.g., “That sounds painful but clarifying,” “It’s okay you’re not ‘all‑in’ yet”). No blaming or corrective comments; warm, supportive stance throughout. Suggested: individualize affirmations by naming specific strengths.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking; frequent accurate paraphrases of Elias’s core phrases and feelings. Reflections sometimes repetitive/verbatim rather than deeper or more complex; could use more inferential reflections to explore underlying shame/fear.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on a concrete behavioral target (15‑minute call). Questions and prompts oriented to actionable steps (line, rehearsal, reminder, backup text). Suggested: add a concise summary linking values to chosen action to consolidate.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Elias’s input and choices (offered options, asked preferences, offered role‑play). Operated as a partner rather than directive expert. Suggested: explicitly acknowledge Elias’s expertise about his kids to deepen shared decision‑making.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Supported choice and control throughout; preserved autonomy around being “not all‑in yet,” offered options and left decisions to Elias. Suggested: use brief permission checks before offering phrasing/advice to keep autonomy explicit.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited values and reasons for change (kids, rebuilding trust) and practical motivations; drew out concrete steps and backup plans. Missed some opportunities to probe DARN and confidence more systematically (e.g., scaling/confidence questions).

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnered stance—invited practice and choices, collaborative planning. Could more explicitly hand agenda control back to Elias at key turns (e.g., “What do you want to focus on next?”) and use summaries that ask Elias to choose the next step.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (desire/need, values linked to action); elicited concrete commitments (call, rehearsal). Did not consistently amplify change talk into stronger commitment language or follow with confidence/commitment scaling.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally and not challenged; clinician avoided reinforcing it but used mostly neutral reflections rather than strategic techniques (double‑sided reflections, querying downside of status quo) to actively soften sustain talk. Suggested: use double‑sided reflections and targeted questions about costs of staying the same.
