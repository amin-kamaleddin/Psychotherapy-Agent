# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed clear unconditional positive regard and a consistently nonjudgmental, validating tone, honoring the patient's requests and normalizing their struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and understanding of triggers and logistics, but reflections focused more on content than deeper affect (anxiety/fear) and missed opportunities for more complex emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and medication planning, progressing logically from assessment to concrete steps and message creation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited the patient to co-design portal and phone messages, iteratively revised wording, and confirmed decisions together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and respecting the patient's timing and preferences without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical motivation and commitment by converting desire into action-oriented plans, but did not deeply explore intrinsic motives, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership and power-sharing by treating the patient as an expert and co-creating the plan and messages based on the patient's input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created multiple opportunities for change talk and affirmed readiness, helping translate intent into concrete plans, but could have more often amplified spontaneous change talk with evocative follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers and redirected toward solutions without arguing, though using double-sided reflections or summaries would better validate ambivalence while guiding toward change.
