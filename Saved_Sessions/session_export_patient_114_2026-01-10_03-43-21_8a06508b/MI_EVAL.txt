# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, supportive tone and avoided blaming, offering harm-reduction rather than admonishment. Could add more normalizing statements and deeper expressions of unconditional positive regard to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used basic empathic acknowledgments and asked about consequences, showing interest in the patient's perspective. However, there were few complex reflections capturing ambivalence, and the clinician moved quickly to solutions instead of deepening understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and academic/legal consequences throughout the session. The work culminated in a concrete, behaviorally specific plan and mitigation letter aligned with the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as a joint enterprise with iterative drafting and revision of the letter and plan in response to the patient's precise requests. Decisions and wording were negotiated and authored by both clinician and patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, checked permission before intervening, and allowed the patient to dictate tone and content, supporting choice and control. The clinician avoided pressuring language and affirmed the patient's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitments like agreeing to a plan and course enrollment but did not robustly draw out the patient's internal motivations or values. Missed opportunities to use evocative questions to expand change language about education and future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked permission and incorporated the patient's wording across multiple revisions, demonstrating strong partnership and power-sharing. The clinician treated the patient's input as essential to the final product.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk by offering a plan and mitigation letter, prompting some change-oriented statements. However, the clinician often provided solutions and phrasing instead of using open questions to elicit and amplify the patient's own reasons and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used harm-reduction strategies that respected the patient's desire to maintain social ties while reducing risk. Could further weaken sustain talk by using more double-sided reflections and selectively amplifying tentative change talk.
