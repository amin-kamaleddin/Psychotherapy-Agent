# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's struggles with a warm, nonjudgmental tone. Reassurances and validations are used repeatedly throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic listening and accurate reflections of the patient's experience and exhaustion. Missed a few opportunities for deeper, complex reflections to explore ambivalence and values more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on concrete behavioral goals with stepwise strategies tied to the target behavior. Each exchange advances specific plans (if–then statements, alarms, tracking) toward greater consistency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co‑creates concrete plans and repeatedly checks patient preferences while integrating worries and choices. The process is collaborative but somewhat clinician-led in problem solving rather than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Supports autonomy by offering multiple options and inviting choice at each decision point, framing suggestions as options rather than prescriptions. Emphasizes client ownership and counts micro‑versions as real wins.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation and commitment (e.g., two‑week trial, values language) but relies heavily on clinician-provided strategies. Could use more open, change‑oriented questions to draw out the patient's own reasons and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displays a cooperative stance and solicits preferences, but interaction leans toward expert-driven advice with many specific scripts. More elicitation of the patient's own ideas before offering solutions would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and strengthens change talk by converting values into concrete plans and prompting commitment with specific if–then statements and tracking. Some change talk was scaffolded by clinician suggestions rather than elicited earlier from the patient.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes concerns while offering very low‑effort alternatives and compassion lines. A few double‑sided reflections could further reduce sustain talk when ambivalence appears.
