# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and accommodates preferences (e.g., shortening Saturday). Feedback felt professional and polite but lacked more personalized affirmations or deeper normalization of the client's experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are basic reflections and acknowledgement of burden, but the clinician moves quickly into logistics without exploring or reflecting the client's emotional experience or using more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on scheduling and workload throughout, with concrete next steps and a specific action (generate .ics). The clinician consistently maintains appropriate directional focus on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers options, solicits preferences, and summarizes edits so the client shapes the plan, but much of the detailed template and technical solution is clinician-driven rather than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers choices and affirms the client's control, asks permission for actions, and implements requested edits, reinforcing the client's autonomy throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to elicit the client's internal motivations, values, or confidence; the clinician primarily supplies solutions without asking questions to draw out personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician invites input on format and edits and accepts the client's preferences, but the interaction leans toward the clinician as the planner rather than equal agenda-setting and idea generation by the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration prompts led to client agreement to try the plan, but the clinician missed opportunities to evoke and amplify the client's own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** When the client raised objections, the clinician adapted the plan rather than arguing, effectively avoiding reinforcement of sustain talk; could be improved by explicitly reflecting ambivalence.
