# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating (e.g., opening summary, explicit support line), normalizing ambivalence and avoiding shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and reflective listening that captures the health-vs-belonging tension, but relies on concise paraphrases and misses opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of affect and underlying values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking behavior and moves logically from readiness assessment to concrete options and a specific plan, including role-play and rehearsal to support change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as a true partnership: clinician invites Elise's input at every step, co-creates language and strategies, and tailors interventions to her social style.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice (e.g., 'I’m here to support whatever you decide'), offers multiple options, and checks which approaches Elise prefers without applying pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation with a readiness ruler and practical options/role-play, but does not sufficiently probe personally meaningful reasons, confidence, or link changes to core values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly co-constructs the plan with Elise, invites permission to practice, and shares power by following her preferences and wording choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and supports change talk (readiness ruler, small-step options, commitment to patch+drink), but could more often reflect and amplify Elise’s own change language and probe confidence/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolls with sustain talk and offers compromise strategies to preserve belonging while reducing use, though using more double-sided reflections would further validate sustain talk while pivoting to change.
