# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized hesitation, validating difficulty without blame. Warm, steady acceptance supported the client's comfort in exploring small steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections captured both affect (chest tightness, nervousness) and behavior (staying home), deepening understanding. Empathic listening was consistent and appropriately matched the patient's tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on the target behavior and repeatedly returned to concrete, small steps (five-minute walk, same worker). Strongly translated ambivalence into actionable planning, though a final explicit commitment could strengthen closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner, co-creating the plan and soliciting the patient's preferences on route, time, and supports. Suggestions were offered and choice was consistently handed back to the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently upheld the patient's sense of choice by offering multiple options and checking preferences, avoiding pressuring language. This approach fostered ownership and reduced resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic reasons for change (less loneliness, feeling proud) and linked them to concrete steps. Could be strengthened by probing past successes, eliciting values, or using scaling/confidence questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and client-centered, inviting input and framing decisions as shared while emphasizing safety and comfort. The clinician consistently involved the patient in choosing details of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited desire and anticipated benefits well and reinforced hopeful signals, but stopped short of systematically amplifying or converting change talk into commitment language. Using scaling/confidence questions and selective amplification would build momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confronting it and reframed toward small, doable steps, which reduced resistance. Could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly name ambivalence then pivot to change talk.
