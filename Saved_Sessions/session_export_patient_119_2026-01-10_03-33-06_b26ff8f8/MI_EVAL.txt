# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (normalizing, affirming, reassuring). The clinician avoided blame and emphasized progress over perfection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening with several reflections and validations of the patient's concerns. Could deepen emotional resonance with more complex reflections around anxiety/tension and rationalizations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and related GI issues with concrete planning and a follow‑up. Repeatedly brought the conversation back to actionable steps tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—co‑wrote the GP message and solicited patient preferences and agreement to a plan. At times shifted into an expert/educator role by listing many specific strategies without first checking the patient's perspective on them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking which change she'd try, and affirming that progress not perfection is fine. No coercive language and frequent invitations for patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's own motivations and secured concrete commitment to a plan, linking actions to family goals. Could have probed more deeply for reasons, confidence, and past successes to amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership through joint planning and repeated invitations for input (e.g., GP message, selecting a strategy). Lost some partnership points by offering many unsolicited recommendations rather than negotiating advice first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and a specific commitment (plate cue, alarms, check‑in). To strengthen, could have spent more time reflecting and amplifying the patient's own reasons, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation and offered practical fixes, which is appropriate. However, the clinician often moved quickly to solutions and risk information instead of using double‑sided or strategic reflections to reframe and soften resistance.
