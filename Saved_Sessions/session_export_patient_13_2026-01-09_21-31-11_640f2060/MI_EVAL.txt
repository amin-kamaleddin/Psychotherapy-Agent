# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard by normalizing and validating the patient’s experience and avoiding judgment; a brief explicit acceptance of the current choice could be added. 

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate empathic reflections that capture both emotion and concrete barriers through the exchange; could occasionally deepen affect labeling (e.g., shame, guilt) when present.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (making initial contact) and keeps the conversation oriented to concrete, doable steps like scripts and checklists. 

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner in joint problem‑solving, inviting the patient’s preferences and co‑creating scripts and plans rather than prescribing a single course. 

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering options, asking what feels most doable, and avoiding pressuring language while offering help if invited. 

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits change talk and practical commitments by asking what would make a next step easier and prompting the patient to choose low‑threat options; could further draw out personal reasons/values for change. 

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership through permission‑seeking, collaborative choice among options, and offering to coach or look things up per the patient’s preference. 

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively evokes and reinforces change talk by offering feasible first steps, practicing scripts, and summarizing choices to strengthen commitment; could add brief scaling questions to deepen commitment. 

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively acknowledges and reframes avoidance with lower‑threat alternatives and manageable steps, but misses some opportunities for double‑sided reflections and selective amplification of embedded change talk.
