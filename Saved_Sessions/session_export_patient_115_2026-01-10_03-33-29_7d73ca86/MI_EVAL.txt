# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with validating statements and explicit acceptance of the patient's choice not to quit. Shows clear unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and acknowledges practical barriers, but sometimes moves quickly to solutions without deeper exploration of ambivalence or emotional meaning. Reflections are present but not consistently emotionally probing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on asthma control and related target behaviors, linking suggestions to clinical goals and progressing logically to concrete steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑creates the script, asks for preferences, and negotiates plans rather than imposing recommendations. Patient's ideas meaningfully shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding coercive language, which reinforces the patient's sense of control. Accepts the decision to cut down rather than quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation for small steps but relies more on providing solutions than drawing out intrinsic reasons or values. Missed opportunities to ask evocative questions or use scaling to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites input and co‑constructs plans, including role‑play and script creation, but sometimes shifts into an expert-driven list of recommendations. Greater elicitation of the patient's priorities before offering options would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports and reinforces small commitment language and practical steps, but does not consistently elicit or amplify the patient's own motivations, values, or deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively accepts and integrates sustain talk, offering pragmatic harm-reduction strategies and honoring the cutting‑down stance. Could increase use of double-sided reflections and strategic reframing to shift sustain talk toward change language more often.
