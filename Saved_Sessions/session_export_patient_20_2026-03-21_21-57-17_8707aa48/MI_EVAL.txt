# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., welcoming language, normalizing slips) and affirmed the client's honesty without labeling or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with complex reflections that named ambivalence, but missed some opportunities to probe deeper emotions or the personal meaning of consequences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on reducing problematic drinking and guided the session toward a concrete, time-bound plan and follow-up without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: solutions were co-constructed, strategies rehearsed together, and logistical details (check-in timing/format) were set jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering multiple low-effort options, asking permission for check-ins, and framing suggestions as choices rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations and used scaling to draw out reasons and confidence, but relied somewhat more on offering strategies than on deeply evoking long-term values or imagined outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated clear power-sharing by inviting Brandon's preferences, co-creating the plan, and treating him as the expert on his life throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk using questions, reflections, and scaling and elicited concrete plans, but could further target DARN elements and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used neutral, option-based language, but could more often use double-sided reflections or discrepancy-focused techniques to soften minimization.
