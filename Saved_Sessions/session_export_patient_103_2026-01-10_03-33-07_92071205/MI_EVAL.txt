# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician conveys respect and nonjudgmental stance with validating reflections and calm responses, but offers few individualized affirmations or deeper normalizing statements to reach a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking with reflective statements and repeated distress checks and adapts to patient preferences, though deeper emotional probing and complex reflections are limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on managing appointment delays and related stress, with every intervention tied directly to the stated target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as shared through rehearsal and preference checks, but frequently supplies ready-made verbatim lines rather than fully co-creating phrasing with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, honors the patient's preferences (e.g., switching to practical planning), and avoids pressuring language, reinforcing patient control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Largely solution-focused and clinician-driven, with minimal elicitation of the patient's internal motivations, values, or personally meaningful reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and adapts to the patient's requests, supporting shared decision-making, but frequent directive scripting reduces the sense of true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some behavioral commitment through scripts and rehearsal, yet does not explicitly draw out the patient's own reasons, importance, confidence, or past successes to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validates frustration and steers toward action to avoid escalation, but does not consistently use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or strategic summaries) to artfully soften sustain talk.
