# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, repeatedly validated the patient's wishes and avoided admonishment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening with timely, specific reflections and summaries that acknowledged frustration and preserved rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, patient-centered focus on dental care with concrete plans and checklist, intentionally not pressing the smoking target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving and co-created practical tools (question list, scripts), acting as a partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's choice and control by honoring requests to avoid smoking discussion and offering permission-based options and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not elicit internal motivations, values, or ambivalence about smoking; respected autonomy but missed low-pressure opportunities to evoke change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed a collaborative stance and invited patient input, co-creating materials, though some clinician-led recommendations made parts of the session more expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did little to draw out change talk about smoking—no scaling, pros/cons, or gentle evocation questions were used; only unelicited patient remarks appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk by honoring the boundary and not debating, but did not employ MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to actively soften sustain talk.
