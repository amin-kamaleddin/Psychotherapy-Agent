# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validation and normalizing statements, creating a warm, respectful tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and clarifying questions with accurate reflections, but missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections of Nadine’s emotions and broader impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on feeding, sleep, and maternal concerns with concrete, goal-directed guidance and safety thresholds throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was generally collaborative—asking permission and soliciting preferences—but often shifted into clinician-led information delivery rather than joint problem-solving and co-planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Nadine’s choice and control by offering options, framing recommendations as decisions for her to make, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some surface-level motivation (requests for strategies) but did not systematically draw out Nadine’s internal reasons, values, confidence, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and invited input, yet much of the session remained clinician-driven with limited co-creation of an individualized plan in Nadine’s own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-oriented language was elicited when offering strategies, but the clinician did not consistently use open questions or reflections to elicit, amplify, and build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized and acknowledged sustain talk and gently redirected toward manageable strategies, though more double-sided or strategic reflections could better resolve ambivalence.
