# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and respect, validated the patient's concerns, and avoided judgmental language throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening by mirroring frustration and summarizing the patient's practical needs and emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on inhaler management and concrete next steps, steering the conversation to actionable goals without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicited the patient's preferences at every turn, adapted to her time constraints, and co-constructed usable scripts and plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported patient control by offering choices, avoiding pressuring language, and asking permission before offering help or information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and used evocative questions, but missed opportunities to draw out deeper personal values and to assess confidence or commitment more systematically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the patient as an active expert in her care, repeatedly sought her input, and deferred to her judgment when tailoring the wording and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created opportunities and reinforced small instances of change talk (questions, alternatives, praise), but could have probed more for intrinsic reasons and values driving change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged skepticism while providing neutral facts and harm‑reduction strategies, though used few double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence exploration techniques.
