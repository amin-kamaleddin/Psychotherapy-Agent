# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating language (e.g., "It’s okay to be conflicted…", "that worry makes complete sense") throughout the session; clinician normalizes feelings and avoids labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent accurate reflections and summaries that capture affect and ambivalence (e.g., "I hear you feeling worried and torn"; "I hear hope and caution"), tying emotions to behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on contraception and harm-reduction strategies (IUD/implant info, sober outings, clinic calls); clinician asks targeted, behavior-linked questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work framed as joint decision-making; clinician solicits preferences and offers options (e.g., "Which of these would feel most doable…?", offers to gather info or plan outings).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms patient choice and pace ("It’s okay to move slowly," asks how patient prefers to receive info), avoids coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits patient reasons and values (family history, desire for reliable contraception) and asks open questions, but misses deeper follow-up on ratings (e.g., explore "eight" importance or past successes).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions interaction as partnership with concrete offers to help (gather facts, call clinics, arrange support); checks communication preferences and defers to patient pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient voices strong change reasons, and clinician reflects them, but clinician does not consistently amplify or sequence change talk into commitment (no follow-up on importance/confidence ratings or proximal commitments).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged and not reinforced ("I fold under pressure," etc.); clinician redirects to options/supports, though greater use of double-sided reflections could further reduce sustain talk.
