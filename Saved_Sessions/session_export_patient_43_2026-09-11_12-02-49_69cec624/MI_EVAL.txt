# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the patient’s low‑drama preference and options. Could add an explicit normalization of ambivalence or a statement of unconditional regard to reach a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective‑taking that matched the patient’s guarded but cooperative tone. Would benefit from more vivid emotional and complex reflections to access feelings behind rationalizations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, focused progression from broad options to a concrete, time‑limited plan (five of seven days, specific smokes to skip). Sequencing consistently moved the conversation toward measurable action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a true partnership with the patient invited into every decision and summaries reinforcing shared understanding. The clinician distributed expertise and co‑constructed the plan effectively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored the patient’s choice by offering options, validating low‑commitment approaches, and soliciting which supports felt easiest. This reinforced the patient’s ownership of the plan throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation and concrete commitment from the patient by asking what felt doable and how success would be noticed. The clinician elicited the patient’s own reasons, strategies, and specific commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and deferred to the patient’s judgment, repeatedly asking which options felt most doable. Co‑construction of the plan and invitation to define success demonstrated strong power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk, leading to specific, actionable language and goals (e.g., skip two cigarettes, five of seven days). Follow‑up questions about supports and success strengthened desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and instead redirected sustain talk toward small experiments and practical swaps, respecting the patient’s minimization. Could more frequently use double‑sided reflections and briefly explore personal costs to acknowledge sustain talk while pivoting to change.
