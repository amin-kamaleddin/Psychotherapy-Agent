# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating effort and normalizing slips to convey unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides basic empathic responses and some reflections but often moves quickly to suggestions; could use deeper, complex reflections of emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing orange juice with every intervention tied to concrete next steps, monitoring, and relapse-reset strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as a team with the patient—solicits preferences, offers options, and co-creates tools the patient approves while avoiding an authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and avoids coercion by offering multiple options, asking permission before drafting materials, and reinforcing patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces some motivation and commitment but leans toward offering solutions rather than systematically drawing out intrinsic motives and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally frames the work as collaborative and co-constructs the plan, though at times offers directives without first eliciting the patient's own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk, co-creating a practical plan and prompts that reinforce the patient's commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and offers harm-reduction options, but could more often use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both the pull of OJ and reasons for change.
