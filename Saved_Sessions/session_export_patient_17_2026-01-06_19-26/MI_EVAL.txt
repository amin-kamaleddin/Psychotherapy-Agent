# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized shame, affirming effort and avoiding blame. Could deepen by adding more individualized affirmations naming specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that captured stress and shame well. Missed a few opportunities for deeper, feeling-focused or double-sided reflections to surface greater affect and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus on smoking cessation and moved logically from values to concrete actions and implementation supports. Linked emotional content to actionable steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively invited the patient’s preferences, co-created the plan, and adjusted recommendations to her comfort (e.g., starting with tracking). The clinician worked as a teammate rather than issuing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered choices, asked permission before offering materials, and consistently respected the patient’s control over decisions. Did not press the patient to set a quit date and supported her selected pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient’s motivations and preparatory change behaviors (tracking, coping tools, shame phrase). Could more consistently use targeted evoking questions and scaling to deepen internal motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as an expert in her life, sought input at every step, and followed her choices for tools and formats. Power-sharing was evident in plan development and implementation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk around daughter and health reasons and helped translate desire into small actions. Missed some targeted questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not argue with sustain talk and redirected toward manageable steps, reframing prior attempts as commitment. Could increase use of double-sided reflections and explore costs of continuing to soften sustain talk more strategically.
