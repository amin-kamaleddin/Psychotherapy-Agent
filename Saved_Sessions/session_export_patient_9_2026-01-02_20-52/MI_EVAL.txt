**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating ("You’re not failing — your concern shows care"; "You’re doing important work"). Encouraged effort and respected feelings, though validations were somewhat brief and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:

- [x] Validated patient’s concerns and effort

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some empathic statements and acknowledgement of distress (“I’m here to listen and support you,” grounding/pause strategies). Missed multiple opportunities for reflective listening of emotional nuance (guilt, fear of being “bad cop”); reflections were limited and often followed immediately by advice.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:
- [x] Acknowledged distress and offered supportive statements

**Comments:** Strong, clear focus on specific behavior change (bedtime/screen limits), concrete two‑week experiment, multiple scripts, tracking sheet, and stepwise pause/de‑escalation plan. Excellent actionable guidance.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited some input and adapted materials when asked (e.g., offering script options, asking if she wanted the tracking sheet). However, clinician predominantly led with ready-made solutions rather than co‑constructing language and plan with the patient.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:

### Autonomy Support

**Comments:** Offered choices (script options, whether to use sheet), encouraged practice, and respected patient agency. At times directive in prescribing lines/plans without first eliciting her own solutions, limiting full autonomy support.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of patient’s own motivations or values. Patient expressed strong change talk (love for son, desire for stability) but clinician mainly supplied solutions instead of deepening or exploring those motivations with evocative questions.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:
- [x] Acknowledged patient’s desire for child’s wellbeing (but did not extensively evoke)

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tone avoided authoritarianism and clinician referred to working together occasionally, but clinician frequently assumed expert role and provided multiple preformed scripts rather than co‑authoring language with the patient.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:
- [x] Maintained respectful, non‑authoritarian tone

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session contained client change statements which were affirmed (“You care a lot, and that matters”), but clinician did not systematically amplify or elicit specifics to convert desire into commitment. Few strategic reflections or summaries to deepen change talk.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:
- [x] Reinforced patient’s expressed care and willingness to try tools

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged sustain-talk concerns (fear of conflict/"bad cop") and offered practical compromises (alternating enforcement, swap duties) that redirected toward workable solutions. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and deeper exploration of ambivalence.

Checked behaviors explicitly observed:
- [x] Acknowledged sustain-talk and provided compromise strategies (alternating enforcement, swap duties)
