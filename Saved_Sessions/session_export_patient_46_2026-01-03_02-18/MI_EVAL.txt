# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance

- [x] Normalized ambivalence ("you’re allowed both things")  
- [x] Avoided blaming language  
- [x] Validated competing goals (health vs. social life)

**Comments:** Clinician consistently used validating language (“I hear you,” “that push–pull makes total sense”), normalized ambivalence, and respected the patient’s perspective throughout.

### Empathy
- [x] Reflective listening used  
- [x] Summaries offered (accurately captured concerns)  
- [x] Elicited elaboration from patient  
- [ ] Probed deeper underlying feelings (not consistently done)

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflections and summaries (e.g., tired of waking up sick, not wanting to be buzzkill). Reflections were sometimes formulaic and could probe deeper emotions (embarrassment/fear) more.

### Direction

- [x] Kept conversation on concrete, behavior-linked goals

#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent steering to specific behavior change (no drinking on school nights, spacing drinks, friend backup) and to measurable outcomes.

### Collaboration

- [x] Shared control; patient-generated solutions emphasized  
- [ ] Explicit co‑construction of plan (limited)

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent invitations to choose options and patient-led solutions, though clinician occasionally assumed a mildly expert/encouraging stance rather than fully co‑constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
- [x] Emphasized patient choice and ownership  
- [x] Avoided pressuring language; accepted hedging ("not promising")  
- [x] Asked for the patient’s preferred approach

#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed autonomy (“you know your situation best,” “you’re allowed both things”) and respected the patient’s right to decide.

### Evocation
- [x] Drew out patient’s reasons for change (tired of being sick, protect practice)  
- [x] Used open questions to elicit alternatives and steps  
- [ ] Amplified/consolidated change talk into stronger commitment (missed)

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk and concrete steps (no-school-nights, friend backup) but missed opportunities to amplify and consolidate those statements into firmer commitment.

---

### Partnership
- [x] Treated patient as collaborator and expert on own life  
- [x] Invited evaluation of options and feasibility  
- [ ] More explicit joint planning language could strengthen partnership

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative tone with patient-driven choices; occasional encouraging expert statements weakened pure partnership slightly.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
- [x] Elicited concrete change talk repeatedly (fewer sick mornings, specific rules)  
- [x] Asked target questions that produced actionable language  
- [ ] Did not consistently amplify or convert change talk into committed actions

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple instances of change talk and practical steps were obtained, but clinician did not sufficiently consolidate these into a strong commitment or if–then implementation plan.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
- [x] Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk/ambivalence  
- [x] Reflected sustain language without confrontation  
- [ ] Did not consistently use double‑sided reflections or pivot sustain talk toward change

#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was recognized and legitimized, but the clinician sometimes mirrored hedging without redirecting toward discrepancy or converting hedges into solvable barriers.
