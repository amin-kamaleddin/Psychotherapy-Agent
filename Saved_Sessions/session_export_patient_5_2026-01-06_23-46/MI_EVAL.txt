# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with personalized statements and validation, and repeatedly honored the patient’s request about sending messages.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some perspective-taking and concise summaries of safety concerns, but reflections of feelings, ambivalence, and deeper emotional content are limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, goal-directed focus on the medication switch and safety logistics, consistently steering the interaction toward a concrete outcome (message and call).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong collaboration by co-authoring the message, checking format and timing preferences, and leaving decisions (when to send) to the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms patient choice with options and refusal to send without confirmation while appropriately providing necessary safety directives that are somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Primarily provides information and practical help rather than eliciting the patient’s internal motives, values, or readiness; few open questions to draw out motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative with repeated permission-seeking and shared creation of materials, though it could more explicitly elicit the patient’s priorities and trade-offs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports preparatory, concrete change-related actions (drafting/rehearsing the message) but does not explicitly evoke or deepen personal motivations, importance, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects to safety and planning, which prevents reinforcing sustain talk, but does not explicitly explore hesitation or use double-sided reflections to soften ambivalence.
