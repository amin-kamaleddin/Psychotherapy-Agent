# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates nonjudgmental, respectful regard and validates Milan’s perspective, affirming his competence and choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with timely empathic statements, though reflections are often summary-level and could probe deeper emotional or values-based meaning (e.g., faith, family).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on social drinking and moves the conversation toward actionable harm-reduction steps while balancing client-centered exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative: the clinician invites choices, asks permission for role-play, and co-constructs the plan so Milan’s ideas shape the strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly supported repeatedly (e.g., “that’s your choice”), with options offered rather than directives and consistent reinforcement of client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Milan’s reasons and preferences and gets concrete change language, but at times offers a detailed menu too early instead of first drawing out the client’s own motives and using scaling to amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong sense of partnership: the clinician power-shares, asks permission, and invites Milan to select and shape the steps and wording used in role-play.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete change talk and specific harm-reduction commitments (skip shots, alternate with water), yet could strengthen by amplifying, summarizing client change statements and eliciting commitment/confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and gently redirects sustain talk toward risk-reduction, but would benefit from more double-sided reflections to actively balance benefits and costs and further reduce sustain talk.
