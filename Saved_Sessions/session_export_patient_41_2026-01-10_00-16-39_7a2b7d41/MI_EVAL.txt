# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through warm, validating language. No instances of minimization or judgment were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated sustained, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating core emotions and ambivalence. Reflections tracked the patient's statements without prematurely shifting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, persistent focus on the specific target behavior and moved logically from problem identification to concrete implementation planning. Repeatedly returned to actionable details (day, timer, cue, backup plan, reward).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was co-constructed with frequent invitations for the patient's input and choices, and clinician contributions supplemented rather than overrode patient ideas. Co-created checklist and draft messages supported shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly preserved the patient's sense of choice by offering options and framing suggestions as collaborative offers. Clinician checked preferences (role-play vs. draft text) and avoided directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently elicited the patient's own motivations, reasons, and strategies, asking what would increase confidence and inviting self-generated solutions. Change ideas were drawn out and amplified rather than imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as a partnership, deferring to the patient's preferences and expertise and inviting selection among options. Power-sharing was evident in decision points and planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, reflections, and prompts for concrete steps that the patient endorsed. Clinician amplified desire, ability, and reasons for change and encouraged specific planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward change with validation and options, but missed a few opportunities for deliberate softening techniques like double-sided reflections or strategic summarization. Overall approach reduced resistance but could use more explicit sustain-talk reflections.
