# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance from the outset, validated Diego's feelings, and honored privacy requests without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking captured motives and fears, inviting elaboration and deepening rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on harm-reduction (Narcan, safer injecting) and consistently re-centered the conversation on concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative stance—co-created the text and handout, invited Diego's input at every step, and framed the plan as joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered options, checked preferences, and affirmed Diego's decision-making authority rather than pressuring for any particular choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out reasons and commitment (e.g., 9/10 confidence, specific text plan), but could have probed deeper into values and secured more specific timing or rehearsal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Diego as the expert on his life—asked him to choose supports and wording and shared power in creating the plan and materials.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited and reinforced change talk, producing clear commitments, but missed opportunities to amplify and summarize change language into a concrete, time-bound commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk neutrally and avoided arguing, yet did not employ more advanced softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or strategic summaries) to further reduce ambivalence.
