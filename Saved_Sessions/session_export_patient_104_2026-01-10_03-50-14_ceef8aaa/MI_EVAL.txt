# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized concerns (e.g., validating exhaustion and worry) without criticism. Language and tone were respectfully steady throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries accurately captured both content and feeling (e.g., fatigue, worry about milestones). Empathic listening was timely and guided tailored, supportive responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on maternal rest and infant feeding/diaper tracking with a logical progression to practical next steps (checklist, tracking grid). The session stayed on topic and organized actionable tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as a problem-solving partner by soliciting preferences and offering to tailor tools, but sometimes provided solutions before fully eliciting the patient's own ideas. Strong collaboration overall with some missed opportunities for co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and framed suggestions as optional (e.g., asking which method feels doable, allowing estimates). Reinforced patient control over decisions and who helps at night.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited priorities and practical motivations (which to track, who can help) and used these to propose strategies. Could have probed deeper into internal motives, expected benefits, and confidence to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited the patient to choose between options and offered to customize materials, sharing power in decisions. At times the clinician supplied concrete advice without first co-creating solutions, making it somewhat clinician-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited readiness and choices (e.g., selecting rest and tracking) but did not consistently deepen or amplify desire, ability, reasons, or commitment. Missed follow-ups on importance/confidence and opportunities to reflect and strengthen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded empathically to skepticism and worry, normalized concerns, and offered very small doable steps to defuse sustain talk. Would benefit from more strategic double-sided reflections or selectively amplifying brief pro-change statements.
