# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—normalizes use of smoking for stress relief and affirms choices without judgment. Tone remains validating even when patient expresses strong sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses multiple accurate reflections and perspective-taking that mirror the patient’s lived experience (stress relief, low‑fuss needs). Demonstrates high‑quality empathic listening through summaries and paraphrases.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on the daytime NRT trial and concrete, measurable goals without coercion. Questions and reflections consistently return to planning, dosing, tracking, and supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in joint problem-solving by offering options, asking preferences, and incorporating the patient’s choices into the plan. The patient’s suggestions shape concrete next steps (samples, tracking method, pickup).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces the patient’s freedom to choose and explicitly protects the after‑work cigarette, framing supports as optional. These moves foster a strong sense of ownership over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits the patient’s own motivations, values, and practical strategies rather than imposing reasons to change. Draws out specific indicators of success and builds on the patient’s language to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invites co‑creation and asks permission before acting, positioning the patient as the primary decision‑maker. Power is clearly shared and the clinician offers to help set up supports the patient requests.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change language with targeted open questions and reflections, prompting concrete planning (patch + gum, tracking). Summaries and reflections strengthen the patient’s own commitment to a short trial.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and acknowledges sustain talk while protecting the patient’s evening smoke, which reduces defensiveness. Could be slightly more deliberate with double‑sided reflections or explicit summaries of ambivalence to better pivot sustain talk toward change.
