# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed consistent unconditional positive regard (opening support, asking permission, nonjudgmental language) and avoided shaming, creating warmth and respect throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and short reflections that matched Marcus’s tone and priorities, but missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections of underlying feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on weekend alcohol use and harm reduction, tying each intervention to practical behavior-linked outcomes without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—co‑created the plan, offered role‑play, and repeatedly checked Marcus’s preferences, framing the interaction as a team effort rather than a lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Marcus’s control and choice (offering options, praising voluntary steps, acknowledging he need not follow everything), reinforcing his agency throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change-focused steps and reasons (avoid trouble, parental concerns), but could better draw out intrinsic values, long-term goals, and use scaling to evoke stronger internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and sought input (asking permission, inviting choices), though the clinician sometimes supplied many clinician-generated options instead of inviting Marcus to generate more solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked and reinforced change talk (choice of strategies, praise for the plan) which produced concrete commitments, but could probe reasons and confidence more deeply to amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or reinforcing sustain talk and shifted to neutral harm‑reduction strategies, though using more double-sided reflections and summaries could better acknowledge and pivot from sustain talk.
