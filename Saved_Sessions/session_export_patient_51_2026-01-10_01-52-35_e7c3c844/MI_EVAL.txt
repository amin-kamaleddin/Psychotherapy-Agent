# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician establishes a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the patient's actions repeatedly, reducing blame. Affirmations feel somewhat formulaic and lack individualized reflective warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are basic validations and acknowledgement of seriousness, but the clinician does not reflect the patient's worry or rushed affect. Responses are largely informational rather than emotionally attuned.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication safety and the risks of combining citalopram and phenelzine. The clinician stays on task with no drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers follow-up and service-oriented language but the encounter is primarily one-way instruction. There is limited elicitation of the patient's perspective or joint problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician supports autonomy by advising not to change meds without prescriber guidance and inviting follow-up, which respects choice. However, firm safety directives and limited permission-seeking make the language somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides necessary information and affirms the patient's plan but does not elicit the patient's motivations, values, or confidence. There are no open prompts to draw out change talk or personal reasons for safety.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some invitations for future collaboration are offered, but the interaction remains expert-driven with limited sharing of control. Few moments invite the patient's expertise or preferences beyond acceptance of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reinforces the patient's change-oriented statements, likely strengthening immediate commitment. However, there is little active elicitation of additional change talk through open questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids amplifying the patient's minor sustain talk (time pressure) and redirects to concrete safety steps, effectively minimizing resistance. A stronger approach would use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while steering toward action.
