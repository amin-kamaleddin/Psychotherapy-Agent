### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respect and nonjudgment (e.g., “I’m here to support your choices,” normalization of struggle). Occasional evaluative praise (“I’m proud you…”) slightly shifted focus from pure acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (summarized feelings, acknowledged relief/nervousness). Missed opportunities for deeper, more complex emotional reflections (e.g., explore the affect behind “I feel gross”).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, sustained focus on behavior change and implementation (scheduling, fallback options, alarm wording, scripts). Skillful, goal-directed questioning that moved session to a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-creation of the plan—offered options, asked preferences, and deferred to Aisha’s choices throughout. Power-sharing evident in phrasing and decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed client choice and control (explicit offers, options for alarm wording, fallback permissions). Avoided pressuring language and reinforced ownership of plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited past supports and motivations (short workouts, sister walks, check-ins) and led problem-solving around barriers. Could more directly probe meaning of change and use tools to strengthen commitment/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership shown by asking permission and centering Aisha’s preferences. Slight clinician-centric affirmations (“I’m proud of you”) briefly re-centered clinician rather than further empowering client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple change statements and planning language; reinforced commitments (scheduling, alarm, fallback). Could amplify and summarize Aisha’s “I really want…” statements to build stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully reduced all-or-nothing thinking with permission to scale down and fallback plans (“10 minutes OK”), normalizing worry about bailing. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections explicitly acknowledging both pull toward and away from change.
