# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed warm, nonjudgmental acceptance with validating reflections and affirmation of constraints, and accepted a harm‑reduction goal rather than pushing abstinence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening and accurate reflections that matched the client's concerns, though several reflections were somewhat formulaic and could be deepened with more complex emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on reducing evening smoking and steered conversation toward concrete, actionable strategies while respecting the client's stated limit about quitting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a clear partner through open, invitational questions and built the plan together, shaping decisions by the client's preferences and lived experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and ownership by offering options, validating the client's preference to cut down, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively evoked internal motivations and resources by eliciting values (protecting her son), specific plans, and past strengths rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance with frequent invitations to choose and co‑construction of the plan, though some prompts were clinician‑led and could more explicitly negotiate the clinician's role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced DARN change talk and asked about anticipated differences, but could amplify, summarize commitment language more strongly and include a confidence/commitment scaling question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward practical steps, but could more proactively use double‑sided reflections or selectively highlight discrepancy to resolve ambivalence.
