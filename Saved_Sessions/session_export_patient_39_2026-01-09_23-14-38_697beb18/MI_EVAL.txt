# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing feelings and reframing setbacks as not failures. Warmth and repeated affirmations reduce shame and validate the patient's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, affect-focused reflections capture Elias's frustration and peaceful moments, making him feel heard and understood. The clinician matches emotional shifts and explicitly validates worries about forgetting or feeling like a failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on increasing small, safe physical activity and creates a concrete action plan with stop rules, schedule, and contingency steps. Summaries and checklists keep the interaction task-oriented and progressive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invites Elias's input, co-creates the plan, and tailors scripts and tools to his preferences, reflecting genuine teamwork. Suggestions are adjusted to Elias's comfort and decisions throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are emphasized throughout, with permission to stop, multiple options offered, and reassurance that any decision is acceptable. The clinician avoids pressuring language and frames suggestions as voluntary, reinforcing control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation is elicited (choice of activity, remembering peacefulness), but the clinician relies heavily on offering solutions rather than systematically drawing out values, reasons, and confidence. Deeper open questions about importance and meaning are limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with invitations for preference and shared decision-making, but at times the clinician shifts to detailed recommendations without first eliciting Elias's own strategies. This slightly reduces the sense that Elias fully shaped every element.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces concrete change talk and implementation intentions (specific activity, schedule, log), leading to commitment. Opportunities remain to deepen and amplify reasons, importance, and confidence to strengthen sustained motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed without arguing, normalizing barriers and reducing shame about stopping early. However, detailed procedural advice at times risks focusing attention on barriers rather than further softening sustain arguments.
