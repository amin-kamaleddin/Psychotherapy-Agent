# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, honored the patient's stated goal of harm reduction, and avoided stigmatizing language while offering practical options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening, safety checking, and responsiveness to the patient's constraints, but missed opportunities to reflect and validate shame/guardedness more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on intranasal meth use and concrete, behavior-related goals (safer-use steps, testing, disposal) with repeated redirection to practical strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acted largely as an equal partner—checking preferences and offering follow-up help—but often supplied solutions instead of eliciting the patient's priorities or co-creating an implementation plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported autonomy by offering choices, honoring the patient's preference for harm reduction over quitting, and avoiding pressure to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some prompts invited choice between options and reinforced health concerns, but the interaction was mainly informational with limited use of evocative questions or confidence/importance scaling to draw out motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed collaboration through choice and offers to help find resources, yet missed moments to invite the patient to select specific steps and jointly problem-solve barriers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were brief invitations that could elicit change talk and emphasis on health consequences, but the clinician relied more on giving information than on eliciting the patient's own reasons, desires, or plans for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation and respected the patient's framing of recreational use, shifting to harm reduction rather than arguing; stronger double-sided reflections or strategic summaries could further soften sustain talk.
