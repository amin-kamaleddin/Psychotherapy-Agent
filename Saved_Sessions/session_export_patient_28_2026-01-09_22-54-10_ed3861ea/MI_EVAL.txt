# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Dante’s struggle with validating language and no judgment; phrasing was slightly formulaic at times but remained supportive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captured values and barriers and helped Dante expand; could deepen emotional nuance (e.g., discouragement/shame) with more complex reflections to strengthen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity via a brief family walk and steadily moved toward a concrete action without drifting to other topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative and egalitarian, with the clinician soliciting preferences, offering options, and co‑creating the plan rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Dante’s choice, offered options, checked permission before advising, and reinforced his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivation, values, and past supports to inform a small action, but could better amplify spontaneous change language and press for stronger commitment/confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Oriented the session as a partnership by offering options, asking permission, and following Dante’s preferences, clearly sharing control of the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced multiple pieces of change talk (desire for energy, past success, 10‑minute walk) but missed chances to selectively amplify key statements and to probe for a commitment/confidence rating or verbal commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and redirected toward planning, yet relied mainly on neutral reflections and normalization rather than using double‑sided reflections or explicit if‑then coping plans to further reduce resistance.
