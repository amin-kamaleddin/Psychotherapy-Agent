# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, conveyed unconditional positive regard, validated feelings, and avoided labeling while responding calmly to defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently demonstrated empathic listening and accurate reflections of feelings and situation, but many reflections were brief and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotion or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained focus on immediate problems (rent, flatmates, auditions) and practical targets, but did not consistently link or set a clear change agenda around methamphetamine use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Solicited the client's input at every step and co-constructed the checklist, messages, and rehearsals, demonstrating strong reciprocal collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered options, respected the client's choices, avoided pressuring language, and provided tools that preserved the client's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some practical motivations and intentions but relied heavily on offering solutions rather than systematically drawing out the client's own reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited the client's preferences and followed their lead, sharing power and co-creating steps like breathing, brainstorming, and role-play.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created space for change talk and elicited intentions, but did not consistently reflect, amplify, or summarize the client's change language before moving to solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation, but did not employ MI-specific strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections, reframes) to soften or shift ambivalence toward change.
