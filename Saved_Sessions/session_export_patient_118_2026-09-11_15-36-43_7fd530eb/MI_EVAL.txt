# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, acknowledges the patient's priorities and avoids lecturing or criticizing while accommodating a low‑key approach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective‑taking that validates frustration, but could deepen empathy by exploring emotional tone and the meaning of repeated failures with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on smoking reduction tied to the patient's dental goal and repeatedly guides the conversation toward a specific, actionable first step (30‑minute delay).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in joint problem‑solving by soliciting the patient's input, offering options, and co‑constructing the plan with the patient's choices shaping next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the patient's sense of choice by offering multiple options, honoring refusals of meds or big plans, and asking how the patient would like to be supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits patient‑generated motivation through focused questions and reflections, which led the patient to propose and commit to a concrete, doable change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites the patient's expertise and preferences, defers to their choices, and negotiates support in a way that centers the patient in decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and strengthens change language by offering options, reflecting tentative change talk, and pressing gently for a realistic, short‑term goal that the patient accepted.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and accepts resistance while pivoting to alternatives, though could more strategically use double‑sided reflections or summaries to better integrate ambivalence and reduce sustain talk.
