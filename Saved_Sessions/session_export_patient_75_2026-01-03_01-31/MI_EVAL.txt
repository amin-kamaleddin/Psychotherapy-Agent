# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance. Multiple explicit validations (e.g., “You’re understandably frightened…,” “You’re doing a great job naming worries and limits”) normalized Linda’s concerns and acknowledged difficulty without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate understanding of feelings and concrete concerns (e.g., good summaries of fear, sleep/walking goals). Missed opportunities for deeper affect‑focused reflections and more complex empathy tracking of recurrent “It’s really difficult.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept session tightly focused on inhaler use and a measurable plan (4‑week low‑dose trial, 1‑week phone check, 4‑week follow‑up, tracking metrics). Repeatedly returned to specifics and organized concrete next steps (drafted written plan, contacting PCP).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated patient as partner: asked preferences/permission, incorporated wording changes and husband attending demo, offered to copy patient on communications and draft plan for PCP — genuine shared planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported patient choice and control (offered options, emphasized time‑limited trial and stop criteria, asked “Does that feel okay?”). Avoided coercive language and reinforced decision authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation (open opening question, asked about specific worries) but session often shifted to provision of information/recommendations rather than systematically eliciting Linda’s own motives, values, or confidence (fewer follow‑up questions to draw out intrinsic reasons).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely fostered power sharing (asked for wording approval, co‑constructed plan). Brief drift toward clinician as medical expert when giving definitive medical information — appropriate but reduced purely client‑led tone at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflected and reinforced some change talk (desire to sleep and walk the dog; tentative agreement to trial) but missed multiple opportunities to amplify and deepen commitment (no scaling, limited follow‑up on “I might try” or “I’d try something different if…”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and redirected toward manageable, time‑limited options; effectively reduced defensiveness. Could have used more double‑sided reflections and explicit summaries contrasting sustain vs change language to help resolve ambivalence.
