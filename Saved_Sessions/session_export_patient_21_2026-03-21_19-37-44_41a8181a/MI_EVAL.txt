# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and respect with affirmations, normalization, and non‑judgmental language; maintains a warm, supportive stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective‑taking and brief reflective listening that validate the patient's concerns and constraints; could deepen empathy with more complex reflections exploring emotional nuance and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on increasing physical activity with clear, actionable steps and a short‑term monitoring plan; maintains momentum without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑creates the plan, solicits preferences (cue, check‑in format, timing) and adapts recommendations to the patient's constraints; frames the interaction as joint problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms the patient's choice and control by offering options, asking preferences, and avoiding pressure; consistently preserves patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some barriers and preferences but primarily provides plans and tips rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations, values, or past successes; more open questions and amplification of change talk are needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance with invitations for input and shared planning, but the interaction occasionally becomes clinician‑led and somewhat prescriptive; more explicit invitations for patient‑generated options would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces some change talk and arranges follow‑up, yet misses systematic opportunities to deepen reasons, importance, and confidence; tends to shift to offering solutions rather than amplifying the patient's motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk, normalizes concerns, and offers flexible, acceptable options and backups without arguing; could further soften sustain talk with double‑sided reflections and more strategic pivots.
