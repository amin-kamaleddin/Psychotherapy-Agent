### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Tyler's perspective without blame, de‑escalating defensiveness throughout the encounter.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Used reflective listening, validation, and permission‑seeking which supported rapport, but reflections were generally surface‑level and missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings like shame or belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on the presenting behavior and immediate, practical goals (de‑escalation, stash management, harm‑reduction) and did not drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored Tyler's choices, framed multiple options, avoided pressuring him to quit, and offered strategies he could accept without committing to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some invitation to choose options was present, but the clinician relied more on offering scripts and strategies than eliciting Tyler's own values, reasons, or discrepancies for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asking permission and co‑constructing scripts—but frequently shifted to directive coaching and ready‑made solutions that reduced client‑generated input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked about choices and offered harm‑reduction, yet moved quickly into scripted advice instead of pausing to elicit and amplify Tyler's own motivations, worries, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
