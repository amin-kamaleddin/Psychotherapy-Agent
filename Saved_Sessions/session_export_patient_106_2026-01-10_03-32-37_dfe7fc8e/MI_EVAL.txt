# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, using reflections and affirmations to accept ambivalence and frame recommendations as options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic listening and pragmatic reflections, but missed deeper emotional reflections (e.g., underlying stress or loneliness of single parenting).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing child exposure with practical, measurable steps, a concrete plan, and a scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—asked for input, adapted the plan to the patient's constraints, and positioned the clinician as a partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, respecting limits, and framing suggestions as voluntary rather than prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation (start day, tracking) but relied more on offering solutions than systematically eliciting the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely co-constructed the plan with the patient and invited contributions, though early prescriptive moments slightly reduced shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generated some commitment-like language through planning, but did not extensively probe or amplify the patient's motivations, values, or change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed toward realistic harm-reduction steps that respect constraints, though could have used more double-sided reflections to address ambivalence.
