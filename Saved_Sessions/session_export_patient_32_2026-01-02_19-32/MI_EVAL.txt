# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated goals (e.g., “Thanks for being honest — that was a serious episode and your goals...make sense”). A more personalized normalizing statement about ambivalence would raise this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate paraphrase and perspective-taking were present (e.g., “I hear pressure from old friends and loneliness…”), and clinician checked readiness/confidence. Reflections tended to be brief rather than complex/emotion‑focused (missed deeper reflection of isolation/pride).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on harm‑reduction (don’t drive, $30 keychain, alarm, Marcus, role‑play) and built specific, actionable steps and contingencies — appropriate and consistent with safety goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician co‑created the plan (asked which step, amount, timing, who to tell; invited role‑play) and elicited client wording. Occasional directive language and frequent suggestions slightly shifted power toward clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly asked for Derrick’s choices and used readiness/confidence rulers, and accepted his reluctance to quit drinking. Occasional phrases that implied expectation (e.g., “I’ll expect that,” “You’ve earned this…”) reduced perceived autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited client change language and strengthened commitment (confidence rose 7→9), using rulers and rehearsal. Could have further evoked deeper motivational reasons/values (linking license/job to meaning).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Role‑play, asking for exact client wording, and shared planning supported partnership. Some clinician-driven suggestions (alarm label, exact supports) limited the sense that the client fully shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple clear instances of change talk were elicited and reinforced (e.g., “I won’t drive if I do,” commitment to keychain/alarm); clinician used rulers and specificity. Missed opportunities to amplify and summarize those statements to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing with minimizations and redirected to practical alternatives, reducing sustain talk’s impact. Did not use explicit double‑sided reflections to acknowledge both minimization and consequences (e.g., “on one hand…on the other hand…”), which could strengthen softening sustain talk.
