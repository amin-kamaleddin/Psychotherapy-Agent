# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician shows clear unconditional positive regard, asks permission, and validates the patient's experience without judgment. Multiple affirmations reinforce acceptance and avoid stigmatizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening with accurate reflections and summaries of the patient's context (shifts, stress, smoking). Reflections are tailored and acknowledge emotional and practical constraints.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on smoking reduction and NRT strategies throughout the session. Questions and information are consistently tied to a concrete, testable plan for the next shift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner by asking permission, offering options, and co-designing a feasible plan (patch timing, gum use, buying options). Offers to help find low‑cost solutions and provides practical scripts and checklists.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering multiple options and framing actions as trialable and optional. Respects the patient's preference not to quit immediately and tailors recommendations accordingly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivations and a specific behavioral experiment (16‑hr patch + gum for a workweek) and prompts preference statements. However, the clinician could do more to evoke deeper intrinsic reasons, values, and confidence/importance ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong partnership through permission requests, eliciting preferences, and shared decision-making. The clinician genuinely co-constructs a realistic plan that fits the patient's schedule and constraints.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change‑oriented language and affirms the patient's test plan, operationalizing it clearly. Missed opportunities remain to deepen and amplify personal reasons for change and to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reframes sustain talk toward harm reduction without arguing, which limits reinforcement of sustain talk. Could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections and link sustain talk to values to increase discrepancy.
