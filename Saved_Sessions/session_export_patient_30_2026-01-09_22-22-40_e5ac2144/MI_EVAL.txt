# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's concerns, offering respectful, workable options (e.g., supervised lifestyle trial) without blame or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows clear validation and perspective-taking (e.g., acknowledging worries and preferences) but leans toward information-giving rather than deeper reflective listening of ambivalence and emotional tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the decision about cholesterol medication and practical next steps, repeatedly guiding the visit toward risk/benefit discussion, monitoring, and follow-up actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a team effort, offers tools (tailored questions, script, note) and co-creates the plan, responding to the patient's requests and preferences throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice by offering options, asking what the patient wants, and avoiding coercion, clearly reinforcing that the decision belongs to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits basic preferences and requests but relies heavily on providing information rather than systematically drawing out the patient's personal motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fosters a collaborative stance by asking permission and tailoring materials and plans, though could more consistently check understanding and invite the patient's ideas for implementation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some steps toward change (risk calculation, note, lifestyle trial) but misses opportunities to amplify the patient's own reasons, confidence, and commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes concerns, avoids arguing, and offers concrete contingency plans that reduce sustain talk, though more double-sided reflections could further shift ambivalence toward change.
