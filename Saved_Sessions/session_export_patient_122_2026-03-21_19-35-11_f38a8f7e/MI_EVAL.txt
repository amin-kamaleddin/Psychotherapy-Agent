# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth with frequent normalization and validation, and offered self‑compassion language to reduce self‑criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and perspective‑taking with accurate reflections and validations, but relied mostly on brief normalizations rather than deeper, complex reflections of underlying emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing light physical activity and repeatedly guided the conversation toward concrete planning, scheduling, and implementation strategies without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as true collaboration—clinician routinely invited Sofia’s input and incorporated her preferences into alarm text, friend nudge, backup plan, and self‑compassion wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and control by offering options, asking which the patient preferred, and using gentle, nonpressuring language that honored Sofia’s limits and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation was present in asking the patient to choose steps and schedule nights, but the clinician relied more on practical suggestions than on eliciting deeper personal motivations, values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the work as collaborative and honored Sofia’s expertise by asking her to pick wording, nights, and backup plans and co‑creating the implementation details together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively encouraged and reinforced change talk by eliciting a specific, achievable plan and commitment and amplifying client language, though could have more directly explored the patient’s reasons and used importance/confidence elicitation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing worries, offering nonjudgmental backup strategies, and reframing misses as data, but could further use double‑sided reflections and elicit the patient’s own resolution of ambivalence.
