# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing ambivalence and respecting Kareem’s choices without shaming or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, empathic understanding via open questions and reflective summaries that captured Kareem’s stress, family priorities, and past quit challenges.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking/harm reduction and moved from exploration to a concrete, measurable plan (patch + lozenges and one‑week check‑in) while staying client‑centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative problem‑solving: solicited preferences repeatedly and integrated Kareem’s choices on products, timing, packaging, and follow‑up into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by asking permission, framing options as Kareem’s choice, offering low‑pressure alternatives, and honoring his refusal to promise quitting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good initial evocation through values and past attempts, but shifted quickly to information and missed chances to deepen intrinsic motivation and vividly connect values to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Kareem as the expert and co‑created the plan with shared decision‑making (selection of NRT, timing, samples, and discreet packaging).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change talk and secured a tentative patch trial, but missed opportunities to amplify spontaneous change statements and deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well with acceptance and harm‑reduction pivots, but could more actively weaken sustain talk by summarizing value–behavior discrepancies and selectively reinforcing change‑leaning ambivalence.
