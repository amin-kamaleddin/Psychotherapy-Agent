# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validated ambivalence, and consistently offered choice without pressure, demonstrating unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately named ambivalence and situational barriers, though reflections stayed more cognitive/behavioral and could probe deeper into emotional meaning and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on smoking reduction with progressive, behaviorally specific steps (delay first smoke, one fewer cigarette, three-shift test) and purposeful narrowing toward an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician and patient co-designed the plan (flavored water, cue, test, backup) with shared decision-making and mutual respect, reflecting true equal‑partner problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice, offered multiple options, and framed suggestions as negotiable, supporting Hana’s ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effective use of open questions, options, scaling, and eliciting specific steps drew motivation and practical solutions from the patient rather than imposing them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—clinician invited Hana’s expertise, asked which options fit her life, and co-created a realistic plan together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician successfully elicited and amplified change talk, converting openness into concrete, time‑bound commitments (delay first smoke, three‑shift test) and reinforced those commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged with double-sided reflections and normalization, but the clinician could more actively elicit discrepancy or summarize costs of staying the same to further shift weight toward change.
