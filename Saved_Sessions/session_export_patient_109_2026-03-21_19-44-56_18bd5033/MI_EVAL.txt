# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating Rafael's struggle and affirming his efforts throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with targeted reflections about barriers, but missed deeper emotional reflections that would capture discouragement or the meaning of exercise for Rafael.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused orientation on returning to exercise with concrete steps, troubleshooting, and a specific follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—co-created plans and tailored message options—but could have invited Rafael's own strategies earlier and checked fit with his routines more often.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering multiple options, asking permission before assisting, and respecting Rafael's preferences about check‑ins and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation through eliciting choices and logistical commitments, but did not sufficiently draw out Rafael's internal motivations, values, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and co‑created the plan, though could more explicitly foreground Rafael's expertise and elicit his ideas before offering clinician-generated options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively encouraged and amplified change talk with small, concrete options and reinforcement, but stopped short of systematically eliciting deeper reasons or stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing barriers and offering alternatives, but relied more on practical problem-solving than reflective double‑sided strategies to resolve ambivalence.
