# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and affirming progress throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated feelings, though deeper, more complex reflections of underlying values or fears were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on staying clean, relapse prevention, employment, and rebuilding trust with concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with co-created plans and choices offered, but occasional expert-driven instructions slightly reduced equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged choice and preferences but used controlling, directive language at times (e.g., repeated commands to act immediately), which undermined autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations and commitments (e.g., calling Sam, 30/60/90 goals) but often offered solutions rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons and values for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displayed collaborative elements and invited input, yet frequently adopted an expert/problem-solver stance that limited power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforced change language and concrete steps (22 days, role-play, scripts), but missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivations and personal meaning of change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation but moved quickly to problem-solving, using few double-sided reflections or explicit pivots away from resistance.
