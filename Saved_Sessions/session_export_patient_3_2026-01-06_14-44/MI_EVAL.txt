# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shows consistent respect and nonjudgmental regard, normalizing setbacks and offering self‑compassion without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates perspective‑taking and reflective acceptance of barriers, but offers fewer deep, emotion‑led reflections of the patient's distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, goal‑directed focus on restarting exercise with concrete scheduling, contingency plans, and actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is cooperative and adaptive to patient preferences, though the clinician often provides the content and phrasing rather than co‑creating it with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers multiple options and invitations to choose, supporting choice, but some advice is fairly prescriptive and could more explicitly affirm the patient's freedom to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some questions to elicit preferences and plans, but relies heavily on clinician‑generated solutions and misses chances to draw out values, strengths, and intrinsic reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and offers choices, supporting shared decision‑making, yet the agenda and many solutions remain clinician‑driven rather than fully co‑constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms existing change talk and builds practical plans, but misses opportunities to deepen and amplify the patient's own motivations with more open, evocative questions and selective reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes lapses, emphasizes small steps and backup plans to reduce discouragement, though more double‑sided reflections could further acknowledge ambivalence.
