# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, using validations and normalization while avoiding blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking and reflective listening with accurate summaries and validations of the patient's worries and emotions throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, sustained focus on smoking reduction tied to protecting the child and guided the conversation toward concrete if‑then plans and a specific reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conducted the session as a joint enterprise by inviting the patient's choices, co‑creating the plan, offering scripting/role‑play, and asking permission before adding details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced the patient's freedom to choose, offered options rather than directives, and checked preferences to preserve the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic motives (e.g., protecting her son) and used a confidence ruler, but could deepen evocation by asking for elaboration on motives and past successes before giving advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the patient as an active partner by asking which strategies felt doable, offering to script and practice, and incorporating her input into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk with open prompts and concrete steps, yet missed opportunities to amplify and elaborate the patient's change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argument and redirected sustain talk toward harm‑reduction options, though more double‑sided reflections could explicitly name ambivalence and further soften sustain talk.
