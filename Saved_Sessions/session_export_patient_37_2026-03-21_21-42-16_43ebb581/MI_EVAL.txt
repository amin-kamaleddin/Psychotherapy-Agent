# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through nonjudgmental reflections, permission-seeking, and validation. Normalized caution and offered options, maintaining a warm respectful stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate paraphrases and perspective-taking that helped Elena soften and redirect, with frequent checks about priorities. Could deepen empathic work by exploring affect more (e.g., reflecting fear or overwhelm) rather than mainly cognitive summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on vaccination planning and returned to relevant subtopics throughout the session. Ended with a concrete, behaviorally specific plan and avoided drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner by offering choices, inviting preferences, and co-constructing the plan (dates, take-home materials). Information was presented as an option rather than a directive, supporting shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by asking permission, offering multiple options, neutrally stating trade-offs, and honoring Elena’s preferences. Avoided pressure and explicitly validated choices like spacing to increase comfort.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited priorities and some planning language, drawing out tentative motivation and willingness to listen. Could strengthen evocation by more consistently inviting Elena’s own reasons for change and asking scaling/confidence questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Elena as the expert in her values, repeatedly asking which topic to start with and co-creating the plan. Demonstrated active power-sharing through joint scheduling and offering take-home resources.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited early change talk through open questions and options, prompting tentative planning language. Could better consolidate change talk by selectively reflecting and amplifying emerging statements of change and asking for reasons/importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged sustain-talk while offering balanced information and options, which redirected conversation productively. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections and summaries that explicitly tip the balance toward the client’s reasons for change.
