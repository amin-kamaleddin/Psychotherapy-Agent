# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing the patient's experience and avoiding blame throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic reflections and checks on safety and functioning, but could deepen emotional exploration to capture nuances like fear about caregiving and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on obtaining a medical evaluation and safety, moving efficiently from assessment to concrete planning (scripts, role-play, follow-up steps).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invites shared tasks (script writing, role-play, prompting) and shapes the plan around the patient's stated needs and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces patient choice by offering multiple options and asking which she prefers, avoiding coercive language and supporting ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment language (e.g., 'I'll try'), but relies more on providing tools than on drawing out the patient's internal motivators, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters power-sharing with repeated offers of choice and collaborative framing, positioning clinician as coach and teammate rather than an authoritative expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generates action-oriented statements through practical supports and scripts, but does not systematically elicit or amplify the patient's own reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids amplifying avoidance by validating fear and offering pragmatic, small steps that reduce barriers, though double-sided reflections could further acknowledge ambivalence.
