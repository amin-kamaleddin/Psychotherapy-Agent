# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, validating fears and affirming small steps; avoided labels and conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and empathy that mirrored Erik’s values and barriers, but at times moved quickly with multiple questions and could probe deeper emotional nuance about fears of medication and dependency.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on concrete behavioral targets and repeatedly steered toward an actionable first step (3‑day log/photos) linked to Erik’s values and routine.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a genuine partner in brainstorming practical strategies, invited Erik to weigh options, and co‑developed a plan tailored to his shift schedule rather than prescribing a single solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by offering multiple options and deferring to Erik’s preferences, affirming partial effort and avoiding pressure or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic motives by linking change to family and work and using scaling questions, but could have used more open questions and reflective summaries to deepen client‑generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited Erik’s input, asked which options felt doable, and shared decision‑making to foster genuine power‑sharing and ownership of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with evocative questions and scaling, yet missed some pauses and opportunities to amplify Erik’s own reasons into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well by normalizing concerns and avoiding argument, though could have used more double‑sided reflections to both validate worries and more explicitly steer toward change.
