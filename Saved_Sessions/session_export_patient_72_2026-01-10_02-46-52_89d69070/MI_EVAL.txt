# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician opened warmly and used affirming, nonjudgmental language throughout, showing respect for Leila's efforts; could add more individualized normalization and spontaneous praise of strengths to reach the top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged practical barriers and reflected the plan, but offered few affect-focused or deeper reflections about how the issues feel to Leila; more complex emotional reflections and validation are needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on restarting exercise with well-structured, actionable steps (call script, checklist, calendar entry) and sustained momentum toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction felt team-oriented with frequent preference checks and adaptations, but the clinician often supplied finished materials rather than co-constructing them with Leila.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choices, asked permission, and respected decisions, using language that preserved Leila's control and avoided pressuring wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of commitments occurred, but the clinician did not consistently draw out Leila's personal motivations or values with evocative questions and strategic reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent invitations for input supported collaboration, yet much content was clinician-generated; greater joint development of wording and plans would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician reinforced and translated Leila's change talk into concrete commitments and plans, but rarely probed deeper reasons or amplified intrinsic motivations behind the change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was redirected with practical backups and scripts, though the clinician could more intentionally use double-sided reflections or empathic reframing to resolve ambivalence.
