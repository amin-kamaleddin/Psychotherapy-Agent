# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Jamal’s feelings and avoiding blame; validations conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate listening and some reflective statements (e.g., reflecting frustration), but empathy remained at the surface and did not probe deeper emotions or meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the immediate target behavior and harm-minimizing, actionable steps (scripts, documentation, legal options) throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as joint problem-solving with role-play and tailored checklists, but the clinician often supplied detailed scripts instead of eliciting Jamal’s own wording more fully.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Jamal’s sense of choice and control by offering options, inviting decisions, and avoiding pressuring language while affirming his right to choose next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to elicit Jamal’s internal motivations, values, or reasons for change; clinician focused on providing solutions rather than drawing out intrinsic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with offers of choice and permission to practice, but frequent directive scripting shifted some authority back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of change language beyond procedural compliance; missed opportunities to explore values, long-term goals, or personal reasons to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and redirected to practical next steps which reduced its influence, though explicit techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) were not used.
