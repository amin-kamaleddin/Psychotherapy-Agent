# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, affirming Hana's choices and reinforcing small wins. Some responses were practical and instructional, which felt slightly transactional rather than deeply warm or normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed clear understanding of triggers and acknowledged Hana's experience, with timely reflections and praise. Reflections were brief and task-focused rather than exploring deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on smoking reduction with concrete, measurable actions and repeatedly returned to behavior-change tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving, invited Hana's input, and co-created plans with roleplay and follow-up. At times the clinician provided many prescriptive tips, which slightly reduced space for Hana to generate options first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored Hana's choice not to quit immediately, offered options, and asked permission before practicing or suggesting steps, reinforcing patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete, client-chosen steps and used practice/tracking to surface motivation, strengthening small commitments. Rarely probed broader intrinsic motives or values to deepen internal motivation and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative, soliciting Hana's judgments and co-creating plans while practicing together. However, frequent clinician-generated suggestions limited opportunities for Hana to lead solution generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced incremental change talk and specific commitments (e.g., delay + gum). Missed opportunities to amplify Hana's reasons for change and to use richer open-ended DARN questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and accepted Hana's readiness level, offering reduction strategies instead of pushing for cessation. Did not employ more advanced softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to actively resolve ambivalence when sustain talk appeared.
