# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Alessia’s experience, repeatedly affirming her pace and uncertainty. Nonjudgmental language was used throughout to support her stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with concise clinical reflections, but stayed more on cognitive/behavioral content. Missed some opportunities to explore deeper emotions and reflections were sometimes repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on safely switching from phenelzine to citalopram and the concrete planning needed. Conversation was repeatedly steered back to washout timing, monitoring, and actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and framed as joint problem‑solving, using inclusive language and soliciting the patient’s plans. The clinician positioned himself as a partner and offered practical assistance (script, reading, writing).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforced autonomy and avoided pressure, offering choices and honoring Alessia’s preferences about timing and supports. Repeatedly framed options rather than directives to strengthen ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited concrete intentions and commitment language (calling prescriber, getting BP checks, written confirmation). Could have amplified motivation more with summaries, confidence scaling, or values-based questions to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited co‑creation of the plan and treated expertise as shared with the patient, asking who to involve and what steps to prioritize. Demonstrated genuine power‑sharing and acceptance of the patient’s choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk through targeted questions that prompted concrete plans and steps. Successfully translated ambivalence into specific actions the patient committed to.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectfully acknowledged sustain talk and offered neutral options rather than arguing, validating caution while exploring options. Missed some strategic techniques (double‑sided reflections, selectively amplifying minor change talk) to more quickly redirect sustain talk into change language.
