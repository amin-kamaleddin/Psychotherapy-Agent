# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Selene’s experience (e.g., “It’s understandable to feel okay now…”). Accepts sustain‑talk without criticism and thanks the patient for honesty. No shaming or pressure; warm, respectful tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking (accurate summaries of constraints and preferences). Uses useful double‑sided reflections but missed opportunities to probe underlying emotions (stress/overwhelm) that drive forgetting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on medication adherence and a specific behavioral target. Uses agenda‑setting and choice, and closes with a concrete two‑week experiment and a day‑7 check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Offers options and invites patient input (choice of experiments, alarm time, check‑in preference). Co‑creates plan and honors patient decisions, reinforcing partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms patient control and choice (asks willingness, offers to check in, provides scripts as optional aids). Avoids coercion and supports agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and offers choices that invite patient solutions, but could more actively evoke and amplify Selene’s reasons for change (e.g., expand “I do want to stay healthy” into specific values/benefits).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance and joint problem‑solving (patient selects plan details; clinician offers supportive options and follow‑up). Treats patient as expert on her routine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some change talk present (“I do want to stay healthy,” willing to try), but clinician missed opportunities to reflect/amplify and to develop commitment language beyond a tentative “I’ll try.” Could ask follow‑ups that link health goals to personal values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Sustain‑talk accepted and reflected (normalizing “it’s not a big deal”), and clinician uses respectful language rather than confrontation. Could more often use double‑sided reflections and strategic phrasing to tip balance toward change.
