# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validating statements and normalization. Avoided blaming language and affirmed Liyana’s efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, consistent reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing Liyana’s feelings of discouragement, embarrassment, and isolation. Reflections tracked emotional context and practical barriers, showing strong empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on evening snacking, weight-related habits, and coping with discouragement while steering toward practical small changes. Targeted questions and concrete options kept momentum toward behavior change without losing emotional concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a collaborative partner by eliciting preferences and incorporating Liyana’s ideas (fruit choices, timing, stepping outside) into the plan. Clinician avoided directive language and repeatedly invited Liyana’s input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, leaving parental involvement to Liyana, and normalizing her right to decide. Actions reinforced ownership of decisions and respected adolescent autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some intrinsic motives and intentions (desire to feel less embarrassed, willingness to try experiments) and used scaling questions. Could deepen evocation by exploring personal values and more frequently amplifying Liyana’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a partner rather than an expert, using collaborative language and co-creating the plan with Liyana. Clinician offered suggestions as options and supported shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language with options and a confidence scale, and affirmed chosen steps like fruit swaps. Would benefit from more consistent amplification and reflection of explicit change talk to build stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to change-oriented strategies using normalization and contingency planning. Could further soften sustain talk by using more double-sided reflections that explicitly name both sides of ambivalence.
