### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance (opening normalization, frequent affirmations). Tone was warm and respectful, validating Matteo's plan-making without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking with accurate reflections and summaries that captured ambivalence and feelings. Clinician frequently paraphrased and checked understanding, showing high empathic engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use and progressed toward a specific, testable plan (avoid drinking before shifts, phone reminder, roommate support, Wednesday). Returned to concrete implementation details without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan with Matteo by offering options, inviting selection, drafting texts, and role‑playing responses. Patient preferences meaningfully shaped the intervention, reflecting a strong dyadic collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering multiple options and asking how to help rather than prescribing actions. Respected Matteo's pacing and reinforced that he chose the approach.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations and elicited self‑motivational statements (importance/confidence, reasons to test a change). Could deepen evocation by linking reductions to personally salient long‑term goals and more systematically amplifying change talk to build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Matteo's input and shared control of the agenda (choice of lines, roommate text, night to try). The clinician fostered genuine power‑sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited change talk and helped elicit a concrete small step that Matteo endorsed. Could improve by more frequently reflecting and amplifying Matteo's change statements and prompting explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain‑talking factors (social pressure, roommates) and redirected to harm‑reduction tactics without reinforcing sustain talk. Would benefit from more double‑sided reflections that validate the pull of sustain talk while gently tipping toward change.
