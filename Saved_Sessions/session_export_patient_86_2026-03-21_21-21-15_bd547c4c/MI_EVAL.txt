# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard (validations, repeated 'no pressure') and avoided moralizing or lecturing. Offered options and permission, maintaining a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, summarizing Marco's situation and guarded tone. Followed the patient's lead and checked meaning before offering information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking and progressed logically from eliciting perspective to tailored, practical options. Balanced non‑directiveness with purposeful guidance toward realistic next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-created materials and solicited preferences about format and content (shopping list, GP script, PDF). Frequent offers to adapt wording and follow Marco's lead show strong mutual problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and framed information as optional rather than prescriptive. Respected Marco's decision to delay starting and reinforced his control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical preferences and some preparatory change talk about cutting down, but did not probe deeper motives, values, or discrepancy to strengthen intrinsic motivation. Missed opportunities to explore 'why' or future benefits.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently asked permission, offered choices, and placed Marco in control of what to receive. Language and actions fostered shared decision‑making throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited preparatory change language (e.g., doable cutting‑down ideas) through open questions, but did not sufficiently amplify or deepen that talk or elicit commitment language. Opportunities to ask about pros, importance, or confidence were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided reinforcing sustain talk and rolled with resistance, redirecting to practical small steps rather than arguing. Could more explicitly use double‑sided reflections to validate sustain reasons while highlighting change reasons to help resolve ambivalence.
