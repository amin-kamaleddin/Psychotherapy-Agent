# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and never shames or dismisses concerns, honoring requests (e.g., omitting the child’s name). The respectful, nonjudgmental tone supports trust and engagement throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality empathic listening with accurate reflections, paraphrasing, and responsiveness to subtle affect shifts. Checks preferences and follows Elena’s emotional lead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on vaccine hesitancy and concrete next steps (PDF, scripts, questions) with little topic drift. Information is organized to support the decision goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages Elena as a co‑creator—solicits input, negotiates wording/formatting, and repeatedly checks priorities. The session reads as a genuine collaborative exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and framing recommendations as options rather than directives. Repeatedly reinforces that the final choice rests with Elena.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some internal reasoning and tentative change language but often shifts into information-giving rather than systematically drawing out values, importance, or confidence. More targeted elicitation of intrinsic motivations would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as collaborative, invites Elena to co-direct priorities, and negotiates next steps (e.g., PDF content and layout). Permission-seeking and shared decision-making are evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces some change talk that leads to tentative commitments (e.g., reading materials, bringing questions) but does not consistently amplify or probe importance/confidence. The clinician often moves quickly to information-giving instead of reflecting and building on change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes concerns to reduce resistance, offering balanced information rather than confronting sustain talk. However, the clinician relies more on corrective information than on strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to resolve ambivalence.
