# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated respect and nonjudgmental support (opening “I’m here to listen and support you,” repeated validations, gentle closings). No judgmental language; strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent accurate reflections and normalization of anxiety (“I hear how stressed and stretched you feel,” “Totally normal to feel anxious calling”). Mostly pragmatic responses; fewer deep or complex reflections of underlying emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on arranging childcare with concrete options, scripts, documents, exact action times (11:00, 3:00) and follow-up text instructions — strong, consistent guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered options and invited choice (“Which would you like to focus on first?”, “Want a script?”). Occasionally shifted to more directive/prescriptive language (repeated instructions and reminders).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed patient control and choice (ok to read script, celebrate any attempt, multiple options). Normalized pacing and supported self-directed decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of choices and commitments (neighbor first, call times) but limited elicitation of deeper motivations, values, or reasons for change (school/work meaning, long-term goals).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated session as joint problem-solving and adapted to patient preferences (accepted neighbor-first plan). At times provided step-by-step instructions without fully co-creating details (e.g., specific rewards, back-out strategies).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient expressed change talk (interest in EHS, cutting back days) but clinician mostly moved to logistics; limited amplification, no scaling or targeted elicitation to strengthen desire/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized barriers/sustain talk (anxiety about calls, paperwork), provided strategies (scripts, alarms, small rewards) that redirected toward doable steps; could use more double-sided reflections.
