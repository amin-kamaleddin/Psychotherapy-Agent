# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys strong unconditional positive regard, normalizing Ahmed’s stance and affirming his values without blame. There are no instances of labeling or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate, affective and complex reflections that mirror Ahmed’s language and capture both content and feeling. Reflections are timely and demonstrate strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on tobacco-related oral health and moves logically from problem identification to specific action planning. Questions and reflections consistently tie back to the target behavior and concrete outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly two-way and co-constructive, with repeated invitations for Ahmed’s input and choice. The clinician offers options and helps craft exact wording rather than issuing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports Ahmed’s freedom to choose, normalizes his position, and repeatedly asks which steps feel doable. Avoids pressuring language and explores Ahmed’s own criteria for change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Ahmed’s own reasons, priorities, and decision rules (e.g., proof, timelines, work impact). Uses those internally generated motivations to shape a patient-centered plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames decisions as Ahmed’s and invites his expertise about what will work, co-creating a plan he owns. Explicit power-sharing is evident in offering options and asking which he prefers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies genuine change statements (cutting back, brushing, mouthwash) and asks values-based questions, but stops short of deepening desire/ability/reasons/need or eliciting scaled commitment. More targeted elicitations and summarizing change talk to invite commitment would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently avoids arguing and uses reflective, double-sided language to acknowledge sustain talk while pivoting to harm-reduction strategies. This approach prevents sustain talk from dominating and reduces resistance.
