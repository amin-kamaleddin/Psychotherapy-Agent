# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized slips, and used warm validating language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and brief reflections that acknowledged frustration and worry, but reflections were often short and followed quickly by solutions rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on reducing impulsive eating with concrete, behavior-specific plans tied to identified triggers like the freezer ice cream.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with invitations to choose options and tailor strategies, though at times the clinician shifted into an advisory tone with long lists of tips.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician offered choices and invited commitments, supporting autonomy, but sometimes provided multiple strategies without first eliciting permission or the patient's openness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of concrete commitments and confidence ratings, but the session relied heavily on clinician-provided solutions rather than drawing out Marco's own motivations or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly partnered with the patient by asking preferences and inviting selection of steps, though frequent prescriptive suggestions slightly reduced co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicited specific commitments and used scaling, producing concrete change language, but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen Marco's spontaneous change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized, which prevented reinforcement, but the clinician moved quickly to problem-solving instead of using double-sided reflections or other MI techniques to reduce sustain talk's intensity.
