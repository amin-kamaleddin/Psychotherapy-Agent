# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a nonjudgmental, supportive tone and normalized ambivalence, but affirmations felt somewhat formulaic and could more explicitly acknowledge specific strengths or past successes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and paraphrases validated the teen's perspective and concerns, though the clinician relied on repetitive phrasing and could deepen empathic listening with occasional more complex reflections naming underlying values or emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept the conversation tightly focused on staying low-risk, linking questions to concrete strategies and a specific commitment to try one step this week, without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuine two-way problem solving: the clinician invited the patient's ideas, offered help sparingly, and used inclusive language to negotiate next steps together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offered multiple options, asked which felt realistic, and framed steps as the teen's choice, avoiding pressure and supporting ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited the patient's own strategies and motivations, but could have probed deeper into personal meaning or values (or used scaling) to further strengthen commitment and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the exchange as collaborative, distributed expertise to the teen, and invited joint brainstorming, demonstrating clear partnership throughout the encounter.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions, reflections, and reinforcement moved the teen from brainstorming to a concrete, testable step (bringing a soda) and strengthened desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimized and barriers were acknowledged without amplification, but the clinician could more strategically use double-sided reflections to validate reluctance while pivoting to change.
