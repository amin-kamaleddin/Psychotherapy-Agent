# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (opened with neutral summary; acknowledged preferences: “You don’t want pills — that’s fine.”; validated ambivalence and desire to avoid being “miserable”). No shaming. Suggestion: continue explicit normalizing language about ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., “You sound torn — smoking helps you unwind, but …”). Repeated paraphrases captured priorities (work hassles, kids smelling smoke). Could occasionally deepen emotional reflection (e.g., explore emotional relief from smoking).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on smoking and workplace goal; offered focused options and asked patient to choose; moved logically from exploration to concrete planning (patch, gum, start day, follow‑up). Goal‑directed throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed plan and offered choices (patch vs pills, follow‑up format/timing); clinician deferred to patient preferences and integrated his input (Monday start, discreet gum technique). Equal‑partner stance maintained.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed patient control and choices (“I respect that you still enjoy smoking,” option to be contacted only if needed). Avoided pressure; reinforced that slips aren’t failures. Autonomy actively supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete preferences and produced change talk (e.g., “Sure. I can quit.”; commitment to start Monday). Missed opportunity to probe deeper intrinsic motivations/values (e.g., more on meaning of kids not smelling smoke or importance/confidence scaling).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Team approach throughout: asked for preferences, negotiated plan details, invited patient to pick start date and follow‑up. Power sharing evident; could further invite patient summaries in his own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced several change statements and secured commitment (start date). Could more actively amplify change talk when it arose (e.g., reflect “You said ‘I can quit’ — what makes you say that now?”) and elicit DARN‑C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used double‑sided reflections and avoided arguing with sustain talk (“You sound torn…”). Additional targeted double‑sided reflections and flipping toward change when patient said “I like smoking” would further dilute sustain talk.
