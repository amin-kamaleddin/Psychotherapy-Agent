# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated respect and positive regard, validating concerns and thanking the patient. Tone remained nonjudgmental and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking with clear, plain-language summaries and tailored safety guidance. Could use more emotional reflections to explore worry or anxiety rather than staying mainly informational.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a strong, consistent focus on medication-safety tasks (MAOI/SSRI guidance, 14-day washout, urgent symptoms). Avoided drifting and repeatedly returned to concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked alongside the patient to co-author messages and phone scripts, making iterative edits per requests. Neither dominated nor deferred, resulting in clear shared outcomes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices (message vs phone coaching, wording options) and framed directives as recommendations. Respected the patient's preferences and checked consent before sending.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical preferences and next steps but did not systematically explore the patient's internal motivations, values, or confidence. Limited use of scaling or commitment questions to surface change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative approach with repeated invitations for input, line-by-line incorporation of edits, and shared decision-making. Power-sharing was evident in final content and delivery planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some action-oriented elicitation (preference for sending message, agreeing to wait), but clinician did not actively evoke or amplify change talk using importance, confidence, or commitment probes. Interaction focused more on information and co-creation than strategic evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing reasons for inaction and provided clear alternatives and safety steps, which reduced sustain talk. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
