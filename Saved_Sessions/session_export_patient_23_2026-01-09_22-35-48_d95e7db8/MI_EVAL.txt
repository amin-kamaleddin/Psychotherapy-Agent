# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental, respectful regard, normalized ambivalence and slips, and used supportive language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening validated Claudia’s experience and worries, though the clinician could have explored the emotional meaning of some statements more deeply before moving to information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use with summaries, concrete reduction strategies, and planned follow-up to sustain forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was a joint enterprise with frequent solicitation of Claudia’s preferences and incorporation of her choices, avoiding an authoritarian stance while co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strongly supported autonomy by asking permission to share information, offering multiple options, and letting Claudia choose specific nights, strategies, and reminder timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited change talk and readiness (0–10 scales) and invited concrete steps, but could have used more open questions to deepen intrinsic motivation and personal values-based reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative with permission-seeking and co-creation of the plan, though frequent clinician-provided suggestions slightly shifted control back toward the clinician’s expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change language, helped develop a specific plan and commitments, but missed some opportunities to amplify and elaborate Claudia’s own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided direct reinforcement of sustain talk but used limited double-sided reflections or strategic softening; the conversation often moved quickly to information and solutions rather than exploring the pull of drinking.
