# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and honored Amina’s boundaries with no judgment. Repeated offers of choice and explicit respect for limits met top standard for acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking with accurate, frequent reflections that matched Amina’s minimal responses. Validated the emotional context and respected her need for control and privacy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on immediate safety and rapport, with appropriate early safety questions. However, repeated restatements of offers limited progressive movement toward exploration or goal-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session read as a true collaborative process—options were offered rather than imposed and decisions were left to Amina. The clinician positioned themself as a partner, continually soliciting brief consent for checks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly affirmed Amina’s control over the interaction with repeated statements of choice and clear limits on intervention. Balanced duty to intervene for safety with respect for autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some invitations to talk were offered (topics, nodding) but MI-specific evocation strategies were underused. Missed opportunities to ask what brought her in today or what made her agree to stay to draw out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly cultivated partnership by asking for preferences and emphasizing co-ownership of the session. Repeatedly deferred to Amina’s expertise about her needs and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered topic choices but did not systematically evoke or amplify change talk. Could have used scaling, curiosity probes, or reflections that amplify any hint of desire, reason, or ability to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed the clinician’s role to reduce pressure, which effectively neutralized much sustain talk. Missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections or gentle pivots toward change when resistance appeared.
