# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating—normalized relapse and accepted limits, creating a safe space for ambivalence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong empathic listening and accurate reflections (validated frustration, named triggers), but could use more complex reflections to deepen emotional resonance and elicit feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation with concrete, measurable actions and a realistic follow-up plan (gum trial, evening ritual, one-week check‑in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—invited input and co‑created plans—but at times gave structured, clinician‑led instructions (detailed gum technique) that reduced some patient ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored autonomy by offering choices, respecting 'I’m not promising' language, and framing suggestions as optional experiments rather than mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivators (daughter’s worry, coughing, past quit) and had the patient describe smoking episodes, but could use more open evocation (values, scaling, deeper questions) to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and invited the patient's preferences when planning, though occasionally shifted to didactic instruction without explicit permission which weakened partnership slightly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and reinforced ability (noted month‑long quit) and small experiments, but did not consistently amplify or probe importance/confidence to deepen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully rolled with resistance and avoided argument, validating skepticism, but could more often use double‑sided reflections and amplify small signals toward change.
