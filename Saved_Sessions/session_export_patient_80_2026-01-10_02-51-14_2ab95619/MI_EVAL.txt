# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with explicit praise and normalization of worries; acknowledges progress and partner support throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective‑taking with reflections, grounding, and attention to cravings, but often shifts quickly into problem‑solving rather than exploring deeper affect or using more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on alcohol use and relapse prevention, moving toward specific, behaviorally concrete goals and actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong joint problem‑solving: offers options, invites choices, role‑plays and co‑constructs the plan with the client’s input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, asking which strategies feel doable, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation (cravings, confidence) but is largely clinician‑driven with many strategies; would benefit from more open questions and amplification of the client's own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and asks permission, but frequently provides specific recommendations before fully eliciting the client's own ideas and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk (planning, confidence question, doable steps) but misses opportunities to reflect, amplify, and deepen the client’s self‑motivating language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and normalizes partial change, yet could further soften ambivalence using more double‑sided reflections and summaries of both sides.
