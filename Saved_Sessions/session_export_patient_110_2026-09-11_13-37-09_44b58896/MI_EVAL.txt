# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, validating concerns about side effects and offering options rather than pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured ambivalence and values, but reflections tended to restate content more than explicitly naming underlying emotions like anxiety or worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on cholesterol management and actionable next steps throughout, efficiently moving from options to a concrete monitoring and medication plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as a team effort with frequent solicitations of the patient's priorities, though the clinician sometimes took the lead in drafting plans rather than co-authoring them with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choices, invited the patient's preferences, and avoided coercive language, consistently reinforcing the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited the patient's reasons for change (staying active with his wife) and practical motivations, but could have probed deeper into personal meaning and asked scaling or commitment questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician adopted a collaborative stance and asked permission before acting, but occasionally shifted control by proposing clinician-led solutions rather than inviting the patient to draft them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited and reinforced change talk and a low-dose trial commitment, yet missed opportunities to amplify values-based reasons and assess confidence or secure a stronger commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without confrontation, but the clinician seldom used strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to more actively soften reluctance and pivot toward change.
