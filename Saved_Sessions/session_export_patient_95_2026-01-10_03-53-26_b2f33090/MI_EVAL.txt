# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with multiple explicit validations and promises of nonjudgmental presence. Avoids blame or minimizing language even when asking direct safety questions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shows strong empathic listening with accurate, timely reflections of affect and content (hopelessness, fear, crying). Validates the patient's experience before moving to safety steps throughout the interaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on suicidal ideation and immediate safety with repeated assessment of plan, means, intent, and concrete mitigation steps. Balances purposeful direction with validating language.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acts as an equal partner by co-creating safety steps, responding to the patient's requests, and staying with the patient through calls and actions. Adjusts approach based on the patient's expressed needs and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and affirming decisions like trying to wait 24 hours. Provides safety guidance while preserving agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some intrinsic motivation (concern for mom, naming a small step) and amplifies protective actions, but does not extensively probe broader values or longer-term reasons for living. More targeted elicitation of what matters to the patient would strengthen evocation when feasible.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative, honors the patient's expertise, and invites patient choice in planning and interventions. Uses permission and shared decision-making to distribute power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces small change talk (waiting 24 hours, concern for mom) and reframes protective acts as meaningful. Could more consistently expand and deepen the patient's reasons for living beyond immediate safety steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while highlighting change-oriented behavior. Could employ more deliberate softening techniques (e.g., explicit ambivalence reflections or amplified sustain reflections) to further shift sustain talk.
