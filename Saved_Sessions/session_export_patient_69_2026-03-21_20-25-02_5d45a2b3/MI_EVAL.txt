# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client's autonomy, validating frustration and respecting boundaries about not discussing feelings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries that capture the client's task-focused stance, but reflections are surface-level and miss deeper affective nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on paperwork and wait-time concerns which suits a precontemplative client, though could more explicitly link tasks to longer-term engagement goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed—clinician repeatedly asks for preferences, offers options, and responds to the client's pacing and choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly affirms the client's control and choice, asks permission before acting, and lets the client decide what is recorded or raised with staff.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to draw out priorities with open questions, but does not probe, reflect, or amplify potential internal motivations or past successes to deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the decision-maker, checking permission and co-creating the plan for paperwork and staff communication.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses appropriate prompts to elicit change-related language but fails to follow up or amplify emerging statements, leaving responses at a logistical level.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and neutralizes sustain talk by validating and offering pragmatic options, though could use double-sided reflections to more actively soften sustain talk.
