# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated respect and nonjudgmental regard (“That sounds like a lot to carry,” “You’re taking practical, life‑saving steps — that matters.”). Accepted Diego’s harm‑reduction goals and offered concrete help without pressuring him to quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated, accurate reflections of feelings and perspective (“You’re carrying a lot… what feels most pressing to you?”; “You’re clear that safety comes first and you’re nervous…”). Reflections opened dialogue and supported rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus on harm reduction from outset (Narcan, safer injecting, informing housemate). Session steered toward actionable safety steps (role‑play, scripts, resource planning) while preserving patient choice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed plan with Diego (offered options, checked preferences, asked permission to role‑play). Inclusive language and shared decision‑making evident throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple choices offered and clinician respected Diego’s stated goals (did not push for quitting). Patient controlled pace and content (chose role‑play, order of tasks).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited Diego’s own reasons and plans (asked what’s most pressing, invited rehearsal). Built on strong change‑talk (multiple “10/10” readiness statements) and translated them into concrete steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician offered to work alongside Diego (help find programs, draft text, role‑play, run phone scripts). Decisions were jointly made and Diego’s expertise was solicited and honored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly elicited and reinforced change talk; noticed and built on Diego’s commitments (practice conversation, locating syringes, naloxone refills). Summaries and planning amplified readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and respected Diego’s choice not to quit, focusing on safety. Missed some strategic opportunities for active softening (double‑sided reflections, explicit linking of sustain talk to safety steps, brief probes for alternatives/barriers).
