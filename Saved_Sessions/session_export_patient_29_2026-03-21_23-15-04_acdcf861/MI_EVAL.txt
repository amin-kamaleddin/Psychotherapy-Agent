# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizing language, affirms David’s goals and avoids blame. Slight repetition of similar phrases felt a bit formulaic but acceptance was clear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening with accurate paraphrases and reflective summaries of priorities. Reflections are sometimes repetitive and could probe deeper emotional nuance (e.g., embarrassment, pressure).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and concrete sub‑targets, repeatedly refocusing and narrowing options to actionable next steps. Guidance toward a start‑low plan, one‑week check, and stop/switch script was steady.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructs the agenda and plan, offering expertise while repeatedly inviting David’s choices and sequencing tasks based on his priorities. Shows genuine two‑way problem solving and mutual influence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers options, emphasizes choice, and defers to David’s preferences, honoring his conditions for starting treatment. Repeatedly asks what he wants prioritized and commits to follow his preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits David’s motivations and preferred strategies and draws out reasons for change and a concrete plan. Could deepen evocation by asking for more elaboration, confidence/importance ratings, and implementation specifics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative with frequent choice language and invitations, soliciting input at every decision point. Responds to David’s priorities and shares power in decision making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and supports change talk, asking targeted questions and summarizing motivations which elicit concrete commitments. Could more often amplify/refelect in‑moment change statements, ask for confidence ratings, and elicit specific implementation steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids arguing for the status quo but uses few active softening techniques. Missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections, strategic reframing, or highlighting past successes to resolve ambivalence.
