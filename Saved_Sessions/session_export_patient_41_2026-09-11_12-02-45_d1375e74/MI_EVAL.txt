# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—validates efforts, normalizes struggle, and avoids judgment. Patient's attempts are repeatedly affirmed, supporting a high level of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately mirroring content and feeling. Reflections acknowledge hesitancy and self-criticism, helping the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior and consistently steers toward concrete next steps. Conversation is narrowed to a specific, actionable plan without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as genuine joint problem-solving with the patient invited into each decision point. Clinician modifies proposals based on the patient's responses, ensuring the plan is shaped by the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices and avoids pressuring language, reinforcing the patient's sense of control. Clinician asks what feels doable and offers help only when invited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's ideas, preferences, and a practical plan, drawing motivation from her own choices. Could deepen evocation by probing intrinsic reasons, values, and asking for importance/confidence or a commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative stance—asks permission, offers options, and co-constructs the plan with the patient. Power is shared and the patient's expertise about what will work is respected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete change talk and converts vague intent into a specific experiment and supports. Missed opportunities to further amplify reasons for change, elicit importance/confidence, or obtain a commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects concerns toward problem-solving and small steps. Relied more on neutral reflections and pragmatic solutions than on explicit double-sided reflections or eliciting the patient's statement of the downside of continued avoidance.
