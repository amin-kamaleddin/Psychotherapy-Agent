# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician showed respect and nonjudgmental support with validating statements and a warm tone, but the approach felt somewhat procedural and lacked extended normalization or explicit unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and timely, helping Mateo elaborate, but they remained more behavioral/logistical than deeply affect-focused (e.g., shame, fear, hope).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, appropriate focus on employment-related steps (re-entry center, scheduling Thursday 2pm) and consistently steered toward actionable goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction felt joint (choosing wording, role-play), but the clinician sometimes offered specific solutions quickly, which reduced space for Mateo to originate alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported Mateo's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and affirming his decisions, reinforcing ownership without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good use of evocation (vision questions, 0–10 confidence ruler) that elicited motivation and concrete plans, though deeper probing into personal values and feelings could strengthen intrinsic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician largely worked collaboratively—inviting input and choices—but at times took a more directive lead by providing drafts rather than co-constructing options with Mateo.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and supported change talk with questions about priorities and concrete commitments, but could more consistently amplify and summarize change statements to bolster commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided strengthening sustain talk and gently redirected ambivalence toward planning, though used limited strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to further soften sustain talk.
