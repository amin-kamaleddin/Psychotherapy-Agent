# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—normalized tension, validated worries, and affirmed persistence and organization throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing fear, frustration, and strengths with timely reflections and summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on obtaining tests/second opinion and modest self-management steps, keeping the conversation action-oriented and building toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner by soliciting priorities, co-creating the script, and offering help tailored to the patient's requests so the plan reflected her preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission before intervening, and framing actions as the patient's choice to preserve ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited the patient's motivations and past strengths, eliciting change talk, but at times supplied content (e.g., full script) rather than eliciting the patient's own wording and commitment measures.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint process, repeatedly invited the patient's input, and allowed her to choose which actions felt most doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced small concrete steps and past strengths that generated commitment language, but could more consistently amplify and elicit specifics and confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated reasons for seeking tests and avoided escalating sustain talk, using collaborative reframes, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and more targeted pivots toward change.
