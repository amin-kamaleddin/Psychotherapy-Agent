# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established a consistently nonjudgmental, supportive stance and repeatedly validated ambivalence and honesty. No shaming or labeling occurred and the clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing ambivalence and validating worry. Reflections were often brisk and pragmatic, with limited deeper exploration of emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing alcohol use with concrete goals, safety monitoring, and a time-limited check-in. The clinician consistently guided the conversation toward a specific, measurable plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with co-created check-ins and requests for patient reporting, creating a teamwork feel. However, frequent structured advice and many specific tactics sometimes made the interaction more clinician-led than jointly exploratory.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized patient choice, offered multiple options, and asked which the patient would be willing to try, preserving control. Advice was framed to support selection of doable strategies without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out motivation with questions about patterns, past attempts, and readiness, eliciting concrete intentions and plans. Could further evoke motivation by asking the patient's reasons for change and linking goals to personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited collaboration and choice and co-created the follow-up plan, fostering partnership. At times the clinician moved quickly into giving many recommendations, which shifted some control away from the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk through concrete evoking questions, readiness/confidence ratings, and offering choice of steps, and reinforced small steps. There was room to more often amplify and reflect change talk by exploring reasons and benefits to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing, normalized slips, and reframed noticing as progress to reduce resistance. Did not use more advanced softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) or deeply explore reasons for maintaining drinking to shift ambivalence further.
