# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens nonjudgmentally, offers safety check, and responds empathically to defensive remarks, conveying warm regard. Could add more individualized normalization of Erik’s specific concerns and brief affirmations of his strengths before moving into information-giving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and timely empathic reflections (e.g., “Totally understandable to feel rattled”) and attends to practical worries. Would benefit from deeper, double-sided reflections and exploring underlying emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on medication, monitoring, and alcohol reduction with clear, actionable next steps and tailored plans. Maintains consistent direction from the opening choice through concrete follow-up items.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input (scripting help, phrasing) and tailors language, which supports collaboration. However, the clinician largely supplies plans and targets rather than eliciting Erik’s solutions or negotiating recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options and alternative phrasings that reinforce Erik’s control over decisions and actions. Could more consistently ask permission before offering prescriptive recommendations and check willingness to try specific suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of Erik’s internal motivation; the exchange is primarily information- and solution-focused. Needs more open, evocative questions to draw out personal reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Begins with collaborative invitations and offers choices, but the interaction shifts to a clinician-led expert role with prescriptive steps. Partnership would improve by explicitly co-creating plans and inviting Erik to shape trackers and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and supports practical commitments (scripts, trackers, alcohol goals) but often moves into giving solutions instead of amplifying Erik’s own motivations. Should reflect and amplify change language and use evocative questions about meaning and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and does not confront minimizing statements, maintaining a neutral tone. Could more actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections and exploration of reasons for maintaining current behavior rather than mainly offering advice.
