# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental—normalized worries, affirmed short wins, and used validating language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking and accurate reflections, but missed opportunities to probe deeper into the client's shame and the emotional meaning of relapse.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, concrete focus on smoking cessation with scaling, a specific first goal, an explicit plan, and momentum toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a clear partnership with shared problem-solving—co‑creating plans, rehearsing scripts, and tailoring interventions to the client's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed choices, invited the client's preferences, and avoided pressure, explicitly honoring the client's right to accept or decline suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited personal reasons and small steps toward change, but could have pushed more for the client to verbalize her own reasons and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with frequent invitations for input, though offering multiple strategies in quick succession sometimes shifted perceived expertise to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and helped specify actionable goals, but could more often amplify and summarize the client's change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used reframing, yet relied more on problem-solving than targeted softening techniques (e.g., strategic pivots and selective reflections favoring change).
