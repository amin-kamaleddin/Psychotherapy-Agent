# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing ambivalence and affirming autonomy and effort without shaming or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and summaries that validate hope and worry; could deepen emotional exploration (e.g., shame, loss) and use more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on reducing gambling and immediate risk management, with each exchange advancing concrete behavioral steps and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is a joint effort—clinician offers templates/options and invites the patient to edit wording, choose contacts, and select role‑play types, demonstrating mutual problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and consent, framing offers as options, asking permission for exercises, and honoring the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations and personalized strategies (e.g., travel image, specific scripts) but could probe deeper into personal values and confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—clinician asks for preferences, offers choices, and co‑creates the plan so power and responsibility are shared with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with evocative, choice‑oriented questions and summarizes emerging commitments, moving the patient toward specific, testable actions.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward practical experiments, though more double‑sided reflections and strategic softening could further diminish sustain talk.
