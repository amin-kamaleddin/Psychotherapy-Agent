# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the patient’s feelings and repeatedly reassuring safety; conveyed warm unconditional positive regard appropriate for a vulnerable adolescent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking (acknowledging frustration, loneliness, symptoms), but some reflections were informational or shifted quickly to advice, missing deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on disordered-eating risks and safety concerns, moved efficiently from assessment to concrete medical and behavioral steps, and offered clear next actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt joint and co-constructive—invited preferences, adjusted plans based on dislikes, practiced responses, and sought consent before offering resources, fostering a collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and agency by offering options, asking permission, respecting boundaries, and avoiding coercive or shaming language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited useful information and some commitments but relied more on providing information and tools than on asking evocative questions to draw out the patient’s values and internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked which focus the patient preferred and offered choices—but occasionally provided expert-driven recommendations without first co-creating specifics with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language and reinforced it, yet did not systematically evoke, amplify, or sequence change talk and often moved to planning rather than exploring importance/confidence or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively acknowledged and normalized sustain-talk concerns and offered practical reframes, though some prescriptive medical language could be experienced as directive and would benefit from more double-sided reflections.
