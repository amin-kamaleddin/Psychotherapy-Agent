# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with validating statements and frequent thanks; no blaming or minimization observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate understanding and validation of caution were present, but the interaction was largely informational with few deeper reflective statements to explore feelings or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on medication safety and immediate next steps throughout the encounter, including creation of scripts and urgent messaging.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reflected equal partnership—clinician invited preferences, co‑authored messages, and respected patient requests for wording and format.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission to personalize, and framing advice as offers rather than commands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical actions but did not sufficiently draw out the patient's own motivations, values, or arguments for change with evocative open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the patient as a co‑author of the plan, asking what they wanted and collaborating on wording and decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Facilitated actionable steps and offers to help but missed opportunities to elicit the patient's reasons, motivations, or commitment language about medication decisions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing hesitation and redirected uncertainty toward concrete safety actions, though could strengthen this with double‑sided reflections and explicit normalization of ambivalence.
