# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician set a clearly nonjudgmental tone, affirmed and normalized Daniela's efforts, and repeatedly honored her boundaries and requests to pause; there was no shaming or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some empathic behaviors were present (waiting, gentle support, honoring pauses), but the clinician offered few deeper reflections of Daniela's ambivalence or emotional experience and became more directive than reflective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a focused, consistent direction on disclosing diabetes to friends, with all suggestions, scripts, and role-plays tightly linked to that target behavior and minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The stance was generally cooperative—inviting practice and tailoring responses to requests—but the clinician frequently provided ready-made scripts rather than first eliciting Daniela's own wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician supported choice by asking permission to role-play, offering options, and reminding Daniela she didn't have to answer everything; more explicit statements emphasizing Daniela's ultimate control would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited through rehearsal and practice, but the clinician missed opportunities to draw out Daniela's own reasons, pros/cons, confidence, and strengths with open questions and reflective summaries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements (permission, role-play, pauses), but the clinician often positioned themselves as the expert with prescriptive scripts, limiting a more equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk by inviting practice and praising statements, and helped polish phrasing, but did not fully explore underlying reasons or ask importance/confidence questions to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and helped plan language and boundaries to reduce its impact, yet did not use explicit MI strategies like double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides of the ambivalence.
