# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✔
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent, nonjudgmental positive regard and affirmation throughout (e.g., “That sounds doable…,” “That wording honors your values…”). Multiple explicit validations of the client’s strengths and choices; no criticism or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ✔
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective‑taking about values and constraints (“fits your values and busy job,” “low‑drama…”). Reflections are more pragmatic than affectively deep — missed opportunities to probe feelings behind “I feel great” or the meaning of self‑respect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✔
**Comments:** Very clear, sustained focus on protecting evenings/boundary setting. Every question advances concrete planning, implementation, or evaluation with no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✔
**Comments:** Work framed as joint throughout — offers options, asks preferences, and adapts to client readiness. Client input shapes the plan and choices are routinely solicited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✔
**Comments:** Repeated reinforcement of client ownership and respect for limits (e.g., offering choices, accepting decision to start with one small step). Language consistently emphasizes choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✔
**Comments:** Actively elicits client’s own reasons, concrete plans, and signs of success (questions about realistic steps, first signs, acknowledgement ritual). Clinician prompts client‑generated solutions rather than prescribing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✔
**Comments:** Interaction positioned as collaborative and co‑constructed (inviting wording choices, testing feasibility together, “treat this like an experiment together”).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✔
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits, reflects, and amplifies change talk; elicits repeated concrete commitment language (blocking nights, alarms, Slack status, checkmark ritual) and deepens planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ✔
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal and clinician effectively steers toward implementation. Fewer explicit double‑sided reflections or anticipatory naming of barriers — missed chance to preemptively soften potential slips (e.g., empathic “I can hear how easy it could be to cave…”).
