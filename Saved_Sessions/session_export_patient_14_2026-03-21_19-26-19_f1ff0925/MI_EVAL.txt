# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the patient's choices and normalizing constraints. More explicit statements of unconditional positive regard or normalizing ambivalence would strengthen acceptance to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were empathic openings and brief validations, but limited reflective listening and perspective-taking as the clinician moved quickly to problem-solving. Deeper reflections and summaries of the patient's feelings and ambivalence would improve empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear, appropriate focus on snacking/grazing with concrete, measurable steps (water-before-snack, 3pm alarm, one-line log) and a focused follow-up. Directional focus was strong and consistent throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The encounter felt largely collaborative with co-created plans and negotiated solutions for barriers. However, an early didactic list of recommendations reduced equal power-sharing; more elicitation of the patient's ideas first would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offered options, asked which felt doable, and avoided pressuring language, supporting patient choice. Explicitly reinforcing that the patient is the final decision-maker and using more reflective ownership statements would elevate autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (patient chose and committed to the water strategy), but the approach relied heavily on solution-giving rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons, values, and confidence. More open-ended evocative questions and reflections would deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative moments and negotiation of follow-up, but the interaction was clinician-driven early on with a long list of recommendations before eliciting preferences. Asking permission to give advice and inviting the patient's expertise would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and a short-term commitment (one-week water trial) and operationalized the plan. Further strategic elicitation of desire, importance, and confidence would strengthen change talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and acknowledged barriers, offering brief, time-efficient options that did not reinforce sustain talk. A deliberate double-sided reflection to highlight ambivalence would further soften sustain talk.
