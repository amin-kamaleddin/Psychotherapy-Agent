# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, validating ambivalence and normalizing imperfection throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate understanding of ambivalence, but reflections are mostly problem-focused with limited deeper emotional or values-level naming.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on phone/social media use and moves the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps (timers, cues, backups, rewards).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as a genuine joint exploration: the clinician invites brainstorming, uses 'we' language, and lets the client choose and shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options, avoids pressure, validates possible lapses, and consistently defers to the client's preferences to support choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits client-generated strategies and implementation details, but could probe more for intrinsic reasons, values, and ratings of importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the client as the expert and co-constructs the plan, repeatedly asking which options fit the client's life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete change talk through decisional and implementation questions, but misses opportunities to amplify desire/reasons and strengthen commitment with follow-ups or rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and pivots to small goals, though could use more double-sided or complex reflections to fully validate the behavior's functions before steering to change.
