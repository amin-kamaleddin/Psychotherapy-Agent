# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful tone with unconditional positive regard, validating Lars's concerns and normalizing struggles. No blaming or shaming language was used and warmth was sustained throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both content and feeling and mirrored Lars’s ambivalence, deepening rapport and trust. Responses were timely and responsive to the patient’s emotional drivers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on specific target behaviors and moved from intention to a concrete, time-bound plan (skip Friday, polytech, limit cash, Donna call). Questions and reflections consistently steered toward actionable steps without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as genuine joint problem-solving with the clinician inviting input and integrating Lars’s ideas into the plan. The psychiatrist checked back and co-constructed specifics, positioning Lars as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed Lars’s choice and offered options rather than dictating solutions, supporting ownership of the plan. Language emphasized control and avoided pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within Lars via evocative questions linking values (Donna’s trust, relief, job goals) to concrete actions. The clinician helped connect identity and personally meaningful motives to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently positioned the interaction as collaborative, asking preferences and inviting Lars to design safeguards. Power was shared and the clinician deferred to Lars’s expertise about what would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced multiple change statements and concrete plans, but missed opportunities to amplify and explicitly summarize strongest change talk or to elicit a clear commitment/confidence rating. More emphasis on scaling confidence and drawing out a commitment statement would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and redirected toward change with practical alternatives and safeguards, avoiding confrontation. To improve, the clinician could use more double-sided reflections and elicit Lars’s own reasons for discounting sustain-talk and potential barriers.
