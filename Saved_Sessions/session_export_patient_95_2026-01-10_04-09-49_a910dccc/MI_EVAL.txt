# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance with multiple validations and a supportive tone throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality empathic listening and accurate reflections name core feelings and ambivalence, mirroring the patient's language while containing distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on immediate suicidal risk and concrete safety steps, repeatedly bringing the conversation back to preventing harm tonight.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as joint problem-solving: the clinician invites input, offers options, and co-constructs the immediate plan using inclusive language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly honors the patient's autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and avoiding pressure while balancing safety priorities.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits reasons for staying and past coping with evocative questions, though there is room to deepen and amplify self-motivational language to elicit stronger commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently uses collaborative language and shared decision-making, checking preferences and offering to 'do this together' to share power with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites and amplifies change-oriented statements and affirms small steps, but could better convert tentative change talk into explicit commitment or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and pivots to doable safety actions, but mirrors 'nothing works' several times and could use more double-sided reflections to reduce rehearsal of hopelessness.
