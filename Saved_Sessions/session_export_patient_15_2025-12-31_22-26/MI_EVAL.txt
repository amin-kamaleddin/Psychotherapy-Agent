# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Repeated use of acceptance language and nonjudgmental statements; affirms goals (avoid meds, stay working) and strengths (quit smoking). Supportive tone fostered cooperation. Some acceptance phrasing felt formulaic and there was a mismatch early (labeling the situation “stressful” when patient denied stress). More tailored normalization would improve consistency.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5  
**Comments:** Adequate reflective listening and accurate paraphrases of goals and strengths. However, notable mismatches (e.g., claiming stress) and limited probing of the patient’s minimizing tone about alcohol. Reflections were often surface-level and repetitive rather than deepening emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5  
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on blood pressure management and concrete behaviors (20‑minute walks, salt reduction, calendar/alarms). Conversation stayed on target and produced a measurable, actionable plan tied to patient goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asks which options feel doable, builds on patient’s prior success, and solicits input on strategies. Session was somewhat clinician‑led in sequencing and summarizing; more explicit co‑creation of the agenda would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5  
**Comments:** Clear support for patient autonomy throughout: respects privacy preferences, frames options, asks which approach “feels” doable, and avoids pressuring the patient. Consistently autonomy‑respecting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5  
**Comments:** Effectively evoked motivation for exercise/salt changes and leveraged past success (smoking cessation). Limited elicitation regarding the role of alcohol—missed opportunities to explore beliefs, ambivalence, or reasons for change on that clinically relevant topic.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Treated the session as a joint enterprise; patient contributed to choice of strategies (calendar, alarms). To reach top score, clinician could have explicitly invited the patient to set priorities/agenda (e.g., whether to address alcohol) and framed the plan as more fully co‑authored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk about walks, salt reduction, and capability (quit smoking previously). Reflections amplified commitment to these behaviors. Did not cultivate change talk regarding alcohol; clinician accepted the patient’s minimization rather than exploring it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5  
**Comments:** Recurrent sustain talk about alcohol was largely validated rather than softened. Missed use of strategies to reduce sustain talk (double‑sided reflections, strategic summaries, exploring discrepancy or consequences). Given alcohol’s relevance to BP, this was a notable missed opportunity.
