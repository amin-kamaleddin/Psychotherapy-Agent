# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Opened with an explicit nonjudgmental statement ("I’m here to listen in a safe, nonjudgmental space")
  - [x] Asked about immediate safety (suicide/harm)
  - [x] Checked/negotiated session constraint (10 exchanges)
  - [x] Avoided labeling or shaming language
  - [ ] Demonstrated deeper, highly personalized warmth (more than formulaic)
  - [ ] Used normalizing statements to personalize acceptance

**Comments:** Clinician communicated respect and basic positive regard (clear nonjudgmental opening, safety check, session framing). Interaction felt professional and somewhat formulaic; frequent directive tips reduced opportunities for deeper individualized acceptance.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Used reflective statements/summaries (e.g., “It sounds like the doctor’s comment stuck with you…”)
  - [x] Named secondary emotions (annoyance, feeling dismissed)
  - [x] Repeatedly confirmed safety
  - [ ] Used more complex reflections exploring meaning and feeling before moving to solutions

**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and validated the patient’s perspective. Opportunities were missed for deeper empathic exploration (more complex reflections about meaning/function of drinking or what being “dismissed” felt like).

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Kept session focused on behavior change (cutting back drinking)
  - [x] Moved toward a concrete plan (A/B/C options → beer→soda)
  - [x] Elicited a specific first step (phone reminder, choice of soda)
  - [x] Established timeline and follow-up (check-in Saturday at 2:00 PM)

**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on a concrete, time-bound experiment and follow-up — clear, pragmatic direction that maintained momentum toward change.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Offered a menu of options and invited choice (A/B/C)
  - [x] Asked which option felt most doable and elicited patient input
  - [x] Elicited confidence and barriers and incorporated patient choices
  - [ ] Relied less on clinician-generated solutions (multiple prescriptive tips were given)

**Comments:** The clinician invited input and the patient’s choice shaped the plan, supporting collaboration. Balance tilted toward clinician advice at times; could invite patient-generated strategies before offering tips.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Emphasized patient choice (asked preference A vs B vs C)
  - [x] Accepted small commitment (“just willing to try it once”)
  - [x] Did not pressure for a large commitment or coerce

**Comments:** Clear, consistent autonomy support — decisions were left to the patient and the clinician honored the small, low‑pressure experiment.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Elicited the patient’s plan and commitment language (beer→soda pattern)
  - [x] Asked about barriers, confidence, and fallback plans
  - [ ] Asked more open-ended questions to elicit personal motivations or reasons for change

**Comments:** Successfully drew out concrete change talk and planning. Could strengthen by eliciting the patient’s own reasons/values for change rather than primarily offering options.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Positioned the work as collaborative (menu of options, mutual check-in)
  - [x] Allowed patient choice to shape the plan
  - [ ] Reduced clinician-led solution delivery to enhance shared problem-solving

**Comments:** Partnership was evident in shared decision-making and scheduling a mutual follow-up. The clinician’s repeated prescriptive tips slightly weakened the co-constructive feel.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Evoked actionable change talk and commitment (patient agreed to experiment)
  - [x] Elicited confidence rating (6/10) and concrete fallback plan
  - [ ] Further amplified and explored the patient’s change statements to deepen commitment

**Comments:** Good elicitation and reinforcement of commitment language; could be improved by reflecting and expanding the patient’s own change reasons to strengthen commitment.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- Checklist of observed behaviors:
  - [x] Did not confront or argue with minimizations (nonconfrontational)
  - [x] Redirected sustain talk to a low-pressure experiment (small test)
  - [ ] Used double-sided reflections or explicitly summarized ambivalence
  - [ ] Briefly explored the patient’s minimization about past smoking

**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and effectively redirected to a manageable test. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and a brief probe of the repeated smoking minimizations.
