### Acceptance

**Comments:** Clinician consistently displayed nonjudgmental regard and validated Nadine's experience, normalizing her exhaustion and avoiding blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening—clinician named Nadine's exhaustion, asked about her emotional state, and picked up on feeding specifics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on maternal rest and getting help with night care, offering concrete strategies and checking safety/mental health.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative: clinician offered choices, co-constructed the script, and adapted to Nadine's preferences through role-play and options.

### Autonomy Support

**Comments:** Supported Nadine's choice by offering multiple options and affirming occasional formula, but at times gave direct recommendations rather than eliciting her preferred strategy first.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Some elicitation via offering plans and practice, but clinician relied more on providing solutions than systematically drawing out Nadine's reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were useful prompts that produced commitment (e.g., trying the script), but the clinician more often supplied solutions than elicited importance, confidence, or past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain-talk (pumping is extra work) and offered low-burden alternatives to reduce barriers, but could use more double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
