# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating language throughout the session, normalizing struggles and discouraging perfectionism. The clinician affirmed the client's experience and avoided blame, conveying unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate empathic statements and mirrored emotional language but often shifted quickly into planning. Missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections to fully process feelings of shame and defeat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on starting an exercise habit with specific, actionable steps and a day‑1 plan. The clinician kept momentum by steering toward concrete commitments and implementation strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with solicitation of preferences and joint troubleshooting, leading to co‑created scheduling and a trial. However, many ready‑made solutions and scripts shifted the dynamic toward an expert role, reducing equal co‑construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported client choice by offering options, framing invitations as choices, and reinforcing no‑guilt resets. The clinician emphasized ownership and multiple pathways, supporting perceived control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited commitment and used practical tools to activate motivation, asking for specific trial commitments and tracking. Relied more on operational strategies than on eliciting deeper personal values, importance, or future‑oriented reasoning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed a collaborative stance and invited input on logistics and plans, co‑deciding on a trial and backups. Frequent prescriptive guidance and scripts limited opportunities for the client to generate her own strategies, weakening power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively prompted commitment language and concrete planning (days/times, 2‑week trial, reporting back). Could have further amplified change talk by using reflections, scaling, or questions about personal reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did well to avoid reinforcing sustain talk, normalizing barriers and offering if‑then backups and self‑compassion lines. Would be strengthened by more double‑sided reflections and explicit reframing of discouraging self‑talk.
