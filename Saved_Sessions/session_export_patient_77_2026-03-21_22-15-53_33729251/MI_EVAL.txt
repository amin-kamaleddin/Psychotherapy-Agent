# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with validating language and praise for effort; the clinician treated the client's feelings as legitimate throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, empathic reflections that validated frustration and loneliness, but tended to move quickly to problem‑solving instead of deepening emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on nighttime/after‑school stress‑eating and used targeted questions to move from exploration to a concrete, specific plan with follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑created the plan by soliciting the client's ideas, integrating her routines and context, and agreeing on a mutually acceptable check‑in time, avoiding an expert‑only stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by asking permission, offering options rather than commands, and leaving decisions and backups to the client, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited client solutions and concrete commitments (e.g., texting brother, alarms) but relied on several clinician‑provided strategies and could probe more for intrinsic motives and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine partnership through repeated permission‑seeking, offering options, and inviting the client to choose and shape the plan, showing clear power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk using confidence rulers and choice questions, though could more frequently amplify spontaneous change language to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and did not confront sustain talk, redirecting to small experiments and backups, but could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections and explicit normalization.
