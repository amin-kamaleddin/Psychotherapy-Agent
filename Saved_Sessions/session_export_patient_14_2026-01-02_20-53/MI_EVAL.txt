# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., “I hear the frustration…”); repeated safety checks and validation of practical constraints. Felt somewhat procedural and brief rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic reflective listening present (“that’s a lot”); limited depth/frequency of reflections and minimal exploration of underlying emotions (irritation, work anxiety), allowing patient to remain guarded.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, focused agenda (urgent GP med review + one small self‑care step). Maintained priorities and brief, goal‑oriented approach throughout, consistent with patient preference.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient involved in decision‑making (offered options; clinician drafted GP line); plan was co‑constructed. Occasional slipping into prescriptive wording rather than fully eliciting patient solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly acknowledged choice and left decisions to patient; offered options, asked permission to help, and framed the walks as voluntary with explicit “stop if breathlessness worsens.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation/amplification of patient’s change talk. Patient volunteered a desire about weight, but clinician did not probe reasons, importance, or values to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed interaction as joint problem‑solving (offered to draft message, summarized plans); patient’s choices guided the plan. Could have better solicited the patient’s expertise about fitting steps into schedule.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunity for a small commitment (two walks) and provided brief comparative info, but did not amplify the patient’s own change statements or elicit commitment/confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and acknowledged sustain talk (time pressure), which prevented escalation. Missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections or reframing to reduce persistent sustain statements.
