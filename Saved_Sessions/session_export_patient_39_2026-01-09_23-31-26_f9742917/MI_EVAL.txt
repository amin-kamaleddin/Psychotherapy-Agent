### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized Elias’s struggles, and affirmed his efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that acknowledged feelings, but reflections focused more on logistics and safety than deeper affect or the emotional meaning of feeling like a burden.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing safe physical activity and safety planning, moving logically from values to options to concrete planning and check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem‑solving, solicited Elias’s preferences repeatedly, and tailored the plan to his limits and values, demonstrating genuine mutual engagement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, offered options, and respected Elias’s control over decisions without pressuring, reinforcing his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations, confidence ratings, and tied the plan to values but at times offered solutions before asking Elias to generate ideas, limiting opportunities to draw out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision‑making by offering options and asking preferences, though the interaction occasionally shifted toward clinician‑directed planning when providing safety instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (confidence rulers, indicators of success) but could have amplified Elias’s own statements more and asked him to generate more options before offering choices.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and did not reinforce sustain talk, reframed ambivalence constructively, and redirected to small safe steps, but could use more explicit double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
