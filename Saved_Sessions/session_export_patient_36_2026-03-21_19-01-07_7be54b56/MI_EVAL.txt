# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, validates the patient's experience and celebrates small wins; treats the approach as an experiment and avoids blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and attends to bodily/emotional cues (chest tight, guilt), but often moves quickly to planning rather than exploring deeper emotional meanings or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with a concrete progression (mapping, scripts, rehearsal, homework) that aligns well with MI aims.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co‑constructed with frequent invitations to practice and joint adjustment of phrasing; decisions and plans are made collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and agency by asking permission, offering options, and framing actions as experiments without coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation, bodily cues, and reflections and invites experiments, but sometimes supplies ready‑made solutions instead of first eliciting the patient's own language and deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a collaborative environment through permission‑seeking and checking preferences, though at times leans toward expert direction by offering scripts and prescriptive sequencing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk and asks for behavioral commitments, but often provides scripts rather than prompting more client‑generated statements and explicit confidence/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes worries effectively, yet could more often use double‑sided reflections to validate both sides and strategically shift toward change.
