# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validated frustration, and honored the patient's boundary about routine follow-ups.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate emotion naming, but reflections were brief and missed opportunities to explore the patient's overwhelm and sarcasm more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on asthma self-management with concrete behavior steps and explicit safety instructions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative—co-created the plan, solicited the patient's exact wording, and adapted language to the patient's blunt style.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and honoring the patient's preferences without pressure or judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of a behavioral commitment was present, but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient's internal motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership: invited input, offered a menu of options, and integrated the patient's choices into the chart and plan, sharing power effectively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited limited change talk and a concrete commitment, but did not systematically amplify intrinsic reasons, importance, or confidence (no scaling or deeper exploration).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed the plan around the patient's preferences to shift away from sustain talk, though more double-sided reflections could strengthen the pivot.
