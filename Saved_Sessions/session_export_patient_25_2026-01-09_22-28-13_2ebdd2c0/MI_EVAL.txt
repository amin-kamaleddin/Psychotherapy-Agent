# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, affirming Daniel’s quitting and linking it to his values; normalizes slips and validates pride throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and accurate summaries of triggers and pride, but reflections are often content-level; could deepen emotional and ambivalence reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related behavior change with concrete limits, role-plays, and actionable planning that keep the session on track.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session reads as joint problem-solving with role-plays and co-created plan, though offering multiple clinician-generated solutions at times shifts some agency away from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking which strategies feel realistic, and inviting the patient to select and rehearse plans without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient’s motivations and past successes (e.g., quitting smoking) and uses role-play to surface resolve, but could ask more evocative questions about values, importance, and confidence to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership by asking what the patient wants to focus on and inviting input, but sometimes moves toward offering expert solutions rather than eliciting more client-generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and supports change talk through focused questions and role-play, yet could do more to amplify spontaneous change talk and elicit importance/confidence language to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward actionable supports, but would strengthen impact by using double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge pull toward drinking while guiding change.
