# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys clear unconditional positive regard, normalizes and validates the patient’s mixed feelings, and consistently offers options rather than blame. Tone and repeated practical supports affirm the patient’s experience and limitations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate empathic listening using reflective summaries that capture content and emotion and references the patient’s context. Reflections validate vulnerability and help the patient elaborate practical concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior (engaging medical care via triage call) and consistently narrows to actionable next steps. Questions and reflections repeatedly circle back to scheduling the nap‑time triage without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructs a concrete plan with the patient, checks fit with the patient’s capacities, and positions the clinician as a helper rather than authority. Patient preferences clearly shape the resulting plan and supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice by offering a menu of options and asking what the patient would prefer the clinician to do. Language is nonpressuring and emphasizes the patient’s decision-making control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s motivations and barriers and draws out concrete reasons and a specific plan. Could deepen evocation by linking action to personal values or using scaling questions to strengthen intrinsic motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the interaction as a joint enterprise, inviting preferences at multiple decision points and offering to act on the patient’s choices. Repeated solicitation of the patient’s authority over choices demonstrates strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and shapes change talk, secures a specific behavioral commitment (nap‑time triage), and invites commitment repeatedly. Missed opportunities include amplifying emerging change language and asking direct importance/confidence questions to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully acknowledges sustain talk with double-sided reflections and redirects toward smaller, viable steps rather than confronting or arguing. Focus on reducing barriers and offering doable options effectively softens momentum toward avoidance.
