# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, using validating language and normalizing Mateo's experience. Judgmental language was avoided and previous coping efforts were affirmed throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were timely and accurately captured both content and affect, creating a strong empathic connection. The clinician paraphrased frustration and doubt in ways that validated Mateo's feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and was purposefully guided toward a concrete plan (mailbox walk, supports, stop rule, scheduling, follow-up). The clinician balanced elicitation with clear steering to measurable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as a joint effort with frequent solicitation of Mateo's input and adaptation to his preferences. Resources and scripts were offered as suggestions and adjusted per Mateo's requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician preserved choice and control by offering options, asking permission before suggesting steps, and emphasizing that the plan belonged to Mateo. Reassurances that stopping is not failure further supported autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within Mateo through open questions, past-support exploration, a confidence ruler, and framing the plan as an experiment. The clinician elicited and developed internal reasons and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created the plan with Mateo, invited his preferences at each stage, and shared power so Mateo made final choices. This collaborative stance was evident in scheduling, wording edits, and follow-up planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and amplified change talk via targeted questions, reflections, and a confidence ruler, then translated intentions into a concrete, doable plan. Affirmations reinforced Mateo's willingness to try.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and reframed with normalization and experiment framing, which reduced all‑or‑nothing thinking. To strengthen this area, more explicit double-sided reflections early on and more systematic redirection of sustain statements would be helpful.
