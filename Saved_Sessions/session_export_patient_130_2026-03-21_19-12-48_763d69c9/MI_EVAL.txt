# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance (opening statement, acknowledgements, normalizing ambivalence) and affirmed Viktor’s efforts when he agreed to harm‑reduction steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective‑taking and asked permission before sensitive questions, but missed opportunities to deepen emotional reflections and probe the affective nuance of Viktor’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on substance use and immediate harm reduction with targeted questions, concrete suggestions, and a follow‑up plan to review the first night.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as a genuine collaboration—co‑drafted messages, invited Viktor to choose rules and supports, and framed follow‑up as joint problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Viktor’s sense of choice by offering a menu of options, asking permission, normalizing imperfect attempts, and avoiding pressure despite external referral.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some intrinsic motivation and used it to build a plan, but could have more explicitly drawn out Viktor’s values, goals, and discrepancy (e.g., importance/confidence scaling).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong power‑sharing: repeatedly asked permission, invited Viktor into planning, and allowed his choices (rule, support person) to shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, converting tentative statements into a concrete commitment, though further amplification and scaling questions could have strengthened commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used reflections (including a mild double‑sided reflection) to soften sustain talk, but could more proactively pivot sustain talk into change talk with selective summaries and discrepancy reflections.
