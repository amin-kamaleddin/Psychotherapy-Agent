# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a clearly nonjudgmental, supportive stance and consistently validated and normalized Kevin's ambivalence and slips, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of feelings and plans, though reflections were often paraphrase-level and could probe deeper emotional content or underlying values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on alcohol risk reduction with behaviorally specific goals and implementation details (e.g., weeknight rule, 11pm cut-off, alarms).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—strategies and tools were co‑developed, the clinician checked for tweaks, and Kevin's choices guided the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Kevin's choice and control by asking permission, offering options, normalizing partial commitment, and avoiding pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation with importance/confidence scaling and brainstorming, but at times offered solutions rather than drawing more fully on Kevin's own reasons and values; more evocative open questions could deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resides with Kevin, asked permission before advising, and frequently invited him to choose and shape strategies, reflecting strong power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete steps and scaling, but could better amplify past successes and elicit stronger, commitment‑level language in Kevin's own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated the functions of drinking while redirecting to harm‑reduction, yet used few targeted MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to strategically tip momentum away from sustain talk.
