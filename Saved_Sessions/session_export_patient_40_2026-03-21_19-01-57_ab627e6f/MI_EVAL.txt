# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating shame and honoring Elias’s preferences while tailoring the plan. Warm, respectful stance evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good empathic statements and some reflections (acknowledging courage, normalizing nervousness) but missed deeper reflections of Elias’s ambivalence and the emotional weight of shame. More complex paraphrase of mixed feelings would strengthen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol/pill use, safety, and relapse prevention with concrete, pragmatic steps from immediate tools to longer‑term supports. The session stayed goal‑directed and action‑oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—ideas were proposed then revised with Elias’s input, and the checklist was co‑created rather than prescribed. Inclusive language and iterative edits demonstrated equal‑partner work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Elias’s choices by offering options, asking permission, and accepting realistic preferences (e.g., NA once/week, text‑only check‑ins). Avoided coercive language and emphasized personal choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Elias’s own solutions and commitments (triggers, rehearsals, concrete tasks) drawing motivation from within. However, the clinician did not sufficiently explore intrinsic values, importance/confidence, or past strengths to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked clearly as a partner—co‑constructing the plan, repeatedly asking permission, and adjusting materials to Elias’s requests. The clinician invited Elias’s input at each stage, demonstrating shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change‑oriented language and actions (planning, rehearsals, triggers), fostering commitment and preparedness. More frequent amplification of Elias’s own change statements and follow‑ups about importance/confidence would deepen and multiply change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk but did not consistently employ MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, strategic summaries) to explore ambivalence or the pull of staying. The session moved toward practical planning rather than deliberately softening sustain talk.
