# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive with validations and affirmations, avoided blaming language and repeatedly respected the patient's limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening, acknowledging stress and practical barriers; could use more complex reflections to deepen exploration of emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the conversation clearly focused on parental smoking and safe quitting strategies, proposed a specific one-week plan, and arranged follow-up and safety monitoring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a co-constructed plan by soliciting priorities, offering options, and drafting a note, but at times offered clinician-generated solutions rather than eliciting more patient-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked permission, offered multiple options, honored the patient's choices, and avoided pressuring language to support the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some attempts to draw out motivation and small commitments, but relied more on providing options and did not sufficiently elicit the patient's intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created shared decision-making through questions and offers (e.g., check-ins, note drafting), though occasionally shifted toward expert recommendations rather than co-generating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some tentative change talk and proposed a doable plan, but did not systematically explore the patient's reasons, importance, or confidence or amplify expressed desire to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used harm-reduction, but made limited use of double-sided or complex reflections to reframe minimizations and resolve ambivalence.
