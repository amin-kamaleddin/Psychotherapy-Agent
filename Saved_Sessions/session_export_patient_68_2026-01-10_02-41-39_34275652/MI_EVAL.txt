# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., safety check, normalizing slip-ups) and avoided blame throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good empathic statements and some reflections validating embarrassment and uncertainty, but missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections and summarizing underlying emotions like shame and loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use and harm reduction with concrete, actionable suggestions and a one-week check-in, with minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal problem-solving partner by inviting Kevin's input, tailoring scripts to his wording, rehearsing lines, and negotiating concrete steps together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering a menu of options, asking which he preferred, normalizing imperfection, and emphasizing that the plan was his decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some personal motivations and specific plans (regret, desire to keep social life intact) but could have probed values, used scaling questions, and summarized change talk to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—repeatedly invited Kevin to choose among options, co-created practical steps, and reinforced shared decision-making with rehearsal and check-in offers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and secured specific commitments (alternating drinks, capping weekends), but could have amplified change talk by asking about consequences and importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did well to avoid arguing and offered alternatives to preserve social belonging, though more double-sided reflections and strategic softening could further resolve ambivalence and reduce sustain talk.
