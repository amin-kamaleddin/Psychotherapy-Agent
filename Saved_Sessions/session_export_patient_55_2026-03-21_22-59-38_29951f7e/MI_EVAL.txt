# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes feelings, and explicitly honors the client's boundary about discussing drinking while affirming her priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that helps the client feel heard, but reflections are sometimes repetitive and could probe deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on a concrete study-behavior target and guides planning, but does not arrange a neutral follow-up or permission-based check-in about the initial alcohol concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites the client's input and co-creates implementation strategies, avoiding an expert-driven approach and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces choice and avoids pressuring language, supporting small-step experiments and the client's sense of control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation tied to protecting grades, band, and sleep and asks evocative outcome questions, but focuses on the secondary study target and misses chances to elicit confidence (e.g., scaling).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative with frequent requests for preferences and co-construction of a realistic, low-pressure plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces specific change and commitment language around the study routine with concrete implementation details, but could further amplify confidence and commitment with additional strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accepts and redirects the client's refusal to discuss alcohol using neutral reflections rather than confrontation, though a double-sided reflection could better acknowledge ambivalence while preserving rapport.
