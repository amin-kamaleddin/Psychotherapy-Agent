# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating with multiple affirmations and normalization, conveying strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and occasional reflections (e.g., enjoying social drinking and sleep concerns) but misses deeper empathic reflections of ambivalence and defensive stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on weekend drinking with targeted questions, a concrete cutback plan, monitoring, and a scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—elicits preferences and integrates plans—but sometimes defaults to offering many specific strategies, which reduces equal power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the patient’s right to choose using permissive language, offering options, and affirming small choices rather than pressuring for abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits consequences and some motivation, securing a behavioral commitment, but could probe deeper for values, use scaling, and amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters collaboration and co-creates a plan, but frequent prescriptive advice and step-by-step instructions sometimes shift control back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk and secures specific commitments, yet could better amplify the patient’s reasons and use importance/confidence scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and corrective feedback is appropriate, but rarely uses double-sided or strategic reflections to acknowledge sustain talk before pivoting to change.
