# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance (e.g., opening normalization and empathic replies); no labeling or criticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflections and good perspective-taking, linking decisions to the patient's values, but sometimes shifts quickly into information delivery and misses chances for deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior (statin decision and adherence), repeatedly returning to numeric risk, timing of benefits, side-effect plans, and concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models mutual problem-solving with frequent back-and-forth, inviting the patient to tailor questions, choose rehearsal lines, and co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports patient choice from the outset, asks permission before offering information or rehearsals, and frames options and monitoring as the patient's decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's motivations and links them to values, but at times moves into expert answers rather than using additional evocative follow-ups to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites collaboration and treats the patient as an equal partner by offering options, asking preferences, and co-creating rehearsal language and question lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces change talk through questions, rehearsals, and value-linking, though it could more consistently reflect, amplify, and elicit elaboration or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids argument, but relies more on medical facts and question lists than on double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to shift sustain talk toward change.
