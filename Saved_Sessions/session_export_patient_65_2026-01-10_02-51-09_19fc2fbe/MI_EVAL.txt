# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental, supportive regard and validated the client's feelings while offering practical help instead of criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and reflections that helped the client feel heard, but frequently shifted quickly into problem-solving and missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on completing the court-ordered hours, elicited key logistics, provided concrete tools, and set a concrete follow-up plan aligned with the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acted as a helpful partner by asking preferences and offering to proofread, but relied heavily on ready-made scripts and clinician-driven solutions rather than explicit co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the client's choice and control by offering options, checking preferences, and avoiding coercive language while respecting the client's pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited limited internal motivation (asked what was useful and secured small commitments) but did not systematically draw out the client's reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with choices and invitations, yet often assumed the expert role by supplying full scripts instead of inviting the client to co-generate language or plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-oriented language was evoked and planning occurred, but the clinician did not systematically elicit or amplify intrinsic reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward doable steps (email alternatives, follow-ups), though repeating the mandate occasionally risked emphasizing external pressure.
