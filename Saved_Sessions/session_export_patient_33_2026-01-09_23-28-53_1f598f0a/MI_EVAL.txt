# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, with affirmations and validations (e.g., “I’m glad you came,” praise for having Narcan). No blaming or minimizing language was used throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking were demonstrated repeatedly (captured function of use, fears, and ambivalence). Timely reflections and summaries matched Diego’s affect and concerns, building rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on harm-reduction goals and operationalized them into concrete steps (safer injecting, naloxone practice, role‑play, checklist). Minimal drift and consistent return to agreed targets.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as a true collaboration—co‑constructed scripts and plans, honored requests to role‑play, and iteratively rehearsed with patient input. Clinician acted as facilitator rather than director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Diego’s choice and pace by offering options and asking him to choose what felt most useful. Information and skills were provided while leaving final decisions to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Started with open questions and elicited readiness and specific intentions but then shifted into directive teaching and procedural coaching. More probing of personal reasons, values, and confidence would strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the interaction as collaborative with repeated invitations to choose and joint problem‑solving (e.g., deciding whether to role‑play or use a checklist). Power-sharing was evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (high readiness, planned steps, desire for harm reduction) and translated it into action. Missed opportunities to deepen underlying reasons, confidence, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated the function of heroin while redirecting to safety-focused goals, which reduced sustain talk. Could use more double-sided reflections and explicit exploration of costs/benefits to further resolve ambivalence.
