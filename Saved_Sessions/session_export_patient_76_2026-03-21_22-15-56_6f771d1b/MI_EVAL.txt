# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, using validating language and affirming small steps without shaming. Tone and wording supported the client throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening and accurate reflections captured Liora's somatic urges and exhaustion and included caring safety checks. Empathy could be deepened with more complex, affect‑laden reflections to probe hopelessness or feeling trapped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on self‑harm safety and practical coping alternatives while balancing necessary clinical/directive elements. Actionable steps (means reduction, coping strategies, texts) kept the encounter centered on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the clinician asked permission, offered options, and co‑constructed the safety plan with the client. The client's preferences drove decisions and readiness was checked before intervening.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and framed recommendations as options, reinforcing Liora's control over decisions. This approach consistently bolstered the client's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful elicitation moves (urge rating, choice of strategies) were used but there was little systematic exploration of intrinsic motivations, values, or past successes. More targeted open questions to draw out reasons for change and confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Partnership was strong: the clinician invited collaboration, positioned tasks as joint efforts, and respected Liora's expertise about her life. Shared decision‑making was evident throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offered alternatives and positively framed small actions, supporting emerging change language but did not actively elicit or amplify intrinsic reasons, desire, or commitment to change. Opportunities to explore values, costs of continuing, and past resistances were underused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized with reflective statements that reduced resistance and reframed small steps as meaningful. The clinician could add more double‑sided reflections to explicitly highlight ambivalence and guide toward change.
