# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, using validating language that avoids judgment. Acceptance is maintained throughout, preserving rapport with the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent empathic reflections and perspective-taking convey understanding of the client's feelings and situation. Reflections match minimal client responses and avoid pressuring disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session keeps a clear, appropriate focus on a concrete, behaviorally relevant target (a small safety experiment with headphones). The clinician repeatedly links the plan to the client's values while respecting pace and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as a joint exploration and asks for the client's input, building on her responses. Some clinician-led scaffolding and offered options slightly shift influence away from entirely client-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported with repeated statements honoring choice and declining pressure to promise. Questions and options reinforce the client's ownership over any decision or experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits the client's reasons, examples, and ideas, drawing motivation out of a minimally responsive adolescent. To improve, change language could be deepened and more actively amplified to build internal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician largely adopts a collaborative stance, inviting co-creation of small steps and asking which options feel doable. Occasional early offering of specific solutions slightly reduces the client's generation of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces change talk and helps move to concrete, low-effort steps, such as headphones by the door. Stronger strategic amplification and consolidation of client-generated change language toward commitment would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids confrontation and normalizes the client's sustain talk, which reduces pressure and preserves rapport. However, sustain language is often mirrored rather than countered with double-sided reflections or selective reinforcement of pro-change statements.
