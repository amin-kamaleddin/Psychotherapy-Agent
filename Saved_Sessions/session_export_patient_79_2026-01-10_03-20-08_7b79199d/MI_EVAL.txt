# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, affirmed the patient's limits, and avoided criticism throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, repeated reflective listening that captured both content and affect, using reflections to deepen understanding and build rapport rather than to lecture.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on practical asthma self-management, guiding the session efficiently toward a single concrete, measurable, low‑burden behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative — decisions were co‑created, the clinician solicited the patient's expertise, and suggestions were offered only after eliciting preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice and control by emphasizing voluntariness, negotiating follow‑up, and avoiding pressure or lecturing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited personally meaningful motivations and reasons for change with evocative questions, though could deepen evocation by linking values to longer‑term goals and using scaling to assess confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership was evident as the clinician repeatedly invited input, deferred to the patient's judgment, and co‑constructed a mutually acceptable plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited specific change talk with concrete, evocative questions leading to commitments, but missed some opportunities to amplify, reflect, and elicit confidence/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well by acknowledging it and avoiding argumentation, though could more actively soften it using double‑sided reflections and explicit validation of the pros of staying the same.
