# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and respect, validated ambivalence and feelings without judgment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking but some reflections were concise/formulaic and the clinician sometimes moved quickly to planning instead of deepening emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on the target behavior (setting a move‑by date and assertiveness) using goal-directed questions, scaling, a script, checklist, and concrete scheduling to advance action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Overall collaborative—invited input, co-drafted scripts and checklists—but occasionally offered ready-made phrasing and tools without first asking, slightly reducing pure co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice by offering options, asking permission before role-play, and respecting Oliver’s decisions, which preserved his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within using scaling, eliciting reasons and small steps, and reinforcing increases in confidence rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Oliver as the expert, invited preferences, and co-created the plan (script, rehearsal, checklist), demonstrating strong power-sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and amplified change talk and tracked confidence gains, though could more often use strategic reflections and summaries to convert talk into explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed sustain talk without arguing and redirected to planning, but at times moved quickly to scripting which may have curtailed deeper exploration of guilt or resistance.
