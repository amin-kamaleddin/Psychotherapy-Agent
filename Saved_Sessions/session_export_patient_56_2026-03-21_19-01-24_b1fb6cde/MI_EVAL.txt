# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and affirming, normalizing the patient's distress and expressing positive regard without judgment. Multiple thanks and validations conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening with several reflective statements acknowledging distress and guilt. Reflections are often brief and the clinician moves quickly to solutions instead of deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on protecting evening family time as the target behavior. Session moves coherently from eliciting the problem to choosing and planning concrete strategies and a 2‑week trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint and iteratively solicited input, especially on message wording and doable actions. However, supplying many ready-made solutions early limited opportunities for patient-led idea generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Routinely offered options, asked which felt most doable, and accommodated patient edits, preserving choice and control. No pressuring language—assistance was offered only when requested.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited values, examples of misalignment, and intentions using scaling and targeted questions. After evoking motivation, the clinician often shifted quickly to providing solutions rather than further eliciting the patient's own strategies and strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative with co‑construction of wording and planning. The clinician's provision of specific scripts and operational definitions occasionally shifted authority away from the patient instead of inviting initial patient-generated wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and supported change language (values, desire, intention) and reinforced specific change goals like protecting evenings. Could more frequently reflect and amplify change talk to deepen sequencing toward stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and redirected concerns with practical options rather than arguing. Could add more double-sided reflections that acknowledge legitimate work demands while gently shifting focus back to the patient's priorities.
