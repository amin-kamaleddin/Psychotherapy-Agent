# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent validation and thanks; warmth is present but often tied to task-oriented guidance rather than deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses clarifying questions and reflections that show perspective-taking and adapts recommendations to the patient's concerns, but reflections remain mostly clinical/informational rather than emotionally deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on the oral/throat irritation and concrete next steps (pause use, rinses, exam) throughout the interaction without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Feels like joint problem-solving with co-created messages and rehearsals, but frequent provision of ready-made scripts and clinical instructions makes the exchange somewhat clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms choice, asks permission, and offers options (e.g., wording help, rehearsal, self-care vs exam), supporting the patient's sense of control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited effort to elicit the patient's internal motivations, values, or change language; the conversation is largely informational and directive with few evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Follows the patient's goals and invites input on wording and preferences, creating a collaborative feel, though the clinician's expert role and scripting reduce full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional prompts (pause use, offer to rehearse) create opportunities for change talk, but the clinician does not systematically evoke or amplify the patient's own reasons, desires, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and sometimes suggests omitting 'not trying to quit,' but rarely uses MI techniques (double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance) to actively soften or reframe sustain talk.
