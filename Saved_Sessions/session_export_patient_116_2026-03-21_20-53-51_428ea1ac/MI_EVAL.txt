# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalizing uncertainty and honoring the patient's boundary when he declines to elaborate. Repeatedly affirms the patient's right to set the pace and offers options without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and accurately reflects surface content and tone, inviting feeling-focused processing. Misses deeper affective reflections about the meaning of Samir's resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on the chosen goal of reducing interpersonal nagging and structures role-plays to build that skill. Does not sufficiently broaden direction to address medication adherence itself or link communication work to underlying health behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient as an active partner by asking preferences, offering role-play options, and adapting pace and content to Samir's goals. Treats Samir as a co-author of the plan rather than a passive recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before role-play, and affirming Samir's control over decisions and timing. Suggestions are presented as optional and invitational rather than coercive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited effort to elicit intrinsic motivations about medication or health; focuses on scripts and coaching instead of drawing out values, pros/cons, or discrepancy. Missed opportunities for scaling questions or gentle exploration of what matters to Samir.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance with shared decision-making and offers to practice together, incorporating Samir's input into session tasks. Occasionally shifts into advising/providing scripts rather than consistently co-constructing language from Samir's own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of change talk about medication adherence; the clinician honors avoidance and pivots to interpersonal skills without probing desire, reasons, or confidence for change. Lacks questions that might draw out any small motivations toward adherence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and respects reluctance to explore reasons, which prevents escalation. However, uses few active strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or gentle reframing) to soften sustain talk and move toward consideration of change.
