# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and avoided judging, using permission language and affirmations to normalize ambivalence and respond warmly to resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and good summaries of Ahmed's perspective, but relied more on content/paraphrase than deeper affective or values reflections that would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, steady focus on mini‑cigar use and harm‑reduction throughout, using scaling, specific options, and implementation intentions to guide toward concrete steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered an equal, collaborative interaction by soliciting Ahmed's preferences, co‑creating the plan, and inviting role‑play and rehearsal to shape decisions jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized Ahmed's choice and control, offered options rather than prescriptions, and avoided pressuring language while supporting harm‑reduction when quitting was resisted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited pros/cons, intrinsic reasons, and problem‑solving (importance/confidence scaling), but could have drawn out deeper values or long‑term motivations to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as collaborative, asking permission, offering choices, and inviting Ahmed to pick doable strategies so the plan reflected his priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk through scaling, exploring likes/worries, and gaining commitments for specific behaviors, though could have amplified and summarized change language more strongly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated minimization while offering information by permission and harm‑reduction options; could strengthen softening with more explicit double‑sided reflections.
