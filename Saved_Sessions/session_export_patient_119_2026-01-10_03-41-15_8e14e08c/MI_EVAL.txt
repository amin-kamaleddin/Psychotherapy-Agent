# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing struggles and reframing slips as expected rather than failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong empathic listening and perspective-taking with pragmatic reflections, but could deepen affective reflections to capture anxiety/guardedness more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on medication adherence throughout, gathering details and steering toward a concrete two-week, testable plan with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient as an equal partner by eliciting their routines and preferences, offering options, and building the plan from the patient's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy with permission language, patient-led experiments, and multiple choices so the patient retains control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits plans and reasons to some extent but relies more on practical strategies than drawing out intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Displays a genuinely collaborative stance by asking permission, offering options, and co-creating the plan and check-in timing with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk through permission and plan-focused questions, though could better amplify and summarize the patient's desire/importance language and probe confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes doubt while reframing slips as data and offering low-effort experiments, but could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
