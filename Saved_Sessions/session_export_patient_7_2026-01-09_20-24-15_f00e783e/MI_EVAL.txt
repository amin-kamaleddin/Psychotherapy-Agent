# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and honored the patient's boundaries (offering to sit quietly and limiting contact). Repeated safety statements were clinically appropriate but sounded slightly scripted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate understanding and reflective responses, matching the patient's desire for low‑key interaction. Balanced safety checks with acceptance and perspective‑taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on engagement and safety rather than pushing family exploration. Interventions and a concrete follow‑up plan (single brief text) aligned with that direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint process, inviting input on exercises, timing, and exact wording and implementing the patient's requests precisely. Decisions were co‑constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly emphasized choice and deferred to the patient's preferences about contact and content, explicitly offering to respect boundaries. No pressuring or directive language was used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created a nonpressuring environment where motivation could emerge but made limited attempts to elicit internal reasons for change. Missed opportunities for very gentle, strategic elicitation of values or small concerns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed a collaborative stance and shared decision‑making, incorporating the patient's exact wording and confirming plans. Language and actions reinforced an equal‑partner dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to elicit language in favor of change, appropriately prioritizing engagement and safety but not offering gentle open invitations to explore preferences or small motives. Could try low‑risk prompts to elicit brief change‑relevant statements in future.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk by accepting boundaries and reframing pressure points into choices. The clinician did not challenge resistance and reduced threat throughout the encounter.
