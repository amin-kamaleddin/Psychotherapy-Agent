# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental opening, safety checks, and acknowledgement of practical constraints conveyed respect. Missed opportunities to normalize ambivalence or explicitly affirm the patient's enjoyment of smoking, so acceptance felt somewhat procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are brief validations and tailoring to shift work, showing some perspective-taking. However, the clinician mostly provides information and advice rather than reflecting feelings or exploring ambivalence, making empathy inconsistent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays firmly focused on smoking cessation/harm reduction with clear, practical next steps and a concrete day-by-day starter plan. There is no drift to unrelated topics and the clinician effectively organizes the conversation toward the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician solicits the patient's preference at times and accommodates requests (e.g., tweaking scripts). Nonetheless, the interaction is often clinician-led with technical instructions rather than jointly constructing solutions, so collaboration is partial.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple pathways were offered and the clinician asked which approach felt doable, validating the patient's choice not to quit cold turkey. Frequent prescriptive dosing and recommendations, however, limited deeper support for patient-led implementation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort was made to draw out the patient's own reasons, values, or motivations beyond choosing a practical option. The clinician primarily supplied solutions and logistics instead of eliciting the meaning of change for the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the clinician invites choice occasionally, the interaction is largely expert-driven with detailed instructions and dosing guidance. Co-creation and shared decision-making were limited, keeping power with the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some prompts and praise led to the patient's agreement to try a patch+gum plan and request a week-by-week schedule. The clinician did not consistently amplify or explore the patient's reasons, values, or confidence, so change talk remained superficial.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient sustain talk (enjoyment of smoking, worry about losing the after-work buzz) was acknowledged but not explored or reflected. There were no double-sided reflections or validations to gently shift sustain talk toward change.
