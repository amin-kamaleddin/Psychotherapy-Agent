# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing Linda’s worries and affirming her preparation; avoids blaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening and accurate paraphrase of concerns, but could deepen by using more affect-focused and complex reflections to probe emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on inhaler use, technique, side effects, step-down planning, and follow-up, with interventions tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is cooperative and iterative with invitations for input, but the clinician often leads content creation instead of co-creating materials and criteria with Linda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding coercive language while reinforcing that final decisions rest with Linda and her clinician.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment and factual info but relies heavily on providing information and tools rather than drawing out Linda’s internal motivations, values, or ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and invites input, yet frequently supplies ready-made solutions rather than co-constructing the plan step-by-step with Linda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides small-step options and invites trial but rarely evokes Linda’s own reasons or amplifies her change talk to build commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes and reframes sustain talk with corrective information and alternatives, though could more often employ double-sided reflections to explicitly name ambivalence.
