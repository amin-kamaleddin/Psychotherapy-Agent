# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—normalizing slips, validating limits, and acknowledging competing demands. These behaviors created a warm, nonjudgmental atmosphere throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflective listening captured both content and feeling (burden, desire to avoid pressure) with clarifying questions and useful summaries. This enhanced rapport and deeper exploration of the client's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear focus on medication adherence and progressed to a concrete, time-limited one-week experiment with measurable indicators. Questions and planning remained tightly aligned to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was clearly collaborative with decisions co-constructed and the clinician inviting the client's input at every step. Inclusive language and shared problem-solving maintained parity throughout the exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported autonomy by offering choices, framing the plan as an experiment, and emphasizing the client's control. Language avoided pressure and respected the client's limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited Selene's internal motivations and values (family, health) and drew on past successes to build self-efficacy. Questions focused on mobilizing the client's own reasons for change rather than imposing external ones.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the session as a joint exploration and shared decision-making power, consistently deferring to the client's expertise about her life. The plan was co-created rather than imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited and reinforced change talk about values and practical strategies, and probed for concrete indicators of success. Opportunities were missed to more explicitly elicit commitment language (e.g., scaling questions or a direct commitment prompt) and to intensify amplification of the strongest change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and normalized slips to reduce resistance, but tended to accept sustain talk rather than strategically using double-sided reflections or highlighting discrepancy. More active redirection from sustain talk toward consequences and change could strengthen momentum.
