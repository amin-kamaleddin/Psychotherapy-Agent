# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes the difficulty, and uses reflective summaries to avoid judgment. These behaviors create a safe, nonjudgmental climate appropriate for a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate empathic reflections capture overwhelm, attachment, and fatigue and validate competing priorities. Could increase deeper affect labeling and occasional complex reflections to probe the emotional core (e.g., guilt, fear).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a focused agenda on asthma management with concrete behavioral targets and measurable success criteria. Questions and reflections consistently return to actionable steps without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co‑constructed—clinician solicits the patient's ideas and jointly develops a realistic plan. Language invites joint problem‑solving and respects the patient's expertise about his life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports autonomy by offering options, emphasizing choice, and avoiding pressuring language. The clinician honors the patient's pace and low‑loss approach when choosing steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits intrinsic motives and links the patient's values to specific behaviors and benefits. The clinician draws out and amplifies the patient's own reasons for change rather than supplying external motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks for the patient's preferences, affirms choices, and builds the plan from the patient's suggestions. The clinician's language conveys that the patient's perspective guides the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, invites concrete success markers, and supports planning. Could more actively amplify strong change statements and probe confidence/commitment (e.g., scaling questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectfully acknowledges sustain talk using double‑sided reflections that legitimize concerns while steering toward change. Would benefit from more reframing of sustain language and deeper exploration of barriers like finances.
