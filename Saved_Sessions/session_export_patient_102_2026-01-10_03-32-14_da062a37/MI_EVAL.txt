# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized Milan’s perspective, and avoided judgment. They honored Milan’s agenda and refrained from shaming or moralizing his drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate, timely reflections and summaries that demonstrated understanding of Milan’s feelings and perspective. Clarifying questions and relevant reflections conveyed emotional nuance and supported rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician kept the session focused and goal-oriented around the social/relationship problem Milan raised. Direction was strong for the photo/removal goal but less so for alcohol use as a target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the clinician invited choices, co-created scripts, and acted as a coach rather than an authority. Milan’s feedback was solicited and incorporated into the plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported Milan’s sense of choice by offering options and respecting his preference against lectures. They reinforced autonomy with language that emphasized Milan’s control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician effectively evoked motivation about removing the photo and practical steps, but did not elicit intrinsic motivations or reasons related to Milan’s alcohol use. Evocation was limited to the immediate social problem.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated strong partnership by inviting Milan to choose focus, role‑play options, and escalation strategies. The clinician shared power and co-constructed messages and plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was minimal effort to evoke change talk about Milan’s alcohol use; questions did not probe desire, importance, or confidence to change. Opportunities to explore discrepancy between values and drinking were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided amplifying sustain talk and honored Milan’s minimizations, keeping the session constructive. They did not, however, proactively use double-sided reflections to gently juxtapose sustain and change language.
