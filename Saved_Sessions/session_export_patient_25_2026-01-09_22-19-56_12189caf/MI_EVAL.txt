# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and positive regard, normalizes ambivalence, and affirms effort (e.g., celebrating smoking cessation), modeling unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate, supportive reflections and checks in about the patient’s experience, but reflections are mostly simple and there are missed opportunities for deeper, affective exploration of emotions and meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on supporting smoking abstinence and managing social alcohol use, progressing logically from options to rehearsal and contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is co-constructed with the patient through alternating suggestions and roleplays, and the clinician adapts to the patient's feedback and preferences throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice by offering options, asking permission to practice, and using noncoercive language, consistently reinforcing the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation by highlighting successes and asking which strategies feel doable, but could more explicitly draw out the patient's own reasons, values, and confidence to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates a strong collaborative stance—sharing power, inviting patient choices, and integrating those choices into the plan and practice exercises.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively affirms change and encourages rehearsal and planning, yet could further elicit desire, reasons, need, and confidence to amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects to coping strategies and rehearsal, but tends to offer many concrete suggestions quickly instead of using more reflective techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to soften sustain talk.
