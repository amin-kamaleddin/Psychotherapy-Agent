# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and nonjudgmental support (offers help, safety check, polite validating tone). Could add explicit normalizations of the patient's worries to deepen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates understanding of safety concerns and accurately summarizes risks, with attentive prompts. Responses are mainly informational/directive with limited reflective listening of the patient's emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on safe medication switching and concrete next steps (washout, contacting prescriber, ER signs). Avoids drifting and repeatedly ties content back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative features—iterative message drafting and asking permission to help—treating the patient as a partner. Some recommendations remain clinician-driven without always eliciting the patient's view at each step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports the patient's right to choose and invites decisions about drafts while cautioning against unilateral actions for safety. Several necessary prescriptive safety statements somewhat limit autonomous exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits actionable commitments (agreeing to send messages, supervised washout) but does not probe underlying values, reasons, or confidence to strengthen intrinsic motivation. Missed opportunities to draw out and amplify the patient's own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the interaction as co‑constructed (drafting together, planning follow‑up) and invites patient input. Occasionally shifts into an expert/instructional stance without explicitly checking the patient's perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Encourages concrete steps by offering to draft messages and scripts, which supports movement toward action. Does not systematically elicit the patient's reasons for change, values, or readiness to deepen and amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to safe, supervised planning, reducing risky unilateral actions. Relies on neutral medical directives rather than MI techniques (double‑sided reflections or reframing) to resolve ambivalence.
