# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys respect, normalization, and support (e.g., “I’m sorry you’re struggling — that’s common and fixable,” “I’m holding that space,” “Be kind to yourself”). Occasional corrective/praise language (“I’m proud of your 8/10”) makes stance slightly more directive than fully unconditional.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Some acknowledgement and brief summaries (“That sounds like a great plan,” “Nice work — well done”) and invitations to notice experience nonjudgmentally. Limited reflective depth and few paraphrases probing feelings or meaning (missed reflections on desires like “I want to feel stronger / body confidence”).

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent behavioral focus and concrete steps: exact start times, 10-minute timer, shoes-by-door, specific cue for resistance, and clear homework/report-back. Kept session tightly on target behavior throughout.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 1 ☑ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Limited co-creation. Clinician occasionally asks which tiny step is doable but primarily issues directives and repeated instructions. Clinician-led agenda and closing language position clinician as director rather than equal partner.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Some acknowledgement of choice and nonjudgmental options (e.g., “If you didn’t, note what blocked you without judgment,” “Be gentle with food changes”), but frequent prescriptive prompts and requests for commitment reduce full autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Elicited a concrete plan and patient-generated change talk, but clinician did little to draw out underlying motivations or values (no questions like “What makes you an 8?” or “What would being stronger allow you to do?”).

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Some supportive phrases (“I’m holding that space”) and acceptance of patient choices, but clinician repeatedly returned to scripted prompts and directives; partnership present but partial.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Patient produced multiple examples of change talk (preference for activity, desire for better mood/energy/strength). Clinician validated and praised but missed opportunities to reflect and amplify that talk or to elicit stronger commitment language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and normalized deprioritizing nutrition while supporting focus on activity. Repeated nonjudgmental framing of barriers reduced sustain talk; could further use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while leaning toward change.
