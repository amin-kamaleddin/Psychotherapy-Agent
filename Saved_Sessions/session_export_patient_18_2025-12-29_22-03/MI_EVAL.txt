# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit positive regard and nonjudgmental language throughout (≥6 clear instances). Examples: “You’ve talked about wanting to protect your academics…,” “That pact and the alarm are a real strength,” “I believe you can make that happen.” No blaming or admonishment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent accurate reflections and summaries capturing content and values (≥6 instances). Examples: “You’ve named grades, rehearsals, sleep…,” “You’ve picked a clear, concrete boundary…,” “You’ve already connected these steps to the outcomes you want.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on protecting grades/sleep and moved toward a concrete plan (one night out, leave-by-11, alarms). A few clinician-framed summaries edged toward telling rather than pure elicitation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Many focused, collaborative moves inviting patient to choose specifics (“Which of those matters most…?”, “What’s one concrete step…?”). Could increase micro-collaboration by offering options before confirming a single plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician respected patient choice and encouraged ownership (patient proposed the pact and steps). Missed a chance to elicit stronger commitment (e.g., confidence ruler) to further support autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated, skillful elicitation of change talk (e.g., “What matters most…?”, “If that plan works, what would the next morning look or feel like?”). Follow-up reflections linked statements to values and outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance was strong; clinician co-created the plan with patient input. Some strong clinician summaries reduced moment-to-moment invitations for the patient to fully lead the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple instances of eliciting and amplifying change language leading to concrete, future-oriented commitments (protecting GPA, reliability, pride, rehearsals). Clinician reinforced and linked change talk to outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and clinician avoided reinforcing it. missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections to explicitly capture mild ambivalence (e.g., social pressure vs. grades) to further soften sustain talk.
