# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental with repeated validations and encouragement, normalizing gradual change and offering harm‑reduction options. No shaming or blaming language; clearly conveys respect and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good, generally accurate understanding with timely validations and tailored suggestions. Relies more on brief affirmations than complex reflections and misses opportunities to deepen ambivalence or emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on blood pressure and related behaviors with concrete, measurable 1‑week plan and safety thresholds. Repeated summaries and action steps keep the session goal-directed and practical.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicits patient input, offers options, and tailors supports (scripts, reminders), creating a collaborative tone. Interaction is somewhat clinician‑led with many specific prescriptions rather than eliciting the patient’s solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and acknowledging the patient's control and readiness. Avoids coercive language and reinforces patient ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation (open agenda, drinks/week, offers to draft scripts) that produces concrete commitments, but relies heavily on education and recommendations. Misses questions to surface intrinsic motives, values, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters joint planning and checks preferences, inviting co‑creation of scripts and reminder plans. Partnership is present but weakened by frequent clinician-driven solutions rather than first asking the patient to generate options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical commitments (monitoring, tracking, alcohol‑free days) but does not probe for internal motivation, importance, or confidence. Rarely reflects or amplifies change language to deepen desire or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing and responds to minimization with harm‑reduction options and affirmations, reducing resistance. Could increase use of double‑sided or strategic reflections to more explicitly acknowledge sustain talk while highlighting change reasons.
