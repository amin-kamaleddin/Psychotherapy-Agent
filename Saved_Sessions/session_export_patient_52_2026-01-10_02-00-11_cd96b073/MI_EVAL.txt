# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing ambivalence and validating slips. Tone remains supportive throughout, which fosters safety and trust.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses solid reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures key tensions, stressors, and emotions. Could deepen empathic formulations with more complex, emotion-focused reflections about guilt, exhaustion, or the meaning of 'me-time.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing nightly alcohol use through a concrete one-week experiment, specific strategies, summaries, and planned follow-ups. Every turn returns to the behavioral target and progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs the plan and checks agreement, scheduling mutually acceptable check-ins which supports collaboration. However, frequent unilateral provision of multiple specific tips slightly shifts the dynamic toward the clinician's expertise rather than full equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports client choice by offering options, asking preferences, and emphasizing agency while avoiding coercive language. Reassures client about slips and encourages restarting, reinforcing autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits behavioral commitments and some change talk but leans toward delivering strategies rather than drawing out intrinsic motivations and values. Missed opportunities for evocative questions and scaling importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership by soliciting preferences and co-creating the plan, which conveys that the client's views matter. At times the clinician moves quickly to offering tips without first asking permission or eliciting the client's own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk through the experiment, choice of replacement rituals, and follow-ups. Could more strategically amplify the client's expressed reasons/values and elicit commitment language or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and pivots to practical alternatives and problem-solving. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections that explicitly name both sides of the client's ambivalence to further soften sustain talk.
