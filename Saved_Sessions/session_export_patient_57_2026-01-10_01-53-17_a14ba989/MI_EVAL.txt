# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm opening and early validation conveyed respect and nonjudgmental support, and practical supports were offered; however the stance became more directive quickly and lacked deeper, individualized normalization of Jamal's ambivalence as a teen athlete.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is basic perspective-taking and a reflective question about prior attempts, but empathic responses are shallow after the opening and the clinician moves into advising instead of exploring Jamal's emotional concerns (embarrassment, loyalty, fear).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, consistent focus on peer-pressured drinking and provided concrete, actionable strategies and follow-up steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session reads as expert-driven advice with multiple ready-made lines and plans rather than a joint problem-solving process; few efforts were made to co-develop language or check which options fit Jamal's voice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive moves (offering role-play and options) were present, but frequent prescriptive scripts and lack of permission before giving advice reduced Jamal's ownership over chosen approaches.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were minimal attempts to elicit Jamal's own motivations, values, or goals (e.g., importance/confidence questions or pros/cons), with the clinician largely supplying solutions instead of drawing out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician positioned themselves as the expert, prescribing tactics and scripts rather than affirming Jamal's expertise about his life or inviting him to generate or adapt responses.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opportunities to elicit and amplify Jamal's change statements were often missed; the clinician asked few eliciting questions and tended to offer ready-made lines instead of deepening Jamal's expressed reasons for avoiding drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician redirected away from sustain talk with refusal strategies and protective behaviors, but did not explicitly validate or explore sustain-talk content nor use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to resolve ambivalence.
