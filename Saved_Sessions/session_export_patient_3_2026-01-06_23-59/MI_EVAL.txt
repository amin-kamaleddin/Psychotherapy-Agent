# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (co‑creation language, normalizing reflections) with a respectful, supportive tone and no blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, but many reflections are slightly formulaic and there were missed opportunities to probe deeper emotions like discouragement or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on restarting brief regular exercise and moves the conversation progressively from desire to concrete implementation details (frequency, cues, fallback).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is a genuine joint exploration with frequent invitations for the patient’s input and co-construction of a feasible plan that honors family priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms choice and avoids pressure, eliciting the patient’s own plan and preferences to reinforce ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits intrinsic motivations and resources by targeting values and past successes, leading the patient to articulate specific reasons and implementation intentions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the expert on her life, invites collaboration throughout, and shares power in shaping the plan (patient selects cues and fallback).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with specific questions and reflections, moving from desire/reason toward concrete, actionable planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to planning, but could more deliberately use double-sided reflections or reframes to further diffuse doubts about follow-through.
