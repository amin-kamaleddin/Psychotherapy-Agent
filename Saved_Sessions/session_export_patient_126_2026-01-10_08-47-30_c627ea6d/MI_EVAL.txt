# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful with normalizing language and gratitude for honesty, conveying unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with several affect-linked reflections, though some reflections are brief and the clinician moves quickly to problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on drinking once selected, using targeted assessment, a readiness ruler, a specific harm-reduction plan, and a concrete follow-up plan without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with invitations for input and check-ins, but the clinician often supplies many specific strategies rather than eliciting the patient's own brainstorming or co-creating options more gradually.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes choice and control by offering options, framing suggestions as possibilities, and inviting the patient to pick what feels doable, reinforcing the patient's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some self-motivational language (readiness ruler, choosing a doable step, commitment to check in) but could more directly draw out and amplify the patient's personal reasons for change and values links.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance with shared decision-making and invitations to choose, though at times the clinician shifts into an expert role by offering long lists of tactics rather than co-constructing options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to elicit change talk (readiness ruler, asking which step feels doable) and secured a small commitment, but did not consistently amplify the patient's own reasons or connect change to values and goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and skepticism, which helps, but uses limited MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or eliciting pros/cons) before moving to practical advice.
