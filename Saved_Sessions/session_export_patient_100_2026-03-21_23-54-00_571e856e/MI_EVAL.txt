# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, using validating language and avoiding judgment or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate reflections, though several reflections are repetitive/formulaic and miss deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and school goals and moves logically from values to concrete, time-bound steps (e.g., no drinking on school nights, exit plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative—solicits the patient's views, integrates preferences into the plan, and uses inclusive language to co-construct strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently preserves choice by framing the plan as a small experiment, asking which options feel doable, and avoiding pressure to promise change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits intrinsic reasons and past strengths that support change, but could more actively amplify motivational statements and probe the costs of not changing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the expert, offers choices, and shares decision-making power when developing practical steps like an exit plan and confirming a ride.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces specific change talk (benefits and small steps), though could better amplify, summarize, and assess confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and avoid escalating sustain talk, but does not consistently probe hedges or strategically reframe reasons to continue.
