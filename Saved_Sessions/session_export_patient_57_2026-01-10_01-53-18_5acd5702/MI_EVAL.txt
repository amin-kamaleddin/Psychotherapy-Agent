# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician adopted a consistently supportive, nonjudgmental stance, affirming and normalizing Jamal’s ambivalence and avoiding shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking by capturing both practical and relational concerns and responding with timely, specific reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use in team contexts with targeted assessment, harm-reduction suggestions, role-play, and actionable next steps tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as joint problem-solving with invites to practice and tailored options, but the clinician frequently offered scripted solutions rather than eliciting Jamal’s own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and emphasized Jamal’s control, supporting incremental, client-led harm-reduction strategies without pressuring him to quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations (protecting practice, team standing) and used them to shape strategies, but missed opportunities to draw out deeper personal reasons and link behavior to core values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with permission-seeking and shared planning, though at times the clinician took an expert/teacher role by supplying many options instead of amplifying Jamal’s language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited change talk about consequences and invited planning, but relied more on clinician-generated solutions and could have asked more to deepen Jamal’s own pro-change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and redirected toward harm-reduction, yet used few double-sided reflections that explicitly balanced belonging pressures with Jamal’s desire to protect performance.
