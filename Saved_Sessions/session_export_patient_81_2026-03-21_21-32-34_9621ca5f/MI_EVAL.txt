# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental warmth and repeated validation (e.g., normalizing friends' influence and responding without blame), reducing shame; acceptance felt individualized rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate paraphrase and timely reflections, picked up emotional nuances and checked safety; could use more complex reflections probing identity/belonging to deepen emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing meth use and stabilizing rent with concrete near‑term goals and minimal drift; questions and reflections were purposefully tied to behavior change and implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician worked as an equal partner, soliciting Lukas's ideas and preferences and co‑constructing implementation strategies (texts, envelope, backups), demonstrating genuine shared problem‑solving and power‑sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently affirmed Lukas's choice and control, offered options and respected his pace (cutting back not quitting), and avoided coercive language to support ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician drew out Lukas's reasons and concrete change language and linked steps to his goals, but could have more directly evoked deeper values and intrinsic motivations to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician clearly framed the work as collaborative, invited Lukas to choose among options, treated him as the expert, and repeatedly asked which steps felt most doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, scaling, and a concrete small step, but could have amplified and summarized Lukas's own motivational statements and used decisional balance more strategically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged ambivalence, avoided arguing, and steered toward planning, but missed some opportunities for double‑sided reflections to explicitly validate sustain talk while highlighting change language.
