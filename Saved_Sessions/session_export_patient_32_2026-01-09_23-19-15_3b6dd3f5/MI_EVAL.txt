# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance (opening support, immediate safety check, validating language) and avoids labeling while normalizing the situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently uses perspective-taking and reflective listening (acknowledging pressure and desire for a self-chosen plan), but misses opportunities to deepen emotional reflection on isolation, the breakup, and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on preventing another DUI and harm reduction, repeatedly steering toward concrete risk-reduction behaviors like cabs, prebooked rides, and exit plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a true partnership with co-constructed plans, role-play, permission-seeking, and offering options rather than directives throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice and agency by offering options, inviting selection of 2–3 items, avoiding coercive language, and providing help only when invited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits specific motivational statements and actionable ideas (scripts, cab plans, confidence ratings) but could do more to draw out deeper values and past successes (e.g., the six months sober).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters shared decision-making with frequent checks on preference, collaboratively drafting the plan and using the patient's wording and choices to guide the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk through choice, scripting, rehearsal, and a confidence check, producing concrete commitments and increased self-rated confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and redirects sustain talk toward safety and harm-reduction, but rarely uses double-sided reflections or explicitly names the sustain arguments to more fully resolve ambivalence.
