# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with unconditional positive regard, using reflective language and normalizing statements. The clinician avoided labeling or shaming and validated the patient's perspective throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent perspective-taking demonstrated via paraphrases, reflections, and summaries that acknowledged the patient's priorities and barriers. The clinician's responses showed clear understanding of the patient's view and emotional stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on mini cigars and oral health, tying every exchange to harm-reduction strategies. The clinician set a specific two-week follow-up to maintain behavioral focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the interaction as a joint enterprise, inviting patient input and co-constructing the plan. Decisions were mutually agreed and tailored to the patient's request for simple, practical steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice and agency by offering options, asking permission before advice, and framing suggestions as offers. The clinician validated small-step approaches and avoided coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some change language (weekday rule, harm-reduction steps, 2-week check-in) using permission-based prompts. Could more actively draw out intrinsic motivations, importance/confidence, and amplify the patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative and power-sharing, repeatedly offering choices and tailoring advice to the patient's preferences. The patient helped shape the concrete strategies adopted in the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced emergent change talk and translated it into concrete steps, such as the weekday rule and rinsing. Missed opportunities to more directly evoke commitment, amplify reasons for change, or use scaling questions to strengthen resolve.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoided reinforcing sustain talk and did not confront minimizations, offering neutral information and harm-reduction instead. Could increase use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge benefits and concerns and tip ambivalence toward change.
