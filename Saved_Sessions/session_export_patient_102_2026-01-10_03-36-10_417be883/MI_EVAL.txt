# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and validating, treating the patient's perspective as legitimate and supportive throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective statements, but could deepen exploration of underlying emotions and social pressures beyond pragmatic responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on limiting drinking with all interventions tied to that target and concrete outcomes like safety and avoiding trouble.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works cooperatively by inviting roleplay and incorporating the patient's choices, though frequent structured tips and checklists slightly reduce parity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice and offers manageable options rather than imposing abstinence, but the many specific recommendations tilt toward an expert stance and explicit autonomy statements are limited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical commitments and solutions but rarely draws out the patient's internal motivations, values, or reasons for change beyond pragmatic concerns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative coaching relationship and invites input, yet also leads with concrete suggestions, so power-sharing could be a bit stronger.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk through setting limits and rehearsal, but could further explore values, importance, and confidence to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with minimizations and redirects to workable control strategies, though more double-sided reflections would better acknowledge ambivalence.
