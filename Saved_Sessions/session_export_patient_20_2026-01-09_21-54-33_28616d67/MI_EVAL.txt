# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and normalized Brandon’s ambivalence without labeling or shaming, which supported openness and tentative commitments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used targeted reflections and paraphrasing that captured both content and affect (e.g., drinking to unwind, concern about mornings), showing strong empathic understanding throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing weekday drinking with concrete, time-bound goals and implementation intentions, moving logically from ambivalence to a specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked as an equal partner, repeatedly asking for Brandon’s input and building on his suggestions so the plan was clearly co-produced and owned by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasized Brandon’s choice and control, avoided pressuring language, and respected his limits (e.g., keeping weekends free), reinforcing client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited personal reasons and concrete benefits for change (less grogginess, better focus) but could have probed deeper into intrinsic values and systematically amplified the strongest motivators.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently framed the work as collaborative, inviting Brandon to choose steps and leveraging his past successes to co-construct a feasible plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively elicited and reinforced with realistic steps, but the clinician missed opportunities to amplify and consolidate it into stronger commitment language (e.g., scaling or a client summary).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation and sometimes used implicit double-sided reflections, yet could more explicitly juxtapose sustain and change talk or explore costs of sustain talk to further soften it.
