# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Communicated respect / positive regard (opening: “I’m here to listen and support you”)
- [x] Affirmations offered (“Nice concrete plan — great job”)
- [x] Normalized slips / nonjudgmental language (“Accept slips as data, not failure”)
- [ ] Individualized affirmation of specific past efforts (missed opportunity)

**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with clear affirmations and normalization of slips. Minor suggestion: further individualize affirmations (e.g., name specific achievements like continuing walks/gardening).

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Reflective listening present (“That hope plus worry makes sense”)
- [x] Acknowledged affect and concerns (hope, worry, tiredness, nervousness)
- [x] Validated energy limits and barriers (snoozing, mornings)
- [ ] Used deeper/complex reflections (missed opportunity)

**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and validating, but often brief and quickly move to planning. Could deepen empathy with more complex or double-sided reflections before shifting to solutions.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Maintained clear focus on target behavior (morning stretching)
- [x] Narrowed and offered concrete options (10-min stretch, 5-min fallback, days/times)
- [x] Resulted in measurable, actionable plan and follow-up (alarm 7:30, Mon/Wed/Fri, Friday check-in)

**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus leading to a clear, specific plan. Occasionally moves into prescriptive suggestions but appropriate for brief behavior-change work.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Invited the patient’s choice and preference (“Which of those… feels doable?”)
- [x] Incorporated patient’s selections into the plan (patient chose morning stretch, cues)
- [ ] Allowed patient to brainstorm first before offering many clinician-generated strategies (missed opportunity)

**Comments:** Good collaborative tone and incorporation of choices; at times the clinician’s solution set dominated the interaction. Consider asking permission before offering many specific strategies.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Reinforced choice and ownership (“Which cue… feels easiest?”)
- [x] Framed slips as information, not failure
- [x] Offered options/fallbacks to preserve control (2-minute chair stretch, reduce to 5 minutes)

**Comments:** Strong support for autonomy throughout; avoids pressure and reinforces patient control over decisions and adjustments.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicited specific preferences, feasibility, and commitment (days, times, cues)
- [x] Drew out practical change talk and commitment language (“I’ll set an alarm… I’ll do Mon/Wed/Fri”)
- [ ] Deep elicitation of underlying values/motivations or confidence ruler (missed opportunity)

**Comments:** Successfully elicited practical plans and some commitment. Could deepen motivation by asking evocative questions about meaning/values and asking for confidence/importance ratings.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Framed session as collaborative (asked preferences, offered check-in)
- [x] Supported shared planning and follow-up (Friday review)
- [ ] Invited patient to summarize plan earlier to enhance ownership (missed opportunity)

**Comments:** Partnership is evident, though clinician often led plan formulation and repeated recaps. Invite the patient to summarize the plan earlier to strengthen ownership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicited and reinforced “I will” commitment statements
- [x] Reflected and affirmed patient’s willingness and plan
- [ ] Systematically amplified reasons/importance/confidence (missed opportunity)

**Comments:** Change talk was elicited and supported with reflections and affirmations; could more actively amplify reasons and confidence (e.g., ask “What makes this worth trying now?” or use a confidence ruler).

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Acknowledged sustain talk (worries about snoozing, tired mornings)
- [x] Normalized ambivalence and slips
- [x] Offered practical mitigations to reduce sustain talk’s pull (fallbacks, alarm strategies)
- [ ] Used double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence before problem-solving (missed opportunity)

**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and addressed with pragmatic strategies; consider using double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence before moving to solutions to further reduce resistance.
