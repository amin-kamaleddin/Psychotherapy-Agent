# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed warm, nonjudgmental regard and validated Marcus’s priorities without blame, sustaining unconditional positive regard throughout the excerpt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking with nuanced reflections linking Marcus’s concrete plans to his values and emotions, showing deep understanding rather than mere restatement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on increasing physical activity and linked concrete, achievable steps to meaningful health goals with no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was a clear joint exploration—clinician solicited Marcus’s ideas and co-constructed a practical plan without dominating or prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly supported Marcus’s sense of choice, offered options, and repeatedly asked what he was willing to try, reinforcing his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic motivators and values (family presence, energy, less guilt) but could probe deeper into emotional core and draw out stronger commitment or confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with invitations to choose next steps, though occasional clinician endorsement language slightly shifted the balance and more explicit agenda negotiation would help.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and specific implementation plans, but did not explicitly elicit a scaled confidence rating or a stronger, summarized commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers were acknowledged and the clinician redirected toward small realistic steps, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections that explicitly contrast sustain talk with change talk.
