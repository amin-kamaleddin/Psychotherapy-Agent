# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, using validation and normalizing language and avoiding blame while affirming practical concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening about pressure and logistics with validating statements, but missed opportunities to probe underlying values, fears, or deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on completing court-ordered community service hours and tied all interventions directly to that target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a genuine team effort with co-constructed tools (logs, texts), frequent checks, and adjustments to Julian's requests demonstrating shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options, asked permission before customizing messages, and respected Julian's preferences, reinforcing his choice and control throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little elicitation of personal reasons, values, or confidence for completing hours; clinician relied on pragmatic supports rather than evoking internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally positioned the work as collaborative and sought Julian's input, though could more explicitly invite him to co-design the plan and acknowledge his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Few attempts to elicit intrinsic change talk; the conversation focused on logistics and options rather than exploring desire, reasons, or personal motivators.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not strengthen sustain talk and redirected to practical steps, but did not use advanced MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk.
