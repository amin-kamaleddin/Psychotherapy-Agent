# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language (e.g., ‘‘safe, nonjudgmental space,’’ normalizing slips) and avoided labels or blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used timely, specific empathic reflections and validation (acknowledging shame, fear, and linking feelings to caring for her daughter), which made the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, explicit focus on smoking cessation with concrete assessment and actionable steps (reduction schedules, quit date, check‑ins, NRT/meds) while balancing empathy and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed interaction as joint problem‑solving and invited preferences (choice of schedules, check‑ins), but at times offered detailed clinician‑generated prescriptions rather than eliciting the patient's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported choice and control by asking permission, offering options (gradual vs quit date), and leaving key decisions (timing, NRT) to the patient, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language (e.g., wanting to quit for her daughter) but did not consistently deepen intrinsic motivation or confidence (missed scaling and targeted evocation questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and sought input, yet occasionally shifted to a more directive role by presenting detailed step‑by‑step plans rather than co‑constructing them with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and received statements of desire/reasons, but missed chances to amplify and deepen motivation (e.g., follow‑up evocation or reflective amplification).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized slips, offered slip scripts and reframing to reduce sustain talk, though could further use double‑sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence more strategically.
