# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Anara’s concerns with affirmations and nonjudgmental language. No shaming or minimizing; clinician validated childcare and cost priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, consistent empathic listening with reflections that matched both facts and feelings. Reflections reduced distance and fostered trust and safety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on engaging with medical care and linked each turn to concrete next steps (short video, scripts, role‑play, social work). Session remained goal‑directed without becoming pressuring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicited preferences, co‑constructed scripts, and repeatedly invited Anara to choose next steps. Treated the patient as a partner rather than a passive recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirmed choice and offered multiple options, preserving agency with questions like “what feels most doable?”. Slightly directive when giving a strong recommendation for an in‑person visit without prefacing with permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used open questions and elicited practical motivations and doable steps (e.g., 10–15 minute video, scripts). Could more systematically draw out reasons, importance, and confidence and amplify self‑motivating statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the interaction as a joint, co‑creative process and treated Anara as the expert on her situation. Offered tailored supports and repeatedly asked which option felt most helpful.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited several pieces of change talk and proposed small first steps that generated tentative commitment. Could strengthen and deepen change talk with follow‑ups to build desire, ability, reasons, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected sustain talk and offered pragmatic mitigations (scripts, role‑play, social work) to reduce barriers. Rarely used double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries specifically designed to tip ambivalence toward change.
