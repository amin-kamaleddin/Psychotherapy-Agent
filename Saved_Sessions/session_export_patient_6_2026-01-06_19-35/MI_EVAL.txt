# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful and validating with frequent affirmations, acknowledging Alexei’s priorities and efforts. Comments sometimes feel formulaic rather than deeply personalized, so more explicit normalization of adolescent experimentation would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries show good attunement to Alexei’s worries and practical concerns. Several reflections remain at content/strategy level rather than probing underlying emotions (anxiety, shame), so deeper affect-focused reflections would increase empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and its impact on college soccer/scholarships, with every intervention tied to that target. The clinician progressively narrows to concrete, implementable steps without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is highly collaborative, with the clinician soliciting Alexei’s input on lines, practice methods, accountability, and integrating his preferences into the plan. Decisions are co-created rather than imposed, demonstrating an equal-partner dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options are consistently offered and Alexei is invited to choose the approach that feels easiest, supporting ownership and self-determination. The clinician reinforces choice by asking which option he prefers and how he wants accountability.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicits Alexei’s own reasons for change, linking strategies to his core values (soccer, scholarships) and amplifying his commitments. Open questions and reflective prompts draw motivation from Alexei rather than providing reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician adopts a collaborative stance and invites choices about practice and accountability, but sometimes leads with suggested options. Greater explicit positioning of Alexei as the expert on his social context and inviting him to generate more options would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is consistently elicited and developed, linking behavior change to valued goals and prompting concrete, doable steps (refusal lines, role-play, backups). Follow‑up on feasibility and implementation intentions amplifies commitment and specificity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge minimizations while nudging toward concern. More explicit normalization of ambivalence and strategic juxtaposition of both sides would better soften sustain talk.
