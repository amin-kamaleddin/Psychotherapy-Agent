# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect with strengths-based language and normalization; no criticism or blaming was present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated frustration and worry, but reflections were sometimes repetitive and missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on securing contact (call/ supervised visit) and moved the session toward actionable steps tied to that goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving, invited Tariq to generate ideas, and co-created the plan including practicing the counselor script together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, asked which options felt most doable, and sought permission before practicing, reinforcing Tariq's control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic motivations tied to family connection and concrete benefits, but could have linked short-term actions to longer-term goals and strengthened commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively shared power by soliciting Tariq's preferences, using collaborative language, and treating him as the expert on workable solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out and amplified change talk with specific options and role-play, though did not fully consolidate stronger commitment or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, but tended to mirror the resistant content rather than using double-sided reflections or explicit strategies to downshift sustain talk.
