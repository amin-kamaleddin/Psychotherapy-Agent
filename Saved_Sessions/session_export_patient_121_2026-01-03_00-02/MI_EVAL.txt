# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, repeated acceptance and respect (e.g., “That sounds really heavy…,” “I’m proud you’ll try”). Tone respectful throughout. Statements became somewhat formulaic/repetitive and lacked explicit normalization of Sofia’s guardedness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening matching defensiveness and hurt (“You want connection…”, “That worry makes sense”). Reflections were mostly summary/supportive and missed opportunities for deeper emotional exploration (shame, isolation); shifted to problem-solving fairly quickly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on a concrete behavioral target (talk to a trusted teacher, improve attendance). Used scaling, produced an actionable script and coping strategy, and established timing (“Wednesday”). Session stayed on task throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited input and choice (asked which part to practice, permission to role-play). Co-created elements of the plan, but at times became prescriptive by offering a near-complete script rather than eliciting Sofia’s own wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong support for choice and control (permission, “what feels possible,” accepting doubts, safety options). Avoided pressuring language and respected Sofia’s pace and ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited goals and a small step and confidence rating (6), but did not sufficiently draw out or amplify Sofia’s own change language or underlying motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and respectful; checked readiness and preferences. Partnership weakened when clinician provided scripted language rather than more actively shaping it with Sofia’s voice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-oriented statements present (misses friends, willingness to try), but clinician did not probe or reflect to strengthen desire/reason/ability/need or elicit commitment language more fully.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinician largely avoided engaging sustain-talk content (blame/defensiveness) and did not employ double-sided reflections or strategies to gently pivot sustain talk toward change.
