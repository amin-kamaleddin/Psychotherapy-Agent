# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful stance (e.g., “That’s fine,” “I hear you — you want to be heard”). Explicitly avoided lecturing and honored Malik’s request not to be given rules. Delivery felt somewhat formulaic and would benefit from more individualized warmth/normalization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and summaries (“you feel pushed to be here,” “you enjoy smoking to relax and fit in,” “you feel annoyed and embarrassed”), demonstrating understanding. Reflections were repetitive and surface-level; missed opportunities for deeper or complex reflections exploring underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behavior (frequency, context, potential harms) and offered brief harm-reduction options. However, clinician repeatedly deferred to Malik’s preference to “just be heard,” reducing momentum toward change planning or problem-definition.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited Malik’s input and choice about session focus (“Which of those feels most helpful?”, “Which would you prefer right now?”). Allowed Malik to set the agenda and demonstrated genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Malik’s right to choose and avoided coercive language (“I respect you not wanting rules right now,” “You can come back anytime — no judgment”). Strong support for autonomy and readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions invited Malik’s perspective (what “it” is, frequency, what he gets out of it), but clinician did not consistently push beyond surface answers to elicit values, motives, or more substantive reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively shared power—offered options, asked permission before suggesting ideas, and deferred to Malik’s wishes. Malik’s choices clearly shaped the session throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was minimal and not systematically elicited or amplified. The clinician did not strategically follow up on the few change-relevant remarks to deepen desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk (e.g., “not all the time,” “I’m not stopping,” “my friends don’t think it’s a problem”) was largely left unaddressed. Clinician avoided arguing appropriately but did not use MI techniques (double-sided reflections, reframes, discrepancy) to soften sustain talk or redirect toward change.
