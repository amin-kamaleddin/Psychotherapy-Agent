# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone (e.g., opening support, affirmations, normalizing slips) and respected the patient's stated stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking with summaries and validations, but many reflections were content-focused and missed opportunities for deeper affective or complex reflections to explore emotions or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking throughout the session and progressed logically from pros/cons to a concrete porch-only plan with operationalization and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction with frequent invitations for the patient's input, co-construction of the plan, and a balanced expert/partner stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced patient choice by offering multiple options, accepting low readiness to quit, and asking permission implicitly before offering advice or suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation via pros/cons, readiness/confidence ratings, and a personally chosen goal, but could more systematically draw out intrinsic reasons and personal values beyond external pressures.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the encounter as shared decision-making, offering choices and jointly developing a realistic, contextualized plan (porch-only) with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change-oriented language and operationalized a small-step commitment, but could better amplify spontaneous change talk and invite elaboration of internal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and skillfully pivoted around sustain talk with harm-reduction options, though using more double-sided reflections would explicitly acknowledge both sides and highlight discrepancy.
