# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the patient's choices and accommodating preferences (e.g., template edits). No shaming or dismissive language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows basic empathic responses and encouragement but relies more on information and reassurance than deep reflective listening. Missed opportunities to reflect the patient's minimization or explore emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use as it relates to blood pressure and repeatedly advances a concrete plan (tracking, follow-up with doctor). Minimal topic drift and each clinician turn moved toward actionable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative, soliciting patient input at multiple decision points and adjusting the plan based on the patient's preferences. Phrases and actions positioned the clinician as a helper rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms patient choice and avoids coercive language, offering options and framing follow-up as optional. Normalizes readiness levels and invites the patient to decide tracking method and duration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical, immediate commitments (agreeing to track, set reminders, bring log) but does not sufficiently draw out broader personal motivations or explore importance/confidence. Evocation centers on logistics rather than intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates active power-sharing by co-creating the tracking template and repeatedly asking the patient's preference. The patient's ideas clearly shaped the plan and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces small-step change talk around tracking and concrete commitments. Missed chances to deepen change talk by exploring reasons for change, pros/cons, or scaling importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and reframes tracking as information-gathering to defuse resistance. Could strengthen use of strategic reflections (e.g., double-sided) to further acknowledge ambivalence and create openings for change.
