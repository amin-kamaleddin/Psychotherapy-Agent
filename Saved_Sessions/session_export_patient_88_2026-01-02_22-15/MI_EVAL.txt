# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Clinician established and maintained unconditional positive regard from the start ("safe, nonjudgmental space; do I have your permission…"), honored Marcus's requests (no lecture, brief text, not telling parents unless dangerous), and avoided shaming or labels throughout.

### Empathy
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate paraphrase of Marcus's perspective (e.g., valuing fitting in vs. avoiding trouble). Reflections were mostly content-level rather than deeper affective/layered reflections — missed opportunity to probe underlying vulnerability.

### Direction
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on weekend drinking, readiness, risks, and concrete planning (limits, buddy system). Used targeted questions (0–10 readiness, moving toward 7–8). Could have pushed further to elicit stronger explicit commitment.

### Collaboration
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Collaborative stance (asked permission, solicited Marcus's ideas, negotiated check-in style). Plan was co‑constructed (Marcus chose limits and buddies), though clinician sometimes offered strategies rather than eliciting a fuller list from Marcus first.

### Autonomy Support
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Marcus's control and choices (permission to discuss, honoring "no lecture," parental disclosure limits, asking which strategy felt realistic). Avoided pressuring language and reinforced ownership.

### Evocation
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Elicited Marcus's reasons for change (fewer hangovers, less conflict/trouble) and practical strategies (limits, alternation, buddies). Good use of scaling question and follow-up to generate actionable responses; could deepen by exploring longer-term values/goals.

### Partnership
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Treated Marcus as collaborator, checked preferences about follow-up and parental notification, and incorporated his input into the plan. Partnership felt genuine though clinician offered concrete suggestions early rather than eliciting all alternatives first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (negatives of drinking, benefits of cutting back, readiness rating, concrete commitment to 1–2 drinks and buddies). Reflections and affirmations helped consolidate plan; could further amplify commitment language with a direct commitment question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
- [x] Criterion met  
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Clinician did not confront sustain talk ("everyone does it," "not quitting"); instead acknowledged it and redirected to consequences and planning. Could have used more double‑sided reflections or summaries to explicitly juxtapose sustain and change reasons to further weaken sustain talk.
