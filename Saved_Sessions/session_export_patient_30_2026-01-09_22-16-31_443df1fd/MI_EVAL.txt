# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and respect, validated the patient's skepticism, and honored boundaries (e.g., accepting 'not promising to start').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and normalization and asked permission before probing, but many reflections were surface-level and the clinician often shifted quickly to information-giving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the medication decision, gathered relevant data, calculated 10-year risk, and developed a concrete stop-plan with follow-up steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—co-created the stop-plan, implemented patient edits, offered scripts and printed materials, and behaved as an ally rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by asking permission, offering choices (trial vs lifestyle/ezetimibe), endorsing non-negotiables, and providing language the patient could use to refuse a statin.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some open questions and elicited preferences, but relied heavily on information delivery and problem-solving and did not systematically draw out deeper personal motives, values, or past successes to powerfully motivate change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the encounter as a joint enterprise—repeatedly solicited preferences, handed control to the patient, and iteratively revised plans per patient input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited tentative change language and offered actionable options, but did not consistently reflect, amplify, or strategically respond to the patient's small change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and legitimized the patient's concerns while offering alternatives and pragmatic safety measures, though there were missed opportunities for double-sided reflections to more artfully highlight ambivalence.
