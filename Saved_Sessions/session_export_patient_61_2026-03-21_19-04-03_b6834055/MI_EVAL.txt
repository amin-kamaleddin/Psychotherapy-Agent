# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized frustration, using validating language and avoiding blame which supported rapport and lowered defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid empathic listening with accurate reflections of overwhelm and barriers, but stayed at a surface level with missed opportunities for deeper, more complex emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on diabetes self-management throughout the session and progressed logically from barriers to a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-created the plan and asked which option felt doable, though the clinician often supplied concrete implementation details rather than eliciting the patient's own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and framed actions as low-stakes experiments, offering options and asking permission which strongly supported patient autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some eliciting prompts (options, pros/cons), but did not sufficiently draw out the patient's intrinsic motives, values, or use scaling questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked in partnership by inviting choices and collaboration on the plan, though at times shifted into more clinician-led implementation steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited minimal change talk and a behavioral commitment, but missed chances to amplify or explore the patient's own reasons, values, or benefits of change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used normalization and low-stakes framing to reduce resistance, yet sustain talk persisted and the clinician rarely used double-sided or pivoting reflections to resolve ambivalence.
