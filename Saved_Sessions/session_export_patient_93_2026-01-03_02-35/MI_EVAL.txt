# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language throughout (“I accept where you are…”, “I accept that tension…”). Repeated explicit acceptance, normalizing ambivalence and reflecting values (independence, social life).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections and summaries that capture content and underlying values (“I hear you enjoy your time…”, “I hear that ‘feeling steadier on my feet’ is the real test”). Regular checks for agreement and validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on reducing risky alcohol use while preserving social connection/independence. Moved logically from values → small plan (alternate with sparkling water) → measurable outcome and date.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly equal partnership: open questions, invited choice, built on patient’s ideas and past successes. Minor moments of clinician endorsement (“I believe you can do this”) slightly shifted away from pure elicitation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms patient choice and control, avoids pressure, preserves preference to cut back not quit. Occasional clinician-framed encouragement could better foreground the patient’s own agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change ideas and linked them to prior successful strategies (morning walks). Secured a concrete, patient-generated plan; could further amplify the patient’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned as collaborative partner using patient wording and choices to shape the plan. A few brief directive affirmations reduced fully shared ownership at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited change talk (willingness to cut down, concrete plan) and committed to short-term test. Would strengthen session fidelity by more explicitly reflecting and summarizing the patient’s change statements to amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reflected ambivalence (pub’s social value vs. safety) and normalized it. Could more often use double-sided or complex reflections to further soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
