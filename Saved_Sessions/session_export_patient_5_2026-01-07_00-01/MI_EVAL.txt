# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, repeatedly validating the patient’s caution and praising carefulness. Language is nonjudgmental and supportive throughout, never minimizing concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate paraphrasing of priorities and plans. Reflections are mostly summary-level with limited deeper exploration of underlying emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on safe medication switching, washout timing, tapering, and monitoring. Interventions consistently return to the patient’s stated goals without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner through co-constructive questions and joint document-building, inviting the patient’s input on details. The patient’s ideas directly shape what the clinician drafts and how the plan will be used.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports patient autonomy by offering choices, asking preferences, and deferring decisions to the patient. Avoids coercive language and affirms the patient’s role in selecting priorities and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits priorities and concrete intentions and prompts the patient to operationalize plans. Could deepen evocation by probing values, importance, and confidence to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative, using joint language and shared decision-making throughout the interaction. Frequently invites the patient to co-create documents and confirms preferences before acting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports movement toward action with offers to draft questions and prompt prioritization and planning. Would be stronger with more amplification of change talk and targeted questions about reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Limits and reframes potential sustain talk by validating caution and redirecting to concrete risk-reduction steps. Could use more MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, selective reflection) to further diminish lingering ambivalence.
