# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly expressed unconditional positive regard and normalized the client's experience, using validating language that avoided judgment and acknowledged the importance of her values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provided accurate, specific reflections of both content and feeling (e.g., weekly review habit, where tension lands), demonstrating strong perspective-taking and emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on the target behavior throughout the session, using structured questions that steadily advanced a concrete, testable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as a co‑investigator with inclusive language and repeated checks of the client's preferences, co‑creating the plan with concrete contributions from the client and family.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the client's choice by offering options and inviting her to decide specifics, affirming agency while helping design supports.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used evocative questions to draw out the client's own motivations, imagery, and solutions, and consistently built on the client's self‑generated reasons and ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as collaborative and power‑sharing, soliciting the client's and partner's input and incorporating their preferences into the agreed plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced the client's change language with focused questions, producing sustained change talk and a concrete plan (including the word 'contentment').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled minimal sustain talk by validating caution and quickly moving to safety and testable solutions, though could more often use double‑sided reflections to juxtapose concerns with the client's reasons for change.
