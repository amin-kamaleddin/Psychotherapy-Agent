# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, honoring Ahmed's preferences and limits. The clinician normalized concerns and treated his informational needs as legitimate without shaming or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening captured Ahmed's ambivalence and tone. Reflections and summaries validated his feelings and matched his request for low-pressure facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a focused, appropriate agenda on mini-cigar use with concrete next steps (one-page PDF, 2-week cutting-back trial, checklist, text reminders). The clinician steered the sequence from information to plan to monitoring without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with joint decision-making about format, content, timing, and trial parameters. Ahmed's input shaped the plan at every step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Ahmed's freedom to choose and offered multiple options to support ownership. The clinician avoided pressure and deferred to Ahmed's preferences for how to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations, preferences, and tentative commitment language but did not sufficiently deepen intrinsic reasons or assess confidence. Could strengthen by exploring values, asking 'what would it mean...' questions, or using scaling to build motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated Ahmed as the expert on his life and co-created the plan, negotiating details and tailoring to his needs. Power was shared and decisions were made jointly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited conditional change talk and a tentative willingness to try cutting back, and co‑designed a doable trial. Missed opportunities to amplify change talk and convert tentative language into stronger commitment (e.g., ask confidence, emphasize benefits).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and instead neutrally reframed and redirected to facts and experimentation. The clinician validated requests for straightforward evidence and offered low-pressure options rather than arguing.
