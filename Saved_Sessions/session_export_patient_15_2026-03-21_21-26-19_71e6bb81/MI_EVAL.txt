# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained unconditional positive regard and respect throughout, normalizing the patient's priorities and strengths without shaming. The warm, supportive tone was consistent and nonjudgmental.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective summaries validated the patient's concerns and actions. The clinician could deepen affective reflection and probe minimization of alcohol with more curiosity to elicit emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on blood pressure management with concrete behavioral targets, clear options, and thresholds for action. The clinician consistently linked actions to the patient's values and maintained goal-oriented momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction read as a true partnership with co-created plans, checks for preference, and offers of practical support. Occasional prescriptive details were balanced by frequent invitations for patient input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were routinely offered and permission sought before suggestions, reinforcing the patient's right to decide. The clinician affirmed decisions and fostered ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited motivations, worries, and used confidence rulers to draw out reasons for change. However, they often moved quickly to planning rather than further amplifying personally compelling reasons or linking change more deeply to values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited patient preferences, co-drafted plans, and checked logistics, reflecting a collaborative stance. Partnership was somewhat diluted when the clinician supplied detailed prescriptive checklists instead of eliciting the patient's own specifics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change language, offered options, and used scaling to strengthen commitment. More frequent reflections and amplification of the patient's change statements would further deepen desire and ability to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about alcohol was acknowledged and not confronted, with a gentle redirect to tracking. The clinician did not use strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or ambivalence exploration) to actively soften or reframe sustain talk.
