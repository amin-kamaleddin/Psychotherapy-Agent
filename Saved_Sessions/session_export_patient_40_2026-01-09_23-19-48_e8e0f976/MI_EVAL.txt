# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized shame without blame, using validating and supportive language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and attunement—checked safety, invited the patient's narrative, and accurately mirrored and normalized the patient's feelings in a timely way.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on relapse prevention and immediate safety with concrete action steps and ongoing monitoring for medical danger.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a collaborative problem-solver—invited preferences, co-created texts and a plan, and stayed engaged with the patient’s choices and requests (e.g., staying for 20 minutes).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported patient choice and control by offering options, asking which step they could commit to, and validating hesitancy without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited immediate change talk and commitments (delay, supports) but did not systematically explore deeper intrinsic motivators or use importance/confidence scaling to strengthen longer-term motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally conveyed a collaborative stance and asked preferences, though at times delivered a structured plan without an explicit permission check, shifting briefly toward an expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced specific, actionable change talk (commitment to delay/breathing and naming supports) but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify desire, reasons, and long-term values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward coping and planning, though could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly integrate sustain and change language.
