# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating (opening line, normalizing, thanking), conveying clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and reflections of context and ambivalence, but reflections are sometimes formulaic with missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings like guilt, shame, or what 'tired of it' means.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on shoplifting reduction with practical strategies and role-plays directly tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction feels collaborative with choices and rehearsal, though the clinician frequently supplies scripted content rather than co-constructing solutions from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports autonomy by offering options and asking 'what feels most doable,' but at times pushes specific tactics without always eliciting permission or patient-generated preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-oriented language and choices but lacks deeper evocation of the patient's intrinsic motives, values, and confidence; missed chances for evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally treats the patient as a collaborator through invitations and shared decisions, though the clinician sometimes shifts into an expert-driven posture with directives and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk and concrete planning and praises small wins, but moves quickly into offering solutions rather than creating more space for the patient to articulate reasons and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes sustain talk toward risk-management strategies, but uses few explicit double-sided reflections or summaries to more strongly weaken sustain talk.
