# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating from the start, offering practical help rather than moralizing. Avoided blaming language and conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and summarizing that helped the patient open up. Could deepen empathy by exploring Maya's feelings, ambivalence, and the meaning of drinking for her with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related concerns and moved logically from assessment to concrete harm-reduction strategies. Kept the session on task and produced a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative with co-created plans, inclusive language, and offers to help draft messages. Early assessment was somewhat clinician-driven (checklist style) rather than fully paced around the patient's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored choice by offering options, asking permission, and inviting the patient to tweak the plan. Reinforced that the patient decides what to try and how to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation via a readiness ruler and offering choices, but limited elicitation of personal values, reasons, or past successes. Missed opportunities to deepen motivation by drawing out importance and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt collaborative with repeated invitations for patient input and shared problem-solving. Could improve by more explicitly acknowledging the patient's expertise about her context and handing agenda-setting more fully to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (readiness scale, goal-setting, affirmations) and invited selection of strategies. Did not consistently probe or amplify Maya's own reasons, importance, or confidence to strengthen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and accepted skepticism, offering pragmatic harm-reduction that prevented reinforcement of sustain talk. Missed chances to use double-sided reflections or reframe sustain talk to more actively tilt toward change.
