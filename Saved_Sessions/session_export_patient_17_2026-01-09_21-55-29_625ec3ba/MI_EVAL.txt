# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, validating feelings without blame and acknowledging shame and stigma in a nonjudgmental way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately reflecting both content and affect and capturing ambivalence to deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation, guiding the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps and a doable first-week plan without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in true partnership by soliciting the patient's input at each step, adapting options to her context, and co-constructing the plan rather than giving top-down advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice and control by avoiding pressure, offering multiple options, and inviting her to pick what felt doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out personal reasons and strategies and prompted past successes, but could further deepen intrinsic motivation by eliciting the patient's own arguments for change and using scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed suggestions as options and repeatedly asked for the patient's preferences, conveying that expertise about her life and choices resides with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with small-step planning and recall of past successes, though missed opportunities to amplify commitment language and ask confidence/importance ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and quickly pivoted to planning and supports to reduce focus on maintaining smoking, but did not regularly use double-sided reflections or strategic exploration to resolve ambivalence.
