# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—normalized imperfection, validated concerns, and honored the patient's limits throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and summaries that captured ambivalence and feelings, enabling the minimally responsive adolescent to elaborate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on improving communication strategies with concrete, actionable steps (short phrase, hand sign, rehearsal) without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged the patient in co‑constructing the plan—brainstormed options, invited preferences, and adjusted approach (role‑play style) based on her comfort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and ownership by offering options, affirming non‑perfection, and respecting hedges rather than pushing for commitment.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's reasons for change and desired outcomes but could deepen evocation by exploring importance/confidence and amplifying intrinsic motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as the expert throughout, repeatedly inviting her decisions about wording, rehearsal format, and pacing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk and planning from a minimally responsive adolescent, but missed some opportunities to amplify and convert statements into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing resistance by neutrally acknowledging doubts and shifting to doable steps; could further soften sustain talk with more double‑sided or strategic reflections.
