# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental, warm, and supportive, normalizing the client's feelings and avoiding blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows clear effort to understand and briefly reflect the client's stress and practical concerns, but uses few deep or complex reflections to explore underlying emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on completing court-ordered hours and provides a concrete, stepwise plan with practical tools and follow-up actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is strongly collaborative: the clinician asks permission, elicits preferences, and adapts templates and scripts based on the client's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, framing help as optional, and checking the client's preferred approach and timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies mainly on providing solutions and logistics rather than drawing out the client's own motivations, values, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and shares tasks, but the clinician often leads agenda-setting and supplies many expert-driven solutions rather than fully equal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical commitments and plans (calls, templates) that support short-term change talk, but does not probe personal reasons, meanings, or intrinsic motives for completing hours.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes nervousness which reduces resistance, but does not consistently use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or ambivalence exploration) to soften sustain talk.
