# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing ambivalence and respecting the adolescent’s perspective throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used perspective-taking and reflective statements that mirrored ambivalence, though reflections were often brief and occasionally shifted quickly to advice rather than deeper affective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use with concrete behavioral goals (pacing, alternating drinks, safe rides) and repeated prompts for weekend commitments and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative interaction: clinician invited the patient to choose strategies, engaged him in role-play, and tailored options to his preferences rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly framed suggestions as choices, asked which options felt doable, and avoided coercive language, supporting the patient’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited motivations and practical ideas for change but intermittently shifted to giving prescriptive lists instead of further drawing out the patient's own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative and invited input, but occasional long, prescriptive lists and limited negotiation before advice slightly reduced the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and secured commitments, but could have amplified change statements more and explicitly probed confidence and past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and reframed sustain talk toward safety and harm reduction, though more double-sided reflections or summaries would have better highlighted discrepancy.
