# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with multiple affirmations and normalization of side effects; validated motivations and challenges without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking and reflective summaries of reasons and stressors, but offered few deeper emotion-focused reflections (e.g., frustration or fear).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on smoking cessation with a logical progression (assessment, medication options, quit date, titration, and follow-up).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed a concrete plan and solicited preferences, though the clinician occasionally adopted a more directive/expert stance when recommending varenicline.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking preferences, checking agreement, and honoring requests for a private check-in.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited reasons, confidence, and medication choice early on, but shifted toward provider-driven information and planning rather than continually drawing out the patient's own motivations and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as partners to set a quit date and follow-up and invited input on spouse involvement, though at times the clinician led the decision-making with strong recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (reasons, confidence, quit date, commitment prompts) but missed opportunities to amplify past successes and deeper values to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and offered pragmatic coping strategies, but used few MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explore and soften ambivalence before moving to problem-solving.
