# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—validates ambivalence, acknowledges fears, and frames steps as patient-driven without shaming. This creates a high level of acceptance and emotional safety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of pros/cons and tailored responses to Rosa's concerns. The clinician consistently shows an accurate understanding of her emotional state.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and moves the conversation toward concrete, time-bound steps. The session progresses from ambivalence to a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly a collaborative partnership—offers choices, asks for Rosa's preferences, and integrates her ideas into the plan. Help is offered in an invitational, non‑directive way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports Rosa's sense of choice and control by offering multiple options and asking permission for interventions. Language avoids pressure and reinforces that actions are Rosa's decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits Rosa's motivation and internal resources well using scales and open prompts, but frequently supplies information and solution options. Could increase open questions and strategic amplification to draw out more intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative and positions Rosa as the expert on her life, asking which options feel doable and co‑creating the plan. Power is shared throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk (scaling, exploring small steps, concrete commitments) but misses opportunities to amplify and reflect Rosa's own change statements to deepen commitment. More strategic reflections and follow-ups could strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, acknowledges it neutrally, and redirects toward change with options and summaries. Could use more explicit double‑sided reflections and selective reflections of sustain talk to emphasize movement toward change.
