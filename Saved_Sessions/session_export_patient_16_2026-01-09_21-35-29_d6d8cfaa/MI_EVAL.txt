# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently displayed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating the patient and avoiding shaming language. This created a safe, respectful atmosphere for change talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate summaries and reflections showing understanding of Anika's perspective. More complex reflections exploring emotional nuances of ambivalence and minimization would strengthen empathic responding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on vaping and concrete behavior change tied to basketball season, moving logically to assessment, coping strategies, and a specific if–then plan. The clinician consistently guided toward measurable steps that matched the client's priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reflected an equal partnership with the client invited to shape the agenda and choose options. Decisions (who holds the pen, timing, lines) were co‑constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed choice and control, offering multiple options and asking permission before role‑play. The approach avoided pressure and respected the patient's final decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited motivation and confidence language (confidence scaling, steps to increase confidence). More probing to elicit intrinsic, personally meaningful reasons beyond external drivers (sport/suspension) would deepen internalized motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician clearly positioned Anika as the expert and actively invited her input on decisions and plans. Power was shared through collaborative planning and acceptance of the client's choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and amplified change talk, summarized commitment language, and developed concrete plans that reinforced change intent. Opportunities remained to probe deeper for intrinsic motives and expand DARN (desire, ability, reasons, need) language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk but did not actively soften or explore it with strategic reflections. Using double‑sided or amplified reflections and explicitly normalizing ambivalence would better reduce sustain talk.
