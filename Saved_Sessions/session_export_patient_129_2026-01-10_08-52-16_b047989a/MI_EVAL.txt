# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (validated feelings, thanked for honesty). Language normalized Tyler's responses and avoided moralizing throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking (paraphrased benefits/costs of use, checked safety). Followed Tyler's preference to be heard before offering options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on substance use, safety, and school functioning from assessment through concrete harm-reduction planning. Moves were purposeful and time-bound.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as joint problem-solving with repeated invitations for input and co-creation of specific plans. Clinician reinforced Tyler's contributions and positioned him as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Tyler's choice and offered options (listening, planning, harm reduction), respected his decisions, and avoided pressuring him to quit. Confidentiality limits and preferences were clarified.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited client-generated motivations, scaling responses, and behavioral steps, but could have probed deeper into values/long-term goals to strengthen internal motivation before offering advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with inclusive language and permission-seeking, but sometimes shifted toward expert-delivery via long harm-reduction lists without more explicit negotiation of agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and built on change talk (scaling, concrete if–then plan), yet missed opportunities to amplify reasons for change and to elicit stronger commitment language before giving advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation and validated sustain-oriented statements, then redirected to planning; could increase use of double-sided reflections to more explicitly balance ambivalence and highlight change talk.
