# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, opening with support and thanking Marcus for honesty. Sets factual boundaries (won't help with illegal advice) while immediately offering alternatives, preserving respect and a warm atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening and some reflective statements (e.g., validating peer pressure and summarizing risk). Missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections to capture emotional nuance like feeling misunderstood or fear of exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol-related risk reduction throughout the session, moving from assessment to specific, measurable harm-reduction strategies. Keeps pragmatic momentum toward behavior change with concrete rehearsals and an exit plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner—offers options, solicits Marcus's choices, and adjusts language to his style while co-constructing tactics. Role-plays and mutual problem-solving exemplify shared ownership rather than expert-driven instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, respecting Marcus's wording preferences, and avoiding pressuring language. Even when limiting illegal advice, preserves agency by offering alternatives and asking what he is willing to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivation and concrete commitments (two tactics and successful rehearsals) but focuses more on behaviors than on deeper intrinsic motives. Could more directly probe values, longer-term goals, or importance/confidence to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invites Marcus to set the agenda, choose tactics, and rehearse language, accepting his preferred phrasing. Interaction fosters client ownership and shared decision-making throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk, asking which tactics feel doable and amplifying commitment through rehearsal and praise. Did not extensively probe intrinsic reasons for change or assess importance/confidence to deepen and broaden change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and skillfully redirects to harm-reduction and normalization of difficulty. Could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both pros and cons and further soften sustain talk.
