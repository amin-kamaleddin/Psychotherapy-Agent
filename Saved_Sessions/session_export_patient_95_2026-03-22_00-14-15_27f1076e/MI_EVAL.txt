# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental respect, repeatedly validating Maria's intense distress and honoring her request not to be rushed. This steady tone supports safety and rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate, affect-laden reflections that mirror Maria's language and demonstrates close emotional attunement. The clinician matches pace/affect and compassionately checks for danger.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on suicidal ideation and immediate safety, asking about plan/intent and organizing concrete steps (removing pills, who will hold them, staying while calls are made). Validation is balanced with safety-directed questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint enterprise using inclusive language and co-constructs safety decisions (e.g., who could hold the pills). The tone conveys equality and mutual problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Maria's sense of choice by offering multiple options and asking which feels right, respecting her prerogative to choose. Recommendations are framed as options rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out internal motivation (the 'tiny' reason for living and past coping) and elicits a small safety behavior. Could more systematically deepen and amplify Maria's values and elicit confidence/commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a collaborative stance and invites Maria's input on choices, brainstorming together about safety options. To reach a top rating, the clinician could more explicitly emphasize Maria's expertise in shaping the safety plan and hand more agenda-setting to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports change talk (noting the tiny part wanting to stay, probing past coping, translating safety steps into action). There were missed opportunities to amplify reasons for staying, ask importance/confidence questions, or summarize commitments more strongly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk by neutrally reflecting hopeless statements and pivoting toward safety actions. Could more actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or reflections that highlight and strengthen change language.
