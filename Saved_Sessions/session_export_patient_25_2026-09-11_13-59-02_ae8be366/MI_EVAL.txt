# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys respect and positive regard, repeatedly affirming the patient’s quitting success and validating concerns without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows understanding by linking drinking to health and parenting concerns, but relies on brief reflections and would benefit from deeper feeling-laden reflections to capture emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on social drinking and progressively narrows the conversation to specific, actionable choices and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed as joint problem-solving, inviting the patient’s ideas and co-constructing a plan in a non-authoritarian, engaged manner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options and repeatedly asks the patient to choose what feels most doable, honoring the patient’s self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out practical solutions and links change to past success, but could further elicit internal motivational language (desire, reasons, confidence, commitment).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions expertise with the patient by asking for preferences and inviting the patient to design the plan, reinforcing agency and shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk through open questions and linking past success, producing multiple concrete change statements and plans from the patient.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing for continued drinking and redirects toward protective strategies, though it could better soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or brief pros/cons when doubts arise.
