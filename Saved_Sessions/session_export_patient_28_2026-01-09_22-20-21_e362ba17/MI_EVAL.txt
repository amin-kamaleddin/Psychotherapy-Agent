# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated effort repeatedly, but sometimes offered directive suggestions and somewhat formulaic affirmations without asking permission, which reduced a sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good perspective-taking and reflective listening and normalized ambivalence, but reflections were generally surface-level with missed opportunities to deepen emotional understanding about identity and meaning of past workouts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity and moved exchanges toward a concrete, time-bound plan with useful summaries and minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as a team effort with frequent solicitation of preferences, but the clinician often led with ready-made strategies instead of eliciting the patient's own brainstorming, reducing equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed the patient's freedom to choose, honored boundaries (e.g., declining accountability texts), and invited choice about options and check-ins, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out change-relevant statements and elicited a specific plan, but could have probed deeper for intrinsic motives, values, and confidence to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed decisions as the patient's and negotiated the plan collaboratively, yet frequently provided specific implementation steps without asking permission to advise, which shifted some power to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk by asking about past routines and offering choices, but could have amplified the patient's own language about desire, reasons, and ability and probed for stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized uncertainty, and redirected to small steps, but seldom used double-sided or more nuanced reflections to explicitly invite resolution of ambivalence.
