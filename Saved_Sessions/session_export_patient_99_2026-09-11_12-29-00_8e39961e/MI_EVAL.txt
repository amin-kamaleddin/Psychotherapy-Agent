# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation, acknowledging values and planning for slip‑ups. Language is warm and accepting throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate summaries that reinforce rapport. Could deepen by using a few more complex reflections to explore underlying feelings like embarrassment or fear of re‑injury.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing risky drinking and links it to the presenting concerns. Questions and structure systematically move from options to a concrete, realistic plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is strongly collaborative, inviting the patient's ideas and jointly designing an experiment. The clinician positions both parties as equal problem‑solvers and lets the patient shape next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly underscores the patient's choice and control by offering options and asking which feels most realistic. Validates a non‑perfect approach and avoids pressure or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits the patient's own motivation, strategies, and confidence ratings to build intrinsic motivation. Uses targeted questions to raise confidence and move toward implementation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the session as a genuine partnership, deferring to the patient's expertise about his social life and preferences. Co‑constructs plans rather than prescribing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk through evocative questions and reflections, leading to concrete implementation steps. Repeatedly draws out the patient's reasons and specific plans for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing for the status quo and redirects sustain talk toward pragmatic problem‑solving. Missed a few opportunities for double‑sided reflections that would acknowledge both social benefits of drinking and reasons for change.
