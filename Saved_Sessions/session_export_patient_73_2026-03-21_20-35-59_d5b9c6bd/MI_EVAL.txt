# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with warm, nonjudgmental language and frequent affirmations, creating a genuinely accepting atmosphere throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and acknowledges emotions and ambivalence, though could deepen empathy by naming underlying feelings like shame, frustration, or hopelessness more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on tobacco cessation and balances exploration with concrete planning (NRT choices, delay strategies, role‑play, check‑ins) to move toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is consistently collaborative, involving joint problem‑solving, co‑created scripts, and tailoring options to the patient's preferences while inviting practice and follow‑up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before suggestions, avoiding pressure, and framing recommendations as options the patient can accept or decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations, importance/confidence ratings, and concrete reasons for change, but could further evoke internal motives with values/future‑self questions and summary reflections to build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly fosters partnership and shared decision‑making by asking what feels doable, offering supports as options, and inviting the patient's input on how the clinician can help.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk through questions about past successes, importance/confidence ratings, and concrete options, but could deepen and extend change talk with more evocative questions and reflective amplifications.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges social triggers and offers practical strategies but uses few MI techniques specifically to soften sustain talk (limited double‑sided reflections, reframing, or strategic rolling with resistance).
