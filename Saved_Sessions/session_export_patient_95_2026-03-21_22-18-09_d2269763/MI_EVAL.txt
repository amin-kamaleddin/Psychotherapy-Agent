# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, with explicit validation and offers to stay while the patient acted; honored the patient's request to have her pain heard before moving to skills.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent empathic statements and accurate reflections captured key content and tone, but many reflections were short and repetitive rather than deeper, more complex reflections that explore the nuance of hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on imminent suicide risk with direct risk assessment and repeated, concrete safety options; direction was clinically appropriate throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative behaviors (co-writing messages, staying while patient acted, soliciting preferences) without an authoritarian stance; could more explicitly elicit the patient's knowledge and co-create a longer-term plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered multiple options and asked which felt most doable, preserving choice while acknowledging limits; occasional firm safety language slightly reduced perceived choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some initial elicitation of goals and a small step, but the session quickly shifted to directive safety planning and did not sufficiently draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good power sharing through joint message-writing and ongoing invitations for preference, but could more explicitly invite the patient's ideas about what helps and frame the plan as co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited limited change talk and reinforced small steps, but missed multiple opportunities to amplify desires, reasons, ability, and commitment language beyond immediate safety steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and validated sustain talk, yet did not consistently use double-sided reflections or ambivalence-focused strategies, often shifting quickly to directive safety planning.
