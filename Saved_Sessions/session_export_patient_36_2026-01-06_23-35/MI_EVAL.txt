# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, using normalizing statements and affirmations that validate the client's experience and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and validation are present, but reflections are often brief and quickly shift to solution-focused scripting rather than deeper exploration of the client's feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remains tightly focused on boundary-setting and managing guilt with a clear, concrete plan and targeted practice exercises, with no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is cooperative with role‑plays and check‑ins, yet the clinician frequently supplies ready-made scripts and stepwise instructions, which at times positions them more as an instructor than a co‑creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supports choice by asking permission, offering alternatives, and framing suggestions as options, reinforcing the client's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation (inviting role‑play, naming guilt) is present, but the clinician largely provides wording and strategies instead of systematically drawing out the client's own motivations, past successes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invites collaboration and tailors tweaks to the client's concerns, but frequently provides exact phrasings rather than co‑constructing language from the client's own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician reinforces and practices change‑oriented statements and highlights values, but misses opportunities to use open questions that elicit more client‑generated reasons, importance, or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal and effectively redirected: the clinician normalizes guilt, teaches naming and self‑compassion tactics, and provides brief scripts to limit further argument.
