# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Clinician maintains respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms Viktor’s perspective (e.g., “You’ve already taken a step by being here,” reflecting “not promising to quit”). Avoids labeling. Not rated 5 because affirmations felt somewhat formulaic/repetitive rather than highly individualized. Recommendation: vary affirmations with more specificity tied to Viktor’s values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and summaries of Viktor’s position and concrete consequences (“nights would be calmer,” “mornings wouldn’t be tense”) show perspective-taking. Reflections stay mainly at behavioral/content level; could probe deeper emotions (tiredness, frustration, loneliness). Recommendation: add occasional affect-focused reflections to invite richer disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Keeps conversation focused on harm reduction and concrete behaviors (11 pm rule, no weekday meetups, buddy call). Moves Viktor from denial to specific steps. Not a 5 because session cycles through planning questions without a clear next-step commitment or follow-up plan. Recommendation: move toward a collaboratively agreed, time-limited trial and schedule a check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Strong joint problem-solving (“Let’s figure this together,” “as equals”); repeatedly asks Viktor to choose realistic options and generate solutions. This approach supported Viktor’s ownership of the plan. Recommendation: continue; when offering ideas, ask permission to preserve ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Explicitly honors Viktor’s choices and preserves agency (reflects “I’m not promising to quit,” does not coerce). This stance reduces resistance and supports engagement. Recommendation: reinforce autonomy by asking how Viktor wants to be supported and by offering to revisit options if the plan doesn’t work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Elicits Viktor’s own reasons and past successes (asked what worked before; Viktor described routines and supports). Promotes change talk about calmer nights and fewer fights. Not a 5 because clinician could probe deeper for personal meaning and long-term values. Recommendation: ask evocative, values-linked questions to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Fosters power-sharing and invites Viktor to lead on what’s realistic; explicitly frames decisions as Viktor’s. Slight steering toward particular strategies occurred (repeated focus on set home time/buddy call), which can reduce purely client-led stance. Recommendation: present suggestions as hypotheses and invite Viktor to adapt them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Clinician reflects and amplifies Viktor’s change talk (calmer nights, fewer fights) and elicits concrete steps. Missed opportunities to deepen and sequence change talk into commitment language (no scaling of importance/confidence, no explicit time-limited commitment). Recommendation: use scaling/confidence questions and invite a brief experiment (e.g., try 11 pm for two weeks) to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without confrontation (e.g., mirrors “not promising to quit,” “keep the guys”), which avoids escalation. However, clinician often restates sustain language and does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to tip toward change. Recommendation: employ double-sided reflections and summaries that name both sides and gently highlight discrepancy to soften sustain talk.
