# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with validating statements that acknowledge ambivalence; could add more explicit normalizing language and individualized affirmations to convey deeper unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and summaries show good understanding of the patient’s perspective, but reflections focus more on behavior/logistics than on emotional nuance; using more feeling‑level, complex reflections would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, specific behavioral focus and repeatedly brings conversation back to concrete, actionable steps and troubleshooting; direction is appropriately goal‑oriented and pragmatic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites the patient’s input at each decision point and builds the plan from the patient’s suggestions, demonstrating clear shared decision‑making and no authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames change as optional and experimental, repeatedly asks which options feel most doable, and avoids prescriptive language; patient’s sense of choice is well supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s own solutions and practical ideas through open questions, but could more explicitly probe values/discrepancy and elicit confidence/commitment language to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language emphasizes joint problem‑solving and power‑sharing, consistently treating the patient as an equal partner in planning; co‑construction of the experiment is clear.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits past successes and specific small experiments that produced explicit commitment language, yet could do more to amplify, reflect, and summarize the patient’s change talk and assess confidence/importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and offers low‑pressure, nonthreatening options that reduce sustain talk, but could use more double‑sided reflections and explicit normalization to better tip ambivalence toward change.
