# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Conveys nonjudgmental positive regard (welcoming opening)
- [x] Normalizes and supports practical needs
- [x] Encourages and affirms client’s actions
- [ ] Uses highly individualized affirmations (area to improve)

**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive (e.g., “I’m glad you reached out,” “You’re allowed to ask for help”). Strong normalization and closing encouragement. Consider more personalized affirmations referencing past strengths or specific successes.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Uses reflective listening and acknowledges concerns
- [x] Elicits client context (ages, daily routine)
- [x] Validates nervousness and suggests calming (slow breaths)
- [ ] Provides deeper, affect-focused/complex reflections

**Comments:** Appropriate empathic tone and contextual understanding; validates affect and offers calming. Could deepen rapport by adding one or two complex reflections that capture disappointment/ambivalence before shifting to solutions.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Maintains clear focus on target behavior (attending the interview)
- [x] Links interventions directly to the goal (childcare solutions → interview attendance)
- [x] Keeps session oriented toward concrete next steps

**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on the goal and barriers; interventions tightly linked to the target behavior. Pacing was effective for this problem-focused visit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Invites client input and choice (asks which options feel possible)
- [x] Co‑creates scripts and role‑plays with client
- [x] Respects client preferences and decisions (e.g., $12 offer, breathing)

**Comments:** Clear joint problem‑solving stance; clinician repeatedly asks for Amina’s preferences and offers options to co‑create solutions. Continue offering brief choices to reinforce partnership.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Offers choices and alternatives
- [x] Acknowledges client’s right to decline or set limits
- [x] Avoids pressuring language; emphasizes flexibility

**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy (“Which of these feels possible?”, “Totally fine if you can’t”). Reinforces client ownership of plan and implementation.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicits practical motivations and constraints (asks what’s possible)
- [x] Asks client to select concrete first steps (1–2 actions)
- [ ] Deep elicitation of underlying values/reasons or past successes

**Comments:** Good elicitation of practical reasons and choices that produced change talk (“it sits about at a 10”). Could strengthen by asking targeted questions about deeper reasons, values, or previous successes to amplify intrinsic motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Fosters collaborative stance (drafts together, role‑plays)
- [ ] Avoids clinician-driven option lists that may overwhelm
- [x] Engages client in deciding next steps

**Comments:** Generally collaborative and co‑creative (scripts, role‑play). At times clinician offered many options early, which could feel clinician-driven — pause after options and ask client to prioritize to keep the agenda client-centered.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicits and supports change talk (client states readiness and takes action)
- [x] Moves talk toward concrete, actionable steps (scripts, role‑play)
- [ ] Systematic amplification/reflection of change talk and explicit commitment elicited

**Comments:** Successfully elicited and translated change talk into action (client sent the text, planned the call). Could further consolidate motivation by reflecting/amplifying change talk and asking a brief commitment question or specific timing.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Avoids reinforcing sustain talk; reframes barriers into problem‑solving
- [x] Validates constraints while steering to solutions
- [ ] Uses double‑sided reflections explicitly to acknowledge sustain talk

**Comments:** Effectively redirected sustain talk (childcare barriers) toward workable solutions without minimizing concerns. Could more explicitly use double‑sided reflections to acknowledge the pull of responsibilities while highlighting steps toward change.
