# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance (opening invitation, repeated assurances, respectful acknowledgements), conveying warmth and respect throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening with validating statements and tailored responses; could deepen reflections to probe underlying feelings and affect more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with specific safety questions, concrete harm-reduction suggestions, a one-weekend experiment, and a scheduled check-in, avoiding topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—offered menus of options, invited patient choice, and co-constructed the plan using inclusive language, positioning decisions as joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, avoiding coercive language, honoring the patient’s framing as an experiment, and checking preferences for wording and approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited specific, testable commitment language and asked for concrete details that revealed motivation; could better draw out deeper intrinsic values and link change to the patient’s broader goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership through permission-seeking, choice, and inviting the patient to define success, but could more explicitly state the patient's expertise and mirror more of their language in summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked and strengthened change talk with permission, specific questions, and offering experiments that produced agreement; could more deliberately amplify and reflect emerging change language to increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and redirected it toward harm-reduction and safety, preventing escalation; could more often use double-sided reflections and explicitly validate ambivalence before pivoting.
