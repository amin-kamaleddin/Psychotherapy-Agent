# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### Score (1–5): 4
Checklist:
- [x] Communicated respect and nonjudgment ("You’re not failing; you’re figuring this out.")
- [x] Normalized ambivalence/validated doubt ("That doubt makes sense.")
- [x] Affirmed patient strengths (noted quitting smoking as evidence of capability)
- [ ] Purely unconditional positive regard without evaluative praise (not consistently present)

Comments: Warm, respectful language and repeated normalizations throughout the session; occasionally formulaic phrasing and some evaluative praise that slightly reduced purely unconditional acceptance.

---

### Empathy
#### Score (1–5): 4
Checklist:
- [x] Accurate reflective listening of content and affect (repeated complex reflections)
- [x] Summarized values and doubt effectively ("you’d prefer to avoid medication but aren’t convinced…")
- [ ] Deeper probing of nuanced emotions and minimizations (limited)

Comments: Strong, accurate reflections and perspective-taking across the session, though reflections were sometimes repetitive and emotional nuance could have been probed more deeply.

---

### Direction
#### Score (1–5): 5
Checklist:
- [x] Maintained clear focus on target behavior (BP control / alcohol reduction)
- [x] Elicited measurable, time-limited actions (one-week trial, start day, specific substitutions)
- [ ] Drift from target behavior (no)

Comments: Consistently steered the conversation toward a concrete, prioritized behavioral experiment with clear measurable steps.

---

### Collaboration
#### Score (1–5): 5
Checklist:
- [x] Invited patient input and preference ("What would feel like a meaningful, realistic first step?")
- [x] Co-created the plan with patient choices (alcohol‑free weekdays, start Monday, walk instead of first beer)
- [ ] Clinician-dominant directives (no)

Comments: Frequent invitations for the patient's choices and joint problem‑solving; the patient’s suggestions directly shaped the plan.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### Score (1–5): 5
Checklist:
- [x] Honored patient agency and choice (acknowledged skepticism, left decision to patient)
- [x] Framed options and short experiment (try for a week, decide afterward)
- [ ] Pressuring language (no)

Comments: Clear respect for autonomy throughout; patient selected the change, timeframe, and supports.

---

### Evocation
#### Score (1–5): 5
Checklist:
- [x] Drew out patient’s past successes and linked them to current change (quit smoking → strategies to reuse)
- [x] Elicited patient-generated solutions and strategies (start day, routine swaps, wife's reminders)
- [ ] Reliance on clinician-provided solutions (no; clinician mostly evoked patient's ideas)

Comments: Excellent use of evocation—asked about what helped with smoking cessation and mapped those strategies to alcohol reduction, eliciting multiple change-oriented ideas from the patient.

---

### Partnership
#### Score (1–5): 4
Checklist:
- [x] Treated patient as collaborator and sought his perspective repeatedly
- [ ] Some clinician-heavy summaries and evaluative framing reduced sense of equal partnership at times

Comments: Generally collaborative and respectful, though several long clinician summaries and occasional evaluative language subtly shifted balance toward clinician-as-evaluator.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### Score (1–5): 4
Checklist:
- [x] Elicited desire/ability/reasons and commitment language (plans, start day, "I'll try")
- [x] Used past success as evidence for ability (quitting smoking)
- [ ] Active amplification/strengthening of commitment (limited; missed confidence/commitment probes)

Comments: Multiple concrete expressions of change talk and a specific behavioral commitment were elicited; could have more actively amplified and consolidated commitment (e.g., confidence ruler, scaling).

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### Score (1–5): 3
Checklist:
- [x] Avoided arguing or directly reinforcing sustain talk (neutral reflections to skepticism)
- [ ] Used double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic softening (minimal)
- [ ] Explored functions/benefits of sustain talk to resolve ambivalence (limited)

Comments: Clinician appropriately avoided confrontation and did not reinforce sustain talk, but made few strategic attempts to soften sustain talk (double-sided reflections, exploring benefits of current behavior, or eliciting discrepancy were underutilized).
