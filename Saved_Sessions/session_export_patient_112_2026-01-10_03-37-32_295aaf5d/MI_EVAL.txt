# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating and normalizing Rosa's concerns throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and validation of anxiety and withdrawal, but misses deeper reflections on identity-related loss and the emotional weight of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation and moves the conversation purposefully from exploration to a concrete plan (quit date, strategies).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invites co-creation, role-play, and responds to Rosa's preferences—though occasionally shifts into an informational/expert role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, honoring the patient's med-free preference, and asking permission for next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical plans and a quit date but relies more on providing information than systematically drawing out Rosa's intrinsic motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely partnership-oriented with shared planning and rehearsal, though at times the clinician provides detailed solutions without first eliciting Rosa's perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change intentions and commitments (e.g., quit date), but could deepen change talk by exploring personal reasons and values for quitting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reduces sustain talk through normalization and concrete strategies, yet leans toward reassurance and information rather than using more double-sided reflective techniques.
