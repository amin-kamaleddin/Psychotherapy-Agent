# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, with frequent validation and honoring of the patient’s preferences. Never minimizes distress and affirms the legitimacy of her concerns, supporting a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening with multiple accurate reflections of affect and content. Repeatedly acknowledges emotions and the burden of symptoms, showing highly consistent understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on diagnostic clarification and symptom management throughout the session. Keeps momentum toward concrete next steps (tests, referral, diary, dietitian) and negotiates actionable options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co-constructed: the clinician offers tools and repeatedly invites the patient to choose, practice, and prioritize. Jointly drafts scripts, role-plays, and adjusts plans to the patient’s stated concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and agency with permission-based offers and option framing. Honors firm preferences and negotiates plans that preserve decision-making control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-relevant language (asks what matters most) but does not consistently draw out or develop the patient’s own reasons, values, or confidence. Missed opportunities to probe importance, confidence, and to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative and shares power, inviting the patient to co-create concrete actions like scripts and referrals. Repeatedly asks for the patient’s input and places her in the lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation of change talk is present, but amplification is uneven and limited strategic reflections are used. The clinician could ask more open, specific questions to deepen and reinforce the patient’s change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and tactfully honors the patient’s preference for further tests while negotiating a compromise plan. Could increase use of double-sided or complex reflections to gently pivot sustain talk toward change.
