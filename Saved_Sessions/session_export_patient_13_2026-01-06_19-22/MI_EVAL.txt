# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through normalization, nonjudgmental language, and reassurance about pacing and safety. Avoids labeling and respects the patient's limits throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, reflective listening by naming emotions and burdens and checking comfort repeatedly. Reflections attend to both content and feeling, showing strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on seeking assessment/care with concrete steps (message draft, calendar/alarm instructions) and ongoing safety checks. Resists drift and consistently moves toward actionable, realistic goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient in co-constructing next steps, invites input on wording and preferences, and offers help only with permission. The interaction is reciprocal and responsive to the patient's concerns and pace.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering options, explicitly refusing to act without permission, and affirming that small steps count. Honors patient choices about communication methods and timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation by asking what feels manageable and endorsing micro-steps, but much content is clinician-proposed. Missed opportunities to draw out deeper reasons, values, or hopes connected to seeking care.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters genuine partnership through repeated permission requests, shared decision-making, and honoring the patient's readiness. Co-creates the plan rather than assuming an expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces tentative change talk (e.g., preferring to send a message and validating that micro-step), but does not consistently deepen desire, reasons, or confidence. More open questions and reflections could amplify change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids confronting sustain talk by normalizing avoidance and offering smaller, manageable steps, including accepting a decision not to act now. Could strengthen use of double-sided reflections and selective amplification to tip ambivalence more toward change.
