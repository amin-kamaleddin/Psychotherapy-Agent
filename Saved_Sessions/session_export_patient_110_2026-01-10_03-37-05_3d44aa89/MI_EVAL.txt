# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Begins supportively and responds respectfully, normalizing worries and validating caution about side effects; however many responses are concise/formulaic and there are few explicit reflections of ambivalence or unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows clear perspective-taking and tailors recommendations to the patient’s feelings and knee limitations, but reflections are brief and the clinician misses deeper exploration of mixed affect and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management and concrete behavior change with time‑bound, actionable steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks questions, adapts the plan to physical limits, and rehearses scripts with the patient, but collaboration is sometimes reduced by long, clinician-driven lists rather than co-creating options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and alternatives and avoids coercive language, yet several firm recommendations and detailed plans are delivered without always checking the patient’s preferences first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits the patient’s goal and links actions to values, but the session relies heavily on information and directive planning rather than actively drawing out the patient’s own motivations and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites collaboration and adapts to the patient, but much of the interaction is clinician-driven with prescriptive plans instead of co-constructing and letting the patient prioritize options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforces some change talk and ties behavior to values, yet there are missed opportunities to systematically elicit and amplify desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframes concerns, normalizes worries, and offers monitoring/alternatives to reduce sustain talk, but does not consistently use MI-specific techniques like double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence summaries.
