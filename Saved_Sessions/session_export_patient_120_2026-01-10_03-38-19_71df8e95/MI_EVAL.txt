# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician showed respectful, nonjudgmental regard (safety check, validating reflections) but could add more individualized affirmations of Sofia’s strengths and normalize ambivalence before moving into advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening (accurate summaries, reassurance about confidentiality) but the clinician tended to move quickly to problem-solving instead of lingering with deeper emotion-focused reflections or labeling intense guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use in pregnancy with concrete planning and safety checks, though at times offered multiple options instead of narrowing to one collaboratively agreed immediate next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moves (offering choices, asking preferences) were present, but the session felt largely clinician-driven with many ready-made scripts rather than consistently co-creating plans with Sofia.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options early, yet used directive statements (e.g., 'Stopping is safest') and limited permission-seeking; greater emphasis on agency and asking before giving advice would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited factual info and occasional motivation but relied on supplying solutions; more open, evocative questions and scaling to draw out Sofia’s own reasons and confidence were needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some partnership language and invitations, but expertise was often positioned with the clinician through prescriptive plans; more mutual problem-solving and eliciting Sofia’s ideas would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted some change statements (e.g., 'I wanna be responsible') and planning, but missed opportunities to reflect, amplify, and explore importance/confidence to deepen and strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk by offering nonjudgmental refusal scripts and harm-reduction strategies, though could add more double-sided reflections to explicitly link sustain concerns to change motivations.
