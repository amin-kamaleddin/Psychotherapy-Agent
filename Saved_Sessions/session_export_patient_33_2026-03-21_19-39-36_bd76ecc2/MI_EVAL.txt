# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, expresses appreciation for disclosure, normalizes safety steps, and uses permission language to convey unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and validation (acknowledges fear, paraphrases), but missed opportunities for deeper empathic reflections of underlying emotions and ambivalence behind use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on heroin use and harm reduction throughout, providing concrete, actionable steps and following the patient’s stated priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is framed as a joint project: clinician solicits preferences, tailors scripts/resources to the patient, invites roleplay, and integrates the patient’s suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice with permission language and multiple options, respecting the patient’s decision to pursue harm reduction rather than quitting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some self-generated motivation by asking focus questions, but often provides information rather than systematically drawing out the patient’s own reasons, values, or strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly conveys shared decision-making and patient expertise, co-constructing scripts and plans while actively seeking the patient’s input and consent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language and offers practical supports, but misses key opportunities to deepen and amplify commitment (e.g., follow-ups on ‘I’m a 10’).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and redirects toward safety, but does not use advanced softening techniques (double-sided reflections or reframing) to actively resolve ambivalence.
