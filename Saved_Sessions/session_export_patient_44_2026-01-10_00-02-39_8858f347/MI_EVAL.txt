# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through affirmations, normalizing skepticism, and praising effort. Avoided labeling or blaming and framed setbacks as expected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking with reflective summaries and validation of discouragement. Could deepen empathic work with more complex, affect‑laden reflections to capture emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors and translated goals into a concrete, measurable 2‑week plan with contingency options and a scheduled follow‑up. Kept the session goal‑directed and behaviorally specific.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort, solicited preferences, and co‑constructed the plan with the patient. Used inclusive language and invited ongoing input and troubleshooting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported client choice by offering options, asking permission, and encouraging the patient to tweak wording or plans. Reinforced the patient's ownership of decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out some motivations and used the patient's language (e.g., back pain relief, pants fitting) to ground goals. Could further evoke intrinsic motives and values and use scaling questions to strengthen importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision‑making and active power‑sharing by asking which goals felt most important and letting the patient choose specifics. The patient's ideas shaped the plan and follow‑up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk by asking about priorities, offering a small goal, and scheduling accountability. Missed opportunities to amplify the patient's own change language and elicit stronger commitment/confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and normalized ups and downs while steering toward change with alternatives to all‑or‑nothing thinking. Could increase use of double‑sided reflections to validate ambivalence and pivot to the patient's reasons for change.
