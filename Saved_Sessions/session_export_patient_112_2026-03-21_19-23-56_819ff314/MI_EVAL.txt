# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing ambivalence and affirming efforts. Language is warm, reframes slips as learning, and avoids shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and timely reflections that validate core emotions (worry, ambivalence). Reflections are often brief and the clinician sometimes moves quickly to problem‑solving, missing opportunities for deeper exploration of identity‑laden sustain talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation with concrete, measurable tiny‑step goals and a scheduled 3‑day check‑in. Balances goal direction with honoring the patient's pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acts as an engaged partner by co‑creating the one‑week plan, tailoring strategies to cost and context, and inviting the patient's choices. Interaction reads as teamwork rather than unilateral instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and emphasizing that slips are normal and reversible. Encourages patient ownership through small, patient‑selected goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's own motivations and commitments and builds on expressed change talk. At times leans toward information‑giving rather than using more evocative open questions and reflective summaries to draw out deeper personal motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asks permission, offers options, and repeatedly checks which approach feels doable to the patient. Occasionally adopts a more expert/informative tone (detailed medication info) that slightly reduces space for the patient's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, suggests tiny goals, and bolsters self‑efficacy and commitment. Sometimes shifts quickly into advice and technical details instead of using reflections and amplifications to deepen the patient's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges ambivalence and offers countering strategies but relies more on information/advice than MI techniques specifically aimed at softening sustain talk. Misses opportunities for double‑sided reflections or reflective pivots when identity‑based sustain talk appears.
