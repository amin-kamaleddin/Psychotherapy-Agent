# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with respectful, nonjudgmental language and explicit affirmation of effort (e.g., praising journaling and the plan). Tone remained supportive throughout with no blaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate empathic listening and perspective-taking, capturing Samuel's ambivalence and concerns through reflective summaries. Used targeted reflections and clarifying questions that guided the conversation without dismissing doubts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking reduction with each question and intervention tied to the target behavior, moving logically from assessment to options to a specific week-long experiment. Ended with a measurable goal and contingency plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co-constructed: the clinician elicited Samuel's ideas, framed information as choices, and built the plan together (journaling → gum strategy → if‑then rule → reminder). The exchange felt reciprocal rather than didactic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored patient autonomy by offering options, checking acceptability, and inviting choice of strategies and backups. Used confidence scaling and offered tiny backups to reinforce patient control over the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation and solutions out of Samuel rather than prescribing them, eliciting change-talk and using it to generate a patient-led, concrete experiment. Asked what would raise confidence and elicited practical nudges from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforced shared decision-making by repeatedly asking preferences, negotiating plans (gum first, patch backup), and offering help to brainstorm or troubleshoot as needed. Language emphasized Samuel's expertise about his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently elicited and amplified change talk, turning desire and reasons into specific strategies and commitments (e.g., measurable two-cig per day goal, tracking and rewards). Prompted concrete planning and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and normalized difficulty, avoiding reinforcement, but relied mainly on neutral reflections and problem-solving. Missed opportunities for more strategic double-sided reflections and selective amplification to more actively shift ambivalence toward change.
