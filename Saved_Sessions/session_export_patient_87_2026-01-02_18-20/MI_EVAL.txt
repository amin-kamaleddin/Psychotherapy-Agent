# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive (multiple affirmations: “that’s a great start,” “I’m proud of you”). Validation present but somewhat formulaic; less deep normalization of relapse or emotional struggle.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3
**Comments:** Accurate content reflections and summaries present, but limited affect‑focused or complex reflections. Clinician moves quickly to problem‑solving rather than exploring discouragement/ambivalence.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on specific target behaviors (short walks, snack plan). Repeatedly narrows and reinforces the plan (choosing nights, backup, scripts); conversation stays on task throughout.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Invited patient choice (which option, which nights, backup) and checked feasibility. However, many strategies were clinician‑provided with limited co‑generation of alternatives or patient‑led phrasing.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Used scaling, asked for decisions, and solicited tweaks to raise confidence. Balanced by several directive suggestions and prescriptive scripts that reduce pure autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3
**Comments:** Elicited choices and confidence ratings, but clinician frequently supplied solutions rather than drawing out patient’s own motivations, ideas, or language.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative tone, offered options and invited selection. Partnership weakened somewhat by clinician-led solutions and scripted advice instead of joint problem‑solving.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3
**Comments:** Patient expressed some change language (willingness, “I’ll give it a shot,” confidence = 6). Clinician did not consistently reflect/amplify or build on these statements to deepen commitment.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (worry about food at home) was noted repeatedly by patient but was mainly met with strategies rather than double‑sided or reflective moves to acknowledge and shift ambivalence.
