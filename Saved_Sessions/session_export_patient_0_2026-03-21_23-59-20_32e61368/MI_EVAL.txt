# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys consistent unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence with validating language and no blame. Multiple explicit statements of nonjudgment reinforced rapport throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing both content and affect. Frequently mirrors the client's language (e.g., “gross, stressful mess”), which deepens rapport and understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on reducing infection risk and consistently steers toward concrete harm-reduction steps. Questions and summaries keep momentum toward specific, achievable actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is strongly collaborative with ideas originating from the client and the clinician building on them to form a joint plan. The clinician checks back and invites input at each step, demonstrating shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms the client's right to choose and frames suggestions as options rather than directives. Repeatedly asks what feels doable and honors the client's preference for small steps, reinforcing agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws motivation from the client's own reasons and language using evocative questions and reflections. Amplifies intrinsic motivation by linking proposed actions to valued outcomes like fewer missed workdays and less stress.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a genuine partnership by inviting the client's expertise and co-creating the plan (keys, jacket backup, reminders). Power-sharing is evident in asking which step the client will commit to first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits, reflects, and amplifies change talk, translating tentative statements into concrete commitments and layered plans. Uses evocative questions to deepen and link change language to practical benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward harm-reduction options, but occasionally echoes repeated 'I'm not quitting' without using double-sided reflections. More strategic contrasts of sustain and change talk could further resolve ambivalence.
