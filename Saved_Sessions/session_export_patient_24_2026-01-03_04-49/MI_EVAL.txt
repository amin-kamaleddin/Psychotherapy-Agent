# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language (e.g., “that tension makes total sense and you’re not judged for it,” “we can explore this at your pace”). No shaming or labeling; clinician maintained warmth and explicit acceptance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective‑taking with repeated, accurate reflections of ambivalence (e.g., “proud of your marathon training … AND you also enjoy winding down…”). Reflections tracked emotional nuance and deepened patient exploration. Minor opportunity to vary reflection phrasing more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on alcohol use as it relates to training/sleep and moved logically toward a specific, measurable plan (6‑week experiment, substitutes, accountability). Clinician avoided topic drift and linked questions to behavior change outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed work as joint exploration and consistently solicited patient input (asked which part of plan felt easiest, what success would look like). Built plan from patient’s own strategies (no‑weeknight rule, training buddy, app tracking).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced patient choice and avoided coercion (“you’re not judged here,” “at your pace,” “try another small change at your own pace”). Offered options and invited the patient to choose what felt doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations, past successes, and concrete strategies (no‑weeknight drinking, substitutes, buddy check‑ins). Missed opportunities to deepen evocation (no use of scaling/confidence rulers or direct exploration of strength of desire/ability and costs of status quo).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated patient as expert on his life; repeatedly invited his perspective and co‑constructed the plan. Language fostered co‑creation (“we can build a simple plan together”) and patient contributions shaped the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple pieces of change talk (surprised by “18/week,” prior success with no‑weeknight drinking, willingness to try a 6‑week experiment). Could have amplified and summarized change talk more to elicit stronger commitment language or a concrete commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized ambivalence (“wanting both enjoyment and peak performance is normal”). Often reframed sustain arguments toward small experiments, but could have used more double‑sided reflections or strategic reframes to further shift sustain talk toward change.
