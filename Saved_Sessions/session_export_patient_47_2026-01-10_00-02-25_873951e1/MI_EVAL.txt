# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing slips and affirming efforts. This created a supportive frame with no shaming or blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated solid perspective-taking and reflective listening, accurately capturing ambivalence. However, emotional reflections were not deepened consistently, with many responses shifting quickly to information or planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with measurable goals and a structured mapping/review plan. Every part of the session tied back to reducing intake and safety.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was treated as a joint enterprise: the clinician solicited preferences, co-designed the log and goals, and adjusted recommendations when the patient felt overwhelmed. This demonstrated genuine responsiveness and power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice by offering options, asking preferences, and framing suggestions as invitations rather than mandates. Patient control was reinforced throughout, encouraging small, self-directed steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited change language and concrete commitments (log, alcohol-free day, pub strategies) and reinforced them. Evocation could be stronger by systematically drawing out values, using scaling questions, and amplifying change talk toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fostered a collaborative stance and frequently solicited the patient's input, co-creating plans and asking permission before advancing. At times the clinician shifted into an expert/teacher role by delivering lengthy structured plans without first eliciting the patient's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited and reinforced change talk, co-creating specific, doable steps and eliciting commitments. Opportunities were missed to pause and amplify the patient's own reasons and confidence before supplying multiple strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing, normalized minimizing comments, and redirected toward constructive steps and safety. More use of double-sided reflections and targeted reflective strategies would better soften sustain talk without provoking defensiveness.
