# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Kevin's experience with nonjudgmental, validating language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections of embarrassment and ambivalence, but missed opportunities to probe deeper affect or the emotional meaning of drinking as stress coping.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol-related risk reduction and consistently tied suggestions and rehearsal back to the concrete behavioral goal of avoiding another injury.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-created the plan with Kevin, used role-play and invited feedback and preferences, showing mutual engagement and shared work on strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, normalized lack of promises, and framed strategies as options, affirming Kevin's right to decide what he will try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical ideas and plans from Kevin but relied heavily on offering strategies rather than using more open, evocative questions to draw out intrinsic motives and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered strong partnership by inviting Kevin to choose, practice, and shape scripts and plans, treating him as the expert on his life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced several concrete change statements and used role-play to amplify them, but could have probed importance, confidence, and consequences to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and pragmatically softened sustain talk with validation and harm-reduction, though more strategic tools (e.g., double-sided reflections, eliciting costs) would strengthen this further.
