# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows respect and nonjudgmental support (multiple “I hear you—” reflections, affirmation of resourcefulness and parenting choices). Some affirmations are formulaic; missed opportunities for deeper, individualized normalization of emotional strain.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and useful reflective listening (paraphrases of barriers, hopes). Patient reports feeling understood. Reflections are sometimes repetitive and could probe deeper emotional nuance (exhaustion, anxiety).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, clear focus on a concrete target (securing childcare). Clinician narrows agenda, offers specific options, and moves to actionable steps (scripts, trial day, role‑play). Maintains momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient treated as an equal partner—preferences solicited, choices honored, and role‑play led by patient’s selections. Interaction is co‑created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors patient choice and control; offers help rather than directives and invites patient to choose/modify scripts and actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits patient’s own ideas/resources around childcare (neighbor trade, trial details). Less systematic in evoking deeper motivations or confidence (e.g., long‑term goals, smoking/drinking change).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear power‑sharing—patient directs agenda, clinician asks permission to help, and repeatedly checks which version to rehearse. Daniela’s expertise shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces concrete change talk about childcare planning (commitment to ask neighbor, offer details). Missed opportunities to amplify commitment/confidence language and to explore change talk about smoking/drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and does not argue with sustain talk (trust, work limits), normalizes ambivalence and redirects to solutions. Could use more double‑sided reflections and reframing into small‑step experiments to further reduce sustain talk.
