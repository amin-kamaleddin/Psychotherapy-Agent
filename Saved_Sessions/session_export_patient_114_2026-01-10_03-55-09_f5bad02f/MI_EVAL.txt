# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalizing Ryan's feelings and affirming his efforts. Tone and language remained warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections captured both content and affect, mirroring Ryan's ambivalence and embarrassment. This created a strong empathic connection that supported further exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on risky alcohol use and progressed toward specific behavioral goals (limits, sober buddy, refusal lines). Questions and interventions consistently advanced concrete planning without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as an equal, cooperative exchange with Ryan's ideas shaping the plan. The clinician invited choices, rehearsal, and integrated Ryan's responses into next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician supported Ryan's sense of choice by offering options, framing suggestions as small experiments, and avoiding pressuring language. Pace and consent for role‑play were respected, reinforcing ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Ryan through open questions, confidence scaling, and role‑play, eliciting practical reasons for change. However, the clinician relied more on pragmatic strategies than deeper exploration of intrinsic values and long‑term motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned the work as collaborative, privileging Ryan's expertise and preferences. Repeatedly checked which options felt doable and co‑created the plan with Ryan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced change talk using strategy selection and confidence ratings, and encouraged concrete steps like asking Alex. Missed opportunities included more frequent amplification of tentative problem statements and deeper DARN exploration to strengthen desire and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized concerns, and redirected toward alternatives and planning. More advanced softening (e.g., double‑sided reflections or eliciting costs of continued drinking) was underused.
