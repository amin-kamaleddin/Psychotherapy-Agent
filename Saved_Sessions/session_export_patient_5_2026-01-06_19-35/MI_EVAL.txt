# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, validating ambivalence and affirming Alessia’s prudence. No minimizing or criticism, with repeated supportive language that reinforced acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing both practical concerns and emotions. Reflections tracked priorities and preferences, validating and mirroring Alessia’s stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on safe, informed antidepressant use and monitoring with concrete options and a time-limited trial. Every clinician turn returned to elements directly tied to the behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative co‑creation of the plan, offering options and deferring to Alessia for wording, contact order, and final approval. The clinician drafted the plan but explicitly sought her review to ensure shared ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by presenting options and asking which she preferred, honoring her request to review the draft first. Language and offers reinforced Alessia’s control over decisions and sharing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Alessia’s motivations (safety, family protection) and translated those values into concrete steps like a monitoring plan and pharmacist–prescriber conversation. Questions drew out her reasons and guided actionable planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Alessia as an equal partner, soliciting her exact wording, contact preferences, and review process. Power‑sharing was explicit, with decisions shaped by her input throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and supported action‑oriented language and concrete commitments (pharmacist–prescriber conversation, written plan), but missed a brief opportunity to amplify and summarize her emerging commitment to strengthen explicit commitment language. Overall strong evocation of actionable steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed concerns into actionable options, normalizing hesitation without endorsing it. Could have used one or two double‑sided reflections to more actively pivot ambivalence toward change.
