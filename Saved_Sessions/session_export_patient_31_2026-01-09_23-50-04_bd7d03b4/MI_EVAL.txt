# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, explicitly validating the patient's choices and avoiding judgment. Repeatedly acknowledged the legitimacy of keeping an evening cigarette as a way to unwind.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely reflective listening and perspective-taking, naming emotions and priorities that clarified what matters to the patient. Summaries and reflections consistently attuned to David's experience and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking reduction and moved logically from exploration to a specific, time-limited experiment. Helped narrow talk into actionable steps tied to measurable outcomes (one-shift patch + gum, BP change).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner throughout, inviting preferences and co-designing the test (patch strength, gum, alarm). Patient ideas clearly shaped the plan, demonstrating shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized patient control and choice, framing the plan as a voluntary, low-pressure experiment and avoiding pressure to quit. Reinforced that the patient could stop the patch if it felt wrong.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal reasons and practical motivations (keeping calm while protecting BP) and asked about realistic options and signs of success. Could deepen evocation by probing broader values, longer-term reasons, and using scaling or confidence/commitment questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative stance, consistently inviting input and co-constructing the testing plan rather than prescribing solutions. Shared power by offering options and asking the patient to choose specifics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and strengths (willingness to try patch/gum, past cutbacks). Missed some opportunities to amplify and deepen change talk with scaling, more exploration of importance/confidence, and summaries to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized sustain talk while exploring harm-reduction options, which reduced resistance risk. Could more strategically use double-sided reflections or selective reframing to redirect sustain talk toward change.
