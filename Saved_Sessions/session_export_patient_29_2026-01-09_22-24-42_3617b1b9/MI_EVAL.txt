# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the patient’s experience and normalizing difficulty. Could add brief affirmations recognizing effort/strengths to further personalize praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking with timely reflections and targeted questions that show attention to detail. Reflections are often brief and informational rather than deep or complex emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation with relevant assessment, medication options, and practical coping strategies. Repeatedly steers the conversation toward actionable next steps tied to stated barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—asked permission to draft the message, iteratively incorporated patient edits, and offered follow-up supports. Interaction felt like joint problem-solving rather than a top-down directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options and asking readiness rather than pressuring decisions. Honored the patient’s wish to consult their prescriber and review messages before committing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some practical intentions and commitments but relied mainly on providing information and recommendations. Would benefit from more open questions and reflections to surface personal motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally partnered with the patient when drafting messages and planning next steps, honoring their input and preferences. Earlier portions were somewhat more prescriptive without first eliciting the patient’s priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change talk and asked planning questions, but did not consistently deepen or amplify the patient’s own reasons or confidence. More strategic evocation and selective reflections could strengthen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers and avoided escalating sustain talk, which helped neutralize resistance. Rarely used explicit MI techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing) to explore ambivalence and actively soften sustain talk.
