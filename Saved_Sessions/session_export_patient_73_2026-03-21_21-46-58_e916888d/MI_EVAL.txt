# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation of Linda's history and fears, avoiding blame or shaming. The steady respectful tone and normalization of ambivalence support a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate, affect‑sensitive reflections and timely summaries that show understanding of Linda's fear, frustration, and hopes. Responds to medication/mood concerns and invites further disclosure, demonstrating strong empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and moves the conversation toward actionable steps. Balances exploration with practical guidance, producing a concrete plan (GP discussion, scripts, weekly check‑ins).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine partnership by co‑creating decisions, checking preferences, and offering resources as options. The patient’s agenda drives the work and the clinician frequently asks permission and preference.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Linda's sense of choice and control by offering options and leaving key decisions (e.g., quit date) to her. Language is nonpressuring and affirms her right to set pace and components of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Linda's own reasons for change (health, money, past successes) using open questions and reflections. Could deepen evocation with more strategic evocative questions, scaling, and amplification to build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly collaborative and client‑guided, repeatedly asking which option feels most realistic and offering help only after querying preference. Decisions are co‑constructed and the clinician uses inclusive language throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk with open questions, reflections, and co‑created next steps, prompting reasons and plans for change. Missed opportunities include amplifying change statements, eliciting strength/importance/confidence ratings, and drawing explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and validates social/routine triggers without reinforcing them, then redirects toward planning. Could be more strategic in softening sustain talk using double‑sided reflections or selective reflections to reduce its strength before pivoting.
