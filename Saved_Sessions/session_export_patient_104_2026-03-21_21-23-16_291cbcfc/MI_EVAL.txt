# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Nadine’s distress without blaming or minimizing. The warm, nonjudgmental tone is sustained throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Nadine’s emotions and concerns (exhaustion, comparison to her mom) and are followed by validating statements and practical offers. Listening is demonstrated through specific, empathic summaries and responsive suggestions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician keeps a clear, consistent focus on Nadine’s exhaustion, infant sleep, and next steps, moving the session toward concrete actions. Checklists, scripts, and nap strategies maintain the session’s goal-directed flow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as joint problem-solving: the clinician solicits Nadine’s preferences and integrates them into the plan while responding to her requests for tools. Nadine’s input clearly shapes the session outcomes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are repeatedly offered and permission sought before giving tools, and the clinician explicitly supports whatever Nadine chooses. These behaviors consistently preserve Nadine’s agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits plans and preferences effectively (home visitor, tracking, nap routine) but often moves quickly to offering solutions. More questions to deepen Nadine’s reasons, values, and confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician largely fosters a collaborative stance and co-creates tools, asking Nadine to choose which supports to try first. Slightly clinician-driven moments occurred when ready-made scripts and routines were offered without first eliciting Nadine’s own wording or ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good elicitation and reinforcement of change-oriented language led to concrete choices (20-min naps, asking for help), but opportunities were missed to amplify motivations and elicit commitment or confidence. Asking about why changes feel doable or using a confidence scale would deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain-talk is acknowledged and reframed into pragmatic steps, redirecting toward manageable changes rather than reinforcing helplessness. Additional use of double-sided reflections or explicit summaries of sustain concerns before pivoting would further soften sustain talk.
