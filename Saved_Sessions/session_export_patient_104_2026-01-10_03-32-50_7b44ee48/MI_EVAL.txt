# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized Nadine’s experience, and asked safety questions in a caring way, conveying warm nonjudgmental support throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and tailored responses acknowledged exhaustion and context, but the clinician often moved quickly to practical instruction instead of exploring emotional impact more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained clear, consistent focus on infant sleep/feeding and maternal fatigue with concrete behavioral targets and safety checks, keeping exchanges tied to goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited Nadine’s input and adjusted recommendations based on her answers, but frequently led with technical solutions rather than co-constructing ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple options were offered and the clinician asked which felt doable, supporting choice, yet some prescriptive guidance limited opportunities for Nadine to adapt or reject plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were attempts to elicit feasibility and willingness, but the session relied more on expert instruction than on drawing out Nadine’s intrinsic motivations and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership was shown by asking preferences and feasibility, but the clinician often adopted an expert role with detailed protocols that reduced client-led problem solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions about feasibility prompted some willingness, yet the clinician tended to supply solutions rather than reflect, amplify, and deepen Nadine’s own change language and commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively validated and normalized doubts and avoided arguing, though using more double-sided reflections and highlighting small successes could further shift ambivalence toward change.
