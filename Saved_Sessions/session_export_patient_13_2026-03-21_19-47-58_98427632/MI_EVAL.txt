# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—validates fear, normalizes avoidance, and reassures about practical concerns without blaming. This stance is steady throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, sustained empathic listening with clarifying checks, reflective summaries, and a grounding exercise. Reflections capture the patient's overwhelm and hesitation well.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on accessing testing/appointments with interventions (scripts, role‑play, resource suggestions) tied to that goal. Information and steps are organized to move the patient toward care.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is highly collaborative—messages and plans are co‑created, language is adapted to the patient's comfort, and preferences are solicited repeatedly. Decisions are negotiated rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and pace by offering options, inviting permission, and avoiding pressure. Clinician affirms the patient’s control over timing and wording of outreach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation and practical concerns but leans toward offering solutions rather than systematically drawing out personal reasons/values. Could use more evocative open questions and importance/confidence scaling to deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Displays genuine partnership—asks permission before offering resources, offers choices, and repeatedly invites the patient to select the next step. Power is shared and the patient's expertise is respected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces small‑step change talk (e.g., choosing to look up clinics, trying chat) and amplifies commitments with concrete supports. Missed opportunities to elicit stronger commitment language and to probe importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk, offers low‑burden options, and uses empathetic reflections rather than confronting. Could strengthen with double‑sided reflections that explicitly name both sides of the ambivalence and gently tilt toward change.
