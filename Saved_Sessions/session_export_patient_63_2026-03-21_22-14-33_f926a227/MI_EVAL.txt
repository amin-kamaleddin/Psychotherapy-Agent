# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful with normalizing language and repeated affirmations; no shaming or blaming, which created strong rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated Jordan's ambivalence and cues, but could use deeper, more complex reflections of underlying emotions and less quick movement to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on reducing shoplifting with concrete tactics (carry less cash, refusal lines, exit plans) and role-plays tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as a cooperative effort, co-creating strategies, inviting input, and treating Jordan as an active teammate during planning and practice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission to practice, and affirming that Jordan could choose what to try without pressure to commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Jordan's ideas, preferences, and commitment language, but at times was more clinician-driven and would benefit from more probing of intrinsic motives, values, and long-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine partnership by asking which options felt doable, seeking permission for role-play, and letting Jordan’s preferences shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, confidence ratings, rehearsals, and translation of client statements into actionable steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward change through reflections and planning, but missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections and exploring the perceived benefits of the behavior before moving to solutions.
