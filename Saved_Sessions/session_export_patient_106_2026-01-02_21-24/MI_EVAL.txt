# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., “that sounds really hard and understandable,” normalizes slips). Affirmations are frequent but somewhat formulaic and not deeply individualized.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and empathic summaries acknowledging stress and guilt. Reflections are generally surface-level and clinician sometimes moves quickly to solutions rather than deeper emotional exploration.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on tobacco harm reduction with specific action items (20‑min timer, coat, text). Occasionally becomes prescriptive with repeated instructions, limiting elicitation of patient priorities.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Mostly joint problem‑solving (asks which option feels doable, co‑drafts texts). At times slips into expert/solution-giving mode, which lessens shared decision-making.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Emphasizes choice and normalizes slips (“If you slip, that’s okay”). Frequent directive phrasing (“Tonight: …”) slightly undermines autonomy framing.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Elicits doable actions and identifies supports (Lena, sister), but limited elicitation of internal motivations, values, importance/confidence scaling or deeper change talk.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Generally treats patient as collaborator (offers choices, asks preferences). Partnership diluted at times by clinician-led scripting and repeated directives.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Elicits some preparatory change talk and reinforces planning (agreement to try tonight). Missed opportunities to amplify desire/importance/confidence and convert planning into stronger commitment language.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5  
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk and offers harm‑reduction rather than confronting. Could use more strategic double‑sided reflections or reframing to reduce sustain talk’s pull.
