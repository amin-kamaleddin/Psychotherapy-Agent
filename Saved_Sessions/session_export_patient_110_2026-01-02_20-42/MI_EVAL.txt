# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., “It makes sense to worry about side effects,” offers of help, printable list). Normalized concerns and affirmed values (retirement with spouse). No evidence of minimization or blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ☑
**Comments:** Used accurate, brief empathic reflections and normalization; acknowledged values (“I’m glad you’re planning for retirement with your wife”). Missed opportunities for deeper, more evocative reflections of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Clear focus on cholesterol management, adherence, and monitoring throughout. Provided actionable guidance (start low, lipid panel 4–12 weeks, reminders, pillbox) and repeatedly steered to practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Invited patient input and co-created tools (questions for doctor, printable list, one-line scripts). Tailored recommendations to patient requests (“nothing fancy, just doable stuff”), demonstrating true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Respected choice and avoided coercion; offered options and framed suggestions as choices (e.g., start low, call before stopping). Reinforced patient agency with scripts and self-reminder phrasing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3 ☑
**Comments:** Some elicitation (patient-generated change talk, doctor-script, morning phrase) but clinician primarily provided information. Few open questions probing intrinsic motives, importance, or confidence — opportunity to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ☑
**Comments:** Generally collaborative (co-created lists, asked about printable list), but sometimes defaulted to giving medical recommendations without explicitly asking the patient to choose between options or prioritize steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3 ☑
**Comments:** Patient produced repeated change statements that clinician reinforced sporadically. Missed opportunities to amplify change talk, elicit commitment language, or use scaling questions to solidify intent and concrete commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ☑
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk; used normalization and factual reassurance to redirect worry into manageable actions (call first, monitoring plan). Could strengthen with more double‑sided reflections to explicitly voice ambivalence.
