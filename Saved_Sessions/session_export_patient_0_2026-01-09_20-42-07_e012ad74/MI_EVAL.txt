# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating concerns and honoring Adrian's social priorities throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that helped Adrian open up, though some reflections stayed at content level rather than probing deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on meth use and harms and moved efficiently from exploration to concrete risk-reduction steps and a specific follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt truly collaborative with the clinician inviting choices, soliciting Adrian's preferences, and co-constructing the plan and logistics together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission before advice, emphasizing no pressure, and accepting Adrian's limits while helping him craft doable steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked useful questions about past attempts and barriers that produced actionable motivation, but relied more on offering practical solutions than eliciting deeper personal reasons or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Adrian as the expert on his life with shared decision-making and frequent invitations to choose what felt doable, reinforcing a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk using a readiness ruler and offering options, but could more consistently amplify and reflect specific change-language to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and accepted sustain talk while pivoting to harm reduction, though more use of double-sided reflections or summaries could better resolve ambivalence.
