# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes imperfection, using warm, nonjudgmental reflections and avoiding labeling. The clinician emphasizes understanding and support while exploring options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing conflict and nuance in the client's experience. Could probe and reflect deeper emotions (e.g., anxiety, embarrassment, shame) and more explicitly validate the difficulty of the choice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, sustained focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from options to planning and rehearsal. Every question and reflection ties back to practical steps for staying safer without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint effort, co-constructing the plan and repeatedly soliciting the client's preferences. Offers to rehearse and refines language together, demonstrating equal partnership and mutual expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's sense of choice by offering options, asking which feels doable, and accepting imperfect effort. Avoids pressuring language and reinforces the client's control over decisions and plan specifics.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's own reasons and strategies by connecting behavior to values and asking evocative questions, producing concrete change talk. Could further probe internal motives, importance/confidence, and strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative atmosphere and invites client input on feasibility and phrasing, communicating shared problem-solving. At times the clinician leads content with specific scripts rather than inviting the client to generate more material first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces desire/ability/commitment statements and asks for concrete specifics about execution. Would benefit from amplifying and consolidating change talk into stronger commitment language before moving fully into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids challenging or escalating sustain talk but uses limited active strategies to soften it, missing double-sided reflections or reframing of social rewards. The clinician often moves into planning without explicitly acknowledging or reframing the client's justifications for drinking.
