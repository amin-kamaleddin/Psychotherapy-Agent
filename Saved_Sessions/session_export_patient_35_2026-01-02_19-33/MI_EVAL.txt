**Comments:** Brief empathic reflections present but shallow. Missed opportunities to reflect complex emotions/ambivalence (e.g., guilt vs need to cope) expressed by the patient.

**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus on exposure reduction and stress management. Direction was predominantly clinician-driven with substantial information-giving rather than jointly negotiated goals.

### Collaboration

**Comments:** Some collaborative invitations early (“Any preference what to focus on first?”) but session shifted to a prescriptive, didactic approach (lengthy checklists, scripted instructions).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1 [ ] 2 [ ] 3 [ ] 4 [x] 5 [ ]
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed patient choice (“It’s okay to postpone quitting,” “When you’re ready…”). Still leaned toward encouraging specific action steps in a directive manner, limiting full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1 [ ] 2 [x] 3 [ ] 4 [ ] 5 [ ]
**Comments:** Minimal evocation of the patient’s own motivations. Missed clear opportunities to explore the patient’s “I’ve thought about quitting” statement and to elicit personal reasons/values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1 [ ] 2 [ ] 3 [x] 4 [ ] 5 [ ]
**Comments:** Early partnership language present, but clinician largely adopted an expert/teacher role. Limited co-construction of plans or drawing on patient’s prior experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1 [ ] 2 [x] 3 [ ] 4 [ ] 5 [ ]
**Comments:** Change talk was sparse and not amplified or explored (no DARN-C elicitation). The clinician did not leverage the patient’s brief change statements to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Sustain-talk acknowledged superficially (“It’s good you’re careful”), but clinician did not use double-sided reflections, reframing, or other strategies to soften sustain talk and draw out ambivalence.
