# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validated Lukas’s concerns and praised concrete progress without shaming. The clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening and accurate reflections of ambivalence with good curiosity about Lukas’s world. Missed some opportunities for deeper affective reflection (shame, guardedness) at moments of nervousness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on meth use and its concrete consequences, steering toward actionable steps like tracking, automatic transfers, repayment and role-plays. Maintained consistent change-oriented guidance tied to Lukas’s priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked jointly to develop plans and practiced skills together, inviting Lukas to choose role-plays and strategies. The clinician adapted suggestions based on Lukas’s preferences, reflecting strong teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices, asked permission, and honored Lukas’s decisions (e.g., replacing locked account with automatic transfers). Framed suggestions as options and reinforced client control without pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Lukas’s reasons for change and used role-play to have him generate his own language. Could increase systematic elicitation (importance/confidence rulers, amplifying client-generated arguments) to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative in many moments, inviting Lukas to pick strategies and co-construct wording, but at times moved toward offering ready-made scripts and directives. More explicit negotiation before offering solutions would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple change statements and encouraged concrete steps (tracking, repayment, scripts), helping Lukas articulate reasons to change. Could do more consistent amplification and follow-up on Lukas’s own change language before offering solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and validated reasons for hesitancy, redirecting toward alternatives and role-plays. Rarely used double-sided reflections to explicitly highlight discrepancy between sustain and change talk, which would further soften sustain talk.
