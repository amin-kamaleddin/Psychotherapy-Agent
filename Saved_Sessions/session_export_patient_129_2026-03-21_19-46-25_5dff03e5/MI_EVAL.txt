# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating throughout the session, normalizing Tyler’s feelings and avoiding shaming; repeatedly affirms and accepts his stance without moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening with accurate, tone-sensitive reflections, but reflections are often brief and cognitive rather than exploring deeper emotions like shame, fear, or belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on substance-use safety and the immediate family problem, maintaining actionable targets (check-ins, 'out of the house', emergency plan) and resisting tangents.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions as a clear joint enterprise: co-creates the agreement, negotiates suggestions, and integrates Tyler’s language and preferences into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly affirms Tyler’s autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and framing suggestions as agreements rather than mandates, empowering his control over change pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses open questions and elicits practical behavior change, but makes limited effort to draw out intrinsic reasons, use scaling, or build discrepancy to amplify internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the work as collaborative, solicits Tyler’s input, offers role-plays and options, and treats him as an equal partner in developing concrete steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates openings for change talk and secures concrete safety steps, but misses opportunities to evoke and amplify desire, ability, reasons, or confidence using scaling or targeted queries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids confrontation and redirects sustain talk toward harm-reduction and family negotiation; could strengthen use of double-sided or complex reflections to more explicitly surface ambivalence.
