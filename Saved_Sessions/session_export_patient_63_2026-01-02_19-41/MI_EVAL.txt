# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
- [x] Clinician used nonjudgmental language (e.g., “This is a safe, confidential space”).
- [x] Clinician acknowledged the patient’s feelings and situation (guilt, peer pressure).
- [ ] Strong, individualized affirmations and explicit normalization of ambivalence were limited.

#### Score (1–5): 4
**Comments:** Clear respectful tone and normalizing language; acknowledged guilt and pressure. Missed opportunities for more specific affirmations of strengths and deeper normalization of ambivalence (e.g., linking arrest/court attendance to effort).

---

### Empathy
- [x] Reflections matched affect and content (e.g., “I hear guilt and pressure from your friends”).
- [x] Asked concrete questions that showed attentive listening (triggers, before/after feelings).
- [ ] Few complex reflections probing deeper emotional nuance (shame/fear).

#### Score (1–5): 4
**Comments:** Solid empathic listening and accurate summarizing; good attention to details. Could deepen empathy with more complex or amplified reflections to surface underlying values/fears.

---

### Direction
- [x] Maintained clear focus on target behavior (shoplifting) throughout the encounter.
- [x] Emphasized safety and practical risk management (legal risk, call 911 if needed).
- [x] Used concrete action planning and confidence scaling.

#### Score (1–5): 5
**Comments:** Strong, goal-directed approach—consistent focus on reducing shoplifting with practical, timed steps and safety instructions. Well executed.

---

### Collaboration
- [x] Repeatedly invited patient input and choice (“Which sounds more doable now?”).
- [x] Co-constructed a concrete plan (leave cards, rehearsed line, urge skill).
- [ ] At times moved quickly to offer many specific instructions, which bordered on prescriptive.

#### Score (1–5): 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance with options and patient choice. Could increase partnership by slowing before offering multiple clinician-generated solutions and eliciting more of the patient’s own ideas.

---

### Autonomy Support
- [x] Asked willingness and used confidence (1–10) scaling to support choice.
- [x] Offered multiple alternatives and emphasized selection of doable options.
- [ ] Occasional language nudged toward commitment (light pressure).

#### Score (1–5): 4
**Comments:** Generally respected autonomy and provided options. Strengthen by framing steps explicitly as experiments and validating the right to decline without judgment.

---

### Evocation
- [x] Elicited triggers, urges, and immediate feelings (before/after).
- [x] Used scaling and asked which option felt more doable.
- [ ] Limited elicitation of deeper motives, values, past successes, or reasons for change.

#### Score (1–5): 3
**Comments:** Elicited important behavioral detail but relied more on clinician-generated solutions than on drawing out the patient’s own motives or values. More evocative open questions would increase change talk.

---

### Partnership
- [x] Treated patient respectfully as a collaborator; invited choice and input.
- [x] Co-developed practical supports (friend check-in, leaving cards).
- [ ] Occasionally reverted to an expert/listing role when offering many resources.

#### Score (1–5): 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative tone overall; could deepen partnership by explicitly negotiating agenda and inviting the patient to prioritize topics or options.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
- [x] Patient expressed clear change statements (e.g., “I have to stop,” “I can’t keep doing it”).
- [x] Clinician followed with concrete step offers and asked for willingness/confidence.
- [ ] Missed opportunities to immediately amplify/evoke reasons, ability, or need (follow-up to “I have to stop”).

#### Score (1–5): 3
**Comments:** Presence of change talk was recognized but not consistently amplified. When scaling, clinician should follow with “Why X and what would move you to X+1?” or ask for specifics that build commitment.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
- [x] Acknowledged sustain talk elements (peer pressure, desire for items).
- [x] Asked about triggers rather than confronting the sustain talk directly.
- [ ] Limited use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or exploration of the behavior’s function.

#### Score (1–5): 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was noted and avoided being challenged, but strategies to soften it (double-sided reflections, exploring pros/cons/function) were underused. More targeted reframing could reduce resistance and support change.
