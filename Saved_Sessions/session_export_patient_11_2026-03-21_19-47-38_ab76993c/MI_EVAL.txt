# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive—validated and normalized distress, praised preparation, and reassured safety. Could add a few more personalized affirmations of the patient's specific strengths to deepen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and reflective statements that acknowledged feelings and practical concerns. Several turns moved quickly into planning rather than deeper, complex reflections or periodic summaries of emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity with a concrete, time-bound 2‑week plan and safety/progression guidance. Promptly adjusted the plan to accommodate constraints while keeping the goal actionable.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co-constructed—clinician solicited preferences and constraints, adapted exercises and reminders, and negotiated implementation details with the patient. Decisions clearly reflected mutual problem-solving rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, permission language, flexible pacing, and deferring to the patient's preferences. Reinforced the patient's ownership of decisions and adjustments throughout the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited readiness and some motivators but shifted into clinician-driven planning and advice rather than systematically drawing out the patient's own reasons, past successes, and solutions. Would benefit from more evocative open questions and reflections to amplify intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance—clinician invited input, co-wrote reminders, and adjusted the plan to the patient's needs, avoiding an authoritative tone. The patient’s ideas meaningfully shaped the implementation details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited and supported change talk and translated goals into a concrete plan while praising readiness. Missed some opportunities to deepen and amplify desire, reasons, ability, and commitment language (DARN-C) through targeted elicitation and reflection.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk, normalized skepticism, and offered low-pressure options that reduced defensiveness. Could further soften sustain talk by using double-sided reflections and reflective summaries that highlight discrepancy and ambivalence.
