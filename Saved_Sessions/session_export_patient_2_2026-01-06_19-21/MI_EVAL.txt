# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., normalizing slips, offering help not criticism). The clinician avoided blaming language and maintained a warm, respectful stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and validation of triggers and embarrassment reduced defensiveness. Could deepen empathic responses by exploring the emotional weight of concerns (e.g., fear for her son) more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking reduction with concrete, time-limited goals and implementation planning. Resisted drifting into unrelated topics and arranged a timely check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly co-created a plan by soliciting the patient's history, preferences, and constraints and inviting iterative troubleshooting. The clinician invited the patient's input before recommending changes, reinforcing a team approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized patient choice, offering options rather than directives and checking willingness to proceed. Framed suggestions as choices and reinforced the patient’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's motivations (worry about cancer, desire to be there for her son) and used them in planning. Could have more systematically explored personal values and the deeper meaning of motivations to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a genuine partnership stance, negotiating goals and asking the patient to choose what felt doable. Repeated invitations to select options and set a start day demonstrated shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with targeted questions, summaries, and concrete planning (e.g., 1‑week trial, lozenges). Could further amplify and reflect the patient’s own change statements to build stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and respected the patient's agenda by redirecting to smoking, but did not consistently use strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or summarizing ambivalence) to soften sustain talk. More explicit reflection of both sides of ambivalence could have reduced sustain language further.
