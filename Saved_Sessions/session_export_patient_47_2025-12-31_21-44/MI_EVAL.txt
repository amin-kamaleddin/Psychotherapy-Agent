# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and offers practical supports (text, tip sheet, printed copy). Validates choices and encourages self‑kindness. Slight overuse of evaluative praise (“I’m proud you’re willing to try”) which edges toward parent‑like tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reliable reflective listening and accurate summaries of ambivalence and plans. Uses complex reflections and a good closing summary. Missed opportunities to probe underlying feelings (minimization, embarrassment, emotional meaning of the pub routine).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use, readiness/confidence, safety, and a concrete one‑week plan throughout. Repeatedly returns to actionable next steps and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites Evelyn’s input and co‑constructs the plan (selection of night, drink, activity, support). Honors preferences for reminders/delivery of materials; strong shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames options as Evelyn’s choice, asks permission to explore steps, and follows patient preferences (text vs call, printed sheet). Avoids coercion and supports agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivations and ideas via open questions and scaling (importance/confidence), and gathers concrete coping strategies. Could probe deeper for personal values and stronger intrinsic reasons to raise motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters collaborative climate and power‑sharing in decisions. Occasional directive safety advice (contact GP if balance worsens) is appropriate but slightly shifts toward expert voice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits change language (plans and small steps) and uses scaling, but does not consistently amplify or deepen change talk into stronger commitment. Predominant language remains “I’ll try” with low confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and uses reflective summaries acknowledging both sides, which prevents escalation. However, few targeted strategies (double‑sided reflections, reframing) were used to reduce minimization; sustain talk remains present.
