# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with validating, nonjudgmental language and normalization. Warm, respectful phrasing was used throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening captured both problem and values, including emotional content like anxiety about dating. Responses demonstrated clear understanding rather than assumptions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol (alcohol‑free weekdays) and moved the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps. Questions and interventions repeatedly brought the patient back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was strongly collaborative with frequent solicitation of the patient's preferences and co‑construction of plans. The clinician tailored suggestions to what the patient was comfortable with rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's choice by offering options, asking permission, and affirming her control over decisions. The clinician avoided coercive language and returned ownership to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations well (importance/confidence rulers, asking why) but at times moved quickly to offering solutions and scripts. More open questions and reflective amplification of intrinsic reasons would deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a partnership and deferred to the patient's expertise, using frequent invitations to choose among options. Power‑sharing was evident in rehearsals and decision points.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk using concrete questions, a confidence ruler, and reinforcement, producing specific change statements and commitments. The clinician reinforced and summarized the patient's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided amplifying it, but relied more on neutral reframing than strategic double‑sided reflections. Could more consistently use selective emphasis on change and double‑sided reflections to reduce sustain momentum.
