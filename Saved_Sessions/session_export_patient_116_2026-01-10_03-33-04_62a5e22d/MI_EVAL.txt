# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, accepting the patient's guarded humor and resistance. Offers practical options rather than criticizing, conveying clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening with some reflections and validation. Reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional exploration of feeling 'off' or the patient's guarded tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and safe modification/tapering throughout the session. Efficiently moves from assessment to concrete planning without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—repeatedly solicits the patient's preferences and adapts to his requests. Co-constructs tools (scripts, checklists, note) and shares expertise while deferring to the patient's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms choice and avoids coercive language, offering multiple alternatives and supporting small, patient-led steps. Accepts the patient's noncommitment without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of internal motivations or values; conversation focuses on pragmatic problem-solving. Missed opportunities to draw out reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership and shared decision-making—asks permission, offers options, and involves the patient in selecting phrasing and plans. Patient shapes content while clinician supplies requested supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively orients the patient toward concrete change-related behaviors using scripts and action steps. Could do more to elicit and amplify intrinsic motivations (desire, reasons, confidence) behind potential change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and successfully neutralizes sustain talk by shifting to planning and alternatives. Rarely uses strategic softening techniques (double-sided reflections or gentle emphasis on costs) to tip ambivalence toward change.
