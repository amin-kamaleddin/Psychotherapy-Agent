# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalizing language (e.g., praising courage, validating preference for control). No shaming or blaming; clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking, naming emotions and summarizing consequences clearly. Reflections captured both behavior and emotional impact, showing strong empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing weekday drinking and preventing hangovers that impair studying, repeatedly steering toward pragmatic strategies. No substantive drift from the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Created a genuinely collaborative interaction by brainstorming options together and inviting Camila to choose strategies to try. Frequent checks of preference and readiness made the session joint problem-solving rather than directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Camila's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and respecting her decision not to seek total abstinence. Framed suggestions as choices rather than mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited some internal motivation and personally meaningful steps (e.g., two-drink limit, texting Maria) but could use more open, evocative questions and scale elicitation to deepen and consolidate motivation. Missed opportunities to draw out values and elicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited the client's input and co-constructed a plan, offering choices about where to start and which strategies to use. Avoided an expert-only stance and honored Camila's expertise about her context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk and offered aligned harm-reduction options, affirming doable choices. Could have amplified importance, systematically elicited ability/confidence, and prompted a clearer commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and reframed goals toward control rather than abstinence while offering tailored suggestions. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections and strategic reframing of sustain language into reasons for change.
