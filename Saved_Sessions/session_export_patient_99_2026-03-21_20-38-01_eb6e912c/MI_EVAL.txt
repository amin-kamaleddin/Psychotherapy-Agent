# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating and normalizing Matteo’s concerns. No labeling, shaming, or minimization, and the clinician supported Matteo’s stated preference to cut back rather than quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections of context and goals. Reflections were sometimes surface-level and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions like embarrassment or identity tension.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on alcohol use and moved logically from assessment to goal-setting and concrete planning. Used targeted questions and set a clear follow-up to support behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-created the plan, drafted scripts together, and adjusted wording per Matteo’s input. The exchange showed genuine two-way problem solving rather than an expert-driven lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Matteo’s autonomy, offered choices, and asked permission before suggestions. Language and structure supported client control and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations, supports, past strategies, and used a confidence ruler to draw out commitment. Could probe deeper into values/discrepancy and amplify intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated Matteo as the expert on his life and shared decision-making power (e.g., choosing nights, wording, and scheduling check-in). The clinician facilitated rather than directed the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with specific goals, past successes, and a confidence ruler that increased commitment. Could have amplified and summarized Matteo’s change statements more to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to planning and coping strategies, offering alternatives to pressured situations. Missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge both the appeal of drinking and its consequences.
