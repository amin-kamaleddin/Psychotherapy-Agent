# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, validating the patient's concerns and time constraints. No labeling, confrontation, or pressure was present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking, picking up on tone, worries, and preferences and adapting interventions accordingly. Reflections were timely and guided subsequent offers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on inhaled steroid use and how to discuss it with the clinician, keeping interventions tied to practical behavioral outcomes. The session avoided topic drift and remained goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Solicited the patient's priorities repeatedly and co-constructed pragmatic tools like scripts and comebacks, framing the work as joint problem-solving. Frequent invitations to steer the agenda reinforced an equal‑partner stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, respected the patient's request for brevity, and framed information as optional, avoiding coercion. Responses reinforced the patient's control over decisions and honored stated preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some attempts to draw out what would help the patient decide but relied mainly on offering information and scripts. Missed opportunities to systematically elicit internal motivations, values, or specific reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited input and tailored responses to the patient's requests, conveying that her perspective mattered. However, the clinician often provided scripted language rather than first eliciting the patient's own wording or ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created some openings about what would help the patient decide but did not actively elicit or amplify the patient's own reasons for better asthma management. Interaction emphasized practical tools over systematically drawing out desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk without arguing and pivoted to information and alternatives, using reflective summaries and option statements. Could increase effectiveness with more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries of any pro-change remarks.
