# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and respect, using normalizing language and invitations that avoided judgment. The clinician accepted Marisol’s perspective even when it differed from the dentist’s concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, consistent reflective listening and perspective-taking, using Marisol’s own language to validate her experience. Reflections matched her responses and invited elaboration without dismissing concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on oral health risk reduction while exploring values and identity. The clinician repeatedly steered conversation toward practical steps and follow-up without overriding autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint enterprise, checking preferences and inviting Marisol to lead content and choices. The resulting plan (backing change, timing, follow-up) emerged from her own decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly honored Marisol’s choice by offering options, asking permission before advice, and avoiding coercive language. The clinician empowered her ownership of decisions and normalized waiting for problems to appear.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited personal reasons and contingencies for change, producing actionable motivations and a small behavior change. Could have probed deeper into intrinsic motives, used scaling, and amplified her emergent commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the session as a shared process, inviting preferences and checking whether to plan or rehearse. Tone and language emphasized joint problem-solving rather than an expert lecture.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk that led to a concrete action and timeline, including asking about when she’d act. Missed opportunities to amplify, summarize, and consolidate the commitment or ask confidence/importance ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and used reflective, nonjudgmental responses plus low-burden harm-reduction options. Could have used more explicit double-sided reflections and earlier, stronger redirection toward change language.
