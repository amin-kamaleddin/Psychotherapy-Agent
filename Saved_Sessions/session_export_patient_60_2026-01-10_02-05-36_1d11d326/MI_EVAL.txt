# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing Jason's concerns and offering practical help instead of blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening (clear summaries of frustration and priorities), but there were missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections of underlying emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on the disciplinary issue (underage drinking) and steered the session toward concrete behavioral goals and next steps like drafting a statement and role‑playing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance—therapist solicited Jason's preferences, integrated his wording into the statement, and co‑constructed the plan rather than directing it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Jason's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language, reinforcing his ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited pragmatic motivation (housing, avoiding a mark) but did not sufficiently draw out intrinsic reasons, values, or longer‑term goals to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine partnership by sharing power, inviting input on decisions, and adapting interventions to Jason's preferences throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and shaped change language (importance ruler, willingness to write a statement/community service) and rehearsed commitments, though could have amplified and reinforced change talk more strategically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and rolled with minimizations, but missed strategic opportunities (double‑sided reflections, sharper discrepancy work) to shift sustain talk toward change.
