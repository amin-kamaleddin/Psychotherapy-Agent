# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly communicates nonjudgment and positive regard (“No judgment here,” “I’m proud you’re trying this”), and uses affirmations that acknowledge effort and normal worry. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic at times and mixed with directive suggestions (plans/scripts), which prevented a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate paraphrases and perspective-taking captured ambivalence and motivations (“avoiding your family’s pattern,” “you feel both ready and unsure”), with validations of emotion (“Feeling nervous is understandable”). Reflections were mostly content-focused; deeper affect-level reflections (fear, loneliness, shame) were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on reducing drinking and protecting contraception. Repeated goal-focused questions and successive moves from elicitation to a concrete safety plan demonstrate strong directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites patient choice frequently (“Which would you like to try first?,” “Which would you like to send and when?”) and co-constructs plans. At times clinician provided full scripts/solutions before eliciting patient-generated wording, slightly reducing true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly asks patient to choose options and affirms her role in decisions (cue selection, which script to send). However, clinician often offered specific advice/scripts without explicit permission, modestly limiting pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited patient motivations and strengths early (importance/readiness, sober friends) and used them in planning. Could increase systematic elicitation of ambivalence and more invitations for patient to elaborate change talk to reach the highest level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative language and joint planning (backup plans, reminder wording). Partnership lessened when clinician supplied ready-made wording and plans rather than first prompting patient to produce her own language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (fear of family pattern, contraception motivation) and built plans from patient-generated reasons. Missed opportunities to deepen commitment (e.g., scaling confidence and asking what would move it one point).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician did not reinforce sustain talk and moved to planning, but did not explicitly explore or soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections or reframing. Sustain language was acknowledged implicitly but not actively diffused.
