# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental. Validates fatigue and skepticism repeatedly (e.g., “That sounds really frustrating,” “Expect doubt; that’s normal,” “Be kind to yourself if you miss one”). No labeling or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., “long days drain you,” “I hear your skepticism and your willingness”), but reflections are often brief/formulaic with missed opportunities for deeper, more complex empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on re-establishing exercise habit. Helped form a specific, measurable plan (8:00 10‑minute walks, alarm, shoes by door, spouse text, 2‑minute backup, one‑week check‑in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—offered options and invited choice (patient chose after‑dinner walk). At times became somewhat directive with specific prescriptions (alarm label, shoe placement), which shifts power toward clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and normalizes doubt; honors autonomy. Could strengthen by asking permission before advising and prompting patient to generate more of the specifics.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of barriers and components of past routine, but clinician quickly moved to solutions rather than deeply eliciting patient’s values, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions work as joint (“Let’s pick one tiny, doable first step,” offers check‑in). Partnership slightly reduced when clinician provided multiple concrete prescriptions instead of eliciting more patient-generated strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced limited change talk (patient’s statements about past exercising and agreement to trial), but missed opportunities to amplify reasons, importance, and confidence or to ask follow-up scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes and reframes sustain talk (acknowledges skepticism and fatigue, offers compassionate alternatives like 2‑minute stretch, advises gentleness if missed). Could further use double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
