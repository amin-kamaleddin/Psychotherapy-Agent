# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, validated small steps and offered options without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking at the content level, but missed deeper affective reflections of guardedness, skepticism, and hopes about family.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on a specific, time‑bound target behavior and consistently narrowed conversation toward a concrete first step and plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem‑solving, co‑constructed the script with the patient, and solicited feedback and choices throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options, normalized small steps, and explicitly left final decisions to the patient, supporting choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client‑generated solutions and practical steps but did not sufficiently draw out deeper motives, values, confidence, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the work as a partnership, invited Mateo to choose among options, and shared power when developing the script.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk around a concrete action and narrowed to an actionable plan, but could have probed confidence and strengthened commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and direct reinforcement of sustain talk but missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections and exploring the costs of staying the same to reduce ambivalence.
