# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming (accurate reflections, praise, normalization). Could add an explicit statement of unconditional acceptance (e.g., normalizing relapse) to strengthen maintenance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening, accurately naming competing values and concerns and noticing housing as the linchpin. Could occasionally deepen affective reflections to capture shame, grief, or fear more vividly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on housing, drinking, and treatment and moves logically from values to a realistic, time-limited plan. Agenda setting and progressive steps maintained momentum and produced concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly a joint enterprise: invites input at each step, offers options, and co-creates tools like scripts, rehearsals, and a shared folder. Facilitates teamwork without imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, asks permission before taking roles, and checks preferences when proposing scripts or mediation. Even directive suggestions are framed with consent, preserving client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Wiremu's core motivators and links them to concrete steps (housing plan, check-ins), using those motivations to shape strategy. Motivation is consistently drawn from the client rather than supplied by the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and invites Wiremu to tweak plans, but occasionally shifts to an expert/mediator role by offering detailed scripts and procedures. Could invite more client-generated phrasing before supplying wording or taking lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits, repeats, and amplifies change language and asks for specifics and confidence ratings, building toward concrete commitments. A direct commitment question (e.g., 'What will you do first?') could further strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects ambivalence toward change, using options that respect concerns about independence, but often accepts sustain talk rather than using double-sided reflections. More strategic reframing (explicitly reflecting both sides and emphasizing discrepancy) would further reduce sustain language.
