# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Elena’s experience (e.g., ‘doesn’t make you a bad parent’), affirming concrete caring actions. No evidence of judgment, labeling, or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening with accurate reflections capturing both practical concerns and emotions (exhaustion, guilt), and closely paraphrased specifics. These moves signaled understanding and invited further disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing child exposure and work-related smoking throughout the session. Questions and reflections consistently moved from values to concrete actions and coping strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner, listening, reflecting, and co-constructing a practical plan rather than directing. Invited Elena to prioritize strategies and decide how to implement them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by avoiding pressure, asking which options felt doable, and normalizing uncertainty. Repeatedly emphasized Elena’s ownership of the decision and goal-setting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out Elena’s motivations and self-generated strategies, eliciting multiple intrinsic reasons for change. Could further deepen evocation by amplifying motives, linking values to concrete goals, and eliciting commitment or confidence ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited Elena’s input, summarized her ideas, and framed decisions as her choice, demonstrating genuine power-sharing. The clinician prioritized Elena’s expertise in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced specific change talk and alternatives (gum, walks, alarms) and asked about measurable goals. Could strengthen by selectively amplifying change statements, asking confidence/readiness, and converting reasons into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or reinforcing sustain talk, acknowledged minimizations, and quickly redirected to feasible small changes. Missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections and strategic reframing to convert sustain talk into transitional change talk.
