# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated consistent unconditional positive regard and normalized harm‑reduction goals, avoiding blame or shame. Warm tone and validation were maintained across sensitive topics.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective‑taking and reflective listening, accurately summarizing ambivalence and supports. Could use more complex/emotional reflections to probe underlying feelings about retirement, shame, or fear after the fall.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol and moved the session from exploration to concrete planning. Used confidence ratings and contingency strategies to sustain forward movement toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint enterprise, repeatedly soliciting Margaret’s preferences and co‑constructing the plan. Tools and next steps were adapted to her needs with strong client input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Margaret’s right to choose, offered multiple options, and asked permission before drafting tools. Emphasized ownership of the plan and avoided pressuring for abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations, supports, and concrete change ideas using rulers and if‑then planning. Could deepen intrinsic motivation by linking changes to personal values and eliciting stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively shared power by asking which options felt most helpful and letting Margaret lead choices (days, scripts, tracker). The session content and next steps were clearly shaped by her contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced actionable change talk leading to a specific plan (Mon/Thu, one drink), and explored barriers and supports. To improve, more amplification and summarizing of her change statements could help convert ambivalence into firmer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and validated the social value of drinking while redirecting toward realistic harm‑reduction goals. More frequent use of double‑sided reflections could further soften sustain talk and tip ambivalence toward change.
