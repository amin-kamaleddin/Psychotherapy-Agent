# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, explicitly supportive opening that validated Dylan’s frustration and created a respectful tone. Overall accepting stance maintained throughout the problem-solving work.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed empathic reflection early (validating unfairness) but did not consistently deepen or explore Dylan’s emotional experience. The clinician moved quickly into advice and problem-solving rather than using open reflections to probe feelings and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on addressing the student conduct referral with a concrete stepwise plan. The direction was strong procedurally but less focused on behavioral targets related to the referral.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple collaborative moves—co-creating and polishing the statement, offering role-plays, and checking Dylan’s preferences. However, the clinician often assumed the lead in producing content and strategy, keeping the interaction somewhat clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, asked permission, and invited Dylan’s preferences which supported his agency. Could strengthen by more explicitly reinforcing Dylan’s ownership of decisions and consequences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation of Dylan’s internal motivations, values, or longer-term goals; interaction relied on clinician-provided solutions. Missed opportunities to draw out client-generated reasons for change or concern about consequences.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displayed moments of partnership by asking outcomes and offering options, but frequently adopted an expert/advisor role with detailed plans and scripted language. Greater elicitation of Dylan’s ideas before offering solutions would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicited brief, pragmatic change-related responses but did not amplify or explore Dylan’s own reasons, desires, or confidence about behavior change. The focus remained on contesting the referral rather than eliciting client-originated change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and avoided arguing, acknowledging Dylan’s minimization of responsibility. However, the clinician rarely used MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to soften sustain talk or highlight ambivalence.
