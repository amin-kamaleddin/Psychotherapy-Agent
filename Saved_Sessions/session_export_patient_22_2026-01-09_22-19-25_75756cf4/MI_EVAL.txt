# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (grounding exercise, validating the patient’s goal), normalizing limits and avoiding blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good empathic statements and some reflections, acknowledging skepticism and goals, but missed deeper affective exploration of social anxiety, the breakup, and freezing when teased.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing problematic alcohol use with concrete, actionable goals and strategies, safety guidance, and follow‑up planning rather than drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively engaged in shared problem solving, offered options and scripts, and repeatedly asked the patient to choose and tailor strategies, demonstrating a joint approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored the patient’s choices (harm‑reduction over abstinence), offered options rather than directives, and framed interventions as optional to reinforce autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient’s ideas, choices, and small experiments, but could have more deliberately drawn out internal motives, values, and stronger commitment/confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative, invited permission and input, and followed the patient’s preferences when selecting plans and role‑play, sharing power throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk around goals and limits and encouraged practice, but did not systematically amplify DARN statements or deepen commitment and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and met the patient where she was with a harm‑reduction plan, which softened sustain talk, though more strategic double‑sided reflections or cost/benefit exploration would strengthen shifts toward change.
