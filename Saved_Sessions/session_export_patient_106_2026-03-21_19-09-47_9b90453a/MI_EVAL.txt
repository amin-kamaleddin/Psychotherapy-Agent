# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician is openly supportive, normalizing the patient’s struggle and avoiding shame; could deepen acceptance with more individualized affirmations and explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and attention to specific worries, but the clinician often shifts quickly to problem-solving instead of using deeper reflective listening and emotional summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction and child protection with practical, stepwise harm‑reduction strategies that move the conversation logically from assessment to plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a team effort with adjustable plans and acceptance of patient preferences, though earlier turns were more prescriptive and power-sharing could begin sooner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Presents multiple options and nonpunitive language that support choice, but sometimes offers fairly strong directives (e.g., specific patch dose) without first eliciting the patient’s view.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open invitations and preference questions draw the patient into planning, but the clinician too often supplies solutions instead of systematically eliciting the patient’s own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments and invitations to tweak plans, yet much of the interaction remains clinician-led and could better co-create options with the patient from the outset.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some patient change language and invites planning, but misses opportunities to ask open questions that deepen desire, reasons, need, and commitment or to reflect and amplify expressed change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and offers harm‑reduction alternatives, but seldom uses MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explore ambivalence and gently steer away from sustain talk.
