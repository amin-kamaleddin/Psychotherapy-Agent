# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses unconditional positive regard and praise (e.g., praising quitting, normalizing uncertainty) with no shaming, supporting the patient's dignity throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses good empathic statements and some reflections that validate pride and worry, but could deepen affect-focused reflections and avoid shifting too quickly into solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol use in social settings and moves methodically from assessment to a concrete, actionable plan with rehearsals and specific steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-created—elicits patient input, co-writes texts, invites rehearsal, and leaves key decisions (strategies, buddy, exit time) to the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering options framed as choices, asking which feel doable, and avoiding pressuring language while reinforcing the patient's decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the patient's ideas and preferences (NA drink, buddy, rehearsed lines) but could use more open questions to elicit deeper personal motivations and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership by inviting the patient to choose specifics (buddy, exit time) and co-construct the plan, though offering many options at once occasionally feels more expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amplifies change talk by highlighting successes and moving toward specific intentions, but could further elicit importance/confidence language and ask the patient to summarize reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges doubts and reframes slips as information, but does not consistently use strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, selectively amplifying faint change talk) and often moves quickly to solutions.
