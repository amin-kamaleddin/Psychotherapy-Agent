# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed Dylan’s priorities throughout, communicating unconditional positive regard. A minor omission was the lack of explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and frequent content reflections kept Dylan engaged and clarified concerns about housing/grades. Reflections were mostly brief and content-level, with missed opportunities for deeper emotional validation or more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear focus on the conduct issue and consistently moved the session toward concrete action steps (drafts, witness templates, hearing prep). Responses stayed on topic and advanced problem resolution.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction felt cooperative and the clinician integrated Dylan’s requests into the plan while checking his preferences. However, the clinician often produced full drafts and scripts instead of co-creating language with Dylan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choices, asked which route Dylan preferred, and let him select tasks to pursue, reinforcing his control over decisions. Pressuring language was avoided and Dylan’s preferences were respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Dylan’s desired outcomes and briefly probed alternatives, which supported practical motivation to resolve the conduct matter. There was limited effort to draw out intrinsic motivation or explore values and ambivalence about drinking behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fostered a collaborative approach by asking what Dylan wanted and offering tailored help. At times the role shifted into an expert problem-solver, providing ready-made solutions rather than co-creating them with Dylan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some choice-focused questions invited preferences but the clinician did not intentionally elicit language favoring behavior change and quickly moved into problem-solving. Opportunities to use evocative open questions to generate desire or commitment to change were largely missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician validated Dylan’s minimizations and focused on contesting the allegation without strategically redirecting sustain talk. Few double-sided or discrepancy-reflecting responses were used to soften sustain talk or resolve ambivalence.
