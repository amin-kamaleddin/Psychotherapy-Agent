# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently displayed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validated the patient's concerns, and prioritized the patient's agenda throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and summaries that showed understanding of the patient's perspective and matched tone, though missed some deeper affective reflections naming core emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on reducing drinking and improving contraceptive reliability, offering concrete, behaviorally specific options and keeping the session goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the session as joint problem-solving by co-writing texts, inviting edits, practicing replies, and repeatedly checking the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy with choice offerings, permission language, and honoring the patient's pacing and decisions without pressuring a single option.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivations and linked them to concrete strategies, but could have used more open questions to deepen intrinsic reasons and elicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the patient as the primary decision-maker, invited input on wording and timing, and incorporated the patient's language into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, helped operationalize the patient's choice, and linked motives to actions, though could have amplified and summarized stronger commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not explicitly address or reframe sustain talk (e.g., ambivalence about contraceptive lapses) and moved quickly to planning, missing an opportunity for double-sided reflections.
