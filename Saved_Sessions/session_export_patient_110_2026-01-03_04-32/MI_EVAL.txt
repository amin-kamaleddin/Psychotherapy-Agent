# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence (e.g., “Whatever you decide, I accept where you are…”; “Wanting health and wanting to keep life comfortable can both be true”). Nonjudgmental tone maintained across turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking (reflections of values/ambivalence: retirement with wife, concerns about side effects). Summaries captured patient feelings and priorities without shifting to didactic lecture.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on target behaviors (start meds, low dose, follow‑up labs, pill with breakfast, short walks). Sequential, goal-oriented questions guided patient toward a concrete action (commitment to call doctor this week).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated patient as equal partner—offered options, asked preferences, co-constructed plan (script, role‑play, which support to focus on). Power was shared and patient choices directed next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirmed patient choice and avoided coercive language (“I’m here to support you,” “I accept where you are”). Elicited patient priorities (side‑effects vs. risk estimate) and respected his ordering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew out the patient’s own motivations and strategies (retirement goal, practical supports). Used open questions to elicit change talk and concrete plans rather than supplying reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed exchange as joint problem-solving (offer to write script, role‑play, ask what to rehearse). Patient expertise about what helps him was solicited and integrated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited substantial change talk (patient agreed to call, link to retirement) and secured behavioral commitment. Missed some opportunities to amplify and deepen change talk (could have used follow-up elaboration, confidence/importance scaling, or “look‑forward” prompts).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk without reinforcing it (double‑sided reflections, normalizing ambivalence). Quickly moved to problem‑solve and establish safety nets (low start, nurse line, follow‑up).
