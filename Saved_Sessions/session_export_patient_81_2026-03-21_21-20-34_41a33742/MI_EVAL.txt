# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through validating language and normalizing statements, acknowledging pressures without blame. Warmth and appreciation were present across the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating ambivalence and responsibilities. Missed opportunities to probe deeper into the emotional meaning of the scene/identity for the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on the urgent, actionable target (rent and stabilizing finances) and repeatedly returned the conversation to concrete steps. Progressed logically from priorities to specific plans and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a joint partner and rehearsal coach, soliciting preferences, adapting language, and inviting Lukas to choose wording. Positioned themselves as a collaborator rather than an authority throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered multiple options and asked permission before interventions, reinforcing choice and ownership. Frequent ready-made scripts slightly limited space for the client to generate his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language about rent and responsibility but relied heavily on clinician-supplied ideas and scripts. Did not consistently draw out broader intrinsic motivations, values, or connections to identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited input and tailored plans with the client, sharing power in decision-making. Providing numerous concrete options and scripted language introduced some expert-driven content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked useful questions that produced change talk and a small-step plan, but frequently shifted into offering solutions instead of reflecting and amplifying the client's own motivations. Missed chances to deepen desire, reasons, and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk about identity and belonging but did not use strategic double-sided reflections or reframing to soften it. The pragmatic pivot to finances left sustain concerns insufficiently explored.
