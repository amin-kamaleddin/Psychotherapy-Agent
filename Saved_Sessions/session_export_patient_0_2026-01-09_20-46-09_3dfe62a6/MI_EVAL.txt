# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive language, explicitly respected privacy and normalized help-seeking. No shaming or moralizing; responses were warm and practical.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with clear summaries and validations of ambivalence. Could use more emotional reflections to deepen empathy around shame or worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on meth use and infection risk and consistently steered toward practical risk-reduction steps. Repeatedly asked for concrete next actions to build momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint problem-solving with offers to help, responsiveness to preferences, and co-constructed next steps. Respected Adrian's priorities and privacy throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice, offered options rather than directives, and accepted declines without pressure. Reinforced ownership by asking which options felt most doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Adrian's motivations and linked goals (keep job, avoid hospital) to behavior with open invitations. Could deepen elicitation of intrinsic motives and use scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with frequent invitations and offers to assist, but at times shifted into an informational/expert stance listing resources. Could elicit Adrian’s ideas before offering options to maximize partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and helped consolidate specific, practical steps (e.g., not sharing straws, wound care). Could more systematically deepen importance/confidence and summarize to build a commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated minimizations while steering toward harm-reduction, which softened sustain talk. Could use double-sided or amplified reflections to more artfully acknowledge sustain talk and nudge toward change.
