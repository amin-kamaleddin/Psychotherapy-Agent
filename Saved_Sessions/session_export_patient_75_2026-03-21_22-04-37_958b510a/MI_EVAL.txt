# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating Linda’s concerns and asking permission before offering information. These behaviors created a warm, respectful atmosphere appropriate for a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries accurately captured Linda’s experience and ambivalence, matching her affect and deepening rapport. Timely, specific reflections strengthened the therapeutic bond throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and inhaler adherence, with every question and intervention tied to that target. The clinician guided the visit toward a feasible, measurable plan and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a true partnership: the clinician invited Linda’s input, co‑created scripts and tools, and adapted plans to her preferences. Language and offers of options kept decision‑making shared rather than clinician‑driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported Linda’s sense of choice by offering options, asking permission before sharing information, and framing decisions as hers to make. This reinforced ownership and respected her autonomy throughout the visit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good use of open questions and scaling to elicit motivation and concrete change ideas, but the clinician relied somewhat on information and tools rather than consistently drawing out values, past successes, and DARN language. Deeper elicitation linking personal values to change would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively invited Linda into role‑play, asked for her preferences, and incorporated her choices into clinical tools, demonstrating shared power and co‑construction. This created a strong collaborative dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective at eliciting change talk through scaling, a specific small step, and crafting practical supports (scripts, tracker), yet missed opportunities to selectively amplify and chain client change statements into stronger commitment language. More reflections that elaborate benefits and link values→steps would increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and provided succinct, evidence‑based corrective information, helping to counter concerns about steroids. However, greater use of MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, eliciting Linda’s own reasons for change before giving information) would more effectively soften sustain talk.
