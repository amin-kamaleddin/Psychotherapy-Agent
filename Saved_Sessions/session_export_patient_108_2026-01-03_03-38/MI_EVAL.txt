# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently expressed respect and nonjudgmental support, normalized ambivalence and validated the patient’s conflict (e.g., “That tug-of-war… it’s okay to feel both”). No shaming or labeling; acceptance sustained throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, nuanced reflections of feelings and internal conflict (e.g., “You want your flat back…and you worry about hurting someone…”). Reflections deepened content and allowed elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior (setting a move-out date/boundary). Repeatedly prompted for one small practical step and narrowed options toward a concrete plan (script, date, printouts, alarm, breathing pause).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan with the patient, solicited preferences (“Which of those feels most doable for you?”), and built on patient-generated ideas (script, rehearsing, printouts).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforced patient choice and control (asked what mattered most, which step felt doable). Avoided pressuring language and emphasized values-driven decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew out the patient’s own motivations and past successes (asked what worked before, which strengths to draw on). Elicited and amplified change talk rather than supplying reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance; clinician language reinforced shared decision-making and power‑sharing. Patient’s contributions shaped the concrete plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly elicited, amplified, and reinforced change talk (asked for exact wording, asked for one small step, summarized the plan), increasing specificity and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively acknowledged and normalized sustain talk (guilt, attachment) and used some double‑sided reflections, but missed a few opportunities to more actively reframe sustain statements into change language and to elicit scaled confidence/commitment.
