# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validation, normalization, reassurance, and honoring patient limits, creating a warm, accepting atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening, accurately acknowledging somatic anxiety, fear of embarrassment, and linking past success to bolster confidence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains tight focus on a single, concrete target behavior with clear implementation details (exact start time, tactics, and follow-up), keeping the session action-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint enterprise by co-creating the plan, negotiating reminder wording and timing, and frequently checking the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and ownership by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding coercive language while reinforcing the patient's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some internal motivation (relief, past success) but relies more on offering strategies than on deeper elicitation of values, confidence, and self-generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and invites input on key decisions, but at times shifts toward providing directives which reduces patient-driven control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk that produces concrete commitments, though there are missed opportunities to amplify desire, reasons, and confidence more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward action, but could more explicitly use double-sided reflections or contrast summaries to resolve ambivalence.
