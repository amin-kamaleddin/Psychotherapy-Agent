# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalizing cautiousness and accepting the patient's limits; warm acceptance supported rapport throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking validated the patient's stance and obstacles, but reflections stayed at content level—deeper emotional/values reflections would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol as a modifiable factor for blood pressure, offering concrete small steps, a 7-day tracker, and a planned follow-up aligned with the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed with multiple invitations for preference, offers of help without taking over, and patient choices shaping the plan and supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by asking permission, offering options rather than prescriptions, and repeatedly acknowledging the patient's right to choose the magnitude and format of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's view on the alcohol–BP link, confidence, and doable options which produced change talk, but could have drawn out personal values or past successes to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership evidenced by joint decision-making, frequent invitations for input, and co-creation of concrete plans and reminders with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and shaped change talk (tracking + one alcohol-free night) and asked about confidence, though the clinician could have amplified and reflected change language more to elicit stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used permission and small-step framing to prevent reinforcing sustain talk; using more double-sided reflections would further acknowledge ambivalence while highlighting discrepancy.
