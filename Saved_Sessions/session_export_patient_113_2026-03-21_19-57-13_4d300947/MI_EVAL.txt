# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, with validations and normalizing language throughout. Affirmed Rowan's autonomy and avoided shaming despite repeated refusals.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid empathic listening and concise reflections (e.g., validating annoyance and pragmatism). Missed some opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of ambivalence or underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on charging the device and guided toward concrete, achievable actions. Resisted drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint problem-solving, soliciting preferences and implementing Rowan's choices. Co-created messages and a brief plan together rather than dictating steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Rowan's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and respecting his decisions. No coercive or pressuring language was used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Rowan's own reasons for charging by asking about time-sensitive tasks and used those reasons to build a plan. Could have probed more for intrinsic motives, importance, and confidence to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invitational, offering choices and co-creating fallback texts and scripts. At times the clinician moved into more directive, expert-driven instructions when problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted some concrete change talk (identifying the client call and deciding to plug in) but did not consistently amplify or explore importance/confidence. Few reflections or explicit requests for a commitment statement limited deeper motivational engagement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively de-emphasized sustain talk by validating annoyance and pivoting to low-effort solutions like a 5-minute plan. Relied more on pragmatic reframing than MI-specific strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to address ambivalence.
