# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with validating language and normalization, clearly conveying safety and respect throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., reflecting the 'ache' and checking safety), though reflections stayed relatively surface-level and could probe deeper emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on self-harm/safety with repeated concrete next steps (delay, texting supports, hiding razor) and urge ratings to keep the session purposefully directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as a shared effort by inviting input and jointly developing the safety plan, but at times led with many suggested options instead of eliciting the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, respecting the patient's preferences about the razor and contacts, and reinforcing that decisions and options (e.g., emergency services) remained hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some immediate change-oriented responses and commitments (10‑minute delay, texting supports) but did not sufficiently draw out internal motivations, values, or longer-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine partnership through co-creation of the safety plan and offers to stay/help draft texts, though the clinician sometimes offered many suggestions rather than pausing for the patient's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted small commitments and behavioral steps but missed opportunities to elicit and amplify intrinsic reasons for change or to reinforce emerging change language more strongly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain talk while offering alternatives, but did not use more strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided or shift reflections) to actively soften sustain talk.
