# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respectful, nonjudgmental regard with validating statements and affirmations throughout the encounter, including supportive responses after slips.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong initial empathic reflections helped move the patient from defensiveness to openness, but empathy was somewhat uneven as the clinician moved quickly into practical suggestions rather than deeper affect‑focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and developing coping alternatives, producing a specific, measurable plan with follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as a joint effort with incorporation of the patient's preferences and an offered check‑in, though providing many ready‑made tactics shifted some problem‑solving toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and reinforcing that slips are not failures, which bolstered the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced some of the patient's own motivations and commitment language, but at times moved quickly to offering solutions rather than using questions to deepen intrinsic reasons or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician largely shared power by asking for specifics and saving the patient's reminders, yet frequent prescriptive suggestions limited the extent to which the patient's ideas shaped the options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician created space for and reinforced concrete change talk and commitment, but could more consistently use open, evocative questions to draw out additional spontaneous reasons, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not challenged, with a pivot to small, manageable options and harm‑reduction; a missed opportunity was using double‑sided reflections or reframing to strategically reduce sustain talk.
