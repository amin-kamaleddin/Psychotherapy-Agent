### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates respect and positive regard (e.g., “taking the moment to consult the pharmacist shows this matters to you,” “That’s a sensible, manageable step”). Language normalizes constraints and uncertainty and offers warm affirmations throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening evident (e.g., “I hear you—you’re juggling work, marriage, and a busy life,” paraphrases of ambivalence). Reflections capture content and affect/constraints responsively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, sustained focus on a clear behavioral target (alarm, pillbox, “cup in hand”) and concrete plan. Deduction reflects a missed explicit response to an apparent initial safety/medication concern (citalopram vs phenelzine), which could be a drift from the patient’s immediate agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work clearly framed as shared—clinician solicits Ingrid’s input, co-creates specifics and backups (alarm details, key‑hook, pillbox attachment). Patient-generated ideas drive the plan; clinician facilitates.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors choice and pace (e.g., “it’s okay to see how it goes,” invitation to try for a week). No pressure or directive language; trial and decision left to patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Ingrid’s own reasons, strategies, and success criteria (4–5/5 mornings, two calmer mornings, not waking husband). Use of targeted questions draws out concrete, patient-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fosters power‑sharing and treats patient as routine expert; co-design is clear. Slight deduction because clinician frequently restates the plan in similar wording (clinician voice prominent) and did not explicitly negotiate or acknowledge deferring an apparent medication-safety agenda item.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk elicited, amplified, and reinforced—patient moves from tentative to concrete commitments (alarm, pillbox, attachment to keyring); clinician asks for measurable criteria and highlights small successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized effectively (e.g., “that uncertainty makes sense”). Mostly supportive reflections used rather than more strategic techniques (double‑sided or amplified reflections) that could more actively shift language toward change.
