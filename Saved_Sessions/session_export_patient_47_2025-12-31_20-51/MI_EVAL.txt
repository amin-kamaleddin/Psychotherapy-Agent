# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard; opening and recurrent normalization of slips (“see it as information, not failure”); multiple specific affirmations of patient effort; no shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent reflective listening and validation of ambivalence and the fall; reflections sometimes brief/surface-level; missed opportunities for deeper emotion-focused reflections (e.g., exploring how the fall and son's worry feel to the patient).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and harm reduction throughout; progressed to concrete, actionable goals and specific coping plans appropriate for a brief MI encounter.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited patient input and co-constructed the plan (patient chose one-drink strategy); at times offered many specific, prescriptive suggestions that shifted toward directive stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforced choice and ownership (asked what “feels doable,” elicited preferences); framed slips as information not failure; safety advice offered non-coercively.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited reasons, costs/benefits, and readiness (0–10 scale) and obtained change-oriented statements; could more often amplify and deepen change talk (values, past successes).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely a joint enterprise with patient selecting strategy and clinician refining supports; frequent concrete prescriptions reduced patient lead—would benefit from asking permission before offering multiple specific options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used elicitation techniques that produced concrete change talk and a specific plan (NA drink, script); missed opportunities to reflect/amplify change talk and to obtain a stronger verbal commitment or confidence rating at the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation and respected ambivalence, but did not consistently use strategic tools (double-sided reflections, evocative summaries) to shift sustain talk; some affirmations risked minimizing sustain arguments rather than reframing them toward change.
