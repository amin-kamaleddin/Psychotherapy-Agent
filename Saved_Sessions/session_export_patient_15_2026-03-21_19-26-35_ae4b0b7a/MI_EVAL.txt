# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language and affirmed choices, reducing shame; however, some acceptance felt procedural and lacked deeper, individualized affirmations or normalizing of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrated adequate empathic moves and brief reflections, but relied more on information-giving than deep reflective listening and missed chances to capture the patient’s skepticism and minimization more precisely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on BP and alcohol use throughout and negotiated realistic, measurable steps that kept momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative with patient input on alarm wording and the plan, though frequent directive information at times shifted authority toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, normalizing doubt, and framing change as a low-effort experiment, reinforcing the patient’s control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited actionable commitment language and concrete steps, but could deepen motivation by drawing out personal reasons, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally positioned the work as collaborative and invited choices, yet sometimes moved quickly into expert instruction without more explicit co-creation of priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively invited and reinforced change language and translated intent into a specific plan, though could more strategically amplify reasons and past successes to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and did not reinforce sustain talk, but did not consistently use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to acknowledge ambivalence and gently shift toward change.
