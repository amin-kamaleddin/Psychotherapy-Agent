# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming (opening praise, repeated validations) and includes a caring safety check, creating a supportive tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and reflective listening with pragmatic reflections, but misses deeper affective probes into feelings like loneliness or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, goal-directed focus on alcohol use and safety, moving logically from assessment to harm reduction, planning, rehearsal, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—asks for input, offers options, rehearses scripts, and repeatedly checks preferences so the patient’s choices shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy with permission language, choice offerings, and tailoring the plan to the patient’s desire not to quit entirely.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk through choices and planning but does not systematically draw out internal motives, values, or use scaling questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership and shared decision-making via permission and options, though some recommendations are fairly directive rather than fully elicited from the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and affirms specific change language (alcohol-free days, limits) but does not systematically develop reasons, benefits, or confidence to amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and moves to planning, but misses opportunities for double-sided or rolling-with-resistance reflections to reframe or soften sustain arguments.
