# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client's position with explicit acceptance statements and nonjudgmental language. Validation of pragmatic choices reinforced a strong accepting tone throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent reflective listening and perspective-taking captured both content and practical constraints, with clear summarizing empathy statements. Could deepen emotional reflections and use more complex reflections to explore underlying values or worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on a concrete target (sleep as harm-reduction) and repeatedly moves toward a specific implementation plan. Could more directly connect the sleep steps to the primary alcohol-risk goal and check client priorities about alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with inclusive language and co-created planning, inviting the client's input and checking back on choices. At times the clinician offered specific strategies rather than eliciting them first, making it slightly less symmetric.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice and ownership by framing the plan as an experiment and asking which option feels most doable. Honors the client's caveats and avoids pressuring for commitment, reinforcing volition.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits the client's own motivations and anticipated benefits, drawing out concrete change reasons like better mornings and focus. Could deepen motivation by linking those reasons to larger values or longer-term goals to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance through brainstorming invitations and shared planning that respect the client's expertise. The clinician occasionally guided the agenda with suggestions instead of eliciting client-generated options first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk about the sleep experiment and reflects the client's language to reinforce motivation. Missed opportunities include explicitly tying gains to the broader alcohol-risk target and using scaling/confidence or commitment prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk nonjudgmentally but does not consistently use double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic redirection to resolve ambivalence. The clinician often moved on rather than elaborating both sides to weaken sustain language.
