# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [x]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly normalizes and affirms patient preferences (e.g., “I accept that you want practical steps…”). Avoids blame and supports the patient’s choices. Feedback: language is sometimes formulaic (“I hear you — …”) and could show more individualized, unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [x]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Consistent perspective-taking and accurate reflections linking concerns to soccer/scholarship. Reflections often mirror content rather than deepen emotional nuance; missed opportunities to explore underlying fear, shame, or peer dynamics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [ ]4 [x]5  
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on the target behavior and movement to concrete steps (options generation → selection of exit strategy → scripting and rehearsal). Each turn advanced toward a tangible behavioral plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [x]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Emphasizes partnership and defers to the adolescent’s choices (asks which to practice, order of role-play). Occasionally uses evaluative language (“that makes sense”) that subtly shifts toward steering rather than pure reflection.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [ ]4 [x]5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms patient control (e.g., “Which would you like to start with?”). Consistently supports the adolescent’s ownership of the plan and how to implement it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [x]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Elicits the patient’s own solutions and specific strategies (sober friend, “now” text, exit lines, comeback). Could more directly probe motivations, values, and discrepancy to deepen intrinsic motivation beyond behavioral tactics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [x]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Positions the adolescent as the expert and invites choices about practice format and sequencing. Slightly diminished by occasional steering comments that endorse the plan rather than solely reflect it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [ ]3 [x]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Reinforces and links existing change talk (protecting scholarship, playing soccer) to concrete strategies and rehearsals. Missed explicit elicitation of commitment and confidence (no scaling question or direct commitment ask).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ]1 [ ]2 [x]3 [ ]4 [ ]5  
**Comments:** Does not shame minimizing remarks and acknowledges them, but often mirrors sustain/minimizing talk (“I’ve only tried drinking once or twice”) without using double-sided reflections or exploring costs; could more actively soften and reframe sustain talk.
