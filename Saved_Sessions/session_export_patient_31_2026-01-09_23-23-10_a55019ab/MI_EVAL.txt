# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, using warm language and normalization (e.g., validating enjoyment of smoking and asking how to be addressed). The tone is supportive throughout with no shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening with brief reflections and acknowledgement of concerns (side effects, avoiding pills). Missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions and ambivalence behind smoking behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction/cessation with concrete, actionable guidance on NRT dosing, timelines, coping strategies, and reassessment. The clinician keeps the conversation tightly aligned with behavior change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames interaction as joint problem-solving, asks permission, and invites adjustments, but often supplies the content and leads recommendations. Partnership is present but leans toward clinician-led decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Presents options and respects patient preferences (daytime-only plan, avoiding pills) and asks if help is wanted. Autonomy is somewhat undermined by directive instructions (e.g., firm step-up orders) with limited negotiation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicits the patient's personal motivations, values, or reasons for change; interaction is largely informational and directive. There are few open invitations or reflections to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates collaborative elements like checking back and summarizing agreed steps, but much of the session is clinician-driven with limited shared decision-making. The balance favors expert recommendation over co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates some space for change by offering plans and a reassessment timeline, but rarely elicits the patient's own reasons, goals, or confidence and does not consistently amplify spontaneous change talk. Change talk is mostly patient-initiated rather than evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk (normalizing enjoyment) yet frequently shifts to risk-focused or prescriptive responses that can strengthen resistance. Limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic affirmations to redirect sustain talk toward change.
