# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful tone, affirmed the patient's choices, and normalized pragmatic harm‑reduction actions, conveying unconditional positive regard without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validated the patient's concerns and matched priorities with practical options, but missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections that would capture emotional ambivalence more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear, consistent focus on heroin use and concrete harm‑reduction goals, repeatedly returning to actionable steps suited to the patient's stage of readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was team‑oriented with offers to find resources and draft scripts, but the clinician sometimes moved into providing ready‑made answers instead of co‑constructing plans with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission before acting, and explicitly avoiding pressure to quit, reinforcing patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some elicitation of motivation and readiness, but the clinician relied more on information-giving than systematically drawing out the patient's intrinsic reasons, values, strengths, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician framed work as a joint effort, offered to help and asked for input, yet at times assumed an expert role by supplying full scripts rather than inviting the patient to generate wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician prompted concrete change statements and logistical readiness, but did not sufficiently reflect, amplify, or explore those statements to strengthen reasons and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and focused on harm reduction, but did not strategically use MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or reframing) to soften sustain talk about using to cope.
