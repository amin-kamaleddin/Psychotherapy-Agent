# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows respect and nonjudgmental positive regard, normalizing concerns and affirming the patient's choices. Language and offers (permission, options) convey unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate paraphrasing of the patient's situation and ambivalence. Missed opportunities to deepen emotional meaning or explore underlying values driving concern about blood pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol to improve blood pressure and guides toward a single concrete goal. The clinician links actions to the BP goal and creates measurable plans (time, tracking, coping).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is co-produced with the patient through choice of wording, rehearsal, and negotiated reminder details. The clinician rarely assumes an expert-only stance and elicits the patient's input to shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and accepting the patient's readiness rating without pressure. Provides tailored choices and affirms the patient's right to decide next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some internal motivation, implementation intentions, and barriers, and asks for readiness and confidence. Could more strongly draw out personal values and reasons for change to amplify change talk and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance with repeated invitations for preference and joint decision-making (scripts, timing, tracking). Demonstrates power-sharing and positions expertise about change primarily with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and strengthens change talk with goal-setting, scaling, and implementation details, and reinforces the patient's choice. Opportunities remain to probe deeper value-based reasons and to further explore confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and instead acknowledges ambivalence and redirects toward planning, which reduces sustain language. Could more explicitly use techniques like double-sided reflections or reframing to soften sustain talk further.
