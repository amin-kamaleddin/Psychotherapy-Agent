# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and avoided judgment, using normalizing and supportive language throughout. The clinician reassured the patient about slips and fears, creating a warm respectful stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections that acknowledged shame and ambivalence. Opportunities were missed to probe deeper into the intensity and meaning of emotions before moving to informational content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on smoking cessation with concrete assessment, a quit plan, NRT dosing, and next steps. The clinician consistently steered the session toward actionable behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-created a practical quit plan with the patient and offered tailored tools and scripts, inviting patient input. At times the clinician defaulted to an expert, offering prescriptive steps rather than eliciting more of the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by asking permission, offering choices (gum vs patch vs meds), and proposing a time-limited gum trial when the patient hesitated. Language consistently preserved the patient’s right to choose and fostered ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of past attempts and readiness occurred, but the clinician relied more on providing information than drawing out the patient’s intrinsic reasons or confidence. More questions to amplify values, meaning, and past successes would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making by inviting choices and co-developing plans and scripts with the patient. The clinician sometimes moved into an expert role with detailed medical recommendations rather than fully privileging the patient’s perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change statements (readiness rating, desire to quit, past successes) but did not consistently amplify or build on them before offering advice. Pausing for elaboration and using selective reflections would have better strengthened change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and constructively addressed sustain talk and ambivalence with validation, reassurance about NRT, and a slip plan to reduce shame. Some responses were information-heavy and mildly persuasive; more double-sided reflections could further contain sustain talk.
