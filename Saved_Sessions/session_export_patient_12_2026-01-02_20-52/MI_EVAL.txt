# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., “I’m here to listen and support you,” “Thank you for telling me — that took courage,” praising setting the timer). Warm, respectful language throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, validating reflective listening (e.g., “that sounds really hard,” “I hear how helpless you feel”) that kept the patient engaged. Missed some opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections naming isolation/shame or the recurring stuck quality of helplessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong focus on target behaviors and safety (asked about suicidal intent, harm‑reduction advice, drafted texts, if‑then plan). Session consistently moved toward concrete, actionable steps and safety planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work framed as joint (e.g., “We can figure next steps together,” offering options, co‑drafting messages). Clinician solicited patient preference and tailored plans to readiness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and control (honored refusals to send texts, offered alternatives, emphasized patient choice). Did not pressure; respected readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of preferences and a small commitment (choosing delay + grounding) but limited exploration of internal motivations or values (did not probe regret or reasons for change in depth).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership through offers to co‑create drafts and asking which option felt better. Could deepen by inviting patient to teach clinician about past strategies or context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk present (regret) and a small behavioral commitment secured, but change language was not systematically amplified or explored (few follow‑up questions to build desire/ability/reasons).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and validated sustain talk, but used limited strategic techniques (few double‑sided reflections or reframes) to explicitly soften sustain talk and highlight ambivalence.
