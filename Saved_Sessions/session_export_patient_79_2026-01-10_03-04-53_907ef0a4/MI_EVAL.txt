# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Luis’s frustration and honoring his limits (e.g., no lectures). The clinician avoids blaming or labeling and responds supportively throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective‑taking and reflective listening, accurately summarizing Luis’s experience and emotional tone. The clinician tolerates and mirrors skepticism rather than challenging it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and a specific behavior change (daily controller inhaler, 7‑day trial). All questions and suggestions tie back to this target and the session progresses logically.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the session as a joint enterprise with choices offered and plans co‑created (pharmacy card, demo, alarm). The clinician checks preferences frequently and solicits Luis’s buy‑in at each step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Luis’s sense of choice and control, asking what he would try and letting him pick steps and timing. Language and actions reinforce that Luis is the decision‑maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits reasons for change and concrete plans (values question, scales, barriers) but often moves quickly into providing solutions. More pause to draw out intrinsic motives and client‑generated arguments would deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative, repeatedly offering options and inviting Luis to shape the plan (role‑play, printed card, alarm wording). The clinician checks permission and preferences before acting, conveying equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk using open questions, importance/confidence scaling, affirmations, and a specific time‑limited experiment. The clinician amplifies and sustains concrete commitment language for the 7‑day trial.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and responds to sustain talk with validation and pragmatic redirection rather than confrontation. However, the clinician could more often use double‑sided reflections or elicit client counter‑statements before shifting to problem‑solving.
