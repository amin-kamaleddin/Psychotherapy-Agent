# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance (“I can help explore options without pressure,” “that makes sense”). Legitimated concerns about reading outside information; no shaming. No major missed opportunities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening and accurate paraphrase of concerns (“I hear you…safety and ‘long‑term’ effects worry you”). Validated anxiety. Could deepen emotional reflection (label underlying fear/responsibility more explicitly).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus on vaccination decision throughout. Maintained momentum toward actionable next steps (documented “varicella only,” 48–72 hr follow‑up). Pacing and closure effective.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Collaborative stance: asked permission to provide info, offered choices, invited Elena’s priorities. Co‑constructed plan and documented patient preferences. Could have asked patient to summarize plan to further co‑construct.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated emphasis on patient control (“You’re the decision‑maker,” option to postpone, documented choice). Offered alternatives and respected right to delay. Strong and consistent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concerns and reasons (safety, long‑term effects, immune overload). Moved to information provision relatively quickly; could ask more targeted questions to deepen intrinsic motivation and clarify values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session read as a partnership: invited input, documented preferences, offered support while patient read VIS. Patient choices shaped the plan (varicella‑only, follow‑up). Could further affirm patient expertise about her child.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (e.g., “I’ll try varicella first,” “I’ll read the VIS”). Affirmations supported movement toward action but clinician could have amplified and linked change talk to values and asked for commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and neutralized pressure (validated concerns, harm‑reduction options). Provided corrective information appropriately; could use more double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence and move sustain talk toward change.
