# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validating the client's wish to focus on dental care and avoiding blame. Honored requests (no smoking lecture, text-only delivery) throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking and reflective listening about practical barriers and priorities. Could deepen empathic resonance by exploring affective content (fatigue, mistrust) and linking emotion to behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear, client-centered focus on actionable dental goals and next steps that matched the patient's priorities. Intentionally de-emphasized smoking cessation, which reduced directional work toward that specific training target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked in true partnership—inviting choices, co-creating scripts, and repeatedly checking preferences. The plan was negotiated and adapted to the client's needs and format requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and honoring the client's decisions (e.g., leave smoking for another time). Actions reinforced the client's ownership of choices without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evoked motivation for the immediate dental goal but made minimal attempts to draw out intrinsic reasons or values related to smoking. Missed brief, low-pressure strategies to elicit smoking-related change talk or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the client as an expert in their own life and co-created the plan with clear choices and permissions. Interaction read as collaborative and client-centered throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk about concrete dental actions (booking, scripts) but did not elicit motivations for smoking cessation. Several gentle opportunities to explore ambivalence about smoking were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and honored sustain talk by deferring smoking discussion and redirecting to constructive alternatives. Tended to deflect rather than use strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to gently resolve ambivalence.
