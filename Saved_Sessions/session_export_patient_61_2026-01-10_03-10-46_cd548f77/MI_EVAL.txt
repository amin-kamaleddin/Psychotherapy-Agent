### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Jonathan’s experience, avoids blaming or pressuring language and validates his limits and doubts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that build rapport, but reflections are sometimes repetitive and could probe deeper underlying emotions like fatigue or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, goal-oriented focus on diabetes self-management and repeatedly steers the conversation toward a concrete, testable action (one morning blood sugar check).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as joint problem-solving by soliciting Jonathan’s ideas, checking how plans feel, and building on his suggestions rather than dictating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirms the patient’s choice and control, invites him to set pace (a one-day test), and avoids coercive language while respecting his skepticism.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits internal motivations and values (work, avoiding hospitalization, family) and uses evocative questions to draw out the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions the interaction as collaborative, repeatedly solicits preferences and acceptable next steps, and treats the patient as an expert on his own routine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively identifies, reinforces, and amplifies subtle change talk, asks about past successes, and elicits explicit commitment to a small, specific step.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Often redirects sustain talk toward small experiments and uses double-sided reflections, but occasionally repeats sustain phrases verbatim which can keep resistance salient rather than briefly pivoting to change.
