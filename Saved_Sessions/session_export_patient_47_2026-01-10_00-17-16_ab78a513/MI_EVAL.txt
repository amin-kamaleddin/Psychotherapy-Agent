# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, creating a safe, nonjudgmental atmosphere. Validated feelings and affirmed the patient’s plan throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing ambivalence and practical concerns. Some reflections remained surface-level; deeper emotional/affective reflections (shame, loss) would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction and safety with specific targets (dry nights, alternating drinks, start date). Repeatedly returned to actionable goals and helped specify timing and barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan with the patient, sought her exact wording, and honored her decisions (e.g., delaying telling her son). Tone and behaviors reflected strong mutual decision‑making and shared responsibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported the patient’s choice and control, avoided pressuring language, and framed suggestions as optional. Let the patient select wording, timing, and which strategies to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivation, asked about values and used confidence rulers to draw out reasons for change. At times the clinician offered several prescriptive options before inviting the patient to generate alternatives, slightly reducing pure evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner—invited preferences, incorporated the patient’s language into scripts, and followed her lead on pacing and disclosure. This fostered shared ownership of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk by asking what to keep/change, eliciting doable steps, and securing a start date. Reflections and affirmations reinforced commitment-oriented language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and used double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while shifting focus to consequences and options. Explored alternatives instead of arguing norms, which reduced sustain talk emphasis.
