# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing shame and validating the patient's efforts while avoiding blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking by acknowledging shame and family motivation, but missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration and sometimes moved quickly to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained focused on smoking cessation with clear attention to specific behaviors, triggers, routines, and concrete planning tied to change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner throughout—co-creating scripts, inviting role-play, and tailoring strategies to the patient's choices and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported choice and agency by asking permission, offering multiple options, and normalizing doubts while framing slips as controllable learning steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's reasons and preferences and encouraged self-generated coping lines, but relied more on offering solutions than on drawing out deeper motivations and confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clearly collaborative stance with frequent invitations to choose, practice, and personalize interventions, demonstrating genuine teamwork.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk and micro-goals (e.g., smoke-free mornings, quit date) but could have more consistently amplified and probed for confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used reframing and delay tactics, though at times moved quickly to skill-teaching instead of using double-sided or summary reflections to gently tip ambivalence.
