# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician conveys respect and nonjudgmental regard (safety check, affirmations) but shifts into directive instruction at times; greater, consistent warmth and explicit normalization of slips would raise this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are basic empathic moves ("I hear you," matching scenarios) but few complex reflections of ambivalence, affect, or underlying values, with the clinician moving quickly into solutions rather than deep reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear, consistent focus on preventing shoplifting and resisting peer pressure, linking all interventions tightly to that target behavior and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Role-play structure and check-ins create a collaborative practice space and the clinician adapts to the patient's requests, but most content is clinician-supplied rather than co-created with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choices are offered (pick a step or line) but frequent scripted directives and commands undermine client ownership; more permission-seeking and invitational phrasing would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some action-oriented responses and agreement to try steps but misses systematic elicitation and amplification of the patient's intrinsic motives, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of shared work (inviting role-play, asking which line feels natural) but the clinician often assumes an expert/coaching stance and does not sufficiently solicit the patient's ideas or co-create solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some small change commitments are elicited (choosing a step, agreeing to practice) but the clinician largely provides solutions and scripts instead of asking open questions to draw out the patient's own reasons, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively redirects sustain talk toward behavioral alternatives and uses graduated role-play to counter temptation, though more strategic MI techniques (double-sided reflections, linking to values) could further soften sustain talk.
