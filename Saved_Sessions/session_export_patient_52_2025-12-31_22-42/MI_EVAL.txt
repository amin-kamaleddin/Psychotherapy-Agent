# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently expresses unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment (e.g., “That makes total sense and you’re not alone in feeling torn,” “No pressure — pick whichever feels more like ‘me’ time this week”). Offers to keep contact “quiet and practical” and honors patient limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, frequent reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., “I hear how pulled you feel…,” “You’re torn in a real, understandable way…”). Reflections capture both stressors and ambivalence (skepticism and willingness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing the second nightly drink and moves to concrete, doable steps and follow‑up (elicits a small step, co‑creates plan, sets timed check‑ins).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames session as joint problem‑solving, offers options and asks patient preference (e.g., “How would you like me to support you this week…?”, jointly choosing text wording/times and drink options).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Respects patient choice and avoids coercion (explicit “no pressure,” honors decision to try a small experiment before referral, asks permission for supports and follows patient limits).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits reasons and ideas for change (values: protecting parenting, concrete swap, strengths). Missed opportunity to probe deeper for confidence/importance ratings or stronger evocative questions to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters equal partnership—co‑creates plan, tailors supports to patient preferences, shares decision‑making (e.g., jointly selecting prep reminder and check‑in content/timing).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and reflects change talk (e.g., “Maybe I could try one drink instead of two,” “I’ll try swapping out the second drink…”). Could have amplified and consolidated change talk more and elicited explicit commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and acknowledges ambivalence (reflects skepticism/willingness). Could more intentionally use double‑sided reflections or reframes to further reduce sustain talk and shift toward change.
