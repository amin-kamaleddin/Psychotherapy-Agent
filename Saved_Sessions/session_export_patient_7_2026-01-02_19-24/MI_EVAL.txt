# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently nonjudgmental and validating (e.g., “I’m sorry you’re going through this — that sounds exhausting,” “Thank you for trying those steps”). Kept a guarded adolescent engaged. Missed deeper normalization of privacy/guardedness (no reflection like “I can see why you’d want to protect your privacy”).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses reflective, empathic statements and checks feelings (“You sound exhausted,” “Good that you tried the breathing — how did that minute feel?”). Reflections are surface-level and task-focused; did not explore underlying meaning of resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong focus on safety and concrete behavioral targets (safety checks, bedside 4-4-6, worry notebook, earplugs). Consistently steers session to practical next steps and crisis guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and invites selection (“Which of these could you try tonight?”). However, many turns are directive with explicit instructions, reducing patient-driven agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers options and reminds patient of responsibility for safety (call if worsens). Some mild pressure from repeated prompts to agree to tasks; could more explicitly invite refusal/modify steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits behavioral commitments (patient agrees to breathing, jotting, earplugs) but does not draw out underlying reasons, values, or intrinsic motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some invitations to choose goals, but interaction remains largely clinician-led with scripted homework and summaries; limited co-construction of plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generates weak change talk (multiple “I’ll try” statements) and affirms attempts, but misses opportunities to amplify or probe importance/confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk to low-risk, small steps. Could further use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence while guiding change.
