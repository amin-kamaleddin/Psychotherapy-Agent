# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, normalizing slips and affirming progress. Safety check and frequent positive reinforcement demonstrated unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician showed good perspective-taking with accurate reflections and validation of ambivalence. Empathy could be strengthened by using more complex reflections to explore deeper values and emotions underlying smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with concrete, measurable goals (morning delay, 3 pm plan, midweek check-in). This maintained momentum toward behavior change and problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited the patient's input, rehearsed responses, and planned shared follow-up, reflecting a collaborative approach. At times the clinician shifted into a more didactic/expert mode without first eliciting the patient's solutions or permission to advise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported choice by offering options, small trials, and normalizing hesitancy, avoiding coercive language. Suggestions emphasized the patient's control to accept or reject strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited and translated tentative change language into actionable steps and time-bound experiments. Evocation could be improved by asking more open questions about personal reasons and values and by amplifying the patient's change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician treated the patient as an expert on his experience, offering choices and inviting preference for strategies. Partnership was occasionally reduced by providing detailed advice without explicitly asking permission or eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and reinforced change talk, asking which options felt doable and setting specific goals. Greater strategic amplification and summarizing of the patient's own pro-change language would help move from tentative to stronger commitment talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized, but the clinician often moved quickly to strategies rather than using double-sided reflections or exploring pros/cons. More explicit juxtaposition of sustain and change sides would better resolve ambivalence toward change.
