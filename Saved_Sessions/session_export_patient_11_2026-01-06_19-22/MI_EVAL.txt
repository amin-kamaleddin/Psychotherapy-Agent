# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the patient's choices while adapting recommendations to her context. Could add more individualized normalizing or empathic statements about specific frustrations to reach the highest rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some perspective-taking (asks about injuries, offers shorter sessions) but uses relatively few reflections of feelings or ambivalence. Interaction is more informational and directive than consistently exploring the patient's emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity with concrete next steps and progression rules. Every response and plan ties directly to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks about preferences and offers adjustable plans, tailoring recommendations when concerns arise. Collaboration is good but not maximized because the clinician often presents detailed plans before eliciting the patient's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and flexible options (session lengths, alternatives, equipment, accountability) and communicates that lapses are acceptable. Language consistently supports the patient's autonomy and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some information and builds on stated motivations by translating them into steps, but largely supplies solutions rather than drawing out deeper reasons, values, or past successes. Missed opportunities to evoke more robust change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and choices at points, which supports partnership, but the interaction is often clinician-driven with prescriptive specifics. The clinician sometimes assumes expert authority instead of co-creating the plan step-by-step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports and translates the patient's clear change talk into a workable plan, yet misses chances to amplify and deepen the patient's own language. Limited elicitation of what change would mean personally beyond pragmatic benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively accepts and accommodates sustain talk, offering scaled options and normalizing lapses to reduce threat. Could further strengthen this by using more double-sided reflections that pair sustain and change talk.
