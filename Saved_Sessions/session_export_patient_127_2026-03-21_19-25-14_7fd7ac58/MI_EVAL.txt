# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys nonjudgmental regard, affirms progress, and conducts respectful safety checks. Responses are competent but often brief and move quickly into problem-solving rather than deeper individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid perspective-taking and reflective summary and checks the client's priorities and safety. Empathic depth is reduced by a rapid shift to prescriptive instructions with limited complex reflections to deepen the client's inner experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on relapse prevention throughout and repeatedly ties interventions to the client's stated priorities. Provides highly practical, context-specific strategies that keep the session on target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative elements like asking the client what to focus on and tailoring responses, but the clinician predominantly delivers concrete solutions. There is limited joint brainstorming or co-creation of scripts and plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers multiple options and asks the client which to prioritize, supporting choice and control. However, much advice that follows is directive, which somewhat reduces client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms 22 days and elicits a focus choice, which supports self-efficacy. Largely clinician-driven problem solving limits elicitation of the client's own reasons, values, and strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partnership by asking for the client's focus and responding to requests, but interaction remains expert-driven. More co-construction of language and plans in the client's words would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforces change talk briefly (praise, asking what to focus on) and responds to requests for help. Misses routine use of open questions, scaling, and reflections to elicit, amplify, and deepen the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and offers practical strategies to reduce temptation rather than confronting sustain talk. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or empathic reframing to explicitly soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
