# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys warm, nonjudgmental positive regard and validates hope and small steps, but some responses feel slightly formulaic and could more explicitly normalize embarrassment, shame, or humor.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing ambivalence and past success, but doesn’t consistently explore underlying affect (shame, nervous laughter, longing) in deeper emotional detail.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing gambling/spending and protecting job/debt, with questions and reflections logically building toward a concrete, doable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High degree of joint problem‑solving—co‑constructs the safety plan, repeatedly checks preferences, and shapes recommendations based on the client’s responses rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice by offering options, emphasizing small steps, and explicitly leaving decisions to the client while shaping suggestions collaboratively.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits the client’s own motivations, values, and memories of success, producing extensive client-generated change talk and a self-authored, specific plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a genuine partnership with language inviting co-creation, checking back before offering suggestions, and treating the client as the expert on their life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies desire, ability, reasons, and tentative commitment by linking past successes to concrete, value‑consistent steps and asking for specific doable actions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and reframes sustain talk toward change, but could more actively use double-sided reflections and explore what maintains sustain talk (pleasure, social identity) or the costs of staying the same.
