# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows nonjudgmental regard and normalizes Tyler's stance, validating his feelings without moralizing. This unconditional positive regard reduced resistance throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections capture Tyler's social motives and practical concerns, demonstrating strong understanding. These reflections appear to lower resistance and elicit more detailed responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on harm‑reduction and substance use throughout the session, repeatedly steering toward concrete behavioral steps. Questions and reflections consistently move toward a specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is co‑constructed with open questions and reflections that build the plan from Tyler's ideas. The clinician invites joint problem‑solving and uses Tyler's contributions to shape the strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasizes Tyler's choice and elicits his preferences, reinforcing ownership of the plan. There is no coercion or imposed quit‑now agenda, supporting perceived freedom to choose harm reduction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete, actionable intentions and commitment to harm‑reduction behaviors, but the motivation evoked is primarily extrinsic (avoid getting caught). The clinician could probe more for internal reasons, values, and confidence to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions Tyler as the source of solutions and repeatedly asks for his ideas, fostering power‑sharing and mutual respect. Invitations like ‘What ideas feel realistic to you?’ support a strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits specific ability/commitment language and small steps (e.g., Mike holding stuff, no school‑night use). However, there was less amplification of internal desire, reasons, or confidence for change beyond pragmatic avoidance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and acknowledges sustain talk while redirecting toward harm reduction, which helped move Tyler toward concrete steps. More use of double‑sided reflections or selectively amplifying glimpses of ambivalence could further diminish sustain talk.
