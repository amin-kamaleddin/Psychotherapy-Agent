# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicated clear, nonjudgmental support from the start, normalizing struggle and acknowledging guilt and exhaustion without shaming. This explicit unconditional positive regard was consistent throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking captured core emotions (guilt, exhaustion, pull of friends/drinking). Some reflections were brief and the clinician moved quickly into problem-solving, missing opportunities for deeper empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on shoplifting and related triggers, tying every major intervention back to reducing risk. The session kept momentum toward concrete, actionable steps rather than drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction largely felt like shared problem-solving with co-developed scripts and patient-adjusted plans. At times the clinician proposed many specific steps before soliciting the patient's perspective, which reduced equal partnership in those moments.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently acknowledged choice and invited the patient to decide which steps to try, reinforcing agency. Occasional prescriptive language and early listing of tactics made some moments more directive than fully autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic motivation through a readiness rating and asking which step felt doable, which produced tentative commitment language. Earlier solution-focused moves limited deeper exploration of the patient's personal values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally adopted a collaborative stance, asking permission and inviting the patient to build the plan together, and adjusted plans to the patient's comfort. Early expert-driven presentation of many steps weakened partnership in a few instances.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective elicitation and reinforcement (readiness scale, asking for a doable first step, affirmations) produced change language and a concrete commitment. However, the clinician often shifted immediately to offering solutions instead of reflecting or amplifying the patient's own motivations, missing chances to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and ambivalence was normalized, which avoided confrontation. The clinician did not consistently use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections, strategic summaries) to soften sustain talk and often moved into advice, leaving ambivalence less explored.
