# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with timely, specific praise (e.g., quitting smoking) and a supportive, nonjudgmental tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated accurate, helpful reflections and linked drinking to valued roles, but reflections were mainly content-level and could deepen by exploring affect and ambivalence more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use and moved the patient toward concrete action using specific goals, step-by-step planning, and rehearsal for an upcoming event.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—patient was invited to choose strategies, co-construct the plan, and engage in role-play, keeping the patient's ideas central to the work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supported patient choice by asking permission before advising, offering multiple options, and affirming the patient's ownership of the plan without prescriptive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited motivation using importance/confidence scales and linking change to values, but sometimes moved quickly to offering solutions rather than further evoking the patient's own motivations and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician framed the work as a partnership using permission statements, soliciting preferences, and inviting practice, which fostered power-sharing and joint decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicited and reinforced change talk with scaling questions and concrete planning, though could further probe desire, reasons, and summarize change language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided amplifying sustain talk and offered practical strategies, but did not sufficiently reflect or normalize reluctance—using double-sided or strategic reflections would better soften sustain talk.
