# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizing the client's tension and validating feelings without blame. Uses nonjudgmental language that supports safety and worthiness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides accurate, nuanced reflections that capture content and ambivalence, matching the client's emotional tone. These reflections help the client elaborate and feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a tight, appropriate focus on alcohol-related decisions and near-term implementation strategies. Questions and reflections consistently move toward actionable plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the client as a co‑creator of the plan, follows the client's lead, and checks preferences throughout. Uses inclusive language and role‑play to build shared ownership of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options and honors the client's choices, avoiding pressure or prescription. Reinforces the client's decision‑making authority about how to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the client's motivations, resources, and concrete ideas, using open questions that invite solutions. Could more systematically draw out intrinsic motives, past successes, and stronger commitment language (e.g., confidence/readiness rulers).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative and locates expertise with the client, inviting their ideas and preferences. Co‑shapes plans and decisions (friend, wording, cue) so the client drives the outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces specific change talk and planning (buddy plan, mocktail, cue word). Missed some opportunities to amplify and convert desire/planning into stronger commitment and confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses double‑sided reflections and nondirective language to acknowledge competing motivations without reinforcing sustain talk. Redirects toward solutions and planning, which reduces the pull of reasons to maintain risky behavior.
