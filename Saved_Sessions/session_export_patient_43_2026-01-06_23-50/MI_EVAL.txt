# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys nonjudgmental regard and normalizes ambivalence, with frequent affirmations and respectful language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good understanding with accurate reflections and useful summaries, but reflections are mostly paraphrase-level and could be deepened with more evocative, emotion-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with measurable, actionable steps and scheduling that keep the session moving toward concrete change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—invites input, asks which options feel doable, and incorporates patient preferences—though offering several clinician-initiated strategies early slightly reduces the equal-partner feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by framing the patient as in charge, offering choices, checking confidence, and acknowledging the right to decline or be imperfect.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own plans, barriers, and confidence using scaling and questions, but could more often probe personal values or deeper reasons for change to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters client expertise and co-constructs the plan, yet the early presentation of multiple concrete options without explicit permission modestly shifts toward an expert-led approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with specific questions and commitment-oriented steps, though the clinician could more frequently amplify and summarize the patient's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and acknowledges sustain talk but relies mainly on neutral reflections and problem-solving instead of using double-sided reflections or evocative discrepancy to soften sustain talk.
