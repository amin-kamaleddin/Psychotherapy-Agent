# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys clear unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance with frequent affirmations and normalization of ambivalence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and accurate, brief reflections of mixed feelings, but reflections are often short and the clinician moves quickly to skill teaching instead of deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on reducing drinking and managing anger, offering concrete relapse‑prevention tools and clear next steps that maintain forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicits preferences, co‑constructs strategies (texts, exit lines), invites role‑play, and adjusts plans based on the patient's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering multiple formats and durations, asking which tool to use, and emphasizing that practice and adaptations are optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation through choices and practical planning, but relies more on offering solutions than eliciting the patient's intrinsic motivations, values, scaling, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly works as a partner rather than an expert—shares power by letting the patient choose tools, wording, and pace while co‑creating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforces and amplifies change talk with praise, options, and co‑created plans, but misses systematic elicitation of deeper client‑generated reasons and scaling questions to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk with adapted options and discrete strategies, though could better use double‑sided reflections to explicitly convert sustain talk into change talk.
