# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing frustration and affirming the patient’s preferences (e.g., honoring no lectures or calls). The clinician avoided judgment throughout and validated boundaries clearly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, specific reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both content and emotion (skepticism, overwhelm, protectiveness). Reflections included concrete details that the patient confirmed, showing strong empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior—improving maintenance inhaler adherence via text reminders. Every question and reflection advanced planning and commitment toward that specific goal without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a partnership of equals with co-created decisions; the clinician solicited the patient’s routines and preferences and committed to implementing them. The clinician positioned themselves as supporting the patient’s plan rather than imposing one.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice and control by inviting preferences, offering snooze/reply/STOP options, and framing the month as an experiment the patient can discontinue. Language was noncoercive and emphasized patient authority over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited motivation from the patient’s own values and desired outcomes (sleeping through the night, fewer wheezy nights) rather than providing external reasons. Open questions drew out intrinsic reasons and concrete commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly framed the work as collaborative with shared decision-making and repeated invitations for input on wording, timing, and stopping rules. The clinician deferred to the patient’s expertise about what will work and co-constructed the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced multiple pieces of change talk and a specific, time-limited experiment (one month of texts). The clinician amplified patient-generated ideas and explored measurable outcomes that would indicate benefit.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or confronting sustain talk, instead validating concerns and redirecting toward small, controlled steps (the one-month trial). Acceptance and reframing reduced resistance and preserved the patient’s sense of control.
