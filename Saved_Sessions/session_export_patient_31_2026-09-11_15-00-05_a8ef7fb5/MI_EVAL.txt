# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the patient's preferences without blaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality reflections and summaries captured both content and feeling (e.g., the 'stupid‑simple' plan and practical constraints), showing strong understanding of the patient's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking reduction and progressed to a concrete, measurable plan (patch+lozenge, two‑week trial, one‑tap tally, silent reminder) without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as collaborative problem-solving: clinician offered options and practical support while consistently deferring to the patient's choices (app, tracking, reminder).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by framing options as the patient's choice, normalizing not being ready to quit, and explicitly allowing the patient to stop the trial if it wasn't helpful.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's own motivations, success criteria, and commitment to a time‑limited experiment, eliciting concrete change talk and personally meaningful goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co‑constructed through questions about preferences and power‑sharing; the clinician offered help but let the patient determine the specifics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited change‑oriented language via directional open questions and experiment prompts, though could have amplified statements of willingness and probed confidence/past success more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged resistance while shifting to feasible experiments, but used few double‑sided reflections and did not explicitly elicit reasons against quitting to resolve ambivalence more strategically.
