# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, avoids blame or shaming while validating barriers and patient agency. Offers choices and frames options nonjudgmentally.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate understanding and reflective listening, summarizing both desires and competing pulls and attending to emotions and practical worries. Reflections are specific and perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on avoiding reoffending and building structured activity, moving steadily toward a concrete action (the 2pm call). Repeatedly redirects to achievable next steps and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly equal‑partnered with frequent invitations for the patient to direct choices and co‑construct the plan. Clinician tailors responses to patient preferences and offers to role‑play or read lines as requested.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering multiple options, asking which feels truer, and avoiding pressuring language. Repeatedly asks for the patient's preference about timing and methods of rehearsal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies concrete motivation and commitment statements and moves toward planning, but could more explicitly link actions to the patient's deeper values and elicit confidence language. Slight opportunity to deepen intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the work as collaborative, invites co‑creation of scripts and steps, and solicits the patient's input on tone and timing. Power is shared throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly elicits, reinforces, and deepens change language with open questions, scaling down to the smallest step, and following up with planning and rehearsal. Capitalizes on patient statements of intent to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses double‑sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence, but misses some opportunities to further explore or reframe the pull of sustain talk (e.g., thrill‑seeking) and highlight past resistance successes. Could use more strategic reflections to reduce the sustain side.
