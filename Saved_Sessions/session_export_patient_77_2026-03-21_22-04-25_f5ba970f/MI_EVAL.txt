# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, validated feelings, and honored boundaries (e.g., not involving parents, avoiding pasting notes). Warm, nonjudgmental stance was maintained throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used solid reflective listening that captured frustration and loneliness, normalizing the patient’s experience. Could deepen empathy by exploring underlying emotions, fears, and values beyond logistical details.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept focused on the target behavior (breaking the plateau) and moved the session toward a concrete, measurable plan (one-day tracking, specific alarms, review).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively solicited the patient’s preferences and co-constructed the plan (format, timing, check-in questions), adapting to Liyana’s requests. Power was shared and the plan reflected her choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered options, asked permission before suggesting, repeatedly affirmed support for the patient’s choices, and respected boundaries—clearly reinforced the patient’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation via past successes, importance/confidence rulers, and concrete small steps that produced commitment language. Could further probe personal values and longer-term reasons to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Liyana as the decision-maker and negotiated specifics (reporting method, timing, questions to ask), incorporating her requests for how the clinician should behave. Demonstrated strong collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used rulers and asked about past strategies and realistic small steps, eliciting explicit change talk and commitment. Could more consistently amplify and summarize the patient’s change language to build greater momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed sustain talk nonjudgmentally, offering neutral options and avoiding argument. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections to explicitly name ambivalence and gently shift emphasis toward change.
