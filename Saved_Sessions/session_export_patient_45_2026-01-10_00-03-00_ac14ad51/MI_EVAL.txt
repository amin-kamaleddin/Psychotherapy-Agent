# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with normalizing and validating statements and accommodation of the patient's preferences (e.g., simple symptom log). The clinician avoids blame and affirms Erik's experience throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows clear understanding and some reflective statements (normalizing overwhelm, acknowledging frustration about lifelong meds) but uses few complex reflections. Empathic tone is present but not deeply exploratory of underlying feelings or worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on target behaviors (medication routine, meal timing, alcohol) and consistently steers the conversation toward actionable steps and follow-through. The clinician repeatedly brings dialogue back to practical implementation and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—asks about shift schedule and med name, checks preferences, and co-creates alarms/log—but frequently provides concrete instructions without first asking permission. Collaboration is strong overall but interspersed with clinician-directed content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering choices, inviting preferences, and avoiding coercive language (e.g., leaving prescription changes to the prescriber while encouraging patient input). The clinician affirms Erik's ownership of decisions and encourages him to call the doctor if needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitments and doable steps (two-week trial, alarms, log) but relies heavily on clinician-provided solutions and information. The clinician could have more systematically drawn out Erik's own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates collaboration by soliciting priorities and input and co-creating plans, but much of the session is clinician-led with authoritative recommendations. Partnership is present but not fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk by asking what feels doable and securing specific commitments, but often moves quickly to advice and instructions instead of amplifying patient's own reasons or confidence. Cultivation of change talk is uneven.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and redirects toward practical, doable steps, effectively pivoting from resistance to action. Could further use strategic reflections (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explore ambivalence without reinforcing sustain talk.
