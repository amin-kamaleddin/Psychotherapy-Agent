# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, using validating reflections that avoid blame and allow the patient to speak freely.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate empathic listening and perspective-taking are evident through well-tuned reflections and clarifying questions that capture both content and feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear focus on resolving confidentiality and logistical concerns, but does not direct the conversation toward the flagged alcohol behavior, which was a missed opportunity if behavior change was the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functions as a true collaboration with shared decision-making, soliciting the patient's preferences and co-constructing a plan for reviewing the confidentiality statement and follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers choices and defers decisions to the patient, affirming control over how to proceed and avoiding pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits the patient's concerns and preferences about the screening process, but does not evoke motivation or thoughts about changing the underlying flagged behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician clearly positions the interaction as a partnership, inviting input and offering options rather than prescribing a single course of action.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to elicit change talk about alcohol use; the clinician focuses on confidentiality preferences rather than exploring reasons, desires, or plans related to the flagged behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is largely avoided and the clinician does not reinforce status-quo arguments, though limited exploration of the behavior means there were few opportunities to explicitly soften sustain talk.
