# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and avoided judgment, validating Alexei's goals and normalizing awkwardness. Tone remained supportive and respectful throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with clear reflections and summaries of ambivalence, capturing both pragmatic and emotional concerns. Occasional deeper affective reflections could further deepen emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related behavior tied to soccer and scholarship goals and set concrete proximal steps. Planning stayed tightly linked to the target behavior and measurable next actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner by inviting Alexei's input, incorporating his language, and co-creating the implementation plan. The clinician scaffolded solutions rather than imposing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and respecting Alexei's pacing and declines. Language reinforced that the patient retained decision-making power.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation from within by using importance/confidence rulers, amplifying Alexei's change talk, and building a plan from his own reasons. The clinician built commitment through patient-selected steps and practice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as collaborative, offering choices and seeking Alexei's preferences at multiple decision points. Expertise was shared and the plan reflected Alexei's selections and ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk using rulers, reflections, and prompting for specific steps, then translated it into concrete actions. Could occasionally prompt the patient to phrase his own reasons before offering scripted wording to increase ownership.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing minimization and successfully pivoted to planning, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or explicitly rolling with sustain talk. Using more strategic reflections of the sustain side before pivoting could further soften resistance.
