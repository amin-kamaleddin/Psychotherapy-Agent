# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a warm, nonjudgmental tone (e.g., validating effort, normalizing steps). The clinician avoids blaming language and models acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing ambivalence and acknowledging barriers. Could occasionally deepen affective reflections (e.g., naming anxiety or relief) but overall understanding is strong and helpful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing weeknight drinking and moves from exploration to an explicit, measurable plan. Offers concrete experiments and repeatedly returns to actionable details (timing, timer, friend text, success metric).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient as an equal partner by inviting input, offering options, and integrating preferences into the plan. Provides resources and checks fit/tone while co-constructing backup plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes patient choice and control, offering multiple options and asking permission before intervening. Reinforces small, reversible experiments to support the patient's sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from the patient using open questions, the confidence ruler, and prompts to define success, drawing out internal reasons and values. Moves the patient from vague consideration toward specific intentions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance and explicitly follows the patient's lead while co-creating the plan. Respects preferences (e.g., letting the patient choose strategies and draft wording) which supports shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and strengthens change talk through options, reflections, and scaling questions, and invites concrete planning that converts desires into specific steps. Amplifies patient statements about benefits and feasible strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward change with neutral reflections and nonthreatening experiments. There are missed opportunities for more explicit double-sided reflections and linking sustain talk to values to further weaken resistance.
