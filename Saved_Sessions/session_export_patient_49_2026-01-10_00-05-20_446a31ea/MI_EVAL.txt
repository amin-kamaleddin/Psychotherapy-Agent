# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with repeated affirmations and validations, and accepted the patient's limits (e.g., reluctance to use patches).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid empathic listening and some reflections but uses few complex or emotional reflections and moves quickly to practical strategies. Deeper exploration of feelings and ambivalence would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with concrete, time-limited plans and actionable steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative with patient preferences shaping decisions and frequent permission-checking, but the clinician often provides scripted solutions rather than eliciting the patient's own brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors the patient's choice and offers options without mandates, framing suggestions as choices and encouraging consultation with other clinicians.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivational content (goals, preferences) but relies heavily on offering strategies and provides few questions to draw out importance, confidence, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invites input, yet the clinician sometimes shifts into an expert/teacher role instead of fully co-creating solutions from the patient's language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change-oriented language and concrete goals, but missed opportunities to amplify the patient's own reasons and probe importance/confidence to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids strengthening sustain talk by normalizing small steps and offering options, though would benefit from more explicit MI techniques (double-sided reflections, summarizing both sides) to resolve ambivalence.
