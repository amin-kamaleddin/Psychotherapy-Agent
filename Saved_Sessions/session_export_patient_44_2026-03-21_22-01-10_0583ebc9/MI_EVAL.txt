# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect with specific, nonjudgmental affirmations that validated Erik’s progress. Language avoided labeling or shaming, supporting rapport and motivation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening, capturing both progress and ambivalence in timely reflections. Frequent, well-timed reflections and summaries matched Erik’s feelings and concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on concrete behavioral targets (days/times, cues, backups, snacks) and sequenced planning logically. Every question and reflection linked back to actionable goals and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in genuine joint problem-solving, inviting Erik’s input at each step and integrating his ideas into the plan. The clinician maintained a co-author stance, allowing Erik’s choices to shape decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally supported choice by asking which options felt doable and avoiding pressuring language, but frequently solicited specific commitments without always asking permission first. Could increase explicit invitations before offering concrete suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited Erik’s motivations, values, and plans, producing rich client language and specific commitments. However, the clinician often supplied options and strategies rather than eliciting more client-generated alternatives first.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative, inviting preferences and co-creating the action plan with Erik’s ideas driving the plan’s details. The clinician offered options and deferred to Erik’s choices, reflecting strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, turning reasons and benefits into specific commitments (schedule, confidence, prep steps). Reflections and summaries amplified Erik’s motivation and led to concrete action planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk without confrontation and redirected toward coping strategies, which avoided strengthening sustain talk. Could more often use double-sided reflections and strategic reframes to explicitly tie sustain talk to reasons for change.
