# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful and nonjudgmental (safety check, empathic label) and works with Julian's request, but positive regard feels somewhat procedural with few personalized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are empathic moves (safety check, invitation, brief acknowledgment), but reflections are sparse and the clinician shifts quickly to problem-solving rather than exploring Julian's internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear, consistent focus on completing and documenting community service, repeatedly tying interventions to logistics, deadlines, and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative—asking permission, offering choices, and co-constructing the message—but the clinician often leads with templates and content rather than eliciting joint generation of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers concrete options and asks permission, supporting Julian's choices and explaining tradeoffs, but sometimes frames options through recommendations rather than eliciting his reasons.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is minimal: the clinician supplies solutions instead of eliciting Julian's own reasons, values, or confidence, and does not use scaling or other mobilizing questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership is shown by asking what Julian wants and offering choices, but much of the session is clinician-driven with authoritative recommendations that limit shared control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is little effort to elicit or amplify Julian's change language—interaction focuses on providing tools rather than asking open questions about reasons, desire, or confidence to complete the hours.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reduces barriers with practical solutions which indirectly lessens sustain talk, but does not use MI-specific strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly soften or reframe avoidance.
