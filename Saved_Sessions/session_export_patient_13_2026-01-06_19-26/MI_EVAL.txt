# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validation and normalization (e.g., "very understandable," "I'm proud of you"). The clinician avoided blame and respected the client's experience throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking (opening reflection naming emotion and behavior, frequent reflections using client language). A minor opportunity was to add more complex reflections highlighting ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on arranging medical care from initial questions through concrete planning (scripts, timing, coaching). Balanced exploration with forward movement to an actionable, specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine partnership by co-creating scripts, offering roleplay/coaching, and repeatedly soliciting the client's input and preferences. Positioned clinician as a supportive partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering multiple options, asking which felt doable, and framing support as optional and client-led. Avoided pressuring language and reinforced client control over pace and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited core motivations (health for her child) and linked them to steps, but relied more on offering solutions than exploring internal reasons, confidence, or past successes. Could deepen evocation with importance/confidence questions and more elicited reasons why change matters.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited collaboration (tweaking scripts, choosing steps, coaching together) and honored the client's preferences, fostering shared decision-making. The clinician framed suggestions as supports rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with value questions and small-step options, and amplified chosen plans as 'doable.' Could strengthen by more systematically following up on commitment language (confidence ratings, eliciting reasons why now).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Compassionately normalized barriers and reframed overwhelm into manageable steps without arguing or dwelling on avoidance. Missed opportunities to use more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain talk while highlighting the client's desire to be healthy.
