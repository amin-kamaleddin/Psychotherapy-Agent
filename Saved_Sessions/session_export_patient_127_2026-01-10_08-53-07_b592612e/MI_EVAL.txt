# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalized cravings and validated the 22-day milestone, creating a safe space for disclosure and practical help without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used empathic statements and some reflections (affirmation, normalization), but missed deeper, complex reflections on shame, identity, and small meaningful moments that would increase attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear relapse-prevention focus throughout and guided the session to actionable, specific steps (one-page plan, scripts, role-play) maintaining momentum toward the agreed goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned largely as a team effort—solicited goals, adapted materials, and co-created the plan—though at times offered many clinician-generated solutions without first eliciting the client's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, respected the client's blunt preferences, asked permission before drafting/role‑playing, and invited the client to shape the plan, supporting a sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation (opening question, highlighting success) but session prioritized practical strategies over eliciting intrinsic motivations, values, past successes, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created a collaborative tone by inviting input and adapting scripts, but occasional directive recommendations reduced opportunities for fuller co-construction of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced existing change talk (22 days) and prompted concrete planning, yet did not systematically elicit or amplify desire, reasons, or commitment language to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and shifted away from rumination to a relapse plan, but did not consistently use double-sided reflections or explicitly name ambivalence to reframe sustain talk toward change.
