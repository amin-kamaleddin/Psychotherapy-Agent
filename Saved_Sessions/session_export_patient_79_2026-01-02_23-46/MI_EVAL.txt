### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Good use of reflections and paraphrase capturing practical and emotional burden (“tiring and maybe a bit overwhelming,” “the whole routine feels like a hassle”). Reflections were largely content-level; missed opportunities for deeper emotion naming/complex reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5  
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on a concrete target (low‑effort medication trial, simplified follow‑up). Session moved effectively from exploration to a specific, feasible action plan (2–3 week one‑a‑day trial, phone check‑ins, stop signs).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Luis’s input and decision‑making (“You decide; I’ll follow your lead.”). Co‑created the plan and honored preferences (timing, sample, no upsell). At times tilted toward carrying out logistics without explicit readiness checks for each step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5  
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control (explicit permission to stop, “you decide,” framed trial as client experiment). Avoided pressuring language and reinforced client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client priorities and reasons (simpler meds, fewer visits, proof it’s practical) and asked what would make a trial doable. Could have further explored underlying values and amplified reasons for change (importance/confidence).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Interaction framed as joint project; plan shaped by patient directives (7:15pm calls, no upsells). Strong co‑creation but clinician sometimes assumed logistical lead without repeated negotiation of timing/readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Elicited and reflected meaningful change language (past efforts, willingness to try 2–3 week trial, concrete success indicators). Missed opportunities to deepen/amplify change talk (e.g., ask “What do you hope will be different?” or scaling questions to strengthen commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk through reflections and pragmatic steps, preventing escalation. Could increase use of double‑sided or evocative reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while tilting toward change.
