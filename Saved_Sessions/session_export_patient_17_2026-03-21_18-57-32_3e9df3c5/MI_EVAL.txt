# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental—validates shame and normalizes slips (e.g., “slips don’t mean you’ve failed”), and maintains warmth when giving recommendations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and checks safety (reflects shame, anxiety, chest tightness), but sometimes moves quickly into information/solutions without deeper exploration of core emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and steady focus on smoking cessation with concrete steps (quit date, 2-week plan, urge and relapse protocols) and minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the patient as an active partner by soliciting history, offering scripts, and inviting choices, but occasionally shifts to an expert-heavy delivery without checking how much detail the patient wants.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by asking permission, presenting multiple options, and emphasizing discussion with the prescriber rather than pressuring a specific treatment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits past attempts and openness to meds but relies more on providing information than systematically drawing out, amplifying, and exploring the patient’s intrinsic motivations or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-creates plans and offers practical tools (scripts, tailored 2-week plan) but could more consistently elicit the patient’s preference and invite selection between options after presenting them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk and supplies actionable steps, yet misses opportunities to amplify desire and ask follow-ups to deepen commitment and confidence (e.g., importance/confidence scaling).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes slips and reframes relapse as learning while avoiding confrontation, but could more often use double-sided reflections to acknowledge the pull of smoking and highlight reasons for change.
