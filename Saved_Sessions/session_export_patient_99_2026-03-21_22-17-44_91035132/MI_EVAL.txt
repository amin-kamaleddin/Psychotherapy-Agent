# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalized Matteo's experience and closed with affirmation. No shaming or labeling was observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking about practical impacts (mornings, classes, social concerns). Reflections stayed at content/practical level with limited exploration of deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use throughout and progressed to concrete, measurable goals. The session avoided drift and produced a time-limited plan with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed with frequent invitations for Matteo's input and negotiated supports. The clinician respected Matteo's limits and incorporated his preferences into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice by offering multiple options, asking what Matteo wanted to try, and framing slips as information not failure. Check-in preferences and optional supports reinforced autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations, barriers, and used scaling to elicit reasons for change and concrete strategies. Could deepen evocation by exploring broader values, long-term consequences, and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as an equal partnership with shared decision-making and co-creation of the plan. The clinician invited input at every decision point and negotiated follow-up that fit Matteo's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk using open questions, scaling, and concrete steps, and summarized Matteo's own language. Missed opportunities to further amplify and arc change talk into stronger 'why' and commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain-oriented concerns (the social 'vibe') and offered neutral, practical alternatives while normalizing slips. Could increase use of double-sided reflections to explicitly name pulls toward and away from change.
