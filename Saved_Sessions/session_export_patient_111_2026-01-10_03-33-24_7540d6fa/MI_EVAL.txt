# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful stance and validated Richard's feelings throughout the session. This warm, supportive tone was evident from the opening and sustained in reflections and offers of help.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and accurate summaries early on, showing perspective-taking about routine and relationship concerns. Empathy could have been deeper and more sustained by exploring the emotional weight when Richard said he didn't want to lose his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on smoking and its relational consequences with concrete strategies and rehearsal plans. The direction emphasized conflict management and preserving routine rather than deliberately exploring small steps toward cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited choices, adapted language to match Richard's voice, and rehearsed responses together, reflecting a collaborative stance. Collaboration was limited at times by supplying ready-made scripts and specific behavioral directives instead of fully co-creating from Richard's words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported Richard's sense of choice by offering options, honoring his unwillingness to quit, and helping him act on his chosen boundary. The clinician avoided pressure and reinforced control over decisions and actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were attempts to elicit motivation (pros/cons, asking what matters most) that produced change talk about not losing his wife. However, the clinician did not systematically develop that internal motivation or explore importance, confidence, and values further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally worked with Richard by offering options, role‑playing, and adjusting language to his tone, showing good power-sharing. Partnership was diluted when the clinician provided prescriptive scripts and specific tactics rather than co-creating alternatives from Richard's own expressions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-language was elicited (concern about losing his wife), but the clinician did not amplify or explore that language to deepen motivation. The session shifted toward crafting scripts that accept his decision not to quit instead of developing reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Missed opportunities to soften sustain talk; interventions often validated and consolidated statements like 'I'm not ready to quit' by incorporating them into scripts. The clinician did not use double-sided reflections, strategic summaries, or reframing to gently redirect sustain talk.
