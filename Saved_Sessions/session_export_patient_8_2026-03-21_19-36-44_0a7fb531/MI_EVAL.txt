# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validated goals and normalized slips throughout the session. Framed change as practical and achievable, showing strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated understanding and tailored recommendations to the patient's concerns, with good responsiveness. Could deepen empathic listening with more reflective and emotion-focused statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing phone/social media use and moved toward concrete, measurable actions. Kept the agenda tied to stepwise plans and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient choices and adapted suggestions to concerns while setting up follow-up, showing collaborative intent. Much content was clinician-generated advice rather than emerging from joint brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice by offering multiple options, normalizing self-paced testing, and avoiding coercive language. Emphasized patient control over decisions and adjustments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited small commitments and micro-tests but did not sufficiently draw out the patient's intrinsic motivations or personal values for change. Session emphasized technical strategies over exploring deeper reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and offered selectable options, indicating partnership elements. Tended toward an expert/problem-solver role by delivering a detailed prescriptive plan rather than co-creating the plan from the patient's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supported action planning and small experiments that produced some change statements, but missed opportunities to actively elicit and amplify the patient's own motivations and values. Relied more on providing solutions than on evoking sustained change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Addressed sustain talk directly with compromises and practical limits, avoiding argument and reducing barriers. Could further strengthen by using more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
