# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (opening, safety checks, validating concerns) and avoided blaming language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking (reflected ambivalence, summarized motivations and triggers), though some reflections were brief and could probe deeper emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with measurable goals and follow-up, while briefly checking safety and drinking without losing the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a team with the patient, repeatedly inviting input and shaping the plan around her choices (reduction strategy, delay techniques, check‑in time).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported patient choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and asking which step felt most doable, while normalizing slips and reinforcing ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited patient reasons and change talk that informed the plan, but could more consistently deepen evocation using importance/confidence rulers and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and co‑created a realistic plan, though the clinician sometimes shifted into a more informational/expert stance when discussing NRT and practical tips.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited and reinforced change talk and set actionable steps (e.g., reduce evenings to three), but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen future‑oriented reasons or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and asked neutral follow‑ups, but did not employ strategies like double‑sided reflections or reframing to actively soften sustain language and pivot toward change.
