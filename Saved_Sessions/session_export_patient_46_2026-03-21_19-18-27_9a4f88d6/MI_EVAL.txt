# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance and normalized Ethan's perspective, and honored his choice not to quit by framing interventions around harm reduction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated mixed goals, though could use more complex emotional reflections to deepen connection and explore underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on adolescent drinking and harm-reduction throughout, using readiness scaling, practical tips, rehearsal, and goal-setting to move toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a joint problem-solver—inviting Ethan to co-create lines, rehearsing with him, and tailoring language to his peer-group tone so his ideas shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported choice by offering options, seeking permission before role-play, and reinforcing ownership of the plan through goal selection and confidence ratings.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation (readiness ruler, self-chosen alternating goal, alternatives) but could better amplify change talk, explore values, and draw out stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly conveyed a collaborative stance—asked for Ethan's input, offered choices, and tailored suggestions to his style, reflecting genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete strategies and praise, yet missed opportunities to amplify spontaneous change statements and elicit explicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, focusing on harm reduction instead, though could have used double-sided reflections to more actively resolve ambivalence.
