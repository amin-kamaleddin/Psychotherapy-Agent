# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, repeatedly validating Maria's pain and honoring her request to be heard while avoiding blaming or minimizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately naming internal states and matching emotional intensity while offering a comforting, present stance (e.g., staying on the line).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on imminent suicide risk and means safety with direct assessment, repeated questions about access, concrete steps to remove means, and escalation instructions when needed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by co-creating the text, asking which safety steps felt doable, and offering to stay while Maria acted; occasionally shifted to necessary directive language for immediate safety, reducing a purely client-led stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and inviting Maria's preferences, though at times stronger directive language about emergency services slightly reduced perceived autonomy (appropriate in crisis).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited immediate, self-motivated protective actions (putting pills away, texting Anna) and used readiness scaling, but focused mainly on short-term safety rather than exploring deeper values or sustained motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Maria as a partner in safety planning (solicited input, co-wrote message, offered to remain present), with occasional clinician-led safety directives that appropriately limited full partnership in moments of acute risk.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflections and reinforcement of change talk occurred (protective concern for sister, praise for not acting), but statements were not systematically deepened or amplified to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated despair, but relied mostly on neutral reflections that affirmed sustain talk (e.g., 'nothing works') rather than using double-sided reflections or reframes to shift ambivalence toward staying safe.
