# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard—validates, normalizes, and praises without blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, mirroring ambivalence and responding to subtle emotional cues like nervousness and guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on assertiveness and boundary-setting with a clear, progressive plan (script, practice, roleplays, and next steps).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—co-constructs language, checks preferences, and follows the client's lead during rehearsal and escalation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, asks permission, and solicits the client's reaction to wording, reinforcing the client's control and voice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete commitment and planning language but at times supplies phrasing rather than deeply exploring the client's own motives, values, confidence, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance but occasionally shifts toward an expert role by providing ready-made scripts and one-liners before fully eliciting the client's own wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change language through rehearsal and deadlines but misses opportunities to amplify intrinsic motivations and explore what change would allow the client to feel or do.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and validates guilt and concerns while redirecting toward action and giving deflection lines, but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or deeply explore sustain talk before moving to scripts.
