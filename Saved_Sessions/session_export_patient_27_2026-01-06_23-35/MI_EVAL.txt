# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides clear, nonjudgmental support and normalization from the start, with consistent warmth and no shaming. Validates fear and normalizes waiting to disclose, promoting unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and strong reflective listening, including a complex early reflection capturing ambivalence. Maintains attunement to anxiety with tailored breathing and pacing strategies.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on preparing for disclosure, using interventions (menu, scripts, role-play, pacing tips) that directly advance the target behavior. Resists drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed—clinician asks preferences, invites practice and feedback, and frames themselves as a coach rather than an authority. Multiple decision points reinforce shared power.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering multiple paths, honoring pacing and audio-only preference, and normalizing waiting to tell anyone. Encourages ownership with adaptable scripts and fallback options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivation and planning (rehearsal, scripts) but does not sufficiently probe intrinsic motives, values, or importance/confidence to deepen internal reasons for change. More targeted open questions would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative by offering options, asking which feels useful, and seeking permission to practice; invites adaptation of scripts and joint decision-making. Reinforces client expertise and choice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates multiple opportunities for change talk and concrete planning, drawing explicit statements of intent, but misses chances to amplify and deepen change language via importance/confidence questions and reflective amplification. Could strengthen elicitation of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and validates fears while offering gentle alternatives and options, which reduces defensiveness. Could further use double-sided reflections to explicitly contrast sustain and change talk and move toward action.
