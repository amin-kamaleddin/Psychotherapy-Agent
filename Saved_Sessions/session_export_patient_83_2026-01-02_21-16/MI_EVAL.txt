# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician was respectful and nonjudgmental (opening “I’m here to listen and support you,” normalizing worry). Responses occasionally felt procedural/clinical rather than warmly affiliative.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated perspective-taking and tailored responses (“That’s understandable,” “Good question”), but missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections of ambivalence about long‑term meds.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong focus on the target behavior (safe statin initiation). Provided concrete scripts, safety instructions, dosing guidance, and a clear follow‑up plan — generated actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked with the patient (offered to draft/shorten scripts, incorporated patient wording and preferences). Tended to move quickly to expert information, limiting co‑construction of rationale.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported patient agency (gave scripts, encouraged contacting provider, advised not to stop unilaterally). Could more explicitly reinforce patient decision authority when offering recommendations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of Mai’s own motivations/values; interaction favored information delivery over systematically drawing out reasons for or against long‑term use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative elements present (asked preferences, incorporated input). Could strengthen by asking more open questions about concerns, goals, and how medication fits her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient voiced change statements (plans to call, switch juice, take med at night) but clinician did not consistently amplify or explicitly reinforce these statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician normalized and acknowledged ambivalence (“It’s normal to worry…”), but did not deeply explore sustain talk or use reflective strategies to reduce resistance.
