### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Elena’s position (“I accept where you’re at,” “I accept that quitting may not feel possible now”). Acknowledges protective actions (not smoking around child) and avoids shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (“That’s a heavy place to be… it makes sense you’d feel overwhelmed”), accurate paraphrases of ambivalence. Reflections are sometimes formulaic; missed some opportunities for deeper affective reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on smoking reduction/protection of child. Sequence scaffolded from ambivalence → modest steps → specific implementation tweaks and supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative language and joint problem‑solving (“Let’s figure this out together”), co‑created small, realistic plans (thermos, alarm, “tea first”).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Respects patient choice and limits, avoids pressuring language, normalizes lack of certainty (“I accept that you’re not promising miracles”), supports self‑determined small steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits ideas and options for change (“what would feel most manageable?”; “what ideas do you have?”). Could further probe for reasons, confidence, and commitment language to strengthen evoked change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains egalitarian stance and shared decision making across the encounter; clinician and patient jointly select and refine a doable plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits concrete change statements and small‑step commitments (porch tea, alarm, thermos). Would benefit from more active amplification/summarization of change talk and eliciting a clear commitment rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids argumentation and reframes protective behavior as strength, normalizes relapse risk. Could use more strategic double‑sided reflections and selective highlighting of the change side to shift sustain talk further toward change.
