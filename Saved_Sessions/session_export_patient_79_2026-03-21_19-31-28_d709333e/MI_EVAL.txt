# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—validated frustration, normalized imperfection, and avoided judgment even when the patient was sarcastic or resistant.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections that tracked the patient's feelings and practical concerns, checked back after the breathing exercise, and demonstrated ongoing perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on asthma self-management and safety, moving from symptom inquiry to concrete, actionable steps (breathing, 3‑day inhaler plan, alarms, placement).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited patient input at each step, followed the patient's lead, and co‑created notes, scripts, and practical tools in the patient's voice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice by offering options, asking preferences, and emphasizing voluntary engagement without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete motivation for small change (3‑day trial, handing note to nurse) and reinforced commitments, but could have probed more for internal reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated true partnership—shared decision-making, invited the patient to choose interventions, and co‑constructed plans and wording together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew out and affirmed change talk around manageable steps, yet missed opportunities to deepen and amplify reasons, importance, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk to practical options and a neutral clinic script, though could more actively weaken resistance using double‑sided reflections and selective amplification.
