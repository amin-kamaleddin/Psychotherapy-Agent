# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard, honors Seamus's preferences (teeth first), and offers practical, nonjudgmental support throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking link Seamus's ratings and history, accurately validating his feelings and reducing defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the smoking-related oral health topic in view while appropriately prioritizing the patient's dental agenda, but could have been more strategic in eliciting and consolidating change talk when it appeared.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is cooperative problem-solving: multiple concrete options were offered and the clinician invited Seamus to choose next steps, demonstrating genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, repeated support for the patient's choice with multiple options and no pressuring language, consistently preserving Seamus's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial questions about importance/confidence and past attempts showed interest in internal motivation, but the clinician missed evocative follow-ups to deepen reasons for change or explore discrepant values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treats Seamus as the expert on his life, shares decision-making, and leaves decisions about actions and messaging to him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change language was elicited (ratings, recognition of harms), but the clinician did not amplify or explore it and instead moved quickly to practical supports.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes and respects sustain talk, avoids confrontation, and reframes offers nonjudgmentally to preserve rapport, though this approach may reduce brief future evocation opportunities if documented too rigidly.
