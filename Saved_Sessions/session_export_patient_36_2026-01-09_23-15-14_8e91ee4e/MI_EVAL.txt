# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized setbacks, and affirmed progress in a warm, nonjudgmental way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic statements and some reflections, but there were missed opportunities for deeper reflective listening and exploration of the emotional meaning of values work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a very clear, consistent focus on protecting evenings/not answering non-urgent emails and tied all interventions to that target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was joint with role-play and invitations for input, but frequent ready-made scripts and detailed tips made the session somewhat clinician-led rather than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician offered choices, asked permission before interventions, and repeatedly reinforced the client's right to choose, supporting ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited intentions and concrete plans effectively, yet relied on clinician-provided tools more than drawing out deeper personal motives, values, and client-generated language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a collaborative stance and role-played with the client, but supplying specific scripts and multi-step plans without always checking preferences reduced equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician encouraged and reinforced change talk through prompts, rehearsal, and concrete scripts, though at times supplied solutions instead of eliciting the client's own reasons and wording.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged and reframed worry about disappointing others and redirected to planning, but could add more double-sided reflections to strategically balance competing values.
