# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful stance (safety check, validating skepticism) and avoided shaming while offering practical options. This conveyed clear unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening captured both content and stance, summarizing ambivalence in ways that kept Milan engaged. Reflections and validations strengthened rapport and understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on weekend drinking and moved smoothly from assessment to concrete, measurable planning. The session culminated in a time-limited plan with tracking and support strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was co-constructed with Milan’s input driving the plan; clinician offered tools and support without taking an authoritative stance. Power was shared and the clinician acted as a helper/advisor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed Milan’s right to choose, asked permission before offering information, and offered options rather than directives. These moves supported ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician focused on eliciting Milan’s own reasons and conditions for change, asking targeted questions that drew out intrinsic motivations. Elicited consequences and self-authored steps rather than imposing reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was framed as collaborative, inviting Milan’s expertise and preferences at each step (e.g., which strategies felt realistic). Clinician and patient co-created the plan together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicited and reinforced change talk, asked for concrete commitments, and operationalized responses into a tracking plan (alternate water, skip one party, 3-drink limit). These moves strengthened commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to pros/cons and options, but missed opportunities for advanced softening (e.g., double-sided reflections or early selective amplification of small change talk). More explicit naming of both sides of ambivalence could have moved sustain talk toward change sooner.
