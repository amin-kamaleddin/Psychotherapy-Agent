# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with multiple explicit validations, acknowledged Maria’s distress, and offered to stay and listen, meeting the highest standard for acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely empathic reflections and summaries, mirrored Maria’s language about being 'unbearable,' and named emotions and her protective factor while inviting more sharing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on current suicidal ideation and access to means, directly assessed intent/plan/access, and persistently steered toward immediate safety steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Often invited Maria’s preferences (who to call, what to tell responders) and offered to stay and help, but collaboration was limited when the clinician took responsibility for contacting emergency services for safety.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently acknowledged choices and sought consent (offering options and help), though necessary warnings about contacting emergency services reduced perceived choice in some moments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some attempts to draw on internal resources (noting the little brother, asking to tell more) but did not consistently use targeted strategies to elicit deeper change talk amid necessary safety assessment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used collaborative language and invited input (e.g., scripting messages, asking what to tell responders), but authority shifted toward the clinician when involuntary safety actions were indicated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited and intermittent elicitation of change talk—reflected brief statements like the brother but did not systematically ask open questions to draw out reasons for living, past coping, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Compassionately acknowledged sustain talk and avoided reinforcing it, but rarely used MI-specific techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing, exploring downsides) to actively soften sustain talk before moving to safety actions.
