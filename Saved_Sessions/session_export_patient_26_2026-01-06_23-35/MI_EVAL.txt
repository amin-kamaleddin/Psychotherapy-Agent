# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing stress and affirming efforts. Avoids blaming language and offers practical help rather than criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking with several empathic reflections and responsiveness to priorities, such as childcare stress. Reflections are more cognitive/problem-focused and miss deeper emotional nuances that could deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on securing childcare and reducing smoking/drinking and consistently returns to actionable next steps. Helps prioritize tasks and outlines concrete plans (Head Start steps, scripts, craving strategies).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as joint problem-solving with options tailored to concerns and offers to help script conversations. Much of the content is clinician-generated; stronger collaboration would elicit more of the patient's own ideas and co-create wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supports the patient's sense of choice by offering options and asking which issue to focus on, framing suggestions as choices rather than demands. Affirms the patient's capacity to decide and avoids pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation occurs (asking priorities, prompting steps) but the clinician mainly provides solutions rather than drawing out intrinsic motivations, values, or past successes. Evocative strategies could be used more consistently to elicit the patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Takes a collaborative stance by inviting priorities and offering help with scripting, fostering shared decision-making. At times shifts into directive problem-solving with detailed scripts without routinely checking back for the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language and affirms progress, but largely supplies solutions and scripts instead of strategically eliciting and amplifying the patient's own motivators and confidence statements. Change talk is present but not consistently deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward practical alternatives (e.g., Head Start, neighbor trade), preventing sustain talk from dominating. Could improve by using more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain concerns while highlighting change reasons.
