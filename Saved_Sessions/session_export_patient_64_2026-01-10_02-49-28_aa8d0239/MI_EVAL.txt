# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating worries and normalizing ambivalence throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening with accurate reflections and summaries that mirror the patient's tone and are endorsed by the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on receiving the flu vaccine and related options, with interventions tied to that target and progression to a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative tone and structure: asks preferences, uses the patient's language to co‑create scripts, and invites mutual rehearsal and decision making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by offering options, seeking permission, normalizing ambivalence, and using tentative, permission‑seeking language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation tied to values (protecting grandparents) and generates change statements, but moves to offering solutions too quickly, limiting deeper spontaneous elaboration and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters partnership and power‑sharing by deferring to the patient's preferences, inviting choices, and co‑constructing session content and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits explicit change talk through value‑focused questions and role‑play, but misses opportunities to reflect, amplify, and explore importance/confidence to convert tentative language into stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids escalating sustain talk using double‑sided reflections and alternatives, though at times relies on informational rebuttal rather than more targeted softening strategies (e.g., rolling with resistance, deeper reflections).
