# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opened with an explicit nonjudgmental statement and repeatedly used affirming language (“I’m here to listen and support you…”, “Great choice”, “well done”). Consistently avoided labeling or blaming and normalized discussion of alcohol use (harm‑reduction focus).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking (e.g., “You’re worried about walking and class…”). Reflections were accurate but brief; missed opportunities for deeper affective reflection and more complex empathic statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent clinical focus on alcohol as it relates to ankle recovery, meds, and school. Asked targeted questions (quantity, blackouts, injuries) and gave concrete safety steps tied to presenting problems.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and solicited the patient’s preference at points (“Would it help…? Which one of these feels easiest?”). However, clinician frequently shifted to offering long lists of prescriptive strategies rather than co‑creating the plan with the patient’s language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered options, sought permission, and accepted a modest patient plan (water on Wednesdays + one weekend night). Occasional directive prompts for commitment (“Will you give that a shot?”) were present but overall respected patient choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some patient change talk surfaced (“it sounds like a lot…”, willingness to try water) but clinician did not consistently evoke or amplify these statements. Missed opportunities to ask curiosity questions that would draw out desire/reasons/need.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tone was collaborative at times (permission, offering options), yet partnership weakened when clinician moved into an expert/advice mode with extended lists of recommendations instead of jointly exploring solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited elicitation and amplification of the patient’s expressed concerns. The clinician provided practical suggestions but did not systematically elicit desire, reasons, or commitment language to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some validation of ambivalence (accepted patient’s reluctance and social awkwardness), but missed use of complex/double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence and reduce sustain talk.
