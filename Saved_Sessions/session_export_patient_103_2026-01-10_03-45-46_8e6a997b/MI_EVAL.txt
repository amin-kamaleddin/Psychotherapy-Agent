# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed respectful, nonjudgmental regard—normalizing and validating Nabil’s anger throughout the session. There was no blaming or moralizing, sustaining a warm, accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate empathic statements and reflections matched Nabil’s frustration and work-related worries, reducing confrontation. A couple deeper emotion-focused reflections (shame/embarrassment/fear) could have added depth, but overall empathic work was strong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear, appropriate focus on managing anger and responses to appointment delays, consistently returning to practical strategies and role‑play. Conversation did not drift from the referral target or client goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was highly collaborative—plans and scripts were co‑constructed, the clinician followed Nabil’s lead, and input was solicited before feedback. Role‑play and shared problem‑solving reinforced genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offered choices and respected Nabil’s control (agenda options, script selection, whether to role‑play), avoiding pressuring language. The stance consistently supported client autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited practical motivation and willingness to try strategies but focused more on tactics than on drawing out intrinsic values or confidence. Deeper questions about what success would mean or why change matters would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as a collaborative effort, invited Nabil’s expertise, and repeatedly checked which scripts/practices he preferred. This partnership-oriented stance shaped the session throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicited behavioral steps and practice, leading to some change language (willingness to try scripts, breathing). However, they missed opportunities to systematically elicit Nabil’s own reasons, importance, and confidence to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skepticism was acknowledged without argument and redirected toward practical tools, avoiding amplification of sustain talk. More explicit double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing could have better tipped ambivalence toward change.
