# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician opens with a clear invitation and safety check, stays polite and nonjudgmental, and acknowledges time constraints; however, there are few personalized affirmations or normalizing statements, so warmth feels professional rather than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows adequate perspective-taking around safety and provides clear factual answers, but misses opportunities for brief reflective listening or validating Ingrid’s worry, frustration, and time pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation tightly focused on medication interactions and safe short‑term management with concise, accurate information and actionable next steps, without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Begins collaboratively but largely delivers unilateral medical advice and safety instructions, offering some small patient tasks but seldom eliciting Ingrid’s views or co‑creating a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides multiple options, avoids coercive language, and invites the patient to contact her prescriber, but could more explicitly affirm choice and ask which option the patient prefers in the moment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is predominantly informational with little effort to draw out Ingrid’s own motivations, values, or readiness; few or no evocative questions or scaling techniques are used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with a collaborative stance but the exchange is clinician‑driven and authoritative most of the time, with limited shared decision‑making or negotiation about tradeoffs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient expressed change‑oriented statements and clinician suggested practical options, but the clinician did not systematically elicit, reflect, or amplify Ingrid’s own reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain‑talk and redirected toward safer alternatives and monitoring, though stronger use of MI strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections) could further soften sustain responses.
