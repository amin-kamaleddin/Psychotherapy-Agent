# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes resistance, validating Rowan’s priorities (keeping his job) and feelings about the device. Avoids criticism and models genuine acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and checks for meaning. Captures both practical concerns and emotional tone, showing high-fidelity empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior (charging/using the device) and repeatedly steers toward a small, testable experiment (the keys trick). Questions and reflections consistently return to practical steps and measurable signs of success.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as joint problem-solving: the clinician asks for Rowan’s ideas, preferences, and indicators of success and co-constructs the plan. Language emphasizes shared exploration rather than directive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces choice and control with statements like 'no promises is okay' and 'I’m not judging,' avoiding pressure and inviting the patient’s input. Affirms tentative commitments and supports a self-directed pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits reasons for change and multiple expressions of tentative change talk and asks evocative questions about consequences and outcomes. Could deepen evocation by probing values, using scaling, and systematically amplifying the patient’s motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and non-expert, asking for ideas and inviting choices, but occasionally offers multiple suggestions that subtly guide the patient. Could strengthen partnership by more explicitly handing leadership to the patient and asking them to choose among options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and reinforces several pieces of change talk and explores concrete ideas for action. Would benefit from more frequent amplification and reflective summaries to consolidate change language into stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes sustain talk, reframing resistance into exploratory questions and preventing escalation. Misses some strategic softening opportunities (e.g., double-sided reflections or selective amplification) to tip the balance toward change.
