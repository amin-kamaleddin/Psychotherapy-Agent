### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, honoring Adrian's self‑description and preferences without shaming. Clinician validated concerns and adapted plans to his wishes throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and a strong summarizing reflection that captured ambivalence, building rapport. Missed some opportunities to probe and reflect deeper emotions like shame, embarrassment, or boredom.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing infection risk with concrete harm‑reduction steps. Kept conversation tied to actionable tasks and next steps despite practical constraints.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: offered options, negotiated preferences, co‑created the plan, and adapted materials per Adrian's requests. Role‑play and script edits demonstrated genuine joint problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, and honoring declines (e.g., naloxone/test strips). Frequent invitations to choose reinforced Adrian's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited specific change language and doable steps that produced commitments. Could more explicitly draw out deeper personal motivations, link to values, and use scaling to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Adrian as an equal partner, repeatedly asking which options felt doable and incorporating his preferences into the plan. Power‑sharing was evident in script tailoring and decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and strengthened change talk around practical harm‑reduction actions, yielding concrete commitments. Could amplify expressed motivations, explore importance/confidence, and connect change talk to broader goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, instead accepting continued use and redirecting to harm reduction. Would benefit from strategic softening (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to further diminish sustain talk.
