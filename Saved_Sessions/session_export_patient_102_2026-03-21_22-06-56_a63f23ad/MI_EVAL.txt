# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating from the start, thanking the client and normalizing defensiveness; conveys unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate reflections and summaries that capture content and feeling, followed by open questions that invite further exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol-related risk reduction and values, steering the conversation logically toward specific, feasible safety planning and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicited Milan's input at each decision point and built the plan from his preferences, positioning the clinician as a helper and Milan as co‑author of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by asking permission, offering options rather than directives, and repeatedly affirming that Milan gets to choose what feels doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Milan's own reasons and ideas for safety (open questions, scaling confidence), building on his statements to generate concrete steps rather than imposing rationale.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership by inviting choices and co‑constructing the safety plan, though providing a relatively long list of tips and moving into action planning somewhat proactively shifted some agenda-setting to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited meaningful change language and used good prompts and scaling, but could have more actively amplified, reflected, and summarized emerging desire/ability/reason/need statements before moving to action.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled minimization without confrontation by reflecting and exploring pros/cons, yet could have used more double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence and soften sustain talk further.
