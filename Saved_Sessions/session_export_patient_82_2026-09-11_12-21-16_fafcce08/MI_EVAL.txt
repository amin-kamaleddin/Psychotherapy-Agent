# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental regard and normalized Lukas's conflicting priorities using warm, respectful affirmations that avoided labeling or blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections and summaries demonstrated perspective-taking and frequent checks of Lukas's experience, capturing both content and feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained tightly focused on refusing early alcohol use and progressed logically to a concrete, actionable plan (tell Mark, be the driver, rehearse/record a line).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was co-constructed: the clinician offered options, adapted to Lukas's preferences, and framed the work as teamwork rather than directive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasized choice throughout, offering multiple options and repeatedly asking which approach felt most doable to support Lukas's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Lukas's own motivations, intentions, and expected benefits, drawing change talk from him rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician invited Lukas to choose among concrete next steps and collaborated on wording and practice, demonstrating genuine power-sharing and respect for his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and amplified Lukas's reasons and plans for change, reinforced specific first steps, and linked them to meaningful outcomes like less worry and pride.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and redirected toward change but missed some opportunities for explicit double-sided reflections and more directly naming sustain-talk language to soften resistance further.
