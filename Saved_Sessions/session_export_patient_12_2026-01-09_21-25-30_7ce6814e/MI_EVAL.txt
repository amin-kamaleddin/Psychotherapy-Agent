# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, offering reassurance and avoiding blame. Warmth and validation were present throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate reflections and validated shame and isolation, helping reduce distress. Missed opportunities for deeper, emotion-focused or complex reflections around the patient’s refrain that “no one wants to help me” and flat affect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the relapse, immediate safety, and concrete harm‑reduction steps without drifting. Could more explicitly summarize and set a single time‑limited next‑step agenda and check readiness to commit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and co-created plans, negotiated decisions, and stayed present (offering to remain during grounding and pill‑securing). The interaction felt clearly team‑based and patient‑shaped.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, respecting the patient’s decision not to give details, and checking preferences. Avoided pressure and reinforced patient control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited agreement to small actionable steps but did not sufficiently draw out the patient’s intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence. Limited use of open evocative questions or scaling to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited the patient’s preferences and decision‑making and offered choices for immediate actions. At times the clinician provided many specific solutions rather than eliciting the patient’s own long‑term ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced tentative change talk with praise and encouraged small steps (texts, securing pills), but did not systematically elicit or amplify the patient’s own reasons for change or use scaling. More follow‑up on importance/confidence and values would strengthen cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized relapse, redirected toward safety and action, and offered alternatives to staying stuck. Could enhance by using more double‑sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge ambivalence while highlighting change language.
