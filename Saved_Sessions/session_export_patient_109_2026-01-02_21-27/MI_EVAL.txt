# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and validation (opening empathy, multiple affirmations). Accommodated patient language change and avoided labeling/blame; repeated warm supportive statements across the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (checked safety, acknowledged overwhelm and doubt). Reflections were pragmatic and solution-focused; missed opportunities for deeper affect-focused reflections on discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on restarting activity. Repeatedly structured and reinforced a concrete micro-plan (alarm, clothes by door, 3 breaths, step outside/put on shoes, text “done,” 9:00 reminder and 9:10 check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Operated as a partner: solicited patient choices (time, micro-step), asked permission to check in, and incorporated patient preferences (reminder wording). Patient input shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Supported choice and avoided pressure (offered options, elicited confidence, framed plans as the patient’s decision, normalized doubt, provided noncoercive safety contingencies).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4  
**Comments:** Elicited logistical ideas and micro-commitment (barriers, concrete step, success marker, confidence rating). Less elicitation of underlying values, reasons, or personal benefits for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5  
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance: negotiated wording, invited follow-up preferences, and repeatedly checked patient’s agreement; clinician and patient jointly constructed the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3  
**Comments:** Some change talk elicited (micro-commitment, confidence ratings), but clinician moved quickly to planning without amplifying or exploring desire/reasons/ability; patient change language remained tentative and was not strengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3  
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk/doubt and redirected to small steps, which helped; however, lacked double-sided reflections or strategic rolling with resistance to more effectively soften sustain talk.
