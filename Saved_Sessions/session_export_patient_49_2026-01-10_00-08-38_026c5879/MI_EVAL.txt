# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalized ambivalence, and treated slips as learning rather than failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and accurate summaries with timely validation, but reflections rarely probed deeper emotional nuance or the personal meaning of smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking and tied every intervention to concrete behavioral goals and actionable steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with role-plays and invitations to choose goals, but frequent clinician suggestions sometimes positioned the clinician as the primary source of solutions instead of fully eliciting Hana's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice and control with multiple options and permission-seeking language, framed slips as learning, and avoided coercive or prescriptive pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitments and rehearsals that moved toward change, but relied more on offering strategies than on drawing out Hana’s intrinsic reasons, values, and long-term motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the work as collaborative by offering choices and co-constructing rehearsals, though occasional expert-driven suggestions shifted the balance toward directive guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced concrete change statements and commitments, but used relatively few open questions designed to deepen desire, reasons, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain-consistent remarks and redirected toward harm-reduction and manageable steps, but tended to use practical redirection rather than MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections).
