# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated respectful, nonjudgmental affirmations ("that takes courage," "well done") and encouraged self-compassion after slips. Affirmations sometimes formulaic and not deeply individualized to Maya's adolescent context or linked to specific actions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate, surface-level reflections of content and nervousness (e.g., named barriers and low confidence). Missed opportunities for deeper empathic exploration of fear of rejection, shame, or emotional meaning behind drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on alcohol reduction and a concrete weekly goal. Maintained clear behavioral agenda and stepped through specific, actionable planning and safety checks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (questions, offering a choice between rehearsal or backup buddy), but frequently shifted to directive coaching with clinician-led prescriptions and time-locked tasks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Preserved choice language and nonjudgmental tone; emphasized safety and nonpunitive response to slips. Autonomy somewhat reduced by repeated immediate instructions (e.g., "text Jasmine now," prescribed scripts).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons for change, triggers, and ratings (0–10), producing change talk and a specific plan. Did not consistently amplify or reflect change talk before moving to solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnering (inviting choices), but clinician often adopted an expert/coach stance by supplying phrasing, exact steps, and directing the sequence of actions rather than co-creating them with Maya.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited change statements (concern about grades, agreement to try alcohol-free party) but did not systematically reinforce or expand on them with summarizing reflections or invitations to elaborate.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence ("Ambivalence at 4–5 is normal") but largely moved past sustain talk into planning without using double-sided reflections or strategies to reframe and reduce sustain talk.
