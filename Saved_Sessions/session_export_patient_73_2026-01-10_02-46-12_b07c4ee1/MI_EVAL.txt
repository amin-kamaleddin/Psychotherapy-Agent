# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validating statements and respectful tone; no stigmatizing or blaming language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening with reflections and validations, but many reflections were brief and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional nuance such as frustration, fear, and social loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on tobacco cessation with logical progression from exploration to concrete strategies and rehearsal while staying patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited patient choice, co-created plans, and provided consultative feedback while treating the patient as an expert on her life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking preferences, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivation with open questions and a readiness ruler and reinforced small gains, but could ask more targeted questions to deepen change language and build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Took a collaborative stance inviting input and role-play, though at times offered concrete interventions in a somewhat directive way instead of eliciting the patient's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked and reinforced change talk (readiness ruler, options, affirmations) and elicited concrete steps, but could more consistently amplify and expand DARN statements and elicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized ambivalence while steering toward coping strategies, though more explicit double-sided reflections and strategic summaries could better soften sustain talk.
