# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened with unconditional positive regard, apologized for the wait, and used consistently nonjudgmental language. Validated the patient's experience and avoided labeling or dismissing anger.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used timely, accurate reflections about frustration and practical concerns, showing good reflective listening. Missed opportunities to deepen emotional content (e.g., naming feeling disrespected or exploring relational impact).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on resolving the appointment delay or creating a plan to avoid work consequences. Interventions consistently tied back to that target with minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving, invited patient input, co-created the script, and offered role‑play. Enacted the patient's choices rather than prescribing a unilateral solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's control by offering explicit choices and checking permission before advising. Avoided coercive language and left decisions and consequences with the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete preferences, commitments, and behavioral language through choices and rehearsal. Did not sufficiently probe intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence behind the patient’s goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared control by asking preferences and inviting the patient to compose wording and rehearse. Some clinician-driven scripting occurred relatively early rather than first eliciting the patient's own phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited one-sentence goals and rehearsed concrete action statements that produced commitment language. Did not explore the patient's own reasons beyond practical consequences or assess confidence and past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying resistance and redirected to practical options using neutral reframing and alternatives. Missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections or summaries to more directly shift sustain talk toward change.
