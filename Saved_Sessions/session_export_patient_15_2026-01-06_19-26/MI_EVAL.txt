# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard—offering choices, using nonjudgmental language, and affirming the patient's decisions. They avoided lecturing or blame and explicitly respected the patient's routines.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used solid reflective listening and useful summaries that captured practical barriers and stance. However, reflections stayed at a behavioral level and did not probe deeper emotions, values, or concerns about risk/medication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on daily habits, especially activity, and produced a concrete, measurable action plan (6:40 alarm, Tue/Thu walks, backups). Scaling, contingency planning, and problem-solving kept the conversation goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician acted as an equal partner by presenting options and integrating the patient's preferences and constraints into the plan. The patient’s input shaped choices like timing, reminders, and backup strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supported the patient's freedom to choose, asked permission before offering suggestions, and respected the patient's decision to keep evening wine and TV. This reinforced client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited through doable steps and confidence scaling, but the clinician largely focused on pragmatic problem-solving rather than drawing out intrinsic reasons or personal values for change. Missed opportunities for value-linked open questions limited deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician clearly positioned the work as collaborative—offering options, asking which felt doable, and co-creating the plan with the patient. Power was shared and decisions were patient-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk around activity using scaling, specific commitments, and affirmations. They could have strengthened motivation further by exploring deeper personal reasons, desires, or values linked to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized keeping wine/TV while supporting feasible changes, which reduced resistance. More strategic use of double-sided reflections or eliciting discrepancy could have further softened sustain talk.
