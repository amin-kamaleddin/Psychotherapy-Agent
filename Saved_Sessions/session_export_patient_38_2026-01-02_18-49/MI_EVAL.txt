# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used nonjudgmental, respectful language (safety check, “I’m here to listen and support you”), normalized reluctance (“We don’t have to pick contraception now”), and validated limits repeatedly. Demonstrates unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and emotion labeling (relief, shaken, panic). Reflections were generally brief and surface-level; missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections of ambivalence and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, focused on contraception harm-reduction. Offered choice (process vs brief review) and kept session tightly on practical steps (condom storage, emergency contraception, scripts, small goals).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Elena’s input and permission, offered options, and co-constructed steps. Power was shared; patient set pace and focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong use of permission language and noncoercive goal-setting (“It’s okay you’re not ready…”; small optional steps). Consistently reinforced patient choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited barriers and preferences (asked what gets in the way, which script to practice) but did not systematically evoke Elena’s own motivations, values, or deeper change talk; relied more on providing information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative and respectful, but occasionally shifted to a more clinician-led, informational mode (listing steps and repeating directives) rather than co-creating every element.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient offered change talk (relief, desire for safety); clinician acknowledged and set small goals but did not amplify or deepen desire/ability/reasons (no scaling or targeted probes to strengthen commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and normalized ambivalence. Generally redirected toward manageable safety steps; could have further used double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence.
