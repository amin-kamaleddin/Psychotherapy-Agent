# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized ambivalence, and avoided judgment (e.g., accepting 'not promising' caveats). The clinician treated resistance as understandable and invited collaboration rather than blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used solid reflective listening and double-sided reflections that reduced confrontation and summarized Rowan's stance. Missed some opportunities to probe deeper emotions or consequences to deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior and moved the conversation from problem recognition to specific, actionable steps (alarm time, charger placement, cue). Direction was strong while remaining collaborative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as genuine joint problem-solving with frequent invitations for Rowan's input and feasibility checks. The clinician co-constructed a practical routine that honored Rowan's knowledge of his own context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Rowan's sense of choice by offering options and accepting caveats without pressure. Questions foregrounded his control (e.g., 'Which option feels least awful to try?').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Rowan's ideas and small commitments, drawing out successive client-generated steps (alarm → cue → placement → backup). The clinician avoided supplying reasons and built motivation from Rowan's solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Rowan as the expert and used power-sharing language to invite collaboration. The clinician repeatedly asked Rowan to choose among options and specify what would work for him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and structured multiple small, practical change statements and pressed for specifics (alarm time, cue). Could improve by amplifying and elaborating change talk and eliciting confidence/importance ratings to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used neutral, validating language to contain sustain talk, redirecting to options and practical steps. A higher score would require more strategic double-sided reflections or reframing to explicitly move sustain talk toward change.
