# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental, warm language with explicit positive regard and repeated validation of ambivalence; avoided shaming or lecturing throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured content and some affect; could deepen by using more complex emotional reflections and occasional reflective summaries of unspoken feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on tobacco use and progressively narrowed to a concrete, measurable first step (morning 'tea first' swap); each question moved the plan toward a testable action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled true collaboration by soliciting and incorporating the patient's ideas at every step; decisions and next steps were co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice and honored the patient's control by offering options and avoiding pressure; ensured the patient retained ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the patient's own reasons and past experiences, using the patient's language to build the plan rather than supplying external reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and client-led, with frequent invitations for the patient's preference and joint problem-solving; positioned the patient as the expert in their life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, reflected reasons and supported small steps; could more intentionally amplify change statements and ask for confidence/commitment ratings to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and shifted toward manageable steps effectively; could further use double-sided reflections and specific reframes to pivot more strongly toward change.
