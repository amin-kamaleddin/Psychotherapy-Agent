# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating and normalizing the patient’s frustration throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, often complex reflections that captured affect and meaning, matched the patient’s terse style, and opened space for expansion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on improving check-ins and securing employment support, repeatedly steering the session toward concrete next steps like drafting a portal message and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the encounter as a joint effort by co-creating language and offering options, repeatedly inviting the patient to select and modify wording to fit priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforced patient choice with multiple options, low-effort alternatives, and by providing a copy/paste message while declining to send it for him, preserving control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical motivation and concrete requests (employment support, availability) but did not substantially explore deeper intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited input at multiple junctures, co-wrote the portal message, and left decisions to the patient, demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encouraged change talk and helped develop a usable plan (portal message and follow-up), though further elicitation of reasons and importance/confidence would have strengthened commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed sustain talk without confrontation and offered low-effort alternatives to reduce resistance; could have used more explicit double-sided reflections earlier.
