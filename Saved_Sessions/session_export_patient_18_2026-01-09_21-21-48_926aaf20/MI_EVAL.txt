# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing the visit and validating the teen's cooperation while avoiding labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and supportive listening, but used relatively few deeper reflective statements to capture emotion or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on teen alcohol use with concrete skill-building (refusal lines, exit strategies) and role-play to keep work goal-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the session as a joint effort and tailored content to the teen's requests, yet often offered clinician-generated scripts instead of co-creating language first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission to role-play, and providing multiple phrasing choices rather than insisting on one approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurred by asking what the teen wanted and inviting practice, but the clinician relied more on information and scripts than eliciting the teen's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displayed a collaborative stance with invitations and shared activities (role-play), but could strengthen partnership by prompting the teen to generate and refine her own lines before offering examples.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered skills and asked about visit goals which may indirectly support change, but did not systematically elicit or amplify the teen's own desire, reasons, ability, or commitment to avoid drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing potential sustain talk and provided practical alternatives and refusal language, though missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence.
