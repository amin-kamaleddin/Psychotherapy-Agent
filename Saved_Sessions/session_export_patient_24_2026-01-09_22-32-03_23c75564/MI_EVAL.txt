# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validated competing goals, and framed the plan as a low-threat experiment. Language of appreciation and normalization reinforced positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective summaries captured ambivalence and surprise at the numbers. Reflections were more content-focused than affective—deeper reflection of feelings would raise this to a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on drinking in relation to training, sleep, and performance and progressed to concrete goal-setting and implementation planning. Used scales and targeted questions to maintain focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co-constructed with repeated invitations for input and confirmation of choices (replacement behaviors, accountability, tracking). Clinician honored client preferences and used inclusive language throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by asking permission before advising, offering options, and framing the plan as an experiment the client could accept or modify. Avoided pressuring language and emphasized client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used importance/confidence scaling and asked why a 5 not a 3, eliciting motives tied to marathon goals and surprise at consumption. Could have probed deeper intrinsic motivations, values, and past successes to draw out more commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally positioned expertise within the client and involved him in decisions, choosing accountability and tracking preferences. A fairly specific intervention was proposed early; eliciting more of the client's own ideas before offering a plan would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked and reinforced change talk using scaling and planning prompts, which tied change to marathon goals and sleep. Missed opportunities to amplify, summarize, and elicit stronger desire/ability/commitment language to deepen preparatory change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not confront minimizations, but used limited targeted techniques to soften sustain talk (e.g., double-sided reflections or strategic reframing). More deliberate reflections that acknowledge sustain talk while pivoting to reasons for change would help.
