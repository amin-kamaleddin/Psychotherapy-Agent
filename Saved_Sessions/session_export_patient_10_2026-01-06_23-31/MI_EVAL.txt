# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalizing difficulties and avoiding blame throughout the interaction to convey unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with repeated validations of the patient’s feelings (e.g., accurate reflections and apologies) across the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on attending interviews and overcoming childcare barriers, moving the conversation to concrete, stepwise actions like drafting messages and screening tips.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative: the clinician co-wrote and iteratively revised messages with the patient, invited preferences on tone/pay/location, and framed work as joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options and honored the patient’s choices without pressure, preserving the patient’s sense of control over decisions and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of choices and affirmation of strengths, but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than drawing out deeper personal motives, values, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as an equal collaborator by repeatedly asking which options felt most helpful and incorporating the patient’s input into plans and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced strengths and elicited concrete next steps and acceptance of tools, but missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motives and elicit stronger, time-specific commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used softeners and practical alternatives, though lingering reluctance to ask friends could have been further explored with empathy-driven reframes or double-sided reflections.
