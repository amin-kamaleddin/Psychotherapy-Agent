# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, validating ambivalence and avoiding criticism. Examples included affirming language and supportive tone around sensitive topics.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of Margaret's concerns. Could increase use of more complex reflections to deepen exploration of emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related risk and moved toward a concrete action plan (rules, start date, reminders). The session stayed goal-oriented without sidetracking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal problem-solving partner, co-creating texts, role-playing, and soliciting Margaret's input on wording and supports. Decisions were jointly made rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Margaret's right to choose, offered multiple options, and asked permission when suggesting strategies. This preserved her sense of ownership over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client-generated motivations and practical ideas (three-glass rule, friend nudge, start date) but sometimes offered concrete solutions that shifted away from pure elicitation. More sustained elicitation of personal values and reasons would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership and power-sharing by repeatedly inviting Margaret's preferences and co-constructing the plan. The clinician conveyed that expertise about her life resided with Margaret.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated it into a feasible plan, including confidence rating and supports. Could increase selective reflection and amplification of spontaneous change statements to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and did not reinforce it, but used few explicit strategies to soften it (e.g., double-sided reflections or deliberate ambivalence elicitation). Opportunities were missed to more actively reduce commitment to sustain talk.
