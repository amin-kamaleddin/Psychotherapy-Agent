# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing difficulty and affirming efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and validation (grounding, acknowledging 'freeze'), but missed opportunities for deeper reflective paraphrase of underlying shame and self-deprecation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing gambling-related financial harm and consistently steered interventions toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-wrote messages, offered multiple options, and revised wording to match the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices and invited the patient to pick actions, but sometimes used prescriptive scripts without explicit permission which reduced autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitments and barrier planning, but did not sufficiently evoke deeper intrinsic motives or link values to the chosen actions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally partnership-oriented with shared decision-making, though some moments were more clinician-led with long lists of steps rather than eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, converting patient statements into concrete commitments (bank message, friend-check, scheduled-send).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and focused on harm-reduction, but did not use double-sided reflections to explicitly explore and resolve ambivalence.
