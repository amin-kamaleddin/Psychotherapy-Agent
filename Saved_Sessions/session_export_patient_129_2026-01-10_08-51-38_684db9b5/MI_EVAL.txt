# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated nonjudgmental acceptance and positive regard (explicit 'no judgment', validating annoyance) and repeatedly offered choice, preserving respect even when the patient was resistant.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used timely reflections and perspective-taking that acknowledged feelings and reduced defensiveness, though reflections stayed at a surface level and did not consistently probe deeper emotions or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on substance use and parent conflict and steered to actionable outcomes (assessment, harm-reduction, and a concrete family plan), but moved to problem-solving relatively quickly before fully exploring motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by inviting input, co-creating the script, and adapting recommendations to the patient's preferences, though providing concrete wording occasionally shifted toward clinician-driven content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice and control by offering menus of options, asking permission, and honoring the patient's refusals and preferences throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some eliciting questions and allowed the patient to state goals, but did not sufficiently draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or discrepancies to deepen personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated power-sharing through asking permission, adapting plans, and co-creating solutions, while occasionally providing expert suggestions that slightly shifted the balance toward clinician direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-oriented language (reduce arguing) and offered harm-reduction, but missed opportunities to deepen desire, reasons, or commitment using scaling, summaries, or further evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledged resistance, but did not consistently use advanced softening strategies (double-sided reflections or strategic reframing) to resolve ambivalence.
