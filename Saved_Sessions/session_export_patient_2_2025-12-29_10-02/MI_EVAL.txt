# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed nonjudgmental acceptance (acknowledged life stressors, respected patient's boundary about alcohol, normalized difficulty). This stance helped the patient move from guarded to cooperative.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflective listening (e.g., “evenings feel overwhelming…”, “wanting to be around for your son”) supported engagement. Could deepen with more complex emotion‑focused reflections to capture guilt/fear and underlying meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, sustained focus on smoking reduction and moved session to a concrete, time‑bound experiment (two weeknights, hide pack, gum/tea, timer).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan was clearly co‑constructed (clinician solicited patient's reward choice, tweaks to reminder text, and accepted her specific strategies); power was shared throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and avoided coercion (accepted “not promising more than that,” asked permission for reminders/contacts); patient retained ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited patient's reasons and past successes (busy periods, gum/tea) and linked them to values (being there for son). Could further probe the personal meaning of motherhood to amplify intrinsic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative language and check‑ins; occasional moments where clinician assumed responsibility (e.g., “I’ll send an 8:45 pm reminder,” “I’ll text Chioma”)—these were preceded by permission but could be more explicitly framed as options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk and increased readiness (5→6/10) and converted motivations into a practical experiment. Missed some opportunities to elicit a concise commitment statement and to amplify thin change talk into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or confronting sustain talk, respected boundaries around alcohol, and normalized slips as “data not failure,” keeping the conversation oriented toward actionable change.
