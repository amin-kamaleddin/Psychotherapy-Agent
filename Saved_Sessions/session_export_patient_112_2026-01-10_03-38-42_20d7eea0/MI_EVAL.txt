# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating (e.g., normalizing worries and affirming planning/effort), demonstrating clear unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and some reflective listening that acknowledged anxiety and ambivalence, but the clinician often shifted to information-giving instead of deeper, more complex reflections of the patient's feelings and identity tied to smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete, behaviorally relevant interventions (NRT, strategies, practice quit date, and scheduled check‑in) throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt like joint problem-solving with co‑created checklists and role‑play, though the clinician at times led with solutions and long lists rather than pausing for the patient to prioritize choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported the patient's right to choose by offering options, asking permission, and inviting the patient to pick dates and decide whether to use certain strategies, respecting decisional control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some planning and change-related statements (practice date, scripts) but relied heavily on providing strategies instead of probing for deeper reasons, values, and intrinsic motivators behind the patient's desire to quit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with repeated invitations for input and co‑construction of a practical plan, but occasionally shifted into an expert/instructional mode that reduced shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and reinforced some commitments through co‑planning, yet missed chances to amplify spontaneous change language or elicit strength and importance (e.g., scaling or reflective follow-ups).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and offered concrete strategies, but tended to respond with information and action plans rather than using double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing to reduce sustain talk.
