# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established strong unconditional positive regard from the opening line, consistently validated Viktor's perspective and avoided blame or confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used solid reflective listening and paraphrases that captured ambivalence and practical concerns, but could deepen affect-focused reflections to explore underlying emotions like shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept appropriate focus on reducing conflict and safety with concrete strategies, but could more directly link substance-use behavior to Viktor's broader values and discrepancy for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was highly collaborative—offered options, followed Viktor's lead, and co-constructed a plan rather than imposing a protocol.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized Viktor's control and choice, framed suggestions as optional trials, and avoided coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete, client-generated steps for cooling off and safety but did not sufficiently evoke deeper motivations or long-term reasons to change substance use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine partnership by inviting Viktor to choose topics, shape the plan, and rehearse options, keeping power-sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk about reducing fights and using time-outs, but missed opportunities to amplify desire/ability/reason/need language and to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized ambivalence, used double-sided reflections, and redirected the conversation toward pragmatic harm-reduction strategies.
