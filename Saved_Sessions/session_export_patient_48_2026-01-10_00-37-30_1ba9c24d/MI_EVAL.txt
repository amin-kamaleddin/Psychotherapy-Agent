# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys nonjudgmental positive regard—normalizes difficulty and removes self-blame—so the patient feels respected and understood.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, ongoing reflective listening that mirrors both content and affect, validating the patient’s experience repeatedly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on increasing brief physical activity and consistently narrows to an actionable, time‑limited goal with concrete supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine two‑way problem‑solving, soliciting the patient’s ideas and co‑creating the plan while offering tools only when invited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering options, inviting selection, and seeking consent for supports and follow‑up without pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits intrinsic motivation by drawing out values, prior successes, and linking small actions to personally meaningful benefits.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative and honors the patient’s expertise, repeatedly inviting choice and input on how to proceed and be supported.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk—asking about values, small realistic steps, and moving toward concrete commitment with reflections and questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally reframes and redirects sustain talk toward solutions and small steps, though more strategic double‑sided reflections or selective amplification could further resolve ambivalence.
