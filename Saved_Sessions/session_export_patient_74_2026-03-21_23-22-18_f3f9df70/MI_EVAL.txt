# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent nonjudgmental, respectful regard with normalizing statements and validating summaries, accepting Linda's ambivalence without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections captured content and affect (guilt, relief), demonstrating strong perspective-taking, though deeper, more complex emotional reflections were occasionally missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on parental modeling and practical harm‑reduction steps, with questions consistently moving toward concrete, behavior-linked goals tied to values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was explicitly collaborative—clinician solicited Linda's ideas, built on them, and co‑created the plan rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed Linda's choice and pace, avoided pressure, and supported self‑efficacy by asking about strengths and preferences for small steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited intrinsic motivations and values (protecting kids, reducing guilt) and invited reflection on signs of success, but could more consistently deepen motives and elicit commitment/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned the patient as the expert and used open questions to share power in decision-making, repeatedly deferring to Linda's choices about which steps to try.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician skillfully elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and doable steps, though could more selectively amplify desire/reason/need language and push toward stronger commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided argument but often restated sustain talk without using double‑sided reflections or reframes, missing opportunities to de‑emphasize reasons for maintaining smoking while pivoting to change.
