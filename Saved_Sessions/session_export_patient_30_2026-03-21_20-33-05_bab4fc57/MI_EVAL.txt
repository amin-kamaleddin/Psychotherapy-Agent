# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened with a clear nonjudgmental stance, repeatedly validated the patient's perspective, and honored his preference to try lifestyle change first. The clinician avoided labeling or blaming and accommodated requests, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrasing of concerns, showing good perspective-taking. Some reflections were information-heavy and did not deeply explore emotions or underlying values, so empathy was very good but not maximal.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the decision about cholesterol medication and safe monitoring throughout the session. Conversation threads were tied back to options, monitoring, and a co-created plan without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as partners to co-create tangible products (checklist, script, PDF), asked for specific edits, and integrated the patient’s preferences into the plan. Language emphasized joint problem-solving and equal input throughout the encounter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's right to choose, asked permission before offering information, and provided options that preserved control (e.g., printable checklist, short monitored trial). The clinician avoided pressure and reinforced patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used open questions to elicit practical preferences and information needs, but did not sufficiently draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence. The session elicited pragmatic reasons rather than deeper, values-based motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly invited and implemented the patient’s input (clinic phone, wording, signature line) and confirmed preferences before finalizing documents, reflecting genuine partnership. The clinician treated the patient as an expert in his own life and co-created the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk with open questions and options, eliciting some tentative change language (diet/exercise, conditional openness to a short trial). Missed systematic opportunities to amplify, use scaling, or reflect and summarize change statements to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing, normalized concerns, provided factual information and alternatives, and reframed resistance toward manageable options. Could have used more double-sided or strategic reflections to shift emphasis from sustain reasons toward change, but sustain talk was handled well overall.
