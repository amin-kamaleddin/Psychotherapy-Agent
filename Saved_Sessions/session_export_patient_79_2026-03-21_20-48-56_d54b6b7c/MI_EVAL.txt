# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating exhaustion and accepting limits, which reduced defensiveness and enabled collaboration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and validation that helped the patient feel heard, but did not fully explore deeper emotions like anger, feeling misunderstood, or chronic burden.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on asthma self‑management with concrete, behaviorally specific steps and a logical flow from assessment to plan and outcome metrics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a clear joint effort—invited the patient's input, drafted language in his style, offered alternatives, and treated him as an expert on his life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's choice and control, respected boundaries about lectures, and offered options rather than imposing a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited meaningful wins and a concrete trial commitment, but could have more strongly drawn out personal reasons, values, confidence, and a clearer commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited preferences, co‑constructed messages, deferred to the patient's wording and boundaries, and explicitly shared power throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited concrete change talk and reinforced tentative commitments (telehealth, 90‑day refills, trial), yet missed chances to deepen personal motivations, past successes, and stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sarcasm and skepticism without arguing, normalized concerns and offered feasible options, but could have used more double‑sided reflections or strategic juxtaposition to further shift ambivalence.
