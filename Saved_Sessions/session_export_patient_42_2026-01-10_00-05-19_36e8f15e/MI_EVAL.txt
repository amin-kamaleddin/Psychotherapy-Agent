# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language, normalized feelings, and affirmed efforts throughout the session. Demonstrates strong unconditional positive regard with little to correct.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating social concerns and emotional reactions. Could deepen evocative reflections of the emotional meaning behind sustain talk rather than moving quickly to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking and related social situations with organized steps and concrete next actions. The session stayed goal-directed and advanced toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was cooperative and invitational with role-play and shared planning. The clinician sometimes led with scripts and stepwise instructions instead of eliciting the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, avoided pressure, and normalized slips as information rather than failure. Strongly supported the patient's sense of control and ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of situations and small commitments occurred, but much of the momentum came from clinician-provided options. Missed opportunities to draw out deeper personal motives, values, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative, inviting preferences and co-constructing the plan with the patient. At times the clinician offered multiple concrete solutions that took the lead rather than first asking for the patient's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced actionable change talk and small experiments, and highlighted successes. Could have more strategically amplified the patient's own reasons for change and elicited stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk without arguing, offering alternatives to preserve social bonds. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to shift emphasis toward change talk.
