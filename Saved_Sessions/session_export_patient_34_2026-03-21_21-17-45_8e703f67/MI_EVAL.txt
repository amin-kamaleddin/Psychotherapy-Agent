# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental regard and respect, offering validations and honoring the client's requests. Unconditional positive regard was evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate paraphrasing that captured Dylan’s practical worries about housing and records. Reflections remained mostly factual and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the conduct referral and moved the conversation toward concrete actions and outcomes. The clinician offered realistic routes and produced specific artifacts (statements, witness messages, timeline).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was clearly collaborative, with co‑construction of drafts, shared decision‑making, and repeated solicitation of Dylan’s input. The clinician implemented Dylan’s edits and checked for final sign‑off.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Dylan’s right to choose by asking permission, offering multiple paths, and complying with his wording preferences. The clinician avoided pressuring and made help contingent on Dylan’s decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did little to elicit Dylan’s internal motivations for changing drinking or behavior, focusing instead on contesting the referral. Missed opportunities to explore values, ambivalence, or broader reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated a strong partnership stance by inviting preferences, asking permission to explore options, and collaborating on drafts. Power was clearly shared and Dylan’s expertise about his priorities was privileged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicited very little language favoring change in the target behavior; questions centered on contesting or mitigation rather than desire, reasons, or commitment to change drinking. Only commitment related to contesting was drawn out.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Dylan’s minimizations and did not amplify sustain talk, but made limited use of double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to shift sustain talk toward change. There were a few gentle contrasts about mitigation requiring some responsibility, but more redirection was needed.
