# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating from the opening through reflections, honoring Lukas's priorities (grades, playing time, friendships).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening and accurate reflections that expanded ambivalence, though a few deeper emotional reflections (e.g., anxiety about social rejection) were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on peer-influenced alcohol use and refusal skills, repeatedly linking questions and interventions to concrete next-time strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed plans by letting Lukas choose lines and rehearse, but the clinician occasionally shifted into instructive script-teaching which reduced full co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Lukas's autonomy throughout, framing suggestions as options and inviting him to choose and practice the lines he preferred.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation by asking what matters and what would help, but then moved quickly to supplying solutions rather than drawing out or amplifying Lukas's change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered a collaborative stance with invitations and checks, though early expert suggestions and scripted responses tilted the interaction toward a coaching role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted change language about priorities and consequences, but did not sufficiently amplify, reflect, or explore those reasons and confidence before offering strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided endorsing it, but did not systematically use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or selectively reinforcing change) to actively reduce sustain language.
