# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard — validates successes and frustrations without judgment and normalizes difficulty. Preferences are acknowledged and Marco is never blamed or shamed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and helpful reflections (e.g., pride, frustration, 'that sounds tiring'). Could increase depth and frequency of evocative emotional reflections and probe implicit values more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing orange juice intake and progresses logically from exploration to concrete implementation steps. Avoids drifting and ties each turn back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is mutual and co-constructed: clinician solicits preferences, offers options, and refines the plan with Marco's input. Marco’s ideas drive the plan while clinician contributes expertise to support them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, and checking readiness/confidence (0–10 scale). Reinforces Marco’s ownership of decisions and avoids pressuring or prescribing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation and turns reasons into concrete commitments and if‑then plans. Would be stronger with more emphasis on drawing out personal values and deeper intrinsic reasons before offering multiple solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions clinician and patient as partners by asking what Marco wants to focus on and making decisions collaboratively. Clinician asks permission and helps operationalize Marco’s chosen strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk well, uses confidence scaling and asks for commitments and specific start dates. Could further strengthen by linking small successes to larger values and eliciting pros for change more frequently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk nonjudgmentally and offers palatable alternatives and fallback plans. Could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly link ambivalence and lean less on clinician-driven solutions.
