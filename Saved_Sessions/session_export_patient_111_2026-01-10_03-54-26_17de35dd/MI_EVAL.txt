# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Richard’s perspective with supportive language and affirmations. No evidence of blaming or shaming and multiple validating statements reinforced a warm, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that captured annoyance, ritual, and relational motivation, building rapport. Could have probed and reflected more on anger/irritability and the deeper emotional meaning of the morning ritual to deepen connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking behavior with concrete, behaviorally specific steps and scaling questions to create momentum. Balanced exploration with task-oriented planning and linked steps to Richard’s values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine partnership by inviting input, incorporating preferences, and negotiating next steps. Offered assistance only when Richard was willing, resulting in a co-constructed plan rather than an expert-driven approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing that the clinician would back Richard’s decisions. Avoided pressuring language and reinforced ownership of any change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic motivation by exploring values (wanting to keep his wife) and asking open questions that produced a self-directed slow plan. Missed opportunities to probe deeper into the personal meaning of that motivation and to more strongly amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shared power and expertise, asked permission before offering suggestions, and used Richard’s wording when refining the plan and backup. Co-constructed solutions and allowed Richard to shape interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through open questions, reflections, and confidence scaling, and reinforced a gradual plan and increases in confidence. Could more actively amplify relational change talk and follow up to deepen desire, reasons, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and used reflections and strategic redirection to acknowledge resistance without arguing. Few explicit double-sided reflections were offered that might have more actively juxtaposed sustain talk with change reasons to shift ambivalence.
