# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating language that normalizes Naomi's experience and affirms her courage. No shaming or blaming; acceptance is steady throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking with reflective statements and emotional validation that tune into Naomi’s body-first anxiety and hesitation. Clinician checks in repeatedly about comfort and listens closely to her concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on managing social/performance anxiety with concrete, achievable strategies tied to photography shoots. Exchanges repeatedly return to specific practiceable actions and a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as cooperative problem-solving with tailored scripts and preference checks, but frequent offering of scripted language and technical fixes sometimes shifts the clinician into an expert role. Overall collaborative but could invite Naomi to generate more solutions before providing examples.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, asks which options feel doable, and avoids pressuring language, reinforcing Naomi’s ownership of change. Clinician affirms her right to pick what fits and supports client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits behavioral intentions and small commitments (breath, practice reps, choosing lines), but explores deeper intrinsic motives, values, and reasons for change only minimally. Good surface-level evocation; would benefit from more open questions probing importance and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative stance with invitations to choose and tailor wording, sharing power in planning, but at times leans toward offering ready-made solutions rather than eliciting Naomi’s ideas first. Strong partnership that could be strengthened by more joint generation of language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete commitments and practice plans, producing explicit change talk and commitments from Naomi. However, the clinician emphasizes solutions over exploring deeper motives and values, limiting richer change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges doubts and normalizes sustain talk but typically moves quickly into problem-solving rather than using reflections or double-sided techniques to explore ambivalence. Limited work to elicit and resolve reasons for reluctance.
