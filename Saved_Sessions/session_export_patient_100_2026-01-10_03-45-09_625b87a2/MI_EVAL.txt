# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating Maya's priorities and using a warm, respectful tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening captured facts and ambivalence, though reflections focused more on behaviors than deeper emotions like FOMO, embarrassment, or guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on weekend drinking and concrete behavioral goals, with each exchange advancing toward actionable steps tied to Maya's priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative—clinician invited Maya's input, adapted strategies to her preferences, and co-created specific wording and plans that Maya shaped.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was emphasized repeatedly with permission-seeking, option-framing, and respect for Maya's choices, reinforcing her control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked motivations by linking change to Maya's college and family goals and using evocative questions, but could probe values and elicit more spontaneous DARN language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated power-sharing and asked permission before advising, but offering a large menu of clinician-generated options and drafted wording sometimes leaned toward expert-driven solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk well using questions and scaling, producing concrete commitments, though could amplify and summarize change language more and elicit clearer commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and avoided arguing, but used few double-sided reflections and moved quickly to solutions rather than exploring sustain-talk drivers like peer pressure and FOMO.
