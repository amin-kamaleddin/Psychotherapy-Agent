# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated respect and nonjudgmental acceptance, affirmed the patient's honesty and choices, and avoided blaming language, supporting unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some perspective-taking and supportive language were present, but there were few reflective or affect-laden statements and the interaction quickly moved into information-giving rather than deeper reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related behavior and harm-reduction throughout, tying every intervention back to the patient's once-a-month/one-drink goal and follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely collaborative with questions and patient input on plans, but the clinician frequently supplied ready-made scripts and lists instead of eliciting the patient's own language and solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported patient choice by offering options, accepting the patient's preference not to quit, and inviting the patient to choose code words and check-in timing without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some elicitation of goals and support, but the encounter was largely clinician-driven with information and skills provision rather than sustained open-ended questioning to draw out the patient's personal reasons and values for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician framed the interaction as collaborative and asked permission for questions, yet the provision of directive advice and scripted lines limited co-creation and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, securing a specific short-term goal and concrete planning, but could have probed more for the patient's own reasons and values to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and normalized harm-reduction, which prevented reinforcement of sustain talk, though using more explicit double-sided reflections could further soften sustain talk while validating the patient's perspective.
