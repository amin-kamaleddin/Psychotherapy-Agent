# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates respect and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing concerns and offering support. Could show more individualized warmth and explicit unconditional positive regard to reach a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes brief empathic gestures but relies more on information-giving than reflective listening. Would benefit from more complex reflections and open questions to explore feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use as it relates to soccer and scholarships and moves toward concrete planning and role-play. Consistently ties feedback and strategies back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Alexei's input, offers tailoring and role-play, and engages as a teammate. Could more consistently elicit Alexei’s own solutions before providing examples and more actively reflect his contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports autonomy by offering options and asking permission for planning while avoiding coercive language. Should routinely ask permission before offering advice and explicitly reinforce Alexei’s right to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Links drinking to Alexei’s goals and invites planning but largely supplies strategies rather than eliciting internal motivation. Needs more open questions and reflections to draw out desire, ability, reasons, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a collaborative feel through co-creation opportunities like tailoring lines and role-play. To improve, elicit Alexei’s ideas first and summarize his chosen plans and commitments more consistently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforces goals and offers concrete refusal lines that support planning and some change talk. Misses multiple chances to elicit and amplify Alexei’s own change language, relying more on clinician-led guidance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confronting minimizations but uses balancing statements that may inadvertently validate sustain talk. Lacks double-sided reflections and pivots that would acknowledge both sides and gently steer toward change.
