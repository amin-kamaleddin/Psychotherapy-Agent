# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating with reassuring statements that convey positive regard; could use more personalized affirmations and explicit normalization of ambivalence to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Makes several accurate empathic reflections and acknowledges worry and uncertainty; there are missed opportunities for deeper reflection on family history and the distress underlying low confidence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing risky drinking and improving contraceptive reliability, tying every intervention to concrete, measurable actions and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functions as a genuine joint enterprise with drafting, inviting input, and accepting patient tweaks; the plan is clearly co-constructed and includes follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respects choice by asking permission and offering options, and affirms the patient's right to be unsure; somewhat prescriptive early on, which slightly reduces the sense of pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits concrete plans and choices but leans toward offering solutions rather than probing the patient's own motivations, values, or the meaning of her family history and ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites collaboration and shared decision-making, allowing the patient to choose and tweak scripts; sometimes assumes an expert role by prescribing specific strategies instead of eliciting ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports and amplifies some change talk and invites a small goal, but does not sufficiently elicit or deepen desire, reasons, ability, need, or stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively redirects sustain talk toward concrete planning and safety, but rarely uses MI-specific softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to validate ambivalence while guiding toward change.
