# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard—normalizes tension, affirms resilience, and validates guilt and helplessness without shaming. No blaming language and frequent validating statements reinforce strong acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, timely reflective listening and perspective-taking, matching the patient's flat, resigned tone and summarizing ambivalence. Reflections and safety attunement show strong empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on reducing drug use through concrete harm‑reduction steps and consistently brings conversation back to a doable behavior. Moves ambivalence toward a time‑bound, specific plan (ten‑minute walk with supports).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative—invites the patient's ideas, preferences, and integrates them into the plan. Co‑construction of supports (alarm, playlist, app) and mutual problem‑solving keep the client central.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly emphasizes the patient's choice and expertise, offering options and avoiding pressure. Repeatedly leaves decisions to the patient and reinforces ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits the patient's own motivations and solutions rather than prescribing, building on self‑generated strategies (alarm, voice note, playlist). Draws out meaningful reasons and small victories to develop a plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as shared and equal, explicitly locating expertise with the patient and inviting their ideas about supports and steps. Demonstrates robust power‑sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively notices and amplifies tentative change language and asks consequence/difference questions to strengthen change talk. Could increase reflections of commitment language and use confidence/commitment scaling or obstacle planning to deepen and consolidate change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reflects sustain talk without arguing, then gently pivots to options and planning, which prevents escalation of resistance. However, frequent verbatim repetition of sustain phrases risks keeping them active; more double‑sided reflections or strategic reframes would better soften sustain talk.
