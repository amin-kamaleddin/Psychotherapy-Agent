# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive; normalized struggle and affirmed safety-focused choices multiple times (e.g., “We can work together…”, validating reasons for use and the Narcan plan). Avoided pressure to quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent accurate reflections capturing content and affect (safety/fear, relief/escape); used clarifying questions to deepen trust. Missed a few opportunities for more complex reflections probing the deeper emotional drivers of “escape.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on harm‑reduction (Narcan plan) throughout; moved conversation from goals to concrete, time‑bounded actions (timing, script, alarm) and repeatedly asked planning questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑created the plan using collaborative language and offers of help (e.g., “Would you like to try a two‑line script together now?”); avoided lecturing and involved the patient in actionable choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit respect for patient choice (repeatedly affirmed “not forcing any quit plan”); emphasized safety and the patient’s control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions and reflections to draw out patient motivations and specifics (readiness, concrete plan, timing); elicited multiple statements of intent and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist framed work as a joint effort (“we can work together”), offered to draft scripts and set alarms together, and maintained a supportive, team stance throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly amplified and reinforced change talk (patient’s 10/10 readiness, explicit plan); translated motivation into specific steps and supported commitment language.
