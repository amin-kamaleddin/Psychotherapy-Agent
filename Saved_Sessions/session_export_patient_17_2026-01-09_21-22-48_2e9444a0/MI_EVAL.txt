# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, validated shame and affirmed the patient’s efforts. Explicitly cautioned against harsh self‑labels and offered gentler wording.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections early on but moved fairly quickly into planning. Missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections exploring shame and emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout and repeatedly tied discussion back to quitting and practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a collaborative exploration, seeking input and integrating the patient’s edits into plans and scripts. Tone consistently framed clinician and patient as partners working toward achievable steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and accepting the patient’s pace and decisions without pressure. Reinforced ownership of decisions (e.g., accepting no quit date yet).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivation using readiness questions, past attempts, and personal reasons, producing meaningful change talk. Could deepen evocation with more open, provocative questions to strengthen intrinsic motives and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked which part to start with and invited wording changes—but at times provided multiple ready‑made scripts and a multi‑step plan that shifted toward clinician‑driven solutions. Greater elicitation of the patient’s own strategy ideas would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (readiness scale, reasons, past successes) and invited commitment to small steps. Could amplify and probe change statements more to strengthen desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk, reframed harsh self‑labels, and quickly shifted to coping strategies to reduce sustain talk’s power. Would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and fewer directive tips at once to better resolve ambivalence.
