# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing skepticism and offering practical nonmoral solutions. The clinician accepted the patient's stance and provided options without shaming or confronting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good understanding with validating reflections and attention to the patient's pragmatic concerns. Deeper emotional exploration and more complex reflections about the personal meaning of smoking were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on managing smoking and workplace coping strategies throughout the session. Every turn tied back to concrete steps, dosing, safety, and a time-limited plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively in practical problem‑solving, soliciting preferences and co‑creating templates and scripts. The interaction leaned clinician-led when detailed medical instructions and dosing were provided, though it remained responsive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized patient choice with permission language, trial options, and multiple alternatives. Encouraged experimentation and respected the patient's decision-making about if and when to stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation by framing interventions as experiments and asking about willingness, producing concrete commitments. Could strengthen evocation by asking about personal values or broader reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership shown via choice and co-created resources, but much of the session was clinician-driven with authoritative instructions on dosing and regimens. More invitations for the patient to generate solutions would deepen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited small commitments and operationalized steps that produced change talk (trialing gum/lozenges and a patch). Missed opportunities to deepen and amplify change language through open questions about reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and instead normalized ambivalence, offered pragmatic alternatives, and redirected focus to manageable solutions. Could further improve by using explicit double-sided reflections that name both liking smoking and emerging openness to change.
