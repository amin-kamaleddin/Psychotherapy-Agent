# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains consistent unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental language, normalization of ambivalence, and caring safety checks. The warm, supportive stance is evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates attentive perspective-taking with accurate reflections and summaries and validates the patient's feelings. Reflections are generally pragmatic and surface-level, with fewer deep or complex emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use with specific, actionable goals and concrete next steps. The clinician repeatedly orients the session back to measurable behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as joint problem-solving, offering options, co-creating a tracking plan, and drafting messages for the patient to choose. Language positions the clinician as an ally and decisions reflect the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language. The clinician explicitly invites the patient to choose days, wording, and reminder time to foster ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation with open, change-oriented questions and invitations to commit, producing useful change talk and action planning. Could be strengthened by systematically probing personal reasons, values, and importance/confidence and by amplifying change statements more often.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative, co-creative stance by inviting choices and letting the patient shape the plan. More explicit language acknowledging the patient's expertise about her life would further enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, operationalizing it into a concrete plan and supporting commitment language. To reach a top score, the clinician could deepen and amplify the patient's own motives, values, and confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and redirects sustain talk with exploratory questions, option framing, and normalization to reduce defensiveness. There is limited use of strategic techniques like double-sided reflections to explicitly reframe sustain talk toward change.
