# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating distress and normalizing ambivalence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking by naming core feelings and strengths, but reflections tend to stay at the surface and rarely probe deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on safety and the target behavior with urge ratings, safety checks, and concrete post-walk contingency plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly framed as a joint effort with co-created options, frequent checks of readiness, and consistent solicitation of the patient's preferences at decision points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering multiple options, normalizing non-compliance, and explicitly reinforcing that steps are the patient's to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical change talk and small commitments by asking about past helpful strategies, but could more directly elicit deeper motives, values, and importance/confidence ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently models a collaborative stance—asks permission, invites choice, and co-constructs plans that defer to the patient's expertise about her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with small, doable actions and affirmations, but could more systematically amplify varied change talk categories (desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and often pivots toward change with alternatives and some double-sided language, though it misses opportunities to explicitly explore exceptions and reframe sustain statements.
