# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated consistent nonjudgmental positive regard, validating Ingrid's situation and offering help without blame. Tone remained warm and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed strong perspective-taking and responsive listening, tailoring responses to Ingrid's time pressure and safety concerns. Questions and plain-language repetition reflected accurate understanding of her circumstances.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear clinical focus on safe management of citalopram/phenelzine, washout timing, tapering, and emergency signs. Clinician consistently returned to actionable safety instructions and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative, repeatedly soliciting Ingrid's preferences and co-creating the exact wording for GP messages and follow-up. The clinician adapted materials to her needs and time constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used invitational language and offered options rather than directives, supporting Ingrid's ownership of decisions and next steps. Multiple choices and questions reinforced her autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some action-oriented planning language was elicited, but the clinician did not probe Ingrid's internal motives, values, or confidence. Opportunities to ask brief open questions to deepen intrinsic motivation were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned Ingrid as an active decision-maker by asking preferences and co-developing messages she would use. Power-sharing was evident in repeated invitations for input and selection among options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively supported practical planning and produced client change language by scripting and offering wording the patient accepted. However, deeper elicitation of Ingrid's personal reasons, confidence, or commitment was limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided amplifying sustain talk and redirected the brief ambivalence toward safety and planning. He could have further used double-sided reflections to normalize and explore any lingering reluctance.
