# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates nonjudgmental acceptance (e.g., neutral reflections, affirming tentative efforts) and normalizes hesitation without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that validates the patient’s stance, though reflections could probe deeper emotions and values (e.g., family priorities, tiredness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays well-focused on specific target behaviors and moves logically to a concrete action plan (dry weeknights, rehearsal lines, reminders) without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a true partnership with the patient selecting strategies and the clinician integrating his preferences into the plan rather than adopting an expert stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supports autonomy with statements like 'I respect that' and repeated invitations to choose strategies, reinforcing the patient’s control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good elicitation of the patient’s own reasons and concrete steps, but missed opportunities to link those steps to deeper personal values or use rulers to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician co-creates the plan with the patient by asking what feels doable and inviting input on specifics (lines, rehearsal timing), demonstrating genuine partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicits and amplifies change talk (concrete goal of no weeknight drinking) but could further probe desire, importance, and confidence to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is managed well through reflective responses and avoidance of confrontation, though more strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing) could better leverage ambivalence.
