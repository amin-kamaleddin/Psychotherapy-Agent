# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation (e.g., opening empathy, praising realistic efforts), avoiding blame or labeling throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid empathic listening and accurate reflections of Sofia’s experience, but often shifted quickly to action without deeper exploration of minimized vulnerability and emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors (stop rumors, reduce avoidance, immediate safety) and repeatedly oriented the conversation to concrete, proximal goals and steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—solicited preferences and negotiated a plan—but relied heavily on ready-made scripts and a clinician-driven multi-step plan rather than co-constructing language with Sofia.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Sofia’s choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and affirming her right to accept or decline suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation (asked what Sofia wanted to change and used a confidence rating), but did not sufficiently develop or amplify those motives into stronger change talk or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and invited input on the plan, yet at times tilted toward an expert role by providing detailed scripts and solutions before eliciting Sofia’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language (goals, tentative commitments, confidence), but frequently moved to offering solutions instead of reflecting and amplifying Sofia’s change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated avoidance as understandable and redirected toward safety, but did not consistently use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to actively soften sustain talk.
