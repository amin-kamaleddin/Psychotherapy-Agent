# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—validated priorities, normalized reactions, and honored the patient’s request not to be lectured. Language was warm and nonjudgmental throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with targeted reflections and summaries (importance/confidence, side effects). Repeated empathic statements captured the patient’s feelings and needs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained appropriate focus by opening on smoking risk but quickly prioritized dental care and gave brief, permission‑based NRT info. The clinician did not, however, progress to a concrete small smoking‑related step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like joint problem‑solving—agenda set with the patient, choices offered, and scheduling decisions co‑constructed. The clinician avoided an expert‑dominant stance and centered patient preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforced choice and permission, honored declination to discuss quitting, and offered to note options for later. Clear statements preserved patient control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used scaling and a follow‑up question to elicit motivation but did not sufficiently probe or deepen the patient’s emerging change statements or elicit commitment language. Evocation was present but shallow.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance by asking what to focus and inviting input on logistics and options. Missed opportunities to co‑create a small, patient‑led behavior change goal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked core evoking questions and offered low‑side‑effect options, but did not amplify or deepen tentative change language or explore reasons behind the importance rating. Change talk was initiated but not reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided reinforcing sustain talk, honored the desire to pause, and reframed medication concerns with alternatives. Could have used more double‑sided reflections and deeper exploration of side‑effect experiences.
