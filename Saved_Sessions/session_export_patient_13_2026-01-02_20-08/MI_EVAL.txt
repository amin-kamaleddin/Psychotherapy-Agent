# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently nonjudgmental and affirming (“That sounds frightening — thank you for sharing,” “You’re doing the right thing asking for help,” “You’re doing a brave thing — I’m here with you”). Normalized fear and acknowledged effort without blame or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and emotional validation (e.g., “I hear it’s been getting bigger…,” “That sounds really scary — you’ve been carrying a lot”). Reflections tied content to feeling and appeared to reduce resistance and increase disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on getting a medical exam/ultrasound. Linked medical concern to actionable next steps (call script, clinic text, partner text, clinic search) and maintained direction throughout the exchange.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited patient preference and choice (offers of multiple first steps, A/B/C options, asked about wording). Deferred execution when patient not ready, co-constructed plan and kept patient in control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Generally supported choice and asked for preferences, but occasionally used slightly directive urgency language (“getting it checked soon is important”) that could be perceived as pressure. Would benefit from more autonomy-prefacing when noting clinical concern.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Elicited barriers and feasible actions (childcare, cost, calling vs. texting) and got concrete commitments to small steps. Missed opportunities to elicit deeper intrinsic reasons/values for checking (e.g., parenting role, what she’d gain).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 5
**Comments:** Positioned self as helper/resource (offered to draft scripts, asked ZIP code, negotiated whether to send or just provide info). Language and options consistently shared power and fostered joint problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Reinforced nascent change talk and practical steps (patient agreed scripts, partner text, clinic info). Could have more actively elicited commitment and confidence language (e.g., scaling, “what would make you more confident?”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4
**Comments:** Validated fears and barriers and offered lower-effort alternatives (one-line text, clinic sliding-scale options). Missed some strategic double-sided reflections that explicitly name ambivalence while tipping toward change.
