# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental—validated Viktor’s pressure to attend and framed suggestions as optional while expressing appreciation for attempts to try the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate reflections and perspective-taking that helped Viktor feel heard, but did not probe deeply into emotions, guilt, or personal values to deepen empathic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on after-work substance use and reducing fights, steering the conversation toward concrete strategies and a follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited Viktor’s input and choices and framed work as joint, but frequent checklists and prescriptive instructions shifted parts of the interaction toward an expert-driven tone.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Viktor’s choice and control by offering options, normalizing skepticism, and framing the plan as his to accept or decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited a client-selected experiment tied to Viktor’s goal of fewer fights, but missed opportunities to ask for his reasons, confidence ratings, or to amplify intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—asked permission and co-created the plan—but at times offered multiple specific action items that reduced the session’s client-led quality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced modest change talk and secured specific commitments for small experiments, though could have amplified reasons, confidence, and long-term motivations to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or confronting sustain talk and offered low-threat experiments to protect autonomy, but could more strategically use double-sided reflections to highlight discrepancy.
