# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—validating, normalizing ambivalence, offering choices, and avoiding shaming throughout the session. Repeated offers of support and referral further reinforced acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates frequent, accurate perspective-taking and some reflective listening (e.g., validating worries and tailoring suggestions). However, the interaction leans toward information-giving with fewer deep or complex reflections exploring emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear behavioral focus on sustainable habits and binge-risk management, repeatedly returning to concrete targets like exercise structure, meal routine, and urge plans. Translates values into specific, measurable action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as genuine partnership—clinician solicits patient input, offers alternatives, and adapts recommendations based on the patient's preferences. The plan is co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering formats and multiple options, asking the patient to pick/tweak goals, and avoiding coercive language. Emphasizes realistic, gentle options and preserves the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits values and reasons for change and links them to actionable steps (e.g., function over appearance). Yet the clinician often shifts to providing strategies rather than persistently drawing out the patient's own motivations, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance—offers choices, invites patient selection of scripts/goals, and adjusts portions and plans based on feedback. Power-sharing and respect for the patient's expertise are evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Routinely elicits and reinforces change language (evocative questions, values selection, non-scale progress). Missed opportunities include more consistent reflection and amplification of the patient's sustainability statements to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects it with concrete harm-reduction strategies (scheduled meals, permission-exposure, urge plans). Could further strengthen this by using more double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence.
