# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a warm, nonjudgmental stance (validations, normalization, respectful language throughout).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good understanding of the client's perspective and summarizes benefits/risks, but reflections are mostly functional rather than deeper affective or amplified reflections that explore emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on coffee use and concrete, measurable actions (tracking, skipping late cups, BP monitoring) and consistently guides next steps and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in collaborative problem-solving (invites data collection/interpretation, adapts tools to phone use) and clearly shares power and planning with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice with low-pressure options and willingness checks, but sometimes offers prescriptive steps without first eliciting the client's preferred approach or asking permission before advising.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits behavioral commitments via tracking invitations, but relies heavily on didactic information and rarely draws out intrinsic motivations, values, or the client's own language for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as joint (we'll look at patterns, I'll help interpret) and asks permission for tools, though could strengthen co-creation by more often inviting the client's choices among options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk through tracking and small experiments, but missed opportunities to use open invitations and amplification to deepen and build commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a low-pressure stance and reframes the process as data-gathering to avoid reinforcing sustain talk, though more double-sided reflections could better acknowledge ambivalence and pivot toward change.
