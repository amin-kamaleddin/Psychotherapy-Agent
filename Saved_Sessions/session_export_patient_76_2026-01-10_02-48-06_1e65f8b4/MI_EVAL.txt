# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalized distress, offering support and gratitude, but occasionally shifted to formulaic, task-focused instructions rather than deeper, individualized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were clear empathic validations and sensitive safety checks, but few nuanced reflections capturing numbness, exhaustion, or bodily ache, missing opportunities for deeper affect-focused understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on self-harm and immediate safety throughout, consistently returning to tool removal, contacts, and concrete urge-management steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative—clinician and patient co-created the safety plan, personalized messages, and negotiated contacts, with the clinician implementing the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by asking permission, offering choices of coping strategies, and emphasizing the patient's control over whether to use or save the drafted plans and messages.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited evocation of internal motivation: the clinician was mainly informational and solution-focused and did not elicit the patient's values, reasons for change, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally framed the work as collaborative and shared power in planning, but sometimes moved into expert mode with lists and specific techniques instead of eliciting the patient's own ideas and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Actions like safety planning elicited some tentative change language, but the clinician did little to deliberately elicit, reflect, or amplify the patient's own reasons, desires, or commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and offered alternatives which reduced sustain talk, but did not use double-sided reflections or reframe the function of cutting, and suggested options (e.g., drawing on skin) risked reinforcing sustain-talk logic.
