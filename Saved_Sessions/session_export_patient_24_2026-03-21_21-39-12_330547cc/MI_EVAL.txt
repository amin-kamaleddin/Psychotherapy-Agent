# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, offering explicit appreciation and normalizing Daniel's experience; avoided labeling and accepted his perspective throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate perspective‑taking, summarizing ambivalence and validating the function of drinking; could have probed emotional nuance (surprise, frustration) more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use in relation to marathon training and progressively moved to a specific, measurable experiment with observable outcomes and coping plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a joint enterprise with repeated invitations for Daniel's input, co‑construction of the plan, and clinician acceptance of Daniel's edits and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Daniel's choice and control by asking permission, offering options rather than directives, and letting him select nights, wording, and supports.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out concrete ideas, supports, and confidence ratings, but could further elicit intrinsic reasons and more strongly link change to personal values and goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership through shared decision‑making, offering options and drafts only after inviting input, and deferring power to Daniel throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and shaped change talk with evocative questions and confidence scaling, but could have amplified and reflected tentative change language to deepen desire and reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected toward a low‑risk experiment, yet could more often use double‑sided reflections or ambivalence summaries to further reduce sustain talk's pull.
