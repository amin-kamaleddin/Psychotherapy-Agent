# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly communicated unconditional positive regard and normalizing language (“I accept where you are,” “I’m with you,” “We can move slowly…”). No blaming or dismissal; acceptance was clear and consistent across turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective‑taking (e.g., “waking up short of breath and feeling like you’re failing…”). Slight weakness: some repetition of reflections without deeper probing of shame/identity (“feeling like a failure”)—could explore emotional nuance more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on target behaviors (asthma management, inhaler concerns, home‑based trial). Clinician steered toward a concrete, time‑limited one‑month test with measurable outcomes (dust‑mite covers, dog out of bedroom, peak‑flow tracking, weekly check‑ins).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treated patient as an equal partner, soliciting preferences and offering choices (clinic vs home demo, which supports she wants). Plan was co‑constructed and responsive to patient’s stated needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician affirmed patient’s right to decide and avoided pressure (e.g., “I’ll support whatever pace you choose,” framed interventions as optional experiments). Respected patient’s reluctance to add medication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out patient’s own ideas and motivations (asked what matters most, what proof would convince her, elicited specific trial and targets). Frequent, sustained evocation of change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership demonstrated—clinician offered hands‑on help, shopping/setup support, and weekly check‑ins while deferring to patient choices. Expertise shared collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified desire/reason/need (willingness to try bedroom changes, peak‑flow tracking). Missed opportunities to consolidate commitment/confidence (no readiness/confidence scaling or explicit commitment statement and problem‑solving for barriers).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double‑sided reflections (“you want stability…AND you don’t want to rely more on meds”). Mostly neutral validation; could more actively soften sustain talk by exploring costs of staying the same and highlighting discrepancy toward change.
