# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and accommodating patient preferences (e.g., monthly check‑ins). Some responses felt procedural and could be warmer and more personalized rather than moving quickly into a checklist.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses reflective listening and perspective‑taking at key moments (e.g., "I hear your skepticism") and reframes stress constructively. Could deepen empathy by reflecting the patient's minimizing tone and underlying concerns with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on lowering BP with concrete monitoring and a 4‑week trial, providing practical steps and targets. The clinician prioritized giving a plan and numeric goals early instead of first eliciting the patient's agenda and internal motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint process, offers options, and invites preferences (log format, check‑in frequency). However, use of a structured medical template and early numeric targets introduced an expert‑driven element that reduced co‑creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and control, offering options and accepting the patient's decisions without coercive language. Leaves monitoring and change decisions with the patient and respects their preferred level of involvement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited evocation—asks assessment questions but rarely elicits the patient's own values, reasons, or motivations for change. The session shifts to providing information and a plan rather than drawing out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative: asks permission, offers choices, and invites input on tools and follow‑up. Early prescriptive planning and numeric targets, however, shifted some power back to the clinician rather than fully co‑creating goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and obtained small commitments (daily log, cut 1–2 drinks), but did not sufficiently explore or amplify the patient's own reasons or the meaning of those commitments. Missed opportunities for evocative follow‑ups to deepen and increase authentic change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes sustain talk while offering alternatives, yet frequently shifts quickly to advice and targets. Could better use double‑sided and summary reflections to gently expose ambivalence and reduce sustain‑talk dominance.
