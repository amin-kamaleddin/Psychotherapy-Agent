# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used nonjudgmental, respectful language and validated Liora's experience while calmly checking safety; no labeling or dismissive comments were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both factual and emotional content and attending to bodily sensations and access to means.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on self-harm and safety, producing a concrete, actionable safety/urge plan and checking logistics and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by asking preferences and co-constructing a plan, but at times led the agenda by proposing a fairly complete toolbox rather than first eliciting the client's solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed choice and sought consent before offering suggestions, provided multiple options, and validated doubt to reinforce client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used standard elicitation techniques (confidence scale, barriers, doable strategies) but did not probe deeply into intrinsic motives, values, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited input and offered choices, framing the work as collaborative, though occasionally leaned toward expert delivery of specific interventions rather than maximizing client-generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced tentative change language and used follow-ups and a confidence scale, but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen change talk by asking more evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation, but used limited strategic moves (e.g., few double-sided reflections or exploration of costs) so sustain talk remained relatively prominent.
