# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance (e.g., validating statements and normalizing slips). The clinician maintains warmth and respect throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing ambivalence and frustration. Could use more complex or amplified reflections to deepen emotional resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation and moves toward concrete, actionable steps (start day, coping strategies, slip plan). The session progresses in a focused, stepwise manner toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions as an equal partner, inviting choices, feedback, and practice so the plan is co-created. The tone and practices support joint problem-solving rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports client autonomy by asking permission, offering multiple options, and emphasizing Linda's control over choices. Normalizing slips preserves agency and reduces shame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and intentions using evocative questions and rulers, drawing out reasons for change and a concrete behavior step. Could further deepen motivation by exploring underlying values, past successes, and amplifying client language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—asks permission, offers options, and invites Linda to select strategies and practice scripts. Power-sharing is evident as Linda's preferences shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk (importance = 8) and affirms chosen steps like delaying the first cigarette. There were missed opportunities to further amplify reasons, strengths, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, acknowledges social pressures, and offers practical strategies and normalization of slips. Could employ more targeted techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, selective amplification) to more actively diminish sustain talk.
