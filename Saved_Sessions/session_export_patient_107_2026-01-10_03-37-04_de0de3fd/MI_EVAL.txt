# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing ambivalence and accommodating the patient's preferences (e.g., skipping home BP). The tone conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing mixed benefits and worries and offering practical, empathy-driven suggestions. Responses matched the patient's emotional tone and concerns across multiple turns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on caffeine use with a concrete, measurable 1–2 week experiment and specific monitoring targets. Recommendations and follow-up plans were directly tied to the target behavior and avoided drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative—preferences were solicited, options offered, and plans co-created (e.g., logging method, rescue rules). Clinician shared expertise while inviting the patient's input rather than dictating steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and control by offering multiple options and framing suggestions as voluntary (e.g., cut one cup, switch to decaf, skip BP). Language preserved the patient's authority to tailor or adjust the experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurred (agreement to try tracking, logging preferences), but the clinician mostly offered solutions instead of eliciting the patient's own reasons, goals, or confidence. Missed opportunities to ask open questions that would draw out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as a partnership by asking permission, offering to send tools, and checking the patient's willingness to try options. The clinician positioned suggestions as collaborative choices rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician proposed practical small steps and an experiment but did not sufficiently elicit or amplify the patient's own change language, importance, or confidence. More open questions and scaling would strengthen client-generated reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively validated sustain talk and offered compromise options (timing changes, decaf, rescue rules) without confronting the patient. Redirection worked well but leaned somewhat advice-driven rather than using double-sided reflections to highlight discrepancy.
