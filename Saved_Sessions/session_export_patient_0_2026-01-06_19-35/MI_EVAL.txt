# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating Adrian's priorities and normalizing ambivalence. No shaming or lecturing; clinician treated minimization and guardedness with respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflective listening and perspective-taking captured both sides of ambivalence and emotional tone. Reflections were often complex and mirrored Adrian's language, lowering defensiveness and eliciting honest change talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing meth use-related risk and moved logically from ambivalence to a concrete, measurable plan. Questions and reflections kept momentum toward actionable harm-reduction steps and an immediate next step (texting Mike).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as joint problem-solving with the clinician soliciting preferences, offering options, and co-constructing the plan. Tone positioned the clinician as a helper rather than an authority, and decisions were shaped by Adrian's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Adrian's right to choose and normalized partial commitments, inviting him to select or tweak options. Even when offering templates, the clinician checked back to preserve client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly elicited Adrian's own motivations and strategies rather than supplying reasons to change, amplifying client-generated motives and solutions. Open questions and reflections encouraged Adrian to articulate and choose his own plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally conveyed that expertise resides with Adrian and invited his input, creating a collaborative stance. Occasionally offered more prescriptive wording/options (text templates), though these were checked back to preserve client ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively and skillfully elicited and amplified change talk, converting vague desire into specific, doable steps and measures of success. Follow-ups deepened and operationalized change talk into a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and used double-sided reflections to acknowledge defenses while redirecting toward harm-reduction. Reframed sustain statements into practical small tests and options, reducing entrenchment.
