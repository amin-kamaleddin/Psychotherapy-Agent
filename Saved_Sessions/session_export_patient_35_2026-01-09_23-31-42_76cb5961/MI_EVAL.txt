# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and normalized compromise, validating Elena’s constraints and coping without blame. Repeated affirmations reinforced a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening were evident in summaries of her life context and the role smoking plays for her. Reflections matched Elena’s tone and helped deepen trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing child exposure through practical steps, and each exchange moved toward a concrete, implementable action plan. The session remained goal-directed without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was authentic joint problem-solving with options framed and decisions shaped by Elena’s input. The clinician deferred to her expertise about daily routines when building the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were emphasized and multiple options offered rather than prescriptions, supporting Elena’s control over the plan. Language consistently reinforced her right to choose harm-reduction steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited practical motivation tied to protecting her son and drew out specific plans, but did not probe deeper into broader values or longer-term reasons for quitting. Evocation was strong for exposure-reduction but less developed for cessation motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was clearly collaborative and co-constructed, with the clinician inviting choices and troubleshooting together. Power-sharing was evident as Elena selected strategies and guided planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited incremental change talk and used confidence scaling and doable-step questions, reinforcing commitment language for harm-reduction. However, there were missed opportunities to amplify and explore Elena’s explicit change language about quitting itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and accepted Elena’s rationale while steering toward protective behaviors, reducing reinforcement of sustain talk. More strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, selective discrepancy) could further soften sustain talk about not being ready to quit.
