# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with affirmations and nonjudgmental, normalizing language; no labeling, criticism, or minimization observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, paraphrasing the patient's content and emotions and mirroring priorities to guide the conversation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target issue (citalopram–phenelzine safety) and purposefully progresses to concrete goals like a script, checklist, and role‑play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the work as joint problem‑solving, elicits the patient's preferences, and co‑constructs materials rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offers multiple options, asks which the patient prefers, and respects wording and approach choices, reinforcing the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out preparatory reasons and strengths but could further evoke deeper personal motives and use questions to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treats the patient as a collaborator, shares decision‑making power, and solicits input to shape the plan and rehearsal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits preparatory change talk (planning, intent, strengths, rehearsal) and concrete next steps, but could better amplify and elicit explicit commitment or importance/confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes uncertainty into problem‑solving, though could more often use double‑sided reflections or explicit normalization of ambivalence.
