### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Kenji’s experience, avoiding judgmental language. Frequent validating statements reduce blame and reinforce that ambivalence is understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with frequent, nuanced reflections that mirror ambivalence and affect. Therapist checks understanding and captures both sides of Kenji’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol with practical, action-oriented questions. The conversation moves logically from concerns to options to a concrete commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as joint problem-solving with repeated invitations for Kenji’s input and choice. Language and planning position clinician and client as partners throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors Kenji’s choice by offering options and asking him to select what feels doable. Emphasis on ownership of the plan reinforces autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical motivations and solutions (work, health, strategies) but misses deeper exploration of intrinsic motives and values. Could strengthen by eliciting personal meaning and using importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently uses inclusive language and distributes expertise, inviting Kenji to co-create the plan. Decisions are collaboratively chosen and negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits several instances of change talk and a concrete first step, including a commitment to remove alcohol tonight. Missed opportunities remain to amplify, reflect, and consolidate that commitment (e.g., confidence/importance rulers or affirmations).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and routinely reflects sustain-side language while normalizing worry about caving. Largely uses neutral reflections rather than strategic moves (double-sided reflections or reframing) to reduce sustain talk.
