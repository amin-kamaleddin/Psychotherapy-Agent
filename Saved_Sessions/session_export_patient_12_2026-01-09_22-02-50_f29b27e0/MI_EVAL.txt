# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed unconditional positive regard and normalized Amira’s experience (e.g., 'you’re not a bad person'), avoiding blame or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening captured both content and affect (regret, loneliness, helplessness) and was timely, helping Amira feel heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on an immediate safety/connection step (a very small, doable action) and repeatedly oriented discussion toward concrete planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited co‑creation (offered to draft the message, asked preferences) but at times repeated specific options and phrasings instead of eliciting more client‑generated alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through offering choices, asking what felt manageable, and framing steps as options rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Some internal motivation and small commitments were evoked (willingness to send a text), but the clinician could have probed values, past successes, or used scaling to deepen motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative and patient‑centered, yet repeatedly offering specific scripts slightly narrowed the client‑led element and could be balanced by inviting Amira to propose wording first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and supported small change talk and concrete planning, but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen commitment (e.g., reflective summaries, importance/confidence scaling).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and normalized using reflections and double‑truth framing, though more deliberate double‑sided reflections or strategic pivots could further soften sustain talk and shift toward change.
