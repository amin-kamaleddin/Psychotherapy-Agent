# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and avoided judgment, normalizing Dylan's perspective. Offered practical help (drafting, reviewing, role‑play) in a respectful manner.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used empathic reflections and check‑ins that acknowledged Dylan's feelings and stress. Could have probed deeper into underlying fears, minimizing, and values to strengthen empathic engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on preparing for the conduct hearing with concrete tasks and next steps. Did not expand the direction to address longer‑term alcohol use or harm‑reduction goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative—offered to co‑draft, refine, review, and role‑play while following Dylan's lead. Language and actions supported joint problem‑solving throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Dylan's right to choose, offered options, and respected his control over documents and decisions. Made clear boundaries (e.g., cannot sign/submit) and repeatedly asked permission to help.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did little to elicit Dylan's intrinsic motivations, values, or reasons for change, focusing instead on external problem solving. Missed opportunities for open questions or reflections to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally adopted a collaborative stance, offering choices and inviting Dylan to decide how to proceed. Occasionally shifted into advisory mode without explicitly checking for permission.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to evoke Dylan's desire, reasons, or confidence for changing alcohol‑related behavior; session stayed procedural and defensive. Did not follow up brief admissions or prompt for change‑oriented language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and kept the conversation neutral, which prevented escalation of resistance. However, MI strategies to actively soften sustain talk (e.g., double‑sided reflections or strategic rolling with resistance) were not used.
