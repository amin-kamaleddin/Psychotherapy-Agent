### Acceptance

**Comments:** Maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, warm stance, validated Eric’s perspective, and avoided shaming; repeatedly reassured him that trying or failing is okay.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and checked emotional/safety state, but reflections were more pragmatic and surface-level rather than exploring deeper feelings about wife/doctor pressure.

### Direction

**Comments:** Stayed tightly focused on smoking with concrete harm-reduction strategies and a specific, time-bound plan (Friday after 6 pm).

### Collaboration

**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and egalitarian, offering choices and co-constructing the plan while inviting ongoing feedback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized autonomy with phrases like 'no pressure' and framed suggestions as optional, supporting the patient's right to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made explicit attempts to draw out motivation (rulers, prior attempts) but relied more on suggestions/education and did not sufficiently probe values or amplify emergent change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Eric as a collaborator and expert on his life, asking permission, offering options, and letting him choose the strategy and timing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered small experiments and asked about past attempts, but missed opportunities to reflect and amplify brief change statements (doctor, wife, cough) to elicit stronger desire or reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized skepticism, using neutral information and harm-reduction framing to reduce resistance; could strengthen use of double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
