# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly communicated respect and normalized difficulty (“I’m glad you reached out,” “It makes sense you feel stuck,” “You’re taking a solid step — I’m rooting for you”). Frequent affirmations (“nice work,” “great work”) supported self-efficacy, but many felt formulaic rather than individualized to Dante’s specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Offered normalizing statements and content paraphrase (“You’ve had a lot shift…”), but few reflections of affect or ambivalence. Missed chances to reflect Dante’s worry and hesitation (“I‑I…”, “nervous I’ll skip it”) or to deepen emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on increasing activity and concrete planning. Progressed logically from problem identification to measurable action (Tue/Thu ~8:15pm, 10→5 min, alarms, spouse reminder) and arranged a follow-up check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Invited patient input (offered options, asked which felt doable, used confidence ruler, offered follow-up). At times moved quickly to a clinician-driven checklist and could have elicited more patient-generated solutions or had the patient summarize the plan to enhance ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Regularly acknowledged choice and preferences (asked which option, confidence scale, offered check-in). Occasionally adopted a directive tone when pushing a specific plan and repeatedly restated the checklist rather than negotiating supports the patient preferred.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some elicitation of preferences and confidence, but limited efforts to draw out Dante’s own motivations or reasons for change beyond surface statements. Did not sufficiently amplify or explore the patient’s expressed past success (“I used to work out pretty good”).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Collaborative moves present (options, questions, follow-up). Relationship generally supportive and team-oriented, but frequent clinician-led recaps and prescriptions reduced opportunities for truly shared planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Clinician failed to amplify or develop Dante’s change talk (e.g., repeated “I used to work out pretty good” was not explored or reflected to strengthen commitment). Missed opportunities to elicit statements about importance, reasons, or desire in a way that would increase change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some normalization and reframe (“treat it as data not failure”) helped reduce self-criticism, but sustain-talk (worry about skipping, need for a nudge) was not frequently reflected or reframed to reduce resistance. More complex reflections of ambivalence would help soften sustain talk.
