# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect for Amina's boundaries, explicitly honoring requests about privacy and information sharing. Avoided labels, pressure, or judgment throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate, calming reflections of Amina's perspective and privacy concerns. Could deepen empathic responses occasionally with more complex reflections of underlying emotions or inferred concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on engagement and safety, repeatedly returning to safety checks and the agreed brief follow-up plan. Did not push for immediate behavioral change and kept the session on the intended target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a collaborative partnership by asking for and following Amina's preferences and avoiding a prescriptive stance. Co-created the contact plan and used inclusive language to support shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering concrete choices (timing, message vs call, one coping idea) and reminding Amina she could change her mind. Reinforced that Amina was in charge of what was discussed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made limited attempts to elicit internal motivation beyond an initial open question and then appropriately backed off after refusal. Could have used very low-intensity evocation (permission-based curiosity, brief scaling) to draw out any small movement toward change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Amina's preferences and positioned her as the expert on her needs, sharing power over decisions like contact method and timing. Followed her choices and checked back to ensure plans matched her wishes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to evoke or deepen change talk after the initial question; the clinician appropriately avoided pressing but missed low-risk opportunities for brief elicitation. Could use single permission-based curiosity or a quick scaling question to elicit even brief change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively neutralized sustain talk by validating privacy and avoiding amplification of resistance, using acceptance and boundary-respecting statements. Could strengthen this further with occasional double-sided reflections that name both privacy concerns and small signs of engagement.
