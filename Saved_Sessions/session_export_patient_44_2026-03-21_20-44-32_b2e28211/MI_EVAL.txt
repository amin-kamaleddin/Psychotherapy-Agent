# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys unconditional positive regard and warmth throughout, normalizing struggle and affirming Erik's progress without labeling or criticism. These individualized, nonjudgmental behaviors consistently support the client's dignity and reduce shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, naming Erik's frustration and paraphrasing his goals and ambivalence. Empathic attunement is sustained across the session, validating emotions without minimizing them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear focus on exercise consistency and diet from start to finish, linking questions and suggestions to Erik's identified goals. Conversation is consistently moved toward concrete, measurable action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuine teamwork with client input at every decision point, and the clinician co-creates plans with Erik rather than directing. Erik's ideas shape the final plan, demonstrating true collaborative problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly invites Erik to choose goals and offers options, avoiding coercive language and reinforcing his control over decisions. This consistent support for choice strengthens Erik's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits Erik's motivations and reasons for change (reduced pain, looser pants) and uses scaling effectively, but focuses more on logistics than deeper personal values. Further exploration of long-term meaning or identity-linked motives could deepen intrinsic commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician fosters a co-constructed plan by asking permission, offering tentative suggestions, and inviting preferences at each step. Power-sharing is consistent, conveying that Erik's expertise about his life shapes the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicits and reinforces change talk, asking for specific small steps and building on Erik's statements with affirmations and concrete planning. Commitment is deepened through scaling, action steps, and operationalizing change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently redirects sustain talk toward change with normalization and smaller options, effectively diminishing resistance. One missed opportunity was using more double-sided reflections to explicitly summarize ambivalence before moving fully into planning.
