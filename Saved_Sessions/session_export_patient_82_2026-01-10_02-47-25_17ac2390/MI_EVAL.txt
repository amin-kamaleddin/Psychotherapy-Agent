# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard with validating, nonjudgmental language that creates safety for disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening and some reflective statements, but missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions and identity conflict (e.g., embarrassment or athlete vs. peer pressures).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, goal-directed focus on early alcohol use and its impact, moving efficiently from assessment to concrete planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is cooperative with invited practice and adjustments, but the clinician often supplies polished scripts rather than co-creating the patient's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and framing suggestions as choices while respecting the patient's decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Limited elicitation of intrinsic motives and values; the session emphasized skills-training and phrasing over drawing out Lukas's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—asks preferences and invites rehearsal—but relies on ready-made lines instead of eliciting the patient's personalized wording first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encourages and reinforces change-oriented statements and planning, yet could elicit more spontaneous change talk about values, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward planning, though more double-sided and complex reflections could better acknowledge and resolve ambivalence.
