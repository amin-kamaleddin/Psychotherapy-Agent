# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive with repeated affirmations, safety checks, normalization, and acceptance of tentative commitments, conveying unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid reflective listening, validation, and accurate summarizing of facts and emotions, but stops short of deeper emotional exploration or probing the personal meaning of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol harm reduction with concrete near-term goals and practical strategies while keeping the interaction client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as cooperative problem-solving, solicits Maya's input and feedback, tailors recommendations, and presents options rather than prescriptions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms choice, offers multiple nonjudgmental options, asks permission before interventions, and accepts tentative commitments without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively uses scaling and elicits small steps and intentions but leans toward practical tactics rather than systematically drawing out deeper personal reasons, values, or visions for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites Maya's input, shares decision-making, and co-constructs the plan, fostering genuine power-sharing and respect for her expertise about her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk (scales, goal-setting, rehearsals) and affirms intentions, but could more consistently probe and amplify Maya's own reasons and values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk, normalizes worries, and redirects toward harm-reduction, though could more actively use double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to shift ambivalence toward change.
