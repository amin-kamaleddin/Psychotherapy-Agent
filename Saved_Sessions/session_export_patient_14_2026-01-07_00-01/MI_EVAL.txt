# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the patient's frustration and acknowledging practical constraints without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures feelings and barriers, though reflections stay pragmatic and could probe deeper emotional meaning or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on target behaviors and moved logically from eliciting a small change to concrete planning, problem-solving, and monitoring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—regularly solicited the patient's preferences, integrated constraints into the plan, and framed decisions as joint.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice and control, accepted conditional commitment, and reinforced patient ownership of decisions and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's own motivations and reasons for change with prompts that elicited desire, reasons, and commitment tied to the patient's values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-created the plan with the patient, repeatedly invited input, and deferred to the patient's priorities, demonstrating genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk through concrete questions and reflections, yielding specific desire, reason, and commitment language (two‑week trial, monitoring).
