# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveyed warm, nonjudgmental acceptance throughout, normalizing Leila's experience and repeatedly affirming her plans and feelings, which conveyed respect and support.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and timely reflective listening by summarizing Leila’s motivations and constraints and validating her feelings, facilitating further disclosure and planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on restarting regular activity, moving logically from values and motivation to concrete planning, obstacle planning, rehearsal, and relapse-prevention without sidetracking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Created a genuine team approach by regularly inviting Leila’s input, negotiating next steps together, and adjusting to her preferences while validating her choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by asking permission before offering help or role‑plays and by presenting practical suggestions as options that Leila could accept or decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently evoked Leila’s own motivations and strategies through questions that prompted her to articulate reasons, plan specifics, and rehearse her own language, then amplified those values into action.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered power-sharing by repeatedly asking for Leila’s preferences and permission, inviting her choices, and co-constructing concrete steps that reflected her input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk using scaling, summaries, invitations to commit, and by locking in specific times and encouraging rehearsal and phone-note creation to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively redirected sustain talk into problem-solving with if‑X→do‑Y plans and preferred alternatives, though at times offered detailed prescriptive tips without explicitly asking permission; more double-sided reflections would further acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change.
