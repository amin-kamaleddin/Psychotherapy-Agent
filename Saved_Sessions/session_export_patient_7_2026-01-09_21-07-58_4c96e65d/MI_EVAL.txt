# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and honored the patient's boundaries (e.g., accepted request to step back). These respectful moves supported engagement with a guarded adolescent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, frequent empathic reflections that captured emotions and meaning, lowering resistance. Reflections were specific and sensitive to tone and function (safety, boundary).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on engagement, safety, and contact preferences and avoided drifting into unwanted problem-solving. Could have used earlier concise summaries and checks of confidence/next steps to build clearer forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint venture, soliciting the patient's expertise and co-constructing decisions about contact. Clinician deferred to the patient's judgment and documented preferences collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively offered choices, respected boundaries, and asked permission before proposing actions. The clinician consistently reinforced the patient's control over contact and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivators and concrete signs of improvement (e.g., better sleep, less edge). Could deepen and amplify these motivators and link them to small, patient-led steps to build toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated a collaborative stance by inviting input and sharing power in planning contact preferences. The clinician honored the patient's contributions and incorporated them into the care plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited nascent change talk (e.g., 'I’ll tell you if I want to talk') and asked useful evocative questions, but did not consistently reinforce or amplify these statements. Missed opportunities for scaling confidence, summarizing reasons, or exploring small action steps to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided arguing and softened resistance by reflecting and offering choices rather than challenging sustain talk. Normalized protective behavior and validated the patient's stance, reducing sustain talk.
