# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validation through affirmations and normalizing language. Avoids blaming or judgmental statements, creating a warm supportive stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and some reflective statements, reducing defensiveness and validating concerns. Could deepen affective reflection by more explicitly exploring and reflecting Daniela's emotional tone (e.g., exhaustion, fear, shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session strongly focused on immediate targets (childcare and reducing substance use) with concrete, actionable next steps. Maintains momentum by anchoring plans to achievable behaviors and priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: co-creates texts, checks preferences, and invites input at each step, making the interaction shared problem-solving. Honors small requests and adapts language to the patient's comfort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports autonomy with choices, permissive language, and leaving decisions to Daniela (e.g., whether to draft texts or wait). Could strengthen by explicitly inviting her to adapt wording and affirming her ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation through prioritization questions and linking actions to goals, but the session relies heavily on clinician-provided solutions. Limited elicitation of Daniela's internal motivations, values, or longer-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters partnership by inviting collaboration, co-writing messages, and checking back frequently. Occasionally shifts toward directive problem-solving without first eliciting the patient's ideas, which slightly reduces shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces some change talk and planning (praise for cutting back, drafting texts), and boosts preparatory language. However, there is limited direct elicitation and amplification of the patient's own reasons, desires, abilities, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges barriers and offers practical alternatives, which avoids reinforcing sustain talk. Does not consistently explore or reframe sustain talk with double-sided or complex reflections and tends to move quickly to solutions.
