# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and a nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and safety checks, reinforcing practical steps. Acceptance feels competent but somewhat procedural, with limited deep empathic validation of Derrick's loneliness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and normalizations at times and notices practical concerns, producing adequate perspective-taking. Misses opportunities for deeper, affect-focused reflections to explore ambivalence and isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on preventing another DUI and develops a concrete, stepwise action plan. Repeatedly returns to specific behaviors (license protection, taxis, refusal lines) and guides toward actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functions largely as joint problem-solving: solicits preferences, co-writes texts, and role-plays with the client. Occasionally defaults to delivering lists and scripted instructions instead of jointly weighing alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice by asking which options feel doable and offering alternatives, inviting practice. A few directive moments (e.g., instructing to send the text now, set reminders) slightly undermine full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some client-generated ideas and behavioral commitments (cash, rides, Marcus), prompting concrete plans. Relies more on solution provision than drawing out intrinsic motives or exploring past successes to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displays collaborative elements by inviting choices and practicing refusal lines, giving the client input on plans. At times the clinician adopts an expert, directive role with long lists and scripted instructions, reducing consistent partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces specific change-oriented statements and prompts rehearsal, producing actionable change talk. Misses chances to amplify motivations, ask about the meaning of keeping a license, or elicit confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimizations, "just beer", social pressure) is largely skirted in favor of prescriptive steps, so minimizations remain unexamined. The clinician rarely uses double-sided reflections or reframing to strategically soften sustain talk.
