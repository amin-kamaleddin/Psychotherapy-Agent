# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and normalized Elias’s disclosure while avoiding blame, remaining warm and supportive even when addressing safety. The clinician validated emotions and offered help without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening with reflections and concrete offers of support, using open questions and validating statements. Could deepen emotional nuance by probing shame and ambivalence more consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing immediate risk and sustaining recovery, quickly moving to a concrete safety plan and actionable steps. The session stayed goal-directed and appropriate given the relapse and safety concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: co-created texts, reply scripts, and a checklist while frequently checking Elias’s preferences. Decisions and materials were shaped jointly rather than being expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Elias’s choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language. The clinician reinforced ownership of the plan and respected patient decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation present through open invitations and eliciting immediate concerns, but the clinician prioritized practical help over probing internal motivations or values. Missed opportunities to explore what mattered about three months sober and deeper drivers for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with permission-seeking and tailoring of materials, allowing Elias to shape content. Could strengthen by explicitly foregrounding Elias’s expertise and asking him to propose or prioritize long-term strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-oriented statements and supported safety-focused steps, but did not consistently amplify or elaborate Elias’s own change language. The clinician moved into pragmatic planning without strategically deepening motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk but did little active work to soften it—no double-sided reflections, ambivalence summaries, or gentle reframing. Sustain talk was acknowledged but not explicitly explored or redirected.
