# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation (e.g., normalizing slips and praising steps). The clinician never scolds or minimizes concerns and maintains a warm tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing worries and ambivalence well. Could deepen emotional reflections to explore feelings behind concerns like ‘being there’ for family.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on post-shift drinking and guides toward a concrete, measurable plan (specific night, activities, and backups). The clinician consistently steers the conversation toward actionable steps and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is a true back-and-forth with repeated invitations for Erik’s input and co-constructed decisions. The clinician shares power by checking preferences, wording, and supports rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering options, asking which change Erik prefers, and affirming that slips are informational rather than failures. Respects the patient’s request for no lectures and lets him own the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits intrinsic motivations and prior supports (family, energy, past small-goal success) that produced change talk. Would be strengthened by more values-oriented questions and strategic summaries to deepen motivation and highlight discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters client expertise and shared decision-making through choice, clarification, and joint planning (e.g., selecting night, supports, and message wording). The clinician consistently treats Erik as the expert on what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces specific change language and concrete plans (one alcohol-free night, swapping activities). Could more often amplify and summarize change statements and elicit stronger commitment language to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation, validates ambivalence, and reframes sustain talk nonjudgmentally (e.g., ‘being at a 5 makes sense’). Could use more double-sided reflections and pivot more frequently to reasons for change to de-emphasize sustain talk.
