### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, validating Elias’s feelings without blame. Tone and language protected his independence and respected his limits throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely empathic reflections that captured both emotion and content, deepening rapport. Reflections helped Elias elaborate rather than merely restating facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on increasing safe activity with a specific, measurable plan (five minutes seated leg lifts tied to coffee). Balanced exploration with steady movement toward concrete, testable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a genuine partner by soliciting Elias’s routines, preferences, and tolerances and integrating them into the plan. Repeatedly invited feedback and avoided top-down prescriptions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice and control, framed suggestions as options, and asked permission before offering help. Emphasized stopping if it hurts and respected Elias’s right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Elias’s motivations and linked change to existing habits, producing concrete change language. Could further probe values, use scaling, or elicit a client summary to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative, inviting Elias to co-create the plan and affirming his expertise about his routines. The clinician positioned Elias as the primary decision-maker throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited specific change talk and a small-step plan (daily five minutes, stop if it hurts), and linked behavior to an existing habit. Could have amplified and consolidated Elias’s reasons and commitment more strongly (e.g., asking why less stiffness or feeling braver matters).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated sustain talk with double-sided reflections and redirected toward manageable steps without reinforcing resistance. Could more actively soften sustain talk by eliciting exceptions, exploring past small successes, or using strategic reflections to shift weight toward change.
