# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance through validating language, normalizing statements, and gratitude for disclosure. Tone is respectful with no blaming or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening and accurate, often complex reflections captured the client's exhaustion, urges, and sensations, helping maintain engagement and disclosure. A minor opportunity existed to probe more into the affective meaning of the pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate direction toward immediate safety throughout, from assessment of means and recent cutting to concrete safety actions and coping options. Balanced empathic exploration with focused steps to reduce risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the safety plan, scripts, and coping choices were co-constructed and the clinician repeatedly checked the client's preferences. The clinician used the client's input to shape interventions and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced client choice and control by seeking permission, offering options, and affirming small steps. Language avoided coercion and emphasized the client's right to choose what to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited behavioral commitments and immediate coping preferences but did not sufficiently draw out internal motives, values, or reasons for change. Limited exploration of how staying safer would align with the client's broader goals reduced depth of intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a joint effort with permission-seeking and frequent checks on preferences, allowing the client to steer the session. Options and collaborative drafting (e.g., the roommate text) reinforced partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical, action-oriented commitments (razor removal, coping strategies) but did not probe for the client's own reasons, values, or desired outcomes that would deepen change talk. Change talk was present but remained transactional rather than evocative.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and nonjudgmentally acknowledged doubts while pivoting to harm-reduction and alternatives. Could increase use of strategic double-sided reflections to more explicitly contrast relief from cutting with costs and hints of wanting change.
