# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language (e.g., normalizing burden, offering permission to explore). Warmth and respect are evident throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with timely, specific reflections that helped the client open up. Could have probed a bit deeper into the emotional meaning of 'escape,' but overall empathy was strong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on harm-reduction goals and moves logically from assessment to actionable safety planning. Minimal topic drift and concrete operationalization of steps (Narcan, exchange, not using alone).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative stance—offers options, invites preferences, and engages Diego in co-creating scripts and plans. Client ideas remain central and power is shared in decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice by asking permission and offering multiple options while respecting Diego's decision not to start medication now. Language consistently framed offers as support rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits intrinsic motivation and concrete commitments (importance=10, specific safety steps), but could deepen evocation by asking about underlying values and eliciting implementation intentions and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats Diego as an expert and partner—asks permission, negotiates limits (can't place calls), and collaboratively decides which safety steps to start. Strong power-sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk using scaling and option-choices and reflects commitments, yet missed opportunities to amplify reasons, consequences, and stronger commitment language to consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids argument and validates the function of heroin while redirecting to safety, but used few double-sided reflections; more strategic reflections could better shift sustain talk toward change.
