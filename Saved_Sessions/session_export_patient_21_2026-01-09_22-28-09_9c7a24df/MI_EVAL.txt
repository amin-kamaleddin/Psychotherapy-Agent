# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed respect and positive regard, normalizing ambivalence and validating Camila's values and constraints without judgment. Language was warm and supportive throughout with multiple affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening captured both content and feeling (e.g., energy for family, barriers like tiredness/weather). Summaries and reflections consistently demonstrated a strong empathic stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on increasing physical activity and moved purposefully to a concrete, measurable plan (Mon/Wed/Fri 10‑min walks, reminders, check‑in). The agenda remained on-target without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was a joint effort: the clinician solicited Camila's ideas, integrated her wording, offered options, and co‑created decisions like nights and check‑in method. Power was shared and choices were honored throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported choice and emphasized Camila's control by offering multiple options and asking permission to help. Suggestions were framed to preserve autonomy and the patient's selections were respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Camila's own motivations and plans through values-focused questions, a confidence ruler, and prompts for what would increase commitment. The approach successfully drew out and scaffolded internal change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as collaborative, inviting input and co‑constructing the plan (e.g., alarm wording, partner message, check‑in content). Choices were offered and refined together rather than prescribed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions, reflections, and the confidence ruler effectively elicited and strengthened desire, ability, and commitment language. The clinician amplified Camila's reasons and specific plans to increase follow-through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and redirected with practical alternatives (shorter walks, indoor backup, timing changes), reducing reinforcement of barriers. To improve, the clinician could use more explicit double‑sided reflections to name both sides of ambivalence before pivoting to solutions.
