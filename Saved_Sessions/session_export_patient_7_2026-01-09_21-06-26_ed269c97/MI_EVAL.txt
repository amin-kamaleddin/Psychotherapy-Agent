# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized the client's experience, and explicitly stated nonjudgment. Boundaries were respected repeatedly, conveying warmth rather than blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, nuanced reflective listening that mirrored both content and underlying meaning. Followed reflections with curiosity-driven, perspective-taking questions rather than interrogation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on engagement and rapport-building rather than pushing for immediate change. Questions and summaries were tightly aligned with clarifying confidentiality and trust.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative, soliciting the client's input and adopting her suggested wording for notes and boundaries. The plan was co-constructed and prioritized the client's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the client's choice about if and when to disclose, asked permission before documenting or sharing, and negotiated limits to confidentiality. Tentative language was accepted and ownership of decisions was reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited client-generated criteria for trust with evocative open questions and used those responses to co-create actionable steps. The clinician drew out internal reasons and preferences rather than supplying them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited the client's expertise and framed the interaction as follow-your-lead, asking what the clinician should do to build trust. Power was shared and the client helped shape next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified tentative change talk (e.g., 'I might try that') with evocative questions and small-step framing. Given the client's precontemplative stance, the clinician rightly avoided pressing for firm commitment, though future sessions could consolidate emerging change language into clearer ability/confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Met sustain talk without confrontation, validating the client's protective stance and de-emphasizing resistance. The clinician redirected toward specific conditions that would support trust and future engagement.
