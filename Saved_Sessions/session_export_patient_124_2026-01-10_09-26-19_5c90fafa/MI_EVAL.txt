# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence with warm, nonjudgmental language, making the patient feel accepted throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained, accurate reflections captured both content and feeling (stress, relief, worry about grades), helping the patient feel heard and understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on marijuana use and coping strategies, guiding the conversation toward a concrete three‑day experiment without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in joint problem‑solving, solicited the patient's ideas, and co‑designed practical steps (reminders, playlist) rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's right to choose, normalized not promising to stop, and offered options and invitations that preserved autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's reasons and ideas for change (cost, sleep, grades) with open questions, but could have probed deeper into intrinsic motives and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative, privileged the patient's solutions, and repeatedly asked permission to co‑create specifics like wording and reminders.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and planning, but missed opportunities to amplify importance/confidence and strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward experiments, though at times repeated sustain statements rather than using double‑sided reflections or emphasizing discrepancy.
