# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing choice and reducing shame. No directive or punitive language undermining acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately mirroring patient content and feelings. Some reflections are close paraphrases and occasionally repetitive rather than probing deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction and moves logically from ambivalence to a concrete, actionable plan. Questions consistently advance toward measurable steps while preserving autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient as an equal partner by soliciting ideas, building on them, and offering to operationalize the patient's choices. Language and pacing avoid an expert/authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly emphasizes patient choice and control, offering options rather than prescriptions and reinforcing ownership of decisions and the implementation plan. Reassures there is no pressure to do more.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations, values, and past strategies, producing self‑generated change talk and a practical plan. Questions draw out internal reasons for change rather than imposing them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the work as collaborative, checks preferences, and invites co‑creation of the plan. Clinician defers to the patient's expertise and shares power in decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively evokes and amplifies change talk through open questions, prompts about past successes, and reflective affirmations that strengthen commitment language. This approach moved the patient toward a concrete implementation intention.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and skillfully redirects to change options, keeping sustain talk minimal. Could more often use double‑sided reflections or selective summaries to explicitly name both sides and pivot to change.
