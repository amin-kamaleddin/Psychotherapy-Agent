# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing imperfections and avoiding labels. This created a warm, supportive environment that encouraged honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking captured both reasons for drinking and its costs, which the patient responded to. Reflections remained mostly content-level and could probe emotional nuance of ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use and moved the conversation to a concrete, measurable experiment with tracking and a scheduled follow-up. The session kept strong momentum toward behavior change and monitoring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician solicited preferences, offered to review entries together, and tailored suggestions, modeling a team approach. However, many detailed solutions were clinician-generated rather than elicited from the patient, tilting the balance slightly toward clinician-led problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized the patient's choice and control by offering options, inviting method preferences, and normalizing imperfection. The clinician avoided pressure and respected the patient's desire to keep nights out.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited enough motivation to produce a practical experiment but relied more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient's internal reasons or values. Few evocative questions about importance, confidence, or personal goals were asked to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative by asking which options felt doable and inviting joint review of entries. At times the clinician provided ready-made scripts and strategies rather than eliciting the patient's own ideas, which limited shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and elicited a concrete commitment (4-drink cap, tracking, delayed drinking after over-limit nights). Missed opportunities included fewer evocative open questions and limited amplification of the patient's change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk and gently pivoted to a small experiment, avoiding confrontation and reducing defensiveness. Could further soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections and strategic summaries that explicitly name both sides of ambivalence.
