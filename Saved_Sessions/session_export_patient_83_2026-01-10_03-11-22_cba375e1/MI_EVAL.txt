# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing Mai’s concerns about side effects, cost, and living alone. No blaming or minimizing; concerns are treated as legitimate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates frequent, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, mirroring the patient's language and checking understanding. Reflections capture ambivalence and validate feelings before proposing solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and safety, moving logically from concerns to a concrete plan. Questions and reflections consistently link back to adherence and immediate steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a genuine team approach: the clinician asks for Mai’s input, offers tailored options, and immediately follows through on her choices. Decisions are co-constructed and responsive to patient preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms Mai’s control and choices, offering options rather than directives and avoiding pressuring language. The clinician asks which strategies feel best for her and supports her selected plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Mai’s own reasons for change (preventing heart problems, safety living alone) and uses them to build the plan. Could be strengthened by deeper exploration of values and use of scaling/confidence questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions Mai as an equal partner throughout, soliciting preferences and shaping the plan around her selections (pillbox style, alarm, labeling). The clinician repeatedly checks what would make her feel confident.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk with doable first steps and affirmations, amplifying Mai’s intentions to take the medication. To reach the highest score, the clinician could more often summarize change statements and elicit explicit commitment or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates and redirects sustain talk without arguing, reframing doubts into practical steps and safety planning. A missed opportunity was using explicit double-sided reflections to contrast skepticism with reasons for change and further resolve ambivalence.
