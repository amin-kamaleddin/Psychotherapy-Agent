# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validating the patient's perspective and choices throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and paraphrase, but reflections remain somewhat surface-level and sometimes sound interpretive (e.g., 'resigned') rather than inviting elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target behavior, moving from broad options to a specific, measurable plan and setting an appropriate follow-up while staying client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently co-constructs the plan by soliciting the patient's input on choices, cues, timing, and backups, signaling genuine partnership rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively offers choices, avoids pressuring language, and respects the patient's decisions while leaving options for follow-up, reinforcing the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own motivations and resources using scaling questions, past-success reminders, and prompts for small, practical changes to raise confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance throughout, sharing power in planning and inviting the patient to choose details and shape follow-up arrangements.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with scaling questions, asking what would move scores higher, drawing on past successes, and converting motivation into concrete implementation steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward planning, but uses limited explicit softening (e.g., double-sided reflections) and could more directly acknowledge reasons for maintaining the status quo.
