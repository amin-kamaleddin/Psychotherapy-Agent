# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Mateo’s experience, using validating and affirming language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate reflections, though reflections were often brief and the clinician moved quickly into planning rather than exploring deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on avoiding risky peers/locations and moved to a concrete, time-limited one-week experiment with specific rules, urge plan, backups, and a follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session functioned as a partnership with joint planning and incorporation of Mateo’s ideas, though the clinician occasionally shifted into a slightly more expert, prescriptive role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Mateo’s choices, offered options, asked permission before formatting messages, and respected his limits (e.g., one agreed check-in).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Mateo’s motivations, goals, and resources and invited him to choose the experiment, but could more often prompt elaboration of values, importance, and confidence to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered client expertise and shared decision-making by asking preferences and negotiating plans, though some structured recommendations slightly reduced pure client-led problem solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk through identifying patterns and proposing a one-week experiment, but missed opportunities to amplify, explore consequences, and assess importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and normalized ambivalence which limited reinforcement of sustain talk, but did not consistently use double-sided reflections or other strategies to pivot sustain talk toward change.
