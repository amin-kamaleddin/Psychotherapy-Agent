# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental language and normalization from the start, creating a warm, safe atmosphere for a guarded teen.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate empathic listening with frequent paraphrase of feelings and direct safety checks; a minor missed chance to reflect deeper vulnerability when the client minimized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on the rumors, school avoidance, and concrete behavioral targets (texting Maya, counselor check-in, class coping) with prompt safety checks and no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear two-way collaboration: invites choices, co-creates message drafts, practices role-play, and solicits feedback and tweaks throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and permission, offers optional suggestions rather than directives, and respects the client’s decisions about what feels doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation and invites small steps effectively (e.g., asking what she wants at school), but often shifts to offering scripted options rather than drawing out Sofia’s own values, reasons, and confidence more fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance—asks permission, invites Sofia to choose approaches, and treats her as an equal partner in drafting and practicing next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk by offering doable options, helping choose one, refining the message, and rehearsing responses to strengthen commitment and ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and explores consequences of avoidance, but relies more on neutral reflections and solutions than strategic double-sided reflections or linking sustain language to the client’s values to create discrepancy.
