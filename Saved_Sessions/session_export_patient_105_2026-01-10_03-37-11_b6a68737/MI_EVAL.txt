# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating, normalizing Naomi's wariness and avoiding blame. No stigmatizing or minimizing remarks were made.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid empathic listening and brief reflections, acknowledging Naomi's discomfort and worries. Reflections were generally practical rather than deeply exploring emotional meaning or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on Naomi's social anxiety in photography settings and tied each intervention to the presenting problems. Session moved efficiently from symptoms to skill practice and concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively through modeling, practice, and asking for preferences, reinforcing teamwork. However, frequent clinician-provided scripts and solutions reduced opportunities for Naomi to generate her own options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, asked permission, and framed recommendations as options, preserving Naomi's sense of control. No coercive language was used and plans were presented as tweakable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some prompts invited Naomi's responses (preferences, practice feedback), but the clinician relied more on offering strategies than eliciting Naomi's values, reasons, or confidence. Evocation was present but remained superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership by inviting Naomi to choose and rehearse, and by praising attempts. The clinician's expert stance with many prescriptive recommendations somewhat limited equal co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change talk and offered doable steps, but did not consistently probe or amplify Naomi's own motivations, reasons, or commitment language before moving to solutions. More strategic reflections could strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged Naomi's wariness and offered low‑barrier options that redirected sustain talk, but did not employ targeted MI strategies (e.g., double‑sided or complex reflections) to soften ambivalence. Sustain talk was often left unelaborated before moving to advice.
