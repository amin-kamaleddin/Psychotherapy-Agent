# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental, respectful regard through validations, praise, and normalization of ambivalence. Warmth and acceptance were maintained throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries demonstrated accurate understanding and helped Derrick feel heard. The clinician could deepen empathy by exploring emotional content (e.g., isolation, defensiveness) with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on preventing repeat DUI and securing the license, moving toward actionable steps. Goal-directed pacing guided assessment, planning, troubleshooting, and practice effectively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-created with the client, offering options and practical supports while soliciting Derrick’s input at each turn. The clinician worked with Derrick rather than directing him, modeling shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice by offering alternatives, asking permission, and letting Derrick decide which steps to take. Interventions avoided pressure and affirmed client control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited concrete intentions and confidence via scaling and targeted questions. Greater probing of intrinsic motives and values would strengthen internal motivation further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician fostered client expertise and shared power by inviting choices, offering to help only with permission, and jointly building the plan. This genuine partnership supported ownership of the steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of scaling and specific planning elicited and developed change talk, producing concrete commitments (e.g., texting Marcus, practice at 8pm). The clinician could more often amplify and reflect change language to consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided argument and did not reinforce sustain talk but missed chances to strategically soften minimization with double-sided or empathic reflections. Explicitly acknowledging both sides of Derrick’s ambivalence would have reduced sustain talk more effectively.
