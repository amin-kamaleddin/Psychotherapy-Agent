# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established confidentiality and used nonjudgmental, supportive language throughout (e.g., “Everything we discuss is confidential…”, “That sounds hard and confusing,” “I’m proud of you”). Communicates unconditional positive regard and avoids labeling. Suggestion: continue pairing affirmations with reflections to deepen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic empathic reflections present (“I hear you want to cut down…but aren’t sure how”), but often superficial. Missed chances to reflect emotional content (regret, loneliness, low mood) and to explore how drinking and mood interact. Recommend more complex/affect‑laden reflections and reflective summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction: set a concrete 7‑day trial, specific strategies (limit two drinks, one bottle at home, alternate with sparkling water), nightly tracking, and a firm check‑in time. Maintains appropriate change‑orientation; ensure plans remain client‑led by ending summaries with questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invites patient input repeatedly (“Which feels most manageable?”, patient chooses check‑in time). Some moments feel more clinician‑driven (repeated directives and safety instructions). Suggest framing suggestions more tentatively and summarizing patient choices before elaborating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong support for client choice and control (offers options, asks what feels manageable, seeks permission for plan and follow‑up). Safety advice respects patient responsibility. Continue to elicit patient’s own commitments in their words.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits past attempts and barriers and invites the patient to select workable strategies. Missed deeper probes into reasons for change (values, what matters most) and did not consistently elicit patient‑generated arguments for change. Recommend direct prompts for reasons/desire and importance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and co‑constructive (“Let’s make it concrete…”; patient selected plan/time). Partnership weakens when clinician gives repeated directive praise. Recommend explicitly acknowledging the patient’s expertise about her life and using summaries that foreground her words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk appears (patient: “I could try two drinks…”, “I’ll try the 7‑day trial…”), but clinician does not consistently reflect/amplify or ask follow‑ups about importance or confidence. Suggest immediate reflection of change talk and use of importance/confidence probes (e.g., “On a 1–10 scale, how confident are you?”).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoids confrontation and acknowledges ambivalence, which prevents strengthening sustain talk. However, did not use double‑sided reflections or reframes to actively soften sustain talk (e.g., about stress or “it makes things more interesting”). Recommend double‑sided reflections and strategic reframing to reduce sustain talk.
