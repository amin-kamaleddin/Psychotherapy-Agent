# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5  
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful tone ("I'm here to listen"), honored the patient's wish to avoid a lecture, affirmed choices (e.g., charging phone, pacing), and avoided shaming or punitive remarks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking (paraphrases like “you want fun without real trouble”). Could deepen emotional reflections (guarded/irritated affect, peer/identity pressures) to better capture underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5  
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus on teen alcohol harm-reduction throughout; session stayed on target behaviors and produced a concrete, measurable safety plan (eat, alarm 11:30, pre-book Uber, practiced lines).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5  
**Comments:** Strong joint problem-solving: asked permission, offered options, elicited patient choices, and practiced refusal language together. Plan was clearly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and respected patient autonomy (asked which strategies felt doable; normalized low confidence), avoided coercion or lecturing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Elicited patient’s own plans and commitment language (choices, confidence rating, practiced lines). Could more directly draw out personal values/reasons for avoiding trouble to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Fostered partnership via permission, practice, and selection of strategies. Slightly clinician-led at times (offered lists of options before inviting patient-generated ideas).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (specific safety actions, practiced lines, tentative commitments). Change talk remained mostly action-level; missed opportunities to deepen reasons/longer-term benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5  
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected minimize/normalizing comments toward harm-reduction and doable steps. Could use double-sided reflections and targeted amplification to better acknowledge sustain talk while highlighting discrepancy.
