# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—validated Elise's experience, normalized ambivalence, and avoided judgment throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and concise summaries of the core tension, but missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions (e.g., loneliness, fear of rejection) with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking and preserving social connection, with interventions logically progressing to a concrete, testable experiment and plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a collaborative exploration—invited preferences, role-play, and accepted Elise's wording tweaks, with decisions largely co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering options, asking which approach Elise preferred, and affirming her ownership of the chosen plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used evocation techniques (importance ruler, values, experiment) to draw out motivation, but shifted to advice and scripting too early, limiting deeper elicitation of Elise's internal reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively and invited input, but at times slipped into expert mode by providing numerous ready-made scripts rather than co-creating them from Elise's own language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk (importance=5, experiment commitment) and generated concrete change language, though opportunities to amplify and deepen desire, ability, reasons, and need were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with sustain talk and validated social benefits while offering alternatives, but could have used more double-sided reflections and agile reframing before moving quickly to skill-building and scripts.
