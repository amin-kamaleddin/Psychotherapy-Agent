# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing the patient's choices and avoiding shame. Repeatedly affirms decisions and offers nonjudgmental support throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and tailors responses to the patient's priorities, asking what she wants help with. Misses some deeper emotional reflections and opportunities to name subtle feelings like frustration or pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the lip piercing and oral‑health risks with concrete monitoring criteria and next steps. Session stays tightly centered on pragmatic management and thresholds for recommending removal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative—agenda is jointly selected, options are offered, and the clinician invites rehearsal and patient choice. The clinician adapts content to the patient's requests and provides brief, co‑constructed plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the patient's sense of choice, accepts her decision not to remove the piercing, and frames recommendations as contingent on documented progression. Encourages patient control by teaching how to request documentation and emphasizing conditional advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation is present (asking what she wants help with and setting up monitoring) but the clinician relies heavily on information and advice. Limited exploration of intrinsic motivations, values, or questions to elicit deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys that expertise about the decision resides with the patient, repeatedly invites preferences, and co‑constructs monitoring and rehearsal plans. Offers choices and role‑play options rather than issuing directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicits very little change talk and does not systematically evoke or amplify it, responding mainly with information and rehearsal for maintaining the piercing. Missed opportunities to use scaling, ask open questions about reasons or confidence, or amplify small openness to monitoring.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively rolls with resistance, avoids arguing, and offers pragmatic compromises (monitoring, photos, follow‑up) and rehearsal strategies. Could improve by using double‑sided reflections and more actively reframing sustain talk toward future‑oriented reasons for monitoring.
