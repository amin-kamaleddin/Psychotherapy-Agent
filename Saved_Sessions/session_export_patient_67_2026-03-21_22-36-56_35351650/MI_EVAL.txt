### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicates nonjudgmental warmth and validates Kenji's ambivalence, normalizing his feelings without blaming. Unconditional positive regard is evident throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Kenji’s concerns, skepticism, and pragmatic stance, helping him feel heard. Empathic listening is timely, specific, and mirrors the patient’s language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing weeknight drinking tied to work and health, moving logically from values to concrete steps. Questions consistently narrow toward actionable planning and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is a genuine working partnership: the clinician elicits Kenji’s ideas and uses them to co‑create the plan rather than prescribing solutions. Kenji’s language and preferences drive the session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirms Kenji’s choice and control, inviting options and allowing flexibility (e.g., keeping weekends). Skepticism is acknowledged without pressure, preserving client autonomy.

### Evocation

**Comments:** The clinician elicits internal motives (protecting work, health) and specific signs of success, producing a concrete plan and confidence rating. There is room to deepen intrinsic motives and amplify commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as collaborative, repeatedly asking what would work for Kenji and inviting him to choose options. Power is clearly shared and the plan is built from Kenji’s ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, prompting concrete options and a willingness to try a sober week. More selective amplification and reflective emphasis on commitment language could strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and not challenged, and the clinician redirects toward safer alternatives and supports. Using more double‑sided reflections and strategic reframing could further soften sustain talk.
