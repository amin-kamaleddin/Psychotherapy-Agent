# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and honored Marcus's stance without shaming, normalizing his position and focusing on safety rather than moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and summaries that showed understanding and validated Marcus's wish to avoid lectures, though missed opportunities to deepen emotional and adolescent-specific reflections (belonging/identity).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on teen alcohol use and moved logically toward harm-reduction steps and a measurable plan (alternating water, planning a ride).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session read as teamwork—clinician offered options, invited Marcus to choose actions, and frequently checked in before offering help, resulting in jointly made decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Marcus's choices by asking permission before advising, avoiding pressure, and repeatedly reinforcing that he was in charge of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Marcus's motivations, concerns, and supports (who could help, confidence scaling) but could have probed deeper into intrinsic values and linked change to identity/goals to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively positioned Marcus as the expert on what would work, repeatedly asked which options felt doable, and co-created concrete strategies like drafting texts together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change-related language and concrete steps, but could have amplified and reinforced Marcus's 'I'll try' statements to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used double-sided framing to acknowledge sustain talk and redirect to safety, though offering many strategies early risked shifting toward didactic advising despite asking permission.
