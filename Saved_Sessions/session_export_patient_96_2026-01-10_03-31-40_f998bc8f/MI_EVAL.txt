# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed nonjudgmental, respectful regard and validated Marisol’s choices throughout, honoring her decisions without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking were evident, though reflections were often brief and the clinician sometimes shifted quickly to advice instead of exploring feelings further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the target behavior (aftercare and red-flag signs) and repeatedly returned to concrete safety steps and monitoring plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative—co‑creating messages and offering options—but the clinician led much of the educational and safety planning rather than eliciting Marisol’s ideas more fully.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly supported Marisol’s right to choose, offered options rather than directives, and consistently framed actions as Marisol’s decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Attempts to draw out Marisol’s own motivation were limited and not pursued when responses were brief, with the session relying more on information and planning than elicitation of client reasons or goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician treated the encounter as collaborative—asking permission, offering choices, and co‑designing responses—though could have more explicitly invited Marisol’s decision-making expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was minimal effort to elicit or amplify change talk; the clinician missed opportunities to follow up on mild change statements and instead moved to advice and information.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided escalating resistance and validated autonomy, but did not consistently use strategic reflections (e.g., double-sided) to gently shift sustain talk toward change.
