# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing and validating the patient's concerns throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and some reflective listening, but the encounter is often information‑heavy with few extended, complex reflections of the patient's underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on safe statin initiation, interactions, monitoring, and clear next steps, with a concise summary at the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the visit as joint problem-solving by inviting medication lists and labs and offering shared follow‑up, conveying equal partnership rather than an authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and decision-making by offering options, advising without pressuring, and emphasizing the patient's role in next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some intentions and asks an initial open question, but relies largely on didactic information rather than targeted evocation techniques to draw out the patient's values, reasons, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and invites patient input and involvement in follow‑up, though could more explicitly acknowledge the patient's expertise and co‑create monitoring plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforces several patient change statements (timing, avoiding grapefruit, safety plans) but misses opportunities to deepen and amplify reasons, importance, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes uncertainty into actionable steps, but tends toward neutral education rather than using double‑sided reflections or other active strategies to soften sustain talk.
