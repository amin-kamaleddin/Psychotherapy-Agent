# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician establishes a respectful, validating tone and normalizes Amina's distress, but moves quickly into solutions and stops short of the deepest, individualized acceptance or extended normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are consistent perspective-taking and reflective statements that acknowledge emotion and context, though reflections are brief and mainly used as transitions rather than to deepen exploration of feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear, consistent focus on the behavioral goal of routine and co‑parenting stability, and all interventions are tightly aligned with that target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some shared decision points appear (asking preferences, which rules matter), but the clinician often takes the lead with scripts and prescriptive plans, making the interaction more clinician-driven than jointly explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist honors choices, asks permission, and offers options, which supports agency, but frequent scripting and directive language somewhat constrain client-led exploration of alternatives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some motivation by asking what matters to Amina, but relies more on offering solutions than on drawing out her own reasons, values, or imagined outcomes for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stance is primarily expert-driven with numerous ready-made scripts and step-by-step plans, which limits co-creation and opportunities for shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful prompts that produce concrete change-related responses, but the clinician moves quickly to scripted solutions instead of amplifying or following up to deepen the client's change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and worries are acknowledged and not argued with, and pragmatic strategies are offered to limit its impact, though more strategic MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) could better resolve ambivalence.
