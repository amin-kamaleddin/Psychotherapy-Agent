# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating from the first turn, with praise and support for the patient's preferences. No blaming language and clear respect for the patient's concerns and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and accurate summaries with attentive safety monitoring and reflective listening. Could improve with more complex affective reflections to explore deeper emotions and fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, sustained focus on smoking cessation with concrete objectives (quit date, med selection/titration, coping strategies, relapse and safety plan). Repeatedly returns to actionable next steps and logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is genuinely co-constructed: the clinician seeks input at key decisions, responds to requests, and commits to follow-through. Power is shared and the patient's priorities shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering multiple options, asking preferences, and honoring timing and check-in decisions. Reassures the patient they can stop or adjust treatment and keeps control with the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and reasons for change with open questions and readiness/confidence scaling. Could more deeply draw out internal motives, past strengths, and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and invites the patient to shape the plan, check-ins, and logistics. At times recommendations (e.g., emphasizing varenicline as most effective) felt slightly directive, though the patient’s choice was still solicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk and converts motivation into concrete planning (quit date, meds). Missed opportunities to explore the confidence gap and to directly elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk about stress and social triggers without reinforcing it and offers practical behavioral strategies and a relapse plan. Could more actively use double-sided or strategic reflections to reframe ambivalence toward change.
