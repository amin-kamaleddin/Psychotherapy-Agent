### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☒5  
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., “You’re exhausted — that sounds really hard,” “You’re doing your best”). Normalizes feelings and offers reassurance; no shaming or blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☒4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections of tiredness/overwhelm and supportive phrases. Some emotional content (persistent guilt, fear of “messing up”) was acknowledged but not deeply explored before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☒5  
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on maternal/infant sleep and functional goal (one 3–4 hour stretch); interventions and safety guidance remain on-target throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☒4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Invites patient input (“Which of those feels doable?”; “Want to try it tonight?”) and offers options, but frequently uses an instructional tone with many clinician-led suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☒4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Affirms choice and frames options as optional (“it’s totally fine if that won’t work”), asks permission implicitly; occasionally favors directive advice over eliciting patient's preference.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☒3 ☐4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Some elicitation (asking which feels doable) but interaction is largely advice- and information-driven; limited elicitation of patient’s own reasons/confidence or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☒3 ☐4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Polite, supportive tone with invitations for input, but clinician frequently positions self as expert (detailed instructions, scripts) rather than co-creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☒3 ☐4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Patient expressed desire/intent (wanting longer sleep, willing to try dream‑feed/script). Clinician affirms but does not consistently reflect or probe to amplify and deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☒4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain‑talk (guilt, skepticism) and offers normalization and practical ways to reduce barriers (gentle script, “not your fault”), though could use more double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
