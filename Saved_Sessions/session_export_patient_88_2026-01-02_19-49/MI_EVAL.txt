# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Communicated unconditional positive regard / nonjudgmental opening ("I’m here to listen and support you")
- [x] Validated patient's perspective ("It sounds like you feel it’s normal… That makes sense.")
- [x] Explicitly acknowledged patient's preference re: parents (supported confidentiality)
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating across the session (multiple turns). Normalized choices and avoided blaming; acceptance evident throughout.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Used reflective statement early (“It sounds like you feel it’s normal…”)
- [x] Offered verbal acknowledgements and validation (“Thanks for being honest — that helps.”)
- [ ] Probed deeper affect or emotional content (not observed)
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with several validating reflections; missed opportunities for deeper affective reflections (frustration, fear of punishment, belonging).

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Kept session focused on alcohol use and harm reduction
- [x] Performed safety check and monitored red flags (blackouts, missed classes, mood/relationship changes)
- [x] Repeated concise summaries and clear purpose across turns
**Comments:** Strong focus and consistent steering toward practical harm-reduction and safety; direction maintained throughout.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Asked permission before offering help/options (“Would you like help with harm-reduction strategies…?”)
- [x] Invited patient to choose which strategies felt doable (“Which of these feels doable?”)
- [ ] Fully co-constructed plan (interaction was more clinician-led with repeated lists)
**Comments:** Collaborative stance present (options, choice), but clinician often delivered lists and information rather than deeply co-creating the plan with the patient.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Explicitly supported patient's choices and right to decide
- [x] Stated limits clearly (“I can’t help you hide from your parents”) while offering alternatives
- [x] Offered confidential care options for later help
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced autonomy and respected patient’s decision-making; autonomy-supportive language used repeatedly.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Used open questions about drinking frequency and consequences
- [x] Asked which strategies felt doable (elicited some change language)
- [ ] Elicited deeper values/reasons or explored motivations (limited)
**Comments:** Some elicitation of change (frequency, consequences, doable steps) but limited depth; clinician often moved quickly to provide strategies rather than exploring underlying motivations or values.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Framed interaction as joint problem-solving (“Would you like help…?”, “Which of these feels doable?”)
- [x] Invited patient's input on realistic steps
- [ ] Plan remained largely clinician-directed rather than fully co-authored
**Comments:** Partnership evident in language and invitations, but the actual plan leaned toward clinician-led suggestions instead of a fully co-created plan.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Elicited and affirmed some patient change language (patient agreed to skip shots, space drinks, eat first, watch cup)
- [x] Reinforced sensible harm-reduction choices
- [ ] Did not systematically amplify or follow up on change statements to build commitment
**Comments:** Change talk occurred but was not consistently deepened or amplified; missed chances to ask follow-up questions to strengthen commitment and specificity.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Avoided confronting or arguing with sustain talk (“It’s not a big deal, everyone does it.” was not challenged)
- [x] Accepted sustain talk and pivoted to harm reduction and safety
- [ ] Did not use strategic double-sided or reframing reflections to actively soften sustain talk
**Comments:** Clinician appropriately avoided confrontation and defused sustain talk, but used passive acceptance rather than strategic reflections to resolve ambivalence.
