# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, supportive tone and validated Sofia’s feelings and efforts throughout, framing feedback around safety and care rather than blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and validations that helped Sofia feel heard and disclose more, but frequently moved quickly to advice, missing opportunities to deepen emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, sustained focus on alcohol use in pregnancy with concrete, safety‑oriented steps (OB contact, ultrasound, stop drinking) and a logical flow from assessment to planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered collaboration through permission‑seeking, rehearsals, and offers to join calls, but often provided scripted responses and checklists instead of eliciting Sofia’s own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported choice by offering options and asking permission for plans and rehearsals, though some prescriptive language around medical steps reduced the emphasis on client autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation via planning prompts and affirmations, but relied mainly on information and scripting rather than asking evocative questions to draw out Sofia’s values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated a collaborative stance with joint rehearsal and choice of lines, yet the interaction at times leaned toward expert‑driven recommendations rather than co‑creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered practical steps and invitations that produced some change talk, but did not consistently follow up to amplify reasons, importance, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and provided helpful alternatives, but did not strategically use double‑sided reflections or reframing to explore and soften ambivalence.
