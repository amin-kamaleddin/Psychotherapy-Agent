# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—opening with a safe, nonjudgmental stance, asking permission, and using affirmations to validate shame and fear. This created a warm, accepting environment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, tracking Elias's language (shame, scared, family) and checking understanding. Reflections and summaries validated ambivalence and emotional content effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol/pill use and relapse prevention, linking questions and interventions to staying sober. Moved conversation toward concrete, immediate behavioral plans (checklist, scripts, pill handoff) without losing client-centered tone.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as a true two-way collaboration—clinician invited input at each step and tailored options to Elias's preferences. Elias's choices directly shaped the interventions, demonstrating shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy via repeated permission-seeking, offering multiple realistic options, and emphasizing Elias's choice of what felt doable. Language avoided pressure and reinforced ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations (family, fear, early improvements) and used scaling questions, but could have probed deeper into intrinsic values and elicited more vivid personal reasons and commitment language. More exploration of fatherhood values or future imagery would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled partnership by asking permission before suggestions, co-creating the tonight checklist, and rehearsing replies with Elias. Power-sharing was evident as the clinician adapted language to Elias's voice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with good open questions, scaling, and affirmations, but missed opportunities to amplify statements like 'I don't want to feel that way again' and to elicit stronger commitment and confidence-building. Could have followed plan formation with more explicit commitment prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and neutrally acknowledged ambivalence, gently redirecting toward change, but relied mainly on reflections. More strategic use of double-sided reflections or highlighting discrepancies would better reduce sustain talk.
