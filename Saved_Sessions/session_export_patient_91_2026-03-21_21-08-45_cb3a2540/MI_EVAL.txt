# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician shows consistent, nonjudgmental acceptance—normalizing feelings and affirming strengths throughout the session. Language is unconditional and avoids labels or criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Empathic listening and accurate reflections convey clear understanding of Marcus’s perspective. Emotional reflections could be deeper at times to explore what 'scary' or 'overwhelming' feels like beyond logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on blood‑glucose monitoring and developing a concrete routine. Questions and suggestions consistently tie back to the target behavior and measurable outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, offering options and asking Marcus to choose while co‑constructing the plan. Inclusive language and joint problem‑solving ensure Marcus’s input shapes the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supports Marcus’s autonomy by offering choices and inviting his preference. Plans are presented as options and the clinician affirms the ability to tweak the plan if needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively draws out Marcus’s own motivations, plans, and confidence using open questions and a confidence ruler. They build on Marcus’s ideas to increase commitment rather than imposing reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the conversation as a partnership, asking which options feel doable and checking confidence. Co‑creation of scripts and backup plans demonstrates an equal, collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is consistently elicited and strengthened through invitations to try small, specific steps and confidence/commitment scaling. Reflections and reinforcement translate ambivalence into concrete, actionable change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician gently normalizes sustain talk and redirects toward practical, low‑barrier options, reducing resistance. There were missed opportunities for more strategic softening (e.g., double‑sided reflections or explicit contrasts) to further resolve ambivalence.
