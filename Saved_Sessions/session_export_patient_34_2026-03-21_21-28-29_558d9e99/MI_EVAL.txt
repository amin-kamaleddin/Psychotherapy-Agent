# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, warm stance and validated Dylan’s experience throughout, normalizing difficulty and offering support. Language and offers to help demonstrated clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening were evident with accurate paraphrases and regular checks for understanding. The clinician consistently validated Dylan’s feelings and priorities.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the conduct/referral issue and concrete outcomes (statement wording, accepting an educational meeting). The clinician efficiently moved from exploration to specific action planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The encounter was clearly joint problem-solving: the clinician invited input at every step, co-constructed statements, and made decisions together with Dylan. Dylan’s preferences shaped both content and process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were consistently offered and Dylan’s choices were affirmed, preserving his sense of control. The clinician avoided coercive language and respected boundaries while providing guidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Dylan’s preferences and immediate plans (statement choice, accepting a meeting) but did not sufficiently probe underlying motives, values, or confidence to deepen intrinsic motivation. Evocation was present but could be expanded with more exploratory questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the interaction as collaborative and shared power (offering choices, asking which version he preferred). The stance was helper-oriented rather than authoritative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some elicitation of change-related language (agreeing to a brief meeting and factual statement) but little amplification or exploration of reasons, importance, or commitment. Opportunities to deepen desire, need, confidence, and commitment language were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician generally avoided confrontation and mirrored Dylan’s goals, which prevented escalation, but did not systematically use MI techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing) to shift sustain talk. Occasional directive advice (e.g., about removing the age line) read as corrective rather than empathic reframing.
