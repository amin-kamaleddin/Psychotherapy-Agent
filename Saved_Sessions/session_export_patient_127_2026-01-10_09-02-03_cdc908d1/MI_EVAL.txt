# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, affirming progress (e.g., ‘22 days’) and avoiding shame. This fostered safety and engagement throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and validated ambivalence and feelings (goals, low confidence, fear of sounding desperate). Could probe more deeply into identity/exclusion concerns and self-labeling to surface underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on building a pro‑social life and moved the session toward specific, time‑bound actions (CV, call, courses). The clinician consistently linked values/ambivalence to concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a joint endeavor with frequent 'we' language, invited preference on scripts and timing, and co‑constructed the plan. The clinician followed the client’s choices and offered to role‑play and stay on the line.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice and agency by offering options, asking permission, and respecting boundaries (e.g., refusing ‘rubbish’ work). Avoided coercive language while offering partnership and practical help.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client motivations (Fiona, routine, 22 days) and asked targeted questions to raise confidence and steps. At times the clinician moved to solutions quickly instead of deepening client‑generated reasons and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong collaborative stance with shared decision‑making and offers to work alongside the client (role‑play, stay on call). This genuine partnership supported engagement and follow‑through.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (scaling, three‑month vision, concrete step to call probation) and summarized change language. Could more systematically amplify and follow up on change statements to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and redirected toward action, but used few deliberate strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to soften sustain talk or explore underlying fears. More validation plus strategic reframing would strengthen movement away from sustain talk.
