# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed respectful, nonjudgmental regard and normalized Ahmed’s perspective while acknowledging his readiness without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used accurate reflective summaries and elicited Ahmed’s concerns, though deeper reflections of emotion and personal values were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear focus on mini‑cigar use with goal‑oriented tools (confidence ruler, concrete plan) and moved toward measurable behavior change without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as collaborative problem‑solving with patient input at every step and adaptations made to fit Ahmed’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice, asked permission, offered options, and accepted refusals, preserving Ahmed’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Ahmed’s own motivations and ideas (open questions, confidence ruler, planning) but could have probed intrinsic values more deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Ahmed as the expert, co‑created the plan, offered a menu of options, and shared power throughout the encounter.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced multiple concrete change statements and converted intentions into specific actions, though could more frequently amplify spontaneous change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with validation and harm‑reduction strategies rather than confrontation, but more double‑sided reflections could further resolve ambivalence.
