# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent validating statements, though warmth is sometimes delivered in efficient clinical language rather than deeper individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening by paraphrasing practical concerns, but missed opportunities to probe underlying emotions or use more complex reflections to deepen connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on managing cholesterol and the medication decision, with actionable steps and a concrete behavioral outcome (patient will call for baseline labs).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner by offering options and co-producing plans and scripts, though the clinician sometimes defaults to an informational expert role instead of eliciting the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice through offering options, validating the decision to delay meds, and repeatedly asking which approach feels most helpful, reinforcing patient ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to draw out preferences and produced a specific next step, but much of the session relied on clinician-provided information rather than eliciting the patient's intrinsic motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative with shared problem-solving and rehearsals, yet could strengthen partnership by asking the patient to generate more of the monitoring/lifestyle ideas before offering solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-oriented action (calling for labs) via options and planning, but did not systematically prompt or amplify the patient's own desire, reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated sustain talk and offered alternatives to preserve options, but used few double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to actively soften sustain-oriented statements.
