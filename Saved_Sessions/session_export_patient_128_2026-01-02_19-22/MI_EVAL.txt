# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an open, nonjudgmental tone throughout ("Hi — I’m here to listen," multiple validations). Normalized feelings, avoided labeling/shaming, and offered concrete help as supportive options (drafting scripts, role-play). Consistent unconditional positive regard across the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, calming reflections and validations that recognized ambivalence and worries (scholarship, parents, social life). Good perspective-taking but missed some opportunities for deeper affect exploration (more probing/reflection of anxiety/shame around parents and hearing).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear agenda and focus on underage drinking consequences. Set concrete goals (hearing prep, gather citation, contact advocate, harm-reduction) and repeatedly guided session toward action steps without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed choices collaboratively (asked permission, offered options), solicited patient preferences (which script to draft), and incorporated patient wording changes — genuine co-construction and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Emphasized patient control and choice, respected decision not to tell parents, offered multiple options and invited preferences. Reinforced ownership of next steps (practice, gather documents).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited incident details and intrinsic motivations (concern about scholarship, desire to avoid trouble). Used patient language in scripting. Could have more directly elicited internal reasons for change and linked values to risks to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned patient as expert, invited input, adjusted wording per request, and engaged in role-play as an equal partner. Demonstrated clear power-sharing throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change-oriented statements (concern about scholarship, enrollment in course, counseling, harm-reduction plans) and provided tools (hearing script, practice). Missed opportunities for systematic amplification (scaling questions, drawing out importance/confidence, summarizing change talk to build commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and redirected sustain talk toward harm-reduction and practical steps. Acknowledged desire to continue socializing. Could have used more double-sided reflections and explicit summarizing of ambivalence to tip balance toward change.
