# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes discomfort, avoids labeling or shaming, and offers help rather than criticism. Language explicitly supports Ethan's autonomy and choice throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and accurate content reflections that capture ambivalence. Could deepen emotional probing and use more complex reflections to explore shame, embarrassment, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on risky drinking and safety planning with concrete steps (NA drinks, refusal scripts, backup rides). Maintains purposeful trajectory toward harm reduction and behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner with Ethan, co-constructing strategies and integrating his phrasing into plans and texts. Solicits preferences and refines solutions collaboratively rather than prescribing one approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms Ethan's right to choose and offers options without coercion, using open invitations to decide what to practice. Reinforces ownership by asking permission and suggesting tweaks to make plans sound like him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Ethan's reasons for change and personal resources, using questions and a confidence ruler to draw motivation from within. Could strengthen evocation by probing values, future goals, and moving more toward explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the session as a shared project, repeatedly asking what feels realistic and inviting Ethan to co-create and tweak strategies. Role-plays and joint drafting keep the adolescent's voice central in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and builds on change talk (specific first steps, confidence ruler, practice comebacks) and increases commitment language somewhat. Could more consistently amplify and reflect change talk to convert desire/need into stronger, specific commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with minimizations and skillfully redirects toward safer alternatives and safety planning. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge social benefits while naming costs to further soften sustain talk.
