# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing the burden and asking permission before offering ideas. Tone remains respectful and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening, accurately summarizing priorities and practical concerns. Could deepen emotional empathy by more explicitly reflecting underlying feelings like worry or defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors (diet, monitoring) with a set agenda and concrete, behaviorally-specific steps. Repeatedly brings conversation back to implementation and follow-up planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly collaborative, inviting the patient to set priorities and co-create practical plans while offering to practice and support. Language emphasizes joint problem-solving and shared contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and framing plans as the patient’s decisions. Avoids pressure and repeatedly elicits the patient's preferences and commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and concrete change talk linked to family/work reasons and confidence scaling. Could further probe intrinsic values and more deliberately amplify the patient’s expressed reasons and successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative and power-sharing, co-creating steps and inviting input, with supportive offers to practice. At times suggestions are somewhat prescriptive and could be more explicitly offered as adaptable options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly elicits and reinforces change talk, uses scaling and specific planning to build commitment. Sometimes moves quickly to solutions instead of reflecting and amplifying the patient's own change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids arguing and rolls with resistance, steering toward feasible steps tied to priorities. Could increase use of double-sided reflections to explicitly name ambivalence and further soften sustain talk.
