# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Clinician used respectful, nonjudgmental language and explicit affirmations (“You’re doing well not smoking around him,” “That’s a solid, realistic start”). Missed deeper personalized validation of parenting-related guilt/stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective statements (“That sounds exhausting… smoking feels like a relief”), mirrored stressors. Reflections were generally brief and not consistently deepened to explore emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Clear focus on smoking reduction with a concrete, measurable experiment (delay first cigarette 30 minutes) and homework/follow‑up. Could have evoked more before offering multiple specific solutions and linked plan to values (child’s health) more explicitly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Invited choice repeatedly (“Which of those feels most helpful…?”, “Which two…?”) and asked for follow‑up. Clinician still provided many solutions rather than eliciting more of the patient’s own coping ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5  
**Comments:** Strong autonomy affirmation throughout (“not ready to quit is understandable,” “not promising is okay,” framing experiment as optional). Consistently avoided pressure and respected patient’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Elicited past attempts and preferences and used the patient’s concerns to shape options. Missed opportunities to probe deeper motivations, values, and to elicit more client‑generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Framed work as collaborative, asked patient to choose and report back, shared problem‑solving. Clinician nonetheless led agenda and provided many specific tactics rather than co‑constructing solutions from the patient’s language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Some elicitation of change-related statements (“I’ve thought about quitting,” agreed to try delaying) and set up a small‑step experiment. Did not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen change talk (no scaling or confidence/commitment probes).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5  
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and reframed postponing as not denying. Limited use of double‑sided reflections or strategic redirections to shift sustain talk toward change.
