# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful—validated the patient's concerns, honored time and language preferences, and normalized her perspective throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed solid empathic understanding with validations and some reflections (acknowledging hurry and wariness), but missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections about frustration and mistrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on inhaler/controller use and steered the session toward concrete, actionable outcomes (copy‑paste messages, one‑line summaries, step‑down plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑produced and iterated messages with the patient, accepted edits (e.g., wording), and shaped content based on the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice by offering options, asking how she preferred to proceed, and repeatedly emphasizing that final decisions rest with her and her clinician.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited only minimal internal motivation indirectly (agreement to send messages, willingness to ask for lower dose) and did not systematically draw out her reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely fostered partnership through shared decision‑making and co‑creation of materials, though early detailed expert advice sometimes shifted the balance toward an expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supported some practical steps and elicited brief change statements, but did not intentionally evoke the patient's own desires, reasons, or confidence using targeted open questions or scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged sustain talk, validating worries and offering noncoercive options while providing necessary factual cautions about safety.
