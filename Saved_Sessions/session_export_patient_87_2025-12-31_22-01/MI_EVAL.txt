# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates respect and positive regard (e.g., “I appreciate your willingness to reflect,” “You’re the decision‑maker here”). Occasional formulaic praise (“well done,” “I’m proud you’re trying this”) rather than nuanced, individualized normalization; could weave more personalized normalizing statements into reflections.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate paraphrases and reflective listening captured content and difficulty (“It sounds frustrating…”, “You’ve shown you can plan and succeed”). Missed opportunities to explore emotional texture (worry about sounding bossy, discouragement); more feeling+meaning reflections would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on target behavior and concrete next steps (specific snack choices, timing, coping rule, scheduled check‑in). Kept session goal‑focused while advancing to a doable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited Marco’s input and co‑created the plan (“Which would you prioritize…?”). At times slipped into directive advice-giving (repeated suggested scripts), slightly reducing equal‑partner feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Marco’s choice and control (“You’re the decision‑maker here,” offering options and confidence scaling). Avoided pressuring language and invited client judgment about steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited past success and used scaling (past meal‑prep success; 6/10 confidence; asked what would raise to 7). Could better deepen and amplify expressed motivations (hope, reasons for change) with follow‑up questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Marco as expert and tailored plan to his context (work schedule, family). Occasional prescriptive moments (multiple offered phrasings) weakened partnership slightly; ask permission before offering wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and concrete commitments (prep, water+10min, check‑in) but did not consistently amplify or string together desire/reason/ability/commitment. Needs more reflective amplification and strategic summaries to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, addressed environmental cues (tucking treats out of sight) and offered coping strategies to pivot toward change. Could use more double‑sided reflections and strategic summaries to actively tip balance away from sustain talk.
