# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, nonjudgmental positive regard throughout (validated relocation stress, normalized ambivalence). Accepted patient's preference to focus on activity; affirmations supported engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections capturing content and feeling (fog, exhaustion, caution). Reflections deepened patient expression and informed the plan (timing after coffee, permission to bail).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on increasing physical activity. Clinician steered toward a specific, feasible experiment (5–10 min after breakfast, coffee pairing, reminders) and asked concrete implementation questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan and offered options rather than directives. Regularly checked preferences (alarm, sticky note wording/placement) and integrated patient choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized patient control and choice (“treat tomorrow as an experiment you control,” explicit permission to stop). Language preserved agency and reduced pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out patient motivations and personally meaningful outcomes (energy, mood, functional strength). Used open questions and reflections to elicit reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered an equal partnership (use of “we,” soliciting input on details). Clinician integrated lived‑experience knowledge and did not assume unilateral authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited substantial desire/reason language (8/10 readiness; benefits such as more energy, easier jar‑opening), but missed opportunities to convert this into explicit commitment/confidence (no confidence ruler, limited elicited commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated hesitation and reframed ambivalence to avoid strengthening sustain talk. Could have more often used double‑sided reflections or summary reflections to highlight discrepancy and further pivot sustain talk into amplified change talk.
