# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming (explicit statements of unconditional regard, normalizing slips), clearly conveying respect and avoiding labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with solid summaries, but tends to stay at content level rather than exploring deeper emotions or personal meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking with a concrete, measurable experiment and a scheduled follow‑up to track progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative with shared planning and options offered, though the clinician sometimes supplies specifics rather than eliciting more of the patient's own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, normalizing slips, and explicitly inviting the patient's decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort to draw out motivation (readiness ruler, small experiment) produced modest change talk, but intrinsic reasons and personal values were not probed deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a sense of joint problem-solving and co‑constructs a plan, yet could further shift session control to the patient by eliciting more of his ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited tentative change talk via the ruler and experiment, but missed opportunities to amplify and explore personal, intrinsic reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses neutral reflections, but offers limited active softening (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring costs of staying the same).
