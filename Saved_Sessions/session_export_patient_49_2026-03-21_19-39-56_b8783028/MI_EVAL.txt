# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing feelings and framing slips as learning; repeatedly offered choices rather than directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and some reflective listening, but reflections tended to be brief and more informational than deep, affect-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, sustained focus on smoking cessation with concrete, actionable steps (schedules, coping tools, reminders) and minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—co-created the calendar, mantra, and coping tools, sought preferences, and offered hands-on support for implementation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the patient’s freedom to choose and tailored options to their preferences, avoiding pressure and honoring reluctance about meds.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal reasons (appearance, breathing, mantra) and preferences, but could have probed deeper for personally meaningful motivations, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative, power-sharing stance—asked permission, offered options, and repeatedly invited the patient’s input in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew out readiness, preferences, and small-step plans, but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen pro-change statements (e.g., probe the 5/10).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk and honored ambivalence, though could more explicitly use double-sided reflections to pivot back toward change.
