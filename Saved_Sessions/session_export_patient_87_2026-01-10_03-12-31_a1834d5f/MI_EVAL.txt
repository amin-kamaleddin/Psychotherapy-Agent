# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Marco’s ambivalence with specific, nonjudgmental praise. There is no blaming or minimizing, supporting a warm, respectful stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking are evident through accurate summaries, complex reflections, and mirroring Marco's language. The clinician also checks the client's experience and reflects emotional impact, signaling strong understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on sustaining dietary changes and protecting playtime, moving logically from values to concrete, actionable steps. Each question advances planning or clarifies obstacles toward a one-week experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician creates a collaborative tone by soliciting Marco's ideas and building on his contributions. Slightly more explicit co-construction (e.g., asking Marco to summarize the plan or generate troubleshooting before offering praise) would strengthen equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offers options, avoids pressuring language, and asks which small, realistic step Marco would choose, reinforcing his ownership of decisions. This emphasis on choice and preference strongly supports autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from Marco through open questions about values, expected signs of success, and day-to-day differences, eliciting intrinsic motives like being present with his kids. The clinician expands internal motivation and self-efficacy rather than supplying reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently invites Marco's input and frames decisions as his, fostering partnership. A few statements were mildly directive or interpretive, so more explicit power-sharing and co-creation would elevate this further.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicits and amplifies change talk, highlights desire/ability/reasons, and uses targeted questions to deepen and specify plans. These moves consistently increase and clarify Marco's statements in favor of change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged repeatedly but often reflected verbatim, which risks reinforcing it; the clinician misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or eliciting counterarguments. More strategic reframing and targeted questions to diminish sustain talk would improve this area.
