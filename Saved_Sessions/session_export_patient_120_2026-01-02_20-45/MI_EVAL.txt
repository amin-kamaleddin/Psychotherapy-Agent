# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates respect and reassurance throughout (e.g., “Thank you for being honest — that’s hard,” “You’re protecting the baby and your values; that’s strong”). Acceptance is somewhat generic and often followed immediately by advice; missed opportunities for individualized affirmations (no targeted praise of Sofia’s courage or specific efforts). Recommend more individualized affirmations and normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses basic empathic statements (“It’s understandable to feel scared and responsible,” “You’re handling a hard choice”), but limited reflective listening and almost no complex or double‑sided reflections. Clinician frequently shifts quickly to solutions rather than exploring feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy and concrete behavior-change steps (calling OB, mocktails, scripts, exit plan). Maintains target behavior focus across the session and summarizes a clear plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choice at points (“calling your OB, committing to no alcohol, or planning…,” “Which script would you like to practice?”) but interaction is largely clinician-driven: clinician supplies scripts and prescriptive suggestions rather than co‑constructing the plan from Sofia’s language. Suggest inviting the patient to generate lines and solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy‑supporting language (“You can still choose different habits”), but mixed with directive statements (“stop drinking,” prescriptive scripts). Better to ask more frequently what the patient sees as a realistic step and emphasize ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Contains evoking questions (“What would help most this week?”; “Which script would you like to practice?”) but few efforts to draw out deeper motives, values, or confidence. Missed chances to amplify change talk (e.g., “I wanna be responsible…” was not explored further).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some inclusive prompts but overall expert stance — clinician supplies most language and solutions. Partnership could be strengthened by co‑creating scripts using Sofia’s own words and foregrounding her knowledge of her friends.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits limited change talk (patient expresses desire/responsibility: “I wanna be responsible… I don’t wanna let my kid down”), but clinician moves to scripting rather than reflecting/amplifying and eliciting commitment/confidence. Recommend follow‑ups like “What makes that important?” and confidence/commitment probes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk present (“I can’t just cut off my friends…part of me still wants to live that out”) was mainly met with advice and strategies rather than double‑sided reflections or normalization. Needs more rolling with resistance, reflective summaries of ambivalence, and reframing to reduce defensiveness.
