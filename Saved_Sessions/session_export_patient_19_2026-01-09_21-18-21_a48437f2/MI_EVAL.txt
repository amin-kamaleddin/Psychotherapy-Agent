# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is warm, nonjudgmental, and normalizes the situation, validating the patient's desire for help. More individualized affirmations or explicit normalization of ambivalence would push this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is basic attention to the patient's perspective and safety, but few reflections of feeling or deeper paraphrases. The interaction leans toward advice over reflective listening that would capture emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session consistently stays focused on gambling and harm-reduction with clear, actionable steps. Every response is tied to concrete first-week actions and risk-reduction strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks preferences and adapts language but primarily supplies ready-made scripts and plans. Stronger collaboration would involve co-creating solutions and eliciting the patient's ideas more.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options are offered and permission is sought, framing suggestions as tools the patient can use. More explicit emphasis on the patient's control and invitations to modify steps would increase autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician links supports to the patient's goal (travel) but uses few open questions or techniques to draw out internal motivations. The approach favors problem-solving over exploring values or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments and choices offered, but much of the session is clinician-driven with prescriptive plans. Greater co-creation and invitations for the patient to choose or adapt steps would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-oriented statements are elicited and reinforced with practical tools, but the clinician rarely amplifies the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence. More open questions and reflective amplification would build stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids validating sustain talk and redirects to safeguards, but uses few MI techniques like double-sided reflections or normalization of ambivalence. Redirects are mostly advisory rather than nuanced reframing.
