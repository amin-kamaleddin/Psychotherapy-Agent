# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with frequent validation and normalization of Amina's feelings and caregiving motives; minimal blaming or shaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and accurate reflections of stress and parenting impact; could deepen empathy by reflecting ambivalence and more complex emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on co‑parenting routines and safety throughout, sequencing problem identification, strategies, scripts, rehearsal, and post‑action care in a goal‑oriented way.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates an interactive feel by inviting choices and rehearsal but frequently supplies ready‑made scripts and exact plans, which reduces co‑creation and patient ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options, checks permission, and frames guidance as coaching which supports autonomy; some directive, prescriptive language could be balanced with more elicitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits values (love for her son) and some motivation but relies heavily on clinician‑provided strategies rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons, strengths, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows elements of partnership (choices, rehearsal) but interaction is clinician‑driven with many scripts rather than co‑constructed language, making partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change‑relevant statements and offers options, but often moves quickly to solutions instead of systematically evoking and amplifying the client's desire, reasons, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reframes toward practical solutions, but misses opportunities for double‑sided or complex reflections that would better validate ambivalence and shift toward change.
