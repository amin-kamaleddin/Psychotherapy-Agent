# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and positive (asked permission, repeated validations). Honored patient requests (included exact wording, formats, PDF/plain‑text). No minimization or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good attunement and frequent reflective/validating comments; mirrors patient priorities (safety, work/family). Reflections tended to be surface‑level and were often followed quickly by facts rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on target behavior (medication switch/washout). Gave concrete safety guidance (contraindication, 14‑day washout), monitoring plan, ER vitals, and specific next steps (follow‑up timing, checklist).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance—asked permission, solicited format preferences, co‑created handouts and checklists. Slightly clinician‑led on clinical logistics; could more explicitly invite patient input on follow‑up timing and trade‑offs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported patient choice and cautioned against unilateral changes. Provided tools to facilitate patient decision‑making and respected the patient's readiness to wait for a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited factual details and documentation preferences but did not sufficiently draw out the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence about switching; limited elicitation of motivational content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership by negotiating formats, offering previews, and asking delivery preferences. At times clinician took the lead on safety and plan details rather than explicitly co‑authoring contingency decisions with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal elicitation and amplification of change talk. Patient expressed change‑oriented concerns and preferences but clinician did not probe desires, reasons, or confidence or summarize/amplify those statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized caution and accepted sustain talk ("not ready to switch"), offering safety plans instead of pressure. Could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections to surface discrepancy and move ambivalence toward change.
