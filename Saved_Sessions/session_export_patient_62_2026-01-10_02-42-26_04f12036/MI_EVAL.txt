# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental support, normalized Jordan's struggles, and offered multiple validations. Affirmations of small progress communicated unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, affect-laden reflections matched Jordan's low energy and discouraged tone and were offered timely and consistently. The clinician acknowledged feelings (exhaustion, skepticism) in a way that demonstrated understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear behavioral focus on a single micro-task (draft/send follow-up) and a 7‑day reminder, keeping work targeted and concrete. Interventions were tied directly to the stated goal, preventing drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician behaved as an equal partner, soliciting and incorporating Jordan's input when shaping drafts, tone, and follow‑up plans. Options and permission-based offers framed the work as shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered (whether to get help, tone options, whether to send), reinforcing Jordan's control over next steps. Skepticism was accepted while still supporting action, preserving autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician evoked enough internal motivation to produce action (Jordan sent the email) but did not probe deeper into values, longer-term goals, or confidence. Deeper questions about meaning or confidence scaling could have strengthened autonomous reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction emphasized co-construction (asking permission before offering drafts, inviting tweaks, checking preferences). The clinician repeatedly invited Jordan's choices and followed his lead in shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited and reinforced change talk (desire for things to improve, willingness to try a micro-task, sending the email). Missed opportunities included amplifying and exploring change language with follow-ups about meaning, confidence, or ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validated skepticism without reinforcing hopelessness and pivoted to a small actionable step, which reduced sustain talk. Earlier use of double-sided reflections or ambivalence summaries would have further softened sustain talk.
