# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental language that validated Ingrid's context and decision to ask for help; warm supportive tone with no signs of criticism or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated perspective-taking and some reflective language (acknowledged time pressure and validated asking), but did not explore deeper feelings or worries about switching beyond safety concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on the medication interaction and safe switching/taper/washout, avoiding irrelevant topic drift and moving efficiently to concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as true collaboration by inviting input, co-writing the SMS, and framing problem-solving jointly; clinician did not dominate decision-making and followed Ingrid's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirmed Ingrid's choices, offered options, and avoided pressuring language while emphasizing she should coordinate with her prescriber, supporting patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited limited information and focused on practical actions but missed opportunities to draw out Ingrid's motivations, values, or confidence about the medication change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the exchange as collaborative and invited preferences, co-creating the plan and SMS, though the clinician led medically and could have better incorporated Ingrid's ideas into decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered concrete actions and affirmed intent to act, but did not actively elicit Ingrid's reasons for change, confidence, or stronger commitment language beyond procedural agreement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and emphasized safety and gradual planning; could have added double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge any reluctance or ambivalence.
