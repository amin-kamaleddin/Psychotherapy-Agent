# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respect and positive regard (apologized for wait, validated frustration) and avoided blaming or shaming throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections picked up key details and acknowledged frustration, but tended to be factual and solution-focused rather than deeply reflecting emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed firmly on target (weight and BP) with progressive movement from problem identification to concrete, immediate actions like BP checks and specific meal options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone was collaborative and responsive to patient preferences, though parts of the encounter became clinician-led with detailed instructions rather than co-constructed problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently offered choices, framed suggestions as options, and honored the patient's constraints, reinforcing the patient's agency in decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some commitment was elicited (BP check, trying a meal) but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient's internal reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered shared decision-making and tailored options to the patient, yet occasionally shifted into an expert/instructional stance instead of eliciting the patient's own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician created openings for change talk and secured concrete commitments but did not consistently deepen or amplify the patient's own motivations or explore why change matters to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by offering feasible, low-effort options that addressed time barriers, though techniques like double-sided reflections or explicit normalization of ambivalence were used sparingly.
