# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized feelings, using nonjudgmental language and explicit statements of support throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking with paraphrases and emotion acknowledgement, but reflections were often factual and could probe deeper emotions like guardedness or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on completing the community service hours, steering the conversation through logistics to a concrete plan (Saturday 10am, bring ID and contact).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative—co-creating scripts, role-playing, and negotiating steps with repeated check-ins about what Julian wanted to do next.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Julian's choice and control by offering options, framing suggestions as optional, and deferring to his preferences about next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical planning and commitments but did little to draw out intrinsic motivation, values, or personal reasons for change, relying more on offered solutions than evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally conveyed partnership by inviting input and co-creating solutions, though at times moved into an expert role without explicitly inviting Julian to put ideas into his own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited action-oriented change talk and concrete commitments through planning and rehearsal, but missed opportunities to amplify personal motives beyond avoiding trouble.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and defused ambivalence by focusing on logistics and choices, though stronger use of double-sided reflections or reframing could further soften resistance.
