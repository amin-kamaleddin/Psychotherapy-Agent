# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—normalized and validated the client's experience and honored requests (e.g., skip Thursdays, vibration-only alerts). The clinician avoided judgment and affirmed the client's feelings throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and checked symptoms while adapting to practical concerns (sleep, distraction, dinner conflicts). Reflections remained at a practical level and did not deeply explore underlying emotions like anxiety or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on preparing for the chemistry test and translated concerns into concrete, achievable actions (Pomodoro blocks, calendar entries, Day 3 check-in). The clinician kept momentum on the target behavior throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicited and integrated the client's preferences (timing, labels, alert type) and co-created the experiment and follow-up. Decisions were made jointly rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and framing the plan as voluntary and time-limited. Emphasized client control over timing, misses, and follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some commitment (agreement to try, Day 3 check-in) but relied heavily on problem-solving and solution delivery. Missed opportunities to draw out intrinsic motivation, values, importance, or confidence using evocative questions or scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as a true partner—asked for preferences, adapted the plan to the client's choices, and shared power in decision-making. The plan reflected the client's input at each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempted to elicit change talk by offering a one-week experiment and asking about willingness, but quickly shifted to logistics and advice. More amplification of the client's own reasons, desires, and confidence would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled skepticism and hedging nonconfrontationally by normalizing doubts and framing a low-pressure experiment, which defused resistance. Could further benefit from double-sided reflections or explicitly acknowledging both the pull of old habits and reasons for trying.
