# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and respect, explicitly honoring the patient's request to avoid quitting talk and using a nonjudgmental, validating tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately paraphrasing Seamus's feelings and attending to his practical worries about anesthetic and driving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Started with a clear steer toward smoking cessation but appropriately shifted to the patient's dental priorities when met with resistance; maintained a gentle open door for future quitting discussion but offered limited sustained steering toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—asked clarifying questions, negotiated the agenda, involved Seamus in decisions, and offered to communicate with the dentist, sharing power in the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by repeatedly affirming choice, offering options rather than directives, and respecting the patient's decision to prioritize dental care.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made an initial open invitation to explore reasons/ barriers but did not follow up to draw out motivations, use scaling, or probe for past successes, so evocation was under‑used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the interaction as a partnership—invited preferences, asked permission before suggestions, and collaboratively built the plan with Seamus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Asked an appropriate open question early but failed to amplify or deepen the limited change language offered and missed simple techniques (reflections, scaling) to elicit more change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided confrontation, validated resistance, and redirected to harm‑reduction strategies and practical steps, though more strategic double‑sided reflections could have nudged ambivalence further.
