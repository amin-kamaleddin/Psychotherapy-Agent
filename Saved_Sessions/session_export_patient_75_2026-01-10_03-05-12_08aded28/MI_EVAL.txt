# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated respect and positive regard (e.g., validating, nonjudgmental opening, normalizing struggles). The clinician avoided blaming language and conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections of feelings and fears and acknowledged anxiety and guilt, but some reflections were brief and often followed by clinical information. Deeper, more complex reflections of emotion and impact would strengthen empathy to a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on asthma management with concrete, behavior-linked outcomes and a stepwise action plan. The clinician consistently steered toward small testable behaviors (spacer + rinsing; tracking; 1‑month check).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the encounter as joint problem-solving and co-created the plan with the patient (choices, edits to PCP message, scheduling preferences). Contributions were balanced and the plan was mutually shaped.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered clear choices, asked which option felt helpful, and respected the patient's hesitancy, reinforcing that the decision was hers. Language avoided pressure and consistently affirmed client choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete commitments (willing to try spacer+rinsing, request for 1‑month check) and practical concerns, but relied at times on information/advice rather than systematically drawing out personal reasons and confidence. More use of importance/confidence scaling and values exploration would increase evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently positioned the patient as a partner, sought her input, and invited her to decide next steps (A/B option, edits to message, scheduling). Power-sharing was evident and Linda's contributions shaped the session materially.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, generating concrete client statements of willingness and a plan, but could have amplified and explored why change matters and used scaling to deepen commitment. Strategic reflections and summaries of change talk would strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated sustain talk without arguing and reframed risks in an MI-consistent way, reducing fear, but relied more on informational reframing than MI techniques like double-sided reflections or selective amplification of pro-change statements. More reflective redirection would better pivot away from sustain talk.
