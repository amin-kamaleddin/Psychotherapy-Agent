# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming from the start, normalizing difficulty and acknowledging barriers without blame. Frequent affirmations communicated respect for Marcus’s efforts and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and perspective-taking throughout, capturing both content and feeling (practical limits, values, ambivalence). Demonstrates strong reflective listening that conveys understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on increasing activity and moves logically from values/barriers to specific, behaviorally concrete steps. Repeatedly brings conversation back to timing, plans, and confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as true co-creation: clinician offers options and drafts messages while Marcus chooses wording, timing, and tone. The clinician adjusts to Marcus’s input and only implements what he prefers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Marcus’s sense of choice by offering options and checking preferences before acting. Language and procedures reinforced Marcus’s ownership of the plan without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly elicits Marcus’s own reasons, ideas, and resources with targeted open questions, drawing out values (family, fun) and concrete plans. Amplifies intrinsic motivators rather than imposing external reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructs the plan with frequent checks about Marcus’s preferences and invites his decisions on details. Power is shared throughout—the clinician proposes and Marcus decides.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk using open questions, confidence scaling, and planning prompts, which produced concrete commitment language. Clinician amplified and affirmed emerging desire and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward small, sustainable options, but missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections and exploring the emotional impact of past failures. Overall effective at not reinforcing sustain talk but could more deliberately normalize relapse and reduce shame.
