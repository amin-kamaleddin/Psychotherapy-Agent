# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, supportive tone with normalizing statements and practical help. Could add more explicit individualized affirmations to convey unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed some basic validation and recognition of anxiety but used limited reflective listening. Interaction relied more on instructions and scripts than on exploring or paraphrasing the client's feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on completing court-ordered community service and provided clear, concrete next steps. Nearly every intervention directly addressed the target behavior and practical barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered tools and invited choices, tailoring materials to the client's requests, which reflected co-construction. However, the clinician still largely drove solutions rather than eliciting the client's own problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported choices by offering options, validating texting, and asking permission before drafting messages. Could further reinforce autonomy by explicitly acknowledging the client's control and asking which approach he prefers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made minimal attempts to elicit the client's internal motivations or values, focusing instead on logistical removal of barriers. Missed opportunities to ask evocative questions about why finishing matters or Explore confidence and concerns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Adopted an expert/information-giving stance with step-by-step instructions and scripts, offering few invitations for the client's ideas. Lacked explicit statements sharing power or asking which options felt doable to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Encouraged some change talk indirectly by removing barriers and reinforcing small commitments. Rarely used open, evocative questions to elicit the client's own reasons, desires, or intentions for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or pressuring and sometimes redirected away from sustain talk through instructions. Missed MI strategies like double-sided reflections to acknowledge reluctance while highlighting reasons for action.
