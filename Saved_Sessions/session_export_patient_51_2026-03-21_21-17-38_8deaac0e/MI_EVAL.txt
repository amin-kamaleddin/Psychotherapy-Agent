# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, normalized Ingrid's context, and avoided judgmental language. Repeated affirmations (e.g., "I’m here to support your choices") reinforced warmth and acceptance throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good understanding of Ingrid's time pressure and pragmatic needs by offering brief, practical options. Deeper reflective exploration of underlying feelings or anxieties about switching meds was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on safe medication switching and the MAOI/SSRI washout, providing concise, accurate biomedical information. Practical next steps (taper, contact prescriber, follow-up) were emphasized and the conversation did not drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—decisions were negotiated and Ingrid's preferences shaped the content, tone, and timing of follow-up. The clinician acted as a resource working alongside the patient rather than directing unilaterally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient autonomy by offering choices (text vs call, inclusion of doctor's name, tone) and avoiding pressuring language. The clinician drafted scripts but left decision-making and action to Ingrid.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited actionable commitments (agreeing to send the text, call after appointment, accept 3:00 PM check-in) through targeted, pragmatic questions. Could strengthen evocation by using brief open questions or a confidence ruler to draw out Ingrid's own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently acted as a collaborative partner, co-creating the text and inviting Ingrid to steer timing and content. The clinician invited preferences and tailored the intervention to her needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively encouraged concrete change-oriented language and steps (clarifying meds, arranging washout, drafting text, scheduling follow-up). Missed opportunities to deepen change talk through scaling questions or reflective amplifications of Ingrid's motivations and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, validated caution about stopping abruptly, and normalized non-emergency status while emphasizing safety. Could use more explicit double-sided reflections or strategic reframing when stronger sustain talk appears.
