# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established and maintained unconditional positive regard with explicit nonjudgmental language and consistent validation; avoided labeling, shaming, or lecturing throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and summaries that acknowledged ambivalence and linked events to feelings; could have probed more deeply into the emotional meaning of the fall and the son's concern using more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on alcohol behavior and progressed logically from assessment to goal‑setting and concrete planning; each intervention tied directly to changing drinking while remaining client‑centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the session as a joint exploration by asking permission, offering options, role‑playing, and adapting plans to Evelyn's preferences; decisions were co‑constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Evelyn's control and choice, offered options instead of directives, and repeatedly asked which strategies felt doable for her; respected her boundaries (e.g., no lecture if she slips).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation with open questions, scaling, and client‑generated strategies that increased confidence; could have further evoked values, long‑term reasons, and past successes to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative balance of power by inviting Evelyn's perspective, offering expertise as options, and co‑creating the plan; consistently positioned decisions as hers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated it into concrete steps and a follow‑up plan; would benefit from more active amplification and deeper questions to increase frequency and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used reflections and redirection toward small changes, which prevented reinforcing sustain talk; could have used more explicit double‑sided reflections to gently highlight discrepancy and tip the balance toward change.
