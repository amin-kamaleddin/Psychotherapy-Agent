# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed respect and positive regard, normalized small wins, and avoided blaming or labeling throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and validation of low energy were present, but empathic responses were sometimes brief and often followed quickly by advice rather than deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on increasing physical activity with concrete, measurable steps (scheduled walk, reminders, backup plan, tracking) and repeated summaries of the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was a strong collaborative tone and co‑creation of the plan with patient input, though the clinician sometimes shifted toward instructive guidance with many specific strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offered choices, affirmed the 5‑minute option as valid, and invited tweaks, but some directive phrasing and multiple proposed solutions slightly reduced autonomous elicitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (choosing doable options and committing to a time), but the clinician spent more time offering solutions than drawing out the patient's values, long‑term reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician engaged the patient in decision‑making and checked permission for follow‑up, yet occasional expert/advice giving (e.g., 'non‑negotiable' language, lists of tips) limited equal‑partner balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced commitment language (selected plan, set time, arranged check‑in) but missed opportunities to deepen change talk by exploring personal reasons, values, or scaling importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized small wins, and offered low‑effort backups and concrete problem‑solving, though could have used more double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
