# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and validated Richard's perspective, reducing threat and maintaining support throughout the session. Minor opportunity to name specific strengths earlier, but overall acceptance was strong and steady.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, multilevel reflections that captured both content and affect, which lowered resistance and built rapport. Could have probed the deeper emotional meaning of the ritual more, but empathic precision was high.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on the one-week smoking-reduction experiment with concrete actions and check-in plans. Summaries and refocusing prevented drift and maintained momentum toward a specific behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shaped the plan around Richard's rules and preferences, negotiating terms and soliciting his input throughout. The clinician clearly adopted a partner stance rather than an expert-driven approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly emphasized choice, deferred decisions to Richard, and honored his conditions (start day, no lectures, stop anytime). This consistent respect for self-determination lowered pressure and supported engagement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked evocative questions and drew out past successes and motivations, but much change talk remained externally motivated (keeping the peace). Could have probed more to elicit deeper internal motives and identity-related reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed the interaction as collaborative, invited Richard's rules, and allowed him to direct the experiment. Power-sharing and shared decision-making were evident throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change language and affirmed small steps (delay, swap one cigarette), which increased commitment to an experiment. More strategic amplification of intrinsic motivators and importance/confidence scaling could have strengthened commitment further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and reframed sustain talk into neutral exploration and experiments, which prevented escalation. The clinician relied mainly on neutral reflections and logistical redirection rather than double-sided reflections that explicitly juxtapose sustain talk with values.
