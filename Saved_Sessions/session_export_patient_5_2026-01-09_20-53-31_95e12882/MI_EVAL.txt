# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizes ambivalence, and praises responsible behavior in a warm, individualized way. Validation is nonjudgmental and tailored rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, summarizing both concrete worries and emotional tone. Reflections are timely and deepen the patient's expression.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on safe initiation of citalopram while on phenelzine and repeatedly steers toward concrete safety planning. Direction is purposeful and linked to the patient's expressed concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a mutual project, offering to draft scripts and role-play while iteratively refining the plan with the patient. The clinician contributes expertise but keeps the patient's ideas central.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors autonomy by offering choices, checking preferences, and asking for voluntary small steps. Language emphasizes patient ownership without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's motivation with open questions and targeted follow-ups that produce concrete commitments (e.g., calling the prescriber). Focus stays on the patient's internal reasons for safety and action.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed, repeatedly soliciting preferences and offering options rather than directives. Power is genuinely shared and the patient's expertise is honored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evokes concrete change talk and a specific action plan (calling the prescriber and arranging monitoring), but could more consistently amplify motivations and elicit stronger commitment/confidence language. Adding scaling questions or reflections linking safety values to broader goals would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges concerns without reinforcing hesitancy and redirects toward planning, which avoids sustaining resistance. Could more actively use double-sided reflections or explicit affirmation of sustain talk to gently tip toward change.
