# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and offers options, acknowledging the patient's low concern; occasionally responses feel more corrective/informational than warmly normalizing ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are empathic validations and attention to practical constraints, but reflective listening is limited and the clinician often shifts to facts/advice rather than deeply exploring the patient's perspective or minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on oral tobacco use and harm‑reduction/cessation options, with every response tied to concrete questions and practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frames the interaction as joint problem‑solving and tailors recommendations to the patient's work context, but the exchange still leans toward clinician‑driven advice rather than fully equal co‑creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasizes patient choice, offers multiple options, and reassures the patient they can stop any intervention at any time, supporting perceived control and reducing pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions about frequency and readiness elicited a tentative willingness to try a trial, but the clinician primarily provided information rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and offers options, supporting shared decision making, but often defaults to expert language and prescriptive instructions instead of co‑creating solutions with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are intermittent attempts to elicit steps toward change that resulted in a tentative 1‑week trial, yet the clinician does not systematically evoke or amplify change talk with targeted open questions about importance or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids direct argument and acknowledges readiness, but responds to minimization largely with corrective information instead of using double‑sided reflections or other MI techniques to pivot sustain talk toward change.
