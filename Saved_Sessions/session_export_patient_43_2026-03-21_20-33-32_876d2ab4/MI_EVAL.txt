# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with a warm, nonjudgmental stance, affirmations, and respect for privacy. Honors the patient's minimization and requests without confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows accurate paraphrases and perspective-taking that validate the patient's experience. Reflections remain mostly summary-level and would benefit from deeper, feeling-focused exploration of emotions like fear or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking and concrete, testable behavioral goals throughout the session. Questions, reflections, and suggestions consistently tie back to the target behavior and barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative with co-constructed decisions that reflect the patient's preferences. Clinician solicits input at each step and follows the patient's lead on sensitive issues.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and honoring the patient's decisions (e.g., not involving his wife). Language repeatedly reinforces the patient's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits pragmatic, proximal motivations and uses open questions, scaling, and planning to draw out change talk. Could do more to evoke deeper intrinsic motives and amplify offered change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as a collaborator and an expert on his life, co-creating the plan and inviting ongoing input. Demonstrates genuine power sharing in decision-making and planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and supports small-step change talk through pros/cons, scaling, and concrete planning, producing volunteered change language. Would strengthen impact by reflecting and amplifying those statements and probing the reasons behind importance/confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses neutral-to-directional pivots and double-sided reflections to shift toward small change. Could more strategically use stronger double-sided and cost-emphasizing reflections to further diminish sustain statements like 'I feel fine.'
