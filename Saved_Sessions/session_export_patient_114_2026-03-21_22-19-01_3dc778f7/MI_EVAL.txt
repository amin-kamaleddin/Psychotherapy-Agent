# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized Ryan’s experience while affirming his efforts to stay safe. This warmth and validation supported a strong therapeutic stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate reflections and paraphrasing to show understanding of Ryan’s efforts and concerns. Deeper affective reflections (e.g., exploring embarrassment or disappointment) would strengthen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on changing drinking-related behavior and aligned strategies with Ryan’s values (protecting grades, avoiding police trouble). Concrete action steps and scaling questions maintained clear goal-directed work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative—clinician solicited Ryan’s input, validated his ideas, and co-constructed practical plans like texting Mark and pre-booking a ride. The clinician repeatedly checked preferences and honored Ryan’s choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported Ryan’s choice by asking permission before offering advice and framing suggestions as optional fits for his life. This reinforced that the change plan belonged to Ryan and avoided coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited goals and specific change steps and used scaling to draw out motivation and confidence. More evocative, open questions linking values to long-term reasons for change would deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician adopted a collaborative stance and co-created plans, but offering many concrete suggestions in quick succession occasionally shifted authority back to the clinician. Eliciting more of Ryan’s own solutions before proposing options would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully elicited and amplified change talk, used a confidence ruler, and helped generate specific commitments (texting Mark, pre-booking ride). Deliberate expansion of ambivalent statements and stronger DARN-C framing could increase commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided challenging sustain talk but missed opportunities to strategically soften it with double-sided reflections or reframing. More selective reflection of rationalizations and eliciting costs of the status quo would reduce sustain talk more effectively.
