# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Erik's feelings and avoiding labels or shame. Frequent validations reinforced unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking to mirror Erik's emotions and concerns, which kept him engaged and softened defensiveness. Summaries and reflections demonstrated strong understanding of his viewpoint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on diet and alcohol reduction tied to diabetes and guided the session toward a specific, measurable action. The conversation progressed logically from concerns to an actionable plan and supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as team problem-solving and invited tweaks, conveying mutual contribution. At times the clinician provided clinician-generated solutions (recipes, exact wording) more than eliciting spontaneous client ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Erik's choice and control by offering options and asking his preference, reinforcing ownership of decisions. Even with concrete help, the clinician emphasized Erik’s freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited change-oriented statements by asking importance/confidence and noting family/work reasons, producing a concrete first step. Missed deeper evocation opportunities to explore personal values, discrepancy, or amplify intrinsic motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-created the plan and checked preferences, which supported collaboration and shared decision-making. However, frequent offering of specific solutions tilted toward an expert-led approach rather than eliciting more of Erik’s own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out and reinforced change talk and raised confidence through concrete options and planning. There were missed chances to deepen and amplify motivations and commitment language beyond planning details.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain talk while redirecting to small steps, which reduced resistance. The clinician relied more on straightforward validation than on strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) that could further soften sustain talk.
