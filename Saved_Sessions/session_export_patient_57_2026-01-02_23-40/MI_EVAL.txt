# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Respectful, nonjudgmental tone
- [x] Normalizing statements
- [x] Affirmation of goals/values

Comments: Clinician consistently respectful and validating (multiple normalizing statements and affirmations). Interaction is pragmatic and solution‑focused; lacks deeper, individualized expressions of unconditional positive regard.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Reflective listening / paraphrase of concerns
- [x] Perspective‑taking statements
- [x] Use of open questions to invite patient's view
- [ ] Complex/emotion‑level reflections (shame, fear, identity) were limited

Comments: Good, accurate reflections and perspective taking that captured ambivalence; missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional nuance (fear of rejection/shame).

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
Checklist:
- [x] Maintained clear focus on target behavior (alcohol/team eligibility)
- [x] Moved toward concrete action steps (coach line, refusals, repair steps)
- [x] Used role‑plays and practice to steer toward change

Comments: Highly purposeful and action‑oriented; consistently steered conversation to specific, doable steps and practiced them with the patient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Asked patient's preference/choice (coach vs refusals)
- [x] Offered to role‑play / practice together
- [x] Shared problem solving with patient
- [ ] At times more directive (offered scripted lines before eliciting patient's own wording)

Comments: Collaborative stance with frequent offers to practice and choice; sometimes moved quickly to clinician scripting rather than eliciting Jamal’s own language.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Offered options and respected patient's choice
- [x] Asked permission to try wording/practice
- [x] Elicited patient commitment/plan (patient agreed to meet coach, set boundaries)
- [ ] Could have asked more about confidence/ability (0–10 style)

Comments: Supported autonomy by offering choices and asking permission; could strengthen by explicitly eliciting confidence and encouraging patient's own phrasing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
Checklist:
- [x] Asked about small, doable steps
- [x] Asked patient to choose between options (eliciting a plan)
- [ ] Limited elicitation of deeper motives/values
- [ ] Limited amplification of spontaneous change talk

Comments: Some evocation present (choice, plan), but clinician relied more on offering scripts than systematically drawing out and amplifying Jamal’s own motivations and commitment language.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
Checklist:
- [x] Worked alongside patient (role‑play coach/teammate)
- [x] Expressed willingness to help/practice
- [x] Maintained collaborative tone throughout
- [ ] Could further promote co‑construction of language (have patient generate phrasing first)

Comments: Strong partner role via role‑plays and offers to assist; could increase patient ownership by eliciting Jamal’s own phrasing before offering clinician scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
Checklist:
- [x] Elicited change statements (patient: “I’ll meet the coach,” “I’ll set boundaries”)
- [x] Helped translate concerns into concrete steps
- [ ] Did not consistently amplify or summarize change talk to strengthen commitment
- [ ] Few confidence‑eliciting questions to reinforce readiness

Comments: Generated and captured some change talk, but missed opportunities to selectively reflect/amplify those statements and to assess/raise confidence toward firm commitments.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
Checklist:
- [x] Acknowledged sustain talk (peer pressure, fear of looking weak)
- [x] Redirected toward protective alternatives and refusal strategies
- [ ] Limited use of double‑sided reflections or reframing to lessen sustain language
- [ ] Minimal strategic rolling with resistance techniques

Comments: Sustain talk was acknowledged and redirected to alternatives and repair steps; however, MI‑specific softening techniques (double‑sided reflections, reframing) were infrequent.
