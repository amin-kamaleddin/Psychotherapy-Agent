# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, validated Elias’s experience, and used a supportive nonjudgmental tone while normalizing caution and allowing him to stop if pain increased.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking were used throughout (paraphrases of feelings and limits), building rapport and demonstrating clear empathic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on increasing safe physical activity with forward-moving, specific questions that culminated in a concrete, time-limited plan (five minutes after coffee for a week).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the work as a joint enterprise, soliciting Elias’s preferences, incorporating his responses into the plan, and offering supports contingent on his requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were offered repeatedly, the plan was framed as an experiment, and permission to stop if pain increased was explicitly affirmed, reinforcing Elias’s control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used evoking questions to draw out Elias’s own reasons, values, and resources, building the plan from his motivations rather than prescribing solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with shared power: the clinician invited input, co-created the plan, and let Elias set pace and accept supports he wanted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively elicited and reinforced into a specific action trial, but there were missed opportunities to more strongly amplify desire/ability/reasons or elicit commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged, normalized, and safety-framed, reducing resistance, yet the clinician primarily reflected sustain talk rather than using strategic reframes or double-sided reflections to tilt toward change.
