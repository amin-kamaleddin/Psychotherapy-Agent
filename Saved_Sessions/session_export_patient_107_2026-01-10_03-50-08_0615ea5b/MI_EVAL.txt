# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, offering low‑pressure options and normalizing exploration throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective‑taking with clear summaries of ambivalence, but could deepen emotional reflections to capture underlying feelings and tension.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on coffee consumption and concrete next steps (pros/cons, tracking, reminders), keeping the agenda tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine collaboration by soliciting preferences, co‑creating the tracking plan, and asking permission before offering tools, with the client's choices shaping outcomes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports the client's autonomy through multiple options and repeated affirmations of whatever the client decides, using neutral, nonpressuring interventions like tracking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes attempts to draw out motivation (choices, pros/cons) but does not consistently use deeper evocation strategies (values, scaling, decisional probes) to amplify intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the client as the expert and co‑creates the plan with repeated checks about what feels doable, demonstrating clear shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits modest change talk via options and weighing pros/cons but misses opportunities to follow up, reflect, and amplify change language to build momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses neutral reframing and low‑pressure experiments, though more double‑sided reflections could better acknowledge sustain talk while steering toward change.
