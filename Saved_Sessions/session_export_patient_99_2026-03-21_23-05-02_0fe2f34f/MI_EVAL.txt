# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Matteo's ambivalence, using validating language and affirmations. The clinician avoided blame or moralizing and acknowledged Matteo's priorities and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, frequent reflective listening and perspective-taking that mirrored Matteo's concerns and language. Reflections prompted deeper self-reflection and showed understanding of his priorities (school, safety, social life).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related risk and moved the conversation toward a specific, actionable plan. Questions progressively narrowed options and finalized concrete steps (alternating, texting Mark, bringing a soda).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as a genuine team effort with the clinician soliciting Matteo's ideas, preferences, and exact wording. The clinician co‑constructed the plan and continually checked what felt doable for Matteo.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Matteo's sense of choice by offering options and avoiding pressuring language. The clinician invited Matteo to select strategies and respected his preferred wording and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out Matteo's own motivations, resources, and past successes to build internal motivation. Questions elicited intrinsic reasons and practical supports that produced commitment-oriented language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making and positioned Matteo as the expert on his life, regularly asking open questions and inviting his input. The clinician checked wording and preferences, reinforcing joint ownership of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated it into concrete, achievable steps (alternating, who to ask, bringing a soda). These prompts produced explicit commitment-like language and a plan Matteo agreed to send.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and often redirected toward change, but missed opportunities for stronger softening techniques like explicit double-sided reflections. Could have explored the underlying needs served by social drinking to resolve ambivalence more fully.
