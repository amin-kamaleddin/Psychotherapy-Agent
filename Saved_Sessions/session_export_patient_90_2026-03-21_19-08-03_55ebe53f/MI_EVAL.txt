# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently shows respect and nonjudgmental support, validates Marcus’s feelings, and honors his refusal to rehome the dog. Could add more explicit normalizing of ambivalence and stronger unconditional-acceptance statements to reach a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (recognizing exhaustion, attachment to the dog, and financial stress) with concrete supportive actions. More complex emotional reflections and softer, less solution-driven responses at times would deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on improving asthma control throughout, linking assessment to concrete clinic-usable steps (inhaler check, scripts, referrals). Conversation stays tightly aligned with the target behavior and actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative with offers to draft questions, role-play, and respond to Marcus’s requests, positioning clinician as a partner. Could increase bidirectional idea-generation by asking Marcus to propose solutions and choose which options feel most doable.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by asking permission, offering options rather than directives, and respecting Marcus’s decision about the dog. Language and choices reinforce Marcus’s sense of control without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some willingness to act (inhaler check, dander steps) but does not systematically draw out Marcus’s internal motivations, values, or confidence. Lacks scaling or questions about personal importance, past successes, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites collaboration (drafting questions, role-play, shared planning) and honors Marcus’s preferences in the plan. Could more explicitly solicit Marcus’s ideas for first steps to strengthen shared ownership of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and supports small commitments, with practical reinforcement of action steps. However, the clinician rarely uses open evocative questions or reflections to amplify desire, reasons, need, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk by offering respectful alternatives (dander-reduction strategies, testing, immunotherapy) and acknowledging barriers like cost. Could employ more double-sided reflections and reframing to explicitly acknowledge both sides and invite movement toward change.
