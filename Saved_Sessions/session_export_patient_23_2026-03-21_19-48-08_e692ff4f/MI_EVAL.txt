### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used nonjudgmental language, affirmations, and normalization (e.g., invitations to speak, reassurance after slips), demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly acknowledged barriers and validated ambivalence (e.g., tiredness, flexible limits) but reflections were more pragmatic than emotionally deep, missing opportunities to explore sadness and the meaning of drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing alcohol use throughout the session with concrete strategies and a follow‑up plan, avoiding drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine partnership by soliciting patient input at decision points, offering choices, and adapting recommendations to Claudia’s limits and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control—asked permission, offered multiple options, emphasized low‑pressure follow‑up, and avoided coercive language while accepting realistic goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some personal motivation and planning (confidence rating, chosen nights) but relied more on offering strategies than drawing out deeper values, reasons, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly invited Claudia’s preferences, co‑created the plan (nights, activities, check‑in method), and used shared planning language that reflected equal influence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and set a concrete two‑week plan, but could have more systematically amplified spontaneous change talk by probing reasons, desires, and personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and negotiated achievable goals to redirect toward change, though more strategic double‑sided reflections would better validate ambivalence while leaning toward change.
