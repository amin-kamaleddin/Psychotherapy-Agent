# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used a warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone throughout, validated the patient's initiative, and accommodated requests (drafting notes, including preferences), conveying clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections acknowledged stressors and social triggers and affirmed motivation, but the clinician missed opportunities for deeper, more affective or complex reflections to fully capture the patient's felt experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on smoking cessation and medication planning, with the clinician repeatedly steering toward concrete elements (past treatments, quit date, prescriber note, follow-up) and actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative: the clinician repeatedly asked for preferences, drafted requested materials, integrated feedback, and invited the patient's input rather than acting as sole expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported patient choice and control, offered options (varenicline vs NRT), emphasized the patient's control and prescriber decision-making, and avoided pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurred (questions about past meds, readiness scale, quit date), but the clinician mainly supplied information and did not systematically draw out the patient's own reasons, values, or past strengths for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as a partnership, sought permission to draft notes, incorporated the patient's agenda into the plan, and repeatedly checked back to include patient-requested items.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicited some change talk (med request, readiness 6/10, quit planning) but did not consistently amplify or probe follow-ups to deepen reasons for change or strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not reinforced, but the clinician primarily reflected and moved to planning rather than using targeted MI strategies (double-sided reflections, reframing) to reduce the influence of barriers.
