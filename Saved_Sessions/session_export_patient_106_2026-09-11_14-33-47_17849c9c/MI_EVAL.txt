# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validating the patient’s circumstances and choices throughout the session. Praises honesty and normalizes ambivalence, creating a warm, accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries accurately capture Natalia’s feelings, constraints, and motivations, showing nuanced understanding. Repeated paraphrasing made the patient feel heard and supported.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target behavior (not smoking inside) and moves logically from options to a single concrete plan. Questions and reflections consistently tie back to specific, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functions as joint problem-solving, with the clinician soliciting and integrating the patient’s ideas at every step. Options are offered rather than imposed, and the plan is co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly honors the patient’s choices and avoids coercive language, asking which options feel realistic and allowing experimentation. Normalizes imperfection to preserve ownership and reduce pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s own motivations, values, and strengths (concern for her son, pride, stubbornness to do right by him). Uses evocative questions to produce commitment-relevant talk and internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative: clinician asks for preferences, frames choices, and defers to the patient’s judgment about practicality. The clinician builds on the patient’s proposals rather than overriding them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk, turning tentative ideas into specific, testable steps and asking for commitment-level detail. Patient-generated plans were strengthened and rehearsed into a concrete anchor.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward change, but misses opportunities for strategic double-sided reflections naming both pulls. More explicit normalization and reflection of sustain reasons would reduce defensiveness and deepen change talk.
