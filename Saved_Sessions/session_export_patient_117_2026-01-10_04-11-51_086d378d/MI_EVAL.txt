# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Samuel’s experience with explicit acceptance statements and nonjudgmental summaries throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, using complex reflections that capture both content and affect to validate and encourage elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction and coping with work urges, progressing logically from review to concrete strategies and implementation planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as a partner with Samuel by offering options, soliciting preferences, and integrating his choices into a stepwise, co-authored plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes Samuel’s choice and control, invites him to select strategies and timing, and avoids pressure, supporting strong autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits client-generated reasons and solutions but could deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring values, past successes, and scaling confidence more explicitly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is strongly collaborative and co-constructed, with the clinician consistently inviting Samuel’s ideas and letting him shape the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evokes and reinforces change talk and planning (e.g., gum trial, breathing routine) but could better amplify and consolidate commitment language (confidence/commitment questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates and acknowledges barriers without arguing, effectively avoiding reinforcement of sustain talk, though advanced softening techniques (double-sided reflections, strategic discrepancy) were used less often.
