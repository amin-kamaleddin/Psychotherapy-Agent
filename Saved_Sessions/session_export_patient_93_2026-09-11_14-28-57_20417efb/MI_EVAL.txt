# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respect and nonjudgmental regard, affirmed Margaret's values and normalized ambivalence (e.g., balancing pub visits and safety). Language was warm and validating throughout, avoiding labels or criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking with summaries that captured ambivalence, and questions inviting the patient's view. Reflections tended to be more content- and logistical-focused rather than exploring deeper emotional nuances like fear or pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use, moving logically from exploring options to selecting specific, actionable steps. Every exchange returned to concrete behaviors tied to the target (no‑wine days, reminders, scripts).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed, with the clinician requesting input at every step and following Margaret's lead on priorities. Offers to draft or rehearse were made only after Margaret agreed, reinforcing shared responsibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized Margaret's choice and control by offering options and asking which felt doable, without pressuring language. Tentative commitments were accepted and supported, respecting her decision not to quit entirely.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong elicitation of Margaret's own motivations, plans, and solutions through open questions that drew out concrete steps (e.g., Mon/Tues no‑wine, phone reminders). The clinician prompted elaboration and ownership rather than imposing fixes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as a joint endeavor, soliciting preferences and co-designing the plan (scripts, reminder times, rehearsal). This consistently conveyed that Margaret's knowledge and choices drove the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and concrete commitments by offering choices that led to patient-generated steps. A missed opportunity was more deliberate amplification and summary of change talk to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not argue and used double-sided language early, skillfully redirecting toward feasible change options rather than confronting sustain talk. Could further soften sustain talk by explicitly contrasting minimizing comments with safety concerns or eliciting downsides of continuing the same pattern.
