# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing difficulty and honoring Brandon’s guarded stance. Tone was warm and supportive with no blaming or shaming statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, specific reflective listening and perspective-taking, repeatedly paraphrasing desires and barriers in the client's language. Reflections and summaries were timely and closely matched what mattered to Brandon.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus on reducing alcohol via a concrete, measurable experiment (two booze-free weeknights) and supporting strategies. Questions and reflections consistently returned to operationalizing supports and follow-up without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a true two-way problem‑solving partnership, inviting input, validating ideas, and co-designing a concrete plan. The clinician sought permission before offering help and negotiated follow-up preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice (e.g., 'your call') and offered options while deferring the final decision to Brandon, avoiding pressure or coercion. The clinician honored his right to change or not and let him set support terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Brandon’s own motivations and practical ideas, producing actionable, client-generated plans. However, the clinician missed deeper evocation opportunities such as linking actions to values or eliciting confidence/importance ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively shared power by framing choices as Brandon’s, asking which options felt doable, and seeking his permission for support tactics. Client preferences substantially shaped the plan throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified multiple change‑oriented statements and a concrete first step, reinforcing small experiments and supports. Missed some chances to strengthen change talk into explicit commitment (e.g., confidence/importance ruler or a commitment summary).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and redirected toward practical solutions rather than arguing, which reduced defensiveness. Could have used more strategic softening techniques (double-sided reflections, exploring costs of status quo) to tip ambivalence further.
