# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing the patient's fears and validating impact without blaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and reflective summaries, but several reflections are somewhat formulaic and could probe deeper affect or name specific emotions more often.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the patient's priorities (obtaining tests/referral) while offering concrete coping options and actionable next steps without getting sidetracked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative—materials and plans are co‑created, preferences are solicited, and the clinician role‑plays and rehearses with the patient as a teammate.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms choice and presents explicit options, accepts the patient's decision to prioritize tests, and supports whichever path she selects without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits useful information and conditional willingness (readiness ruler, 'what would need to happen'), but misses opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation, values, and confidence toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a genuine partnership by inviting the patient's input on format and wording, co‑producing letters/scripts, and rehearsing responses together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generates some change talk and offers low‑risk options, but does not consistently follow up to amplify desire, ability, reasons, or move toward specific commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframes sustain talk by validating biomedical concerns and presenting psychological approaches as additive, though could use more double‑sided reflections to explicitly name ambivalence.
