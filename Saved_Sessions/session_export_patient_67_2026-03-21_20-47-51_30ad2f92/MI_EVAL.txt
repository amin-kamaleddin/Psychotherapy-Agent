# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, asked permission before offering suggestions, and affirmed small steps without any pressuring language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating the patient's worries and shaping further elaboration so the patient felt heard and understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing alcohol use with targeted assessments (scales, barriers, prior attempts) and a concrete, behaviorally anchored plan to preserve momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively invited the patient's input at every step and co-constructed a specific, feasible plan based on the patient's preferences, reinforcing genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly emphasized choice and normalized ambivalence, repeatedly asked permission before offering options, and avoided coercive language to support patient control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations and barriers using open invitations and scaling, leading the patient to articulate change language and propose actionable steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance throughout, checking preferences, negotiating supports, and letting the patient's choices (start day, seltzer, walk) shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with pros/cons and scaling and translated desire into a specific trial, but could more often amplify and follow up on the patient's own change statements to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and redirected to small, doable steps using some double-sided language, yet missed opportunities for more strategic reflections and evocative questions to reduce sustain talk's pull.
