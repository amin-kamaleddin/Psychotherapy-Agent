# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Nonjudgmental opening statement (“This is a confidential, nonjudgmental space”)
- [x] Normalizing statements (“It’s understandable…”, “Be kind to yourself…”)
- [x] Avoids labeling/blame (“You’re not lazy — you’re planning”)
- [x] Present throughout the session
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalizing language from start to finish; strong acceptance throughout.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Empathic reflections and validation (e.g., “That sounds frustrating,” “I hear the doubt”)
- [x] Reflections captured practical barriers (work hours, meetings, low energy)
- [ ] Used more complex/amplified reflections or explored deeper feelings/values
**Comments:** Frequent concise empathic reflections that validated feelings and barriers; could deepen with more complex reflections linking feelings to values or longer‑term concerns.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
- [x] Maintained clear focus on target behavior (increasing exercise)
- [x] Guided toward narrowly specified actions (days/times, calendar blocks, alarms)
- [x] Repeatedly brought session back to plan and next steps
**Comments:** Strong, consistent directional focus with concrete, actionable planning and repeated linking back to the target behavior.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Solicited patient preferences early (“Which would you prefer to focus on now?”)
- [x] Asked patient to select days/times and confidence rating
- [x] Incorporated patient choices into the plan (accepted Tue/Thu, 10/5/2 options)
- [ ] Fully co‑constructed plan (clinician often offered detailed prescriptive steps)
**Comments:** Good collaborative moves and incorporation of patient input, though clinician often offered a clinician‑generated checklist that reduced some power‑sharing.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Affirmed choice and agency (“Which would you prefer…”, “try it this week; we’ll review”)
- [x] Avoided pressuring language; respected ownership of decision
- [ ] Sequence of prescriptive steps slightly reduced pure autonomy support
**Comments:** Respectful of patient choice and reinforced agency; somewhat prescriptive planning slightly lessened pure autonomy support.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Elicited specifics about change (asked “what does ‘getting started’ look like?”)
- [x] Used a confidence ruler to draw out ambivalence and commitment
- [ ] Missed opportunities to elicit intrinsic reasons/values for change
**Comments:** Effective at drawing out pragmatic change talk and concrete plans; could have elicited more intrinsic motivations and values to strengthen evocation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Framed work as joint and offered to troubleshoot together
- [x] Checked for agreement and incorporated patient decisions
- [ ] Could have invited more patient‑generated planning before offering full clinician plan
**Comments:** Strong sense of partnership with checks for agreement; deepen by asking patient to generate more of the plan to increase ownership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
- [x] Elicited initial change talk and commitment‑like language (patient agreed to try Tue/Thu, set alarm, lay out shoes)
- [ ] Did not sufficiently reflect/amplify or explore desire, reasons, need, or past successes
**Comments:** Secured actionable commitment language but did not sufficiently amplify or deepen change talk (limited exploration of reasons/values or confidence‑building).

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
- [x] Avoided arguing with sustain talk and self‑criticism
- [x] Normalized and reframed sustain talk (“You’re not lazy — you’re planning”)
- [x] Redirected to feasible small steps and backups (10/5/2‑minute options)
- [ ] Could use more explicit double‑sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence before pivoting
**Comments:** Effectively defused sustain talk with normalization, reframing, and pragmatic fallbacks; could strengthen by using more strategic double‑sided reflections.
