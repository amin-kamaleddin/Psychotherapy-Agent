# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician is respectful, normalizes feelings, and offers praise, conveying clear positive regard; however, the interaction often shifts quickly into directive problem-solving that can feel prescriptive rather than purely accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are several empathic reflections and validations that show understanding of Marcus's perspective, but deeper reflective listening and complex reflections about his discouragement and emotional weight are limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity and BP monitoring with concrete implementation steps and a firm follow-up, demonstrating excellent directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Marcus's input and co-develops a plan, yet frequently provides many concrete solutions without first eliciting Marcus's ideas, creating a slightly asymmetrical dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers multiple options, asks Marcus which he prefers, and reassures flexibility, consistently reinforcing Marcus's sense of choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and amplifies commitment language and concrete planning, but could deepen intrinsic motivation by asking more open, values-linked questions and scaling importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a partnership with inclusive language and shared planning, but frequent unsolicited recommendations leave some decision-making power with the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits and reinforces change talk and specific commitments, though missed opportunities remain to further explore importance, reasons, and confidence to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids argument, normalizes barriers, and reframes slips as information to reduce shame, but does not explicitly use double-sided reflections or deeper exploration to strategically resolve ambivalence.
