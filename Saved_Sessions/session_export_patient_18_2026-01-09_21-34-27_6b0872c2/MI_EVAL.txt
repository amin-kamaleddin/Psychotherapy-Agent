# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating with permission‑seeking and affirmations (e.g., praising strengths and normalizing the check‑in). The clinician maintained supportive tone and respect for Anya's perspective throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking that captured priorities and surprise, but reflections stayed at a paraphrase level. More feeling‑focused reflections exploring emotions behind 'stressed' or 'surprised' would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on early alcohol exposure/party safety tied to Anya’s goals, moving logically from assessment to skills practice and planning. Interventions were practical and aligned with the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt co‑constructed with invitations to role‑play and shared decision‑making about strategies and cues. The clinician tailored options to Anya’s preferences and engaged her in selecting what fit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong use of permission, choice language, and multiple options that reinforced Anya’s ownership of decisions. The clinician avoided pressuring and emphasized that she could pick what felt doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Anya’s reasons for protecting grades and generated practical solutions she already uses, but missed opportunities to deepen internal motivation (e.g., values link, scaling, or having her summarize her plan).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with permission and invitations to choose, but the clinician sometimes moved quickly from elicitation to offering many clinician‑generated solutions. Pausing to solicit Anya’s own ideas first would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk about protecting grades and safety and used rehearsals to build commitment. Opportunities remain to amplify and deepen change talk with values linkage, scaling, or eliciting stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, offering realistic low‑profile strategies instead. Could more explicitly use double‑sided reflections to acknowledge competing pulls and pivot gently toward change.
