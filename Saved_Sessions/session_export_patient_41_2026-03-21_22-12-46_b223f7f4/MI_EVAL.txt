# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—normalized difficulty, affirmed effort, and reframed setbacks nonjudgmentally without shaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, specific reflections of content and affect, validated frustration and somatic signs, and responded supportively; minor opportunity to probe deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing avoidance and building a rehearsal routine, moving the session from problem identification to specific, time-bound experiments and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled an equal partnership with inclusive language, joint problem-solving, and adjustment of recommendations to the patient's hesitancy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering options, avoiding coercive language, and explicitly inviting the client's choices and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some intrinsic motivation via choices and confidence scaling, but could more systematically explore personal reasons, values, and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently asked for preferences, invited tweaks, and sought permission, communicating that expertise about the client's life resides with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change-oriented language and successes, yet could more actively draw out the client's own reasons, values, and explicit commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk through normalization and reframing and offered incremental experiments, though using more double-sided reflections would further defuse sustain arguments.
