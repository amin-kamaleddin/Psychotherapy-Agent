# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—normalized Marcus’s struggles, validated his priorities (e.g., not giving up the dog), and praised small efforts without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate understanding with complex reflections that captured symptoms, functional impact, and emotional burden, which reduced resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on improving asthma control via nightly controller adherence and guided discussion toward concrete, measurable steps and logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co‑constructed the plan by soliciting Marcus’s preferences, adapting wording to his voice, inviting rehearsal, and making decisions jointly rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Marcus’s choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and validating his right to decline larger changes while preserving ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation linked to goals and used scaling to deepen commitment, but could have probed values and past successes more to amplify intrinsic motivation and self‑efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Marcus as the expert on his life, offered choices, and invited him to select supports, demonstrating a collaborative, nonprescriptive stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and strengthened change talk—asked a change‑oriented question, affirmed the plan, used a confidence ruler, and translated intentions into a specific, actionable plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and accepted barriers, steering toward a workable low‑burden option rather than pressuring change; could have more explicitly used double‑sided reflections or summaries of ambivalence.
