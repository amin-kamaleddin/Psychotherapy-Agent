# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental positive regard. Explicitly honors patient's preference to wait for written confirmation (“I respect your plan to wait for written confirmation — take care”). Opening and closing remarks acknowledge values and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening throughout (“You’re being careful — that’s sensible given phenelzine”; “I hear how important precise, written confirmation are to you”). Attuned to caution and need for reassurance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on medication‑switch safety. Provides concise clinical guidance (don’t combine, 14‑day washout, start dose 10 mg) and drives toward an actionable safety plan (EKG, sodium, contacts, dates).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames work as shared enterprise; repeatedly asks permission and solicits patient input (who to coordinate with, what to include). Incorporates patient’s specifics (names, dates, contact windows) into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces patient choice and refrains from pressure. Honors decision not to start until written confirmation, asks permission before contacting others, and offers options (taper vs abrupt per prescriber).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits patient priorities and preferences (“What would reassure you most right now?”), invites additions, and uses patient responses to co‑construct the safety plan rather than imposing directives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates power‑sharing—asks permission to contact clinicians, copies patient on communications, and builds the plan from patient-stated preferences (dose, lab location, contact times).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits language leaning toward acceptance (willingness to proceed once reassured) and creates concrete path forward. Missed opportunity to explicitly elicit/strengthen commitment/confidence (no readiness/confidence ruler or direct commitment probe).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reframes caution into logistical solutions, reducing sustain talk by addressing safety concerns. Could further weaken sustain talk with double‑sided reflections and explicit naming of ambivalence or affirmations of steps toward change.
