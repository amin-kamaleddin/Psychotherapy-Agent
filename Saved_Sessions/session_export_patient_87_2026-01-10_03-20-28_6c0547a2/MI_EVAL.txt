# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, validating effort and avoiding judgment to create a warm, nonjudgmental tone that supported disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and frequent and validated Marco's feelings, but could be deeper and more affect-focused or amplified to explore the emotional weight of repeated setbacks.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on concrete target behaviors tied to the goal, sequencing questions to move from values to specific, doable commitments without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the session as joint problem-solving, inviting Marco's input and refining his ideas while sharing power rather than directing unilaterally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly reinforced Marco's autonomy with choice-oriented language and options, supporting his ownership of the plan rather than pressuring him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic motivations and past successes that produced change talk, but could do more to amplify mobilizing language and confidence-building statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and client-centered, repeatedly asking for Marco's preferences and building the plan from his prior successes and ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk through values and concrete-step questions, though there were missed opportunities to amplify and convert desire into stronger preparatory or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided argument, but mostly mirrored or normalized it rather than using strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, reframing) to reduce its impact.
