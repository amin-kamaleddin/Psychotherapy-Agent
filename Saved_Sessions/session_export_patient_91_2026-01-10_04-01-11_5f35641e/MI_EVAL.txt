# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Marcus's experience without shaming; warmth and respect were present throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, frequent reflective listening and perspective-taking, using complex reflections and mirroring Marcus's language to deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on blood glucose monitoring and establishing a simple routine, narrowing overwhelm to a single, manageable behavioral target with concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session exemplified collaborative problem-solving with joint planning and integration of Marcus's ideas, offers of clinician support, and co-constructed next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking preferences, and proposing help rather than imposing solutions, preserving Marcus's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out values and practical reasons for change (family, role, strategies) but could have probed deeper into personal meaning and asked for confidence/commitment to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership stance—clinician invited Marcus's priorities and ideas, treated him as the expert on his life, and negotiated plans collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and manageable steps, but missed opportunities to amplify it into firmer commitment language (e.g., scaling or explicit readiness/when questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully redirected sustain talk toward change and normalized ambivalence, yet rarely used explicit double-sided reflections to more strategically pivot to change talk.
