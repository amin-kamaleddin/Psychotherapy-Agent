# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing difficulty and praising effort. No minimizing, labeling, or judgmental language occurred.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking that matched Mateo's subdued tone and validated both physical and emotional states. This helped Mateo feel heard and led to fuller disclosure about pain and actions taken.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on increasing activity/pacing with concrete, measurable steps (2‑ and 3‑minute sits) and pain ratings. The agenda stayed tightly linked to rehab goals and safety monitoring without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as a genuine partner by inviting Mateo's input on timing and logistics and co‑creating the plan. Language and decisions reflected shared problem‑solving rather than expert prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, requested permission, and affirmed Mateo's right to stop, avoiding pressuring language. Safety/stop rules and respect for his decisions reinforced ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation through small experiments and reinforced observed benefits, which prompted action. However, the clinician did not sufficiently draw out deeper values, reasons for change, or assess confidence with scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and emphasized Mateo's agency by offering options and asking which he preferred. Power was shared in concrete ways (timing, stopping rules, when to notify rehab/MD).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change language via doable experiments and highlighting small wins that led to repeated practice. Missed opportunities included more open, evocative questions to deepen reasons and explicit exploration of commitment/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated skepticism and reframed suggestions as low‑risk tests to neutralize sustain talk, preventing it from taking hold. Could have more frequently used double‑sided reflections or strategic reflections to further undermine sustain talk.
