# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, repeatedly acknowledging the patient’s limits and validating his choice to test rather than quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing practical concerns well, but seldom probed deeper emotions or the emotional meaning of smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking/NRT throughout, consistently steering the discussion toward concrete, behavior-linked next steps tailored to the patient’s context.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was genuinely co-constructed—clinician solicited preferences, adapted plans to shifts and partner role, and offered materials after invitation, showing authentic collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and control, offered options and permission to test the plan, and avoided pressure or directive commands, protecting the patient’s autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical reasons and benefits and encouraged a test run, but could more systematically draw out intrinsic motives, importance/confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively invited the patient’s input, negotiated how the partner should be involved, and tailored recommendations to the patient’s life, reflecting strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reflected change talk about reduced cravings, health, and cost savings and encouraged a two-week trial, but did not deepen or scaffold toward stronger desire/ability/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed ambivalence neutrally, acknowledging sustain talk while moving forward, though it used few strategic double-sided reflections to tip the balance toward change.
