# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—validates effort, normalizes frustration, and praises openness without blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses solid reflective listening and mirrors content and tone, though deeper, more complex reflections to explore emotions (disappointment/embarrassment) would strengthen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing orange juice with concrete behavioral options, a measurable two‑week plan, and a planned follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative—solicits the patient's input, builds on his phrasing, co‑constructs the plan, and invites ongoing partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking which feels doable, and inviting permission to help, without pressuring or directing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivation and self‑generated solutions and affirms them, but could probe more for intrinsic values and deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as equal partnership, defers to patient preferences, and shares power through jointly developed strategies and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk via options, scripting, confidence ratings, and follow‑up, yet could more systematically ask why the change matters to amplify reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk with pragmatic compromises and coping scripts, but would benefit from explicit double‑sided reflections to acknowledge enjoyment while highlighting discrepancy.
