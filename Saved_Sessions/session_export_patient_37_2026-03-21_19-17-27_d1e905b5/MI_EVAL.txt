# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used explicit nonjudgmental language and consistently validated the parent's feelings, avoiding blame or dismissal and normalizing anxiety about parenting decisions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking and reflective listening (e.g., grounding technique, capturing emotional weight) but could use more complex reflections and summarizing to show deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on child vaccination and concrete next steps (drafting questions, focused search plan, testing/vaccination logistics) without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the interaction as joint problem-solving by co-creating question lists and a search plan, soliciting patient input, and adapting recommendations to the parent's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options, asked permission before drafting and tailoring materials, and reinforced the parent's control over decisions, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some concerns and facilitated tentative openness, but relied more on providing information and skills than on drawing out the parent's intrinsic motivations or linking values to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited collaboration (e.g., 'would you like help drafting?'), shared planning, and allowed the parent's preferences to shape the plan, reflecting strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities and practical tools that led to tentative willingness, but rarely elicited or amplified explicit change language such as desire, reasons, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and legitimized doubts without arguing, offered strategies to manage concerns and accommodated the parent's preferences; only one mildly directive moment that was promptly adjusted.
