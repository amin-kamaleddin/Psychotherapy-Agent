# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing Linda's experience and affirming her efforts throughout the session with no blaming or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate reflections and perspective-taking that helped Linda feel heard, but emotional content (frustration, fear, shame) was sometimes only briefly explored before moving to action.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on tobacco cessation with interventions (triggers, meds, scripts, rehearsal) tightly linked to the quit goal and minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—clinician invited input, asked permission to role‑play, and adjusted plans to Linda's preferences throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and framed suggestions as options rather than directives, reinforcing Linda's right to decide and ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical reasons for change and provided rehearsal opportunities, but missed chances to deepen intrinsic motivations, values work, and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership with role‑plays and shared decision‑making, though at times offered many concrete recommendations that shifted slightly toward clinician direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions and affirmations, but could have probed more to deepen DARN elements and amplify commitment (e.g., scaling, future‑focused questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk to practical strategies, but used few explicit double‑sided reflections to validate ambivalence before pivoting to change planning.
