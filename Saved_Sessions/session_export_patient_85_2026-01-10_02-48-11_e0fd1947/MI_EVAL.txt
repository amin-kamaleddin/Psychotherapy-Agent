# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (validations, normalizing ambivalence, supportive reframing). Warmth and acceptance were evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, reflective empathic statements and perspective-taking that helped the patient elaborate. Could deepen emotional reflection and use more complex reflections to capture underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing excess orange juice with concrete, measurable steps and a scheduled check‑in. Minimal topic drift and strong linkage between concerns and actionable plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: invited patient input at every step, used the patient’s own phrasing, and co‑constructed reminders and follow‑up. Decisions and plans were mutually shaped rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and offered options while avoiding pressure, and transparently handled limits (SMS) with a workaround. Consistently preserved the patient’s agency and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient’s motivations, specific strategies, and a plan with a check‑in to sustain motivation. Could strengthen evocation by linking values/discrepancy and exploring past successes for deeper intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked genuinely as a partner by soliciting preferences, co‑creating the reminder text, and asking permission to use the patient’s wording. Demonstrated clear power‑sharing and respect for the patient’s expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete planning and affirmations, and secured a strong commitment for a check‑in. Would benefit from more frequent amplification, summaries, and explicit elicitation of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided strengthening sustain talk by normalizing ambivalence and offering pragmatic, nonjudgmental strategies to manage cravings. Could use purposeful double‑sided reflections to more explicitly acknowledge both sides and emphasize change language.
