# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained warm, nonjudgmental positive regard throughout, using validating language and normalizing slips to protect the patient's dignity. Examples included affirmations of practical choices and gentle, respectful framing of setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured both content and feeling, demonstrating accurate perspective-taking and nuanced understanding of ambivalence and hope. The clinician used empathic summaries that mirrored the patient's internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the target behavior—establishing a short morning yoga practice—and repeatedly narrowed to concrete, actionable steps. Goal-directed questions and planning advanced movement toward change without losing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as an equal partnership with the clinician soliciting the patient's ideas and co-constructing the plan. Decisions were shaped jointly and reflected the patient's priorities and language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasized choice and feasibility, asking rather than telling and affirming the patient's ownership of the plan. Language preserved control and avoided pressure while supporting compassionate self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician drew motivation from the patient by asking evocative questions and amplifying internally generated reasons for change like calm, pride, and momentum. Motivation was elicited rather than imposed, linking values to specific actions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The provider consistently framed the work as collaborative and deferred to the patient's expertise, using permission-like checks and reflective prompts. This promoted shared decision-making rather than an expert-driven plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was actively elicited with targeted open questions and then reflected and affirmed to amplify desire, reason, and intention. The clinician linked the patient's change language to realistic, values-aligned steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Barriers and sustain talk were acknowledged without reinforcement, then reframed and redirected toward change (e.g., slips framed as data). Constructive follow-ups and normalization defused defensiveness and supported re-engagement.
