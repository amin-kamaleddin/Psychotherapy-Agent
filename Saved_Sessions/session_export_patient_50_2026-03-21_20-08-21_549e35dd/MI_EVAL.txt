# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental, warm, and validating throughout the session, normalizing Hana’s experience and affirming her steps. The clinician avoided labeling or blame and conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and accurate paraphrases that captured content and tone. Could deepen emotional reflections and use more complex reflections to surface underlying shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on preparing for and attempting the certification, guiding the conversation to concrete, measurable steps (timed practice, day/time, coping strategies). These moves translated motivation into a specific action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan with Hana, repeatedly checked preferences, and revised language at her request so the session felt like a team effort. Shared decision-making was evident throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice by offering options, asking permission before suggestions, and framing options non-coercively. Statements consistently respected Hana’s control and fostered ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivation and strengths (past success, reasons for change, confidence ruler) and drew out change-relevant statements. The clinician provided practical scripts more often than inviting Hana to generate her own wording, which slightly reduced exclusive evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Hana as the expert, asked permission, and offered a menu of strategies to choose from, demonstrating shared control. The interaction repeatedly invited Hana’s input and co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, used strength-based questions and the confidence ruler, and co-created a doable plan. Could more strategically amplify and reflect Hana’s own change statements to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated sustain talk, avoided arguing, and offered practical strategies to reduce its power (reframe, plan for setbacks). To improve, the clinician could more directly pivot sustain talk using double-sided reflections and linking ambivalence to change reasons.
