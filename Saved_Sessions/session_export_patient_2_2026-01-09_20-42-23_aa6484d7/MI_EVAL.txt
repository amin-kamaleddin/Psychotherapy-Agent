# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, normalizing slips and affirming the patient's struggles. Respected boundaries (declined to focus on alcohol) and affirmed effort throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking, capturing emotional stakes like motherhood and cancer worry. Timely, specific reflections softened the patient and increased cooperation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on evening smoking reduction and linked strategies to identified barriers. Elicited a concrete, measurable plan and set a one-week follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving and built the plan from the patient's input. Consistently consulted rather than instructed, with the patient shaping key decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored the patient's choices and emphasized control by offering options and asking which felt most doable. Preserved autonomy while suggesting optional boosts when confidence was low.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's own reasons and motivations (family cancer risk, parenting) and used scaling and commitment questions to draw out change talk. Amplified and operationalized the patient's stated reasons into a plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resides with the patient and invited collaboration through brainstorming and choice. Decisions about nights, cues, and strategies were explicitly co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions and reflections, prompting specific goals and strategies. Followed up with concrete planning and secured tracking and a check-in.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and respected boundaries, effectively refocusing on smoking; however, missed some opportunities for double-sided reflections or gently exploring minimizations. Could more actively name both sides of ambivalence to resolve sustain talk further.
