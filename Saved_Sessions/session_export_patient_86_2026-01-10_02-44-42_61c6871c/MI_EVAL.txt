# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful: validated Marco’s experience, honored his request for brevity, and offered options rather than directives throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of facts and stance, but missed opportunities to deepen emotional content with more complex reflections of resignation and fatigue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking/harm reduction and moved logically to specific, actionable steps (patch+gum dosing, timeline, safety instructions, relapse options).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reflected true teamwork: plan was co‑created, the clinician deferred to Marco’s preferences, and decisions came from the patient with clinician support.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored patient choice by framing options, inviting selection, and respecting minimal follow‑up preferences, which supported Marco’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation present (open questions, choice of options) but largely superficial; the clinician relied more on information and practical steps than drawing out personal motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong co‑construction and power‑sharing: asked permission before offering info, followed patient preferences, and jointly drafted the cardiologist text and follow‑up plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently encouraged and reinforced brief change talk and helped set a quit date and concrete plan, though could have amplified reasons and confidence with more selective reflections and evocative prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing and reframed sustain talk toward practical, low‑burden options, but rarely used double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sustain and change sides.
