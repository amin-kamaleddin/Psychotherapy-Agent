# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard through normalization, validation, and appreciation, avoiding blame or judgment. The clinician frames the plan as low-pressure and accepts the patient's skepticism throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking that mirror the patient's overwhelm and priorities. Demonstrates clear understanding of practical barriers and emotional tone, strengthening rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, specific focus on a single, realistic diabetes self-management behavior and moves the conversation toward a measurable action and follow-up. Goal-directedness is present without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient in shared problem-solving and co-creates the experiment, offering options and soliciting the patient's choices. The patient's ideas directly shape the plan, reflecting strong two-way collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice by offering options, normalizing failure, and giving permission to decline or change the plan. Repeatedly reinforces that the patient controls pace and follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits core values (protecting family) and links them to small behavioral options, converting intent into a concrete experiment. Could deepen evocation by exploring the meaning of the value, assessing importance/confidence, and eliciting past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions the patient as the expert and shares decision-making by asking preferences and offering choices. Language and actions consistently defer to the patient's comfort and input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits some change talk and reinforces the patient's chosen small step, turning it into a specific cue and follow-up. Missed opportunities include amplifying the family-protecting value and asking about confidence or past successes to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes resistance as a low-pressure experiment, using reflections to de-emphasize pressure. Could further blunt sustain talk with more double-sided reflections and selectively reinforcing tentative pro-change statements.
