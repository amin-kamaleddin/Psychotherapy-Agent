# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental support, normalized uncertainty, and affirmed effort throughout the session, demonstrating unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries were accurate and frequent, showing solid perspective-taking, but the clinician stayed more behavior- and plan-focused and missed deeper emotional reflection of anxiety and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear, consistent focus on following through after rehearsal and co‑created a concrete, measurable experiment without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session modeled an equal‑partner approach: options were offered, the client was invited to choose and tweak plans, and wording and reporting preferences were negotiated jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was emphasized repeatedly and the clinician respected Elira's preferences and decisions, avoiding pressure and supporting ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited via scaling and small experiments, but the clinician relied more on proposing solutions and did not sufficiently probe values, past successes, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician fostered client expertise and co‑construction of the plan by offering options, accepting modifications, and inviting input on how to report back or phrase messages.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change talk with scaling questions and small‑step invitations and amplified commitments, but could more deliberately draw out intrinsic, future‑oriented reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was avoided and redirected with smaller alternatives and backups, reducing its weight, though more double‑sided reflections or explicit contrasts could have further softened sustain talk.
