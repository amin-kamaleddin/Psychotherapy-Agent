# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently used nonjudgmental, respectful language (permission, thanks, normalizing) and affirmed Maya’s perspective, demonstrating clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and timely perspective-taking captured Maya’s ambivalence and functions of drinking (e.g., socializing, coping), showing strong empathic attunement throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on weekend drinking and its impact on college/grades, moving logically from assessment to concrete behavioral planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt cooperative and equal—options were offered, Maya was asked to choose what felt doable, and the clinician invited role‑play and joint planning to tailor strategies to her context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice (asking permission, offering options, removing pressure) and reinforced that any change is Maya’s decision, supporting her sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited values and a self‑generated strategy (importance rating, reasons for college, pacing plan), but could have probed deeper intrinsic motives and strengthened commitment/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as co‑constructed—soliciting Maya’s input at each step, adapting suggestions to her voice, and jointly developing specific plans and backups.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and shaped change talk (importance ruler, menu of options, asking which feels doable) and supported a concrete plan, though more amplification and elicitation of commitment/confidence would strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rather than arguing, the clinician accepted Maya’s reluctance to quit and reframed the conversation toward manageable alternatives, effectively reducing sustain talk and keeping momentum for small changes.
