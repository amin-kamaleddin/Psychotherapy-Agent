# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validation, avoids judgmental language, and affirms the patient's pace and choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrase of concerns, but reflections are sometimes formulaic and could probe deeper emotional nuance (e.g., shame, physical anxiety).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and moves from exploration to concrete implementation steps (queued text, alarm, if‑then backup) without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuinely collaborative interaction by inviting input, offering options, and building the plan from the patient's ideas rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and control, normalizes contingencies (e.g., stopping after five minutes), and presents options without pressure to reinforce patient agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal reasons, values, and concrete strategies from the patient, but could more actively amplify change talk and elicit stronger commitment and confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly adopts an equal‑partner stance, explicitly treating the patient as the expert and co‑constructing the plan with shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits specific change talk and implementation intentions, yet could better reinforce and build ability/confidence and convert reasons into firmer commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and mirrors sustain talk without confronting it and pivots to problem-solving, but could more deliberately use MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to diminish sustain talk.
