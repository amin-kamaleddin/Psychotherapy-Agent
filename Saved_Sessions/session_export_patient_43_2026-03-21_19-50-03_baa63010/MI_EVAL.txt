# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offered choices, which conveyed positive regard; could strengthen by adding more individualized affirmations and explicitly normalizing ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and followed the patient's lead, showing good perspective-taking; missed opportunities for more complex reflections to name underlying feelings and validate emotions more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on smoking and immediate concerns and moved toward concrete behavioral steps (household plan, texts, replies); consistently tied information and options to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-created messages, asked preferences, and adjusted interventions based on the patient's input; the clinician and patient shared equitable back-and-forth problem-solving throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the patient's autonomy by offering choices, normalizing not being ready, and avoiding pressure, thereby reinforcing the patient's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations (pros/cons, family concerns) and used them for practical planning, but relied more on information-giving and problem-solving than on sustained elicitation of intrinsic reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a clear collaborative stance by asking which options the patient preferred and deferring to his expertise, shaping the session around his priorities and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted some change language (pros/cons, concern for kids) and generated concrete steps, but did not consistently amplify or follow up to strengthen emerging change talk with scaling or elaboration questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and pragmatically reduced harm by focusing on child safety, yet did not use strategic softening techniques (double-sided reflections, permission before facts) to explicitly acknowledge and shift sustain talk.
