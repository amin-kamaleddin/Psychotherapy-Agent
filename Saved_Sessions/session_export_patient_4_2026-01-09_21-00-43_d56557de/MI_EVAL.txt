# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with multiple validating statements, avoided blame and normalized the patient’s distress throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections that named emotions and circumstances (e.g., exhaustion, fear) and summarized content in ways that appeared to relieve the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on obtaining timely investigations, referral and a written follow-up plan, repeatedly returning to concrete next steps and resisting topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed decisions and the referral letter by soliciting and incorporating the patient’s exact wording and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options and preserved choice, asking which action the patient wanted and honoring her preference to prioritize testing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical reasons and priorities (e.g., what would reassure her) but did not sufficiently probe intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence/readiness for parallel coping strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the work as collaborative and power-sharing, asking what the patient most wanted help with and involving her in drafting the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some brief evocation of tentative change talk and offer of symptom-relief options, but missed opportunities to deepen, amplify or use scaling/confidence questions to strengthen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered parallel options to reduce exclusive focus on physical causes, though more strategic double-sided reflections could have further shifted momentum.
