# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful stance, validated Dylan’s concerns, and offered practical support (e.g., drafting a truthful statement) without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and summaries demonstrated clear perspective-taking and understanding of Dylan’s worries, and the clinician offered options that matched his needs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused and structured around the immediate procedural goals (protecting housing, drafting statements), but it did not redirect toward broader behavior change around drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative: the clinician invited input at each step, co-wrote materials, asked permission before drafting messages, and tailored wording to Dylan’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported Dylan’s right to choose, offered options, and deferred to his preferences, avoiding pressure throughout the interaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician elicited little intrinsic motivation beyond logistical cooperation, missing opportunities to draw out Dylan’s values, longer-term goals, or reasons to change drinking behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as collaborative, shared power and expertise, and repeatedly asked Dylan which approach felt most helpful before proceeding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was minimal elicitation of Dylan’s desire, reasons, or commitment to change drinking; the conversation prioritized protecting residence/reputation over amplifying change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although nonconfrontational, the clinician largely accommodated Dylan’s minimizations and rationalizations and missed opportunities (e.g., double-sided reflections, highlighting discrepancy) to resolve ambivalence.
