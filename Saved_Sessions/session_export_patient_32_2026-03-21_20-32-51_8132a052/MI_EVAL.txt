# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and nonjudgmental support, normalized difficulty, and praised strengths; framed the session as safe and client‑led.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and accurate summarizing of ambivalence and context; reflections tended to stay at the content level and could probe emotional nuance (isolation, shame) more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on preventing repeat DUI with concrete safety planning and progressive implementation steps; used history to inform rather than distract from the behavioral targets.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled genuine collaboration by co‑constructing scripts and strategies, inviting the client's preferences, and practicing together through role‑play; decisions emerged from mutual problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the client's choice, offered clear options, and asked permission for interventions like role‑play, avoiding pressuring language and reinforcing ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical motivations and past successes (six months sober) and linked them to concrete commitments, but could have probed intrinsic values more systematically to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed a strong collaborative stance with shared decision‑making, offering choices and tailoring interventions to the client's feedback while acting as an equal partner in rehearsal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change language and highlighted past strengths, creating space for commitment and planning; could further deepen change talk by asking more evocative, value‑based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used a useful double‑sided reflection early on, but did limited strategic work to diminish sustain talk (e.g., amplifying discrepancy or empathic reframing) before moving to planning.
