# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, legitimizing the patient's perspective and preferences, but the stance is more practical/informational than deeply individualized warmth or empathic validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and accurate paraphrasing and tailors recommendations to shift work, but reflections are pragmatic and there are few deeper reflections of ambivalence or emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session clearly focused on smoking reduction/cessation with concrete, safety-minded plans and repeated return to realistic steps for the patient’s 12‑hour shifts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engages the patient with choices and adapts suggestions to concerns, but often presents ready-made, expert-driven plans rather than co-constructing solutions with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice by offering options, normalizing pacing and slips, and respecting preferences, preserving the patient's decision-making throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on information and practical strategies and rarely elicits the patient's own reasons, values, or intrinsic motivations with evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displays some collaborative elements (asking permission, checking preferences) but interaction remains fairly expert-led with authoritative instructions rather than shared problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language indirectly by offering options and plans, yet seldom uses open, evocative questions or reflections to draw out and amplify the patient's change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes occasional smoking, which reduces threat, but misses strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly shift sustain talk toward change.
