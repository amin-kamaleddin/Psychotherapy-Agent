# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes Elias’s experience with explicit validations and a nonjudgmental tone. The clinician avoids labeling or blame while acknowledging shame and struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, timely reflective listening and perspective-taking that clarify Elias’s values and emotions. Summaries and reflections consistently deepen understanding and rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on protecting recovery with concrete, proximal steps (texts, reminder, meeting supports). The clinician steers toward actionable planning throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine co‑creation by soliciting Elias’s preferences and offering options for support and rehearsal. Decisions about who does what are repeatedly negotiated and followed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options rather than directives and affirming Elias’s control over the plan. Language and offers foster ownership and avoid coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Elias’s own reasons for change (care for children, reduced shame) and prompts him to articulate meaningful signs of progress. Could more often amplify spontaneous change talk and explicitly elicit commitment/confidence (e.g., scaling or commitment questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as a joint effort and repeatedly asks permission to support and set up reminders. The clinician invites Elias to choose steps and collaborates on message wording and rehearsal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and reinforces change talk by highlighting the concrete plan and asking about benefits for the children. Could more frequently use strategic reflections to amplify desire/ability/reason/need and explicitly draw out commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges ambivalence without reinforcing sustain talk, reflects fears, and pivots to planning and supports. Could add more double-sided reflections to normalize sustain talk while gently reframing toward change.
