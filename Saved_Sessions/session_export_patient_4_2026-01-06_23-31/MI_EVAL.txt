# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and validating with normalizing statements, but occasionally shifts to procedural/listing responses rather than extended, individualized empathic reflections.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows accurate, timely empathic listening and mirrors core concerns, yet often moves quickly into diagnostic recommendations rather than exploring deeper emotions or meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on obtaining medical clarity and safety, providing concrete steps, urgent referral actions, and criteria for emergency care.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Highly practical collaboration—co‑produces diary, letters, and scripts and adapts them to patient requests—while still occasionally defaulting to an expert-led testing plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options, asks permission, and reinforces patient choice (e.g., asking if she wants help drafting materials), though some prescriptive language reduces pure sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's immediate goals for medical answers but does not sufficiently draw out intrinsic motivations or link coping strategies to valued outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates shared work by offering help and co-creating materials, but frequently assumes an expert, directive stance with prescriptive recommendations rather than fully eliciting patient priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides tools and suggests actions (diary, scripts) that support change, but misses opportunities to evoke and amplify the patient's own reasons, confidence, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and validates fears about organic disease, but uses limited strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) and at times reinforces a biomedical frame that may sustain external seeking of certainty.
