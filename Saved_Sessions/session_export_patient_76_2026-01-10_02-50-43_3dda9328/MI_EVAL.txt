# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, validates the patient's distress, and thanks/affirms concrete actions without shaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening and accurate reflections are used throughout, acknowledging emotional and somatic experience and checking in about how grounding/breathing felt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on self-harm safety from initial assessment through concrete steps like removal of implements, delay strategies, grounding, and linkage to resources.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a partnership—clinician solicits preferences, co-constructs the safety plan, and uses inclusive language while checking back frequently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offers options, asks which the patient prefers, and frames necessary safety guidance as suggestions to preserve the patient's sense of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some immediate practical commitments are elicited (handing over razor, delaying urges), but the clinician does not sufficiently explore values, reasons, or longer-term motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative tone and invitations to choose are evident, though the clinician sometimes more strongly guides content for safety reasons, limiting maximal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments eliciting preparatory or commitment-adjacent language (safety plan, delay, handing over items), but few evocative questions or reflections to deepen change talk about reasons, desire, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist skillfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk through empathy and pragmatic alternatives, though could further use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
