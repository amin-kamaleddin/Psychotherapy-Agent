# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Kieran’s experience, creating safety for honest responses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate paraphrase of content, but reflections stay focused on logistics and rarely deepen exploration of affect or the emotional cost of compliance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on a specific behavioral target and repeatedly steers to actionable, time‑bound steps resulting in a concrete two‑week trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative tone with shared decision‑making and co‑creation of the plan, though the clinician sometimes re‑frames the same options rather than drawing more directly from the client's language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's sense of choice and control with open invitations and acceptance of contingencies, avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical motivators and concrete commitments, but less successful at drawing out broader intrinsic values or deeper personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly adopts a collaborative stance, repeatedly inviting the client's input and allowing Kieran to shape the intervention rather than taking an expert, directive posture.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and shapes specific change talk and commitments (e.g., the two‑week trial), but could better amplify and summarize the client's own commitment language to build confidence and plan for barriers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and neutralizes sustain talk to some degree, but often redirects to logistics instead of using double‑sided reflections or exploring ambivalence to shift away from reasons against change.
