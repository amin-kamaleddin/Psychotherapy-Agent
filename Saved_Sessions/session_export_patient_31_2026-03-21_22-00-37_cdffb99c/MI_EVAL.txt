# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the patient's practical choices. Warmth is somewhat clinical and could include more explicit validation to deepen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and useful reflections about shift work and sleep concerns. Reflections are often brief and quickly followed by technical instruction rather than exploring the emotional meaning of smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, specific focus on reducing cigarette use via daytime NRT with concrete, actionable plans and safety guidance. The session stays tightly on the smoking-reduction goal and tailored shift strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices and tailors recommendations to the patient's schedule, showing some collaboration. Much of the session remains clinician-led with detailed instructions, so patient-driven planning is limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, framing decisions, and avoiding pressure. Repeated invitations to tailor timing, pricing checks, and plan type reinforce the patient's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk by asking about benefits and worries, which produced health- and cost-related concerns. The clinician does not systematically build on this material to draw out deeper personal motivations or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative moments like asking preferences and offering to tailor plans, indicating some partnership. However, the clinician often takes an expert role with step-by-step instructions rather than co-creating the plan with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for change talk early on and affirms the patient's choice to try NRT. The clinician does not consistently deepen or amplify change language with follow-up questions about importance, confidence, or desired differences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers practical harm-reduction options that respect the patient's preference to keep some smoking. Could be stronger with explicit double-sided reflections to more actively resolve ambivalence.
