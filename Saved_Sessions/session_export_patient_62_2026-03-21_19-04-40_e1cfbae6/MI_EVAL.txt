# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (empathic statements, normalization, praise) and avoided blame, reflecting a warm, accepting stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing symptoms and validating exhaustion, though some reflections were brief and missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration of statements like "I can’t get a break."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on a concrete target behavior (the 10‑minute walk) and built an actionable plan with time, route, cues, and contingency options, consistently returning the conversation to that goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the session as a joint enterprise by soliciting the patient's input at each step, inviting modifications, and checking fit, which promoted teamwork and shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and avoided pressure by offering options, accepting limits, inviting the patient to pick details, and framing follow-up as optional, reinforcing ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited some personal motivations and commitments (desire for things to go right, specific plans) but relied on clinician-provided ideas and could have used more open questions and scaling to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invitational, asking the patient to choose and shape the plan, but at times the clinician offered scripted, directive instructions that subtly shifted expertise toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete options, reflections, and affirmations, yet missed chances to amplify change statements (e.g., exploring what the walk would mean or scaling importance/confidence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and neutralized sustain talk with validation, normalization, and small-doable options, but could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly juxtapose sustain and change talk to enhance discrepancy.
