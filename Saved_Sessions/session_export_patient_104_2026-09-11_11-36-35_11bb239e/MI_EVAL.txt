# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language and validating reflections, conveying unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained, accurate empathic listening with complex reflections captured both content and feeling, demonstrating strong perspective-taking and emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on a concrete target behavior (a 10-minute bedtime routine and brief milestone check) and repeatedly steered toward achievable steps without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed the plan with the patient by offering options, asking preferences, and inviting her to shape the plan, reflecting a genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and pace, asked what felt doable, and presented options rather than directives, supporting the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer elicited the patient's own motivations and concrete intentions through targeted questions, leading Nadine to generate specific, intrinsic reasons and plans for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language and questioning positioned the patient as an equal contributor; the clinician deferred to Nadine's expertise about her life and invited shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and amplified change talk with behaviorally specific prompts and small-step options, reinforcing the patient's concrete intentions and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively redirected sustain talk toward manageable options and validated ambivalence, though there were missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or explicitly eliciting reasons against larger changes.
