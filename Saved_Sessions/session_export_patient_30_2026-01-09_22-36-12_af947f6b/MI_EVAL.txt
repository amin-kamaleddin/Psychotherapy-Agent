# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, validating David's priorities and honoring his concerns throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, specific reflections and empathic summaries that matched the patient's tone and captured both content and feeling, demonstrating strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on medication acceptance and side-effect management, tying each intervention to measurable, actionable steps like scripts and monitoring plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction: co-produced the script and handouts, solicited David's input on wording and contacts, and positioned the clinician as a teammate rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and offered options rather than directives, avoiding pressure and supporting David's ownership of decisions about starting medication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited values (staying strong) and some internal motivation, but could have deepened intrinsic reasons with scaling, pros/cons, or more targeted elicitation of commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated a strong partnership stance by inviting preferences, negotiating decisions, and co-creating materials that reflected the patient's priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language and small commitments (e.g., printing handouts) but missed opportunities to amplify commitment or use follow-up questions and scaling to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to sustain talk with practical alternatives and concrete safety plans that reduced avoidance reasons, though relied more on problem-solving than on double-sided or pivoting reflections.
