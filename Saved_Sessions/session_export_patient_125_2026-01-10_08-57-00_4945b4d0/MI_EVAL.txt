# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated unconditional positive regard and respect throughout, validating and normalizing Tariq's feelings and avoiding judgmental language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective statements acknowledged the unfairness and likely emotional volatility, but many reflections remained surface-level and missed deeper vulnerabilities or meaning behind Tariq's feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on regaining brief family contact and de-escalation, repeatedly returning to concrete tasks and structured practice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt genuinely joint—clinician asked permission, offered options, and co-constructed messages and role-plays with Tariq, frequently soliciting his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported Tariq's choice and agency by offering options, asking which practices he preferred, and avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some of Tariq's own language was elicited during rehearsals, but the clinician often provided scripts and solutions instead of using open questions to draw out motivations and amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a collaborative tone and invited Tariq's input about priorities and rehearsals, though at times shifted to a solution-providing stance rather than fully sharing power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were efforts to evoke action-oriented language and role-play, but the clinician did not systematically expand desire/ability/reason/need or ask confidence/commitment questions to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged and normalized sustain talk but relied more on offering behavioral alternatives than on double-sided or strategic reflections to soften and redirect ambivalence.
