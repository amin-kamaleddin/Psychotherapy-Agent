# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing ambivalence and appreciating engagement throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured the patient's conflict and practical concerns; could deepen emotional reflections (e.g., anxiety or embarrassment) to reach a higher level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the alcohol incident and academic/disciplinary consequences, consistently steering toward concrete next steps like drafting emails and role‑play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Behaved as an equal partner by offering support and expertise while repeatedly soliciting the patient's input and preferences for next actions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforced the client's choice and control, offered options, and checked permission before providing scripts or role‑play, supporting ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation by linking to the patient's scholarship values but favored procedural planning over deeper exploration of internal motivators, ambivalence, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created a collaborative atmosphere and frequently invited the patient's preferences, though occasionally provided ready-made scripts instead of co-constructing phrasing to boost ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited change‑relevant statements by focusing on academic consequences and planning, but moved quickly into problem‑solving without systematically drawing out desire, reasons, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to change-consistent topics, but could more strategically use double-sided reflections or selective amplification to tip ambivalence toward change.
