# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful—asked permission, validated priorities, and honored readiness (e.g., not pressuring about telling his mom). Tone remained supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening with helpful reflections and check-ins. Missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions (fear about parental reaction) and to use more complex, affect-laden reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use linked to soccer and scholarships and progressed logically from eliciting concerns to concrete strategies. Offered relevant skills (refusal lines, coach text/email) and a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—co-created messages, offered choices, and repeatedly invited Alexei's preferences and practice. The clinician shared control and tailored work to Alexei's readiness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control by asking permission, offering multiple options, and honoring Alexei's decision not to tell his mom. Left decisions (which line, whether to email) to Alexei and probed confidence and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation using the importance ruler and choice-driven prompts and connected behavior to values. Could strengthen evocation by prompting Alexei to articulate his own reasons, barriers, and a personal commitment statement more often.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shared control and invited input, co-creating refusal lines and coach messages and asking which felt natural. Practices and choices reinforced that expertise about his life resides with Alexei.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk tied to soccer/scholarship goals and used ruler and reflections to amplify motivation. Could elicit more spontaneous, client-generated change language (e.g., asking him to describe how staying sober would look day-to-day).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontational responses and did not amplify sustain talk, but missed several chances to explicitly soften minimizations and ambivalence (e.g., no double-sided reflections for 'I've only tried it once'). More strategic reflective responses could have reduced sustain talk and surfaced ambivalence.
