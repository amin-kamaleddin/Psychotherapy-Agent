# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental language, reflective summaries, and affirmations that respected Ethan's perspective. The clinician never shamed or criticized and normalized his concerns throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking with timely, accurate reflections of both content and underlying motivations, validating ambivalence. Summaries deepened rapport and showed attention to practical and emotional dimensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus on limiting to one drink and used successive questions to move toward a specific, time-bound test (Friday). The clinician consistently steered the conversation toward concrete implementation steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with alternating questions and reflections, and the patient was invited to lead choices. Decisions and strategies were co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Ethan's choice and offered options rather than directives, reinforcing ownership of the plan. The clinician asked which steps he preferred and avoided coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Ethan's own motivations and resources, drawing out alternatives, confidence ratings, and implementation ideas. The clinician used questions and role-play to strengthen internally generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a joint effort and solicited Ethan's expertise about what would work for him. Frequent invitations to choose and co-create steps demonstrated authentic power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, translating desire into a concrete, actionable plan and commitment language. The clinician amplified and practiced specific steps (e.g., buddy call-out, $10 limit, practicing the line).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward risk-reduction and alternatives, but missed some chances to use double-sided reflections that explicitly name both the appeal of drinking and the downsides. Greater use of those reflections could further validate ambivalence while gently pivoting toward change.
