# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing Jonathan's experience and avoiding blame. The clinician framed the trial as optional and respected resistance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate empathic listening with complex reflections that captured ambivalence and lowered defensiveness. The clinician named barriers and mirrored affect, sustaining engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on blood sugar monitoring and progressively narrowed to a specific, measurable experiment. Every exchange supported the behavioral objective without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was consistently collaborative, inviting Jonathan's input at each decision point and co‑creating practical plans. The clinician iteratively offered options, elicited choices, and adjusted to feasibility concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering multiple choices, framing the trial as short and nonbinding, and repeatedly asking what felt doable. Language reinforced client control and reduced pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete, client‑generated solutions and a measurable plan, but focused more on logistics than on eliciting internal motives or values. More questions connecting the plan to personal reasons for change would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Jonathan as the expert on what would work, offering options and asking which felt most doable. Decisions were co‑created and power was shared throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited concrete commitment language and a behavioral success marker, but missed opportunities to deepen change talk by exploring personal reasons, values, or importance/confidence ratings. More amplifying reflections could have strengthened commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used double‑sided reflections and reframing to reduce resistance, keeping sustain talk from escalating. Could further diminish sustain talk by exploring costs of the status quo or reframing barriers into small, meaningful gains.
