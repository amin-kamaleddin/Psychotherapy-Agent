# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and validated Isabella’s identity and choices. They normalized ambivalence and avoided judgment or coercion throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated highly accurate, nuanced reflective listening with repeated paraphrases and summaries that captured Isabella’s ambivalence and priorities. Reflections showed clear emotional attunement and perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use during pregnancy and guided the conversation toward concrete, actionable risk‑reduction steps. There was no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled an equal‑partner stance by repeatedly asking for Isabella’s input and building the plan together. Decisions were co‑constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and repeatedly supported autonomy, framing suggestions as choices and affirming the patient’s right to stop or modify the trial. Strategies were tailored to preserve Isabella’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Isabella’s values, past successes, and self‑generated strategies to draw out intrinsic motivation. The clinician linked those internal motivators to concrete steps for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited Isabella’s ideas and positioned her as the expert on what would feel respectful and workable. The plan reflected shared decision‑making and mutual problem‑solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited and reinforced change talk and concrete planning, producing specific steps like a one‑month trial and substitutes. Could more often amplify spontaneous change language and elicit commitment/confidence (e.g., scaling or a commitment summary).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used neutral acceptance while shifting to practical risk‑reduction options. Would benefit from more deliberate double‑sided reflections and selectively emphasizing brief change language to further diminish sustain talk.
