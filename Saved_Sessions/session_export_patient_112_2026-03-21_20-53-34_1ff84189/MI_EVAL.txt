# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, warm stance, validating Rosa's concerns and normalizing her fears. Unconditional positive regard was evident throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were timely and accurate, showing strong perspective-taking and sensitivity to Rosa's emotions, including affect-focused grounding for anxiety. The patient clearly felt heard and understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed appropriately focused on smoking cessation and moved from exploration to concrete planning (quit date, NRT/meds, strategies, timelines). The clinician consistently steered toward practical behavioral steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician acted as an equal partner, soliciting Rosa's input, offering options rather than directives, and co-creating a step-by-step plan. Repeated invitations to choose and practice reinforced joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were explicitly offered and preferences checked throughout, with no pressuring language; recommendations were framed as options. Autonomy was repeatedly supported and respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician drew out important motivations and used importance/confidence rulers, but missed opportunities for deeper, open-ended exploration of Rosa's internal motives and identity-related meanings. More evocative questions could have expanded intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work was consistently framed as collaborative, with menus of options, role‑play invitations, and frequent checks of what felt most helpful to Rosa. The tone and content reflected genuine power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change talk and helped produce commitment language and planning, but could have amplified Rosa's reasons more and asked for a brief verbal commitment to strengthen resolve. More specific future-oriented prompts would have strengthened change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled without arguing and was neutrally reflected, then redirected to problem-solving and coping strategies. However, the clinician used few double-sided reflections or targeted softening techniques to reduce the pull of sustain talk.
