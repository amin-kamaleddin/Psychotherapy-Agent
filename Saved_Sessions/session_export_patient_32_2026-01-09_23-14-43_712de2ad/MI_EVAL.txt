# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental language, normalizes feelings, and affirms practical efforts (e.g., cab money, one-liner). Could add more explicit, individualized affirmations (like acknowledging six months sober) to further build strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with reflective statements and repeated safety checks, mirroring social pressures and isolation concerns. Could deepen rapport with more complex emotional reflections (e.g., loneliness, shame/fear about the DUI).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on preventing another DUI, protecting the license, and managing peer pressure with interventions tightly tied to these goals. Redirects effectively to actionable safety steps and planning without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session functions as a joint project with co-drafting of texts, plans, and role-play practice, and clinician checks back on preferences. Several moments featured clinician-provided lists and multiple suggestions rather than eliciting the client’s ideas first, so more client-generated content would improve balance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice and agency by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language. Affirms that the client decides what feels right and leaves room for commitment on the client's timeline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some concrete change steps (cab money, refusal lines), but much of the session was clinician-driven with many offered solutions. Missed opportunities to draw out intrinsic motives, values, and confidence using open questions or scaling to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operates in a collaborative stance—co-creating scripts and plans and soliciting preferences—balancing expertise with deference. A few clinician-initiated step lists and repeated offers of options made the interaction slightly more directive than fully co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces specific change language and small commitments, using invitations to pick options and affirmations that support further change. Could strengthen by more strategically evoking desire/need/ability/confidence and selectively amplifying client's own change statements to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing with sustain talk and redirects to practical harm-reduction and planning, minimizing reinforcement of minimizations. Could use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain talk while linking back to change to resolve ambivalence more directly.
