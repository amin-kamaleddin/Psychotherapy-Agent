# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language (e.g., “Thanks for telling me this,” “It’s okay to be cautious…,” repeated validations). Multiple clear acceptance statements (5–7 instances) acknowledged Jordan’s perspective and values throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections/paraphrases were used several times (“You’re weighing personal comfort…,” “You clearly care about keeping your grandparents safe”). Reflections were mostly pragmatic; missed opportunities for deeper feeling‑level reflections to elicit emotion/affect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on the target behavior (flu vaccination). Offered choices about information, provided efficacy figures, delivered practical low‑hassle plans, and guided follow‑through to a concrete plan (pharmacy after work, needle preferences, 15‑minute wait).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine shared decision‑making: clinician repeatedly invited Jordan’s input (which info to provide, which plan felt doable) and co‑constructed the plan. Multiple instances of asking preference and building from patient responses.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Emphasized choice and permission (“If you want, I can help…,” “It’s okay to choose what’s right for you”). Some affirmations/praise were used that could subtly push; could increase ownership by reflecting and amplifying Jordan’s own pro‑change statements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced Jordan’s motivating reasons (protecting grandparents) and asked what would help decision‑making. Evocation remained at the values/practical level; more probes for underlying motives or feelings could deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a collaborative stance, avoided expert‑dominance, and worked alongside Jordan to develop an actionable plan (timing, logistics, coping strategies). Partnership evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient expressed explicit change talk (“I’ll do it for my grandparents,” plans for walk‑in, reminder). Clinician reinforced and translated this into a plan but could have amplified and reflected change talk more immediately to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician normalized hesitancy and avoided arguing (e.g., validated doubts about needles/side effects). Sustain talk was not confronted and was acknowledged; additional double‑sided or amplified reflections could further soften sustain talk.
