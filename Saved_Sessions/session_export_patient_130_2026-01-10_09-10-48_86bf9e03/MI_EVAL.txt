# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with multiple affirmations and normalizing language that communicated unconditional positive regard. The clinician avoided blame or moralizing and validated Viktor's choices throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of ambivalence and behavior that helped Viktor feel heard. There were missed opportunities to probe deeper emotions (shame, fear of rejection) and to mirror affect more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on reducing drug-related conflict and testing one sober night, using scaling and concrete steps to maintain momentum. The clinician repeatedly tied discussion back to measurable implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited Viktor's input at every decision point, rehearsed scripts together, and acted as coach rather than prescriber. Viktor's preferences shaped the plan and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Viktor's autonomy with multiple options and nonpressuring language, emphasizing his ownership of the decision. The clinician respected Viktor's limits and framed change as his choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked pragmatic motives and strengthened self-generated ideas using scaling and options, producing meaningful change talk. To deepen evocation, the clinician could probe values and identity-linked reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Viktor as the expert on his life, offering choices and co-constructing the plan. The clinician avoided directives and collaborated on scripts, backups, and rewards.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk with open questions, scaling, option menus, and rehearsal, increasing readiness and confidence. The clinician reinforced Viktor's own language and built specificity around the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and neutrally reflected ambivalence, redirecting toward low-risk experiments rather than confronting sustain talk. Could more actively soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections or reframes that name both sides.
