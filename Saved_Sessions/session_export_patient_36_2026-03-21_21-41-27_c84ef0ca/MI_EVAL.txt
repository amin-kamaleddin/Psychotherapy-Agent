# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—normalized guilt, affirmed progress, and validated bodily experience without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking and reflected bodily affect, but some reflections were cursory and missed deeper exploration of guilt and worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on protecting leaving time, moved from values to a specific action, rehearsed boundary language, and addressed likely barriers without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited choices, co‑constructed role‑plays and templates, and consistently adjusted language to match the patient's voice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's choice and offered options rather than directives, allowing the patient to select phrasing, supports, and timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out motivations with open questions and a confidence rating, but could more deliberately amplify and summarize the patient's motivational statements and elicit an explicit commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a genuine collaborator rather than an expert—asked preferences, shared roles in rehearsal, and let the patient's ideas shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and strengthened change talk via questions, practice, and confidence scaling, though sometimes moved quickly to offering solutions instead of reflecting and amplifying change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing and reframing guilt and steering toward strategies, but could use more double-sided reflections to acknowledge competing pulls.
