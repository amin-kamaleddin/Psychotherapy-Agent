# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, validating the patient's choices and offering practical help without criticism. The clinician respected autonomy and responded supportively throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good understanding with accurate reflections and tailored responses, showing perspective-taking and validation. Missed opportunities for deeper emotional or value-based reflections that would capture underlying meaning beyond surface statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior (oral health related to the piercing) and consistently steered the conversation toward actionable harm-reduction steps. There was no drift into unrelated topics and clinician returned to concrete actions repeatedly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged the patient as an equal partner using inclusive language, repeatedly soliciting preferences and co-creating scripts and practical materials. The clinician tailored recommendations to the patient's requests and worked jointly on concrete plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's right to choose, avoided lecturing, and accommodated her request for brief, usable information. The clinician offered options rather than directives, supporting the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client-driven content (choice of tips and scripts) but did not consistently draw out intrinsic motivation, values, or reasons for change. More open, evocative questions and probes about motivation or confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine partnership by offering a menu of options, asking which the patient preferred, and collaboratively producing textable lines and routines. The clinician shared decision-making and respected the patient's expertise about her choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of practical commitments (e.g., choosing protective steps) but limited effort to amplify or explore change talk about broader behavior change. The clinician could have used more strategic open questions and reflections to evoke desire, reasons, or ability for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing or reinforcing sustain talk, validating the patient's decision and shifting to harm-reduction strategies. The clinician accepted resistance and pivoted to actionable steps rather than contesting the patient's stance.
