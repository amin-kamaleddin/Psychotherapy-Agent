# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validations and respect for the patient's limits, creating a safe tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, empathic reflections and normalizing statements showing understanding of overwhelm, but reflections stayed somewhat surface-level and did not probe deeper feelings or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session clearly focused on diabetes self-management with concrete behavioral targets (3‑day alarm, one meal, educator referral) and actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a collaborative problem‑solving conversation by inviting patient input, offering options, and adapting suggestions to his stated limits.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by honoring refusals, offering multiple choices, and emphasizing voluntary, low‑stakes testing without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited minimal self‑motivated language and relied more on offering solutions than drawing out intrinsic reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an explicitly collaborative stance throughout, deferring to the patient's choices and framing suggestions as options rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities and secured a concrete small commitment (3‑day alarm) but missed chances to elicit and amplify the patient's own reasons, benefits, or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to sustain talk with acceptance, reframing, and lowered stakes which reduced resistance, though could use more double‑sided reflections to highlight ambivalence.
