# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respect and nonjudgmental regard—validating Erik's concerns, normalizing feelings, and avoiding labeling while affirming his preferences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured practical constraints and worries; could probe deeper into the emotional meaning of being 'stuck on meds' or the fear of 'dropping dead' to strengthen empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear behavioral focus on diet, alcohol reduction, and glucose monitoring, repeatedly returning to concrete, measurable steps that fit Erik's shift-work context.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was overtly collaborative—offering options, practicing phrasing, checking confidence, and tailoring supports while sharing decision-making with Erik.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported Erik's sense of choice by offering multiple options, asking which felt doable, and normalizing modest steps, reinforcing his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation and used a confidence ruler, but often shifted quickly to solutions instead of systematically drawing out and amplifying Erik's intrinsic reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Erik as an active collaborator—inviting his preferences, co-constructing plans, and treating him as the expert on his life and context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported change talk with concrete, doable options and conversion into an action plan, but missed opportunities to pause and amplify Erik's own change language and elicit more client-generated reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and gently redirected sustain talk toward small experiments and reframing, yet could use more double-sided reflections and strategic rolling with sustain talk to further reduce its strength.
