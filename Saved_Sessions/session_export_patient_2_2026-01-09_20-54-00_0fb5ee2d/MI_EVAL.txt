# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and validates the patient's circumstances and efforts without judgment. Responses affirm the patient's plan and feelings, creating a warm, accepting tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, consistent understanding with timely, tailored reflective summaries that capture practical details and underlying motivation. Empathic listening helped the patient deepen her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on evening smoking reduction and repeatedly returns to the specific target behavior. Guides the patient from values to concrete, time-limited actions without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine two-way collaboration by soliciting the patient's ideas, reflecting them back, and organizing them into a workable plan. Recurrent collaborative language and checks-in integrate the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's sense of choice and control by offering options and asking what feels doable. Encourages ownership of the plan through open questions and patient-selected strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits the patient's intrinsic motives by linking behavior to personally meaningful values and asking for concrete, salient indicators of success. Invites the patient to articulate why change matters and what she will do.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a partnership where decision-making is shared and the patient's expertise guides the plan. Co-constructs realistic strategies and explicitly frames the plan as collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk by asking for priorities, concrete next steps, and signs of success tied to values. Reinforces and strengthens the patient's own change language, producing specific commitment behaviors.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk and respects boundary statements, redirecting ambivalence toward actionable steps. Could be more strategic by using explicit double-sided reflections that name both pulls before pivoting to change.
