# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician set a clear nonjudgmental tone and consistently respected the patient’s agenda, avoiding labeling and adapting care (e.g., creating the checklist exactly as requested).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and timely, with validation of concerns about pain, timing, cost, and side effects that showed a clear attempt to understand the patient’s viewpoint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on the patient’s immediate dental goals with clear, actionable guidance, but the clinician did not progressively steer the conversation toward smoking cessation as a future target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative: the clinician framed joint problem-solving, negotiated the checklist contents, and tailored decisions to the patient’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered choices, sought permission before raising smoking, and explicitly deferred to the patient’s readiness, respecting his decision not to engage in cessation counseling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was minimal effort to elicit the patient’s internal motivations or ambivalence about smoking, with no permission‑based exploratory questions or evocative prompts employed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the patient as an expert on his preferences and co‑created a practical plan, asking what to focus on and how to format the checklist to meet his needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opportunities to evoke and amplify change talk were missed: aside from a brief safety tip, the clinician did not ask open invitations to explore any reasons or desires related to smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively avoided argumentation and acknowledged the patient’s boundary, which blunted sustain talk, though the checklist note to 'limit discussion' may somewhat foreclose future openings.
