# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validates ambivalence (e.g., normalizing doubt and affirming willingness). Avoids labeling or criticism and affirms strengths, supporting a high level of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate, reflective listening and mirrors the patient's language to build rapport and summarize facts and surface emotions. Could deepen by offering more complex emotional reflections (e.g., discouragement, loss) to capture underlying feeling more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (short walks and concrete supports) and repeatedly steers questions and reflections back to practical steps. Directional focus is strong and sustained throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is conducted as a two-way exploration with frequent invitations for the patient's input and integration of his ideas into the plan. Language and decision-making reflect equal partnership and co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and patient control with open invitations and options rather than directives. Encourages ownership of the plan by asking what would help and which supports feel safest.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits personally relevant, practical motivators and reasons for change through targeted questions. Could be strengthened by exploring broader values or eliciting importance/confidence ratings and explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as a partnership and co-creates practical steps with the patient, inviting brainstorming and shared planning. Would reach a 5 with more explicit relinquishing of expert authority and co-designing follow-up structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks targeted questions that elicit concrete change talk and small-step plans, and highlights achievable signs of progress. Could better amplify and summarize change statements, elicit importance/confidence, and prompt specific commitments or timing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk empathically, avoiding confrontation, but often repeats sustain language verbatim. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections, reframes, and selective reinforcement of change language to shift momentum.
