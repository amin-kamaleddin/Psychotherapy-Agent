# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [x] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent respect and nonjudgmental support (validations: “That sounds really exhausting…”, “You’re doing a hard job…”). Warmth is somewhat procedural and often followed quickly by problem‑solving; could add one or two brief reflective turns before moving to solutions.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [x] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective‑taking; asks clarifying questions about baby’s age and concerns. Tends to shift rapidly to advice rather than deeper exploration of guilt/self‑judgment (e.g., “I feel like I’m failing”); use one complex reflection when self‑judgment appears.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [ ] 4  [x] 5  
**Comments:** Strong focus on target behaviors (sleep, feeding/weight, maternal rest, help‑seeking). Provides clear, actionable checks, concrete timelines, and asks the patient to choose a step. Maintain this clarity while balancing brief empathic summaries.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [x] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Invites input and offers options; patient makes choices (sitting while feeding). At times becomes prescriptive with multiple scripts and checklists—frame scripts as options and invite patient to adapt them.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [x] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Repeatedly acknowledges choice and offers alternatives; asks which feels doable. Occasionally offers directive advice (breathing rates, multiple scripts) without brief permission—preface advice with a quick check when possible.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [x] 3  [ ] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Elicits some commitments (agreeing to try strategies) but relies heavily on information/advice rather than drawing out patient motivations or values. Use more open, evocative questions (e.g., impact of getting reliable rest; confidence scaling).

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [x] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Generally positions interaction as a joint effort and asks for patient choices. Shares expertise appropriately but sometimes slips into expert/provider role with detailed scripts—invite patient to modify suggestions to increase partnership.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [x] 3  [ ] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Patient expresses preparatory change talk (curiosity, small commitments) but clinician misses opportunities to amplify and reflect this language or ask confidence/importance questions. Reflect and reinforce change talk when it appears.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [ ] 1  [ ] 2  [ ] 3  [x] 4  [ ] 5  
**Comments:** Normalizes concerns (e.g., typical waking patterns) and redirects toward manageable steps rather than confronting sustain talk. Could further use double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence when sustain talk is strong.
