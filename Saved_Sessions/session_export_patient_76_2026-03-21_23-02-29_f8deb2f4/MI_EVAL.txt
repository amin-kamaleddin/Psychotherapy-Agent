# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed clear nonjudgmental acceptance which likely made the patient feel safe, but the repeated formulaic phrasing sometimes sounded scripted rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and used the patient's metaphors, validating her experience, but they tended to be brief restatements rather than deeper, affect-focused reflections probing shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a strong, appropriate focus on reducing self-harm and moved from exploration to a concrete, measurable plan (ice in a ziplock, timing, outcomes).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone was collaborative with joint problem-solving and frequent invitations about logistics, but the clinician often led idea generation by proposing specific techniques early.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported the patient's sense of choice, framed options as the patient's decision, and avoided pressuring language while reinforcing control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good elicitation of short-term motives and concrete trial rooted in the patient's language, yet the clinician could have probed deeper for values, confidence, or longer-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used collaborative language and invited co-creation of a plan, but much of the content remained clinician-led with fewer explicit invitations for the patient to generate alternatives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist elicited and reinforced tentative change talk and a small trial, but missed opportunities to amplify, scale confidence, or summarize and consolidate stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged nonjudgmentally and the clinician shifted to alternatives, though double-sided reflections and explicit summaries of ambivalence were underused.
