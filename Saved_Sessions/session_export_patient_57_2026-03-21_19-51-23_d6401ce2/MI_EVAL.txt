# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys consistent nonjudgmental warmth and validation, normalizing peer pressure and affirming Jamal’s competence; no moralizing or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening and tailored reflections (e.g., worries about looking weak), but could deepen emotional understanding and explicitly explore ambivalence and feelings behind 'not wanting to start drama.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on teen alcohol use and on-field peer situations with interventions that directly target refusal strategies and practical behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively through role-play and adapts to Jamal’s comfort, though the clinician often supplies scripted lines instead of co-creating phrasing from Jamal’s own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports Jamal’s sense of choice by offering options, asking which feels doable, and respecting his preferences (e.g., not involving Marcus).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses prompts and role-play that generate some change talk, but relies more on offering solutions than on eliciting Jamal’s personal motivations, importance, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions Jamal as an active decision-maker by inviting choices and practice, yet frequently provides expert suggestions rather than jointly creating language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-oriented responses via options and role-play, but seldom uses open, evocative questions to draw out Jamal’s own desire, reasons, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and shifts to low-conflict alternatives that reduce sustain talk, though could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly surface ambivalence.
