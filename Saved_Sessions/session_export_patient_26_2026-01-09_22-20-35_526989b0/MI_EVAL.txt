# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard with validating language, repeated safety checks, and affirmations of progress; tone was respectful and warm with no blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good perspective-taking and reflective listening (validating overwhelm, summarizing plans), but reflections were pragmatic and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional nuance like frustration or grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on childcare and reducing smoking/drinking and guided the session to concrete, actionable steps (checklist, scripts, enrollment, NRT) without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal problem‑solving partner by inviting the patient’s ideas, incorporating preferences (neighbor barter, part‑day mornings), and supporting shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice with options and invitational language, asking what the patient wanted and respecting preferred contact and scheduling without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited relevant internal material (cravings, safety, reasons for change) but tended to offer solutions rather than more deeply drawing out intrinsic motivations, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the interaction collaboratively and co‑constructed scripts and plans, though at times shifted to providing detailed information without explicitly asking permission first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk and affirmed small wins while supporting concrete next steps, but could better amplify motivations, values, and commitment language (e.g., scaling confidence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers nonjudgmentally and redirected to practical alternatives, effectively avoiding amplification of sustain talk though missing some opportunities for strategic double‑sided reflections.
