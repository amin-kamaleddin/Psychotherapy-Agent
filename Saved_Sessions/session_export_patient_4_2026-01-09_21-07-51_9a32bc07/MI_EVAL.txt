# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expresses unconditional positive regard and validates the patient’s experience with repeated, individualized affirmations and normalizing statements. Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate perspective-taking and strong reflective listening that capture emotion, context, and content. Reflections track the patient’s language and repeatedly check what matters most to her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on obtaining clearer diagnostic answers and returns each turn to actionable steps related to that target. Resists drifting and maintains a tightly linked agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as shared and consistently solicits the patient’s values and preferences so her choices shape next steps. Co-constructs scripts and options in partnership with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and agency by offering multiple options, asking which the patient prefers, and respecting her firm preference for a strictly firm tone. Avoids pressuring and honors patient-led decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits intrinsic motives and meanings (reduced panic, return to work) and amplifies desire for relief and being taken seriously. Could more consistently probe ambivalence, elicit confidence ratings, and draw out the patient’s exact words about importance and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operates largely as a collaborator offering options and co-creating wording, inviting the patient’s input on tone and content. To reach top score could more explicitly frame the patient as the primary expert and negotiate responsibility and follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces reasons for change and asks evocative questions that produce intentions and benefits. Missed opportunities include deeper amplification of desire/ability/need statements and eliciting stronger commitment language or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids direct confrontation and does not reinforce sustain talk, but does not actively use strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring costs) to shift attention from fear-driven reassurance-seeking. Some clinician language supported testing rather than gently pivoting away from sustain talk.
