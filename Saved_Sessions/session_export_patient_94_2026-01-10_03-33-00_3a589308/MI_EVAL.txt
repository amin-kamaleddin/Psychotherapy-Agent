# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opened nonjudgmentally, checked safety, validated frustration, and avoided blame; more normalizing statements and empathic summaries would deepen unconditional acceptance to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening by acknowledging hurry and concerns and tailoring explanations, but relied on information-giving and used few reflective statements to explore feelings or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on appropriate asthma management and inhaler use, consistently steering to measurable criteria, monitoring plans, alternatives, and concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged the patient as an equal partner—elicited needs, co‑constructed scripts, offered choices of formats, and adapted to time constraints, reflecting mutual problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's right to decide, offered options rather than directives, and explicitly supported delaying treatment while reviewing risks and monitoring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Primarily supplied expertise and solutions rather than eliciting Maria's own motives, values, or reasons for change, missing opportunities to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited input, respected preferences and time, and co‑created usable materials and scripts, demonstrating strong shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicited minimal change talk—provided useful information and tools but did not ask open, evocative questions to surface desire, reasons, or commitment for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying resistance with validation and neutral information and supported delaying meds, though more double‑sided or strategic reflections could have further shifted sustain talk.
