# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, highlighting successes (less back pain, looser pants). The warm, supportive approach avoids labels or criticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with effective reflective listening and frequent checks for understanding. Reflections name specific barriers and emotions in the patient's language, validating mixed feelings and discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on exercise consistency and brief dietary/craving strategies with early agenda-setting and scaling questions. Clinician links conversation to actionable steps and concrete goal-setting (specific days/times, contingency plans).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative with decisions and plans co-constructed and the patient's input solicited at every step. Clinician summarizes and back-feeds the patient's ideas rather than delivering expert-driven directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports the patient's sense of choice by offering multiple options and asking which he prefers, avoiding pressure or directive language. Tone and questions reinforce the patient's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from the patient's own statements, using scaling questions and eliciting client-generated strategies tied to valued outcomes. Clinician builds on expressed values like reduced pain and improved fit of clothes to expand change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the session as a joint enterprise and defers to the patient's expertise about what will work, repeatedly asking which options he wants to try. Co-creates plans and checks permission before drafting materials, demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with scaling and concrete prompts (what helped, what would move an 8 to a 9). Could pause more to amplify spontaneous change statements and explore their personal meaning to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk nonjudgmentally and redirects toward change-oriented planning with practical contingency strategies. Could more often use double-sided or complex reflections to highlight both the sustain concerns and the patient's reasons for change to further diminish sustain talk.
