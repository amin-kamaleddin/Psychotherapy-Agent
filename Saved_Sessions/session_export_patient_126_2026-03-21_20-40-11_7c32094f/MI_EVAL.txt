# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing Thomas's views and affirming his efforts while inviting him to set the agenda.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate paraphrases captured minimization and context, though deeper, more complex emotion-focused reflections (shame, identity, stress) would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on smoking and drinking and progressed logically to a concrete, patient-centered plan with actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly cooperative with repeated invitations for Thomas's input, negotiated decisions, and co-development of realistic strategies like role‑play and chosen weeknights.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently affirmed choice, offered options, asked permission for interventions, and avoided pressure, supporting Thomas's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited Thomas's own motivations, past successes, and solutions using scaling and open questions, drawing strategies from the patient rather than prescribing them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist shared power and treated Thomas as the source of solutions, inviting collaboration on concrete steps and honoring his preferences when building the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions, pros/cons, and scaling elicited meaningful change talk and prior success, though more frequent reflection and amplification of specific change statements would consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward change via scaling and exploring consequences, but could have used more targeted double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain motives while steering change.
