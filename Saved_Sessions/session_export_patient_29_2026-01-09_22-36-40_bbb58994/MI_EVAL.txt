# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with affirmations and validations, honoring patient preferences (e.g., asking permission to CC spouse). Maintained unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and accurate summaries of concerns with open questions. Could deepen affect-focused reflections and pause less for information-giving to explore emotional nuance more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, concrete focus on smoking cessation with actionable steps (medication options, quit date, check-ins) and handled relevant logistics. Consistently steered toward behavior-linked goals and tailoring (cigs/day, pharmacy).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as a joint enterprise, co-constructing the plan and repeatedly inviting patient input. Adjusted recommendations to fit the patient's preferences and practical concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices, asked the patient's preference, and sought consent before actions (e.g., CC spouse, arrange prescriber visit). Reinforced patient ownership of decisions throughout the visit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation effectively with evocative questions and scaling but often shifted quickly to information and logistics. Would benefit from more amplification of the patient’s own motivations, strengths, and change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the patient as an active decision-maker and collaborated on treatment choices and planning (combo NRT with varenicline backup). Demonstrated power sharing and joint decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (importance/confidence scales, quit date, affirmations) and asked useful follow-ups. Could amplify the patient’s own commitment language and probe past successes to strengthen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk empathically and offered alternative strategies (patch+gum, behavioral plans) without arguing. Could use more strategic softening techniques (double-sided reflections, gentle discrepancy) to pivot ambivalence further.
