# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance with warm, validating language and normalizing statements that validate Linda's experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic accuracy with multiple complex reflections that capture emotions, values, and shifting ambivalence throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on asthma management and decision-making about a daily inhaler, repeatedly steering the conversation toward concrete, measurable steps and an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative, inviting Linda's input at every turn, privileging her expertise, and co-creating the plan using inclusive language and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports autonomy by offering options, framing choices rather than directives, checking what feels acceptable, and emphasizing Linda's right to decide at her own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits intrinsic motivation (desire for 'normal life' and concrete markers) but could more systematically expand motivations and elicit commitment or importance/confidence ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Partnership is a clear strength: the clinician frames the work as joint, solicits preferences, and shares power in planning, ensuring the plan reflects Linda's priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces useful change talk and concrete first steps, but missed opportunities to amplify change language and to elicit an explicit commitment statement or confidence rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates and reflects sustain talk without arguing, which prevents resistance, but relies mainly on validation rather than more strategic techniques like double-sided or selective reflections to pivot toward change.
