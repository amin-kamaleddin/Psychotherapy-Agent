# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's feelings with validating reflections and nonjudgmental options, reducing shame and respecting capacity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing ambivalence and using timely paraphrases to deepen understanding rather than merely summarize facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on engaging with medical care and guides the conversation toward a concrete, actionable, time‑bound plan (a tiny text with bullets, childcare line, and a contact).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructs the plan with the patient by soliciting feasibility details and preferences, checking back frequently, and shaping actions around the patient's context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers options and asks for the patient's preferences (format, wording, timing), normalizes moving slowly, and leaves final choices to the patient to foster ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits doable steps and the patient's reasons for choosing them (e.g., reading during naps) but could more deeply draw out internal values and assess confidence or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the encounter as joint planning, privileges the patient's expertise about her life, and invites her to choose specifics, reflecting an equal, collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and affirms small, practical steps and the chosen plan, but explores underlying values and confidence less thoroughly to more strongly enhance intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and neutralizes reluctance with acceptance and smaller alternatives, though occasional double‑sided reflections naming both sustain and change talk could better pivot ambivalence toward action.
