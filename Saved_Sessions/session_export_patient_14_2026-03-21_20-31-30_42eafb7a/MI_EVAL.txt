# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated consistent unconditional positive regard—apologized for the wait, normalized frustration, and accepted the patient's pragmatic framing without judgement. Tone remained warm and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurately reflected the patient's perspective and matched his efficiency orientation, acknowledging time pressures and practical barriers. Reflections summarized his situation clearly and reinforced understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, specific behavioral focus on increasing water intake via visible bottle and calendar‑based reminders. Repeatedly steered conversation toward actionable, time‑limited steps and avoided drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint problem‑solving and invited the patient to shape the approach, offering choices and deferring to his preferences. Co‑constructed implementation details like exact notification text and timing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored the patient's autonomy by offering options rather than directives and adapting to his request for brevity. Respected constraints and reinforced ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's own motivations and resources (change desire, confidence, barriers) and used his responses to shape the plan. Asked about confidence and contingencies to prompt patient-generated strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the patient as the expert and repeatedly asked which options felt most workable, then implemented his choices (template, one‑word prompt). Language and offers demonstrated true power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change language with a menu of small, concrete options and affirmations of the patient's selections. Used scaling and follow‑ups to draw out reasons and increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward tiny, doable options, reducing reinforcement of reasons against change. Missed some opportunities for double‑sided reflections or summaries that explicitly name both sides of ambivalence.
