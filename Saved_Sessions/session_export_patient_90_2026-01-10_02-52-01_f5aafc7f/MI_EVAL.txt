# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard, validated Marcus's feelings, and explicitly worked within his boundary about keeping the dog. This respect for his values and context was evident throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were strong empathic reflections (e.g., overwhelmed, pressure to give up the dog) that built rapport. However, empathy was not always sustained or deepened before moving into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on asthma control and related barriers with concrete, sequenced next steps. Agenda-setting and repeated prioritization kept the visit purpose-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the psychiatrist co-created scripts and checklists, solicited Marcus's preferences, and adapted plans to his constraints. Marcus's language was integrated into plans, reflecting genuine two-way work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently reinforced choice by offering options, asking permission, and respecting Marcus's decision not to give up the dog. Repeated invitations to choose next steps supported client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some priorities and willingness were elicited (meds first, openness to low-disruption steps), but the clinician relied heavily on offering solutions rather than drawing out Marcus's internal motivations or values. More open questions and probes about why change matters or confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as collaborative and asked which area to tackle, but at times took an expert/problem-solver lead by supplying many concrete recommendations. Greater solicitation of Marcus's ideas would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was supported (acknowledging out-of-control asthma and agreeing to med strategies), but the clinician did not consistently deepen desire, reasons, or confidence for change. There were missed opportunities to amplify Marcus's own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided reinforcing sustain talk and validated Marcus's attachment to his dog while offering alternatives that preserved it. Use of more strategic MI techniques (double-sided reflections, ambivalence summaries) could further shift sustain talk toward change.
