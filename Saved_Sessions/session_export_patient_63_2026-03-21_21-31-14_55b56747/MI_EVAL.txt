# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with frequent validation and positive regard (e.g., normalizing reluctance and affirming efforts). No blaming or dismissive language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, empathic reflections that captured emotional tone (guilt, fear of rejection) and helped the client elaborate. Reflections and validations were frequent and well-timed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on shoplifting risk and moved from exploration to concrete action planning and rehearsal. Goals, scaling, and targeted strategies maintained clear momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-way collaboration with the client invited into each decision point and plans co-authored (choice of refusal, who to text, role‑play). Frequent check-ins and shared planning demonstrated mutual engagement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, asked permission, and framed suggestions as choices rather than directives. Even concrete scripts were presented as trial options to preserve ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation (avoid court, desire to stop, scaling confidence) and small commitments, but leaned toward offering strategies rather than deepening values- or identity-based reasons for change. More prompts to elicit personal meaning would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance, inviting client preference at each step and respecting expertise (permission to role-play, choice of strategies). The plan reflected Jordan's priorities and input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk using vision and scaling and affirmed progress, but missed some opportunities to amplify intrinsic motives and values beyond practical/legal outcomes. Follow-up questions to deepen 'why' would increase momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, normalized ambivalence, and reframed toward strategies without arguing. Could use more explicit double-sided reflections and brief summaries of sustain talk before pivoting to reduce defensiveness.
