# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—opening support, multiple affirmations, and normalization of choices—without shaming or judgment. Interaction maintained warmth and clear respect for Marek’s perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used strong reflective listening and affect labeling to summarize ambivalence and notice barriers. Some reflections could have been deeper or more complex to explore emotional meaning and identity around drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on weekend alcohol use and progressed toward a concrete harm‑reduction plan. Elicited typical behavior and produced specific steps and a follow‑up check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner by offering options, asking which felt doable, and co‑creating the plan and follow‑up method. Decisions were negotiated and Marek was treated as an active collaborator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Marek’s right to choose with noncoercive language and multiple options, emphasizing small steps and respecting refusals to promise big changes. Reinforced his control over decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Marek’s reasons for change using values questions and scaling, producing internal motivation and a trial plan. Could strengthen by amplifying and linking change talk to values and discrepancy to consolidate commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership through joint problem‑solving, rehearsing language together, and asking Marek to choose reminders and check‑in preferences. Communication fostered genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and developed change talk with values questions, a 0–10 scale, and a concrete harm‑reduction plan and follow‑up. Could more consistently amplify and reflect emerging change talk to strengthen ability and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and reflected sustain talk while exploring ambivalence and options. Would benefit from more double‑sided reflections to further soften sustain talk and gently shift the balance toward change.
