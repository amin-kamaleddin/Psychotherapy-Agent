# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, opening with a clear invitation and safety framing; validated concerns and honored the patient's time constraints throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reassuring reflections, but many responses were information-heavy with missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused emphasis on safe management of citalopram/phenelzine and provided concrete, goal-directed steps without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by offering expertise and practical help while inviting the patient's preferences, co-constructing messages and checking approval before finalizing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirmed the patient's choice and control, offering options and recommendations while avoiding pressure and leaving decisions to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited immediate plans and agreement but the interaction was largely clinician-driven problem-solving with few open questions to draw out the patient's internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a partnership by asking permission before helping, offering tailored supports, and letting the patient's choices shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encouraged and shaped actionable steps (contact prescriber/pharmacy, continue citalopram, draft messages) and asked permission before assisting, though could have further amplified the patient's change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated reluctance to stop and steered toward safety and practical alternatives without reinforcing sustain talk; could more explicitly use double-sided reflections to shift sustain talk into change.
