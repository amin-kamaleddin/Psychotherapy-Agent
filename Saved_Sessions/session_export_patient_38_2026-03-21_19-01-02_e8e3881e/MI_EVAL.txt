# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalized ambivalence, and praised effort. Avoided blaming language and reassured the patient when she hesitated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and named emotions which helped the patient elaborate, but reflections were mostly surface-level. Could use more complex reflections and deeper exploration of the emotional meaning of the scare.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on contraceptive planning and adherence while allowing the patient to set the pace. Offered relevant information and closed with a specific follow-up plan tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a true collaborative partner by inviting choices, co-creating practical plans, and asking clarifying questions before offering solutions. Negotiated follow-up and honored the patient’s preferences throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission before suggesting, and normalizing ambivalence. Reinforced the patient's control and avoided pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical, personally meaningful steps and got the patient to commit to a small action and follow-up. Could deepen evocation by eliciting the patient’s own reasons, values, and past successes to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the patient as an active decision-maker and repeatedly solicited input, co-creating solutions like alarm labels and telehealth preferences. Demonstrated genuine power-sharing and client-led planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk through option-setting, asking for a small step, and arranging follow-up, which encouraged commitment to alarms. Missed opportunities to amplify and probe stronger change language (e.g., scaling or evocative follow-ups).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled resistance nonconfrontationally, normalized hesitation, and reframed obstacles into practical strategies. Could more explicitly use double-sided reflections and strategic redirection to further weaken sustain talk while honoring autonomy.
