# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental stance from the opening (“I’ll listen without judgment,” safety check) and repeatedly affirmed the patient’s effort and honesty (“good choice,” “I’m proud you’re trying this”). No shaming or labeling.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Nonjudgmental opening / safety check
- [x] Multiple affirmations/validation across the session
- [ ] Any shaming or dismissive language

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One clear reflective summary captured ambivalence (“common trade-off — comfort now, cost later”), but the clinician moved quickly to problem-solving with few deeper affective reflections or complex reflections of skepticism/worry.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Brief reflective summary of ambivalence
- [ ] Multiple/depth reflections of emotion or complex reflections
- [ ] Sustained empathic exploration before offering solutions

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on reducing phone/social media use and clear, concrete behavioral experiment (two Pomodoro cycles, phone placement, DND, 10‑minute pause, friend check‑in). Repeatedly summarized action steps and homework.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Clear focus on target behavior
- [x] Specific, measurable homework/behavioral experiment given
- [x] Repeated summaries to maintain direction

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician offered options and asked the patient to choose early (collaborative), but then shifted to a more prescriptive delivery of a single plan with many clinician-driven restatements rather than co‑creating alternatives.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Offered options and invited patient choice
- [ ] Co‑creation of multiple alternatives / patient-generated solutions
- [ ] Shared decision‑making maintained throughout

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed patient choice and did not coerce; options framed as the patient's choice and safety/permission language used. Praise and repeated direction slightly reduced pure autonomy emphasis but remained supportive.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Options presented; patient choice elicited
- [x] Safety reminders and respect for patient's decision
- [ ] Explicit use of scaling/confidence questions to bolster autonomy

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change‑relevant material by asking which option felt realistic and what barriers might arise, drawing out ambivalence and pragmatic barriers. Did not systematically elicit the patient’s own reasons, values, or confidence.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Asked which option felt realistic
- [x] Asked about barriers (what might get in the way)
- [ ] Elicited patient’s personal reasons/values for change
- [ ] Explored importance/confidence with scaling

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early session displayed partnership (choice of options), but clinician assumed expert role thereafter by prescribing and repeatedly restating the same plan rather than actively integrating more patient‑generated ideas.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Invited patient input early (choice among options)
- [ ] Ongoing shared problem‑solving / explicit leveraging of patient expertise
- [ ] Joint contingency planning (co‑designed backup plans)

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient produced clear change talk (sleep, tired in class, grades slipped) and committed to try the Pomodoro plan. Clinician acknowledged and reinforced the plan but did not consistently amplify desire/reasons/confidence language to strengthen motivation.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Patient generated change talk (reasons/costs of current behavior)
- [x] Clinician elicited a specific commitment to try (two Pomodoro cycles)
- [ ] Clinician amplified or reflected change talk to deepen commitment
- [ ] Follow‑up questions to strengthen confidence/importance

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (phone as comfort/connection, skepticism about sticking with plan) was acknowledged but not strategically softened with double‑sided reflections or reframing; clinician mainly pivoted to solution statements.

Checklist of observed behaviors:
- [x] Sustain talk was acknowledged by clinician
- [ ] Use of double‑sided reflections to validate and then lean toward change
- [ ] Strategic reframing or summarizing to reduce sustain talk
