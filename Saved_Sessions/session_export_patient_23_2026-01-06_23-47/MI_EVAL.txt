# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating language conveyed respect and unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and summaries showed strong perspective-taking and tied Claudia's drinking to mood, sleep, and living situation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing evening alcohol use and guided the conversation toward specific, measurable steps and relapse planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly joint problem-solving with the patient’s preferences driving decisions and the clinician offering help only after checking Claudia’s input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered a menu of options, asked permission to brainstorm, and repeatedly emphasized Claudia’s control over what to try, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Claudia’s motivations and ideas effectively, but could deepen intrinsic motivation by linking change to core values and eliciting stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated Claudia as the expert, co-constructing the plan and inviting choice about strategies and wording, demonstrating strong power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change language (desire, ability, reasons) and asked for plans, but missed some opportunities to amplify spontaneous change talk and elicit firmer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectfully acknowledged ambivalence and redirected toward solutions, though used fewer targeted techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk.
