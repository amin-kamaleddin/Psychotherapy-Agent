# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates David's concerns while offering options instead of blame. Could add more explicit normalizing language and brief affirmations to convey deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges stress, BP, and workplace worries and asks relevant questions, but responses are mainly informational rather than reflective of feelings. Missed opportunities to mirror tension or explore emotional impact of relapse to build rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with medication planning, coping strategies, scripts, and concrete next steps. Everything in the session is goal-directed and linked to quitting or harm reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuine two-way exchange by soliciting preferences, asking permission for role‑play, and adapting suggestions to David's concerns. Co-constructs monitoring, stop rules, and practical scripts with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers multiple options and frames decisions as collaborative, refraining from pressure and inviting the patient's choice. Could more explicitly emphasize David's control (e.g., check preference before detailed recommendations).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation via questions about past attempts and medication preferences but is largely clinician-driven with information and planning. Misses opportunities to draw out personal reasons, use rulers, or amplify spontaneous change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites input, offers role‑play and scripts, and co-creates practical plans, supporting shared decision-making. At times moves quickly to medical recommendations without first eliciting the patient's full priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits change talk through questions about patterns and offers concrete strategies that support action, but rarely amplifies or deepens change statements. Could use targeted reflections and open questions to evoke desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain-talk concerns and offers safer alternatives, avoiding argumentation, which prevents reinforcing ambivalence. Does not consistently use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to actively soften sustain talk and shift toward change.
