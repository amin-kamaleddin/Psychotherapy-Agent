# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician communicates respect and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing caution and validating concerns. Some language is clinical/directive and lacks explicit unconditional positive regard tied to the patient's values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and reflective responses (e.g., validating worry, open invitation to share). Missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflections and more complex reflections to amplify feeling and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on medication safety, contraindications, washout timing, and concrete next steps. Provides practical tools and guidance without drifting from the primary issue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-creates message drafts and asks the patient's preference, iteratively adjusting wording in response. At times the clinician supplies expert directives rather than consistently inviting the patient to generate options first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's right to choose, offers options (role‑play vs draft) and respects the wish to wait for written instructions. Reinforces patient control and avoids pressuring immediate action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some practical input (preference for script/email, commitment to wait) but largely offers solutions and information. Limited use of evocative questions to draw out intrinsic motivations, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative relationship through shared decision-making and joint drafting of messages/scripts. Occasionally adopts a more expert, directive stance when delivering firm medical instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports safe actions with concrete offers (help contacting clinician, drafting messages) but rarely elicits the patient's own reasons, desires, or confidence. Tends to provide solutions rather than draw out change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates hesitation and redirects toward safety with clear, supportive guidance, avoiding reinforcement of reasons against change. Missed chances for double-sided reflections to explicitly surface and resolve ambivalence.
