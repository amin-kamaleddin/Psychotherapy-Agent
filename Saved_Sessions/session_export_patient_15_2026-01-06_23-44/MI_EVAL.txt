# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated the patient's perspective while offering practical options; could increase explicit unconditional positive regard and personalized affirmations (e.g., praise for quitting smoking).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and captured practical concerns and some feelings; missed deeper emotional reflections and occasionally assumed stress rather than exploring underlying values or subtle emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on blood pressure management and modest alcohol reduction, producing a concrete, actionable plan (start low‑dose med at night, 1–2 week check‑in, two alcohol‑free nights).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Created a genuine collaborative dynamic with co‑created decisions, rehearsal, and adaptation to the patient's style; patient actively participated in crafting the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing the patient's preferences, reinforcing a strong sense of control and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited client language with confidence scaling and questions about concerns and preferences; could use more evocative open questions and reflections to draw out deeper reasons, values, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative with regular invitations for input and co‑construction of plans; partnership was slightly weakened at times by offering ready‑made scripts without always eliciting permission first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (willingness to try meds, 7/10 confidence, two alcohol‑free nights) but did not consistently amplify or elaborate reasons, strengths, or past successes to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and respected the patient's minimizations while offering options, but missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections and reframing to more actively soften sustain talk.
