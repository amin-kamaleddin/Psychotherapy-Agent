# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys respect and support, avoids blaming, and offers affirmations (e.g., praising the quit date), but some responses feel procedural and could use more individualized warmth and explicit validation of repeated attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and perspective-taking with reflections and checking, yet rarely uses deeper or complex emotional reflections to name frustration or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on quitting throughout, moving logically from assessment to concrete planning, rehearsal, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames work as a partnership—asks what the patient wants, co-creates the quit plan, offers role-play, and adapts the plan to patient concerns like side effects and cost.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices (meds vs reduction, role-play, quit-date selection) and responds to worries, but could more often ask permission before advising and more explicitly emphasize patient control over pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits intrinsic motivations (health, money) and past attempts, translating them into plans, but could use more evocative questions to draw out deeper values and confidence ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly invites input and shares decision-making, co-constructing the plan, though at times shifts to an expert/technical stance without first checking the patient's readiness for detailed recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk (quit date, reasons, NRT plan) and secures commitments, but could better amplify and summarize the patient's change language and explore its meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Identifies triggers and offers practical strategies, but uses few MI techniques (double-sided reflections, rolling with sustain talk) to acknowledge ambivalence and soften resistance before moving to problem-solving.
