# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, asked permission before advising, and affirmed the patient’s choices throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections that captured concrete drivers, but missed some opportunities for deeper affect-focused reflections to explore emotions like frustration or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on vaping tied to relevant goals (basketball, school) and consistently returned to actionable steps and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative stance with co-constructed plans, joint problem-solving, and reciprocal contributions between clinician and client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong support for autonomy—asked permission, offered multiple options, and framed decisions as the client’s to reinforce ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited motivations and ideas and drew out change talk, but at times shifted to providing a long list of strategies which reduced space for client-generated motivations and deeper values exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the client as the primary expert, solicited her preferences, and let her choose strategies and rehearsal options, demonstrating genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk using scaling questions, eliciting specific reasons and plans, and reinforcing concrete commitments and practice steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used neutral and double-sided reflections to avoid reinforcing sustain talk and redirect toward change, but could more deliberately explore costs of continued use in the client's own words and avoid lengthy clinician-driven strategy lists.
