# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental throughout ("I hear you…", "I’m here to support whatever you decide."). Affirmed patient priorities and avoided moralizing; created a safe atmosphere for honest disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking (used patient language, summarized content and meaning). Tracked ambivalence and constraints (work/family) with a steady empathic tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on smoking management and moved to a concrete plan (offered options, asked which was doable, elicited confidence and a success criterion). Session ended with actionable plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated patient as partner—offered options rather than directives and repeatedly asked which strategies felt useful. Plan was co‑constructed from patient preferences (scheduled smoking, alarm, telling Jamal).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed patient choice and avoided pressure ("You’re not quitting now…", offered gum as optional). Validated short‑term, self‑selected goal (one workday).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivators (avoid trouble, family) and confidence ruler, but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify spontaneous change talk (did not follow up on "I can quit" with probes to strengthen motivation).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited input and shared planning authority ("Which feels most useful?" "Which of those feels most doable?"). Built plan collaboratively and offered follow‑up only as requested.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Some change talk elicited (e.g., "I can quit," commitment to try one day) and used confidence ruler, but clinician did not consistently reflect/amplify those statements or link them into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not confront sustain talk and reframed toward practical coping. Could more actively use double‑sided reflections and strategically evoke discrepancy to further weaken sustain talk.
