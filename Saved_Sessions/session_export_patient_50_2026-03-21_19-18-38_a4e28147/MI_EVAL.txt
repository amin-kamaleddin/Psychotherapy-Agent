# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalizing statements, validated feelings, and praised effort without shaming or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and normalizing language that matched Hana’s experience, but reflections were often brief and quickly followed by directives rather than deeper exploration of affect and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with every intervention tied to concrete, actionable steps toward sitting the exam.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Felt collaborative overall with invitations to choose tasks and debrief, but the interaction leaned toward clinician-driven problem solving rather than fully equal co‑investigation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed Hana’s choice and offered options, avoiding pressure, but provided many concrete prescriptions that limited space for Hana to generate her own strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit commitment (choosing tasks, readiness checks), but relied more on delivering solutions and missed opportunities for open evocative questions or scaling confidence/importance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some collaborative moves (asking preferences, co‑constructing details) yet largely led the session with detailed instructions instead of eliciting Hana’s ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk (task choice, readiness), but was uneven and often shifted to directive planning instead of reflecting and amplifying Hana’s own reasons and motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and provided contingency plans to reduce avoidance, though could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
