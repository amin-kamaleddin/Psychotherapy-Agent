# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing slip-ups and acknowledging the patient's burden. Avoids blaming and communicates respect for the patient's circumstances and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating the function of smoking in the patient's life. Reflections stay at practical/logistic levels; deeper, more complex reflections exploring underlying emotions would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking and reducing the child's exposure with each intervention tied to that target behavior. Balances empathy with purposeful steering toward concrete, actionable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as an equal partnership: clinician offers options and defers to the patient's preferences while co-creating the plan. Decisions and implementation details (if-then phrasing, check-in time) are mutually agreed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms the patient's right to choose, asks permission before suggestions, and offers multiple options. Reassuring language and invitations to select doable steps support the patient's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own solutions and commitment to a specific plan, drawing out change-oriented statements. Could further evoke stronger, value-based motivation (e.g., asking about meanings as a mother or using scaling questions) to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative throughout, inviting choices and co-constructing plans while positioning the patient as the decision-maker. Practical supports (options, reminders, shared planning) reinforce the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and concrete commitments, securing a realistic, patient-selected goal and follow-up. Missed opportunities to probe for personal reasons/values and to use summaries to further amplify change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and redirects sustain talk toward feasible harm-reduction strategies while normalizing ambivalence. Could employ more double-sided and complex reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain talk and nudge toward change.
