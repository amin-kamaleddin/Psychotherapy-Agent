# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through affirmations, normalization of ambivalence, and direct safety checks, creating a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with useful summaries, though could deepen empathic exploration by probing emotional nuance and using more complex reflections to surface underlying values or fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on marijuana use and moved the conversation toward a single, measurable experiment with concrete planning around timing, reminders, and refusal lines.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was highly collaborative—clinician repeatedly invited Sofia's preferences, co-constructed the plan, and coached options rather than prescribing solutions, reflecting strong equal-partner work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy through permission language, offering multiple alternatives and experiments, accepting limited commitment, and emphasizing Sofia's control over choices and follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation with importance/confidence rulers and barriers, but could strengthen evocation by linking change to Sofia's personal values or future goals and amplifying spontaneous change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered clear partnership by asking permission, letting Sofia choose strategies, tailoring scripts to her friends, and inviting practice and feedback, demonstrating high-quality power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk using rulers and suggested experiments, yet could have amplified Sofia's reasons (costs, doubts about effectiveness) with follow-ups or double-sided reflections to deepen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and neutrally named ambivalence, gently redirecting toward change; missed opportunities include more explicit double-sided reflections or reframing to reduce the persuasive pull of sustain talk.
