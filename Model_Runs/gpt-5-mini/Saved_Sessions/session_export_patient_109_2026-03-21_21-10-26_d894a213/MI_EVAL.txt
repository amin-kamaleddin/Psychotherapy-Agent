# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session, with repeated normalizations and respect for Rafael's hesitancy. Language conveyed unconditional positive regard and avoided blame or shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captured Rafael's values and frustration, showing perspective-taking and normalization. Reflections were accurate but often brief and missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration of discouragement or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on restarting exercise and concrete follow-through, repeatedly returning to specific behavioral steps. Resulted in a concrete if–then plan and actionable supports.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with co-created plans, offers to draft texts and role‑play, and frequent invitations for Rafael's input. Decisions were constructed jointly rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Rafael's sense of choice through permission-seeking, offering multiple options, and avoiding pressure. Affirmed his right to choose the plan that fits his life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited intrinsic reasons and past facilitators using rulers and options, producing some commitment language. Could have probed values and discrepancies more deeply and amplified Rafael's own change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed partnership and shared decision-making, inviting Rafael to choose options and co-creating the plan. Followed his lead and treated him as the expert on what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and supported change talk with importance/confidence rulers, past successes, and a doable step, leading to specific commitments. Missed chances to amplify and elaborate change statements to convert reasons into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk but did relatively little to actively soften it or redirect ambivalence. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections, reframing, and strategic reflections to reduce persistent skepticism.
