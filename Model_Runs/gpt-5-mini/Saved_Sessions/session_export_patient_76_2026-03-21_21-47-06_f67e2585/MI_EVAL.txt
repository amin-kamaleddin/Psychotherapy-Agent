# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing distress and affirming courage; no blaming or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, attuned reflections of content and feeling and summarized the client's experience well, inviting elaboration and showing strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on self-harm and immediate safety, repeatedly returning to concrete risk-reduction steps and a structured time-limited coping plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited the client's input throughout and co-constructed texts and the safety plan, tailoring materials to her preferences and showing genuine shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering multiple options, avoiding pressuring language, asking which steps felt possible, and honoring ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation and invited small doable steps, but missed deeper exploration of values, discrepancy, and past successes to strengthen intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively and sought permission, but occasionally shifted to more expert direction for safety planning; could invite more client-generated solutions and weighing of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk around wanting to be okay and small actions, yet did not fully deepen or amplify reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectfully acknowledged sustain talk and redirected to alternatives without arguing, but could more strategically soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections or explicit affirmation of the desire for relief.
