# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician shows respect and nonjudgmental support, validates distress, and offers practical help. Not a 5 because responses often shift into problem‑solving without explicit, individualized affirmations of the patient's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate empathic reflection and tailors responses to anxiety and safety concerns. Falls short of top score as many turns become directive with fewer emotion‑focused reflections or checks of understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on co‑parenting, child safety, de‑escalation, and actionable communication strategies throughout. The session stays goal‑directed and moves toward concrete behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts partnership (asks permission, offers help) but largely supplies ready‑made scripts and directives rather than co‑creating language with the patient. Collaboration is present but uneven and clinician‑led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice through trial framing, offering options, and presenting safety alternatives. Some language is prescriptive for safety reasons and the clinician does not consistently elicit the patient's preferred next steps or confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some practical commitment (asks if patient wants a script) but misses opportunities to draw out values, motivations, and confidence. Interaction is more solution‑focused than consistently evocative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows moments of partnership (permission, brief invites) but largely positions the clinician as expert providing wording rather than co‑constructing it with the patient. Would benefit from more collaborative drafting and reflection.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation of action (trial, scripts) but few open questions or reflective amplifications to deepen the patient's own reasons and confidence for change. The clinician tends to supply solutions rather than elicit and amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges obstacles and offers strategies to respond to resistance but does not use MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, reframing) to soften sustain talk. Moves quickly into corrective scripts rather than exploring ambivalence.
