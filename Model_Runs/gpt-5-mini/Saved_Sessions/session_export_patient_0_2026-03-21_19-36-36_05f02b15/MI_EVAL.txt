# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and warm, validated pain and isolation, and accepted Adrian’s preference not to quit while offering harm‑reduction options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and concise reflections that validated feelings, but could use deeper, more frequent complex reflections exploring shame and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the presenting issues (nasal infection, testing, safer use) and steered toward concrete, relevant actions and recommendations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly two‑way—clinician solicited preferences, tailored scripts/resources, and co‑constructed next steps with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking which approach felt most useful, and honoring the patient’s decision not to quit while suggesting acceptable harm‑reduction steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked about reasons for use and offered choices that produced some change language, but did not systematically draw out intrinsic motivators, values, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance—asked permission to explore, deferred to patient priorities, and invited the patient to choose next steps and resources.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created openings for change talk and elicited some concern and willingness, but did not sufficiently reflect, amplify, or deepen desire, ability, reasons, or need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and did not reinforce sustain talk, gently redirected toward health and safer behaviors, though missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections.
