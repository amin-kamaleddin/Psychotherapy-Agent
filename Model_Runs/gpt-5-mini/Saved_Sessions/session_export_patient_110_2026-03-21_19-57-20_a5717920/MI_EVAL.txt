# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalizing anxiety and affirming the patient’s priorities (e.g., staying active with his wife) without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and brief reflections that validate worry, but the clinician missed opportunities for deeper, more evocative reflections of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management and statin adherence with stepwise plans, scripts, and concrete follow-up actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the interaction as a joint effort, invites patient choices, and incorporates the patient’s goals into scripts and tools throughout the visit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice, asks permission, offers options, and frames steps as the patient’s decisions, reinforcing ownership and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation and commitment language (retirement goal, action steps) but relies heavily on information delivery and missed chances to draw out deeper personal motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and co-creates content with the patient, though the clinician often provides templates rather than eliciting more patient-generated options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk with scripts, affirmations, and concrete steps, but could use more open questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and need language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, normalizes concerns, and offers practical safeguards (labs, symptom log), but rarely uses double-sided reflections to explicitly explore ambivalence.
