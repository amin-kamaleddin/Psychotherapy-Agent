# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalization (warm openings, affirmations, and nonjudgmental validation). The clinician avoided labels and maintained a respectful tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections capturing both content and emotion (e.g., relief, hesitancy) to validate Luka’s experience and build trust. Perspective-taking and reflective listening were strong and consistent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on reducing violent incidents and alcohol use with targeted risk questions and stepwise safety planning. The session moved logically from assessment to planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine collaborative partnership by inviting co-design of scripts, roleplaying on request, and negotiating plans rather than imposing them. Decisions were clearly two-way and negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, respecting Luka’s preferences (limits vs. abstinence), and seeking consent for roleplay and planning. Questions preserved agency and reinforced his control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation using open questions and confidence rulers, prompting some client-generated plans and supports. However, the clinician provided many solutions and missed deeper evocative questions to draw out values and personalized reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by inviting input and co-creating scripts, but at times shifted to offering multiple specific solutions rather than eliciting Luka’s own ideas first. Overall respectful but slightly expert-driven in places.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (e.g., doable steps, confidence increases), yet missed opportunities to deepen and systematically amplify reasons and benefits for change. The clinician often offered options quickly instead of pausing for spontaneous client language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence nonjudgmentally and avoided reinforcing sustain talk while handling safety concerns directly. Could enhance use of double-sided reflections to explicitly name both sides of ambivalence and further redirect sustain talk.
