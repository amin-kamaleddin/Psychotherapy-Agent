# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated consistent unconditional positive regard and validation, honoring the patient's wishes and avoiding blame or minimization. Accommodated requests (sticking to dental care, formatting the note) and prioritized the patient's concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely empathic statements and reflections that reduced defensiveness and kept the patient engaged. Repeatedly summarized priorities and acknowledged frustrating past experiences with medications and lecturing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained an appropriate, respectful focus on the patient's immediate dental goals but did not steadily move toward smoking-related behavior change. Linked smoking to healing and offered harm-reduction tips but missed building toward a small, specific change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-created the plan, scripts, and formatted notes while checking preferences and incorporating the patient's wording. Decisions were shaped by the patient's input rather than clinician-driven directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's right to choose and avoided pressuring language, offering options and asking permission before drafting materials. Supported the patient's decisions and provided tools he could use if and when he wanted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not meaningfully elicit the patient's intrinsic motivation or explore values and reasons for change. Conversation was primarily informational and pragmatic rather than using evocative questions or scaling to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine partnership and power-sharing by inviting input at each step and checking formatting/name/date preferences. Shifted control to the patient and used his preferences to shape concrete outputs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made minimal efforts to elicit or amplify change talk and largely honored the patient's wish not to discuss quitting. Missed opportunities for open questions and reflections to explore desires, abilities, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided reinforcing sustain talk, honored repeated refusal to discuss quitting, and reframed the conversation toward neutral, practical options. Offered harm-reduction steps and alternatives rather than arguing or confronting.
