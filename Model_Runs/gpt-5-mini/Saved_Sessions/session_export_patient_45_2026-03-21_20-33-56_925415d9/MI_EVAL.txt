# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's frustration without judgment. Meets the patient's guarded tone with warmth and validating reflections.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, naming emotions and summarizing priorities. Reflections help the patient soften and engage.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on diabetes management and alcohol reduction with specific, actionable planning. Consistently guides the conversation toward concrete behavior-change steps suited to the patient's stage.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative, asking preferences and checking back frequently. However, the clinician often supplies pre-formulated scripts and lists rather than eliciting the patient's own wording or plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and framing recommendations as options. Avoids pressure and preserves agency when discussing safety and tapering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits intentions and preferences that generate some change talk, but clinician supplies much of the content. Could strengthen evocation by asking more open, motivational questions and drawing out the patient's own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance through permission and choice, repeatedly asking what the patient wants to start with. The frequent provision of detailed scripts and tactics slightly shifts the interaction toward expert-driven instruction instead of co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports change talk with practical options and a stepwise plan, and responds well to expressed readiness. Missed opportunities include amplifying specific change statements, using scaling/confidence questions, and asking evocative follow-ups to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing and neutrally reflects the patient's minimization while redirecting to options and safety planning. Could improve by using more double-sided reflections and explicitly validating sustain-side concerns while guiding toward change.
