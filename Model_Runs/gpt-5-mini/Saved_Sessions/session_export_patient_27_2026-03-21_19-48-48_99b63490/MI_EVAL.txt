# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (opening safety check, affirmations, normalizing). Offers pragmatic help rather than blame, maintaining a caring tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses validating reflections and attends to anxiety (grounding breaths, honoring role‑play discomfort), but sometimes shifts quickly to problem‑solving instead of exploring deeper feelings about fear of rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on disclosing diabetes to peers and guides the session toward a specific, achievable action (texts, rehearsals, planned replies).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a joint effort—co‑creates messages, rehearses with the patient, and adapts to her preferences (e.g., switching from role‑play to drafting).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly fosters choice and ownership by offering options, honoring the patient's decisions, and avoiding pressuring language while returning decision‑making to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete plans and readiness to act (goal at the outset, rehearsal, commitment), but elicits less of the patient's deeper values, reasons, or confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and invites patient input in shaping the plan, though occasional directive phrasing slightly reduces full power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Helps translate intention into an actionable step and refines wording repeatedly, but uses fewer open questions to deepen the patient's own verbalized reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and redirects toward planning with scripts for difficult reactions, but uses limited MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided or strategic reflections) to explicitly resolve ambivalence.
