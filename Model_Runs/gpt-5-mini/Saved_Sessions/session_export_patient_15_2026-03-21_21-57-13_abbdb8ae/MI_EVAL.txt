# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, affirmed the patient and normalized practical steps; offered options and reinforced patient choices without blaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of the patient's practical concerns; reflections stayed at a behavioral level and did not probe deeper feelings or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on blood pressure management and moved logically from assessment to concrete actions and follow‑up without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—invited edits, incorporated the patient's additions (alcohol, cost), and negotiated formats and reminders so the patient helped shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering multiple acceptable paths, asking which option to start, and respecting the patient's preference for clear practical steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivations (importance rating, what would move it, willingness to start readings) but could deepen evocation by exploring personal values, long‑term meanings, and intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine partnership and power‑sharing through repeated invitations for preference, asking permission, and integrating patient edits into the plan and materials.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (importance ruler, choice questions, affirmations) and secured actionable commitments, but could amplify the patient's own reasons and confidence more to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk but made limited use of MI strategies to soften it—few double‑sided reflections or reframes to address minimizations about stress and alcohol, and missed opportunities to pivot to change reasons.
