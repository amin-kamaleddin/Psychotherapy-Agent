# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing feelings and honoring preferences (knee-friendly, quiet options) throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections, validating constraints and emotions, but missed some opportunities for deeper exploration of intense language like 'I feel gross.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on starting and sustaining an exercise routine and moved the client from motivation to concrete action steps without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan step-by-step, solicited preferences, and tailored exercises and accountability to the client's needs, showing strong joint decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported client choice by offering multiple options and framing suggestions as negotiable, reinforcing the client's control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited practical commitments and scheduled steps, but could have used more strategic open questions and amplifying reflections to draw out deeper personal reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the client as the expert on her life, repeatedly asked for input, and allowed the client to choose accountability and wording, demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with action-focused questions and commitment prompts, though could have more often amplified the client's own reasons and asked direct evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed barriers into manageable alternatives, but occasionally could have used double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence more explicitly.
