# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing Marcus's perspective and inviting safety disclosure in a caring way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and validated ambivalence, demonstrating solid perspective-taking, but missed opportunities for deeper feeling-focused or complex reflections to enhance empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related safety throughout the session with agenda-setting and role-play directly tied to the targeted behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the conversation as a shared effort by inviting Marcus to choose options, role-playing with him, checking in, and shaping the plan based on his input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, avoiding pressure, and explicitly stating Marcus did not need to promise to quit while providing harm-reduction options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out Marcus's own motivations and plans through open questions and role-play, but could more actively probe values and amplify spontaneous change talk to build intrinsic motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered collaboration by asking what Marcus wanted to focus on and inviting choices, though at times provided lengthy, prescriptive lists rather than co-constructing details step-by-step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change-relevant statements and rehearsed concrete steps, but could more explicitly reflect, amplify DARN language, and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and redirected sustain talk toward pragmatic harm-reduction, yet could use more strategic techniques like double-sided reflections to reduce sustain talk further.
