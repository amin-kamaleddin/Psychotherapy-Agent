# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and affirmation, validated the patient's limits, and avoided judgment or minimizing; language normalized ambivalence and supported the patient’s pace.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with frequent paraphrases and summaries, but missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration of stress, guilt, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction throughout the session, linking questions and interventions to concrete, actionable steps toward the stated goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving and elicited patient preferences, though presenting multiple structured options quickly sometimes limited space for the patient to generate her own alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice from the start, asked permission before advising, and respected the patient’s decisions (e.g., declining NRT), reinforcing control and self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out concrete motivations, past strategies, and specific plans, but could further probe personal values and chain change talk to strengthen internal motivation toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and co‑created the plan, yet at times offered expert-style lists that could be more consistently tied back to the patient's own language and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (e.g., cap of 4/day, if‑then plans) and used confidence scaling, but could amplify and follow up more to elicit richer, elaborated reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing for change and neutrally accepted hesitancy while shifting to doable options, though double-sided reflections could more explicitly surface and resolve ambivalence.
