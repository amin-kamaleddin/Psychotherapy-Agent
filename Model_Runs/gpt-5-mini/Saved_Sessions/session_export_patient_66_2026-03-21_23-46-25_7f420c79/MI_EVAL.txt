# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Kareem’s stance, avoided judgmental language, and framed suggestions as options rather than directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and summaries, but made fewer deeper affective reflections to name underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing workplace smoking and moved the conversation toward a concrete two-week test and specific behavior changes without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a genuine partner by soliciting Kareem’s input at every step, offering options, and co-constructing a realistic plan shaped largely by the patient’s ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by framing actions as patient-led experiments, affirming choice, and avoiding pressure to quit altogether.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations and practical strategies (protecting job, family, routine) but could have probed deeper intrinsic motives and used techniques to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently established a collaborative stance, asking which options felt doable and deferring to Kareem’s expertise in choosing specific steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk and specific plans (gum, cut one cigarette per shift) through evocative questions, but could have amplified and reinforced those change statements and probed confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reflected sustain talk without arguing, using double-sided reflections to redirect toward change, though could more actively reframe or selectively amplify brief pro-change statements.
