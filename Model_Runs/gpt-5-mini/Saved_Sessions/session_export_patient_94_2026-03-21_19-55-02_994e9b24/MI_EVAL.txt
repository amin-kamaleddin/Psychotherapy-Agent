# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental (e.g., explicit validation and honoring time constraints); some affirmations felt procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated competent empathic listening and brief reflections that matched the patient's hurried style, but reflections remained surface‑level without deeper emotional probing or complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and inhaler options, producing a concise, actionable script and targeted recommendations without drifting from the clinical target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a true partner—co‑wrote and repeatedly tailored the patient-facing script, asked wording preferences, and aligned recommendations to the patient's time constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly reinforced patient choice by offering options, honoring requests for brevity, and explicitly avoiding persuasion or lecturing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited factual information and provided practical tools, but did not systematically draw out the patient's own motivations, values, or use scaling to evoke change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—invited input and co‑constructed the script—but limited explicit elicitation of the patient's expertise about integrating solutions into her life or longer‑term planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some opportunities to support change talk (symptom questions, script creation) but missed chances to amplify desire/ability/reasons or to elicit commitment or confidence measures.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing and validated medication concerns while offering alternatives and neutral phrasing, though could have used double‑sided reflections or summaries to resolve ambivalence more actively.
