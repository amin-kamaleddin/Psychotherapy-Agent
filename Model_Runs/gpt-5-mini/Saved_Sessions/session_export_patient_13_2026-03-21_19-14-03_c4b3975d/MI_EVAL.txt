# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating the patient’s experience and acknowledging practical constraints. No blaming or shaming was present and affirmations were used effectively.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, checking safety and matching pacing to the patient's anxiety. Attunement was clear through mirrored language and invitations to go at the patient's pace.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on getting the patient evaluated for the abdominal change, with interventions tied to concrete next steps. Clarifying, resource-offering, and rehearsal all supported the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a joint effort with co-constructed rehearsals, choice of formats, and offers to stay while the patient acts. Language and pacing positioned clinician and patient as partners rather than an expert directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the patient's choice and control by offering options and alternatives without pressure, and accepting limits on what the clinician could do. Steps reinforced patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some fears and internal material but did not consistently draw out the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. More targeted prompts (scaling, exploring past actions or outcomes) could have strengthened intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asked permission, offered choices, and rehearsed together—but some expert-driven elements (providing lists and specific script wording) slightly shifted influence toward the clinician. Could foreground the patient's expertise more often.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created conditions that supported action with practical options and rehearsals but did not systematically elicit or amplify the patient's own reasons for change. Missed opportunities to ask about importance, confidence, or personal gains from getting checked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized avoidance and offered practical, low-effort options to reduce barriers. Could have used more explicit double-sided reflections to deepen resolution of ambivalence.
