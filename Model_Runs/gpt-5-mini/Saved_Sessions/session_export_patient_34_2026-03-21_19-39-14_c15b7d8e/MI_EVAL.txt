# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established unconditional positive regard from the first turn and consistently used nonjudgmental, respectful language, avoiding blame or shaming even when the patient minimized responsibility.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective listening and validated the patient's perspective (e.g., 'that sounds frustrating'), but reflections were somewhat cursory and did not probe deeper feelings underlying defensiveness or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on the conduct/write-up, offered two clear pathways (appeal vs harm reduction), and provided concrete next steps, templates, and momentum aligned with the patient's choice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited the patient's input at multiple decision points, iteratively drafted documents to match his voice, and checked back frequently rather than imposing a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, honoring his decision not to change social behavior now, and leaving final logistical decisions to him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motives and concrete commitments (agreeing to send the letter, contact advocate) with open questions, but did not sufficiently probe deeper values or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a power-sharing dynamic by asking which focus to pursue, co-creating the appeal text to his specifications, and treating the patient as an equal collaborator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made occasional efforts to elicit change-related language and leveraged actionable steps, but missed deliberate opportunities to amplify desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and offered harm-reduction options, but did not actively use techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or targeted summaries) to pivot repeated sustain talk toward curiosity or discrepancy.
