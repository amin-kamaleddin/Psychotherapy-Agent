# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently modeled unconditional positive regard with warm, nonjudgmental language, validation, and early safety checking. Praised choices and normalized doubts, supporting the client's experience throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the client's dilemma about connection and smoking. Missed some opportunities for deeper affective reflections about belonging and the emotional meaning of 'instant friends.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with a concrete proximal target (Thursday test) and repeated actionable steps. Maintained agenda and returned conversation to specific tactics and follow-up planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked co-operatively with the client, asking for input and integrating her choices into the plan. Offered supports only with permission and used mutual problem-solving rather than expert-driven directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, used noncoercive language, and framed the plan as a voluntary experiment to preserve agency. Framed lapses as information rather than failure to encourage continued self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation (readiness ruler, small-step commitment) but relied heavily on clinician-suggested strategies rather than drawing out the client's own values, reasons, or confidence. Could strengthen by asking more open, value-oriented questions and amplifying client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as a shared endeavor, inviting choices and co-constructing the plan (meet-up, tactics, rehearsal, check-in). Used inclusive language and asked permission before offering ideas, demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and built on change talk through a readiness ruler and a concrete, low-risk experiment that led to a commitment. Could have further amplified spontaneous client statements about desires, reasons, or confidence to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided arguing and rolled with sustain talk, reframing the plan as a test and reducing shame or defensiveness. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge social rewards while noting any client-expressed costs.
