# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shows unconditional positive regard—validates privacy and repeatedly honors the client’s choice to pause. No judgment or pressure is evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and reflective statements that acknowledge the client’s perspective. Reflections are sometimes brief or formulaic and could more often name underlying emotions to deepen connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on engagement and safety and repeatedly brings the conversation back to the client’s control. Occasional repetitiveness of offers and safety reminders is safe but limits movement toward a next collaborative step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint process by offering choices and soliciting preferences, fostering an equal-partner tone. Because the client is minimally communicative, much of the session is clinician-offered options rather than co-constructed planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy with explicit statements that the client controls what and when to share and by honoring requests not to be checked on. Avoids pressure or directive language throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some gentle attempts to draw out the client (open question about priorities, asking what would help comfort), but misses low-intensity opportunities to elicit values, reasons, or small expressions of motivation. Given the client’s stance, the clinician appropriately restrains but could use brief, nonintrusive evocation probes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance by inviting boundaries and offering options, communicating respect for the client’s perspective. Interaction remains somewhat clinician-led in tone due to limited client input, which constrains full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicitation of change talk is minimal—conversation shifts to offering options and safety rather than exploring desires, reasons, or hopes for change. The clinician could try brief curiosity questions or scaling to invite small expressions of motivation while respecting autonomy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing and validates the client’s resistance, backing off when the client declines to share which reduces sustain talk. Could further use double-sided reflections to acknowledge both reasons for privacy and potential openness later.
