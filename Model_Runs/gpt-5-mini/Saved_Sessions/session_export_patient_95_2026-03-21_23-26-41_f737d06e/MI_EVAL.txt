# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth through repeated, genuine validations that created a safe space for disclosure of suicidal thinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality reflective listening captured both content and emotional tone, balancing compassionate understanding with acknowledgement of risk in a timely and accurate way.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on safety and a specific behavioral experiment, with repeated safety checks and logical progression from validation to evocation to concrete, monitored steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative, involving shared decision-making, negotiated tasks (drafting/pasting the text, staying for check-in), and practical support aligned with the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice and control by offering options, asking permission before interventions, and affirming the patient's ability to decide pacing and limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations with evocative questions and reflections, using the tiny emergent reasons for change to co-create a doable experiment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as the expert on her experience and repeatedly invited her preferences, resulting in a jointly developed, patient-centered plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified limited change talk with concrete prompts, affirmed hope, and helped convert that language into a specific, actionable step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation using double-sided reflections and validation, then gently redirected toward a small, manageable test rather than arguing or dismissing distress.
