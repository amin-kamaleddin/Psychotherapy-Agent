# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the patient’s perspective; acceptance was clear and sustained throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and helped the patient feel heard, though empathy could deepen by naming underlying emotions (e.g., FOMO, loneliness) and using more complex reflections at times.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing phone/social media impact on sleep/study and guided the session logically toward a concrete, time-bound experiment and coping plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with the patient co-constructing the plan (choices, wording, supports), showing genuine two-way decision-making and iterative input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered options, respected skepticism, and framed the trial as optional 'useful data,' reinforcing the patient’s sense of choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited specific, actionable change language and confidence scaling, but could further probe personal values and longer-term reasons to strengthen motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned the patient as the expert on what would work, invited choices, and co-created the experiment step-by-step, fostering genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced change-oriented language and concrete experiments, though more frequent amplification and summarizing of the patient’s change talk would help build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argumentation and solutions respected both sides, but strategic double-sided reflections or selective amplification of change language could more effectively shift momentum away from sustain talk.
