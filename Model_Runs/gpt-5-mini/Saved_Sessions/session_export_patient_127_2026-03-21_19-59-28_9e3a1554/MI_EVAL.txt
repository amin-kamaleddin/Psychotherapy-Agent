# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with routine safety checks and frequent affirmations, validating progress without shaming. The clinician accepted ambivalence and acknowledged the client's agency and constraints.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, noting fear, frustration, and valued small pleasures. Missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections about shame, identity, and self‑labels that would have strengthened empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on relapse prevention and immediate urge management while monitoring safety. When other issues arose (jobs/identity) they were deferred appropriately to preserve the session target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone and structure were collaborative with negotiated decisions and invitations for input. However, the clinician frequently supplied ready‑made scripts and routines instead of eliciting the client's own wording, making it somewhat clinician‑driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the client's sense of choice by offering options, asking which felt most useful, and respecting declines (e.g., no role‑play). Language avoided pressure and reinforced that actions were the client's to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change‑oriented planning and selected goals but relied heavily on clinician‑provided strategies and phrasing. Could have drawn out more internal motivations, values, past successes, and stronger client‑generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by inviting choices and co‑creating a relapse plan with the client. At times the clinician led content creation (timers, exact scripts) instead of prompting the client to generate solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced and amplified change talk (e.g., '22 days', small pleasures, Fiona's ultimatum) and invited choices about plans. Did not consistently deepen or convert emerging change language into stronger desire/ability/reason/need or commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and shifted to practical planning rather than arguing, which partially reduced sustain talk. Rarely used MI‑specific techniques (double‑sided reflections, strategic summaries) and at times moved quickly to giving solutions instead of exploring ambivalence.
