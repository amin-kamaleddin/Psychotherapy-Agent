# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—normalizes tension, honors requests (no paperwork, no hovering), and avoids judgment. Warm, validating stance supports the client’s boundaries and pace.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and accurate summaries that capture ambivalence and practical preferences. Could deepen by naming underlying emotions (fatigue, frustration) and using more complex reflections to surface feeling-level experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, focused direction on increasing meaningful engagement via a one-time tolerable trial and repeatedly brings conversation back to concrete task/logistics. No drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently co-constructs the plan with the client, asks for preferences at each step, and makes offers contingent on acceptance. Interaction functions as a partnership rather than an expert-driven approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering options, honoring limits (leave anytime), and avoiding mandates. Reinforces voluntary participation and client control over conditions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits client-generated solutions and concrete commitments (task, time, non-negotiables) but leans toward logistics over eliciting internal motives. Could amplify desire/reasons/need and confidence language to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently invites the client to co-design the plan, defers to his choices, and keeps decision-making shared. This sustained power-sharing fosters client ownership of the trial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited actionable change talk (commitment to one 30-minute session) and reinforced small steps. Missed opportunities to explore deeper reasons/values for change and to use scaling or summative questions to strengthen commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully avoided escalating sustain talk by offering limited trials and removing triggers, which reduced resistance. Rarely used double-sided reflections or explicitly explored the disadvantages of continued disengagement to further weaken sustain talk.
