# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized Marcus's situation, and affirmed his priorities without judgment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate simple reflections that validated fatigue and attachment to the dog, but missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections to probe the intensity of Marcus's overwhelm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on asthma management with concrete, realistic next steps and balanced exploration with action planning tied to Marcus's priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine teammate by coaching, role-playing, and inviting Marcus to choose what to practice, with decisions led by Marcus's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by asking permission, offering options instead of directives, and explicitly honoring Marcus's refusal to consider giving up the dog.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited meaningful change ideas and used scaling questions, but could more systematically draw out personal values, discrepancy, and follow scaling answers to build commitment and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-level partnership by co-creating the script, asking permission, and positioning Marcus as the decision-maker throughout the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with scaling and action-oriented prompts, though further amplification of Marcus's own reasons and consolidation into stronger commitment language would help.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and accepted sustain talk about the dog and finances while offering practical, dog-friendly alternatives, but could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly resolve ambivalence.
