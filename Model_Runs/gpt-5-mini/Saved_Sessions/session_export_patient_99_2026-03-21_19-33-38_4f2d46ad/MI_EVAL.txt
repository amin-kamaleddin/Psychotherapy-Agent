# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and warm, using validating language and normalizing statements while honoring Matteo’s limits. Unconditional positive regard was clear throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were several validating reflections and normalizing comments, but empathic responses were often brief and more feeling-focused exploration was limited. The clinician moved to planning relatively quickly instead of deepening understanding of Matteo’s internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and its consequences, with a specific, measurable experiment and a set timeframe. Conversation did not drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited input, co-constructed the checklist and text, and used inclusive language about tweaking the plan, supporting teamwork. However, the clinician often supplied most strategies rather than first eliciting Matteo’s prior ideas or solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and control were emphasized repeatedly (offering options, asking permission, framing suggestions as optional), and Matteo’s ownership of the plan was reinforced. Coercive language was avoided and decisions were left to him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good use of the readiness ruler and invitations to plan elicited commitment language and a concrete experiment. The clinician could further deepen evocation by asking more about Matteo’s personal reasons, values, and what would increase his readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally took a collaborative stance, offering options and co-creating a plan with Matteo. Partnership was reduced somewhat by quickly offering many concrete tips rather than eliciting Matteo’s own solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited a readiness rating and a specific experiment, and reinforced the plan positively. To strengthen change talk further, brief client change cues could be amplified with reflections and questions that draw out reasons and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and redirected minimizations toward practical experiments and supports, using strategies to counter social sustain forces. They missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and deeper exploration of reasons for maintaining the behavior.
