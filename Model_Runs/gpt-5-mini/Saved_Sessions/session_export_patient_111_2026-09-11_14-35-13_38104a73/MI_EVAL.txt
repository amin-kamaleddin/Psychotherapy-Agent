# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, affirming the patient’s priorities without judgment or confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate, timely reflections and summaries that match the patient's content and affect, which keeps him engaged and lowers defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, continuous focus on reducing the after‑dinner smoking and sequentially advances a concrete, behavior‑specific plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as a true partner—solicits and uses the patient's input at each step, checks agreement, and adjusts to his boundaries and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces choice and control by framing the change as a self‑owned test and offering options rather than directives, including asking for exact wording and choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits concrete, immediate motivation for a week‑long experiment but relies mainly on external reasons (wife) and could probe deeper personal values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative and client‑centered—co‑constructs the plan with the patient and treats him as an equal expert on what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies action‑focused change talk about specific steps, though it misses chances to deepen intrinsic reasons and boost self‑efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids escalating sustain talk and rolls with resistance well, but could more often use double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to gently pivot toward change.
