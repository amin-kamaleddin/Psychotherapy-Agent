# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized slips, and used validating language without blaming or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and some reflections showed understanding of stress and triggers, but the clinician moved quickly to information rather than exploring emotional nuance and ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking with repeated steering toward concrete, actionable steps (reduce-to-3–4/day plan, NRT selection, start date).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative with invitations for patient input and choice, though the clinician often delivered procedural recommendations in a one-way manner instead of co-creating implementation strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician supported patient choice by offering multiple options, framing cutting down as legitimate, and checking the patient's preferences and readiness for a start date.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some questions about past attempts and readiness, but the clinician tended to offer solutions and advice rather than eliciting the patient's internal motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A generally collaborative stance was fostered with choices and invitations to decide, yet at times the clinician shifted into an expert mode giving detailed instructions before fully co-constructing the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change language and the patient expressed willingness to try reducing, but change talk was not sufficiently amplified, followed up, or summarized to deepen desire and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and the clinician avoided reinforcing it, using normalizing and reframing language, though there were missed opportunities for strategic double-sided reflections and summaries.
