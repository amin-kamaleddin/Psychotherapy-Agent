# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and positive regard, validating Samuel's efforts and normalizing help-seeking. Avoided blame and repeatedly affirmed progress and attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some empathic acknowledgements of progress and work-related difficulties but used few reflective or complex reflections. Missed opportunities to probe feelings and ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking reduction with concrete steps, dosing, troubleshooting, and a one-week plan. Regularly steered the conversation back to practical next actions tied to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort and co-created a work-friendly plan and checklist with Samuel. However, the clinician still provided many directive recommendations rather than eliciting Samuel's own strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, asked permission, and affirmed Samuel's control over decisions. Avoided pressuring language and supported trying options for a week to preserve patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client-driven content (plan acceptance, journal review) but rarely used evocative open questions to draw out intrinsic motivation, values, or confidence. Interaction relied more on information-giving than on eliciting Samuel's reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sought Samuel's preferences and invited input on products and scheduling, conveying collaboration. The session remained somewhat clinician-led with prescriptive technical guidance rather than fully equal co-discovery.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced and affirmed existing change talk and offered a concrete starter plan, but seldom asked open questions to elicit or amplify reasons, strengths, or commitment. Change talk work was present but reactive rather than intentionally generative.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward problem-solving and NRT options. Did not explicitly explore or normalize ambivalence using double-sided or reflective techniques to soften sustain talk more effectively.
