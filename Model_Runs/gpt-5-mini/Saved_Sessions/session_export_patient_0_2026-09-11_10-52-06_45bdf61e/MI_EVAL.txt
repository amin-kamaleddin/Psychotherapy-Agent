# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm—normalizes social ties and honors the patient's preference for harm‑reduction; supportive tone and offers (e.g., drafting/sending a casual text) with no blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and reflections of ambivalence and realism, but focuses more on pragmatic steps than on deeper affect (shame, fear); a more affective reflection would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on reducing infection risk with each question and reflection tied to concrete behavior changes, narrowing effectively to a specific, feasible action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative problem‑solving—builds on the patient's suggestions, solicits his expertise about what will work, and repeatedly checks preferences, showing equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by emphasizing choice, avoiding pressure, offering a menu of supports, and acting only after explicit patient preference, preserving the patient's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete motivations and change language through open questions, but could deepen evocation by exploring personal reasons, importance/confidence, or commitment strength.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates robust partnership—invites patient decision‑making, offers options rather than directives, asks permission, and co‑creates the plan based on the patient's priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces specific change talk that yields concrete steps ('I’ll try that this week'), but could better amplify, summarize, and link change talk to personal reasons to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to feasible harm‑reduction options, limiting escalation; adding double‑sided reflections or explicit acknowledgments of social benefits could further resolve ambivalence.
