# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with choices offered, but few explicit affirmations or normalizing statements to convey deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens empathically and invites the patient's perspective, but reflective listening is inconsistent and doubts or emotional content are not routinely explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on homework management with concrete, actionable steps aligned to the patient's request.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements and offers to help, but the clinician largely assumes an expert role by providing ready-made plans rather than co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides meaningful choices and asks permission before offering help, though at times delivers detailed instructions without fully checking the patient's preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Begins with an open invitation but quickly shifts to solution-giving, with minimal elicitation of the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership is present through questions and offers, but the interaction is clinician-driven and patient input is often subordinated to the clinician's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports small commitments (e.g., trying a Pomodoro) but does not systematically evoke or amplify the patient's own reasons, desire, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does not validate or explore the patient's skepticism and misses opportunities (e.g., double-sided reflections) to acknowledge and redirect sustain talk.
