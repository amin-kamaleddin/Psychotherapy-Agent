# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established and maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, validated ambivalence, and repeatedly affirmed Elise’s agency and choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking accurately captured Elise’s ambivalence and the social meaning of smoking, though the clinician could have probed deeper into the emotional texture of belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking and its social role and progressed logically from exploration to concrete experiments, rehearsal, and a specific next-step plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist worked as an equal partner—brainstorming, eliciting preferences, and bringing in tools only after permission—so plans were co‑created rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was emphasized from the opening line through offering options and framing suggestions as optional experiments, consistently avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Elise’s motives, past workable strategies, and confidence, but could have probed deeper into intrinsic reasons for change and amplified nascent change statements toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session moves (offering options, asking which experiment felt doable, asking permission to role‑play) clearly shared power and treated Elise as the expert on what would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited specific change-oriented language and concrete experiments and asked for confidence ratings, but could have more actively amplified and reinforced Elise’s willingness to try.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist rolled with sustain talk, reflected the pull of connection, and pivoted to low-risk trials, though more strategic double-sided or selectively biasing reflections could further reduce sustain talk.
