# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental support, praising disclosure and validating worries while avoiding shaming or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows a solid empathic stance with accurate understanding and validation, but reflections were mostly informational and missed deeper emotional ambivalence and fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on preventing alcohol escalation tied to soccer/scholarship goals and repeatedly returned to actionable prevention strategies and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative with role-play and feedback, but the clinician occasionally shifted into an instructor role by offering prescriptive delivery tips instead of eliciting the patient's own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and ownership by offering options, asking permission, respecting decisions, and framing suggestions as offers rather than commands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation by linking avoidance to valued goals and prompting planning, but often supplied scripts and solutions instead of eliciting the patient's own reasons, strengths, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and invited co-creation and practice, yet at times provided many prescriptive scripts rather than foregrounding the patient's expertise and wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively linked alcohol risks to salient goals and prompted concrete planning and practice that produced change talk, but could use more open questions to elicit and amplify the patient's own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempted to counter minimization but relied partly on corrective information and directive redirection to action instead of using double-sided reflections or eliciting ambivalence to reduce defensiveness.
