# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, using validating language and normalizing the patient's struggle to communicate unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking captured ambivalence and practical constraints, but the clinician missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional content and use more complex empathic reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed sharply focused on medication adherence with every question and reflection tied to concrete, observable behavior change and a measurable short-term test.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was clearly collaborative, inviting the patient to generate solutions, choose options, and co-create the plan rather than imposing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reinforced the patient's freedom to choose, framed plans as experiments, and offered options and tweaks to support autonomy without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician drew out the patient's own reasons and strategies using scaling questions and elicitation of supports, allowing motivation to emerge from the patient rather than supplying it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician ceded expertise to the patient, checked preferences repeatedly, and worked as a teammate to co-develop practical reminders and backups.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and affirmed change talk with importance/confidence scales and doable steps, but could more often reflect and amplify change language into stronger, commitment-oriented statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and used double-sided reflections and redirections toward experiments, but missed some opportunities to explicitly explore the costs of sustain talk and strengthen discrepancy.
