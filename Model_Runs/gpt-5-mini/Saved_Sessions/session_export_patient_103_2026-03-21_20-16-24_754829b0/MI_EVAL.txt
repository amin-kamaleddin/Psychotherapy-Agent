# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validated Nabil’s frustration, normalizing his feelings without judgment. Maintained a warm, respectful stance throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and summaries that kept the client feeling heard and understood. Could deepen empathic connection by probing more into the emotional meaning of feeling “invisible” with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on addressing appointment delays and protecting work time, with each intervention tied to that goal. Session built toward concrete actions (scripts, rehearsals, call/reschedule options).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—co‑constructed scripts, invited role‑play, and adjusted to the client’s preferred tone, positioning the clinician as coach and partner. Frequent invitations for the client to choose what would help most reinforced mutual problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and offered multiple options, supporting Nabil’s sense of control without pressure. Encouraged the client to select steps that fit his priorities and confidence level.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited actionable intentions via open questions, confidence scaling, and rehearsals, drawing out specific plans. At times supplied scripts early rather than first eliciting the client’s own wording and motives, missing opportunities to amplify client‑generated reasons and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine power‑sharing by inviting the client to shape language and plans, and by honoring his expertise about what felt believable. The client’s adjustments to scripts show effective co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively invited and shaped change talk through options, scripts, rehearsal, and confidence ratings, producing clear intent to act. Could strengthen results by more frequently reflecting and amplifying the client’s own reasons for change and prior successes to boost desire and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and effectively redirected toward action with pragmatic options and rehearsals. Would be stronger with more double‑sided reflections that acknowledge skepticism while highlighting even small hopes or the costs of inaction.
