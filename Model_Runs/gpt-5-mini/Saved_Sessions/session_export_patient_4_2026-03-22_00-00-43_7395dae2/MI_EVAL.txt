# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, validates mixed feelings, and incorporates the patient's concerns into the plan rather than dismissing them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid empathic listening with accurate paraphrases of details and goals, but often uses content-focused reflections and misses opportunities to name deeper emotions like fear, mistrust, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on concrete target behaviors (symptom diary, one-paragraph summary, tests) and consistently steers the conversation toward actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is genuinely co-constructed: the clinician invites choices, checks feasibility, and offers help preparing or practicing the plan rather than directing it unilaterally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering multiple options, asking preferences, and avoiding pressure while validating the patient's right to pursue tests alongside immediate steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's own reasons for change and links plans to values (feeling useful, respected), but could probe deeper into intrinsic motives and sequence change talk toward stronger commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames the interaction as shared decision-making, invites patient preferences, and treats the patient as an expert on her life while collaborating on solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk (diary, short walks, summary for GP) and asks for a manageable next step, but could amplify specific gains and elicit firmer commitment or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and integrates the patient's desire for tests into a joint plan which reduces resistance, though it could use more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to pivot sustain talk toward change.
