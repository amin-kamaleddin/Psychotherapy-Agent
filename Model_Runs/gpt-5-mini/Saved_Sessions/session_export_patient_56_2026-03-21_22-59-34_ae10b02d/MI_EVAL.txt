# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes mixed feelings (e.g., “I accept that mix of pride and pressure”), validating Isolde and avoiding judgmental language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture content and affect accurately and show perspective-taking, though deeper, more complex emotional reflections (e.g., on exhaustion or vulnerability) could further deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on a specific target behavior (a short family check-in) with questions and planning tied directly to concrete next steps and outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly invites Isolde's input, co-constructs wording and timing, and uses inclusive language that positions Isolde as a joint decision-maker rather than a passive recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices and alternatives are offered (exact wording, softer version, trade), the plan is framed as a test, and the clinician affirms Isolde's ownership and ability to adjust the approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits intrinsic motivators and desired outcomes (family happiness, feeling supported) but could more consistently amplify client-generated reasons and use scaling or commitment probes to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing is evident through language like “Let’s shape it together” and repeated invitations for Isolde to choose wording, timing, and backup plans, centering her expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and reinforces practical change talk (the check-in, signs of success) and amplifies desire and willingness, though explicit summarizing of commitment and confidence scaling were missed opportunities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and the clinician avoided reinforcing the status quo by redirecting to small actions, but could have used double-sided reflections to acknowledge any lingering reluctance while steering toward change.
