# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and affirming, normalizing difficulty and creating a safe space. These behaviors convey unconditional positive regard and encourage honest disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent reflective listening and validation accurately capture the patient's perspective and social pressures. Could deepen emotional exploration and probe underlying values or feelings rather than focusing mainly on logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on reducing risky drinking and protecting academics, with each intervention tied to that behavioral goal. Iterative problem-solving stays on target throughout the exchange.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly a partnership: the clinician invites options, refines patient-generated ideas, and avoids imposing solutions. Decisions are co-created (e.g., drafting texts, selecting supports), reflecting full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and reinforcing what feels doable. This approach fosters ownership and reduces coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits internal reasons and anticipated benefits by asking what would make a difference and which steps feel doable. Could further deepen evocation by linking plans to broader values and eliciting stronger commitment (e.g., scaling questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly invites the patient to be the expert and co-constructs the plan, taking up and refining patient ideas. The clinician shares power and avoids an expert-imposed agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and strengthens change talk with open questions and concrete planning, and the patient offers specific change language. To improve, the clinician could more explicitly amplify and summarize desire, ability, reasons, and commitment language to solidify change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** When doubts appear, the clinician acknowledges them and redirects toward practical solutions using normalization and acceptance. Could more actively shift momentum away from sustain talk by using double-sided reflections or explicitly contrasting reasons for change.
