# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opens with an explicit nonjudgmental statement and maintains respectful, validating language throughout, with multiple normalizing statements and no blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, acknowledging emotions and barriers, but could deepen emotional nuance (e.g., explore self-criticism and the emotional cost of past attempts).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on short daily movement and related planning from start to finish, with concrete scheduling, tracking, and barrier strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as a genuine two-way partnership, soliciting the patient's input at each step and co-creating alarms, tracking, fallbacks, and review plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking what feels doable, requesting permission for check-ins, and inviting tweaks rather than imposing a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits the patient's ideas, confidence, and plans, but leans toward offering many concrete suggestions which slightly reduces opportunities for deeper client-generated motivation and values-based reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Displays strong power-sharing by repeatedly inviting choices (alarm wording, timing, tracking) and adapting language to the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies change talk using the confidence ruler and commitment questions, but could further probe values and reasons to strengthen sustained change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates and normalizes sustain talk and offers practical fallbacks that reduce its power, though greater use of double-sided reflections or exploring reasons for maintaining habits would help further soften it.
