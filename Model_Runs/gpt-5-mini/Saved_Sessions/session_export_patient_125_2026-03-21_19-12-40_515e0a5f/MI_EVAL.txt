# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear nonjudgmental stance and safety check, using respectful, validating language that normalized Tariq’s feelings and demonstrated unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately named Tariq’s frustration and family concern and the clinician offered stress‑reduction options, showing strong attunement and an empathic, supportive tone throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the concrete target behavior (obtaining a brief call), moving logically from validation to script creation, rehearsal, and practical next steps without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician and Tariq co‑created and adapted scripts, practiced together, and the clinician elicited Tariq’s preferences, demonstrating active shared problem‑solving and immediate feedback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were offered (script vs. grounding), Tariq’s choices guided the work, and advice was framed as suggestions, consistently preserving his sense of control and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited immediate motivation (the birthday call) and translated it into action language and rehearsal, but did not probe deeper reasons, meaning, or confidence/commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally adopted a collaborative stance and incorporated Tariq’s preferences, but at times supplied ready‑made scripts that shifted some control back to the clinician instead of full co‑construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician amplified Tariq’s expressed desire and converted it into specific action and rehearsal, though missed opportunities to deepen change talk by exploring additional reasons, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing blame and redirected toward problem‑solving, which reduced sustain talk, but could have used more explicit softening techniques to explore ambivalence and Tariq’s role.
