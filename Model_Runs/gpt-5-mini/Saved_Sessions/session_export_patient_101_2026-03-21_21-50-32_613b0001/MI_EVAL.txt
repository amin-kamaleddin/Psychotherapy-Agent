# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a warm, nonjudgmental tone (asked permission, validated concerns) with no shaming or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening (summarized ratings and family history), though could deepen emotional nuance and use more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use tied to reproductive risk and offered concrete, goal-aligned options and follow-up steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem-solving by co-constructing the plan, asking for preferences, and tailoring materials to the patient’s requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy by asking permission, offering multiple options, respecting readiness, and checking logistical preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient’s own reasons and preferences (family history, desire to avoid risks) but could strengthen evocation with deeper open questions and commitment elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong power-sharing by offering choices, asking which to prioritize, and integrating the patient’s input into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (patient chose LARC, noted links to goals) but could better amplify and summarize change statements and elicit concrete commitment steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk but did little to explicitly reframe it or use double-sided reflections, instead moving to practical solutions without linking sustain talk to change motives.
