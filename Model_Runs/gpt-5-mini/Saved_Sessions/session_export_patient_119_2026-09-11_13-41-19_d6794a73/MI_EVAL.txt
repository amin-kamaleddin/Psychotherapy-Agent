# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician conveys consistent unconditional positive regard and validating language, normalizing ambivalence without blame. Acceptance is evident in reflections and endorsements of the client’s pragmatic approach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking build rapport, with frequent paraphrases of ambivalence. Reflections are sometimes close paraphrases and occasionally repetitive rather than deepening emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and small habit changes throughout the session. Moves the conversation toward a concrete, time-bound trial and elicits a specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction with the clinician asking for input, offering options after eliciting preferences, and constructing a shared plan. Checks acceptability and invites contingency planning, reflecting joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes the client’s choice and control, avoids pressuring language, and accepts honest limits on commitment. Invitations to choose among options preserve autonomy and respect the client’s perspective.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the client’s own ideas and practical plans through brainstorming prompts. Tends to focus more on practical strategies than on eliciting deeper motivational content like values, confidence, or long-term reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co-constructed with repeated invitations for the client to choose and make decisions. The clinician frames themselves as a collaborator and respects the client’s knowledge of what’s doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and supports change talk with open questions and choice invitations, producing multiple change statements. Misses some opportunities to amplify and reflect change language to deepen commitment and explore importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently acknowledges sustain talk using double-sided reflections and redirects toward small, practical change instead of debating. Could further reduce sustain talk by exploring underlying reasons for minimization and emotional barriers.
