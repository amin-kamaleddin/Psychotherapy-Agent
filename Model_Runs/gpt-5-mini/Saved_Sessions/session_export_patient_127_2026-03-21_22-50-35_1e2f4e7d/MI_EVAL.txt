# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalization, using affirmations and validation while avoiding shaming; demonstrates high and stable acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and notice of emotional nuances, but reflections are sometimes repetitive and the clinician misses opportunities for deeper exploration of shame and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on staying crime- and drug-free and moves the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps (e.g., trades/licenses, one-week texting trial).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a team effort with joint problem-solving and plan development, though at times the clinician narrows options or supplies content which slightly reduces parity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms client choice and offers options while avoiding coercion, but gently steers toward a particular plan and could more explicitly invite rejection of the plan without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's own motivations, past successful strategies, and specific change language, culminating in a client-generated and actionable commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invites client input to shape the plan, though some prompts direct toward one solution rather than equally exploring alternatives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly elicits, reinforces, and amplifies change talk (e.g., 22 days, morning-text routine) and converts desire into a concrete, implementable one-week plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and redirects to strengths and solutions, but misses opportunities for double-sided reflections and for eliciting the client's own arguments against returning to crime.
