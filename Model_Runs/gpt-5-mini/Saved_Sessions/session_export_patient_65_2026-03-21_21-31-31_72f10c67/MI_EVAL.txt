# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, validating the client and avoiding labels or punitive language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate, practical reflections and perspective-taking that lowered resistance, but rarely explores deeper emotions or uses complex reflections to invite more openness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on completing community service hours and repeatedly translates responses into concrete next steps and plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in reciprocal problem-solving where the client shapes the plan and the clinician integrates his input into concrete outputs like messages, forms, and schedules.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options, checks preferences, and asks permission before acting, supporting the client's sense of choice without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical commitments and preferences but does not explore intrinsic reasons or values; misses opportunities to evoke internal motivation with open, evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong power-sharing and mutual decision-making, consistently inviting collaboration and respecting the client's expertise about his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits preparatory, logistics-focused change talk and small commitments, but does not probe for personal reasons, importance, or confidence to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to solutions, yet uses few explicit softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or normalizing ambivalence) to resolve deeper resistance.
