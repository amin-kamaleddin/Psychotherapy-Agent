# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, with normalization, affirmations, and repeated validation of the patient's priorities; no shaming or blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and strong reflective listening (including complex reflections) that tracked affect and sustained rapport and engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on tobacco use as it affected relationship conflict and moved the conversation toward specific, doable plans without drifting from the target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner throughout—inviting the patient's choices, offering options, role‑playing on request, and co‑developing pragmatic strategies together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering low‑commitment experiments, respecting limits, and framing suggestions as optional rather than coercive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited motivations and relational reasons (fear of losing his wife) but relied more on external pressures and practical fixes; could probe deeper into personal values and long‑term goals to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the patient as the decision‑maker, invited his ideas repeatedly, and deferred to his expertise when selecting and tailoring strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change talk via scaling and offers of experiments, but missed opportunities to amplify, summarize, and follow up to deepen desire, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed sustain talk with double‑sided reflections and low‑commitment options, but could more actively pivot sustain talk toward change by eliciting downsides and comparative reasoning.
