# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicated clear unconditional positive regard and normalized Jamal’s control over pace and choices throughout the session. There was no blaming or moralizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate paraphrases and reflective listening that captured Jamal’s dilemma and pragmatic concerns. Deeper exploration of affect and more complex reflections about feelings (e.g., fear of isolation or letting teammates down) would push this to a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on reducing alcohol exposure within the team context and moved toward concrete planning (roles, buddy, lines). It lacked a single, negotiated, time‑bound commitment and a clear follow‑up arrangement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician and Jamal co‑constructed language and plans (draft texts, role‑play) and the clinician consistently invited Jamal to choose and refine options. The interaction felt mutual rather than expert‑driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly supported autonomy by asking permission before offering ideas and framing suggestions as optional, repeatedly deferring to Jamal’s preferences. There was no pressuring or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited Jamal’s internal motivations and resources (performance concerns, desire for team connection, Malik as a buddy) and used scaling to assess confidence. More evocative questions linking values to change and stronger elicitation of commitment would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned Jamal as the expert, asked permission, and shared decision‑making in drafting and rehearsing language. Power was clearly shared with Jamal influencing the session content and plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited intentions and confidence (choice of role, naming Malik, scaling) and asked what would increase confidence. There were missed opportunities to amplify expressed change talk into stronger desire/need/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation and redirected toward workable steps that preserved belonging. Using more double‑sided reflections to explicitly normalize ambivalence would further diminish sustain talk.
