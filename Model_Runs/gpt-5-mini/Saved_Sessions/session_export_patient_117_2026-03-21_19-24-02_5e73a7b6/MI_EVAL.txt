# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, with affirmations of progress and normalization of setbacks and cravings that reinforced unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and validation of experience, but could deepen empathic work with more complex reflections of emotional nuance and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on smoking reduction with concrete steps, if/then planning, tracking, contingencies, and a scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative—preferences were solicited, recommendations adapted, and plans were mutually constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, asked permission before offering tools, and reinforced the patient’s agency in selecting strategies and committing to plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited change talk and concrete plans, but at times leaned toward directive advice and could further elicit the patient’s own reasons, strengths, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered client expertise and shared decision-making by inviting mapping of triggers, choice of tactics, and co-creating the implementation plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited, reinforced, and built on change talk by highlighting successes, asking for specific commitments, and arranging focused tracking and follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized challenges, but did not fully explore why smoking is appealing in social contexts or use double-sided reflections to reframe ambivalence.
