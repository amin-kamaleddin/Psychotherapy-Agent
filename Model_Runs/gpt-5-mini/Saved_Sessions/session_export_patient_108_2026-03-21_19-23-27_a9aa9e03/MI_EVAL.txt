# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Oliver’s mixed feelings and affirming his efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validates ambivalence, but could use more complex reflections to capture deeper emotional nuance and subtext.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with concrete, sequential steps (deadline, rehearsal, exit plan) that keep momentum toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively works as a partner through role-play, iterative tailoring, and negotiating wording, demonstrating a strong, mutual collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers options, frames recommendations as choices, and lets the patient decide wording and actions, supporting the patient’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation (open focus question, rehearsal) but clinician more often supplies language and solutions instead of drawing out values, past successes, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—asks permission to role-play and tailors scripts—but at times is expert-driven and relies on ready-made solutions rather than co-creating wording from Oliver’s ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforces and rehearses existing change talk and praises delivery, but relies on offering scripts rather than eliciting or amplifying Oliver’s own motives, desires, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges guilt and worry and avoids reinforcing sustain talk with practical strategies, but does not use double-sided reflections or deeply explore the compassionate motives behind reluctance.
