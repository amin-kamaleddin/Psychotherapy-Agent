# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, used validating affirmations, and respected the patient's preferences (e.g., privacy for the tracker) without shaming or directive language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were strong and captured ambivalence and triggers, but the clinician missed opportunities to probe deeper into the emotional context (e.g., links between drinking and low mood).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on reducing alcohol use throughout, moving from exploration to a concrete, testable plan with specific strategies and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative — the clinician co-created the plan, repeatedly solicited the patient's preferences, and adapted scripts and supports to her wording and needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasized choice, offered options rather than directives, and respected the patient's decisions (e.g., private tracker, choice of alcohol‑free night), reinforcing patient ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited the patient's own motivations and concrete solutions, but could have probed values and longer‑term meanings of change to deepen motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician modeled partnership throughout by asking for input, offering choices, and integrating the patient's suggestions into the plan, demonstrating genuine power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was well elicited and reinforced with affirmations and planning, though the clinician could have more systematically reflected and amplified key change statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation and worked with a realistic target to reduce sustain talk, but could have used more double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing to more actively resolve ambivalence.
