# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client's experience, using warm, strengths-focused language and repeated affirmations of small steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and captures both content and feeling frequently, though reflections could be deeper and more evocative (e.g., explore the meaning of 'gross' or fatigue tied to family role).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on starting to exercise with stepwise problem solving and concrete action planning that ties every turn back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed—clinician invites input at each step, follows the client's lead, and lets client choices shape the plan throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by offering choices, asking which option feels most doable, and avoiding pressure while affirming the client's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from the client using questions that elicit client-generated reasons, plans, and values, and sustains intrinsic motivation with concrete steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the client as an active collaborator by inviting choices and tiny steps, though occasionally offering multiple clinician-generated options which slightly shifts decision burden back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and shapes change talk with doable options and tiny steps, producing repeated change statements, but could more explicitly amplify and summarize DARN-C language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk by validating barriers and offering manageable alternatives, but could more often use double-sided reflections and reframes to further pivot toward change.
