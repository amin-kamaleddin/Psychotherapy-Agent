# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established clear unconditional positive regard from the start and used supportive, nonjudgmental language throughout, normalizing uncertainty. Avoided labeling or blaming despite the patient's minimizing tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, acknowledging uncertainty and past successes. Reflections remained mostly at the content level and missed opportunities for deeper, double-sided or emotional reflections to explore ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use as it relates to blood pressure and moved efficiently from exploration to specific, measurable goals. Used scaling and planning to keep the session goal-directed with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem-solving, repeatedly solicited the patient's preferences, and adjusted suggestions based on patient feedback (e.g., dropping tracking). Role‑played and co-created language for the spouse, demonstrating shared work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed choice and asked permission before offering information, provided multiple options rather than directives, and reinforced that slips are information not failure. These moves supported patient ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's own motivations and resources using evocative questions, scaling, and client-selected strategies, building on prior successes like quitting smoking. The plan was largely client-generated, reinforcing internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly invited the patient to lead and co-constructed a two-week plan together, asking permission and offering choices throughout. Demonstrated genuine power-sharing and adaptation to the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk through open questions, importance/confidence scaling, offering doable options, and moving to a client-selected plan. Reinforcements of willingness helped deepen and solidify the patient's commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not amplify minimizations, using neutral reflections and permission-seeking to prevent confrontation. Could have more consistently used double-sided reflections or selective amplification of even small change language to further tip the balance.
