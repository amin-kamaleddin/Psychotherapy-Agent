# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used warm, nonjudgmental language, validated Mateo's feelings, and avoided blame or minimization, demonstrating clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and statements accurately captured Mateo's experience (slow progress, pervasive pain) and normalized his emotions, showing timely and sustained perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior (a short after-breakfast walk) with concrete monitoring rules, stop thresholds, and a scheduled mid-week check-in to maintain momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as joint problem-solving with repeated invitations for input, co-constructed monitoring steps, and clinician deferral to Mateo's choices throughout the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly reinforced choice and control (asking permission, offering options, emphasizing that trying is optional), avoiding coercive language and supporting self-efficacy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some commitment language was elicited (agreeing to try the walk), but the clinician relied heavily on practical advice and missed opportunities to explore Mateo's deeper reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician involved Mateo in decision-making and invited preferences, but delivered many concrete recommendations in an expert register, so power-sharing could be deeper with more open elicitation of his ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited specific, actionable change talk and a concrete plan (walk, tracking), but could have amplified motivation by probing importance, confidence, and personal meaning more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged and normalized skepticism and avoided reinforcing sustain talk, though they relied more on practical redirection than on double-sided reflections to explicitly hold ambivalence.
