# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the patient's experience, offering support and resources. The tone became fairly clinical and solution-focused quickly, with fewer individualized affirmations or explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of basic reflective listening and attunement (e.g., 'That makes sense'), showing understanding of concerns. Reflections are brief and quickly followed by solutions, with limited deeper or complex emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing excessive phone/social media use with concrete, organized strategies and a specific 1-week trial. Questions and guidance were goal-directed and actionable throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-created messages and plans, invited preferences, and tailored wording to the patient's voice. The interaction was somewhat clinician-led in content, but the clinician repeatedly incorporated patient choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple options were offered and the patient was asked which felt doable, preserving choice with a time-limited trial. At times recommendations were framed prescriptively, which reduced the strength of pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited small commitments (choice of strategies, trial) but rarely drew out the patient's internal motivations, values, or confidence. The approach relied more on supplying solutions than on evoking the patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements like offering options and tailoring templates, showing some shared decision-making. However, the clinician often assumed an expert role and led most of the agenda with prescriptive strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician created opportunities for change talk (asking which ideas felt doable, proposing a trial) and elicited commitments. They did not consistently elicit or amplify the patient's own reasons for change or use evocative questions to strengthen desire, need, or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and reframed with practical compromises that respected social benefits. Responses were largely solution-focused rather than using empathic or double-sided reflections to explore reasons for maintaining the behavior.
