# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed respectful, nonjudgmental regard, validated Isolde's priorities, invited input, and honored her preferences throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic moves with reflections and normalization, but empathy could be deeper by reflecting emotional nuance (e.g., perfectionism-related worry) and using more complex affective reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on aligning behavior with the value of family happiness and moved the conversation toward a concrete, actionable plan with specific steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a joint enterprise: the clinician solicited wording preferences, offered phrasings, invited tweaks, and co-constructed decisions like message text and scheduling.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and ownership by asking permission before advising, offering alternatives, and honoring the client's decision when she declined practice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from Isolde's core value and used her language in planning, but could further elicit personal reasons, confidence, and amplify change statements to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative stance with repeated permission-seeking, co-creation of the family message, and centering the client's expertise and voice in decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and shaped change language with concrete action prompts and scaling, but could more consistently reflect and amplify spontaneous change talk and elicit firmer commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not dwell on barriers and used pragmatic solutions and normalizing language to reduce sustain talk, though double-sided reflections could more directly reframe remaining barriers.
