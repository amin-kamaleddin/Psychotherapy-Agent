# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—normalizes and validates the patient’s constraints and avoids blame, creating a respectful nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate empathic listening with timely content and affect reflections that deepen understanding of the patient’s guarded, overwhelmed tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on diabetes self-management and repeatedly steers toward one concrete, time-limited micro-step (the 10‑minute conversation).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructs the plan with balanced input—offers options, asks preferences, and invites role-play rather than delivering expert-only advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering options, using scaling, framing steps as trials, and explicitly respecting the patient’s caveat of 'not promising.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits reasons, barriers, and supports with open questions and scaling, but misses opportunities to probe for deeper personally meaningful motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly conveys shared expertise and power‑sharing—frequently solicits the patient’s input and tailors suggestions to his preferences and voice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and scaffolds change talk through scaling and small-step planning, but could better amplify and reflect tentative commitments to build stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids arguing and normalizes reluctance while offering micro-steps, though could use more double-sided reflections and summaries to resolve ambivalence.
