# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician shows strong unconditional positive regard with a warm opening, validation of ambivalence, normalizing slips, and respectful treatment of Elise's social concerns. Tone is consistently nonjudgmental and supportive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening that captures ambivalence and pressure as 'hard.' There were missed opportunities to probe deeper emotions (e.g., the loss of social role or the reluctance in her tone).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on smoking and social triggers with actionable, targeted strategies (tracking, realistic cuts, NRT, rehearsed lines). The clinician repeatedly orients interventions to the target behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative: the clinician invites Elise to shape the plan, role-plays with her, and adopts her wording, reflecting equal partnership. Elise's preferences meaningfully influence the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently preserves Elise's choice by asking permission before advice, offering multiple options, and normalizing slips. Language and approach maintain the client's agency throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to draw out motivation (readiness ruler, past attempts) produced tentative change talk, but the clinician leaned toward providing strategies rather than eliciting Elise's personal reasons and values. Key evocation questions (e.g., 'Why a 5 and not a 2?') were missing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician fosters client expertise and shared decision-making, asking permission and co-constructing the plan with Elise. Power is clearly shared and Elise's input is respected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited pragmatic change language (readiness, small steps, rehearsals) but did not sufficiently probe or amplify Elise's personal reasons, values, or benefits for change. Opportunities to deepen change talk were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and reframed rituals to preserve social needs, rolling with sustain talk rather than confronting it. However, there were missed chances to use double-sided reflections to pivot more strongly toward change.
