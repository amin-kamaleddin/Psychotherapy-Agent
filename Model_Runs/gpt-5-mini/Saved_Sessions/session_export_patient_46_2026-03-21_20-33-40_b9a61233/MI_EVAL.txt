# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental supportive tone throughout, consistently validating Ethan's ambivalence and respecting his choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that acknowledged both sides of ambivalence, but missed some opportunities to probe deeper emotions and social pressure more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and realistic goals, guiding the conversation toward specific, actionable strategies and concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a genuine joint enterprise—inviting Ethan to choose strategies, rehearsing role-plays, and requesting his input—demonstrating authentic teamwork rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Ethan's choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and honoring his limits, reinforcing ownership of any change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Ethan's own motivations and ideas and generated change language, but could more systematically amplify change talk and explore importance/confidence or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative, client-driven plan by offering options and asking preferences, though could more explicitly acknowledge Ethan as the expert and co-create follow-up or measurement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced multiple change statements with choice questions and small-step options, yet would benefit from more amplification, confidence queries, and invitations to commit.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and shifted to pragmatic harm-reduction strategies, but could improve with more double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to highlight discrepancy.
