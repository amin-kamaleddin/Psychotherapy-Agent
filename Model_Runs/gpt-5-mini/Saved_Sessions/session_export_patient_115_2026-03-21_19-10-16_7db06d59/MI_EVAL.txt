# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence while affirming patient steps; no evidence of shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking, reflective comments, and routine checks of understanding, but could deepen empathy with more complex emotional reflections about worries and fatigue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear clinical focus on asthma control, medication adherence, and harm reduction while eliciting key details and providing necessary safety guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is overtly collaborative with frequent invitations to practice, choice of next steps, and adjustments to patient requests, demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering multiple options, framing steps without pressure, and tailoring suggestions around barriers like cost and shifts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits symptom details and concrete behavior commitments but offers many solutions rather than systematically drawing out intrinsic reasons, values, confidence, or commitment to harder changes like quitting smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance with permission-seeking and joint practice, though at times shifts into more directive medical instruction rather than co-creating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites goals and affirms progress and concrete steps, but seldom uses evocative open questions or summaries to systematically elicit and amplify the patient's reasons, desire, or commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk and avoids confrontation, steering toward feasible harm-reduction strategies, though could more often use double-sided reflections to highlight discrepancy and pivot to change talk.
