# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used nonjudgmental, normalizing language and affirmed Hana's strengths (routine, planning), avoiding blame and validating her choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening that captured ambivalence and practical concerns, though reflections were sometimes repetitive and occasionally shifted into directive language rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with concrete, measurable first steps and questions about how Hana would notice success, keeping the session goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—elicited Hana's ideas and co-created a plan—yet occasional recommendation-style phrasing and 'I believe' statements made the interaction slightly more clinician-led than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options, respected Hana's readiness, and avoided pressuring language, clearly supporting client choice and control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations, past successes, and concrete change talk, but could further deepen intrinsic values and elicit confidence/commitment via scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a partnership by inviting brainstorming and choices, although occasional expert-like statements slightly reduced the sense of equal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and past coping strengths, and guided Hana to a specific small step, but did not fully probe for stronger commitment or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and avoided arguing for change, yet often pivoted quickly to solutions instead of using more double-sided reflections to explicitly soften sustain talk.
