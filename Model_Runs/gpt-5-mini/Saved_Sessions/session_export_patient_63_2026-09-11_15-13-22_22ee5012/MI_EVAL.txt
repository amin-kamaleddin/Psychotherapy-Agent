# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing ambivalence and validating honesty; avoided labeling or shaming throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured ambivalence and practical concerns; could deepen affective reflections (e.g., guilt, feeling left out) and use more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on shoplifting/recidivism and progressed to concrete implementation intentions; questions consistently tied back to specific behavioral plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like an equal, co‑constructed process with frequent solicitation and validation of the patient's ideas; clinician and patient refined plans together rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly fostered autonomy by offering options and asking the patient to choose what feels doable; avoided pressuring language and affirmed ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations, past successes, and concrete steps; could be strengthened by exploring values, asking importance/confidence scaling, and amplifying desire to avoid trouble.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative language invited preferences and framed the work as something to do together; consistently deferred to the patient to select and shape strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, producing concrete commitment language and plans; missed opportunities to amplify and consolidate change language (e.g., scaling, elaborating personal reasons).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk neutrally using double‑sided reflections that avoided reinforcing it; could more strategically redirect sustain talk by highlighting costs and using evocative questions to contrast consequences with values.
