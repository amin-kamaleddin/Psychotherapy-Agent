# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; the opening invitation and repeated acknowledgments preserved positive regard and framed recommendations consultatively.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and attends to safety worries with screening and concrete cautions, but uses few reflective or complex empathic reflections to mirror the patient's emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication safety (MAOI contraindication, washout, warning signs, ECG, tapering) and provides concrete next steps with minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functions as a knowledgeable partner by asking the patient’s agenda and offering scripts, but much content is delivered as expert recommendations rather than elicited from the patient’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice and agency by encouraging prescriber involvement, offering wording the patient can use, and presenting options without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Prioritizes information transfer and practical tools but rarely elicits the patient's own motivations, values, or readiness, with few evocative questions or scaling prompts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and shares power via scripts and suggested shared steps, yet the encounter remains clinician-driven and would benefit from more explicit elicitation of patient input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Provides thorough factual information and actionable scripts but seldom asks questions or uses reflections to draw out, amplify, or strengthen the patient's change talk or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain-oriented concerns and offers alternatives and caution about abrupt stopping, but uses few MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance) to soften sustain talk.
