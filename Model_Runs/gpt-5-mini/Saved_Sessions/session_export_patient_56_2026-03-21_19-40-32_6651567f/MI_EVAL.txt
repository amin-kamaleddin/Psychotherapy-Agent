# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validated the patient's state, and normalized struggles (e.g., interruptions), conveying clear positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening were present (safety check, noticing calm/reflective tone), but the clinician missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflections about the patient's commitment to family.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on the target behavior with concrete, time-bound steps (15-minute window, Do Not Disturb, timer) and a plan for follow-up, keeping momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as joint problem-solving, solicited the patient's routines and preferences, offered options rather than prescriptions, and adapted recommendations to the patient's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice by offering options, asking preferences, and leaving room for the patient to accept or modify suggestions, reinforcing the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively linked the patient's core value (family happiness) to behavior and elicited a concrete trial, but could have further elicited and amplified the patient's own solutions and confidence (e.g., confidence ruler, problem‑solving prompts).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-created with the patient, with the clinician checking preferences, offering tweakable wording, and validating the patient's choices, demonstrating strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited and helped concretize change talk into a specific experiment and plan, though more explicit reinforcement and summarization of the patient's change language would strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** When barriers appeared (work intrusion), the clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered pragmatic strategies, but did not use explicit double-sided reflections to more directly soften resistance.
