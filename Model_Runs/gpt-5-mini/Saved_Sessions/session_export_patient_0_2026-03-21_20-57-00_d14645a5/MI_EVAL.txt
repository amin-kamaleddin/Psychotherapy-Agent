# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, which reduced defensiveness and allowed open disclosure. Language was nonjudgmental throughout, meeting the highest standard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections of frustration and ambivalence. Could deepen empathy by exploring underlying shame and more complex emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on meth use and infection risk and moved the conversation toward concrete harm-reduction actions. Progressed logically from exploration to a specific plan and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a true collaboration, inviting input and co-creating the plan while rehearsing language together. Decision points were shared and client ideas shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's right to choose, asked permission before advising, and offered multiple options so the patient could select what felt doable. Tone and offers strongly supported autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited concrete intentions, confidence ratings, and plans but relied more on offering solutions than drawing out intrinsic motivations or values. Would benefit from more open, evocative questions and amplifying change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engaged the patient in choosing doable steps and wording, showing strong partnership behaviors. At times provided many prescriptive medical suggestions which slightly leaned toward an expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited specific change language and a plan with confidence scaling, but missed opportunities to amplify and deepen desire, reasons, and commitment. More reflective reinforcement and evocative questioning would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully accepted the patient's decision not to quit and pivoted to harm reduction, preventing sustain talk from escalating. Could enhance this further with strategic double-sided reflections to guide toward change talk.
