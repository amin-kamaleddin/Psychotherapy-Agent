# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and validating, nonjudgmental language, normalizing difficulty and appreciating efforts. Could further individualize empathic statements to explicitly normalize specific struggles (e.g., parenting guilt).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries that captured content and some affect, demonstrating solid empathy. Reflections often remained at the content level and could be deepened with more emotional labeling and complex reflections to name underlying feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior (reducing reactivity to be more present) and consistently linked questions and reflections to a concrete plan. The clinician kept clear movement from values to triggers to a specific small experiment without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as joint problem‑solving with co‑construction of if–then plans, soliciting the patient’s ideas and involving her husband as a support. Language and choices maintained an equal partnership rather than an expert lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly supported choice throughout, offered options, asked permission, and respected the patient’s preferences (e.g., declining role‑play). Suggestions were framed as invitations, reinforcing the patient’s ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently drew out the patient’s own reasons, resources, and concrete ideas using evocative questions and scaling. The concrete plan, cues, and measures of success all originated from the patient’s input.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A collaborative stance was maintained with repeated invitations to choose and co‑create the plan, keeping expertise with the patient. The clinician offered to help brainstorm and craft language while leaving final choices to Isolde.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful elicitation and reinforcement of change talk through open questions, scaling, and helping the patient articulate a specific, doable step. Clinician amplified commitments and encouraged concrete planning that strengthened the patient’s change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and was handled appropriately with normalization and compassionate reframing to redirect toward change. Could be strengthened by using more explicit double‑sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while guiding toward change.
