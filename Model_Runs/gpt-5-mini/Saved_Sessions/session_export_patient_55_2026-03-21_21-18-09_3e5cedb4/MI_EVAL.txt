# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit nonjudgmental framing and consistent respectful language; validated the client’s experience and avoided blame or moralizing throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and perspective-taking helped the client feel heard, with empathic labels; could deepen by probing emotional nuance (e.g., what 'unwind' or hiding from parents means) and using more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related risk and safety planning from assessment through a concrete plan, while remaining client-centered and progressive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like collaborative problem-solving—clinician invited input at every step, offered options and tools, and incorporated the client’s choices into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported client choice by offering multiple options, asking which felt doable, respecting the wish not to quit, and asking permission before offering drafts or extras.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew out safety-oriented motivations and implementation details using the client’s language, but could strengthen evocation by exploring values (IB/band) more deeply and eliciting stronger commitment statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the client as the expert on her life, co-constructed decisions (e.g., naming Maya, text wording) and tailored support to her preferences, demonstrating active power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change-focused language around safety and concrete plans (who, what, when, confidence), but could amplify links between safety behaviors and valued goals and elicit stronger desire/ability/reason language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Did not confront the client’s sustain talk about not wanting to quit; acknowledged both sides and strategically shifted to harm-reduction and safety planning without provoking resistance.
