# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, validating Elena's feelings and avoiding blame or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses reflective listening and perspective-taking well and acknowledges practical barriers, but could deepen emotional reflections (e.g., explore shame or the strength of her anxiety).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on contraception and immediate, practical steps (condom trial, scripts, safety plan) and moves to an actionable plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs practical plans and asks about preferences, though frequent provision of ready-made solutions shifts some idea-generation back to the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes choice and control, offers options and low-pressure framing, and leaves the final decision to Elena.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation and a doable step but relies heavily on information and scripts instead of systematically drawing out Elena's values, importance, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative relationship by asking what feels doable and co-creating scripts and plans, though the clinician sometimes leads the agenda with detailed medical options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits occasional change talk and a trial step but does not consistently use open questions, scaling, or summaries to deepen and amplify Elena's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and normalizes ambivalence, but uses few specific MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to strategically soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
