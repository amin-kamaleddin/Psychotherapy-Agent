# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth through validating statements and normalization, with no critical or minimizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., summarizing juggling of responsibilities), but several reflections stayed at a pragmatic level rather than exploring deeper emotions like stress or overwhelm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused target throughout—planning a specific short timed study block—and kept questions and follow-up tightly linked to that behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a joint problem-solving effort with the client choosing specifics (time, cue, reward), clinician soliciting ideas and offering support only with permission, and co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported client choice by asking permission, offering options, and leaving control with the client rather than pressuring or prescribing actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician drew motivation from the client with evocative questions, elicited a concrete commitment (15 minutes at 7:30), and identified a personal booster (Maya) to strengthen follow-through.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and client-centered, with repeated offers of choice and power-sharing in planning and follow-up decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (asked what change would look like and confidence), but could have amplified commitment language and probed more for intrinsic reasons and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and contained sustain talk (skepticism) and normalized lapses, but could have used more strategic double-sided reflections or reframing to more fully reorient sustain language.
