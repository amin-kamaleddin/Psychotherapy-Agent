# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—normalizes ambivalence and validates the patient’s priorities without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displays good empathic listening and accurate reflections of the patient’s situation and priorities, but could deepen exploration of emotions and use more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation with concrete decisions (medication choice, dosing schedule, one‑week test) and advances to a logical, testable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine co‑construction—asks for the patient’s input, integrates their responses, and frames decisions as joint problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers options and defers to the patient’s preferences, reinforcing patient ownership of choices and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation and priorities (e.g., sleep concerns, willingness to test a plan) but could probe more for intrinsic values and confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—repeatedly invites the patient’s thoughts and choices and treats the patient as an equal partner in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with open questions and a one‑week trial, though more reflective amplification and prompts for commitment/confidence would strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and redirects to practical strategies without arguing, but could use more double‑sided reflections and strategic softening techniques.
