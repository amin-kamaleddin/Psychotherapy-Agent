# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing Marcus's constraints and choices. No shaming or pressure was used and concerns about the dog and finances were respected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases of Marcus's situation. Reflections were more summary than deep affective reflections and could better capture his overwhelm and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused goal (meds check) and repeatedly steered toward concrete, actionable steps. Conversation moves consistently advanced planning and avoided drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created the plan, offered to draft the script, rehearse, and set reminders, showing mutual problem-solving. Marcus's input was solicited at each step and plans were adjusted to his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician emphasized Marcus's choices and offered options rather than directives, checking what felt doable for him. His reluctance about the dog was respected and suggestions minimized imposition.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited Marcus's personal motives for change (breathing, sleep, showing his wife), using them to support planning. Could have probed more for commitment and confidence language (scaling, past successes).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treated Marcus as an equal partner, inviting him to choose approach and co-create wording. Language and offers demonstrated genuine power-sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk and invited concrete planning which produced explicit change language from Marcus. Missed opportunities to more deeply explore confidence and past follow-through to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by offering acceptable alternatives and acknowledging trade-offs, which prevented escalation. Could have used more MI-specific moves (double-sided reflections, linking sustain talk to values) to further soften ambivalence.
