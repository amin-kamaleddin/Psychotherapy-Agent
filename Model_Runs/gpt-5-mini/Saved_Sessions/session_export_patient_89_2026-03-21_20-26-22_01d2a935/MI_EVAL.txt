# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opens with an explicit nonjudgmental statement and consistently affirms strengths and normalizes difficulty, maintaining respectful language and never blaming Marcus.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated Marcus and moved him toward planning; could be improved with a few deeper, more complex reflections naming emotions like discouragement or fear of relapse.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on increasing manageable physical activity and repeatedly narrowed discussion to actionable, time-bound steps without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly a joint enterprise with frequent invitations for Marcus's input, inclusive summaries, and co-constructed decisions treating him as a partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician preserved choice by offering options, asking which would be most helpful, avoiding pressure, and reinforcing Marcus's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician drew motivation from Marcus by eliciting confidence ratings, asking what would raise them, and surfacing intrinsic reasons (family enjoyment) that led to self-generated commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used collaborative language and co-created the plan, but occasional concrete suggestions shifted slightly toward advising rather than first eliciting Marcus's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly elicited and amplified Marcus's change talk, captured his language and confidence gains, and built these into a clear, specific plan and commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged barriers and pivoted to solutions without arguing, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and more explicit normalization of past lapses.
