# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with supportive reflections and affirmations (safety check, praise for calm role-play). Could add more individualized affirmations and explicit unconditional acceptance to reach the top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that kept Dylan engaged and validated his frustration. Missed opportunities to probe deeper into emotions like shame, fear, or loyalty to friends.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on the conduct concern and practical next steps (what to say, role-play, documentation). The session did not drift from the immediate target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked with Dylan by asking preferences, inviting role-play, and tailoring scripts to his wishes. However, the clinician often provided finished scripts rather than eliciting and refining Dylan’s own language, making the interaction slightly clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Dylan’s autonomy through offering choices, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language. Decision-making about wording and rehearsal was reliably left to Dylan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw out Dylan’s motivations or values; the work was largely tactical and defensive. Missed chances to explore discrepancy or ask questions that would elicit internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with options and permission-seeking, creating a sense of working together. At times the clinician reverted to supplying ready-made scripts instead of co-constructing language with Dylan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session focused mainly on scripting and damage-control with few open questions to elicit desire, reasons, or commitment to change. Clinician did not follow up or amplify the brief change talk Dylan offered.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and sometimes redirected it (e.g., advising against ‘everyone drinks’). However, the clinician did not use strategic MI tools like double-sided reflections or summaries to actively transform sustain talk into change talk.
