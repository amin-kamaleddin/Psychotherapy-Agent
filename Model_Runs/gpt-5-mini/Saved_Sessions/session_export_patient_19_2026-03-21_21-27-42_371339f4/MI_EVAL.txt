# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation, normalizing feelings and praising effort to foster safety and trust.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflected ambivalence and normalized emotions well, but reflections were often summary-level and missed opportunities to probe deeper feelings like shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on gambling-related risks and moved the session toward concrete, time-limited behavior changes with linked role-plays and scripts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem-solving, co-drafting scripts and plans while repeatedly checking preferences and adapting to the client's requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently preserved client choice by offering options, asking permission for interventions, and letting the client select steps and wording to maintain ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited values, reasons, and readiness/confidence, but sometimes transitioned to solutions before fully deepening intrinsic motivation or linking values to stronger commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the client as the expert, shared power through agenda choices and collaborative planning, and invited the client to lead decisions throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk via importance/confidence questions and concrete planning, though could more often amplify and summarize change language to elicit firmer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing minimizing sustain talk but did not consistently use double-sided reflections or pivoting language to explicitly soften or process it.
