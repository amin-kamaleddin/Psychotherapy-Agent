# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated nonjudgmental regard and normalized Dylan's experience, explicitly saying they were there to understand not judge. Avoided labels and validated concerns about housing/record throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, frequent reflections and perspective-taking that helped Dylan feel understood. Could deepen empathic work with more complex reflections exploring underlying feelings and pausing for emotional elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, practical focus on preparing Dylan's account to protect housing/record, which matched the immediate goal. However, the focus was narrow and did not advance the broader change agenda regarding underage drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the clinician invited Dylan's preferences, co-created the script, and role‑played as an equal partner. Decisions about content and approach were jointly made rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and asked permission, reinforcing Dylan's control and decision-making. Language avoided coercion and explicitly supported client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did little to elicit Dylan's intrinsic motivations or personal goals related to drinking beyond a brief question about worries. The clinician moved quickly to practical skill-building and did not probe values, costs/benefits, or discrepancies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly fostered shared decision-making and deferred to Dylan's preferences, asking how he wanted to come across and what would be useful. Co-created role‑play and phrasing that reflected Dylan's goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to evoke change language beyond one question about worries; Dylan's brief concern about housing/record was not followed up. The session focused on crafting denials rather than exploring desire, reasons, ability, or commitment to change drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk but did not redirect it toward consideration of change, instead polishing minimizing language. Few attempts were made to use double-sided reflections, reframe sustain talk, or highlight discrepancies.
