# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing ambivalence and respecting the patient's choice not to quit while offering options they'd try.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries that capture the client's conflict and priorities, helping him elaborate practical alternatives.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing risky drinking while preserving social ties, repeatedly steering toward concrete, short-term behavioral strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative and egalitarian—co-creates solutions, asks for preferences, and follows the client's lead in selecting and rehearsing plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and avoids coercion, tailoring harm-reduction options to what the client is willing to try and reinforcing ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical, intrinsic reasons for change and doable strategies, but misses opportunities to deepen motives or link steps to broader values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance throughout, inviting shared problem-solving, role-play, and treating the client as the expert on what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change-oriented statements and plans, though could more often amplify desire/importance and probe confidence or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects to harm-reduction strategies, but relies mostly on neutral summaries rather than targeted techniques to shift sustain talk toward change.
