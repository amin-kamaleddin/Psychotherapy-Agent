# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, normalizing Liora's experience and thanking her for disclosure which created a strongly accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used frequent accurate reflections and perspective‑taking, tracked emotional tone, validated feelings, and repeatedly confirmed safety, supporting strong rapport and understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on self‑harm and immediate safety with repeated safety checks, concrete steps (delay, securing implements), and a planned 30‑minute follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a joint effort—clinician invited input, offered options, helped craft messages, and stayed available in chat to support the agreed plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by asking permission, offering alternatives, framing recommendations as options, and inviting Liora to pick or tweak suggested messages.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation and highlighted behavioral evidence of capability (urge reduction), but missed deeper elicitation of intrinsic motives, values, or longer‑term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with permission‑seeking and shared decisions, though the session was somewhat clinician‑led and could have included more explicit co‑creation of goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced brief change talk and concrete steps (30‑minute delay, alternatives), but did not sufficiently deepen or amplify internal change language about significance or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain talk while offering alternatives and safety planning, but could have used more double‑sided reflections or evocative rephrasing to tip balance toward change.
