# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized the patient’s stance, and avoided judgment while accommodating his reluctance to quit. The psychiatrist validated the routine and supported a pragmatic, time-limited test.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong empathic reflections captured both the calming function of smoking and the patient’s ambivalence, mirroring his language to build rapport. This likely made the patient feel understood and safe to share concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior—testing daytime NRT—with specific, measurable steps and a one-week experiment. The agenda stayed tightly tied to reducing workplace cravings without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was co-constructed with the patient, inviting his input on NRT choice, alarm wording, and reminders at every decision point. The clinician framed the encounter as shared problem-solving rather than an expert monologue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician emphasized voluntary choice throughout, offering options and repeatedly signaling that the plan was a self-directed, time-limited test. Language like 'no pressure' and asking what felt manageable strengthened perceived autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good use of evocation through questions about likes/worries and confidence scaling, which produced useful change talk, but the clinician relied more on practical supports than deeper elicitation of personal values. More values- or consequence-focused prompts could have deepened intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the decision-maker and invited choices about specifics, demonstrating strong power-sharing. Jointly creating the plan (alarm, partner nudge, backup) reinforced a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited some change talk via questions, a manageable test, and confidence scaling, producing concrete commitment to try the lozenge. Could be improved by amplifying and reflecting change talk more deliberately and probing personal reasons/values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When the patient expressed sustain talk the clinician avoided confrontation and reframed it into a compromise (time-limited experiment), preserving rituals and reducing resistance. Neutral, accepting language and practical problem-solving prevented escalation of sustain talk.
