# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the client's experience and offering practical support without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate summaries and an affect label and tracks logistical concerns well; could probe deeper into underlying emotions, ambivalence, or what completing the hours means to the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on completing the court-ordered hours with concrete planning, timelines, and stepwise problem solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint effort with frequent invitations for client choice, but often supplies ready-made materials and solutions rather than eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, asks permission, and avoids pressuring language, thereby reinforcing the client's control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical commitments and options but focuses on logistics rather than drawing out intrinsic motivation, values, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and choices that convey collaboration, yet the clinician often leads with expert solutions instead of co-creating alternatives with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompts concrete, change-oriented responses about logistics but misses opportunities to amplify desire, reasons, or confidence to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing avoidant statements and redirects toward actionable steps effectively, though could more actively acknowledge ambivalence with double-sided reflections.
