# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows respect and nonjudgmental regard, normalizing ambivalence and avoiding blame. Open, client-centered language and willingness to adapt the plan reinforce acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking capture ambivalence and summarize concerns. Missed opportunities for deeper, feeling-focused reflections that would further explore emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on coffee consumption with concrete planning and a scheduled follow-up. The session stays goal-directed and results in a specific, short-term experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly collaborative and co-constructed, with the patient shaping the checklist, reminder wording, and timing. Clinician positions themselves as a partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly reinforces the patient's freedom to choose and offers options instead of prescriptions. Noncoercive language and normalization of noncommitment support autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some internal motivation is elicited (what feels doable, signs of progress) but the clinician relies heavily on offering solutions. There is room to draw out the patient's intrinsic reasons, importance, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is clearly shared with the patient, who directs choices about tracking and interventions. The plan is co-created and reflects the patient's preferences throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits modest change talk (agreeing to track, try decaf) but does not systematically reflect, amplify, or probe for desire, reasons, or commitment. Techniques like importance/confidence scaling or follow-up questions were underused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses neutral redirection and low-effort experiments to honor ambivalence. More strategic double-sided reflections or reframing could better highlight discrepancy and move toward change.
