# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with validating statements and a nonjudgmental tone (e.g., opening support, safety check). Avoided blaming and affirmed the patient’s motives throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate paraphrase, plus useful clarifying questions about context. Reflections were sometimes brief and solution‑focused rather than exploring deeper emotional nuance or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on creating child consistency and co‑parenting routines, with every suggestion tied to that target behavior. The session avoided drifting into unrelated topics and produced concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed suggestions as offers and provided multiple options, creating a generally collaborative tone. Collaboration was limited by giving polished materials without iteratively eliciting the patient’s own wording or priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported choice by offering options and asking willingness to try things for a week, respecting the patient’s agency. Occasionally presented ready‑made solutions without explicitly asking permission or checking which option the patient preferred.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurred (inviting help, prompting a one‑week trial) that elicited willingness and reasons. However, the clinician primarily provided recommendations instead of systematically drawing out intrinsic motivations, past successes, or scaling importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked what the patient wanted to focus on and offered help, indicating some partnership. The clinician’s delivery of ready‑made routines and scripts reduced co‑creation and the patient’s sense of ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited change‑oriented language and willingness, but frequently moved to offering solutions rather than amplifying or reinforcing the patient’s own change statements. Missed opportunities to ask follow‑ups like 'What would be different if this worked?' or to scale commitment/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and offered practical fallback strategies that reduced resistance. Rarely used MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or reframing) to explicitly normalize ambivalence and soften sustain talk.
