# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations and normalizing statements that conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and timely reflections that reassured the patient, but missed some opportunities for deeper, more complex emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing risky drinking and improving contraceptive reliability, guiding the session from assessment to concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative — invited patient input, offered to assist with tasks, and co-constructed the plan so the session felt like joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and emphasizing that the patient decide what feels right.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited reasons for change and linked drinking to contraception concerns, though could have probed deeper into values and long-term motivations to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the patient as an equal partner, asking which options felt doable and deferring to her expertise about her life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked and amplified change talk (scaling, option choices, link to IUD), but there were missed chances to deepen and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered harm-reduction options, but did not consistently use double-sided reflections or explicitly acknowledge ambivalence to soften sustain talk.
