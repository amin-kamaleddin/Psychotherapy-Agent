# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing ambivalence and avoiding judgment. Tone and affirmations reinforced a warm, nonshaming stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective‑taking with reflective listening and validations that tracked both content and emotion. Summaries and reflections captured surprise, guardedness, and values around marathon training.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on alcohol use in relation to training goals and steered toward a concrete, time‑limited experiment. Moved the conversation toward measurable tracking and a specific plan without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Structured the session as an equal partnership by soliciting Daniel's input at every step and adapting the plan to his preferences. Summaries and invitations showed mutual shaping of the plan and shared decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options and emphasized Daniel's choice, asking which approach felt most doable and how he wanted follow‑up. Language and tone reinforced his agency in planning and evaluation of the experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation from Daniel by eliciting his own reasons, values, and criteria for change and linking them to concrete actions. Used his marathon goals to frame the experiment and foster intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and deferred to Daniel's expertise about his life, asking permission and offering options. The plan was co‑constructed with Daniel selecting nights, tracking, and check‑in preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk through open questions, scaling, and reflecting client‑spoken reasons for change. Built on expressed willingness with a concrete experiment and commitment‑relevant planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward a pragmatic experiment, but missed a few opportunities to use double‑sided reflections or explicitly summarize both sides of ambivalence. Could more actively soften sustain talk with strategic reflective techniques to further amplify change talk.
