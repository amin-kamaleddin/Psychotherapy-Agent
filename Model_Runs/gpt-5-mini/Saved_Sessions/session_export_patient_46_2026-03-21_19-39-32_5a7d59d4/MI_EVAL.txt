# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (e.g., opening statement, thanking honesty, affirmations). No evidence of blaming or shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking with accurate reflections and summaries that mirrored the patient's language and ambivalence. Validated adolescent concerns and responded sensitively to reluctance about calling 911.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus on alcohol use and harm reduction, repeatedly returning to specific safety steps and producing a concrete plan. Kept the conversation targeted on practical strategies for the upcoming event.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a cooperative effort by inviting choices, rehearsing skills, and co-developing the safety plan with the patient. Patient's ideas consistently shaped the plan and follow-up steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supported the patient's sense of control by offering options, asking permission, and normalizing imperfection. Avoided pressure and affirmed the patient's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited useful change-related statements and alternatives (e.g., willingness to alternate with water, use a lookout), but could have probed deeper into personal values or used scaling to strengthen intrinsic motivation. Good evocation that could be deepened to amplify internal drivers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Built a strong partnership by inviting input, deferring to the patient's preferences, and co-creating the plan (rehearsal, selecting lookout, backup). Power and decision-making were clearly shared.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with options, praise, and a one-sentence plan, producing concrete commitments. Could more systematically amplify spontaneous change language and use confidence/importance scaling to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used strategic redirection and double-sided language to respond to sustain talk, focusing on harm reduction. Could further soften sustain talk with more explicit reframes that name the pros/cons and tip toward change.
