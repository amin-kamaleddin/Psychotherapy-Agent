# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys respect and nonjudgmental support, normalizing worries and affirming efforts throughout the session. Examples include explicit statements of support and repeated affirmations of Camila's steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening with a consistently empathic tone, but the clinician sometimes stays at a pragmatic level. Deeper reflections on emotions like loneliness or fear of losing connections would strengthen empathic work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, sustained focus on reducing problematic drinking with concrete goals and repeated returns to planning. The clinician uses tools like the importance ruler, role‑plays, and scheduled check‑ins to keep direction toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with co-creation of plans and invitation for input, but the clinician supplies many specific solutions in quick succession. Allowing more space for the client to generate ideas would enhance collaboration further.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly supports choice by offering options, asking permission, and framing suggestions as experiments to try. This preserved Camila's agency when deciding nights, scripts, and preferred strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and plans (importance ruler, commitment to two practice nights) and draws out preferences that build ownership. More sequencing and deeper questions linking values to change would amplify self‑motivating statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing is evident through asking permission, offering options, and letting Camila choose specifics of the plan. The clinician consistently invites Camila's input and tailors strategies to her preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicits and reinforces change language and amplifies commitment via the importance ruler and concrete planning. More selective amplification and summarizing of brief change statements would strengthen momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but often followed quickly by solution-focused advice rather than strategic MI moves to soften it. Using more double-sided reflections and explicit exploration of both sides of ambivalence would better reduce sustain talk.
