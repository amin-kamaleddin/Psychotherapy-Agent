# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, honoring Samir's expertise and choices which preserved rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of tension were used, but the clinician did not probe deeper emotional nuance or underlying reasons for reluctance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conversation stayed focused on medication adherence and moved efficiently to a concrete, time‑bound plan with clear contingencies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly invited Samir's preferences and co‑constructed the plan, ensuring his brief replies shaped the outcome without imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were offered, refusals respected, and steps framed as voluntary experiments (three‑day trial), reinforcing Samir's control and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited practical adherence ideas and commitment language but missed opportunities to draw out deeper motives, values, or broader reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned Samir as the expert and used joint planning language throughout, resulting in genuine power‑sharing and a user‑selected plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation and amplification of concrete change talk led to a specific plan, though DARN language and personal reasons for change were not fully developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was accepted and rolled with rather than challenged, with skillful redirection to feasible, private strategies that avoided argumentation.
