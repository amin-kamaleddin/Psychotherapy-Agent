# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence, accepting minimizations without judgment and validating both sides of the patient’s experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking and summarized concerns well, but reflections stayed at pros/cons and could probe deeper emotions (e.g., family worry, shame, stress).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use and moved from exploration to a specific, measurable plan with concrete coping strategies, confidence scaling, and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a true two-way partnership with shared problem solving, repeatedly inviting the patient's preferences and adapting interventions to his context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and agency, offered multiple options rather than directives, normalized slips, and used noncoercive language to preserve ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's reasons for change and prior attempts and used confidence scaling, but relied more on pragmatic strategies than exploring deeper intrinsic motives or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan by asking which options felt doable, offering suggestions as choices, and asking permission for role-play, signaling shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk (likes/dislikes, small goals, confidence rating), but could deepen and amplify values-based reasons and stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used double-sided reflections to reduce defensiveness, yet sustain talk persisted and the clinician could more proactively pivot to amplify fleeting change talk and summarize discrepancies.
