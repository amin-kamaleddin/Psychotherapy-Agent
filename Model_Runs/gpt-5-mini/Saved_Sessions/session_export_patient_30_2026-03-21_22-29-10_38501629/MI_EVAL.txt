# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation (e.g., "I accept where you are"). The clinician normalized ambivalence and repeatedly affirmed David's priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate naming of David's core worry about muscle weakness and need for safety/control. Empathy focused more on pragmatic concerns than deeper emotions or long‑term worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, specific focus on the target behavior (a short monitored trial of cholesterol medication) and steered toward concrete next steps and safeguards. Questions and reflections consistently aligned with actionable planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with joint problem‑solving and integration of David's ideas into the plan. The clinician invited David to set priorities and offered help phrasing requests to his doctor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported David's sense of choice and control, repeatedly offering the right to stop and avoiding pressure. The clinician honored autonomy while proposing safeguards and options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client‑generated solutions and past successes (e.g., prior BP med experience) to motivate consideration of a trial. Missed opportunities to draw out core values, use scaling, or elicit a clearer commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision‑making and framed the encounter as co‑creation rather than clinician‑led instruction. The clinician deferred to David's judgments and invited him to choose which step to arrange first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced multiple pieces of change talk and reflected hopeful statements about a monitored trial. Could have amplified and summarized change talk more strongly and used scaling or direct prompts to elicit commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used acknowledgment, reframing, and double‑sided reflections to contain sustain talk about side effects and deferral. The clinician was pragmatic but did not consistently redirect sustain talk toward strengthening change arguments.
