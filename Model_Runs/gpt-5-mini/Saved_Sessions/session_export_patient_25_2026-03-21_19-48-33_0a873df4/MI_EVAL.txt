# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows clear respect and positive regard (congratulates three months smoke-free, validates feelings) with nonjudgmental language. Could increase individualized normalizing statements and more explicit affirmations of strengths beyond the smoking quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking, safety checks, and some reflective language, with warm behavioral feedback during role-plays. Missed deeper reflective paraphrasing of the patient's ambivalence and emotional content (pride, worry about family) that would enhance empathic accuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol harm-reduction with targeted questions, a structured plan, and role-plays that move the patient toward concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative and framed as joint problem-solving, inviting the patient to pick options and practice while adapting to his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors the patient's choices by offering options, asking permission, and shaping plans around his preferences rather than issuing directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits some internal motivation by inviting choices and asking safety/frequency questions that surface pride and concern. Could more systematically draw out the patient's own reasons, values, confidence, and commitment language with evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shares power and invites collaboration (offers options, co-constructs plans, role-plays together), demonstrating respect for the patient's expertise about his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates space for some change talk by offering choices and asking which plan the patient prefers, but does not consistently elicit or deepen change talk. Could use targeted questions about why change matters, importance/confidence scaling, and past successes to amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk by proposing harm-reduction and redirecting focus to safer behavior. Limited use of strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or naming ambivalence) to explicitly soften sustain-oriented rationalizations and steer toward change.
