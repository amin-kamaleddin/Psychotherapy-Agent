# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally respectful and nonjudgmental with normalization and pragmatic supports, but occasional prescriptive instructions reduced fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plain empathic statements and concern for safety were present, though there were few complex reflections exploring ambivalence or deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on alcohol-related safety and concrete harm-reduction steps, with logical sequencing and regular checks on priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly team-oriented with option-giving, co-writing messages, and role-play invitations, but occasional expert-driven directives reduced parity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices and preferences were emphasized and permission was often sought, though some direct recommendations were given without prior elicitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivational language was elicited, but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than drawing out Tyler's own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative moments and shared planning, but the clinician frequently adopted an expert stance with detailed instructions that limited equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opportunities for change talk were created and produced concrete statements, yet the clinician missed chances to reflect and amplify Tyler's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confronting sustain talk and redirected to harm-reduction and normalization, though double-sided reflections acknowledging social value were sometimes missed.
