# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established unconditional positive regard from the opening line and consistently affirmed and normalized the patient's experience without blame or judgment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that validated anger and practical concerns; could deepen empathic reflections to explore the emotional intensity and meaning of feeling disrespected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on anger and appointment-management throughout, with interventions logically tied to the target behavior and no significant topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like joint problem-solving: the psychiatrist co-constructed scripts, invited edits and practice, and integrated the patient's ideas without adopting an authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the patient's choice and control by asking permission, offering options, and framing plans as experiments the patient could accept or decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited the patient's own motivations and resources using questions, confidence scaling, and rehearsals, leading the patient to generate concrete plans and contingencies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative approach that shared power—asking permission, soliciting preferences, and polishing the patient's own wording to co-create the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and strengthened change talk through past-attempts inquiry, a confidence ruler, offering scripts with permission, rehearsals, and contingency planning, producing concrete commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to low-effort experiments and backups, but could further soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections and exploration of costs of staying the same.
