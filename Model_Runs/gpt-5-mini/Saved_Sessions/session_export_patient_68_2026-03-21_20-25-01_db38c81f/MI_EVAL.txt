# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard, normalized Kevin’s experience, and validated mixed feelings throughout without shaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured ambivalence, but some reflections stayed surface-level and the clinician moved quickly into advice rather than exploring emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained clearly focused on drinking and its consequences, progressing logically from assessment to concrete, feasible strategies and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative approach—options were offered, Kevin chose strategies, and plans/rehearsals were co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported client autonomy by asking permission, offering multiple options, and avoiding pressuring language, reinforcing Kevin’s control over choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation of internal motivation using scaling and past-success prompts, but could sequence and reflect client change language more to deepen and consolidate commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear partnership stance—clinician invited input, asked preferences, and shared decision-making throughout the exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and affirmed change talk (importance ruler, past successes), yet missed opportunities to amplify statements and convert them into stronger preparatory or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledged its legitimacy, but could have used double-sided reflections or selective reinforcement to more effectively shift toward change.
