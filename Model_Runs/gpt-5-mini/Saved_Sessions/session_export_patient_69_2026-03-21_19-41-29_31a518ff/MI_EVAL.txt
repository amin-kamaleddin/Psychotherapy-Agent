# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing the patient's experience and thanking them for speaking; created a warm, supportive stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with timely complex reflections and emotion labeling, showing high empathic attunement to the patient's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on the core issue of disengagement and broken promises, but stopped short of strategically steering toward concrete goals or a small next step when appropriate.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was strongly collaborative—offered options, invited the patient to direct pace and content, and consistently shared power by seeking permission before offering alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly used choice language and explicitly acknowledged the patient's right to decline planning, reinforcing client ownership over decisions and timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to draw out motivations (e.g., offering to sketch a way to ask for help) but relied mostly on listening and did not consistently elicit the patient's own reasons or values for change when opportunities arose.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the interaction as a joint exploration, deferred to the patient's preferences, and invited the patient to set the pace and direction of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to elicit change talk were minimal and weak—choices were offered but the clinician did not follow with evocative questions to draw out desire, reasons, or commitment, missing clear opportunities (e.g., missed housing intake).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoided pushing back on sustain talk and honored the patient's wish to just be heard, but used few deliberate techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to gently pivot toward any seeds of change.
