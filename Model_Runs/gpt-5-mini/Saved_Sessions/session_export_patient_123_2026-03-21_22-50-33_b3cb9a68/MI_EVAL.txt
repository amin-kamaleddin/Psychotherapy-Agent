# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with affirmations and normalizing language, validating the patient's competence and choices in a supportive, nonjudgmental tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing feelings, ambivalence, and values while summarizing and checking in effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the concrete target behavior (a 20‑minute study/task routine) and consistently brings questions and reflections back to implementation strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as joint problem-solving, frequently inviting the patient's input and letting the patient's ideas shape a practical plan rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports patient choice by offering options, deferring to the patient's preferences, and avoiding pressure, thereby reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical reasons and ideas for change but could probe more deeply into underlying values, confidence, and readiness to strengthen intrinsic motivation and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently adopts a collaborative stance with co-planning language and invitations to choose among options, demonstrating respect for the patient's expertise and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and develops change talk with concrete steps and choices, but could better amplify and reflect the patient's own commitment language and use scaling to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately accepts and normalizes sustain talk without arguing, yet makes fewer strategic moves (e.g., double-sided reflections or eliciting counterarguments) to pivot toward commitment.
