# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, reframed behavior nonjudgmentally, and avoided labels, creating a safe space for the client to voice concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking were used throughout, paraphrasing both content and feelings to build rapport and deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on the concrete target (securing immediate paid work) with recurring, behaviorally specific goals and actionable next steps that avoided drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed tasks as joint work, repeatedly invited the client's input and preferences, and co-constructed decisions rather than directing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered and the client's non‑negotiables were respected, reinforcing that the client retained control over how the plan proceeded.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited the client's own motivations and criteria (e.g., 'no surprises', start-this-week), amplifying internal drivers rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was clearly collaborative, with the clinician inviting preference, offering options, and positioning themselves as a helper rather than an authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was actively elicited and reinforced (notably 'If it’s real and starts this week, I’ll go'), with concrete questions converting desire into specific commitments and steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician validated sustain talk and redirected toward solutions effectively, but missed some MI-specific opportunities (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring costs of staying disengaged) to more actively soften sustain talk.
