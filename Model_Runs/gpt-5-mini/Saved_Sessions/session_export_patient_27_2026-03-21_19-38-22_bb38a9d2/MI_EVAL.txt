# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive—normalizes feelings, affirms courage, and respects privacy throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening, accurately naming the patient's emotions and tailoring soothing strategies to their concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on disclosure to peers, linking each intervention (scripts, role-play, planning) back to that behavioral aim.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works jointly with the patient through options, permission-seeking, and gentle feedback during role-plays, treating the plan as a shared effort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice and control by offering multiple script options, asking permission for role-play, and honoring the patient's preferences about privacy and timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relies heavily on clinician-provided scripts and advice rather than eliciting the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence, missing questions that would deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—asks permission, offers choices, and co-constructs language and plans with the patient rather than prescribing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for practice and brief expressions of intent but tends to supply scripted wording instead of systematically eliciting and amplifying the patient's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk and redirects to manageable steps (scripts, role-play) but would benefit from more double-sided reflections and explicit amplification of any change language.
