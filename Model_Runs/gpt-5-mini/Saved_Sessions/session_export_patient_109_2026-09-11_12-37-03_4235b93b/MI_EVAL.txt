# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, with frequent empathic validations and normalizing language that demonstrate strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking and accurate reflections of ambivalence and barriers, but makes fewer deeper affective reflections to capture Rafael's discouragement and emotional tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the 20-minute lunchtime walk with all questions and options directed toward concrete, testable steps and a single experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative stance that co-creates options, checks back with Rafael, and shapes decisions around his preferences, signaling an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly reinforces choice and ownership by offering options, asking which feels most doable, framing plans as experiments, and avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical solutions and plans but focuses mainly on logistics and problem-solving, with limited elicitation of deeper values, motives, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the patient as the expert, invites him to choose and refine the plan, and avoids an authoritarian stance throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces change talk around concrete plans and small tests, though it stops short of eliciting stronger desire, importance, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections and redirection to experiments, but could more actively diminish sustain talk by exploring past successes or reframing avoidance.
