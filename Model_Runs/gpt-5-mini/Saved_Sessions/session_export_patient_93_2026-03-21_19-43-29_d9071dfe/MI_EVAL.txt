# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, expressing unconditional positive regard and accepting the patient's preference to cut down rather than quit. Frequent affirmations and respectful language reinforced a safe, accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and some reflective statements that acknowledge ambivalence and pressures. Missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings about retirement, the fall, and the emotional meaning of pub evenings to reach a higher level of empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained tightly focused on reducing alcohol-related risk with concrete harm-reduction strategies, a specific goal, scripts, and follow-up tasks. Guidance toward change was clear while staying patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as an equal partnership with the clinician repeatedly soliciting and following the patient's preferences for wording, activities, and reminders. Language and choices reinforced shared decision-making throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and honoring her decision to cut down rather than quit. Medical advice was framed as recommendation rather than coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited a concrete commitment to two alcohol-free nights but did not sufficiently draw out internal motives, values, or confidence. Could strengthen evocation with more open, evocative questions and scaling to surface personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and co-constructed the plan with the patient, repeatedly inviting her input on all elements. The clinician deferred to her preferences and implemented choices consistently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, set a small specific goal, and helped operationalize steps that produced concrete commitments. Could further amplify desire, ability, and reasons by asking more evocative questions and reflecting change language more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected ambivalence toward harm-reduction while normalizing mixed feelings. Rarely used double-sided reflections or explicit paraphrases to acknowledge sustain talk while pointing to change, and did not frequently challenge minimizations.
