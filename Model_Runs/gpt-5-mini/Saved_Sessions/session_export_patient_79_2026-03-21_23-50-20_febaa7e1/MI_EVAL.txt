# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes the patient’s experience, and explicitly honors boundaries (no lectures, no med changes).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate perspective-taking with frequent reflections that capture both content and affect, building rapport and engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on a single target behavior (an 8:00 AM text reminder) and repeatedly returns to concrete implementation details to move toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invites the patient’s input at each step, follows patient-led preferences, and co-constructs the plan rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, checking preferences, and affirming ways to stop the intervention, avoiding pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from within the patient through targeted questions about desired outcomes and past successes, using those motivators to shape a feasible plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly treats the patient as the expert, asks permission, offers choices, and shares decision-making to create a patient-centered plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with focused questions and summaries, producing explicit reasons for change that are reinforced and operationalized.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids argument and neutrally reflects dismissive remarks, but at times mirrors sustain language without using double-sided reflections; could more explicitly juxtapose reasons for and against change.
