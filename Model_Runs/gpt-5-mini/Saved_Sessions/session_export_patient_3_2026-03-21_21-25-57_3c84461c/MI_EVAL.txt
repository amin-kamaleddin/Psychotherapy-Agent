# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, with repeated validation and explicit honoring of Aisha’s preferences (e.g., avoiding shaming/weight-focused language). The clinician demonstrated reliable unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with multiple validations that built rapport. Could deepen empathic attunement by exploring the emotional texture behind “I feel gross” with more complex reflections or an empathic summary.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on initiating brief regular exercise and translated motivation into a concrete, time-framed plan. Agenda-setting, progression weeks, scheduling, and follow-up kept the session tightly goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner by co-creating routines, alarm wording, and check-ins, inviting negotiation and adjusting plans to Aisha’s wishes. The session felt genuinely two-way rather than expert-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy with invitational language, offering choices and explicitly deferring decisions about pace and follow-up to Aisha. The clinician avoided pressuring language and reinforced client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Aisha’s own motivations using her language and prompts about non-scale wins, inviting internal reasons for change. The clinician consistently cultivated motivation from within rather than imposing external reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked for Aisha’s input and co-created specifics (alarm line, check-in phrasing, progression), sharing power in decision-making. The approach honored the client’s preferences and kept the plan collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk and converted motivation into concrete steps, habit strategies, and accountability options. A missed opportunity was to more systematically amplify Aisha’s own reasons and confidence (e.g., using 'what would it mean...' or a confidence ruler).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimized by normalizing doubts, offering low-pressure 'tiny starts,' and reframing barriers into manageable troubleshooting. The clinician emphasized self-compassion and avoided arguing for change, reducing resistance effectively.
