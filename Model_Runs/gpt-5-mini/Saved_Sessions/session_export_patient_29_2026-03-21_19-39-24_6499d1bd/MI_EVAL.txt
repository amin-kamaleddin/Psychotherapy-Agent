# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalized difficulty, acknowledging nervousness and readiness. Responses sometimes felt formulaic and the clinician moved quickly into information-giving rather than explicitly expressing individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good basic reflective listening and validation demonstrated understanding of David's situation and mixed feelings. Reflections were often brief and the clinician missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection on anxiety and frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear and consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete assessments, treatment options, coping strategies, and follow-up plans. Practical next steps (2‑week NRT starter, BP check, follow-up) were clearly established.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the session as a joint enterprise, co-creating the doctor message, rehearsing scripts, and tailoring refusal lines per David's input. These behaviors invited active participation rather than imposing a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and respecting David's preference to try NRT first. Language emphasized David's control over decisions and avoided coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some open questions that elicited motivation and concrete actions, but the clinician relied more on giving information and teaching than on drawing out David's personal reasons, values, or confidence. Few targeted evocative questions or scaling to deepen internal motivation were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally adopted a collaborative stance, asking for preferences and editing materials per David's requests. Some parts of the encounter were more clinician-driven with heavy information provision rather than co-constructed problem exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change language (preferences, desire for meds, drafting messages) and reinforced commitment through rehearsals. However, change talk was not consistently amplified or deepened with targeted reflections and evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (stress, social triggers) was acknowledged and redirected to problem-solving with practical tips and rehearsals. The clinician used few MI strategies (double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, tipping summaries) to explicitly soften or reframe sustain talk.
