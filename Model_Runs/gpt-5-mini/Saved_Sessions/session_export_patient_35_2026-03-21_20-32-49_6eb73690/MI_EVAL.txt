# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—validates priorities and affirms protective choices while avoiding blame. Warm, respectful language encourages disclosure and collaboration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing stressors and barriers. Could probe deeper into emotional nuance (guilt, shame, exhaustion) and pause more to amplify feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on parental smoking tied to the child's health and progresses logically from assessment to a concrete, time-linked plan. Repeatedly returns to specifics and implementation steps to keep momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction feels like a joint exploration with frequent invitations for patient input and adaptation of suggestions. Occasionally relies on clinician-framed plans or scripts, which slightly limits patient-led content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice and control by offering options, asking permission before giving information, and framing decisions as the patient's. Avoids coercive language and affirms patient ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the patient's motivations and resources using questions about prior attempts and readiness ratings, producing client-led commitments. Could further probe intrinsic values and amplify client-expressed reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance—asks permission, offers choices, and co-constructs the plan with the patient. At times presents structured clinician-directed lists rather than eliciting the patient's version first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change language, reflects statements like 'I'd like to cut down,' and supports specific commitments. Would benefit from more consistent amplification, summarizing of change talk, and probing for personally meaningful reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids escalating resistance but primarily offers practical strategies rather than using targeted MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, strategic reframing) to resolve ambivalence. Missed opportunities to gently shift sustain talk toward change.
