# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the patient's limits and choices. Uses affirmations and avoids labels or admonishment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing the patient's views and preferences. Validates ambivalence and tailors options to the patient's needs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on mini‑cigar use and related throat/gum irritation and moves to a specific, measurable behavioral experiment. Little drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—invites choice, co-designs the 7‑day experiment, and problem‑solves practical barriers together. Checks for fit with the patient's routine throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering options, asking which is most doable, and affirming the patient's right to decide. Avoids pressure and frames the plan as the patient's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out immediate motivations and preferences (facts first, willingness to try a test) but could deepen evocation by exploring deeper reasons, importance/confidence, and amplifying change language. More targeted questions could strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as joint problem‑solving and defers to the patient's knowledge and choices. Repeatedly checks that the plan fits and asks for adjustments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports change talk through a doable experiment and choice questions, leading to explicit change statements. Misses opportunities to deepen reasons for change, use scaling, and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to a low‑threat experiment and neutral facts. Could further soften sustain talk by using double‑sided reflections and explicitly acknowledging the pull of the cigars before pivoting.
