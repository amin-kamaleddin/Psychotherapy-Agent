# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with validating language and strength‑focused reflections, creating a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective‑taking and reflective listening by tracking emotions and summarizing multiple elements of the patient's experience, which supported rapport and disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained strong, appropriate focus on reducing alcohol use in social situations, with each question and reflection tied to practical, actionable plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly reciprocal and co‑creative, repeatedly checking preferences and tailoring support while following the patient's lead on plan details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, avoiding prescriptive commands, and affirming the patient’s right to choose pace and strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation linked to values and anticipated benefits but missed some opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation (e.g., confidence/importance ratings and explicitly leveraging past smoking‑cessation success).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine partnership by co‑constructing plans, offering supports like rehearsal or drafting texts, and sharing decision‑making power with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively evoked and amplified change talk through targeted questions and reflections, leading the patient to articulate concrete intentions and rehearsable strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward change, though could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections and longer summaries to better soften ambivalence.
