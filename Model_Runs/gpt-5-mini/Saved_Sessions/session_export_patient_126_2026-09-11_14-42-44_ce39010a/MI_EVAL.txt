# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, acknowledging the patient's roles and pragmatic choices. Few explicit affirmations or warm normalizing statements, so tone feels more clinical and efficiency-focused than expansive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate paraphrase and perspective-taking, summarizing plans and strengths well. Empathic work is mainly cognitive/task-oriented with limited reflection of the patient's feelings or deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, persistent focus on reducing weeknight drinking and progressively narrows to specific supports and contingencies. Questions steer logically toward a concrete, actionable plan without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as collaborative problem-solving, using inclusive language and eliciting the patient's history and preferences. Summaries and repeated invitations for the patient to choose demonstrate genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently asks what feels doable, offers options, and frames choices as the patient's, reinforcing ownership of the plan. There is no coercive language and the clinician leaves final decisions to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from the patient by linking values (being present for his child) and past success to current change efforts. Elicits and amplifies the patient's own reasons and strategies rather than supplying them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the primary source of solutions, frequently asking for his judgment and preferences. The clinician scaffolds without taking over, clearly sharing power in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly elicits and amplifies change talk with open questions that generate specific desire, ability, reasons, and commitment language. Builds on the patient's statements to convert vague intention into concrete, actionable commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids confrontation and does not reinforce sustain talk but misses chances to acknowledge or reframe the patient's minimizations. The rapid move to planning reduces sustain talk but does not actively soften ambivalence with double-sided reflections or explicit validation.
