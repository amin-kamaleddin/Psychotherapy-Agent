# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates clear unconditional positive regard from the opening line; tone stays nonjudgmental and consistently affirms the patient's concerns throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking with accurate reflections of practical concerns and normalization, but could use deeper emotional labeling and more summaries of the patient's feelings to reach the highest level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the safe transition between medications; all information and offers are tightly linked to pragmatic next steps without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicits and integrates the patient's input at every step, co-creates messages, and offers rehearsal and follow-up planning as joint work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by discouraging unilateral changes, offering options, and asking permission before adding details, reinforcing patient agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical actions like drafting messages and role‑play but does little to draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or questions about importance and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the encounter as a partnership—co‑creates content, repeatedly invites the patient's preferences, and transfers power to the patient in decision planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Prompts preparatory and commitment-oriented language through concrete supports and offers, but does not systematically elicit internal reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain concerns and reframes risks neutrally while cautioning against abrupt changes, though could more actively pivot sustain talk toward change with double-sided reflections.
