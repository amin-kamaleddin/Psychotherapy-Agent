# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental, respectful regard using double-sided reflections and validations of Rowan’s constraints. No blaming or minimization; clinician worked from Rowan’s limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking helped Rowan elaborate on practical barriers. Reflections were somewhat repetitive and missed deeper exploration of emotional undertones behind resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on charging and using the device with stepwise, concrete problem‑solving. Every question and reflection kept progress toward a specific adherence plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as genuine joint problem‑solving with Rowan’s suggestions shaping the plan and clinician building on his responses. Clinician repeatedly invited input and framed solutions as shared decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and tailored suggestions to Rowan’s preferences, validating his limits and avoiding pressure. Options were co‑created so Rowan retained control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively drew out Rowan’s own practical ideas and motivations for workable change. There was some clinician-provided structure and options, so deeper elicitation of intrinsic reasons or values could be increased.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician adopted a collaborative stance and invited Rowan to choose what fit his routine, making him the decision‑maker. At times multiple clinician suggestions slightly reduced pure power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and amplified specific, low‑effort commitments that produced change statements from Rowan. Could more consistently reflect and strengthen tentative language (e.g., 'I'll try') and probe confidence to consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and reframed sustain talk into workable, low‑friction options that reduced resistance. More explicit reflective responses to sustain talk and strategic summaries contrasting pros/cons could further resolve ambivalence.
