# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, with frequent validation and explicit accommodation of Marcus's attachment to his dog. The clinician demonstrated clear unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displayed solid empathic listening and validation of overwhelm and conflict, but sometimes moved quickly to solutions. More deeper, complex reflections unpacking feelings and values would strengthen empathy to a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on improving asthma with a concise prioritized plan and concrete steps, repeatedly refocusing when concerns arose. The session stayed on-task and purposefully goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively on scripts and rehearsals and adjusted language to Marcus's comfort, but often supplied full content rather than inviting Marcus to generate wording first. Greater co-creation would increase collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by asking permission, offering options, and encouraging Marcus to pick what felt manageable. The clinician emphasized Marcus's right to decide and seek alternatives, avoiding coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical change-talk (symptom recognition and willingness for steps) but primarily offered solutions instead of drawing out intrinsic motivations or values. More open-ended questions and amplification of personal reasons for change are needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by inviting role-play and tailoring scripts, yet frequently took a directive lead in designing the plan and exact phrasing. Inviting Marcus to propose drafts or prioritize items would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reactive elicitation of change talk and willingness for small steps occurred, but the clinician did not systematically elicit or amplify Marcus's motives, goals, or imagined benefits. More strategic reflections and summaries to draw out and reinforce change talk are needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk by acknowledging attachment to the dog and offering management alternatives and scripts, reducing pressure to choose. More explicit double-sided reflections naming both sides of ambivalence and pivoting to change talk would further strengthen this skill.
