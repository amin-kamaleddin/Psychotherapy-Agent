# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing Irina’s struggles and respecting her situation. Language and affirmations reinforced dignity without labeling or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and timely checks demonstrated accurate understanding of Irina’s ambivalence and feelings, matching her affect and content. The clinician summarized values and concerns clearly and validated her nervousness and willingness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing drinking with concrete, time-bound steps (one NA drink, 10-minute delay, Friday trial). The clinician consistently returned to specifics, confidence ratings, and backup plans to move toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was highly collaborative, inviting Irina’s ideas and treating her as the expert on what will work. The clinician co-constructed the plan, checked feasibility, and integrated her choices throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasized choice and invited consent for options and check-ins, allowing Irina to modify wording and select strategies. No coercive language was used, preserving patient ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Irina’s own values and reasons for change through targeted questions and amplification of her change language. Use of the readiness ruler and prompts to deepen commitment effectively evoked internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a partnership stance by offering options, asking permission, and inviting preferences rather than directing. Decision-making was framed as Irina’s and the plan was co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and amplified change talk using open questions, the readiness/confidence ruler, and concrete planning prompts. Reflections built on Irina’s reasons and commitment language to deepen her intention to try the experiment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided argument and redirected toward values and alternatives but missed some opportunities for explicit double-sided reflections. More frequent use of reflections that name both sustain and change sides could better resolve ambivalence.
