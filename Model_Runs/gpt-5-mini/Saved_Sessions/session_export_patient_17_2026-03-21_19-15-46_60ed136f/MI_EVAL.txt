# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Anisa’s shame and distress, but responses were sometimes efficient rather than deeply person‑centered; more individualized affirmations and explicit normalization of lapses would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated accurate perspective‑taking and reflective listening (e.g., paraphrasing feelings, calming language), but could have deepened empathy by exploring vivid imagery like the 'leper' metaphor and the emotional meaning of relapse fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking cessation with concrete pre‑quit/quit/early recovery plans and specific next actions, effectively tying dialogue back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as co‑created—soliciting input on the plan, co‑writing the CRAVE text, rehearsing responses, and inviting the patient's preferences—demonstrating genuine two‑way teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple options were offered, permission asked before advising, and slips were framed as information rather than failure, consistently reinforcing the patient’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician drew out key motivations (daughter’s disappointment, desire to quit) and elicited commitment (choosing a quit date), but missed chances to deepen internal motivation with evocative questions or scaling importance/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shared power by asking which options the patient preferred, inviting choice of a quit date, and co‑creating the day‑by‑day plan, showing strong partnership throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively elicited and reinforced (highlighting the daughter’s influence, choosing April 4, concrete strategies), but could be amplified more systematically with summaries and evocative prompts to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician neutrally acknowledged barriers, reframed slips as data, and offered alternatives, yet occasionally moved quickly into solutions instead of exploring sustain talk more deeply or using double‑sided reflections to hold ambivalence.
