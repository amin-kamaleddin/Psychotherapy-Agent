# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed consistent unconditional positive regard and respect, validating Tariq’s experience and avoiding blame. Early and repeated affirmations framed the situation as understandable and nonjudgmental.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that matched Tariq’s feelings about family loss and release anxiety. Empathic statements were frequent and helped the patient feel heard and understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on restoring a one-time phone call and protecting release, with concrete steps like drafting a script, compliance log, rehearsing, and escalation plans. Interventions consistently stayed tied to the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the interaction as a joint effort, co-creating the script and log and rehearsing with Tariq. Coaching was collaborative, incorporating Tariq’s wording and preferences rather than dictating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple options were offered and Tariq’s choices were solicited and respected throughout the session. Language emphasized that decisions were his, with no pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out intrinsic motivation linked to family and release and used those values to prompt action. However, opportunities to deepen evocation (scaling importance/confidence or eliciting a verbal commitment) were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician fostered a true partnership by inviting Tariq’s input, incorporating his edits, and rehearsing together, demonstrating power-sharing. Tariq’s contributions clearly shaped the plan and language used.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited and reinforced (family contact, protecting release) and concrete supports were offered to amplify it. There were missed chances to further elicit importance, confidence, and stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed grievances into actionable steps (script, written denial, caseworker involvement). More explicit double-sided reflections could have softened sustain talk even further.
