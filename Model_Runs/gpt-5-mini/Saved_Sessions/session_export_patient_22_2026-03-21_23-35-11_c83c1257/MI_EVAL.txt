# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizes Camila's experience, and validates ambivalence without judgment throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries mirror Camila's language and perspective, though several reflections are somewhat formulaic and could deepen emotional exploration (e.g., loneliness, shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician keeps a clear focus on reducing problematic drinking while preserving social connection and narrows discussion toward a specific, time‑bound plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is highly collaborative with solutions co‑constructed and decisions left to Camila, demonstrating an equal‑partner interaction throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly reinforced by offering options, asking what feels doable, and validating Camila's preference for control rather than total abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits motivations, values, and concrete strategies effectively, but could do more to amplify change statements and elicit explicit commitment or confidence ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly frames Camila as the expert, invites her choices, and allows her input to drive the session, reflecting strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is consistently elicited and reinforced and a specific plan emerges, but the clinician seldom amplifies or consolidates commitment language (e.g., no confidence rating or explicit commitment request).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accepts sustain talk and redirects toward workable harm‑reduction strategies without confrontation, though could more actively use double‑sided reflections to resolve ambivalence toward change.
