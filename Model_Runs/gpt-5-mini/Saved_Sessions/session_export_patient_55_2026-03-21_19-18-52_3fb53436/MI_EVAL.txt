# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and normalizing language; clinician avoided labeling or blame and repeatedly reassured Andrea, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of surface concerns, validating Andrea's experience; missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections about emotional meaning and impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on screening and then on sleep/concentration strategies with a follow-up plan; could have framed the primary target more tightly earlier to increase directional potency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was balanced and collegial with tailored suggestions and a negotiated midweek check-in; decisions were clearly co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and permission to decline, accepting noncommitment without pressure; safety recommendations were offered as resources, reinforcing Andrea's ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some willingness to experiment and notice changes, but did not probe deeper into personal values, reasons for change, or confidence; stronger evocation would draw more on Andrea's intrinsic motives and expected benefits.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session demonstrated clear partnership through frequent offers of choice and joint creation of a doable plan; Andrea's preferences directly shaped strategy selection and follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively invited change-related language and choices, which produced client commitments to try specific tips; could have amplified and explored reasons, importance, and confidence to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided introducing arguments for the status quo and used permissive language, minimizing sustain talk; missed opportunities for strategic softening (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) when ambivalence was expressed.
