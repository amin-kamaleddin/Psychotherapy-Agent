# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and avoided judgment, normalizing choice and praising realistic steps; honored the client's request for low‑pressure follow‑up and nonjudgmental language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used succinct reflections and perspective‑taking that validated Farid's experience and barriers, but could deepen empathic resonance by reflecting underlying discouragement and self‑doubt signaled by his sighs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on increasing brief physical activity and moved the session toward a measurable, sequential plan (prep clothes, alarms, Tuesday 7:15 walk) without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative throughout — repeatedly invited Farid's input on tactics, timing, and message wording and adjusted the plan to his preferences and limits.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking which tactics felt doable, and explicitly honoring Farid's preferences about nudges and pressure level rather than pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited reasons and motivations effectively (importance ruler, doable options) leading to change statements, but sometimes moved quickly to solution suggestions instead of further drawing out values, confidence, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co‑constructed with shared decision‑making; the clinician positioned themselves as a partner, asked permission for check‑ins, and located expertise with Farid.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk using importance ratings, concrete planning questions, and reflections, which produced multiple verbal commitments and a specific implementation intention.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframed and validated sustain talk (e.g., 'not laziness; your workload is draining') and avoided argument, but could more often use double‑sided reflections to hold ambivalence before offering solutions.
