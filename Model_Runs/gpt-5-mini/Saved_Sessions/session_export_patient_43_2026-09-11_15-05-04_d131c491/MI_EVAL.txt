# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed steady unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, validating the patient’s perspective and avoiding lecturing. This respectful tone supported openness to exploring practical steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and summaries tied together ambivalence and choices, demonstrating understanding. There were missed opportunities for deeper affective or identity reflections (e.g., defensiveness, frustration about the wife) that could have deepened rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on smoking and moved purposefully from broad options to a single, actionable commitment. Prompts consistently targeted concrete tactics (gum, alarm, dash note, delay first cigarette).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was a joint problem‑solving effort with inclusive phrasing and co‑constructed plans tailored to the patient’s routines. The patient’s practical knowledge shaped realistic, mutually agreed strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently emphasized choice and control, normalizing small goals and letting the patient set the pace and terms of partner support. This reinforced the patient’s sense of ownership over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician effectively drew out the patient’s own ideas and motivations through open invitations and scaffolding, leading to multiple self‑generated strategies. The patient articulated concrete implementation plans and contingency steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as collaborative, offering options and deferring to the patient’s expertise to choose what felt doable. Plans (location of gum, alarm, dash note, wording of text) were co‑created rather than prescribed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicited and reinforced the patient’s change language with strategic questions and summaries, helping the patient produce multiple concrete commitments. Repeated invitations narrowed options to a clear, actionable commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and did not amplify sustain talk, instead orienting to small steps, but did not routinely use targeted MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or selective summaries) to explicitly tip the balance toward change. More deliberate softening of sustain talk could have helped resolve ambivalence sooner.
