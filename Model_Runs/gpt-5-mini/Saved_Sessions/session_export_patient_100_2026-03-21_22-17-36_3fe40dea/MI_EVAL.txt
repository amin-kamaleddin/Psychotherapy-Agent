# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent nonjudgmental regard, normalizes difficulty, and invites collaboration rather than imposing directives, creating unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrase demonstrate understanding of consequences and worries; could deepen empathy by naming emotions (embarrassment, fear) and using more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays focused on alcohol use and its impact on school/college, elicits importance and goals, and moves toward concrete, measurable change steps and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is mutual problem‑solving with clear invitations for the client to choose options, rehearse, and adapt plans, using role‑play and shared decision making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently reinforces Maya’s choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and leaving decisions about strategies to her, supporting agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively links behavior to personally meaningful goals and elicits reasons for change, but could more systematically draw out desire/ability/reasons/need and consolidate commitment and confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician frames the work as a collaborative effort, privileges the client’s expertise, and shares power by offering options and inviting Maya to select and rehearse strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with open questions, affirms specific change language, and translates it into concrete, doable steps that are rehearsed and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers practical strategies to manage peer pressure, though could strengthen use of double‑sided reflections to gently highlight discrepancy.
