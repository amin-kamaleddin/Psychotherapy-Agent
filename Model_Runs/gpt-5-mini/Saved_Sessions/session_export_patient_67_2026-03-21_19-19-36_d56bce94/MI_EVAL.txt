# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language and validation (e.g., safety check, normalizing struggles), conveying unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and several accurate reflections, but missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections linking Kenji’s affect and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol reduction with targeted questions and a concrete stepwise plan, though offering many strategies sometimes reduced opportunities to elicit the patient’s own priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—invited preferences, co-created the one-line template, and negotiated check-in—yet occasionally proposed ready-made plans instead of first eliciting the patient’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, emphasizing 'choose what you’ll actually use,' and avoiding pressure or coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made attempts to elicit motivation and past attempts, but relied more on offering solutions than exploring personal values, importance, or confidence (no scaling or deeper elicitation).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and invited input on tools and reminders, though could strengthen partnership by explicitly acknowledging the patient’s expertise and asking him to design the plan first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk through open questions and options, but did not consistently amplify or reflect desire/ability/reasons/need or elicit commitment language and scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized sustain talk, but missed strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, summaries) and sometimes shifted quickly to advice rather than softening sustain talk first.
