# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Mateo's goals and ambivalence. To reach top score they could have normalized struggles more explicitly and used more individualized affirmations of strengths beyond compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections matched Mateo's language and acknowledged triggers, helping him elaborate his goals. Greater empathic attunement could be achieved by naming underlying emotions (fear, shame, hope) and mirroring affect when his voice wavered.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing reoffending risk with concrete, time-bound actions tied to values. Every intervention linked back to the target behavior and moved toward practical steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was a strong collaborative tone with joint decision-making, role-play, and solicited preferences. The clinician could increase collaboration by more frequently asking Mateo to teach back or propose alternate wordings.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and framing scripts as optional tools. This strongly reinforced Mateo’s control over decisions and actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and links from values to action effectively drew out Mateo's reasons for change. Further development of internal motivation would come from probing confidence, past successes, and eliciting his own summary of why now is the right time.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician treated the session as a joint enterprise, inviting Mateo to lead practice and choose options. To reach a 5 they could have asked Mateo to co-create longer-term plans and deferred more to his expertise about relationships and likely reactions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited meaningful change talk and translated values into concrete actions, reinforcing a specific plan. They could amplify change talk further by reflecting/summarizing desire/ability/need and explicitly eliciting commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided amplifying sustain talk and provided practical refusal strategies, but moved quickly to solutions without using double-sided reflections or exploring the felt pressures. More rolling-with-resistance techniques and reflections of ambivalence would help reduce sustain language.
