# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, using validating, normalizing language and avoiding criticism or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of the patient’s situation, though missed opportunities for deeper affective or values-level reflections to deepen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking with a logical progression to a concrete, manageable behavioral goal and avoided drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—framed work as joint problem‑solving, solicited the patient's input throughout, and implemented patient-selected choices in the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking which felt doable, and validating a small-test approach without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited pragmatic motivation and past strategies (rulers, options), but could have probed deeper for personally meaningful, value‑based reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the patient as the expert, asked permission before offering suggestions, and co‑constructed the plan with shared decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and used scaling, but did not consistently amplify or strategically reflect and expand the patient’s pro‑change statements to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged both sides, but did not routinely use MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, reframing) to actively soften sustain talk.
