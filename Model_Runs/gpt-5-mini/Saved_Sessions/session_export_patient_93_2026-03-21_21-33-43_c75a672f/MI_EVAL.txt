# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, normalizing concerns and affirming Margaret’s plan. Language avoids labels and validates her experience throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing ambivalence and safety concerns. Could deepen empathic probes into underlying emotions related to retirement and social routine to reach a higher rating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related risk and safety steps, moving logically from exploration to a concrete plan. Balances direction with noncoercive guidance and specific next-week goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: co-creates goals, asks for preferences, tailors scripts and safety plans, and repeatedly checks comfort and readiness. The clinician frames the work as joint problem-solving and offers to practice with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering multiple options, asking which feels most manageable, and avoiding pressuring language. Invites Margaret to set her own goal and decides which supports she wants.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits internal motivation and practical reasons for change using open questions, readiness/confidence scales, and implementation intentions. Could further explore personal values and more consistently amplify intrinsic motivators toward commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly conveys that expertise resides with the client and fosters shared decision-making, inviting Margaret to choose strategies and practicing scripts together. Power-sharing is evident in planning and offers of support.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and shapes change talk with specific suggestions and scaling questions, and converts statements into a workable plan. To improve, the clinician could amplify and link change statements to values to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and does not reinforce sustain talk, but relies on neutral reflections and factual feedback rather than strategic softening techniques. More double-sided reflections, selective reflection of change language, and exploration of costs of current behavior would better redirect sustain talk.
