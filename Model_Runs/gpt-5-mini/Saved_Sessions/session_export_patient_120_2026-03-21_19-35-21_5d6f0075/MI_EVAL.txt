# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows clear unconditional positive regard and affirms the patient, reducing shame; however, later language becomes more directive and lacks deeper, individualized validation of guilt and conflicted identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Includes accurate reflections and recognizes fear and ambivalence, summarizing feelings well; missed opportunities to probe and deepen understanding of guilt, shame, and identity conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and pregnancy safety, repeatedly returning to concrete medical steps and practical behavior strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers to practice lines and gives options, but the interaction is clinician-led with many supplied solutions rather than eliciting the patient's own ideas or past strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Seeks permission for tools and provides options which support choice, yet early prescriptive directives reduce the overall autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks some factual and preference questions and affirms help‑seeking, but largely provides content and scripts instead of drawing out intrinsic motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Displays some partnership (invites practice, offers choices) but primarily holds the agenda and supplies recommendations instead of co-constructing the plan with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits relevant information and offers concrete steps, yet does not systematically explore or amplify the patient's own change talk (desire, reasons, or confidence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and provides practical scripts to counter peer pressure, but tends to bypass sustain talk rather than exploring or reframing ambivalence with double-sided or complex reflections.
