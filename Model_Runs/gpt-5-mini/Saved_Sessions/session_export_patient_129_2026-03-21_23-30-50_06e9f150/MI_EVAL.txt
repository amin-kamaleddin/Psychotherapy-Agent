# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, using reflective openings and explicit acceptance of Tyler’s privacy needs to create a safe stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections and summaries matched Tyler’s perspective and guarded tone, effectively conveying understanding of his priorities and concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stayed focused on substance-use–related risks and produced a concrete, time-limited harm-reduction plan, but guidance emphasized concealment and risk management rather than exploring long-term goals or discrepancy toward reducing use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative with negotiated decisions (timing and wording of texts) and ongoing solicitation of Tyler’s views, reflecting equal-partner problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Tyler’s choice and control, offering options and honoring privacy preferences while avoiding pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited Tyler’s practical ideas and past strengths, but did not sufficiently probe or amplify intrinsic motivation (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, Confidence) to change substance use itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Tyler as a collaborator and decision-maker, asking how to support his plan and co-creating details together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some pragmatic, harm-reduction change talk and doable steps, but failed to deepen or amplify language about reducing or stopping use long-term (limited DARN-C exploration).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Often accepted sustain talk and at times inadvertently supported it by helping plan concealment strategies, with few double-sided reflections or reframes to shift toward change.
