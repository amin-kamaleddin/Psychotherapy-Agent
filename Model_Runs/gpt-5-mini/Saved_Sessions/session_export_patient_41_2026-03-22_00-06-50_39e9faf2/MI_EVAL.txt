# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with warm validations and normalizing language; no labeling or criticism observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, frequent reflective listening that captures both content and emotion and deepens rapport by normalizing uncertainty.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (the 10‑minute post‑practice step) and ties questions and reflections to concrete implementation strategies without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models an equal partnership by offering options, inviting the client's choices, and integrating those choices into a co‑created, practical plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices, elicits the client's preferences, and affirms those decisions, avoiding pressure and supporting ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the client's motivations and resources (the 'tiny win' and organizational strengths) but could more consistently deepen and amplify change talk and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the work as collaborative, deferring to the client's expertise and co‑constructing solutions that fit the client's routines and strengths.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits useful change talk through specific, small steps and questions about signs of progress, but would benefit from more explicit amplification, reflection, and summary to consolidate motivation and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and redirects to small options effectively, though strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or reframing) were used less often and could further reduce sustain talk's pull.
