# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized concerns with validating statements, creating a nonjudgmental atmosphere that supported disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking by accurately paraphrasing core concerns and barriers; could use more complex empathic reflections to surface deeper emotions (e.g., fear of losing independence or letting family down).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management and alternative risk‑reduction steps, progressing logically from concerns to concrete, time‑bound actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Behaved as an equal partner by soliciting the patient's ideas, building on them, and offering practical tools that the patient could accept or decline.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice with low‑commitment options and language that preserved ownership of decisions, avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out practical solutions and implementation intentions but did less to evoke deeper intrinsic motivations or use scaling questions to strengthen personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—repeatedly invited patient preferences and co‑constructed a plan that positioned the patient as the decision‑maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and translated ideas into doable actions, but missed opportunities to amplify change language and probe importance/confidence to elicit stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoided argument and validated sustain talk, but used few strategic MI techniques (double‑sided reflections, exploring costs of status quo) to actively soften resistance and highlight discrepancy.
