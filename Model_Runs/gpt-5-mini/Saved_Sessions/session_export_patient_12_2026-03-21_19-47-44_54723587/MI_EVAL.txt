# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive with explicit validations and avoidance of blame (e.g., normalizing relapse, offering to stay). These behaviors conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, including safety checks and empathic statements. Some reflections stayed at a surface level and missed opportunities to probe deeper feelings of chronic helplessness and isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on safety, triggers, urge-level, and concrete harm-reduction steps, and ensured follow-through (e.g., staying while pills were sealed). Direction was purposeful and effectively steered toward actionable coping.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint problem-solving by offering choices, checking preferences, and letting the patient lead decisions. The patient’s selections materially shaped the session, indicating strong partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice by offering multiple options, asking permission, and affirming the patient's control over actions. Clinician avoided coercive language and emphasized the patient's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used open questions, scaling, and reinforced the patient's actions to elicit motivation, and highlighted harm-reduction success. Could further draw out underlying values and intrinsic reasons for change with more evocative questions and summaries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance, invited input, offered to stay during a difficult task, and honored the patient's expertise. Language and actions positioned clinician and patient as partners working together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change-relevant language, offered options, and praised actions which supported small commitments. Missed some opportunities to amplify and follow up on change statements to build stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing relapse and redirecting to coping and safety strategies. Rarely used double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledged both sustain and change sides, which could further soften sustain talk.
