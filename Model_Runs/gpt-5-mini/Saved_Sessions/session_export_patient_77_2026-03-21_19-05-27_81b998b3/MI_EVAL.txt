# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates nonjudgmental support, validates feelings, and avoids blaming language. Explicit statements (e.g., “I’m here to listen…”, “no judgment”) demonstrate stable positive regard for the adolescent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that mirrors frustration, exhaustion, and loneliness with timely reflections. Could deepen empathy by exploring underlying emotions and meanings (e.g., identity, fears, peer comparisons) with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays focused on weight-management targets with an early safety check and clear, actionable goals. The clinician consistently guides toward specific behavior changes and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as a joint effort, offers options, and invites rehearsal, showing strong collaborative intent. Interaction sometimes skews to clinician-provided lists, so more elicitation of the patient’s solutions would enhance power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirms choice, presents options, and asks which strategies feel doable, avoiding pressure. Acknowledging skepticism and framing suggestions as choices supports the adolescent’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some action intentions are elicited (e.g., adding protein, selecting days) but much of the session relies on offered strategies. More open questions to draw out personal reasons, values, and confidence would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Overall collaborative stance with invitations to choose and plan together, including rehearsals and permission-seeking. Partnership weakens when the clinician shifts into expert mode and delivers numerous scripted recommendations instead of co-constructing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and reinforces a specific doable step but does not consistently evoke the client’s own motivations or commitment language. Using more strategic open questions and amplifying spontaneous change statements would increase change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Therapist validates doubts and avoids escalating sustain talk, which prevents resistance. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and rolling with resistance mean sustain talk could be softened more actively to shift ambivalence toward change.
