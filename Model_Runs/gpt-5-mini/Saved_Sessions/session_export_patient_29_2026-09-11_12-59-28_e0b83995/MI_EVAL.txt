# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, names both sides of ambivalence and affirms the patient's choices. Tone remains respectful throughout with no shaming or blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflections and shows good perspective-taking about stress and concerns, but often shifts quickly to planning. Could deepen exploration of the patient's emotional nuance (e.g., fears about side effects, feeling 'wound up').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and moves the session toward concrete action steps. Minimal drift with each turn advancing the plan (patch choice, timing, rotation, check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—asks permission, offers options, and negotiates the plan with the patient. Language frames decisions as a joint project rather than an expert monologue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient autonomy by offering choices, accepting decisions, and inviting future adjustments. Reinforces the patient's control over the process and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's ideas and motivations and helps operationalize them, producing concrete change talk. Could enhance evocation by asking deeper value-based questions and soliciting explicit commitment or confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates genuine partnership through shared decision-making and checking the patient's preferences at each step. Co-constructs the plan (site rotation, timing, check-in) with clear power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk and the patient articulates a specific plan to start the patch. Could more often amplify change language and explicitly elicit commitment/confidence (e.g., scaling questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids reinforcing it, then pivots to feasible strategies. Would benefit from more strategic double-sided reflections or summaries to gently resolve ambivalence.
