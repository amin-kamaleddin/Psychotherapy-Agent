# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through permission‑seeking, nonjudgmental language, and explicit acknowledgment of Adrian’s limits and goals. Framed recommendations as practical harm reduction rather than moralizing about cessation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and attunement (e.g., reflecting the infection story and concerns), validating practical fears. Missed deeper exploration of shame/avoidance and more nuanced affective reflections that would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on safer snorting practices and concrete harm‑reduction targets throughout the session. Kept discussion oriented to causes, red flags, supplies, and next steps without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner in problem‑solving by soliciting Adrian’s preferences (mail vs SSP) and co‑constructing the plan for supplies and testing. Invited Adrian to steer next steps and adjusted to his logistical needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Adrian’s autonomy by respecting his choice not to quit and offering options rather than directives, using permission and choice framing. Emphasized his control over decisions like delivery method and what steps to take.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited actionable intentions (not sharing straws, rinsing, wanting vendor info) by asking what felt doable and offering choices. Could have probed deeper into personal reasons, used importance/confidence scaling, or elicited a concrete implementation plan to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed strong partnership by repeatedly asking permission, offering choices, and tailoring resource searches to Adrian’s preferences. Power was clearly shared and the clinician invited Adrian to guide priorities and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk when asking which steps felt doable, leading Adrian to state specific intentions. Did not consistently amplify or follow up to deepen reasons, importance/confidence, or elicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and skillfully reframed the conversation toward harm reduction rather than pressing abstinence. Could further soften sustain talk with double‑sided reflections explicitly acknowledging both the positives of use and the harms experienced.
