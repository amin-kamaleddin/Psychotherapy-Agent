# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard with a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and accurate summaries of practical and emotional content, though reflections could more often probe deeper underlying feelings (e.g., shame, exhaustion).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained focus on actionable targets (childcare, stress, smoking) and offered concrete steps, but could sequence more explicitly toward eliciting commitment and scheduling follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as a clear partnership with co-created plans, shared tasks, and respect for the patient's expertise and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and inviting the patient to tweak scripts and select roles—reinforcing ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician drew out the patient's own solutions and priorities effectively but could use more evocative questions to deepen intrinsic motivation and elicit desire/ability/reasons/commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance—clinician acted as coach not expert fixer, asked about preferences for rehearsal, and jointly developed plans and wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk around childcare and practical steps, but missed opportunities to amplify smoking-reduction successes and elicit stronger commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated barriers and gently redirected toward problem-solving without confronting sustain talk, though more double-sided reflections could better resolve ambivalence and highlight change reasons.
