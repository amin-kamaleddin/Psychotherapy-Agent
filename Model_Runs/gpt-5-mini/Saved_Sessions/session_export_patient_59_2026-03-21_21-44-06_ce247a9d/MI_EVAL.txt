# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating from the opening line onward, normalizing Jamal’s experience and acknowledging his priorities. No criticism or labeling was used, which supported strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries of Jamal’s motives and worries, with checking for understanding. Reflections tended to be paraphrase‑level and missed some opportunities for deeper, feeling‑laden reflections of irritation or impatience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the referral requirement and reducing home conflict with practical, task‑oriented planning. The emphasis was pragmatic and short‑term, with less effort to elicit broader longer‑term goals or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a genuine cooperative exchange with Jamal invited into every decision point and plans co‑constructed based on his preferences. Practical tools were offered only after his agreement, demonstrating authentic teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Jamal’s right to choose and avoided pressuring language by offering options and asking permission. Framed suggestions as optional and consistently respected his control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client‑generated, practical reasons for limited change (avoid drama, probation) and used open questions and a confidence ruler. Evocation focused on immediate instrumental motives and could be strengthened by exploring broader values or longer‑term aims.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shared power and decision‑making, offering explicit choices and involving Jamal in shaping the plan. The clinician deferred to Jamal’s preferences and co‑created doable steps rather than imposing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited useful change language (concrete steps, confidence ruler) and generated some commitment language, but did not consistently reflect or amplify Jamal’s change talk. More deliberate highlighting of strengths and commitments would build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and neutrally reframed Jamal’s minimizations, redirecting to practical planning. Missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to explicitly soften ambivalence and contrast pros/cons.
