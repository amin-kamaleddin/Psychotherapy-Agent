# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicates nonjudgmental acceptance and positive regard, validates ambivalence, and affirms small steps (e.g., the mug-by-keys idea) without blame or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries accurately capture Kenji's perspective and ambivalence, are personalized to his context, and deepen understanding rather than remaining purely factual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear focus on reducing evening alcohol use, progressively narrowing to a concrete implementation intention tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is a joint exploration that co-creates a specific plan, but the clinician often offers multiple clinician-generated options instead of first eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently honors choice and control by offering options, asking which feels doable, and accepting hesitancy without challenge, supporting ownership of the experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits practical motivators and moves Kenji from vague awareness to specific benefits, but could probe deeper values and strengthen explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites preferences and builds the plan from Kenji's responses, yet at times steers with multiple options rather than privileging Kenji's generation of strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and reinforces change talk and concrete steps, generating reasons for change, but misses chances to amplify benefits and ask for confidence or readiness ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and not reinforced, with a redirection to small experiments, though more double-sided reflections or exploration of the emotional meaning could further reduce sustain pull.
