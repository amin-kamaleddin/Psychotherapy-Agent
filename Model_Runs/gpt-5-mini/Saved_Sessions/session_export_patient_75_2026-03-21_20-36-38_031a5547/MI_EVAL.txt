# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validates the patient's fears while avoiding blame. Offers nonjudgmental support and practical help throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing concerns and priorities well. Could deepen empathic attunement with more complex reflections about day-to-day emotional impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on asthma management and safe inhaler step-down, repeatedly returning to actionable goals. Session stays goal-directed with practical next steps (checklist, restart criteria, monitoring).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as cooperative, invites input, asks permission, and offers rehearsal and formatting to fit the patient's needs. Materials and plans are shaped by the patient's requests, supporting joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors patient choice by offering options, asking preferences, and avoiding pressure to pursue a specific course. Emphasizes safe, clinician-guided changes and respects patient control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivations, concerns, and concrete conditions that would make change acceptable (monitoring, restart rules). Could further probe values, past successes, confidence, and use scaling or commitment questions to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance, invites co-design of the checklist, and frequently asks permission to proceed. At times the clinician offered prebuilt solutions rather than more explicitly co-constructing wording or priorities with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and supports change-oriented language and concrete alternatives, prompting the patient to express willingness to raise options with their doctor. Could more consistently amplify, summarize reasons for change, and ask about importance/confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk without reinforcing it and redirects toward safety-focused planning and alternatives. Would be stronger with more deliberate double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while emphasizing change reasons.
