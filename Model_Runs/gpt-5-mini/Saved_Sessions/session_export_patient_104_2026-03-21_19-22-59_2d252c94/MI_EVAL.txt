# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, validated Nadine’s exhaustion, and normalized infant sleep patterns. They avoided blame and explicitly acknowledged the difficulty of the situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician used accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking (e.g., asking the baby’s age, reflecting exhaustion and worry). Reassurance and practical support were offered in an empathic, understanding manner.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained tightly focused on baby sleep and Nadine’s rest, repeatedly returning to these target behaviors. The psychiatrist translated the discussion into clear, actionable steps (breathing, grounding, nap swaps, scripts).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative: the clinician invited Nadine to try exercises together, adjusted interventions to her preferences, and co-created wording for texts and partner requests. The clinician repeatedly sought Nadine’s input, making it joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were offered, permission was asked before exercises, and the clinician avoided pressuring language. Nadine was allowed to choose techniques and tailor scripts, reinforcing her sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some intrinsic motivation was evoked by encouraging small tools and noting that tiny changes can help, but broader elicitation of values, reasons, and confidence was limited. More open-ended questions to draw out personal motivations and past successes would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally adopted a collaborative stance, asking permission and offering choices, but at times provided long lists of tips that felt more directive. Partnership could be strengthened by inviting Nadine to generate and prioritize solutions together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician created opportunities for change talk, reinforced small steps, and elicited commitment to try doable strategies. To deepen change talk they could more frequently amplify and explore Nadine’s desires, reasons, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk, used normalization and reframing, and offered boundary language and concrete supports. There were missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while pivoting toward change.
