# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and validated the client’s suffering with multiple empathic statements and affirmations, avoiding blame or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking that mirrored emotional intensity and stayed with affect, helping the patient feel heard and remain engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on suicidal ideation and imminent safety with thorough risk assessment and targeted, pragmatic safety planning while allowing space for empathy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as teamwork—inviting preferences, co-writing messages, and negotiating timing and involvement while sharing clinical expertise and privileging the patient's choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and agency by offering options, asking permission, and explaining/negotiating limits so the client retained a sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited immediate motivation for safety and highlighted reasons for living (e.g., mother, past coping) but focused mainly on short-term behaviors and could probe deeper values and longer-term motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance throughout—asking permission, offering choices, and co-constructing the plan rather than imposing decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced small change statements and concrete commitments (moving pills, grounding, texting Ana), though there were missed opportunities to elicit more elaborate reasons for staying and stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by acknowledging pain and redirecting to safety and coping, but could strengthen use of double-sided reflections to contrast sustain versus change talk.
