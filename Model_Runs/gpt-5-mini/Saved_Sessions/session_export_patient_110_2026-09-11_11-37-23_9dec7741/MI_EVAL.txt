# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and nonjudgmental regard, normalizing and validating the patient’s choices. Uses summaries and positive framing without labeling or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, paraphrasing goals and ambivalence into actionable choices. Reflections are more practical than affect-focused and do not fully explore underlying safety worries or deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management with concrete, behaviorally specific options and a first-week plan. Avoids tangents and steers each turn toward doable action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine partnership by offering phrasing alternatives, co-designing supports, and following the patient’s lead. Clinician contributes expertise as options rather than directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice by presenting multiple options and asking which fits best, then respecting the patient’s selected plan. Encourages the patient to craft his own words and decide next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation tied to the patient’s values (retirement, activity) and produces concrete change plans. Could deepen evocation by probing the meaning of those values, exploring ambivalence about long-term meds, and eliciting confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co-constructed with repeated invitations for preference and refinement, demonstrating power-sharing and respect for the patient’s expertise. Clinician asks permission before offering phrasing and lets the patient choose and tweak.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces change talk, asks for specific first steps, and practices commitment language with the patient. Missed opportunities include amplifying and quantifying commitment/confidence (e.g., scaling questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and consistently redirects toward feasible change steps, reframing concerns in terms of retirement goals. Did not explicitly use double-sided reflections or explore minimization as a target to resolve ambivalence.
