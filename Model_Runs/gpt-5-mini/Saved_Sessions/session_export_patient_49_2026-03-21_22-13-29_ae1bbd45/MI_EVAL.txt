# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used a warm, nonjudgmental tone and explicit positive regard throughout, validating Hana's concerns and framing setbacks as manageable. There was no shaming or labeling and choices were affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were frequent and accurate, naming Hana's worries and context. Deeper probing of emotional nuance (e.g., how social smoking or potential health loss feels) would strengthen empathic work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on cutting back/quitting with concrete behavioral targets, scaling questions, and a structured plan and follow-up. Conversation consistently returned to actionable steps for smoking change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was clearly co‑created with Hana choosing start day, strategies, and rewards, and the clinician solicited and incorporated her barriers and preferences. Language framed the work as joint and equal‑partnered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly asked permission, offered options rather than directives, and explicitly deferred to Hana's preferences about aids and check‑ins. Flexibility for adjustments was encouraged, supporting her sense of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited motivations and capacities using rulers and concrete reasons, drawing out preparatory change talk and realistic steps. More open questions linking change to Hana's values and systematic amplification would deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly client-centered and collaborative, with repeated invitations for Hana to choose specifics and direct the plan. The clinician communicated that expertise about her life resided with Hana.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited importance/confidence, asked what made Hana a '6' and reinforced her own ideas and small steps toward change. Additional elicitation and amplification of Hana's personal reasons/values would have multiplied change talk further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without judgment but often shifted quickly to planning rather than using double‑sided reflections or strategic summaries to explicitly surface ambivalence. More deliberate softening techniques would better reduce sustain talk.
