# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating ambivalence and normalizing worries. Warmth and respect were maintained throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely, reflective listening that tracked Lukas’s feelings and concerns. Reflections verified understanding and allowed the patient to elaborate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on peer-influenced alcohol use and progressed logically from exploration to a specific, achievable plan. Interventions repeatedly tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited co-creation of solutions and used inclusive, partnership-oriented language while avoiding lecturing. The plan reflected Lukas’s preferences and constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and honoring preferences. Framed plans as Lukas’s decisions, reducing pressure and reactance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited motivations, confidence ratings, and a self-generated plan, but sometimes provided many solutions rather than repeatedly drawing out Lukas’s own ideas. More value-focused, evocative questions could have strengthened intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making and treated Lukas as the expert on his situation, repeatedly inviting his preferences. Collaborative language and joint planning were evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk (e.g., specific goal, confidence ratings, picking an event) but did not consistently amplify or deepen brief change statements. Could use more strategic reflections and evocative follow-ups to strengthen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed sustain talk nonjudgmentally and used double-sided language implicitly, but offering a long list of strategies sometimes shifted focus away from exploring the pull of friendships. More explicit double-sided reflections and eliciting Lukas’s own responses to sustain talk would strengthen this domain.
