# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating the patient's perspective and choices without shaming. The clinician affirmed difficulty and autonomy throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing concerns and ambivalence effectively. Could deepen empathic reflections by naming emotional nuance (e.g., fear/anxiety about family history and lapses).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on reducing alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability and guided the session toward a specific, measurable plan. Planning and barriers were addressed to keep momentum toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in a genuine two-way partnership, routinely asking preferences and co-constructing the plan and role-plays. The clinician adjusted to the patient's requests and invited feedback throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding pressure when giving advice or scripts. This reinforced the patient's sense of ownership over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited importance and confidence ratings and encouraged patient-generated strategies and supports. At times the clinician moved quickly to offer scripts rather than further amplifying intrinsic motivations or linking to values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by distributing expertise, inviting choices, and asking how to proceed during skill practice. The clinician and patient co-created plans and rehearsal approaches.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated ideas into a concrete weekly plan. Could more consistently reflect and amplify the patient's own change language and elicit stronger commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward protective strategies using neutral, acceptance-based responses. Could more often use MI softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections, explicit discrepancy) to acknowledge sustain talk while guiding toward change.
