# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear unconditional positive regard from the start with nonjudgmental language and frequent validation, avoiding blame or moralizing. Multiple affirmations respected Milan's choices and constraints throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrase that captured ambivalence and concrete concerns. Could deepen affective reflections to draw out underlying emotions or values (e.g., belonging, anxiety, shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on social drinking and moved toward concrete small changes tied to school functioning. Could strengthen by explicitly linking steps to Milan's core goals and setting a specific measurable plan or follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative; the clinician invited Milan's input at every step, validated choices, and rehearsed strategies together as joint problem‑solving. Milan's ideas clearly shaped the session outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Milan's control and choice, asking permission before offering suggestions and emphasizing noncoercive language. Options were offered rather than directives, reinforcing the patient's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Milan's reasons and small commitment language and rehearsed client-generated plans. Could probe more for intrinsic motivators and amplify change talk to strengthen desire and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Milan as the expert in his life, co‑creating plans and asking which strategies felt doable. Power-sharing was evident with decisions clearly influenced by Milan's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk by asking about benefits, warning signs, and specific strategies, which produced concrete commitment language. Missed chances to amplify value‑based reasons and explore confidence more deeply to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation and used double‑sided reflections and normalization to reduce sustain talk and redirect attention to early signs. Could more strategically reflect and summarize change language to further decrease sustain talk and highlight discrepancies.
