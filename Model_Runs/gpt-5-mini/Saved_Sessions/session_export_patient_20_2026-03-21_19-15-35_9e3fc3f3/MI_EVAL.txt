# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized the client's experience without judgment or labeling, creating a respectful tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used solid reflective listening and perspective-taking that captured ambivalence and some emotion; could deepen rapport by offering more complex, affect-focused reflections exploring guardedness and family tension.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and moved the conversation toward specific, measurable strategies (dry nights, limits, scripts).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan by soliciting input, checking preferences, and tailoring suggestions to the client's choices, demonstrating strong joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly emphasized choice and control, framed changes as experiments, and offered multiple options without pressuring the client to commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the client's own motivations (fewer hangovers, less tension) and invited experimentation, but leaned toward clinician-generated solutions; more prompts for client-generated reasons and scaling questions would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shared control effectively by asking permission before advice and involving the client in selecting nights and tactics, reinforcing a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced meaningful change talk (desire for fewer hangovers, choosing dry nights) but could more actively amplify spontaneous change statements and probe for importance, confidence, and concrete commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and gently redirected sustain talk toward small experiments, yet used few double-sided or strategic reflections that would further reduce sustain talk intensity.
