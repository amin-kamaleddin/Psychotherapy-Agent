# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes Jordan's experience, reducing blame. Occasionally shifts quickly into problem-solving and offers limited individualized affirmations which prevents a maximal expression of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and validates Jordan's feelings, accurately summarizing ambivalence. Reflections tend to remain surface-level and are often followed by immediate practical solutions rather than deeper exploration of guilt, shame, or social meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on shoplifting/relapse prevention with concrete, behaviorally specific planning and goals. Questions and interventions repeatedly return to triggers, timing, refusal scripts, support persons, and a two-weekend goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently checks in, asks Jordan to choose steps, and invites rehearsal, supporting an equitable working relationship. However, the clinician often leads with pre-made plans and scripts rather than co-creating options from Jordan's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices, asks consent for planning and rehearsal, and invites Jordan's selection of steps, which supports autonomy. Many recommendations are nonetheless presented in a directive way before eliciting Jordan's preferred strategies or wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment and change talk through readiness rulers and step selection, producing specific commitments. Relies heavily on offering solutions instead of drawing out Jordan's own reasons, values, past successes, or confidence, missing deeper evocation opportunities.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative elements like asking which steps to start and offering role-play, but also assumes an expert role by presenting a pre-made relapse plan and multiple scripts. This mix results in an inconsistent partnership where the agenda is often clinician-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language and concrete commitments (e.g., two-weekend goal, refusal line). Misses chances to selectively reinforce and deepen desire, reasons, and ability—opting instead for advice, scripts, and solutions rather than amplifying change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk (peer pressure, temptation) and offers realistic tools to reduce its impact. Does not consistently use MI strategies like double-sided or reframing reflections to explore ambivalence, often countering sustain talk with directives.
