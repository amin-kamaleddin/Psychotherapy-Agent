# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opened with explicit unconditional positive regard and maintained a nonjudgmental, validating tone, normalizing Mai's concerns and affirming sensible safety steps while offering supportive help like drafting texts and role‑play.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective‑taking were accurate and concise, anticipating information needs, but the clinician focused more on logistics than deeper emotional concerns (e.g., worry or trust issues) that could have been explored further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, consistent focus on understanding and safely starting the statin with all interventions tightly linked to actionable next steps (texts, calls, monitoring), without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative—summaries, question lists, SMS and voicemail scripts were co‑created with patient edits implemented in real time, reflecting joint problem solving and equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strongly supported choice and control by offering options, using permission language before drafting, and emphasizing that Mai decide wording and next steps, avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited concrete intentions and a readiness rating, but missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring personal values or linking the medication to Mai's broader goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session demonstrated strong power‑sharing with decisions co‑constructed and frequent invitations for the patient to specify wording and preferences, conveying respect for Mai's expertise about her own needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively elicited and amplified into a clear plan (timing, juice swap, call for pain), though the clinician could have probed importance and confidence to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not reinforced, with neutral redirection toward information and practical supports, but more MI‑strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) could have better softened lingering resistance.
