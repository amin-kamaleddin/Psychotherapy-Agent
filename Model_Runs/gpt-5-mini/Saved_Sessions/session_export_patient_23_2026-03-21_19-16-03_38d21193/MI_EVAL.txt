# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and validates the patient’s experience, using nonjudgmental language throughout. Warmth and normalization of ambivalence are evident in multiple reflections and affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., reflecting mixed feelings and nervousness) are present, but some reflections are brief and the clinician occasionally moves quickly into advice. More complex reflections and deeper exploration of affect would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol use with concrete, behaviorally specific guidance (goal, schedule, tracking, substitutes, safety) and a set follow-up. The clinician balances empathy with a purposeful plan and a Monday check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co‑constructed and egalitarian, repeatedly soliciting patient preferences and tailoring interventions (texts, check‑in format, one-liners). The clinician frames work as joint problem‑solving and adapts suggestions to the patient’s life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offers choices (start date, message vs call, one‑liner options) and honors the patient’s partial goals, reinforcing control over decisions. Language and options avoid coercion and support flexibility.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits motivation with target questions, a confidence ruler, and invitations to choose strategies, but much content in the middle is clinician-delivered. More open questions to draw out the patient’s own reasons, values, and past successes would increase evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent partnership is demonstrated by asking what the patient wants, offering options, and co‑creating the plan (tailored texts, brief check‑ins). The clinician treats the patient as the expert on their life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces change talk (drinks/week target, confidence ruler, affirming patient ideas) and supports ownership of strategies. To improve, selectively reflecting and amplifying client change statements and eliciting more specific reasons and commitment would help.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and redirects ambivalence toward achievable harm‑reduction (compromise goals, substitutes, delay tactics). Using double‑sided reflections and more strategic elicitation could further weaken sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
