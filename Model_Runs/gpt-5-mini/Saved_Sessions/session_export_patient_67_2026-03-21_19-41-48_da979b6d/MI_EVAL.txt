# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard by normalizing slips, validating limits, and offering choices, creating a warm nonjudgmental tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used timely reflections and validation that matched pacing and acknowledged concerns, but reflections were often brief and pragmatic rather than deepening the client's emotional ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol reduction with an appropriate safety check, concrete skills, harm‑reduction strategies, and a concrete follow‑up plan tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—solicited preferences and incorporated client additions—but some exchanges were clinician‑led and would benefit from pausing more to invite the client's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by offering multiple options, framing choices, acknowledging limits, and reinforcing the client's ownership of decisions and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited emerging motivation and commitments (tracking, check‑ins) but could strengthen evocation by asking more values‑based or importance/confidence questions to deepen self‑generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative partnership by treating the client as the expert, co‑creating the tracker, offering choices, and checking in about preferences throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced early change talk and staged small achievable steps, but missed chances to amplify spontaneous change language and use scaling/confidence probes to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing doubts and offering contingency plans, though more double‑sided reflections would better acknowledge both sides and help resolve ambivalence.
