# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth (affirmations, normalizing, avoiding blame). Language and actions reinforced respect and support throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and emotional attunement with frequent paraphrases and empathic summaries. One minor paraphrase slightly shifted focus, but overall the patient felt heard and understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on evening smoking and guided the conversation toward a concrete, actionable plan. Used scaling questions and collaboratively arranged specific follow‑up without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked in genuine partnership by soliciting the patient's priorities and preferences and integrating them into the plan (alarm, hide ashtray, text wording/time). The patient’s ideas consistently shaped next steps and follow‑up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice, offered options, and asked permission before sharing advice, honoring the patient’s decision not to discuss alcohol. Language and actions reinforced patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's own motivations and resources using scaling questions and targeted prompts ("What would it take to move one point higher?"). Client‑generated reasons were used effectively to build the plan and elicit commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invited the patient to direct choices and co‑constructed plans and follow‑up (choice of strategies, text tone, check‑in format). The clinician shared power and treated the patient as the expert on what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk through scaling questions, option choices, reflections, and summaries that produced a specific, doable plan. Follow‑ups and coping strategies were used to deepen and consolidate change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately avoided confronting minimization and respected the patient's boundary around alcohol, refocusing on smoking and priorities. Missed a few opportunities to use double‑sided reflections or explicitly link sustain concerns to the patient's values to tip ambivalence further.
