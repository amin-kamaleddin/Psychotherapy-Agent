# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, with repeated validations and normalizations that reduced shame and honored the client's reluctance. Permission statements and acceptance of minimal change fostered unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and summaries matched the client's tone and helped him move from terse replies to slightly more elaborated responses. The clinician demonstrated strong perspective-taking without minimizing the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on a specific behavioral target (a low-effort 10-minute break) and consistently steered the session toward concrete, measurable steps. Follow-ups and scheduling reinforced the directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction read as cooperative and respectful, soliciting preferences and following the client's pace. However, frequent clinician-supplied instructions and checklists slightly reduced the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and minimized pressure with multiple options and clear statements that no change was required. Inviting the client to pick times and methods preserved volition and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked small behavioral commitment and amplified the shift from 'meh' to 'okay,' but offered limited exploration of intrinsic motivators, values, or longer-term reasons for change. More open elicitation of personal meaning would strengthen sustained motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected the client's input and invited choices, supporting a collaborative alliance. The clinician's tendency to provide detailed step-by-step instructions reduced opportunities for joint co-design of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced small pieces of change talk and asked for doable steps, celebrating incremental movement. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk by exploring why the shift mattered or what felt different about the experiment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk without arguing, which avoided reinforcing resistance. The clinician rarely used double-sided or complex reflections that might more strategically shift sustain talk toward change.
