# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental, supportive tone, normalizing frustrations and showing clear respect throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking by capturing constraints and frustration, but reflections are brief and the clinician moves quickly to solutions rather than exploring deeper feelings or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on a single concrete behavior (one snack swap) and repeatedly brings the conversation back to practical steps and the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as genuine teamwork with joint creation of the plan and frequent invitations for the patient to shape details, honoring the patient's preferences and decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering multiple options, honoring no‑alarm/no‑check‑in preferences, and asking which approach feels easiest for the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits practical motivation and commitment language (confidence ruler, natural cues), but focuses more on logistics than drawing out deeper intrinsic reasons or values for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the patient as the expert in their life, co-constructing decisions (snack choice, cues, backups) and repeatedly soliciting the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk with concrete options and a confidence ruler, though could better amplify the patient's own reasons and broader meaning for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward small, doable changes with sensible backups, but would benefit from more explicit double-sided reflections that acknowledge both sides of ambivalence.
