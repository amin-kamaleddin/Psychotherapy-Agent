# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respected the patient's priorities, repeatedly validating his choice not to discuss smoking. Avoided shaming or pressure, creating a safe, nonjudgmental tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with reflective summaries and attention to affect and fatigue. Mirrors and concise reflections showed strong listening and understanding of the patient's stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on the patient-chosen goal of restoring chewing and arranging dental care, offering concrete options and securing an appointment. Maintained pragmatic momentum toward a concrete action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a partner by soliciting the patient's preferences, adapting scripts to his wording, and practicing role-plays per his request. The patient's choices materially shaped the session and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported control by asking permission, offering choices, and honoring the patient's decision not to discuss smoking. Avoided pressuring language and reinforced ownership of decisions and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked motivation and planning for dental care, using importance/confidence ratings and securing action. However, evocation around smoking was minimal; a brief permission-based probe could have left a seed without breaching boundaries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Established genuine shared decision-making with co-constructed scripts and repeated checks for preference, demonstrating power-sharing. Followed the patient's lead and incorporated his exact requests into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited change talk about dental care (importance, confidence, concrete plan, appointment booking) and reinforced commitment. Did not attempt to evoke any change talk about smoking, which respected boundaries but missed a brief permission-based exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and sidestepped arguments by respecting the patient's request not to discuss smoking and redirecting to dental priorities. This approach reduced defensiveness and preserved the alliance.
