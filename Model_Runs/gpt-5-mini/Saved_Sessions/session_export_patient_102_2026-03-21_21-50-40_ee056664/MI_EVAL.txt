# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and respect, using validating phrases and normalizing ambivalence without shaming. Tone and repeated affirmations supported a warm, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and summaries of ambivalence. Could deepen empathy by exploring underlying feelings and the meaning of drinking (e.g., faith or social identity) more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on drinking at social gatherings and logically moved from exploration to concrete planning. Interventions stayed tied to relevant consequences (practice, tired mornings, faith) and implementation intentions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt jointly problem-solving with frequent invitations for Milan to choose strategies and co-creation of a practical plan (buddy, text, rehearsals). Clinician consistently solicited input and worked alongside Milan to build the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported autonomy by asking permission before advising, offering options, and framing plans as low-pressure tests. Language reinforced Milan’s ownership and avoided pressuring him toward a specific choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Milan’s own motivations and plans using open questions, scaling, and eliciting specific doable options and rehearsal. Clinician avoided supplying reasons for change and amplified client-generated strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership with permission-based advice and co-created strategies, inviting Milan’s input on choices and wording. Score not maximal because several concrete tips modestly shifted direction toward clinician-suggested actions, though given with permission.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, confidence scaling, and rehearsals, and helped generate realistic steps. Missed opportunities to more strongly amplify fleeting change statements to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and neutrally reflected minimizations while exploring discrepancy, effectively softening sustain talk. Could increase use of strategic double-sided or amplified reflections to further reduce sustain talk.
