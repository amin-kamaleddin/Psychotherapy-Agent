# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's feelings with warm, nonjudgmental language. Concerns about finances and childcare are acknowledged rather than minimized, modeling strong acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality empathic listening captures both content and affect, using reflections that integrate ambivalence and fear. Complex reflections and summaries show deep perspective-taking and understanding of the patient's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction stays clearly focused on engaging medical care and progressively narrows to a concrete, achievable action (short call, script, practice). Each turn reinforces movement toward that specific behavior while preserving autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician functions as an equal partner, co-constructing the plan, offering role-play, and soliciting the patient's input at each step. Proposals are adjusted based on the patient's responses, demonstrating genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasizes choice and the patient's expertise about her life, offering multiple options and asking which feels most doable. There is no pressure or directive language, supporting ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicits the patient's own motivations and values (wanting to be there for her child) and draws out concrete change ideas from the patient. Intrinsic motives and practical self-efficacy are amplified rather than supplied by the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the conversation as a collaborative exploration and treats the patient as the expert on her situation. Options are presented and chosen together, reinforcing shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is actively elicited through values-based questions and reinforced with invitations to plan small steps (script, call, practice). The clinician builds on the patient's commitment language and develops practical next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged with reflective, double-sided statements and not argued with, then reframed into manageable alternatives (phone triage, brief script). This approach respects concerns while redirecting toward feasible actions.
