# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with warm, validating, nonjudgmental language; normalizes concerns and respects Margaret’s priorities throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good understanding and accurate paraphrasing of Margaret’s situation and values, but largely stays at the content/goal level and misses opportunities for deeper emotional or double-sided reflections of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on reducing alcohol to protect balance and independence, guiding the conversation toward concrete planning and safety considerations without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—invites input, offers multiple options, solicits preferences, and co-constructs the plan (start date, scripts, coping strategies) with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and ownership by asking permission, framing suggestions as options, and offering help only if the patient wants it, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses evocative questions that elicit concrete change language and commitment (steps, start date, confidence), though could more systematically amplify and summarize the patient’s reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates a strong partnership stance—shares power, defers to the patient’s expertise about her life, and adapts materials to her preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and shapes change talk leading to specific, doable steps and a commitment plan, but would strengthen outcomes by reflecting and selectively amplifying the client’s change language more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids argument and gently redirects sustain talk with neutral information and permission, though could more deliberately use double-sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence and soften sustain talk.
