# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Richard’s honesty and honoring his boundaries (no lectures, no wife involvement). Demonstrated steady unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, validating the emotional load, but missed opportunities for deeper affect-focused reflections (e.g., naming anger, frustration, or feeling coerced).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on smoking and progressively moved to a concrete, measurable behavior change (7‑day porch trial, start day, coping strategies, follow-up). Maintained clear direction without overriding client autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner—asking preferences, negotiating logistics, and allowing Richard to shape the plan and follow-up details. The plan was clearly co-created and client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly framed choices and emphasized client control, offering options and honoring limits (no nagging, time-limited trial). Reinforced that the plan and any adjustments were Richard’s decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited Richard’s own motivations and resources using evocative questions and scaling, and translated those into a self-endorsed experiment. Avoided imposing reasons and invited the patient to generate solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance by repeatedly checking what felt realistic and negotiating terms of the plan and midweek check-in. Deferred to Richard’s preferences and co-created the strategy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk with open questions, reflections, and scaling, and translated ambivalence into a concrete trial, but could have further explored and amplified marriage‑linked change talk. More targeted follow-up on reasons for change would strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and rolled with resistance, offering nonjudgmental options and honoring sustain talk, but missed chances for more double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge both sustain and change sides. Strategic reflections could better pivot sustain talk toward change.
