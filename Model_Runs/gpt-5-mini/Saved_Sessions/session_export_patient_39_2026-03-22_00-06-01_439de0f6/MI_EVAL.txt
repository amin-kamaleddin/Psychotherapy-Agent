# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys strong unconditional positive regard with frequent normalizing and nonjudgmental language, validating Elias's hopes and cautions. A brief added normalization about discouragement after a single try could further reduce shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently uses accurate, timely reflections that integrate content and affect and track shifting emotion. Reflections deepen understanding rather than merely repeating facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, repeated focus on the specific target behavior (ten-minute porch sit) and guides the conversation from values to a small, testable action. Questions and reflections consistently return to the plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as collaborative problem-solving, asking for preferences and co-constructing the plan so Elias's input directly shapes the trial. Language invites shared decision-making rather than clinician-driven instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice by offering options, honoring Elias's control, and validating stopping if it causes pain; the time-limited trial preserves agency. Suggestions are framed as optional rather than prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Elias's intrinsic motives and anticipated benefits and converts them into a concrete behavioral experiment. Could occasionally deepen elicitation of confidence/commitment (e.g., brief scaling) to further strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions Elias as the expert and invites his input at every step, offering options and power-sharing in decision-making. The chosen plan (timer, breakfast anchor, one-day test) reflects genuine partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies multiple strands of change talk and secures a concrete trial commitment (one ten-minute sit). To reach top score, the clinician could more proactively amplify and summarize client change language and use brief scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and reframes concerns into manageable testing, which prevents escalation. However, sustain talk is often reflected verbatim and there are limited strategic moves (e.g., double-sided or selectively reflecting change language) to shift the balance toward change.
