# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalization (e.g., “I accept where you are,” “that mix makes total sense”), repeatedly reassuring safety and pacing to reduce blame and support the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used strong, accurate reflections that captured both concrete consequences and emotion (e.g., waking up gasping, feeling 'more normal') and built on them rather than shifting topic, increasing felt understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on asthma self-management and a concrete target behavior (two-week symptom/trigger diary), steadily moving toward a practical plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as equal partners by soliciting input at each decision point (triggers, template format, reminders) and adapting the plan to Linda’s preferences, demonstrating genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Linda’s choice and control by offering options, asking her to choose formats and triggers, and honoring her rule that nothing changes without her doctor’s approval.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited useful change talk and self-generated plans (diary, triggers), but could have probed deeper for intrinsic motivators, values, and confidence (importance/confidence scaling) before offering solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-created the plan with collaborative language and frequent invitations for Linda’s input (e.g., drafting a one-line example together), sharing power throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (sleep, walking, playing with dog) and developed a small-step plan, but missed opportunities to deepen reasons-for-change and elicit stronger commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated and normalized sustain talk without arguing and redirected to low-risk steps, but often reflected worries verbatim rather than using double-sided reflections or emphasizing change-oriented statements to resolve ambivalence.
