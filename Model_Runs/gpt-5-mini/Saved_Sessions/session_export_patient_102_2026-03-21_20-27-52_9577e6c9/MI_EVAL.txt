# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Milan’s stance, avoiding judgment and framing harm-reduction options rather than pressuring for abstinence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Displayed strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately paraphrasing emotions and using summaries to check understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on weekend drinking and moved toward a specific, measurable plan with a follow-up, but did not sufficiently link the plan to longer-term goals or personal values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative—clinician solicited preferences, used inclusive language, and co-constructed the plan with Milan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported client choice throughout by offering options, praising realistic goals, and avoiding pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical change talk (pros/cons, confidence ruler) and produced concrete intentions, but relied more on offering strategies than drawing out intrinsic motives or long-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed the work as a shared endeavor, invited Milan to choose strategies, and built the plan together, positioning him as the expert on his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk and a specific plan with confidence ratings, but could have more strongly amplified spontaneous change statements and explored deeper personal motivators.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and honored resistance, skillfully redirecting sustain talk into nonjudgmental, practical harm-reduction problem-solving that reduced reactance.
