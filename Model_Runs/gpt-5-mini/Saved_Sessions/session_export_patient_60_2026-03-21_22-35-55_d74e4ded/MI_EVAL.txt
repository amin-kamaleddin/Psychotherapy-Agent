# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicates nonjudgmental, supportive regard, using explicit acceptance and validating language that creates a safe tone for disclosure. Priorities were affirmed and blaming language was avoided.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrates accurate, sustained perspective-taking through complex reflections and summaries that capture the client's meaning and build rapport. Empathic reflections consistently acknowledge stress and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing risk while preserving the client's social life, repeatedly returning to concrete, behaviorally specific strategies. The conversation progresses logically from values to concrete implementation steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician works as an equal partner, asking for preferences and co-developing practical steps rather than imposing solutions. The client is invited to shape the plan and decisions throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and pace are explicitly emphasized and pressure is avoided, reinforcing the client's sense of control. The clinician repeatedly invites the client to select and design the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits client-generated ideas and increases readiness (about 70%→80%), but could probe deeper into internal motives or discrepancies to strengthen autonomous motivation and commitment language. More linking to long-term goals would deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly shares power and frames the interaction as collaborative, repeatedly inviting the client's input and choices. Co-construction of the plan is evident in iterative questions and checks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits concrete change talk and actionable steps (roommate plan, soda, leaving early) that increase readiness. Missed opportunities include deeper probes into desire, ability, and confidence to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to practical risk-reduction strategies, preventing escalation. However, more advanced softening (double-sided reflections or selective reflection to expose discrepancy) could further reduce sustain talk.
