# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validated the patient's views, and repeatedly offered options rather than directives to support engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and attention to details (drinking, non‑smoker, low stress), but reflections were mostly factual and did not frequently explore underlying feelings or the meaning of the importance rating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on blood pressure and related behaviors and translated the discussion into concrete, actionable steps and materials.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—asked for preferences, revised materials with the patient, and co‑created the BP log and question card as shared work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices, asked which options were most useful, and honored the patient's decisions, clearly fostering a sense of control and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used evocation techniques like an importance scale but did not sufficiently probe values, past successes, or discrepancies to draw out stronger internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the interaction as a partnership—co‑constructed plans, honored patient input, and shared decision making throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some mild change talk (importance rating, agreement to a log), but did not systematically amplify or explore those statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and accepted minimizing statements, but missed opportunities to use double‑sided reflections or gentle reframing to shift sustain talk toward change.
