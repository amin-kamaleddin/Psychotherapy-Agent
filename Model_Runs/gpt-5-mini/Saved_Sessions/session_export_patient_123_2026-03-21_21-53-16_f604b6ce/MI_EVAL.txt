# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validation (e.g., honoring the patient's wish not to review the screening) and reflected strengths, creating a warm nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used strong, accurate reflective listening that captured both content and affect, helping the patient feel understood and comfortable sharing specifics (homework timing, transition preference).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, actionable focus on establishing a homework routine with concrete steps and follow‑up, though the clinician did not briefly assess any link to the referral/screening when permission‑based exploration might have been appropriate.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑constructed the plan by offering options and inviting the patient to choose supports and specifics, ensuring decisions were driven by the patient's preferences rather than clinician prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, avoided pressuring language, and normalized imperfect adherence, reinforcing the patient's ownership of decisions and flexibility in the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's values (doing well, keeping college options open) and patient‑generated solutions, but could have deepened intrinsic motivation by more explicitly linking the routine to long‑term goals and exploring confidence/barriers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently worked as a collaborator, inviting input (brainstorming options, selecting a start day/back‑up) and sharing decision‑making with the patient throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced practical change talk and commitment (specific plan, start day, back‑up), but missed opportunities to further amplify intrinsic motivation (e.g., 0–10 importance/confidence scaling or deeper reasons why college goals matter for this plan).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Addressed sustain talk nonjudgmentally with normalization and problem‑solving, which maintained momentum toward change, but could have used double‑sided reflections or probed past barriers to more fully soften resistance.
