# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, explicitly honoring the patient’s preferences (e.g., ‘‘no nagging’’) and normalizing his perspective throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, capturing both the patient’s factual history and felt experience to validate his concerns and preferences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking and progressively narrows the conversation toward a specific, measurable trial (swap after-dinner cigarette for a walk, no indoor smoking, shoes rule).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently co-constructs the plan by asking permission to brainstorm, offering options rather than prescribing, and repeatedly inviting the patient’s input and choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms and protects the patient’s autonomy by highlighting choice, offering options, and following the patient’s lead rather than pushing for quitting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s own motivations and strategies with questions that draw out intrinsic reasons and personally meaningful, feasible actions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a collaborative, power-sharing process, treating the patient as the expert and leaving final decisions to him while jointly planning next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk through targeted open questions and reflections, drawing out concrete, actionable statements and a specific trial plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully rolls with sustain talk and avoids confrontation, using validating language to reduce defensiveness and redirect toward small, patient-driven change options.
