# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, repeatedly framed the patient as the expert and respected her limits. Normalized a low‑key approach and avoided blame even when the patient was defensive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflective listening that labeled affect and linked it to behavior, helping the patient move from minimal responses to concrete planning. Reflections mirrored subtle cues (safety, calm, control) without pushing beyond her comfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, behaviorally defined target (staying out of fights/feeling safer) and tied questions and reflections back to that goal. Resisted drifting and kept the session focused on an achievable, near‑term plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like true teamwork with inclusive language and joint brainstorming of supports. The plan was built from the patient's input, integrating her strengths and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed choice and control, offering options rather than directives and framing the plan as the patient's to try. This preserved agency and minimized reactance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation from within the patient through open questions and reflections, drawing out goals and past strengths. Reflected and amplified the patient's own reasons for change and elicited a concrete commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely handed expertise to the patient and invited her preferences, demonstrating power‑sharing in decision making. Repeatedly asked what she preferred and built the plan around her choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited, narrowed, and amplified change talk, moving from minimal responses to specific planning and a near‑term commitment. Reflections and questions reinforced desired outcomes (safety, calm, control).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by not arguing or pressing, instead pivoting to low‑effort, client‑centered options. Used reflections that acknowledged limits to redirect toward change without invalidation.
