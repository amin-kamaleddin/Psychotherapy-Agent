# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support through validations and affirmations. The clinician avoided criticism and repeatedly acknowledged barriers and the patient’s perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, frequent reflective listening and perspective-taking that matched the patient’s language and emotional tone. Reflections and validations showed a strong effort to understand the patient’s viewpoint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus on reducing children’s exposure and consistently steered toward actionable, relevant steps. The clinician resisted drifting and kept the agenda aligned with the patient’s concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine collaboration by asking for input, offering options, and jointly developing the plan. The clinician rehearsed language with the patient and adjusted support based on her responses.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported the patient’s sense of choice by asking permission, offering options, and honoring her decisions (including not quitting). Language and approach consistently reinforced that the patient was in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation around protecting the children and used tools (open questions, scaling) to draw out readiness and resources. Evocation could be deeper by exploring broader personal values and longer-term reasons for change beyond children’s health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly framed the session as a joint effort, asking permission before advice and deferring to the patient’s expertise. The clinician co-created the plan and repeatedly invited the patient to choose what felt doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change-oriented language around harm-reduction and protecting the children, producing concrete commitments. The clinician could have amplified intrinsic motives and explored deeper personal values to broaden and strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, but engaged in limited active softening techniques. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections, strategic reframing, and summarizing ambivalence to diminish sustain talk.
