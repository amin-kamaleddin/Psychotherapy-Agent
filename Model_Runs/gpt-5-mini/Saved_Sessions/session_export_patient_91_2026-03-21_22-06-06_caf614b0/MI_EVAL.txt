# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent normalization and explicit validation of Marcus’s feelings and efforts. Language conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with focused reflections and adjustments to Marcus’s concerns (privacy, forgetting). Responses showed consistent understanding of his emotional and practical experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on establishing a routine for blood glucose checking and iteratively refined a specific, measurable one-week plan. Every question and suggestion returned to the concrete target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative: invited Marcus to choose the small step, co-created the plan, rehearsed together, and followed his preferences for specifics. Marcus’s ideas directed the plan, indicating genuine two-way problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice by offering options, asking which he preferred, and deferring to his decisions (e.g., not involving spouse). Questions preserved Marcus’s decision-making and reinforced ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation and ideas from Marcus using open questions, scaling, and eliciting acceptable strategies; the patient generated the detailed plan which the clinician amplified. Evocative techniques strengthened internal motivation and capability.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and co-constructed the plan, but occasionally used mild expert recommendations without permission which reduced pure power-sharing. Pausing to elicit Marcus’s rationale before advising would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk via scaling and plan-building, moving confidence from 4 to 7. Could more often amplify change statements, ask about past successes, and explicitly elicit commitment language to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and redirected to problem-solving (privacy options, alarms), which shifted focus away from avoidance. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections that validate ambivalence while gently contrasting reasons for change.
