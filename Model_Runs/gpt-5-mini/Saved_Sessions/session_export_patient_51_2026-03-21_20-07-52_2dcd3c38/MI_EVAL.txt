# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, using affirmations and nonjudgmental language throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking by accommodating the patient's time constraints and validating safety concerns, though could have used an explicit reflective statement naming the patient's hurry or anxiety to deepen connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the conversation tightly focused on medication safety (citalopram–phenelzine interaction, washout timing, emergency symptoms) and on actionable next steps for the patient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked jointly with the patient to co-create and iteratively refine the one‑liner and symptom prompts, inviting the patient's preferences and adjustments at each step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by asking permission before offering phrasing help, advising confirmation with the prescriber, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction relied mainly on information delivery and practical phrasing rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations, concerns, or confidence with brief open questions or reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance, sharing expertise while inviting the patient's input and co-creating the communication strategy for the prescriber visit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced and amplified the patient's safety-oriented statements and facilitated concrete action (phrasing), but missed opportunities to elicit the patient's own reasons or confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was little sustain talk and the clinician appropriately redirected toward safety and concrete steps, though more explicit acknowledgment of any hesitation (e.g., double-sided reflections) could further soften sustain talk.
