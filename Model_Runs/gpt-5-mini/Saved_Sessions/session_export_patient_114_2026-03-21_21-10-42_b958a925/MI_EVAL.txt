# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened with an explicit nonjudgmental statement and maintained a warm, respectful tone throughout, normalizing tensions and validating the client without shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that acknowledged ambivalence, but missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional texture (shame, anxiety) and reflect tentative language more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related risk reduction and progressed logically from exploration to a concrete, actionable plan with rehearsals and confidence ratings.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-created the plan with the client by inviting choices, incorporating his ideas (sober buddy, check-in, refusal lines), and practicing together, demonstrating genuine joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported client choice through permission language and option framing, avoiding coercion and reinforcing that the client decides what to use.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivations and specific strategies (values, confidence, rehearsal) but could have more strongly elicited values-discrepancy, past successes, and summarized change language to build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked in genuine partnership by asking permission, offering a menu of options, inviting the client's input, and engaging in collaborative role-play and planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk (plan, refusal lines, confidence rating) and reinforced it, but did not sufficiently amplify or deepen statements into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated social reasons without arguing and redirected to risk-reduction, yet missed tactical double-sided reflections and strategic pivots that would more effectively soften sustain talk.
