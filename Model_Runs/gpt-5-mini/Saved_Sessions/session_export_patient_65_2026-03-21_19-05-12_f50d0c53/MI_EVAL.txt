# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Julian's stress, offering practical help rather than blame. Could increase individualized affirmations that explicitly validate his specific constraints and guardedness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were brief empathic moves and acknowledgement of practical stresses, but listening remained fairly surface-level and the clinician did not deeply reflect Julian's anxiety or hesitant language. More reflective statements amplifying his emotional experience would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior (completing ~40 hours by the deadline) with questions and interventions directly linked to that goal. The clinician provided clear, structured next steps and practical planning throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like joint problem-solving with the clinician co-constructing the plan, offering choices, and tailoring scripts to Julian's preferences. The clinician was responsive and engaged Julian in selecting options and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported Julian's choices by offering multiple options, validating his preference to email first, and avoiding coercive language. These moves reinforced client ownership of decisions and how to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician took a consultative problem-solving role and did not elicit Julian's internal motivations, values, or importance/confidence about finishing the hours. There were no targeted evocative questions to draw out intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally adopted a collaborative stance and invited Julian's input, but at times led the agenda by offering many solutions rather than first eliciting Julian's own ideas. More consistent power-sharing would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Practical commitments were obtained (e.g., emailing, selecting Goodwill) but the clinician did not deliberately deepen or evoke intrinsic change talk about why finishing matters to Julian. Opportunities to explore personal reasons, meaning, or benefits were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided joining sustain talk and redirected the conversation to actionable planning, which helped move toward action. However, they did not explicitly use techniques like double-sided reflections or summarizing ambivalence before shifting to problem-solving.
