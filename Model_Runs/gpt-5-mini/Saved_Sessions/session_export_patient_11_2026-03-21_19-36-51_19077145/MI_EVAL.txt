# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with warm, validating language and normalization; suggestions were framed as options rather than moralizing, showing strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrated understanding of Amira's context and tailored recommendations, but used few reflective or emotionally explorative statements and relied on empathy mainly to inform intervention.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a very clear, sustained focus on increasing physical activity with every question and suggestion tied to the SMART goal and concrete actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicited constraints and preferences and adapted the plan accordingly, offering tools the patient requested; however, the clinician often led solution development with ready-made plans rather than eliciting the patient's own formulations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered alternatives, permissive language, and safety cues to support choice, but also provided fairly directive prescriptions which slightly reduced client-driven planning; more permission before advice would enhance autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced expressed motivations and used readiness ratings to structure the plan, but did not extensively elicit deeper personal values, narratives, or past successes to amplify intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making through preference checks and co-created rules, yet sometimes assumed the expert role with prescriptive programming instead of equal co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reinforced commitment (e.g., booking and readiness rating) and asked practical questions, but missed chances to elicit more elaborate change talk via open questions and reflective amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively validated ambivalence and provided realistic fallback options without arguing, shifting sustain talk toward action; a minor gap was limited use of double-sided reflections before pivoting.
