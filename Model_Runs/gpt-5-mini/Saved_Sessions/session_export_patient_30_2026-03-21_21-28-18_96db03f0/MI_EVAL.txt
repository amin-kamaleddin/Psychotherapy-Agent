# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating and normalizing David's concerns. Offered options rather than directives and avoided labeling or pressuring language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and accurate reflections of David's worries and priorities. Could probe and reflect deeper emotional content (e.g., fear of loss of control or frustration about lifelong meds) to deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management and medication decisions throughout the session. Routinely steered the conversation toward practical next steps like baseline labs and a 3-month plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was carried out as an equal partnership with co-created tools and frequent requests for patient input. Clinician positioned themselves as a helper, seeking consent before offering resources and following David's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice, avoided coercive language, and rehearsed neutral phrasing to reinforce ownership of the decision. Repeatedly affirmed David's right to take time and choose the approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked some useful questions that produced concrete commitments (scheduling labs, choosing a 3-month trial). However, the clinician relied heavily on information-giving and missed chances to draw out intrinsic motivations, values, or use scaling to strengthen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Felt genuinely collaborative—offered choices, co-created the set of questions, and invited David to shape the plan. The clinician consistently sought David's preferences and respected his expertise about his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created practical openings for change talk and elicited behavioral commitments like labs and a 3-month trial. Did not consistently evoke or amplify David's internal reasons, importance, or confidence regarding change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and offered alternatives without arguing. Missed strategic MI moves (double-sided reflections, strategic summarizing) that would actively soften sustain talk and invite more change talk.
