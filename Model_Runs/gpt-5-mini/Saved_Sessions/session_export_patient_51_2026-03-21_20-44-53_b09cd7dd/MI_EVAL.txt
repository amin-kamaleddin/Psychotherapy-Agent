# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalized safety concerns, and affirmed the patient's planning efforts, creating a warm, accepting tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and factual reflections (e.g., acknowledging time pressure and safety worries), but reflections stayed pragmatic and did not explore underlying emotions or use more complex reflective techniques.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, focused agenda on safe medication management and washout/taper planning, with every turn moving toward concrete next steps and safety guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly cooperative: the clinician co‑constructed scripts, asked preferences, and made joint decisions about wording and format, supporting shared work on next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored patient choice by offering options (scripts, formats) and encouraging the patient to request a supervised taper, reinforcing agency without pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited actionable intentions and preparatory language, but relied more on information and drafting than on eliciting the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and power‑sharing through choices and co‑creation, though some clinician‑led medical guidance limited opportunities for the patient to further shape the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced concrete commitment (e.g., asking for a supervised taper and 14‑day washout) but did not probe strength or confidence to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimized sustain talk by redirecting to planning and safety and avoiding reinforcement of reasons against change, though more strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) could further soften residual resistance.
