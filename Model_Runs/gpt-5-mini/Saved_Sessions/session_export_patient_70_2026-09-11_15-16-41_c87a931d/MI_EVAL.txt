# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through nonjudgmental language, normalization, and explicit validation of Lars’s motivations. Avoids blame or shame and respects the patient's feelings throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing Lars’s ambivalence, fears, and priorities. Reflections are timely and deepen understanding rather than merely repeating facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on concrete, actionable steps to reduce reoffending (polytech call, draft, alarm) and orients the plan toward skills and safer connections. However, broader risk-management (alcohol triggers, Friday-night planning, thrill-seeking alternatives) was acknowledged but not developed into specific strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative—options are offered, the patient is invited to choose, and the plan is co-created with clinician support. The clinician frames the work as a joint project and incorporates Lars’s input into next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Lars’s choices and offers multiple options rather than directives, allowing him to shape the plan. Language repeatedly supports self-direction and avoids pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits core values and motivations (Donna, staying out of prison, being a different dad) and links them to concrete steps. Opportunities remain to deepen evocation by probing confidence, past successes, and using scaling or decisional-balance techniques.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats Lars as the expert on his life, offering choices and asking which options feel most possible. Co-creates the plan (drafting text together, saving as draft) and consistently defers to Lars’s judgment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk by asking about what feels doable, reinforcing reasons and values, and obtaining concrete commitments (calling polytech, drafting text, setting an alarm). Clinician labels and strengthens Lars’s moves toward change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (loyalty to mates, boredom, alcohol) without reinforcing it and redirects toward compromise options like a class. Could improve by using more double-sided reflections and explicit reframing to turn sustain-talk themes into concrete coping alternatives.
