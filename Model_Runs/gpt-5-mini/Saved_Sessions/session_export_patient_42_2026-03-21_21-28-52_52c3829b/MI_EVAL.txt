# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence from the opening line. They affirm the patient's preferences and accept her wording tweaks, avoiding criticism throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening are evident, capturing Elise's tension between health and social connection. The clinician validates subtle anxieties about teasing and social awkwardness, showing strong attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on smoking behavior and its social context throughout. Every suggestion and rehearsal is tied to concrete behavioral goals (specific breaks, gum/lozenges, refusal lines).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction reads as an equal partnership: the clinician invites preferences, co-constructs language, and practices aloud together. They adapt wording based on Elise's feedback and treat her as a partner in the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly honors Elise's choice, offers multiple options, and asks her to pick what feels doable, resisting pressure. This supports Elise's ownership of the plan and readiness to try small steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician assumes motivation lies within Elise and elicits it effectively by asking her to choose breaks, lines, and strategies. Rehearsal and asking Elise to reflect on how phrases feel bring out her own motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fosters a collaborative stance by asking permission, offering options, and co-creating language with Elise. At times they provide multiple concrete scripts before eliciting more of Elise's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist elicits and reinforces actionable change talk and develops concrete steps (e.g., a 3 pm break, carrying gum). There were fewer explicit probes to deepen desire, importance, or confidence (e.g., scaling questions) to further consolidate change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to small experiments that preserve social connection. They could increase use of double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge social benefits while guiding toward change.
