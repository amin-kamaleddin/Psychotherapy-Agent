# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician took an explicitly nonjudgmental, supportive stance and consistently affirmed the patient's perspective and goals, avoiding labels or blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and checks about feelings and safety showed good perspective-taking, though more complex reflections exploring underlying emotions would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use and safety, moved toward concrete behavioral goals, and established a specific weekly plan and follow-up procedures.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as cooperative, offered options rather than prescriptions, and incorporated the patient's wording and follow-up preferences into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were emphasized throughout, goals were framed as the patient's decision, and slips were normalized to preserve the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change talk and commitment, but relied more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient's own arguments, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician invited input and decision-making, co-created plans with the patient, and actively shared power in developing strategies and follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several prompts and positive reinforcement elicited change talk and commitment, but the clinician missed opportunities to deepen and amplify those statements (e.g., importance/confidence queries).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation and aligned plans with the patient's preference to cut back, but used few explicit softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to gently pivot sustain talk toward change.
