# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing and validating the patient's distress while accommodating her preference for tests without blame or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflections and summaries that show perspective-taking and active listening, but could deepen by exploring the emotional texture and meaning (e.g., impact on identity or shame) more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on the GI problem and the patient's request for investigations, moving the session toward concrete actions (tests, letter, timeframe).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative — co‑creates and revises the referral letter, solicits the patient's wording and rehearsal preferences, and mirrors her priorities throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choice and respects the patient's decisions (which to start, tone of letter, whether to role‑play), reinforcing the patient's control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited effort to draw out the patient's intrinsic motivations, values, or long‑term goals; the clinician primarily assumes a problem‑solving role and focuses on external actions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance and honors the patient's agenda, but could more explicitly elicit and foreground the patient's expertise and co‑construct a plan integrating her priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers change‑related options (diary, relaxation, bowel routine) but does not pursue reluctance or actively elicit/amplify DARN‑C language; change talk is only lightly encouraged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tends to accommodate and reinforce sustain talk (strong insistence on organic causes) by drafting a firm letter and scripts, with few MI strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to highlight ambivalence.
