# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician adopts a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Maya's concerns, but the interaction becomes somewhat clinical and solution-focused fairly quickly, which reduces a warmer, individualized sense of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are clear empathic moves (acknowledging distress, safety checking, offering alternatives), but reflections are brief and the clinician rarely mirrors the emotional nuance of Maya's guardedness or fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related risk and college consequences and follows a logical flow from assessment to education to safety planning, consistently returning to the agreed focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently invites preferences, rehearses scripts, and role-plays, creating a largely joint effort, but offering many clinician-generated suggestions sometimes positions them as the expert rather than co-constructing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are offered and the clinician respects Maya's preference not to quit, yet some directive recommendations are given without Elicit-Provide-Elicit framing, slightly diminishing autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are openings for eliciting Maya's motivations and statements about avoiding blackouts, but the clinician frequently shifts to teaching and advice instead of systematically drawing out intrinsic reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and practices skills with Maya, supporting partnership, but the early provision of numerous specific steps shifts some control back to the clinician rather than fully co-creating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited (e.g., desire to avoid blackouts, protect grades), but the clinician moves quickly into solutions and does not consistently reflect, amplify, or probe for importance and confidence to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician redirects sustain talk by offering alternatives and safety strategies, but uses few MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or tilted summaries) to acknowledge the appeal of drinking while highlighting costs.
