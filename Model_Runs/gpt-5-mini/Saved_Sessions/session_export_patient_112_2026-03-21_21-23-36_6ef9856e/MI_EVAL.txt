# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and warmth, validating ambivalence and avoiding judgment. No moments of criticism or pressure were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely, complex reflections that mirrored Rosa's fears and social meaning of smoking. Tone and supportive coaching matched the patient's affect and shaped the conversation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking change and deliberately narrowed from ambivalence to a concrete, time‑bound plan. Momentum toward a specific, actionable step (asking Maria/text + coffee) was sustained without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was highly cooperative with joint drafting of the text, role‑play, and choices offered throughout. Rosa's preferences shaped the plan, demonstrating equal partnership in planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and normalized uncertainty, offering suggestions only with permission and as options. The clinician honored Rosa's agency and reduced pressure throughout the interaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited internal motivation and concrete reasons for change but could have probed deeper into personal values, longer‑term meaning, and confidence for the chosen step. Opportunities existed to amplify brief change talk into stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Built a genuine partnership through frequent permission‑seeking, co‑creation, and shared decision‑making. Power was consistently shared and expertise about Rosa's life was respected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and helped formulate a doable action, but missed chances to deepen reasons and elicit firmer commitment (e.g., probing why a 7 and what would make it higher).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to practical, affordable supports that address barriers. Could increase use of double‑sided reflections or explicit reframing to further weaken sustain arguments.
