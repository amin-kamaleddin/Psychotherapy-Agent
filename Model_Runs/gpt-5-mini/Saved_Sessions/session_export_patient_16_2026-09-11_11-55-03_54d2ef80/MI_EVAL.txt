# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys nonjudgmental, supportive regard and normalizes the adolescent's struggle, affirming the patient's ideas and choices throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening about values and pressures, though deeper emotional reflections (stress, fear of disappointing teammates) were less frequent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, steady focus on vaping and concrete behavior-change steps, continually steering toward implementation (scripts, cues, scheduled check‑in) with little topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative—clinician and adolescent co-construct the plan, clinician solicits the patient's wording and preferences, and responds to those choices rather than dictating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms choice and offers multiple options (role‑play, text, check‑in), repeatedly asking which support the patient prefers and letting them shape the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical commitment and change talk (plans, confidence rating), but could further draw out intrinsic, personally meaningful reasons for longer‑term change beyond pragmatic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint effort, defers to the adolescent’s expertise about what will work, and asks how the clinician should best support the chosen strategy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk by asking for past successes, inviting a concrete plan, probing for confidence, and summarizing commitments to strengthen resolve.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting sustain talk and redirects to the patient's own reasons for change, though missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and more explicit discrepancy work.
