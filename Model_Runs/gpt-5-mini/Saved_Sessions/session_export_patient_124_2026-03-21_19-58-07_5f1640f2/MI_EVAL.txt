# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm—normalized use, reassured about slips, and affirmed the patient’s perspective throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and occasional reflections, but offered fewer complex reflections exploring deeper emotions (e.g., family pressure, anxiety).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on marijuana use with concrete, measurable steps (one-line log, 9pm reminder) and a one-week follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative—co-created a brief log and scripts on request—but the clinician often supplied wording and strategies rather than eliciting the patient’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control by offering multiple options, asking which felt doable, normalizing missed entries, and emphasizing the patient’s decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to draw out motivation (cravings, whether it helps anxiety), but limited exploration of values, goals, or deeper reasons for change using evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed patient expertise by offering options and letting the patient choose tracking, yet frequently steered the plan with specific recommendations and templates.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited basic change talk (agreement to track, trial swaps) but did not consistently amplify or deepen spontaneous statements about doubts or reasons to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk gently—normalized ambivalence and focused on small tests—but could use more double-sided or summary reflections to more actively pivot toward change.
