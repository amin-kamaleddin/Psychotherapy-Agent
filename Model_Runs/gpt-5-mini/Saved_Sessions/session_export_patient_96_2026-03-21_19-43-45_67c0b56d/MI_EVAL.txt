# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, explicitly affirming the patient’s choices and showing respectful regard throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and several accurate reflections and validations were used, though deeper emotional or value-level reflections (e.g., pride in autonomy or appearance concerns) were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on oral health and aftercare for the lip piercing with concrete, safety-focused guidance and no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative with joint problem-solving and patient input on messages and follow-up, though clinical instruction was sometimes more clinician-led for safety reasons.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirmed the patient’s control and choices, offered options rather than directives, and invited the patient to set the agenda and make decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions invited the patient’s agenda and preferences, but the clinician did not sufficiently probe internal motivations, values, or explore discrepancies to elicit change from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was clear power-sharing and co-creation of plans (e.g., drafting messages, choosing follow-up), though occasional directive clinical guidance reduced a fully client-led tone.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to elicit change talk were minimal—after the patient expressed intent to keep the piercing the clinician moved to information-giving rather than systematically evoking reasons, importance, or confidence to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and accepted the patient’s sustain talk, using harm-reduction strategies and practical guidance, though double-sided reflections acknowledging both sides were underused.
