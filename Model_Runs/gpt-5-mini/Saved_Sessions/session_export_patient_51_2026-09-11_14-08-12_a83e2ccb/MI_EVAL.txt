# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validating the patient's efforts and constraints. Tone and language convey strong supportive regard without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking with timely, complex reflections that capture both practical constraints and emotions. Demonstrates strong reflective listening and understanding of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on protecting brief lunch breaks as the target behavior. Conversation moves logically from identifying a small change to concrete implementation tools.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly a joint process with co-creation of a practical plan and frequent invitations for input. Clinician adapts offerings to the patient's stated constraints and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers options and frames suggestions as experiments, deferring to the patient's choices. Reinforces patient ownership over decisions and respects limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits doable options and preparatory motivation but does not consistently probe underlying values or confidence. Could more directly draw out internal motives and longer-term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative language and power-sharing, asking which option feels most doable and tailoring support accordingly. Patient expertise is consistently respected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits change talk and concrete commitments through specific options and planning. Missed opportunities to amplify commitment and assess confidence or importance more directly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and redirects toward small experiments and practical steps. Could better use double-sided reflections or summaries that explicitly diminish the pull of barriers.
