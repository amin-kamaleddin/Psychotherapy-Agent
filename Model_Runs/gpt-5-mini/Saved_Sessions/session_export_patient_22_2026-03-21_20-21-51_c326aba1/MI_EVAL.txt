# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with normalization and validation, maintaining a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, but reflections were mostly content-level; more complex affective reflections (e.g., naming shame or loneliness) would deepen resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and moved the conversation toward specific, actionable plans with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly invited the client's input, co-created tactics, and used role-play and rehearsal to solve problems together, reinforcing equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Respectfully supported the client's choice not to quit entirely, offered options rather than directives, asked permission before advice, and used scaling to elicit client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited the client's motivations and change talk through questions and planning, though linking change to core values and eliciting firmer commitment language would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by offering agenda choices, co-creating strategies, and repeatedly checking which tactics felt natural to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (evocative questions, confidence ruler, reinforcing plans), but could more strategically amplify and summarize change statements to build momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confronting it but rarely used double-sided reflections, reframing, or discrepancy questions and moved quickly to strategies, leaving ambivalence less explored.
