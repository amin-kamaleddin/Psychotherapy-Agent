# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard—nonjudgmental opening, normalization of ambivalence, and validation of caregiving values without confrontation or criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking throughout (e.g., reflective summary capturing needle fear and family concern) and offered information with permission, demonstrating strong attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on vaccination and resolving barriers, moving the conversation toward concrete action steps (booking, script, coping strategies) while remaining patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner—solicited input, offered choices, co-created the script and step‑by‑step plan, and used role‑play to tailor solutions to the patient's needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by offering options, asking permission before sharing information, and repeatedly reinforcing that the patient could choose the next step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's family‑focused motivation and highlighted it effectively, but could use more evocative open questions and scaling to draw out the patient's own arguments for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making with collaborative language, joint problem‑solving, and tailored recommendations that centered the patient's priorities and expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively amplified change talk around protecting grandparents and encouraged doable steps, though more open, evocative questions and selective amplification could deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reflected sustain talk without arguing and redirected to practical solutions, while using more double‑sided reflections and summaries could better diminish sustain talk.
