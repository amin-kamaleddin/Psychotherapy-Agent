# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with validating reflections and affirmations, but occasionally shifted quickly into problem-solving and advice rather than deeper individualized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate listening and paraphrase of frustration and ambivalence, though reflections tended to stay at the content level and did not consistently explore deeper emotions or personal meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear behavioral focus on tobacco use with concrete, achievable targets (no smoking indoors, avoid after‑dinner, weekly check‑ins) and sustained planning throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Felt like joint problem‑solving with options and invited preferences, but the clinician often supplied concrete scripts and tips before eliciting more of the patient's own strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly respected choice and offered options, asked permission before advice, and supported small self‑directed steps which reinforced the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some information (willingness rating, past attempts) and leveraged relationship concern, but did not sufficiently probe the personal meaning, values, or deeper reasons behind the patient's statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance and co‑constructed a practical plan with the patient, yet the interaction was somewhat clinician‑driven in idea generation and solution content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and achieved concrete commitment language (two‑week no‑smoking‑indoors plan), but missed opportunities to amplify or explore the patient's own reasons, strengths, and motivations before moving to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated sustain talk while reframing toward compromise to reduce defensiveness, though more strategic double‑sided reflections or contrasts could further shift language toward change.
