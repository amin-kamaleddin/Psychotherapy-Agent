# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalized ambivalence, and invited permission; no shaming or lecturing was observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and summaries demonstrated understanding of the patient's perspective, but reflections stayed at the content/behavior level and could deepen emotional nuance and affective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on mini‑cigar use and harm‑reduction throughout the visit, moving logically from assessment to a specific, measurable implementation plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked largely as an equal partner with shared problem solving and co‑constructed plans, though the clinician sometimes offered multiple options before eliciting the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice and control by seeking permission, offering options, normalizing slips, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivation with open questions, rulers, and a patient‑selected plan, but did not sufficiently explore deeper values, discrepancies, or past successes to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated a collaborative stance by inviting input and negotiating a feasible test, yet could enhance partnership by soliciting the patient's ideas before listing clinician suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change‑leaning language and concrete steps (gum, reminders, telling a buddy), but missed chances to deepen change talk by linking to values and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and gently redirected sustain talk toward harm‑reduction options and small steps, though more double‑sided reflections and amplification of fleeting change language could further weaken sustain talk.
