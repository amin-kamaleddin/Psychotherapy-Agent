# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and warmth, normalizing and honoring the patient's priorities without judgment. Multiple affirmations and an attuned opening establish a nonjudgmental stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective paraphrases and summaries demonstrate strong understanding of the patient's perspective and emotional tone. Reflections consistently track concerns, supports, and cautious stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior (a phone-free meal) and translates values into a measurable, time-bound action with follow-up plans. The session stays tightly aligned with planning and supporting that specific change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly framed as a joint effort, with frequent invitations for input and integration of the patient's choices into the plan. The clinician offers drafts then asks which version the patient prefers, enabling co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honors and supports the patient's autonomy by offering choices, asking preferences, and avoiding pressure. The clinician emphasizes small voluntary steps and respects the patient's wish to draft partner text herself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations, values, and supports using values clarification and scaling, but at times moves to offering clinician-generated solutions before eliciting the patient's ideas. More consistent prompts for the patient to brainstorm options would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a shared, co-creative process by inviting collaboration and deferring to the patient's preferences when making decisions. Wording and plans are co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses open questions, scaling, and affirmations to elicit and strengthen change talk leading to a concrete commitment, but misses some opportunities to have the patient generate solutions and to amplify mid-level commitment statements. More deliberate reflections to deepen client-produced language would increase ownership.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and explores barriers without judgment, redirecting toward practical planning and supports. The clinician honors concerns while steering the conversation to manageable steps and fallback options.
