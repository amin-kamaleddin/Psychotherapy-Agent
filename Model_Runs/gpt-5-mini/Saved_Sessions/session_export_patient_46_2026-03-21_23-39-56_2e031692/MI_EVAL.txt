# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician communicated unconditional positive regard repeatedly, normalized ambivalence, and affirmed Ethan's priorities while avoiding blame, which reduced defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, empathic reflections and perspective-taking tracked Ethan's feelings and priorities and were used to advance the conversation rather than merely repeating content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on risky alcohol use and protecting school/team functioning, moving from values to specific, actionable steps and a concrete commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invited Ethan's input, co-created the plan (texting, driving, rehearsing), and positioned clinician and client as partners solving the problem together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was supported throughout by affirming control and pace, offering multiple options, and framing interventions as choices that preserved Ethan's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician elicited motivation from within Ethan by focusing on his values, strengths, and past strategies, drawing out clear change language and implementation ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance with repeated invitations for choice and input, treating Ethan as the expert and co-constructing feasible strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited and reinforced change talk through open questions, reflections, and summaries, leading to specific plans Ethan volunteered (texting, driving, rehearsing).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and normalized social pressure but tended to pivot quickly to strategy, missing some opportunities for deeper double-sided reflections or exploring costs of continued use.
