# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes difficulty (e.g., validates burden and frustration) while avoiding blame. Acceptance of limits and nonjudgmental responses foster a safe, respectful tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening by naming and paraphrasing the patient's concerns (job, wife, feeling overwhelmed). Reflections reliably mirror priorities and limitations, showing strong empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on diabetes self-management and consistently narrows to one concrete behavior (one morning BG check). Every question and suggestion advances that specific behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is equal‑partner problem-solving with frequent solicitation of the patient's input and tailoring of options (alarms, meter location, backup plan). The patient's preferences meaningfully shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, affirms limits, and asks permission, emphasizing that the one‑week trial is the patient's decision. These behaviors support ownership and avoid coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively links the plan to existing motives (protecting job and easing wife's burden) and elicits a small‑win commitment. Could strengthen evocation by probing deeper into personal values/reasons and using scaling questions to assess readiness/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is co‑constructed with repeated invitations for preference before offering specifics and choices rather than directives. This demonstrates genuine power‑sharing and patient influence throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces small change language (choice questions, affirming the plan, setting a one‑week goal) and secures a concrete plan. Missed opportunities include deeper exploration of why the change matters to increase desire and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reframes sustain talk without arguing, normalizing missed days and offering manageable steps. Could be stronger with explicit double‑sided reflections to highlight both sustain concerns and change motives.
