# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and normalization of Rafael's struggle, avoiding blame and frequently affirming his intentions and difficulties.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained empathic listening with accurate reflections and perspective-taking that capture both motivations (family/energy) and barriers (exhaustion, time).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, appropriate focus on building an exercise routine, repeatedly returning to the target behavior and moving toward a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as a partnership by inviting brainstorming and eliciting preferences, but occasionally leans toward directive phrasing and could more explicitly invite Rafael to shape next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking which feels realistic, and avoiding pressuring language so Rafael retains control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out values and past successes to elicit motivation, though could deepen evocation with importance/confidence scaling and more probing to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language and builds on Rafael's responses to co-create options, but sometimes labels or steers the interaction rather than fully co-constructing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and helps generate a concrete small-step plan, but would benefit from more systematic elicitation of ability/confidence and consolidating change language into stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and reframes toward doable options, though more frequent use of double-sided reflections or exploring costs of status quo could further soften sustain talk.
