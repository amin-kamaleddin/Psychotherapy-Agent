# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance with gentle corrective feedback and multiple affirmations. No shaming or dismissive language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing enjoyment and ambivalence about drinking. Could more consistently probe deeper emotional meaning and values beneath the behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on drinking throughout and moved the session toward a specific, measurable harm-reduction plan and follow-up. Balanced exploration with goal-oriented steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly co-created the plan, negotiated follow-up, and repeatedly framed the work as a shared effort. Decisions and implementation strategies were developed jointly rather than dictated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and respecting reluctance without pressure. Explicitly invited the patient's preferences for follow-up content and timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivation and converted tentative reflection into a concrete experiment with planning and check-ins. Could deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring values, importance/confidence, and amplifying change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and deferred to the patient's judgment while offering options and invitations. Strength could be increased by eliciting the patient's own solutions before supplying multiple expert suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced specific change language and implementation plans, including concrete steps and a follow-up check-in. Could more strategically amplify subtle change statements to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and need and move toward commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and softened sustain talk through validation, calm factual feedback, and harm-reduction framing. Could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence and help the patient weigh pros and cons.
