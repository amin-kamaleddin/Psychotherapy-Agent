### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized Mei Lin's struggle, and avoided blame. Language was warm and repeatedly affirming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking captured ambivalence and emotion. The clinician regularly checked understanding and mirrored the patient's concerns and affect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear behavioral focus on reducing pregnancy risk and improving contraception reliability. The clinician steered toward a concrete, actionable plan (calling to schedule an IUD/implant Saturday at 10 AM).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was highly collaborative, with co-creation of the script, reminder, and role‑play. The clinician framed the work as joint problem-solving and negotiated next steps with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were offered and permission was checked, reinforcing the patient's ownership of decisions. The clinician emphasized that the patient would decide what felt right and selectable options were provided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocative questions drew motivation from the patient (values, benefits, and concrete steps) rather than imposing reasons for change. The clinician amplified and supported internally generated motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Partnership was evident as the clinician invited the patient's preferences and co-constructed the plan. Power was actively shared and the clinician deferred to the patient's expertise about what would help.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Targeted, evocative questions and reflective reinforcement elicited and strengthened change talk. This moved the patient from ambivalence to a specific proximal plan (call, script, reminder, check‑in).

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** The clinician avoided amplifying sustain talk and gently redirected to protective goals, but missed some opportunities for explicit softening strategies like double-sided reflections. More exploration of the emotional processes behind 'I don’t really care too much' could further resolve ambivalence.
