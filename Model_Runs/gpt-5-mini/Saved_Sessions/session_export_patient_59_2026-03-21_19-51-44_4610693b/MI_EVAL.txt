# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established clear unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone from the start, checked safety, validated frustration, and thanked the client for honesty throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good basic empathic listening and pragmatic reflections (e.g., validating frustration) but reflections remained surface-level and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a focused, consistent direction on immediate practical goals (minimizing legal/school consequences) with aligned interventions, scripting, and role-play practice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was framed as a joint task with choice about focus and rehearsal order, though clinician expertise guided much of the content during legal instruction and scripting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician honored the client's preference for practical help and offered options, but frequently provided direct advice and scripted language without explicit permission or co-creation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to elicit the client’s own motives, values, or ambivalence; the approach emphasized information and coaching rather than drawing out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—offering choices and inviting the client to choose rehearsal items—but the clinician shifted into an expert role when delivering concrete legal advice and exact phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rare and weak attempts to elicit change talk; the clinician focused on scripting and compliance rather than asking follow-up questions to deepen desire, reasons, or commitment for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing with sustain talk and prevented escalation, but did not use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to actively soften minimization or explore costs of continued behavior.
