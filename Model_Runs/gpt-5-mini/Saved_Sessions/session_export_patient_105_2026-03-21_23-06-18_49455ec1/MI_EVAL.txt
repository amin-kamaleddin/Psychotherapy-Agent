# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing ambivalence and using affirming language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening that captured both content and affect, which helped the patient feel understood and stay engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, tight focus on the pre-shoot routine target, repeatedly tying questions and reflections to a concrete, actionable plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as an equal partnership—clinician frequently asked for the patient's input and integrated her ideas into a mutually co-constructed plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options and invited the patient to choose what felt doable, avoiding pressure and reinforcing the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited personal reasons and meaningful outcomes (e.g., steady hands, better photos) but could deepen evocation by exploring longer-term values, using scaling, and eliciting a commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance that emphasized the patient's expertise, offered choices, and framed the work as shared problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk about concrete benefits, though the clinician rarely pushed for stronger commitment language or explored confidence/ability explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk using double-sided reflections, but more active reframing and exploration of how to overcome barriers would strengthen this domain.
