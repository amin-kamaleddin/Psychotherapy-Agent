# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalizing ambivalence and validating embarrassment; praised client choices (e.g., water‑alternating plan) which conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective‑taking, capturing ambivalence early; reflections tended toward summary/directive rather than deeper affect‑focused responses (e.g., shame or stress).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on alcohol use in social settings and logically progressed to a specific, measurable experiment (Mike, alarms, visible water, three nights, Sunday check‑in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Overall collaborative—solicited preferences, rehearsed replies, and checked buy‑in—but frequent scripted suggestions reduced parity by supplying strategies before fully eliciting the client's brainstorm.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice by framing change as an optional, time‑limited experiment, asking permission to rehearse, and avoiding pressure or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation out with open questions and the confidence ruler, eliciting concrete reasons, intentions, and a follow‑through plan from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed teamwork and co‑construction of a plan, but offering multiple specific scripts and options sometimes shifted agency toward the clinician rather than fully privileging the client's solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk using targeted questions, scaling, and reinforcing reflections, which led to concrete commitment and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged positives of drinking while steering toward low‑risk experiments, though presenting several options early could be experienced as guidance rather than curiosity; more double‑sided reflections would help.
