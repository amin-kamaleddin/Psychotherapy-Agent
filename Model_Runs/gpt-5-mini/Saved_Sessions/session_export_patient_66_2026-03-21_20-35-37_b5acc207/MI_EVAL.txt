# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth (e.g., welcoming opening and validation of enjoying smoking). Honored the patient's limits and choices, offering options without pressure and supporting a low‑commitment plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective‑taking with accurate paraphrases and empathic exploratory questions. Could probe deeper emotional nuance and use more complex or amplified reflections to convey greater understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking behavior and moved the session toward a specific, actionable one‑week trial. Consistently translated discussion into concrete steps (gum/inhailer/patch guidance and a pocket checklist).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine collaboration by inviting patient input and adapting materials to his preferences. Co‑created the plan and honored the patient's ideas and constraints throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by emphasizing choice, offering optional strategies, and proposing a trial rather than forcing commitment. Repeatedly reinforced the patient's control over decisions and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal reasons and tentative commitments through questions about routine and past attempts and by proposing a small test. Could increase use of value‑focused questions, scaling, and amplification of change language to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and sought preferences, but sometimes shifted to an expert/instructional tone when giving technical dosing details. More explicit shared decision language and checks of understanding would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and nurtured change talk (past attempts, willingness to try a week) and offered manageable options. Could better amplify and follow up on brief change statements to strengthen desire, ability, reasons, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argument and reframed sustain talk into practical solutions, reflecting ambivalence rather than confronting it. Could use more double‑sided reflections and strategic summaries to further defuse sustain talk and shift toward change.
