# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with normalizing statements, praise, and explicit validation that created a safe, nonjudgmental space. Avoided blaming or shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that named feelings and summarized content. Patient responded by elaborating and accepting parts of the plan, indicating empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking behavior and progressed the session toward a concrete, time‑bound plan with specific strategies, start date, and follow‑up. Balanced exploration with purposeful movement to action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative—clinician repeatedly solicited preferences, negotiated details, and co‑constructed the plan with the patient. Power was shared and the clinician acted as supporter and co‑planner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently sought permission, offered multiple options, and emphasized the patient's right to choose and pace, reinforcing internal ownership. Recommendations were framed as choices rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation and a change statement tied to the doctor's warning and concrete planning, but missed deeper elicitation of intrinsic motives, values, and past successes to amplify commitment. More targeted questions could have strengthened internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership demonstrated by inviting input, honoring the patient's expertise about her life, and collaboratively drafting messages and reminders. Decision‑making was clearly shared and patient‑centered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced tentative change talk and supported a concrete first step, but could have probed values and long‑term personal reasons to deepen desire and commitment. Opportunities to amplify DARN (desire, ability, reasons, need) statements were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argumentation and acknowledged sustain concerns with empathy and practical options, redirecting to small experiments. Could have used more double‑sided or complex reflections to explicitly summarize both sides and help resolve ambivalence more efficiently.
