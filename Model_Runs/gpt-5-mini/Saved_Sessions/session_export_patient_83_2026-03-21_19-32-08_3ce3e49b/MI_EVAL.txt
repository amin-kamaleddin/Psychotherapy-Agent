# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, explicitly validated the patient's concerns and began with a safety check. Avoided blaming or minimizing and demonstrated unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good perspective-taking and some reflective listening, especially early on, with clear patient-centered explanations. Empathy was more informational than deeply affective, with limited exploration of the patient's emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on safely starting and monitoring atorvastatin (purpose, interactions, labs, when to stop). Every turn advanced practical, safety-oriented steps for adherence and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicited the patient's med list, exercise routine, and preferences and provided tailored scripts and follow-up steps. Interaction often shifted into clinician-led information delivery rather than exploratory, shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered options (monitor vs hold, scripts to use) and avoided coercive language, supporting the patient's ability to choose. Missed explicit autonomy-enhancing invitations like asking the patient what she prefers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited necessary factual information but did not draw out the patient's intrinsic motivations, values, or importance/confidence about taking the medication. Relied largely on supplying information rather than evoking and amplifying change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited patient contributions and shared tasks (checking interactions, drafting scripts) which supported partnership. The interaction nevertheless leaned toward clinician expertise and directive recommendations rather than full co-creation of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced and made concrete the patient's change intentions with practical follow-ups and planning (timing, labs, scripts). Missed opportunities to elicit more change language via open questions, scaling, or summarizing and amplifying commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and neutralized ambivalence with nonconfrontational education, normalization, and practical steps, effectively redirecting toward safety and adherence. Could strengthen double-sided reflections to more explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence while steering toward change.
