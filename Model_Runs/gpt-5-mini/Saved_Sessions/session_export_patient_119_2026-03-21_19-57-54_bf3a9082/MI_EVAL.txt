# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with a warm supportive tone; validates the patient's preferences and treats her as capable rather than blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and practical reflections but under-explores the patient's emotional ambivalence and anxious/guarded tone; deeper reflective listening would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and specific behaviors, and establishes a measurable seven-day trial with concrete outcomes to report.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session reads as collaborative with a jointly assembled plan, but the clinician often presents lists of solutions without first eliciting the patient's prior attempts or ideas, making parts clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the patient's sense of choice by offering multiple low-hassle options, asking which feels easiest, and avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language and a commitment to a trial but relies heavily on offering solutions rather than drawing out the patient's personal motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and co-creative of the plan, yet would benefit from more explicit sharing of expertise and asking the patient about prior strategies before proposing many options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, praises choices, and sets a concrete trial, but could amplify and deepen motivation by exploring what staying healthy would mean and assessing confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and offers low-effort strategies, but does not consistently use strategic softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing) to reduce sustain talk.
