# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized participation, offering choices and practical suggestions. However, acceptance felt somewhat procedural with few explicit affirmations of the patient's strengths or efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were basic reflective statements and attention to comfort during breathing, but deeper, emotion-focused reflections were limited. The clinician did not explore the patient’s minimization or broader perspective with complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, appropriate focus on blood pressure management and actionable steps, returning to concrete follow-up items. The clinician consistently guided the conversation toward monitoring and behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative with choices offered, preferences solicited, and tools co-constructed (reminders, note template). Parts of the encounter were still somewhat clinician-driven with rapid presentation of recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice and control by offering options, seeking permission, and honoring the patient’s preference for once-daily practice. Language throughout respected the patient's agency in decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivational work occurred through offering options and small-step commitments, but the clinician did not sufficiently draw out the patient’s own values or reasons for change. Few evocative questions or scaling techniques were used to elicit intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited collaboration and co-created the plan (scheduling after coffee, reminder options), demonstrating a strong partnership stance. It fell short of maximal partnership when elements became more clinician-led and prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offered change-oriented options that elicited modest commitments (daily breathing), but did not systematically evoke the patient's own reasons, importance, or confidence. Missed opportunities for evocative questions limited strengthening of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing with sustain talk and did not reinforce minimization, but also did not actively use MI techniques (double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance) to soften it. Handling was neutral and passive rather than explicitly addressing ambivalence.
