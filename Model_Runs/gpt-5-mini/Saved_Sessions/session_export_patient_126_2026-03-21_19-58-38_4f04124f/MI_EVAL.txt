# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician established unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone that reduced threat; however, affirmations were brief and lacked warmer, individualized validation to merit a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic reflective listening and perspective-taking appeared but reflections were limited and the clinician often shifted quickly to facts and advice, missing deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on immediate substance-related risk reduction with concrete behavioral targets, but it did not strategically move toward broader change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offered options and accommodated patient requests, fostering some joint problem-solving, yet the encounter was largely expert-driven with didactic instructions dominating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was consistently acknowledged and options were offered, with invitations for patient preference; more explicit permission-seeking before advice and stronger affirmations of the patient's right to decide would improve this area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction prioritized information and recommendations rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations or values, missing opportunities to draw out reasons for or against change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were moments of collaborative language and offers to co-decide, but the clinician primarily supplied expertise and directed the plan instead of fully co-constructing options with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to evoke change talk were minimal—one direct preference question and an offer to set limits—but the clinician did not follow up to elicit desire, reasons, need, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used neutral harm-reduction advice, but did not employ MI techniques like double-sided or strategic reflections to soften or explore the patient's minimization.
