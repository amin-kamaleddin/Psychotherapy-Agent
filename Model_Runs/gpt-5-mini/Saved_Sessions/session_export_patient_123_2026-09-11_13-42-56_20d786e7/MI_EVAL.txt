# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental, respectful regard and affirmed the client's priorities and comfort with low‑effort options. There was no shaming or prescriptive language, supporting unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective‑taking and accurate reflections throughout the session made the client feel understood. The clinician used reflective language and matched interventions to the client's concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, appropriate focus on understanding the screening and protecting academics/social functioning. Each turn clarified or moved toward a concrete, achievable action (teacher check‑in, wording, role‑play).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like joint problem‑solving with the client shaping the plan at each step. The clinician solicited preferences and adapted options to fit the client's wishes for privacy and low effort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were repeatedly offered and the client's decisions were respected, reinforcing ownership of the plan. The clinician avoided pressure and framed options as the client's to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited a specific, client‑owned action (teacher check‑in and text), but did not probe deeper into values, reasons, or confidence (no scaling or long‑term exploration).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the encounter as collaborative and shared power, offering decision points and co‑constructing the plan with the client. Language emphasized teamwork and equal contribution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concrete change talk was evoked for the small, practical step (agreeing to the teacher check‑in and drafting text). The clinician could have more consistently deepened broader change talk about importance, desire, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without confrontation and the clinician pivoted to acceptable, low‑effort options to avoid reinforcing it. More strategic techniques (double‑sided reflections, normalizing ambivalence) could further soften sustain talk.
