# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalized frustrations, and affirmed the patient’s efforts and limits, maintaining a warm nonjudgmental stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used accurate reflections and perspective-taking about logistics and irritation, but reflections were mostly surface-level and could probe deeper affective content or emotional impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician kept a clear, consistent focus on the concrete target (on-the-go snacking tied to breathlessness and BP), progressing logically from target selection to a concrete experiment and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a team effort with co-created choices, but the clinician offered many specific technical solutions which slightly shifted the balance toward clinician-led problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly honored patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and normalizing trade-offs, which preserved and reinforced the patient’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively drew out motivation using target selection, a small experiment, and a confidence ruler, but could have explored deeper personal values and amplified the patient’s own motivational statements more.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician invited patient input and co-created the plan, yet providing many concrete recommendations at times reduced opportunities for the patient to generate more of the logistical solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced change talk (experiment agreement, desire to address weight, confidence ruler), though they sometimes moved quickly into technical solutions instead of amplifying the patient’s change language further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician acknowledged barriers, avoided arguing, and reframed toward feasible small steps, but could have used explicit double-sided reflections or ambivalence summaries to further soften sustain talk.
