# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent nonjudgmental, respectful stance and repeatedly affirmed Ethan’s contributions and perspective, normalizing his experience throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained, accurate reflective listening and complex reflections matched Ethan’s tone and ambivalence, demonstrating strong attunement and validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on risky alcohol use and progressed logically from exploration to ambivalence to concrete goal-setting and action planning using focused questions and rulers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as a joint project, invited Ethan to co-create the plan, role-played with permission, and operationalized Ethan’s ideas rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized Ethan’s choice and control, offered options instead of directives, and sought permission to practice, reinforcing client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician focused on eliciting Ethan’s own motivations and resources (scaling questions, asking what would increase confidence) and successfully drew out self-generated strategies and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reflected shared power and collaboration: the clinician invited Ethan’s input on wording, choices, and contingencies, building the plan together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicited, amplified, and reinforced change talk using open questions, rulers, summaries, and requests for specifics, which increased Ethan’s stated confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled nonconfrontationally with double-sided reflections and redirection, but there were a few missed opportunities to more strategically explore the social rewards of drinking and selectively amplify change language.
