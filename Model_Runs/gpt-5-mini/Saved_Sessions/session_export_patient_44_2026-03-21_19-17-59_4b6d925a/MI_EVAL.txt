# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session with supportive affirmations and normalization of concerns. Tone and language conveyed clear respect and encouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good attempts to understand Erik’s perspective and asked feeling‑focused questions, but used relatively few explicit reflective statements. Interaction leaned more toward problem‑solving than deep empathic reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on exercise consistency, back‑friendly activity, and craving management from assessment through concrete homework. Session stayed on task and progressed logically to actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent solicitation of Erik’s preferences and repeated co‑construction of the plan (scheduling, checkpoints, log template) created a genuinely collaborative interaction. Clinician adapted recommendations to the patient’s choices and constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered options, asked permission, and left final decisions to Erik, supporting his sense of choice and control. Language avoided coercion and emphasized patient agency in planning and follow‑up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations, barriers, and prior helpful strategies and used those to tailor the plan, drawing on Erik’s reasons for change. Could deepen evocation by exploring broader values, confidence, and more explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership through invitations to choose questions and by adapting the plan to Erik’s preferences, but sometimes shifted toward expert advice. Greater solicitation of Erik’s own solutions before offering multi‑point recommendations would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change‑oriented language and invited commitment behaviors (scheduling, logging, 6‑week plan). Missed opportunities to amplify and reflect change statements to intensify commitment and elicit stronger explicit next‑step language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and redirected toward pragmatic solutions and personal success metrics, validating discouragement while focusing on action. Could further soften sustain talk with double‑sided/complex reflections and immediate pivots to eliciting change talk.
