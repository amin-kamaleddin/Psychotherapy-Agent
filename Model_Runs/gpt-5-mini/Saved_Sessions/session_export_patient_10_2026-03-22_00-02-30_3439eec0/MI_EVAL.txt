# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing Amina's experience and explicitly acknowledging competing priorities without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective‑taking that tracked affect and content, validated priorities, and helped Amina clarify specifics without minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on solving childcare barriers to attending interviews, with nearly every question tied to actionable next steps (who, what, when, backups).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High degree of co‑construction: the clinician invited Amina's input, adapted the text to her preferences, and offered concrete supports while responding to her choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Amina's choice and control by offering options, checking preferences, and acknowledging she could decline, which supported ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited internal motivation and practical reasons for change, but could more often amplify the client's motivational language and link it to longer‑term values or explicit commitment statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely treated Amina as the expert on her life, used power‑sharing language, and followed her lead when shaping the plan and message.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited multiple change statements and concrete steps and helped specify a plan, but missed opportunities to amplify and consolidate change talk with reflective summaries and commitment prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it and redirected toward feasible experiments, though more double‑sided reflections could have more actively resolved ambivalence.
