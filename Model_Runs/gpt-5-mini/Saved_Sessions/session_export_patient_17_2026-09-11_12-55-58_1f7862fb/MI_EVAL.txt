# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, supportive tone with explicit affirmations and reassurance, but some affirmations felt slightly formulaic and the patient’s strong stigma language (e.g., “like a leper”) was not deeply explored or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good reflective listening and perspective‑taking that elicited disclosure and validated stressors, but missed opportunities to probe and label the deeper shame/stigma emotions more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and established a concrete, measurable first step (delaying the first cigarette each morning) with specific implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the process as joint problem‑solving and co‑constructed practical steps, though at times offered a menu of options in a somewhat clinician‑driven way rather than eliciting more patient‑generated ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored the patient’s choice by offering multiple options, asking which felt most doable, and refraining from pushing any single approach, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited motivations, plans, and tentative commitments (e.g., 5/7 mornings) but could have probed deeper intrinsic values and past successes to strengthen internal motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the patient as the primary decision‑maker and invited her preferences, but occasionally steered toward practical steps instead of eliciting more of her spontaneous solutions or strengths.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified change talk with forward‑looking questions, offered specific options, and secured concrete commitments and implementation details that consolidated change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledgeed sustain talk without arguing and redirected to practical backups (gum, breathing, reminders), yet relied more on pragmatic redirection than advanced softening techniques like double‑sided reflections.
