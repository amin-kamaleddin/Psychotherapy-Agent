# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated warmth, nonjudgmental language, and respect (e.g., opening praise, honoring client choices). This maintained unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective statements captured priorities and strengths, but reflections stayed more on content and planning than deeper affect. Adding a few affect-focused reflections (e.g., around guilt or pride) would strengthen empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on values-based self-care and an email cutoff, moving toward concrete, actionable steps and a troubleshooting plan. The clinician balanced exploration and planning without drifting off target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created materials and integrated the client's language and preferences into drafts (autoresponder, urgency criteria, calendar note). The interaction functioned as an equal partnership rather than an expert lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively supported through explicit invitations, offering choices, and asking permission before proposing steps. The clinician consistently reinforced Elena's ownership of decisions and respected her pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited motivations, resources, and past successes and translated values into strategies, but could have more systematically amplified and consolidated intrinsic reasons for change. Asking for a brief commitment statement or selective summaries would deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the interaction as collaborative (asking permission, offering options, confirming drafts) and actively solicited the client's preferences at each step. This sustained power-sharing and mutual decision-making throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was frequently elicited and reinforced (values-to-steps translation, confidence scaling), but the clinician missed opportunities to amplify and consolidate it into a stronger commitment. More explicit reflections of readiness and prompts to raise confidence (e.g., 'What would make this a 9?') would build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and the clinician avoided reinforcing status-quo reasons, framing solutions to reduce pull toward old habits. A few strategic double-sided or normalizing reflections around guilt could further soften any lingering sustain talk.
