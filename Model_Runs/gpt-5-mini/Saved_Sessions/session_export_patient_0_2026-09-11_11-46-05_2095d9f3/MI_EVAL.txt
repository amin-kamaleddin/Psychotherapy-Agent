# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician communicated nonjudgmental, practical support and honored Adrian’s autonomy (e.g., accepted “I’m not quitting” and offered tangible help). This demonstrated consistent unconditional positive regard and respect for his social context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and questions showed clear understanding of Adrian’s ambivalence and context, using timely, accurate phrasing. To reach top score, reflections could have engaged more affective nuance (shame, fear) and deeper validation of feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing infection risk and consistently guided conversation toward concrete harm‑reduction steps and follow‑up. Questions and offers repeatedly returned to actionable supports and resources.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction read as a genuine two‑way working relationship: the clinician solicited Adrian’s preferences, negotiated options, and presented help as choices rather than prescriptions. Practical details (drop‑off, script wording, timing) were co‑produced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Respect for choice was explicit throughout, with open invitations to choose among options and no pressure to abstain. The clinician reinforced ownership over the plan and tailored follow‑up to Adrian’s preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out Adrian’s motives and specific strategies (avoid infection, spares, habit cues) using scales and elicitation. To improve, probe deeper into values and amplify his own change language to elicit stronger commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was framed as joint problem‑solving, repeatedly asking for Adrian’s opinion and letting him select first steps. Offers of help and planning were negotiated, reflecting high-level power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited preparatory and commitment‑leaning talk with concrete steps and a confidence scale, producing actionable plans. More forceful reflections and summaries highlighting Adrian’s willingness could have amplified change and elicited stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, but did limited active work to soften it when Adrian repeatedly said “I’m not quitting.” Using double‑sided reflections or strategic reframes would better reduce the pull of sustain talk.
