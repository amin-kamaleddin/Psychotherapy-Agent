# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating language, normalizing setbacks and avoiding blame or shaming. Reframed failures as data and gave the patient permission to set the tone of the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic reflections that matched the patient's emotional tone and validated skepticism and loneliness. At times responses moved quickly into advice, missing opportunities for deeper, complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on target behaviors with concrete, actionable experiments tied to the presenting problem. Frequently checked acceptability and returned the conversation to specific behavior changes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt largely collaborative with flexible strategies and a planned follow-up, and adaptations for the patient's constraints. Could strengthen collaboration by inviting more patient-generated solutions and summarizing choices together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported patient choice throughout, offering options and normalizing partial attempts while emphasizing lack of pressure. Consistently reinforced the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some commitment language and doable options but generally leaned toward offering solutions rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations. Limited use of open questions probing values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the encounter as collaborative and solicited input about what felt doable. However, the clinician sometimes provided directives without first eliciting the patient's ideas, so co-creation could be deeper.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced discrete change talk and framed small experiments as progress, encouraging follow-up and accountability. Would be stronger with more targeted open questions and reflections that amplify the patient's pro-change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully normalized and reframed sustain talk without arguing, acknowledging ambivalence and offering low-pressure options. Could further use double-sided reflections to explicitly name both sides and shift toward change talk more assertively.
