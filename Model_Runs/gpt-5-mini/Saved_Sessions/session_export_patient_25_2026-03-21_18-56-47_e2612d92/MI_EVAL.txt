# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed respect and positive regard, validated successes (quitting smoking), and avoided judgment or shaming throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and use of brief reflections (breathing exercise, acknowledging worry), but missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection on role conflict and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related risk and relapse prevention, guiding interventions toward an actionable plan (refusal lines, code word, leave time).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership in co-creating the plan, adjusting tone to the patient's preference, role-playing together, and treating patient input as central to the strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options rather than directives, asked which approaches felt doable, and framed advice as optional, respecting the patient's choice throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical ideas and choices (which option felt doable, rehearsal) but did not probe deeply into personal values, motivations, or confidence to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and shared power in planning and practice, though early unprompted listing of many clinician-generated options made the interaction slightly more expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively supported and reinforced change-oriented language with options and rehearsal, but could more strategically amplify the patient's own change statements and elicit explicit commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed relapse risk supportively, yet did not consistently use double-sided reflections or explicitly name the pull toward drinking while pivoting to change.
