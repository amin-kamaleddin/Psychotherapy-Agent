# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth from the start, normalizing feelings and affirming the patient's efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking summarizing ambivalence, but stopped short of deeper emotional exploration or more complex reflections of feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear harm‑reduction focus and steered toward concrete safety steps, though could more explicitly prioritize a single time‑limited SMART next step to consolidate momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like a true partnership with frequent invitations for input, co‑construction of messages and plans, and tailoring to logistical constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used invitational language, offered options rather than directives, and reinforced that choices and pacing were the patient's to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk and commitments (texts, trainings, safety steps) but could deepen motivation by drawing out personal values, reasons for change, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively shared power and invited the patient's preferences throughout, asking for details and co‑creating practical, patient-selected steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk leading to specific steps and readiness language, though could amplify and selectively reflect change statements to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and ambivalence without reinforcing it, but did not consistently use double‑sided or reframing reflections to pivot toward change or explore alternatives to 'escape.'
