# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, avoided blame, and legitimized Tariq’s feelings (e.g., “I won’t grill you”). The clinician prioritized his immediate concern and used validating language throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with immediate reflections of emotion and content. Also validated his history of compliance, matching the patient’s self-presentation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (securing the brief phone call) from opening to role-play. Interventions were repeatedly tied back to the immediate goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was highly collaborative: preferences were solicited, scripts were co-constructed, and the clinician role‑played and coached rather than directed. Tariq’s choices meaningfully shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported choice by asking permission, offering a menu of options, and inviting Tariq to choose tone and strategy. Language consistently framed actions as his decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked immediate motivation tied to family and compliance history, using these as levers for action. Could have further explored deeper values, long-term goals, and confidence/commitment strength.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Tariq as a collaborator and expert, offering options and co-creating role-plays while asking permission before interventions. Decision-making was clearly shared and patient-centered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified available change talk about the family connection and phone call, and created concrete practice opportunities. Missed chances to deepen reasons for change, probe confidence, and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk about officer blame and redirected to practical options, which reduced focus on maintaining the status quo. Could add more double-sided reflections and explore costs of not acting to further soften sustain talk.
