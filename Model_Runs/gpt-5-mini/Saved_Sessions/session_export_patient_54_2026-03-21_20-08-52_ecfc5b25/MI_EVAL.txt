# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating language that respects the patient’s cultural perspective and priorities. Offers affirmation and avoids blame throughout the conversation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking with frequent reflective statements and summaries that capture ambivalence and values. Checks permission before offering information, showing respectful attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, focused agenda on alcohol use in pregnancy and concrete next steps such as removing alcohol, partner communication, and scripts. Interventions consistently align with the stated target behavior and patient needs.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as an equal, team-based process with frequent invitations to set priorities and incorporate the patient’s choices. Co-constructs wording and plans while following the patient’s lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice and control by asking permission, offering multiple options, and avoiding pressuring language. Reinforces that the patient will decide next steps, honoring independence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively builds on the patient’s motivation by translating values into steps and asking what would be helpful, but relies somewhat on supplying wording and information. Could increase open questions to elicit more personal reasons, confidence, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the work as collaborative, asking what would help and offering to practice while following the patient’s preferences. Power is shared through choice and joint planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encourages preparatory change talk and concrete planning (brochure, scripts, role-play) and affirms chosen plans. Would be stronger with more frequent open-ended elicitation and amplification of the patient’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids argumentation and offers neutral, evidence-based information when the patient expresses doubts, steering away from escalation. Could more actively use MI strategies (double-sided reflections, selective amplification of change statements) to more directly soften sustain talk.
