# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect through normalizing, affirming language and nonjudgmental stance. Validates constraints (work/family) and acknowledges feelings, supporting a high rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with timely, reflective listening and summaries that capture both motivation and barriers. Repeats the patient's language and emotional content to build strong rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on starting exercise and moves from motivation to concrete planning with specific options, schedule, and fallback strategies. Session purposefully progresses toward actionable steps and follow-ups.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as an equal partnership—offers options and tools while the patient selects and tailors them. Negotiates reminder wording and accountability together, keeping the patient's voice central to the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports patient choice by offering options, checking preferences, and repeatedly emphasizing the patient's control over decisions. Suggestions are offered with consent and framed as choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits intrinsic motivation and uses change-talk prompts and confidence/readiness rulers, but could probe deeper into personal values and longer-term meaning to strengthen internalized reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly collaborative and client-led, with the clinician inviting selection and co-constructing the plan. Language emphasizes shared work and joint problem-solving throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk and past successes, and uses readiness rulers, but could more consistently amplify and sequence the patient's change statements to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (tiredness, perfectionism) without reinforcing it and immediately pivots to specific strategies and fallback options. Validates the barrier briefly then reduces its weight through problem-solving.
