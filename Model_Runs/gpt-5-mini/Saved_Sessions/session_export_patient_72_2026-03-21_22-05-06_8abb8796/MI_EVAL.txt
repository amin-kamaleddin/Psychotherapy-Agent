### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language and affirmed Leila’s strengths and choices, offering options rather than directives. Endorsed her autonomy and competence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, consistent reflective listening and perspective-taking by paraphrasing and summarizing Leila’s experience and priorities. Questions were framed to understand what mattered to her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on restarting sustainable physical activity and consistently guided the session toward concrete planning and follow‑through. Each turn linked values/motivation to specific actions, times, and backups.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the exchange as a joint enterprise and treated Leila as an equal partner, co‑creating the plan and offering phrasing options. Built on Leila’s ideas rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Leila’s sense of choice by offering options, inviting tweaks, and respecting her decisions (e.g., not signing up if staff are pushy). Language reinforced that Leila controls what will work for her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Leila’s own motivations, reasons, and plans rather than supplying them, amplifying her change talk and using it to deepen commitment. Elicited concrete motives and specific steps she valued.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicited preferences, invited Leila to set specifics, and co‑constructed reminders and celebratory messages. Repeatedly checked what support she wanted from the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, turning desires and confidence into concrete commitments with timing and backups. Reinforced her reasons for change and used them to build a feasible action plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and quickly redirected to workable solutions and backups, acknowledging barriers without judgment. Could increase use of explicit double‑sided reflections to name ambivalence before moving fully into planning in more ambivalent cases.
