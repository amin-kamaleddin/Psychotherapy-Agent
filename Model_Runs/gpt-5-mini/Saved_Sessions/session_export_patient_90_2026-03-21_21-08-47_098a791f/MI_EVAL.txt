# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect, warmth, and a nonjudgmental stance, validating Marcus's overwhelm and constraints. Offered options rather than directives and avoided shaming or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections that captured content and affect, normalizing Marcus's fears and overwhelm. Could deepen empathic connection by probing the emotional meaning of the dog and the toll of repeated medical demands with more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on asthma management with specific behavioral targets and a time-limited 4–6 week trial. Recommendations stayed practical and tied directly to the clinical concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine partnership by inviting Marcus to shape the plan and adapting suggestions to his constraints. Provided tools and options that reflected Marcus's preferences and needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and framed decisions as Marcus's to make, accepting his preference to keep the dog and pick a small, feasible step. Avoided pressure and reinforced ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical commitment and preferences using open questions and a feasible plan, successfully drawing out change-oriented statements. Could further evoke intrinsic motivation by eliciting values, consequences, and using importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Marcus as the expert on his life and co-created the plan, asking permission and offering choices. Power sharing was evident as Marcus's choices directly guided the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively invited and reinforced change talk, eliciting a concrete commitment to the bedroom rule and tracking. Missed opportunities to amplify and systematically elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, and confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and proposed alternatives that preserved the dog, effectively softening resistance. Could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain concerns then pivot toward change.
