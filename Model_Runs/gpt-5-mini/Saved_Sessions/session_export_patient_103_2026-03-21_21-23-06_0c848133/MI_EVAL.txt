### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing and validating Nabil’s anger without blame. The clinician accepts his feelings and builds from them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, matching language and body cues (tight chest, jaw clench) and advancing the conversation. Reflections and questions moved the interaction forward empathetically.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused agenda on managing anger in appointment delays with concrete tools tied to that target behavior. Interventions (grounding, scripts, rehearsal, contingency plans) consistently relate back to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a joint endeavor, inviting input at every step and offering suggestions rather than directives. The clinician follows the patient’s lead and adjusts the plan to fit his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and emphasizes the patient’s control over what to try, reinforcing ownership of the plan. Language and options support Nabil’s sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and specific commitments (scripts, time, readiness rating) but could probe deeper into Nabil’s personal reasons and values to strengthen intrinsic motivation. More exploration of consequences or values would deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructs the plan with the patient, asks preferences, and offers role‑play only with consent, sharing power throughout the session. The stance is clearly collaborative and respectful.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces concrete change talk (in-the-moment strategy, scripts, readiness/confidence), and arranges rehearsal and a specific time. Missed chances to amplify and summarize the patient’s own reasons for change to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirects toward action with discreet tools and confidence queries. Could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides and further soften sustain talk.
