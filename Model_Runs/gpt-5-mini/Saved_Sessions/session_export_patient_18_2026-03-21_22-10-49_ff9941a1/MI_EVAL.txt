# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys clear unconditional positive regard and respect, using affirmations and permissive language to reduce threat and normalize the client's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate, facilitative reflections and responds to concerns, but reflections are mostly surface-level and could probe deeper feelings, values, or emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol risk reduction with concrete skill-building and consistent guidance toward specific, observable behaviors and preparations for high‑risk moments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is co-constructed—clinician solicits the client's input at each step, follows her lead in role-plays, and offers invitational feedback rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's agency by offering options, asking permission before interventions, and reinforcing that the client decides what to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits actionable motivation around tactics and plans through practice, but does not sufficiently draw out intrinsic motives, values, or confidence to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as a collaborator rather than an expert—offers choices, asks which options feel helpful, and incorporates the client's preferences into session direction and practice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces practical change talk and commitment via options and role‑play, but stops short of systematically probing importance, reasons, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing current behavior and shifting to prevention, though it misses opportunities for explicit double-sided reflections to address ambivalence.
