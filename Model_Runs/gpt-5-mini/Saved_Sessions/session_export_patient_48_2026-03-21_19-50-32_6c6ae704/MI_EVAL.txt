# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing and validating Farid's concerns and tentative choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely, specific reflections (e.g., linking sighs to meaning and acknowledging embarrassment), showing strong attunement to Farid's affect and hesitancy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on increasing brief exercise with every intervention tied back to that target behavior and no significant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative with options and invitations to decide, but the clinician often led with clinician-generated proposals rather than eliciting Farid's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Farid's sense of choice by offering options, asking which steps he'd prefer, and respecting his reluctance to involve friends.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort to elicit motivation (commitment time, confidence rating), but the session relied heavily on clinician-provided tools and templates instead of drawing out Farid's intrinsic reasons, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making and invited input, yet provided many concrete recommendations which slightly shifted control back to the clinician instead of co-creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions and a commitment ask, but could further amplify intrinsic reasons and commitment language through strategic reflections and summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used empathic reframing and low-pressure backups, though more double-sided reflections could have better acknowledged ambivalence while steering toward change.
