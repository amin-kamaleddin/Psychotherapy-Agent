# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly conveyed nonjudgmental support and used respectful language throughout, accepting Tariq’s feelings without minimizing them. They honored Tariq’s request not to lecture and maintained unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate, timely reflections captured Tariq’s frustration, guarded vulnerability, and family focus, deepening rapport. The clinician’s perspective-taking was strong and responsive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the behavioral target—preparing the call and managing interruptions—with targeted questions and concrete planning. The clinician maintained appropriate goal-directedness throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked as an equal partner by soliciting preferences, tailoring the script to Tariq’s words, inviting practice, and building an agreed follow-up plan. Language and actions supported joint problem-solving rather than directive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirmed Tariq’s choice and control by asking permission, offering options, and framing suggestions as choices. This consistently fostered agency and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Tariq’s internal motivation around family connection and translated those values into actionable language and a concrete plan. Motivation and reasons for action were drawn from Tariq rather than supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician invited Tariq’s input, incorporated his wording into the script, and checked preferences for practice and follow-up, reinforcing a collaborative stance. Tariq’s ideas were accepted and integrated immediately.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited and reinforced change talk and co-constructed a doable script, encouraging practice. They could have more actively amplified change language and sought a confidence/commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk and blame were acknowledged non-confrontationally, which avoided escalation, but MI-specific strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections, exploring costs) were not used. The clinician mainly redirected to call prep, missing opportunities to resolve ambivalence and reduce externalizing blame.
