# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed sustained unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation throughout, repeatedly thanking and affirming the patient's choices and concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective statements matched the patient's worries about stability and need for clear information, with supportive guidance for emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear safety-focused agenda (pause citalopram, washout, call prescriber/pharmacy) and consistently returned to concrete, actionable steps and contingency plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction: the patient set the agenda and the clinician tailored scripts, asked permission, rehearsed responses, and co‑constructed the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician preserved patient control by offering options (call vs text, rehearse vs not), avoided pressure, and reinforced that final decisions rest with the prescriber and patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective at eliciting concrete commitments and rehearsals for action, but did less to draw out the patient's underlying values, motives, or confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear power-sharing throughout the visit: the clinician invited input, adapted to requests, and worked alongside the patient to craft usable scripts and plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully generated actionable change language and commitments via scripts and rehearsal, but relied more on giving wording than on open questions to elicit the patient's own reasons or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician validated the patient's concern about stability and provided harm‑minimizing advice (do not stop phenelzine), yet could more often use double‑sided reflections or redirecting phrasing to gently shift sustain talk.
