# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and validates ambivalence with warm, nonjudgmental language and supportive reflections. No punitive or directive tone was present, reinforcing a safe, accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with accurate, content-focused reflections of the patient's perspective and pragmatic concerns. Missed opportunities for deeper affective or complex reflections to explore underlying emotions driving use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on harm reduction and immediate safety actions, repeatedly steering toward concrete, proximal steps (practicing wording, safer injecting, timing). Directional control was MI-consistent and respected the patient's priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as mutual problem-solving with the clinician adapting options to the patient's preferences and inviting active participation. The clinician positioned the patient as an agent in co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options and choices were routinely offered and the clinician checked preferences before suggesting phrasing, supporting the patient's sense of control. The clinician avoided pressuring for abstinence and affirmed the patient's priorities.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations and values (e.g., housing, safety) and used them to scaffold concrete change language and plans. Questions drew out readiness and converted it into implementable steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative language ("we're in this together") and frequent invitations for the patient to choose priorities demonstrated strong partnership. Power-sharing was evident in how options and plans were negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, converting general willingness into specific, imminent steps and rehearsals (e.g., telling housemate by the weekend). Reflections and invitations to plan amplified commitment language and implementation intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged the positive functions of use and pivoted toward safety without arguing, helping reduce sustain talk. Could strengthen practice with more double-sided or summary reflections and explicit reframes to further soften sustain talk.
