# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard, normalized ambivalence, and used warm, nonblaming language throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and accurate, complex reflections captured Rosa's feelings and worries, with regular checks of understanding before proposing options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on smoking behavior with a concrete, achievable goal (30‑minute delay) and linked questions and reflections back to that goal without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The encounter was framed as joint problem-solving, with the clinician soliciting Rosa's ideas, integrating them into the plan, and deferring decision-making to her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly reinforced choice and control by offering multiple options, asking which felt doable, and emphasizing the small‑test nature of the plan with no coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked Rosa's practical solutions and intentions (delay, supports) but relied mainly on short‑term, pragmatic steps and missed deeper elicitation of values, long‑term motives, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative with shared power—clinician invited choices (Plan A/B), used Rosa's knowledge of routines/supports, and co‑created the implementation plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited and reinforced concrete proximal change talk (delay, reminders, supports) but missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motives and assess importance/confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged with double‑sided reflections and redirection toward small experiments, though the clinician could more actively explore smoking's functions and amplify discrepancy to resolve ambivalence faster.
