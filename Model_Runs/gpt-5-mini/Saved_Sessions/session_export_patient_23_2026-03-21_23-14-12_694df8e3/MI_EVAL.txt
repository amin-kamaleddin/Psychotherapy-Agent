# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect through validating reflections and affirmations, creating a warm nonjudgmental stance. No labeling or minimization of the patient’s concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates high-quality empathic listening with accurate reflections of both emotional and practical aspects, deepening rapport. Reflections mirror the patient’s experience and encourage further disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol and concrete strategies, tying each question and reflection back to the target behavior. Session progresses logically from ambivalence to specific, measurable plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine co‑construction of the plan by eliciting options, preferences, and monitoring methods, making decisions mutual and context‑based. Builds on the patient’s routines and strengths for joint problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, asking for preferences, and framing suggestions as choices rather than directives. Affirms that the plan belongs to the patient and avoids pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits the patient’s intrinsic reasons, values, and expected outcomes, and links them to concrete actions. Avoids supplying reasons and instead helps the patient articulate motivation and resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the patient as the expert and invites collaboration through questions that transfer authority and invite co‑creation of the plan. Regularly checks in about what feels doable and respects the patient’s knowledge of her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively and skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk using open questions, affirmations, and requests for elaboration, resulting in multiple instances of patient change language. Links change talk to values and concrete steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and validates ambivalence, using reflections to shift toward change, but misses a few opportunities for explicit double‑sided reflections or reframing skeptical statements into preparatory change talk. Overall redirects effectively but could more consistently summarize ambivalence before pivoting to change.
