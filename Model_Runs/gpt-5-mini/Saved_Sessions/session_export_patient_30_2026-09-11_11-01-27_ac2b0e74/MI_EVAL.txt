# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows nonjudgmental, respectful regard and normalizes ambivalence with repeated acknowledgments, sustaining unconditional positive regard throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrases demonstrate understanding of ambivalence, though the clinician could probe deeper into the emotional weight and specific fears (e.g., what scares you most about muscle weakness?).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete behavior (short walks) and reaches a time‑bound plan, but the medication concern remains a parallel issue without a plan to address or revisit it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work is clearly co‑constructed—patient preferences, prior successes, and support strategies are elicited and integrated rather than the clinician prescribing a solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently emphasizes choice, offers multiple options, asks the patient to pick among them, and validates the patient's decision to delay medication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits intrinsic motives for walking (family time, energy, routine strengths) but could better evoke personal reasons or values related to cholesterol management and medication decisions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction reflects genuine partnership with joint decision‑making, invitations for the patient's input, and plans built around the patient's routine and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is actively elicited and shaped into a specific SMART plan, but the clinician missed opportunities to amplify commitment/confidence language and use scaling or stronger affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and double‑sided reflections are used to avoid argument, yet there is limited strategic reframing or exploration of the underlying fear so sustain talk about meds persists.
