# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with validations and normalizing language (e.g., acknowledging desire for clear answers). Would reach a 5 with warmer, more individualized affirmations and explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and accurate reflections that mirror the patient's priorities about LDL and muscle symptoms. Lacks deeper emotional or value-focused reflections linking concerns (e.g., father's MI or what weakness would mean to him).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on the decision about cholesterol medication with clear thresholds and a concrete decision pathway (labs → risk calculation → lifestyle trial/med options). Provides actionable next steps and maintains clinical focus throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative, offers co-created tools (checklist, script) and invites input such as bringing labs. Still clinician-led in content and could better elicit the patient's own ideas for feasible lifestyle changes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors the patient's preferences by offering a lifestyle trial, low-dose/alternative options, and explicit permission to delay medication pending numbers. Strongly reinforces patient control over the decision-making process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to draw out priorities (asking what the patient wants and offering to calculate risk), but relies mainly on delivering medical information. Misses opportunities for evocative questions to elicit intrinsic motivations, values, and change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites patient input and co-creates practical tools that support shared decision-making. Interaction remains somewhat clinician-driven and would benefit from more explicit statements recognizing the patient's expertise and daily context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Encourages practical steps (3-month lifestyle trial, checklist) that create opportunities for change talk, but does not strategically evoke or amplify the patient's own reasons, desires, or confidence. Change talk is mostly superficial and information-focused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively acknowledges and reframes sustain talk, offers alternatives (dose changes, monitoring) and a lifestyle trial to reduce concerns about muscle symptoms. Would be stronger with double-sided reflections to explicitly balance sustain and change talk.
