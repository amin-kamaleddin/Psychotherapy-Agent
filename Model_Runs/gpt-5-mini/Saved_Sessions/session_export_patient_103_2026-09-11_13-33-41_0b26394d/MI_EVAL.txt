# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating the client's priorities and constraints without blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate, and timely reflections capture both content and feeling, de-escalating emotion and keeping the client engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on managing anger and responses to wait times, steering every prompt toward specific, actionable solutions and resisting drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong mutual problem-solving with repeated invitations for the client to choose wording and priorities; interventions are co-created rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers options and respects the client's judgment, framing trialing the plan as voluntary and low-cost to reinforce choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivations linked to protecting time and job and translates them into steps, but does not deeply probe underlying values or explicitly strengthen commitment/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as collaborative, treating the client as the expert and co-creating practical strategies together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk, operationalizes the client's plan (one-liner, phone note, 10-minute rule), and checks for small wins that build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes skepticism and reframes it as a low-cost experiment, but could more often use double-sided reflections or strategic summarizing to tip ambivalence toward change.
