# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician used a warm, nonjudgmental tone, normalized ambivalence, and consistently validated the client's limits without shaming. This created a safe, accepting atmosphere throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking captured the client's situation and readiness (e.g., “You sound nervous but hopeful”). More complex reflections probing the emotional weight of mood concerns and finishing a bottle would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing evening wine with concrete action steps (limits, measured pours, delay techniques) and follow-up planning. Questions about relapse response, start time, and confidence sustained momentum toward implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked as an equal partner, offering options and inviting the client's choices for supports and follow-up. Decisions were co‑constructed and client-driven, demonstrating genuine collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported autonomy by asking permission, offering options rather than directives, and affirming the client's right to choose. This reinforced client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited the client's own solutions, confidence rating, and supports, encouraging self-generated strategies. Greater probing of intrinsic motives, values, and amplified reflections of change reasons would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership was evident through joint problem-solving, asking permission to offer ideas, and inviting input on check‑ins and priorities. The clinician framed the work as collaborative and shared power effectively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete harm‑reduction options and commitment planning (start tonight, supports, confidence rating). Opportunities remain to amplify change reasons and draw out stronger commitment language and implementation intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and acknowledged reluctance, steering toward practical harm‑reduction strategies. Using more double‑sided reflections and selective reflections of sustain talk would better soften sustain language and help resolve ambivalence.
