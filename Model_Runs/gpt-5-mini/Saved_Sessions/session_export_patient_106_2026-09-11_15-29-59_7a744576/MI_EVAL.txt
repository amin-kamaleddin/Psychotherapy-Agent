# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the patient's dilemma and affirming small steps. Language avoided blame and repeatedly validated the patient's constraints and efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking captured the core dilemma and summarized the patient's statements. Could deepen emotional reflection at times to more fully validate underlying stress, loneliness, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing smoking/exposure and steadily guided toward concrete, immediate steps. Questions narrowed toward actionable planning without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created the plan with the patient, frequently checking preferences and offering options. The session treated the patient as an equal partner and followed her lead in deciding next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly honored the patient's choice and control, offering options rather than directives and asking what the patient preferred. Offers of help were conditional on the patient's desire, supporting agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own motivations and intentions, generating several self-motivating statements and concrete plans. Could more actively deepen and amplify intrinsic motivations and elicit importance/confidence ratings to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with joint decision-making and shared planning (selecting a friend, wording the text, setting reminders). The clinician consistently invited the patient's input and honored her real-life constraints.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and shaped change talk through targeted open questions and concrete planning, producing statements favoring change. To improve, the clinician could more consistently amplify and reflect the patient's own change language and commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided confrontation and redirected sustain talk toward feasible harm-reduction steps while acknowledging both sides. Could use more double-sided reflections or summaries that name sustain arguments then pivot to the patient's reasons for change.
