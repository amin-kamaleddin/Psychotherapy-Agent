# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful with explicit positive regard, normalizing ambivalence and acknowledging the patient's choices. No shaming or labeling was used, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and supportive, summarizing context and consequences well. Could deepen rapport with more complex/emotional reflections to capture defensiveness and social pressures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on weekend drinking and harm-reduction throughout the session, moving logically from assessment to concrete planning. Interventions repeatedly returned to concrete goals and a one-line plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative exchange by soliciting preferences and co-creating a checklist and backup plan. Could strengthen by eliciting more of the patient's own ideas and checking that plans fit his social context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the patient's autonomy by offering options, asking permission, and framing plans as voluntary. Avoided coercive language and respected the patient's right to accept or decline strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out small commitments and change talk (eating beforehand, water, planning a ride) using open invitations to plan. Could increase evocation by probing personal values, reasons, and using scaling to surface internal motivators.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered joint problem-solving, asked which options felt doable, and invited patient choice. At times the clinician proposed specific plans which slightly shifted toward expert guidance rather than fully privileging the patient's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced actionable change talk that led to concrete commitments and low-effort strategies. To improve, explore the patient's own reasons, importance, and confidence more deeply (e.g., scaling questions, linking to values).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation, normalized ambivalence, and shifted to harm-reduction strategies. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections that could further soften sustain talk and invite resolution.
