# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys warmth and unconditional positive regard (e.g., thanking the patient, normalizing anxiety) without blame or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking that deepen rapport and explicitly links the patient's feelings and ratings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on reducing alcohol use and protecting reproductive health, guiding conversation toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Truly collaborative — co-creates scripts and plans, invites patient choices, role-plays, and follows the patient's lead in setting priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's freedom to choose by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding prescriptive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits internal motivation and links it to strategies (family history, desire to avoid alcoholism) but could use deeper open-ended prompts and follow-up scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the patient as the primary decision-maker, invites collaboration, and shares power in planning rather than prescribing actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk (asks about small steps, links benefits) but missed opportunities to amplify reasons and deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to planning, but employed few MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly soften ambivalence.
