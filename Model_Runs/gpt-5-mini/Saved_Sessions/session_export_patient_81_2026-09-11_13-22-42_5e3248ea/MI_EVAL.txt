# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and explicitly validated Lukas’s perspective, normalizing ambivalence and avoiding lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking with summaries of ambivalence and practical concerns, but reflections stayed behavior-focused rather than probing deeper feelings about identity and belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on reducing meth use with a concrete one-night-a-week experiment and successive implementation steps that purposefully moved the plan forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction: the clinician invited Lukas to set specifics, offered options, and co-constructed the plan based on his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice by presenting options and asking which felt most doable, reinforcing Lukas’s ownership without pressuring or prescribing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Lukas’s own reasons for change (protecting rent/auditions) and generated concrete steps, but could have probed deeper values, emotional significance, and confidence to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician positioned Lukas as the expert, repeatedly asking for his preferences and sharing power in planning, demonstrating strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change language and concrete plans, though the clinician could more actively amplify and summarize change talk and assess importance/confidence to consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used double-sided reflections, steering toward implementation, but did not deeply explore or normalize sustain concerns about identity and belonging.
