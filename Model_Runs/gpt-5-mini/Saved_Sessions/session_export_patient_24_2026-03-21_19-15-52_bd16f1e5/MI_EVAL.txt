# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, with explicit affirmations and safety checks; avoided shaming and validated the patient's perspective throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used validating statements and some reflections that acknowledged concerns about sleep and recovery; often shifted quickly into didactic information instead of deeper, complex reflections of ambivalence or minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use tied to marathon training and moved logically from feedback to concrete behavior-change steps and measurable outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was a collaborative problem‑solving process with co‑created logs, check‑ins, and negotiated targets; invited patient input and tailored the plan to his schedule and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by offering multiple options, asking what felt doable, and framing the trial as the patient's decision while emphasizing non‑shaming responses to slips.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation by linking change to the patient's goals and using choice questions to prompt commitment, but could have used more open questions and amplifications to have the patient articulate his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative with permission‑seeking and shared planning, though at times the clinician gave expert recommendations and technical details without checking the patient's preference for that level of information.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and helped set realistic targets tied to personal goals, but could more explicitly reflect and amplify the patient's own change language and elicit stronger commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward a low‑threat experiment while normalizing slips; missed some opportunities for double‑sided reflections that acknowledge both reasons for drinking and training goals.
