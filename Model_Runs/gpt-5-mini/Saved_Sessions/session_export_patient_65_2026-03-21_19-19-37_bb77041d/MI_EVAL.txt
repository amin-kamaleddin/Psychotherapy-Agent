# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed consistent nonjudgmental support and positive regard, normalizing anxiety and offering low‑pressure options. Boundaries and reluctance to explore feelings were respected throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic statements and normalization were used (e.g., 'calling can feel scary') and some reflective listening occurred. However, reflections stayed surface‑level and missed deeper emotions like shame, fear of consequences, and guardedness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear behavioral focus on completing and documenting court‑ordered hours, with nearly every intervention tied to that target. Practical, actionable steps (scripts, checklist, backup plans) kept the work on task.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative: clinician adapted scripts and role‑play to Julian's requests and repeatedly asked for his preferences. The clinician co‑created contingency plans and invited Julian to take the lead on next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice by offering multiple options (call, email, bring someone, rehearse now or later) and checking what Julian wanted. Language avoided coercion and reinforced client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician briefly invited action (rehearse, call, debrief) but relied on practical problem‑solving instead of drawing out internal motivation or personal values. Opportunities to assess importance/confidence or elicit personal reasons were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used collaborative language and solicited input frequently, promoting shared decision‑making. Some interactions shifted to expert/informational delivery (full scripts, checklists) rather than co‑constructing solutions from Julian's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were attempts to invite commitment and brief encouragements, but the clinician did not actively elicit or amplify Julian's own reasons, desires, or confidence. Change talk remained largely clinician‑led through practical steps rather than client‑generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered alternatives to avoidance, but did not strategically explore or reframe Julian's concerns. Missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections to reduce resistance and shift ambivalence were noted.
