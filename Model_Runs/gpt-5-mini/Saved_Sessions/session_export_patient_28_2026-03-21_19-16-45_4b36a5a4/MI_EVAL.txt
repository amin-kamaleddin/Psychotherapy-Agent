# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validation (e.g., normalizing and supportive language). Could occasionally state explicit acceptance of the patient's current choice to further reduce perceived judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and acknowledged life constraints, matching the patient's affect. Missed opportunities for deeper affective reflections when the patient expressed discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on restarting physical activity and guided the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps and a follow‑up plan. Direction was strong without losing the patient's concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone and many decisions were collaborative, with patient input on days/times and check‑ins. The clinician often supplied ready‑made solutions, which modestly reduced equal partnering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered multiple options, asked preferences, and emphasized that misses are data, which supported choice. Occasional prescriptive language (e.g., 'make it non‑negotiable') risked undermining autonomy; asking permission before advice would help.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of change (start date, doable steps) occurred, but the session relied heavily on clinician-provided strategies rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons, values, and past successes. More open questions and reflective amplification of the patient's motivations are needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by inviting choices and co‑creation of a plan. However, supplying detailed schedules and plans shifted some authority toward the clinician instead of eliciting the patient's solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced explicit change language and commitments (two‑week trial, schedule). Could deepen change talk by using fewer prescriptive solutions and more strategic reflections/amplifications of the patient's own statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers without confrontation and offered pragmatic adaptations and normalization, which reduced sustain talk. Using more double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence would further soften sustain language.
