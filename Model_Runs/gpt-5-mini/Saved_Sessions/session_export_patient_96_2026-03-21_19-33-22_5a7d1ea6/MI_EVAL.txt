# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Marisol’s experience, honoring her choices and avoiding labeling or admonishment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that named Marisol’s frustration, though reflections stayed relatively surface-level and missed deeper exploration of values or identity related to the piercing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on the lip piercing and oral-health implications, guiding the session toward concrete outcomes (phrases, role-plays, aftercare and 3-month checkups).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—invited Marisol to co-create responses, offered choices, and used role-play while adapting to her input to solve the problem together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Marisol’s right to choose, offered options rather than pressure, and asked which approach she preferred, reinforcing her sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some commitments to aftercare but often supplied scripts and solutions instead of using evocative questions to draw out Marisol’s intrinsic motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a partnership stance by asking permission and inviting input, but providing multiple scripted lines shifted some control toward the clinician rather than privileging the patient’s own language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited behaviorally specific change talk about aftercare and checkups, but did not strategically elicit or amplify intrinsic reasons for monitoring or protective behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward harm-reduction and boundary strategies, though could use more double-sided reflections to acknowledge both appeal and risks.
