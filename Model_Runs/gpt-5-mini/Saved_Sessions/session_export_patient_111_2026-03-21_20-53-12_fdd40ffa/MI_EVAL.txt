# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician opened with a clear nonjudgmental stance and consistently validated Richard's perspective, avoiding labeling or moralizing. Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout interactions, even when he expressed resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections captured both content and feeling and moved the conversation forward. The clinician demonstrated strong perspective-taking and reflective listening, summarizing ambivalence effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking and practical change steps, using agenda-setting, scaling, and a concrete experiment plan. Summaries and follow-up logistics kept the conversation goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician solicited Richard's input at multiple points and co-constructed the plan, framing suggestions as experiments and implementing the client's choices. Interaction felt like genuine two-way problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were emphasized, permission was sought, and options were offered rather than directives, preserving Richard's control. The clinician reinforced that slips are normal and supported self-efficacy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited client reasons for change and used scaling to generate change language, but could have more deeply drawn out intrinsic motivations and values. More targeted probes to amplify relational reasons would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Richard as the expert on his experience, asking permission and offering options while deferring to his preferences. Power-sharing was evident in selecting experiments, reminders, and follow-up details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited and reinforced through questions, scaling, and endorsing a small-step experiment, but the clinician missed opportunities to amplify relational change talk. More selective reflections and probes could deepen and multiply client-generated reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing, used double-sided framing, and redirected sustain talk toward low-risk experiments that preserved valued benefits. Additional strategic softening (stronger double-sided reflections and explicit discrepancy work) would further reduce sustain talk.
