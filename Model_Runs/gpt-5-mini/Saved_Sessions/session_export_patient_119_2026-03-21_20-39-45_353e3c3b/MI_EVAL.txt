# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental, respectful language and frequent validation of constraints and strengths. The clinician normalized difficulties and avoided labeling or moralizing throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking and accurate reflective listening that helped Selene open up about ambivalence and barriers. Timely paraphrases and summaries captured context and feelings effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on medication adherence and moved from exploration to concrete implementation planning. The clinician set specific tasks (reminders, calendar times, pharmacy blister packs) tied directly to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as genuine joint problem-solving with frequent solicitation of Selene’s views and co-creation of tools (scripts, checklist, reminders). The clinician adapted plans to the patient’s preferences and reinforced shared responsibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options and asking permission before preparing materials, respecting Selene’s pace and preferences. The clinician avoided pressure and preserved the client's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic reasons and practical self-generated solutions (e.g., desire to stay healthy, pharmacy packs) but could deepen evocation by linking to personal values and eliciting stronger commitment language. More elaborative questions and value-focused prompts would strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the process as collaborative and repeatedly asked for Selene’s input, allowing her choices to shape the plan (pharmacy packs, calendar reminder). Permission-seeking and shared decision-making were evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk using scaling and choices and highlighted motivations, but missed chances to amplify and summarize change language into firmer commitment. Could have probed for more concrete if-then plans or verbal commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk without arguing, reframing barriers as solvable and avoiding confrontation. Could improve by using more double-sided reflections to validate sustain talk while nudging toward change.
