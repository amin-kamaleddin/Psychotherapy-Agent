# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician shows respect and normalizes Jamal’s struggle several times, which builds nonjudgmental rapport. However, acceptance feels somewhat transactional and quickly shifts into problem‑solving rather than sustained unconditional support.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician uses reflections and validation that convey understanding of Jamal’s feelings and social pressure. Reflections are brief and often followed by advice, missing opportunities for deeper, complex reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing drinking at team hangouts while preserving Jamal’s team spot. Every intervention is tied to that concrete behavioral target with rehearsals and specific plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone—clinician invites choices, role‑play, and adapts suggestions to Jamal’s preferences. Still, the clinician largely sets the agenda and supplies content rather than co‑constructing options from Jamal’s ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offers choices, multiple scripts, and asks which options Jamal prefers, supporting his ownership of decisions. There is no evident pressure to accept a single plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little was done to elicit Jamal’s intrinsic motivations, values, or reasons for change with open, evocative questions. The interaction emphasizes giving solutions rather than drawing out Jamal’s own motivations or meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician invites Jamal’s input and role‑plays with him, sharing some power through choices. However, the interaction remains clinician‑led and would benefit from more elicitation of Jamal’s expertise about his social context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to elicit or amplify Jamal’s change language; clinician mostly offers advice and scripts instead of reflecting or evoking his reasons for change. Few strategic reflections or summaries were used to strengthen desire, ability, or reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about team norms and not wanting to be left out was lightly acknowledged but not strategically softened. The clinician relied on rebuttals and tactical advice rather than double‑sided reflections or summaries to reframe ambivalence.
