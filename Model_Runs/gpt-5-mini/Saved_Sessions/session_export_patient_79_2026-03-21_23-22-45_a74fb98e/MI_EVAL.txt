# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, honored the patient's request to avoid lecturing, and validated autonomy and practical constraints in a sustained nonjudgmental tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that captured frustration and overwhelm, but reflections were sometimes repetitive and could have probed deeper into emotions behind sarcasm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on improving preventer inhaler adherence with each question and reflection tied to the two-week test and measurable outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality collaboration: solicited the patient's expertise, co-designed practical strategies, and negotiated clinician involvement so decisions were jointly constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced the patient's freedom to choose by framing the plan as a temporary, optional experiment and honoring limits on clinician involvement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's own motivations and reasons for change using evocative questions that produced specific, self-directed solutions and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the patient as the primary expert, invited and accepted the patient's solutions, and used language that shared power and fostered co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and concrete commitment language, moving the patient to a measurable two-week behavioral test with clear success criteria.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and reframed ambivalence toward practical testing, but could have used more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain talk while shifting to change.
