# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys consistent respect and validation (supportive opening, affirmations, validating choices). Could add more individualized normalization of struggle and more explicit statements of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses basic reflections and brief affirmations but seldom mirrors emotional tone or elaborates the client's internal experience. Deeper perspective-taking and more complex reflections of worry or pride are needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, specific focus on saying no with interventions consistently tied to that target behavior. Provides structured follow-up and practical steps while keeping the session goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely co-constructed with choices and offers to role‑play, supporting teamwork. At times the clinician leads with detailed plans, so inviting the client to set the agenda more would strengthen equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Routinely respects choice by offering multiple script options and encouraging tailoring (use what feels true). Could more often elicit the client's ownership and ask what would make strategies feel like theirs.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws out motivation by linking tools to the client's goals and eliciting choices and commitments. Would benefit from more questions about internal reasons/values and more amplification of the client's change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options, invites role‑play, and shares planning responsibilities, reflecting a collaborative stance. Some expert-driven scripting slightly reduces the sense that the client's wisdom is primary; elicit more client-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and builds change talk through concrete planning and commitment invitations. Missed opportunities include amplifying positive change statements and asking confidence/commitment scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, reframes awkwardness, and invites rehearsal rather than arguing for change. Could further soften sustain talk by using double-sided reflections that validate both sides of the client's ambivalence.
