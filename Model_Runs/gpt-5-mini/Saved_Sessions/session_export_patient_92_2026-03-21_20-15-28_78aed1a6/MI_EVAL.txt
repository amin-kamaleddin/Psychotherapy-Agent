# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized the patient's perspective, and avoided judgment or labeling while inviting the patient to lead decisions. Multiple affirmations and offers of choice demonstrated strong acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate paraphrases and named barriers, showing solid reflective listening and perspective-taking. Could deepen empathy with more affective reflections exploring the emotional meaning of identity tied to drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on weekend alcohol use and moved systematically from feedback to concrete next steps (doctor tests and portal message). Repeatedly steered toward practical, behavior-linked planning to create momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively solicited the patient's input at each decision point and co-authored the portal message, framing the interaction as joint problem-solving. Decisions were shaped by the patient's preferences, reflecting strong collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly reinforced choice and control by offering options, asking permission before intervening, and accepting the patient's hesitancy. Language and approach consistently supported the patient's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation from the patient with open questions that elicited personal reasons and concrete change-oriented ideas (alternation, telling a friend, getting tests). The patient generated the chosen next step, indicating effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as the expert on his life and co-created plans, repeatedly asking which option felt most workable. Power-sharing and joint decision-making were evident throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited explicit change talk using targeted open questions and offered manageable harm‑reduction options that produced patient statements in favor of change. Could improve by more actively amplifying and reflecting change statements to deepen desire and elicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used neutral or double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence while offering harm‑reduction choices that lessen defensiveness. Could be more strategic with explicit double-sided reflections and selective amplification of fleeting change talk to further reduce sustain language.
