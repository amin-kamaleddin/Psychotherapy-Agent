# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful—validates feelings, praises honesty, normalizes small steps, and avoids shaming throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate summaries that build rapport, though a few reflections stay at a factual level rather than naming deeper emotions like irritation or protectiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking with concrete next steps, timelines, and a small testable experiment that advances toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly joint—clinician solicits preferences, offers options, and co-creates the plan using inclusive language and negotiated, concrete actions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supports choice by asking permission, offering options, normalizing limited commitment, and repeatedly inviting the patient to choose what feels doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivators (daughter, cough) and uses readiness/confidence questions and an experiment, but could use more evocative open questions and amplification of spontaneous change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters genuine power-sharing—offers to follow the patient's lead, asks permission before advice, and negotiates the if‑then plan together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces change talk (asks why a 4, affirms motivators) and invites concrete experiments, but could probe more for desire/importance and past successes to deepen change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and rolls with resistance using validations and a useful double-sided reflection, though more strategic reflections and evocative reframing could further pivot sustain talk toward change.
