# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used respectful, nonjudgmental language and frequent affirmations but at times sounded somewhat procedural; more personalized normalization and naming specific strengths earlier would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and validation captured Lukas’s ambivalence and consequences, yet the clinician could deepen affective listening with more complex reflections of unspoken emotions (fear of exclusion, identity tension).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol-related behaviors tied to Lukas’s goals and moved logically from elicitation to scaling to concrete strategies and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a true partnership: the clinician invited Lukas to choose strategies, practice them, and adapted to his preferences throughout role-plays and planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently honored through asking permission, offering multiple options, and asking which strategies felt most doable, reinforcing Lukas’s ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician reliably elicited Lukas’s own motivations and resources (open questions, rulers, asking what would raise confidence) and used role-play to build from his values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created concrete plans with Lukas, invited his input on wording and rehearsal, and maintained power-sharing through iterative, client-led practice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent elicitation and amplification of change talk via questions about likes/dislikes, importance/confidence rulers, and reinforcing doable strategies and refusal language to boost self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not argued with, and the clinician redirected to options effectively, though more frequent double-sided reflections would better honor the sustain side while pivoting toward change.
