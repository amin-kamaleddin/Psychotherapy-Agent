# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated warm, nonjudgmental regard and repeatedly validated Isolde’s priorities and choices, normalizing small realistic steps. Language avoided blame and honored the client's values throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking captured priorities and specific stresses (rushed evenings, screens). Empathy could be deepened by more feeling-focused reflections naming emotional experience beneath the content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear, consistent focus on small evening-focused behavior change and guided the session toward a concrete, measurable plan. Agenda-setting, option menus, and arranging follow-up maintained strong directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was a clear joint exploration with co-created language, plan details, and follow-up timing, using the client’s input to refine everything. The clinician repeatedly invited the client's choices and incorporated her tweaks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was well supported through offering choices, noncoercive language, and inviting acceptance or decline of suggestions. The use of a confidence ruler and offers to tweak the plan reinforced client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited motivation tied to Isolde’s core value of family happiness and asked for concrete reasons, signs of success, and confidence ratings. Elicitation techniques (menu of options, confidence scaling, planning) strengthened intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician operated as a collaborative partner, asking permission before offering scripts and checking which options felt doable, sharing power in decision-making. Isolde’s choices directly shaped the resulting plan and implementation details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and reinforced change language by offering concrete options, inviting choice, using the confidence ruler, and encouraging specific planning (start date, wording). Affirmations and tracking the shift from 7→9 strengthened commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client sustain talk was minimal and the clinician used normalizing language and contingency planning to avoid reinforcing objections. To improve, more explicit double-sided reflections or explicit acknowledgement of ambivalence could be used when sustain talk appears.
