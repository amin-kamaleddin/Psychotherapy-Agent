# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opened with explicit nonjudgmental warmth, repeatedly validated ambivalence, normalized the patient’s experience, and affirmed tentative efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries accurately mirrored Kenji’s experience and the clinician checked in on feelings, but there were missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional meaning behind ambivalence and 'unwinding.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on reducing drinking, moving logically from exploration to concrete planning and rehearsal while keeping momentum toward doable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative—ideas were co-constructed, the patient chose options to try, and role-play and practice were negotiated and driven by the client’s concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice and control by offering options, checking readiness and confidence, and avoiding coercive language, which reinforced patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer elicited reasons for change (e.g., waking up foggy) and alternatives and used scaling, but could have probed values and amplified change statements more systematically to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session conveyed partnership and respected the patient’s expertise, though the clinician sometimes provided many scripts and strategies that nudged toward an expert role rather than letting the patient’s language fully shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced concrete small-change ideas and used a confidence scale, but could have more consistently reflected and expanded on change statements to strengthen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and acknowledged reasons for drinking while gently redirecting toward change, but offering several scripted solutions quickly reduced space to explore and resolve sustain talk and would benefit from more double-sided reflections.
