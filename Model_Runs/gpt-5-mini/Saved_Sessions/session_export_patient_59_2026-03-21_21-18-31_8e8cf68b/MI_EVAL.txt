# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed nonjudgmental regard and warmth, normalized Jamal’s perspective, and avoided moralizing. They respected requests (e.g., not contacting the PO) and affirmed practical goals without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and summaries that validated Jamal’s priorities and impatience. There were fewer complex affective reflections exploring underlying feelings like shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the presenting problem (avoiding legal trouble) and steered the session toward practical, targeted strategies (police and peer scripts). The clinician avoided drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly collaborative with joint decisions about topics and interventions, and pacing negotiated with Jamal. The clinician invited his preferences and adjusted accordingly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Jamal’s autonomy by offering choices about session content and whether to contact the PO, and by emphasizing that he was in charge. Preferences were honored throughout the visit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation but largely external/avoidance-focused (stay out of trouble); did not systematically draw out internal motives, values, or use scaling and discrepancy techniques. Opportunities to amplify spontaneous change language were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Jamal as the expert and partner, co-creating the agenda and allowing him to shape the session’s focus. Power-sharing was evident in repeated invitations to choose next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical change-oriented language around compliance and avoiding consequences but did not deepen intrinsic change talk or explore desire, reasons, or commitment beyond procedural goals. More questions to elicit internal motivations would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Jamal’s minimizations and used reflections and redirection to problem-solving and safety. Could improve by using double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to gently highlight discrepancy.
