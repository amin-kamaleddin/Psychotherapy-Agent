# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—validating, normalizing relapse, avoiding blame and affirming the patient’s efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections and perspective-taking tracked the patient’s emotions and concerns, including stigma and shame, demonstrating strong empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking cessation and progressed logically to concrete steps (call doctor, April 5 quit date, scripts, coping tools) that linked directly to the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked side‑by‑side with the patient to co-create scripts and a plan, soliciting preferences and positioning self as a supportive resource rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and offered options, framed help rather than directives, and honored the patient’s decisions (e.g., choosing the quit date and which supports to use).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out values, past successes, and used scaling, but missed some opportunities to deepen and amplify client-generated change talk into explicit commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly partnered with the patient by eliciting priorities, offering choices, and co-creating materials only after asking what the patient wanted, demonstrating genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk and helped convert desire into plans, yet could more often reflect and amplify brief change statements into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized fears and sustain talk while redirecting to planning and coping, but would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and immediate juxtaposition of change language.
