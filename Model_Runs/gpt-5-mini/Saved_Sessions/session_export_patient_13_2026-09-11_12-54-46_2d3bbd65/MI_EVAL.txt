# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, acknowledges burden of childcare and fear throughout. Never criticizes or minimizes and normalizes the patient’s reluctance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections capture ambivalence and subtle cues (fear of freezing, logistics) and the clinician checks preferences. Reflective listening conveys strong perspective-taking and emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation focused on the target behavior with concrete, sequential steps (10–15 minute call, script, nap timing). Session progresses goal‑oriented without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative co-creation of a script and rehearsal process with repeated invitations to choose format and pace. Offers supports (reminders, scheduling) framed as partnership rather than takeover.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and defers to the patient’s preferences using option-focused language. Respects expressed reservations and avoids pressure while supporting control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits patient reasons and preferences and draws out specific small-step motives (phone call, script). Could further deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring meaning, importance/confidence, or past successes to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates genuine power sharing, repeatedly asks for the patient’s input and choices, and collaborates on wording and rehearsal. Treats the patient as the primary decision-maker throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and supports change talk through small, concrete options which produced explicit commitments (choosing the call, requesting a script). Could more actively amplify and summarize change talk and elicit ability/confidence language to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk, normalizes hesitancy, and gently redirects toward doable options. Missed opportunities include double-sided reflections and exploring costs of the status quo to further tip the balance toward change.
