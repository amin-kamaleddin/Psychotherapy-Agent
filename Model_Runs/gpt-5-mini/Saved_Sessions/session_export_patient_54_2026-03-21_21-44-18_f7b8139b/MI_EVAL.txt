# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, validating Isabella's values and thanking her for honesty. They avoided labeling or shaming and normalized the difficulty while offering support.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening showed understanding of cultural and autonomy concerns. The clinician could deepen affective reflections to mirror defensiveness or anxiety more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use in pregnancy with clear, actionable goals (removing wine, brochure, scripts, coping strategies). The clinician balanced information provision with planning and avoided topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician acted as an equal partner, co-constructing the one‑page PDF and incorporating Isabella's wording edits. Decisions were negotiated and guided by the patient's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was emphasized throughout via permission-seeking, choice language, and framing suggestions as options. The clinician repeatedly reinforced that Isabella decides what feels right.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited preparatory change talk and practical motivations (plans to remove wine, desire for a healthy baby). They could more systematically deepen intrinsic motivations and draw out commitment language using scaling or discrepancy questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong shared decision-making was evident through offers of choice, co-creation of materials, and invitations for Isabella to decide phrasing. The clinician treated Isabella as the expert on her life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change language and doable steps, reinforcing Isabella's plan to remove wine and prepare scripts. More targeted questions about desire, ability, reasons, and need could convert preparatory talk into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument and the clinician redirected toward planning and permission-based information. Use of double-sided reflections and reflective summaries could more actively diminish sustain talk and highlight ambivalence.
