# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, normalizing Jamal's priorities and offering options rather than imposing decisions. Acceptance was evident throughout the interaction with no blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used timely reflections that captured content and tone, demonstrating practical understanding of Jamal's perspective. Missed deeper emotional reflections (e.g., shame, embarrassment) that could have deepened rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on the target behavior (possession and immediate consequences) and aligned questions/suggestions with the client's stated goal of resolving the issue quickly. Provided concrete, actionable steps tied to that focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem‑solving, solicited Jamal's preferences, offered menus of options, and invited input on scripts and referrals. Collaboration was consistent and client‑centered throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Jamal's choice and control by asking how he wanted to be helped and offering multiple options. The clinician avoided pressuring and reinforced Jamal's agency in decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some pragmatic motives (protecting team, avoiding mom finding out) but mainly provided information and solutions rather than drawing out intrinsic values or confidence. Limited exploration of personal importance or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a partnership by asking permission to share information and inviting Jamal to choose options, scripts, and next steps. Could increase co‑construction (e.g., have Jamal draft wording first) to be more client‑led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited brief, pragmatic change talk and offered concrete steps, but did not amplify or deepen Jamal's own reasons, desires, or confidence for change. Missed opportunities to explore importance, pros/cons, and build toward commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and provided neutral factual consequences, which avoided escalation. However, the clinician rarely used explicit MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or reframing) to strategically soften sustain talk.
