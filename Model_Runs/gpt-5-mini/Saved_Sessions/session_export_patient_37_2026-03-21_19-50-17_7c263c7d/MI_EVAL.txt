# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, validating tone and normalized the parent's feelings, offering help rather than criticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated clear understanding and reflective statements that matched the parent's concerns, but used few complex reflections and often moved quickly to information provision instead of exploring emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a focused, appropriate emphasis on the vaccination decision (timing, safety, contagion) and structured information to address the parent's repeated concerns, moving toward actionable items.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem‑solving, asked about priorities, and tailored resources and wording to the parent's needs rather than imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the parent's sense of choice by offering options, asking preferences, and encouraging consultation with the pediatrician rather than pressuring or directing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some information about concerns and preferences, but relied more on supplying facts than drawing out the parent's own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by inviting input and tailoring responses, though much of the exchange shifted into expert information delivery rather than co‑creating a concrete plan with the parent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created conditions for change talk to appear (patient expressed new concern), but did not consistently elicit, reflect, or amplify change language with evocative questions to move toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward balanced information and harm‑reduction, but could have used more double‑sided reflections to explicitly resolve ambivalence.
