# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and avoided judgment, validating Malik’s perspective and normalizing ambivalence. Multiple affirmations and non‑punitive tone supported a safe, accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective‑taking, accurately summarizing Malik’s position and concerns. Reflections stayed at content/practical level and could be deeper by naming underlying emotions and tensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, sustained focus on cannabis use and its link to valued outcomes like college and family, moving to a concrete, realistic action plan. The session remained goal‑oriented and translated discussion into an agreed exit plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as joint problem‑solving: invited choices, role‑played, and tailored plans to Malik’s preferences (code, wording, exit script). Decisions and next steps were co‑constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Malik’s sense of choice by asking permission and offering options, and avoided coercive language despite the parental referral. Reinforced that Malik controlled which steps to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used open questions to elicit concerns and modest internal motivation, resulting in some change‑oriented statements and agreement to try strategies. Could further probe personal meaning and intrinsic motives to deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance with permission seeking and asking Malik to choose strategies, conveying that his input mattered. At times the clinician led with specific recommendations rather than eliciting Malik’s own ideas earlier.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked useful open questions that produced mild change talk but did not consistently amplify or deepen those statements. The clinician often moved to offering solutions before reflecting and strengthening emerging change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided argument and rolled with minimization, using neutral reflections and reframing toward risk reduction. Could enhance impact with double‑sided reflections or summaries to more actively highlight discrepancy.
