# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard with strengths-based affirmations and nonjudgmental normalization of limits, avoiding shaming or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used timely, accurate reflections and summaries that matched the patient's perspective and affect, demonstrating strong perspective-taking and reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the behavioral target throughout, moving logically from aspiration to a concrete, narrow plan without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in mutual problem-solving by offering options, soliciting the patient's preferences, and co-constructing the plan so the patient's choices directly shaped next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and control by presenting options, validating limited commitment, and deferring to the patient's knowledge of her schedule and limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew out the patient's own motivations and solutions with open questions and iterative prompts, amplifying self-generated reasons for change and implementation ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine power-sharing—offering choices, asking which feels easiest, and integrating the patient's ideas into a shared plan rather than prescribing actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions and reflections, then shaped and strengthened it into a specific, doable plan (alarm, shoes, one paragraph, walk).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled minimal sustain talk appropriately by normalizing limits and not reinforcing hesitation, but missed a few opportunities for brief double-sided reflections or confidence elicitation to further reduce ambivalence.
