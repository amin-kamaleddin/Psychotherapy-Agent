# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing Farid’s struggle with supportive validations. Repeated reassuring language and avoidance of blame modeled strong acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were several accurate, attuned reflections acknowledging fatigue, schedule barriers, and hesitation, which conveyed empathy. Empathy could be stronger with more complex reflections of underlying feelings (e.g., self-judgment, frustration) behind sighs and doubts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on increasing physical activity and moved to a concrete, specific action plan (days, alarms, fallback options, accountability). The clinician consistently steered toward implementation intentions and troubleshooting barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative—clinician invited choices, co-created alarm labels and texts, and checked feasibility. However, frequent provision of ready-made solutions slightly shifted the agenda toward the clinician rather than privileging the patient’s spontaneous ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered options, asked permission before drafting reminders/texts, and emphasized Farid’s control over decisions. Language consistently affirmed choice and respected the patient’s agency in shaping the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician effectively evoked behavioral intentions and concrete steps but did limited elicitation of Farid’s deeper motivations, values, or reasons for change. Missed opportunities to probe importance, confidence, and internal meaning weakened intrinsic motivation work.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician largely worked in partnership by offering options and soliciting input, co-creating reminders and texts. Partnership was somewhat reduced by leading with expert suggestions rather than eliciting the patient’s own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited and reinforced change talk through concrete options, commitments, and affirmations, strengthening intent and planning. Opportunities remained to amplify change language with reflective highlights of reasons, values, and confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and validated but the clinician often pivoted quickly to problem-solving and directives. Using double-sided reflections, exploring ambivalence, or reframing sustain talk would better soften resistance and elicit resolution.
