# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Oliver's feelings and avoiding blame; normalized ambivalence and offered practical support without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflections and empathic coaching but moved fairly quickly into problem-solving; could deepen listening to explore guilt and fear more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, focused direction on the move-out goal with concrete steps, rehearsals, and contingency planning; the conversation stayed on task.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co-constructed messages, role-played the flatmate, and solicited Oliver’s wording, making the interaction joint problem-solving rather than a lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, checking what Oliver wanted, and framing recommendations as suggestions while respecting legal boundaries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited a goal and some change language but mainly supplied scripts and solutions instead of drawing out Oliver’s own motivations, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered partnership through permission and options and joint scripting, though an expert/problem-solver stance at times diluted fully client-driven exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced and strengthened change talk via goal confirmation and rehearsal, but could have asked more open questions to elicit deeper reasons and values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Took steps to limit sustain talk but emphasized logistics and consequences more than double-sided reflections or explicit normalization of ambivalence, which risked increasing resistance.
