# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and normalized setbacks, validating Amira's choices without criticism. No instances of blaming, labeling, or minimizing were evident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries showed good understanding of Amira's motivations and practical barriers. Deeper affect-focused or complex reflections could further capture emotional nuance like discouragement or meaning around body confidence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on increasing physical activity with concrete, actionable planning (specific days, times, reminders, fallback). The clinician consistently steered conversation back to behavior change and implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The routine and materials were co‑created with iterative input from Amira, and the clinician invited edits and preferences throughout. Interactions read as a genuine two‑way partnership rather than top‑down instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician offered multiple options, sought permission before suggestions, and explicitly affirmed that the 5‑minute fallback was acceptable, supporting Amira's choice and control. Language avoided pressure and preserved ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited Amira's reasons, benefits, and readiness (importance/confidence 8/10) and linked actions to goals. More selective amplification of her strongest change statements and an explicit brief commitment would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interactions demonstrated clear power‑sharing: the clinician asked preferences, deferred to Amira's expertise, and co‑crafted the plan and spoken track together. This consistent inclusion evidenced a strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced numerous change statements through evocative questions, scaling, and planning, and invited celebration of success. The clinician could more often amplify and summarize the client's strongest change talk to build momentum toward a commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician strategically avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing concerns and offering practical if‑then supports and fallbacks. Using more double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence would further soften sustain talk.
