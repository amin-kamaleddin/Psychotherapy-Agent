# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with normalization, repeated affirmations of strengths, and nonjudgmental language that acknowledges shame without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that validates feelings, though many reflections were repetitive and missed deeper exploration of shame and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on reducing drinking and preventing outbursts, consistently steering toward concrete, actionable plans like the two-drink limit and check-in procedures.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models an equal partnership by soliciting the patient's ideas at every step and letting the patient's proposals drive the plan, using inclusive language throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly emphasizes choice and ownership, asking what feels doable for the patient and inviting him to design the agreement rather than prescribing solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits internal motivation and values through evocative questions and imagery of success, drawing out the patient's own reasons and specific strategies for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames the work as collaborative and invites co-construction, regularly asking the patient to choose details so expertise remains with him and his partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and strengthens change talk with open, evocative questions and affirmations, producing specific pro-change language and concrete steps (e.g., two-drink rule, handing over keys).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward strategies for change, but seldom uses double-sided reflections or explicit contrasts to actively resolve ambivalence.
