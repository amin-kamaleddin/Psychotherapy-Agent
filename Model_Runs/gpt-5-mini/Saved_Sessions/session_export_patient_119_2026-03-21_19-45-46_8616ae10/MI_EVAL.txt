# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, normalized lapses, and thanked the patient for honesty, creating a strongly nonjudgmental tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening with pragmatic summaries, but missed opportunities to explore emotional nuance (e.g., mild anxiety or defensiveness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence, side effects, and a concrete follow-up with no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as shared with joint planning and a check-in, though the clinician often offered multiple solutions before fully eliciting the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by asking permission to offer suggestions, inviting selection of strategies, and emphasizing that imperfection is acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out the patient's reasons for change and set up accountability, but did not probe deeply into values, importance/confidence, or use scaling questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and choices but interaction leaned toward clinician-driven solutions; greater power-sharing and asking the patient to generate ideas would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with doable steps and a follow-up, yet could have amplified and deepened change language by exploring meaning, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, reframed lapses nonjudgmentally, and offered low-effort solutions, though more double-sided reflections could further address ambivalence.
