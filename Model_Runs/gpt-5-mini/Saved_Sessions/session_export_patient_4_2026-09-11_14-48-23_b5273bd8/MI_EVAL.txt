# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental respect, normalizing the patient's conflicting concerns and validating practical worries without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening with summaries and reflections attuned to both content and affect throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, practical focus on an achievable symptom-tracking behavior and steps to obtain tests, steering each exchange toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited patient input at every decision point and adapted the plan to her preferences, offering drafts and scripts only after eliciting her choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Routinely affirmed the patient's choice and capacity, provided options and varying levels of help, and emphasized the patient's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical solutions (card, cover note, script, timeline) but probed less for deeper internal motivations, values, or confidence to strengthen intrinsic commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine shared decision-making with frequent choice questions and co-construction of materials, reflecting an equal‑partner approach rather than an expert prescription.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change-oriented statements and options leading to a plan, but missed opportunities to amplify and summarize change talk or elicit explicit commitment/implementation intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Responded nonconfrontationally and accommodated strong biomedical concerns which avoided conflict but did not actively reframe or use double-sided reflections to reduce sustain-talk focus.
