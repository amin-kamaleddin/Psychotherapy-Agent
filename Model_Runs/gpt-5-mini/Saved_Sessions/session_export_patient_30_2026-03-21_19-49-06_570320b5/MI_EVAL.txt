# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—validated concerns, avoided judgment, and offered non‑drug options. Language was respectful and supportive throughout with no blaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective‑taking and reflective listening with relevant clarifying questions. Reflections were mostly pragmatic and could probe deeper emotional ambivalence and underlying values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on cholesterol management with actionable steps (lifestyle trial, monitoring, scripts). At times information was delivered didactically rather than negotiated in smaller chunks and could have elicited a specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative and egalitarian—co‑produced shopping lists, monitoring plans, and scripts while inviting patient choices and feedback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, affirming the patient's right to decline meds, and helping craft language for asserting preferences. Encouraged ownership of decisions and offered rehearsal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurred (asking what to do first) but the clinician did little to elicit internal motivations, values, or confidence. More open questions about why change matters and confidence would strengthen self‑motivated statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent partnership—repeatedly invited input, asked permission, and deferred to the patient's preferences in co‑creating the plan. The clinician positioned themselves as a collaborator throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional prompts produced brief change talk, but the clinician rarely followed up to deepen Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, or Commitment language. More targeted elicitation and follow‑up would amplify and sustain change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively softened sustain talk with validation, reframing, and by offering alternatives rather than arguing. Could increase use of double‑sided reflections to explicitly link sustain and change talk and help resolve ambivalence.
