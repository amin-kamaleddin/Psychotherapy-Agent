# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing the patient's worries and offering help without blame. The clinician repeatedly invited patient preferences and avoided directives, supporting a warm accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear validation and reflective listening that helped the patient soften, with useful paraphrases and clarifying questions. Reflections tended to stay at the factual/content level rather than deeper affective or complex reflections of underlying fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on vaccine concerns throughout and provided concrete next steps (scripts, a single trusted resource). There was no drifting to unrelated topics and the clinician maintained goal-directedness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked cooperatively by offering to co-create question lists, rehearsing language, and asking the patient's preference. The clinician nonetheless often assumed an expert informational role rather than fully distributing expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's choice and control, asked permission before offering help, and provided options for next steps. The clinician avoided pressuring language and respected the patient's stated skepticism.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made minimal effort to draw out the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence relevant to vaccinating, relying mainly on information and tools. Missed opportunities for questions that would elicit internal reasons for change or scaling confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and supportive of the patient's input, offering choices and rehearsals that shaped the plan. Partnership was strong but tempered by frequent expert-driven information delivery rather than full co‑construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created some opportunities for change talk via practical supports and phrasing that the patient accepted and planned to use. However, the clinician did not consistently elicit the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence to vaccinate.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and provided clear factual corrections that helped the patient soften. The approach relied mainly on information rather than MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to gently redirect ambivalence.
