# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, with clear validation of shame and praise for progress throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that normalizes ambivalence, though deeper, more complex reflections of Elias's internal conflict were sometimes missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session clearly focused on alcohol/pill use and relapse prevention, moving logically from reflection to specific, concrete action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—co‑created scripts and checked comfort—but at times the clinician framed solutions rather than eliciting Elias's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirmed choice and offered multiple options while avoiding coercion, though occasionally shifted toward more directive, specific recommendations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Elias's motivations and drew on family values, but relied somewhat on clinician-generated solutions instead of continuously amplifying Elias's own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance with permission‑seeking and joint planning, though some practical, directive advising reduced the sense of Elias as primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change language and successes, but could have amplified and summarized Elias's change statements to build greater commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized ambivalence, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and deeper exploration of reasons for staying the same.
