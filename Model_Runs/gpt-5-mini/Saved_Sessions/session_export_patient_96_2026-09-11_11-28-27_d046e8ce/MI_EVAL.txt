# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient's choice, validating aesthetic reasons while exploring harm-reduction options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately paraphrasing motivations and concerns and inviting the patient's viewpoint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on reducing oral-health risk and consistently guides the conversation toward a specific, measurable plan (e.g., 11:00 PM alarm, travel mouthwash).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as joint problem-solving by inviting and building on the patient's ideas to co-create a practical plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by asking which options the patient prefers, avoiding coercive language, and offering to set plans only with permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's internal motivations and commitment language, prompting her to generate concrete steps and contingency plans from her own values and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the patient as the expert on what will work, repeatedly soliciting her preferences and co-constructing the plan from her suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk through targeted questions that prompt specific, actionable commitments (daily rinse, alarm, travel bottle).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk by using double-sided reflections and harm-reduction framing to redirect toward practical risk-reduction strategies without confrontation.
