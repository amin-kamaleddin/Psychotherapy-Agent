# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, using affirmations and normalizing language (including a nonpunitive slip text). This acceptance was evident throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and accurate summaries that captured both sides of ambivalence and acknowledged emotions. Could probe or reflect more deeply on underlying feelings and the emotional meaning of running and relaxation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session clearly focused on reducing alcohol to support marathon training and moved toward a specific, testable behavior (alcohol‑free weeknights). Operationalized the plan with concrete metrics, start date, and templates without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked in genuine partnership, inviting the patient to shape the plan, responding to requests, and co‑constructing practical tools like logs and texts. The interaction felt team‑based rather than expert‑driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice and control by offering options, asking preferences, and accepting the patient's decisions (e.g., start date, check‑ins). Avoided pressure and reinforced the patient's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited the patient's motivations and resources, linking marathon goals to potential benefits and asking what would raise confidence. Could further deepen intrinsic values and more consistently amplify patient‑generated reasons for change to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the patient as the expert and co‑created the plan, offering options and adapting materials to his voice. Power was clearly shared throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and scaffolded change talk using pros/cons, importance/confidence scales, and concrete planning, producing actionable change statements. Could more consistently reflect, amplify, and summarize those statements to build momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used normalization and double‑sided reflections to soften sustain talk, and offered practical reframes. Would benefit from more strategic pairings of sustain and change language to guide resolution of ambivalence.
