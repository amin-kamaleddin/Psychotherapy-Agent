# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent validating reflections and statements that reduce pressure; could occasionally name the difficulty more explicitly to deepen individualization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, sustained reflective listening and perspective-taking with reflections of affect and concrete bodily cues that supported engagement and elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on self-harm coping strategies and moved progressively to a concrete, measurable plan without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative stance: built on the patient's ideas, offered choices, and co-constructed the plan so the patient retained authorship of decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported client choice and framed the intervention as voluntary and experimental, avoiding coercion or prescriptive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical coping ideas and past successes but missed deeper evocation of values, longer-term reasons for change, and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as a partnership, inviting the patient's input on options and timing and sharing power in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified concrete change talk (ice, text, signs of success) but could more systematically strengthen desire, confidence, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain-talk and used double-sided reflections to nudge toward change, though more strategic weakening of sustain talk and exploration of costs of staying the same would help.
