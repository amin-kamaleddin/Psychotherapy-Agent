# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offered positive reinforcement, acknowledging Margaret's choices. Some affirmations were formulaic and there was limited deeper normalization or explicit unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and concise, showing understanding of retirement, the fall, and family concern. Opportunities to deepen empathy with more complex reflections about feelings, ambivalence, and meaning were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and fall risk, moving logically from assessment to specific, measurable goals. Planning and follow‑up were well integrated throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co‑constructed a plan, invited role‑play, and asked the patient to choose options, supporting a shared process. However, frequent clinician-led suggestions and specific strategies shifted some control away from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered and suggestions framed permissively, legitimizing the patient's preference to cut back rather than stop. The clinician avoided coercive language and reinforced that slips are information, not failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were attempts to draw out motivation using rulers and choices, producing some change talk. The clinician relied more on giving options and strategies than on eliciting Margaret's own reasons, values, and evocative language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Many collaborative elements were present—inviting choices, planning together, and offering check‑ins and supporters. The clinician often offered specific strategies before eliciting the patient's ideas, which slightly reduced parity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited via importance/confidence rulers and goal selection, yielding concrete statements and plans. The clinician did not consistently amplify, reflect, or follow up on change language to strengthen and develop commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation and acknowledged sustain talk, but early framing of the drinking as 'social and manageable' risked reinforcing it. More reflective techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) were not used consistently to redirect ambivalence.
