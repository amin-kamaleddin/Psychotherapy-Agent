# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and normalized Milan's responses, avoiding moralizing language. Autonomy was affirmed when offering suggestions, reinforcing acceptance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, validating Milan's feelings about fitting in and embarrassment. The clinician asked about experiences and consequences, showing consistent effort to understand his viewpoint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on adolescent alcohol use, safety, and harm-reduction throughout the session. The clinician guided the conversation toward concrete, behavior-linked next steps without losing engagement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the clinician invited Milan to choose strategies, co-created texts, and followed his pacing and limits. Decisions were framed as joint problem-solving rather than directive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice and control by offering options and framing advice as optional. The clinician respected Milan's ownership of next steps and acknowledged that he was not committing to quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation (safety and avoiding embarrassment) and prompted selection of a realistic step, but did not fully draw out deeper intrinsic reasons or confidence. Missed opportunities included scaling questions and more reflective amplification of self-motivating statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the session as a joint enterprise, inviting input on focus and co-creating concrete plans like texts and refusal lines. Power-sharing was evident in offers to role-play and in adjusting language to Milan's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change talk (e.g., agreement to a sober-ride plan and refusal lines) but did not systematically deepen it. The clinician missed chances to use scaling or to ask for elaboration to amplify desire, ability, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce sustain talk, using harm-reduction rather than confrontation. However, there was limited use of double-sided or strategic reflections to explicitly reframe sustain talk alongside change reasons to resolve ambivalence.
