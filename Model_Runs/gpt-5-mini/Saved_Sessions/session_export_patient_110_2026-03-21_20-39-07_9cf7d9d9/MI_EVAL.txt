# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, validating concerns and welcoming preferences. Avoided blame and minimized language, creating a warm accepting stance throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking and advanced reflective listening with accurate paraphrases and complex reflections. Normalized feelings and showed clear understanding of both pragmatic and emotional concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on medication adherence and repeatedly guided the conversation toward concrete next steps. Kept the session purposeful while balancing empathy and problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively invited the patient's input, co-created scripts and strategies, and adjusted materials to his preferences. Interaction functioned as a genuine two-way partnership rather than an expert-driven encounter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking which felt doable, and framing suggestions as offers. Respected the patient's pace and reinforced ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out internal motivations and linked change to personally meaningful values (retirement, staying active with his wife). Encouraged the patient to verbalize intentions and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the patient as an equal partner, co-creating the plan and inviting role-play when requested. Demonstrated strong power-sharing and incorporation of the patient's expertise and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and amplified several strands of change talk and linked change to values, producing explicit statements of willingness. Missed opportunities to consolidate commitment and confidence with scaling questions or a time-bound commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and gently redirected it with neutral reflections and practical problem-solving. Could more actively soften ambivalence by using double-sided reflections that name both concerns and values.
