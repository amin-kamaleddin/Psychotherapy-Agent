# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing the patient's experience and avoiding judgment. Affirmations reinforce responsibility and safety, supporting a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, repeated empathic reflections using the patient's language and links behavior to feelings and context. Reflections deepen rapport and move the conversation forward.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on immediate harm‑reduction (Narcan, safer injecting, concrete plans) and repeatedly returns to actionable steps. Questions translate motivation into specific, achievable actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner, offering options and inviting the patient's choices in wording, timing, and backup plans. The clinician defers to the patient's expertise and co‑creates the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports patient choice by offering options, asking preferences, and emphasizing pace. These moves preserve agency and avoid pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits short‑term motivational language and reasons for harm reduction (relief, reduced panic) and asks about anticipated feelings after action. Could be strengthened by expanding evocation to underlying values and longer‑term goals, and amplifying statements about learning safer injecting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames the patient as the expert and invites collaboration on wording and plans, sharing power throughout. The clinician follows the patient's lead and integrates their preferences into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk around immediate protective steps and commitment, prompting concrete planning. Would benefit from deeper elicitation and amplification of the patient's language about learning safer injecting and potential barriers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges the sustaining functions of use and avoids confrontation, then pivots effectively to harm‑reduction steps. Could use more strategic softening (double‑sided reflections, explicit discrepancy) to explore ambivalence further.
