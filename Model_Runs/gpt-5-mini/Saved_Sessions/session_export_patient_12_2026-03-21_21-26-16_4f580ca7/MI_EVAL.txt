# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating language throughout the session normalizes hardship and praises disclosure, conveying strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking capture Amira’s loneliness, shame, and ambivalence, fostering safety and rapport through strong reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, practical focus on immediate support-seeking (drafting a text and role‑playing), but could more explicitly link these micro-steps to longer‑term goals like reducing drug use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co-constructed—clinician offers options, asks preferences, and builds messages and role-plays with Amira, positioning her as an active partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly reinforces choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and affirming that Amira can decide whether or when to send or save messages.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes several attempts to elicit motivation (readiness scale, small step) but spends more time offering solutions than drawing out Amira’s own values, reasons, and deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance with shared decision-making, asking permission before intervening and letting Amira’s preferences shape the content and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language (readiness rating, small-step plan) but misses opportunities to deepen and amplify change talk by exploring importance, past successes, or what would move the rating up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to low-risk actions (drafting, role-play), though could use more strategic moves like double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
