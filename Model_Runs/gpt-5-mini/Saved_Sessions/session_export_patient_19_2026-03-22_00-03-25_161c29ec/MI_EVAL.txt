# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, validating the client's struggle without blame. Acceptance is explicit and steady throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrasing validated both emotional burden and practical constraints. Could deepen with more emotion-focused and complex reflections to capture ambivalence's nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on gambling/financial risk and immediate, measurable actions (leave cards, $40 cash, Masha's text). Questions and reflections consistently reorient toward protecting job and finances.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician invites brainstorming and negotiates the plan with the patient, offering suggestions only after eliciting preferences. The plan emerges from mutual problem-solving rather than clinician directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasizes choice and respects the client's expertise, normalizing imperfection and avoiding pressure. The interaction supports self-determination throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client's own reasons for change and specific behavioral plans, drawing out motives like protecting the paycheck. Could further deepen evocation by exploring values, discrepancies, and confidence/commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the work as collaborative and explicitly credits the client’s expertise, co-constructing supports and wording with her. Power-sharing is evident in decision-making and plan development.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Targeted evoking questions and reflections successfully draw out desire and reasons for change and reinforce specific commitments (cash-only, Masha's text). There is room to amplify and consolidate change talk into a clearer commitment and assess confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward consequences and concrete risk-reduction strategies. Missed opportunities include using more double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to contrast sustain and change talk.
