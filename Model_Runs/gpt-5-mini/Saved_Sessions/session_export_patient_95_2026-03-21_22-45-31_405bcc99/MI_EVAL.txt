# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validation, honoring the client's request to avoid fixes and affirming strengths. Nonjudgmental warmth and explicit worthiness statements supported a strong therapeutic stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, consistent reflective listening and emotional attunement, mirroring the client's language (e.g., 'drowning', 'relentless'). The clinician held the client's need to be heard and used gentle clarifying questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on suicidal ideation, safety, and immediate coping by asking about plans/means and offering narrowly-scoped options. Balanced validation with necessary safety questions and concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as joint problem-solving with inclusive language and negotiated decisions (e.g., staying on chat while calling Ana). The clinician co-created a concrete, immediate plan based on the client's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and respected the client's preferences, honoring 'don't give me a bunch of fixes' and offering several optional actions. Moves preserved agency and avoided coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew out key motivations (importance of mother, a 'spark' wanting relief) and coping behaviors, but did not systematically deepen importance, confidence, or commitment language. More targeted elicitation of personal reasons and future-focused prompts would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and deferred to the client's expertise, offering choices and asking preferences about support. Power-sharing language and joint planning demonstrated strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced existing change talk and adaptive behaviors, highlighting a 'real spark' of hope. Missed opportunities to deepen and amplify commitment (e.g., scaling questions or inviting the client to elaborate on what staying would mean).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while pivoting to reasons for living, but sustain talk remained present. Could have more proactively used contrastive double-sided reflections to explicitly highlight costs of the status quo versus the client's reasons for staying.
