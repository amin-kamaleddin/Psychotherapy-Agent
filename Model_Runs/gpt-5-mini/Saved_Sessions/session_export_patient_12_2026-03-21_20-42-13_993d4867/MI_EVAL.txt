# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout, using affirmations and normalizing language. Warmth and unconditional positive regard were evident across turns, supporting a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and validations accurately captured the patient's experience (shame, loneliness, helplessness) and invited elaboration, including a sensitive safety check. Empathic listening was sustained and skillful throughout the encounter.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept clear focus on heroin use, immediate safety, and concrete harm‑reduction steps (naloxone, referrals, check‑ins). The session consistently translated ambivalence into specific, behaviorally focused next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The encounter was co‑created with the patient, offering choices, asking preferences, and providing options rather than prescriptions. The clinician repeatedly sought permission and tailored supports (scripts, lookups, role‑play) to the patient's wishes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasized the patient's control over decisions, offered multiple alternatives, and avoided pressure while validating ambivalence. Options were framed as choices and the patient was invited to pick what felt doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited change by asking what felt doable and prompting selection of small steps, producing concrete commitments (naloxone, call, alarm). However, evocation could be deeper by exploring personal values, confidence, and past successes to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as a collaborative effort, inviting the patient's input and jointly building the plan (alarm, script, check‑in). Power was shared throughout, with offers to assist only as the patient wanted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was regularly elicited and reinforced through praise and practical steps, leading to specific commitments like obtaining naloxone. Missed opportunities included more active amplification and consolidation (scaling confidence, summarizing change talk) to build stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validated sustain talk and redirected to harm‑reduction options without arguing, which reduced reinforcement of resistance. Additional strategies like double‑sided reflections or exploring beliefs (e.g., 'no one wants to help me') could further soften sustain talk.
