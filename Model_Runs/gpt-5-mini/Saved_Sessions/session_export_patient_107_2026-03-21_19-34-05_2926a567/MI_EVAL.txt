# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client’s ritual while raising safety concerns compassionately. Warm, nonjudgmental language preserves the client’s values and choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and some reflective listening, tailoring recommendations to the client’s preferences. Could deepen empathy with more complex and double-sided reflections of ambivalence and emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on caffeine use and symptom management with a logical session flow from assessment to trial design. Every suggestion is tied to the target behavior and moves the plan forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session reads as collaborative problem-solving with co-created tracking and trial plans that incorporate the client’s choices. Occasional long lists of unsolicited tips and detailed instructions shifted some control back to the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice with options, explicit permission, and flexibility (e.g., start day, defaulting on hectic days). Avoids pressuring language and honors deviations while preserving learning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment through planning and trials but underuses strategies to draw out intrinsic motivation or values. Few questions probe why change matters, importance/confidence, or elicit anticipatory change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally treats the client as a collaborator, asking which option to try and co-designing the protocol. Partnership weakens when the clinician delivers multi-item recommendations without always prefacing with permission or soliciting input first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sparks some change talk via concrete options and a 7-day check-back, leading to specific commitments. Tends to offer solutions rather than strategically elicit or amplify the client’s own reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and preserves the client’s sustain-talk about keeping the ritual, which prevents reinforcement of resistance. Misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or summaries that would more actively soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
