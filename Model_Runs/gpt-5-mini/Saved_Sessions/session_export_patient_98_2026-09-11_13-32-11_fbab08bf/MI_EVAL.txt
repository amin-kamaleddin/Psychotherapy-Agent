### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Mateo’s caution, validating his limits and choices without blame or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used frequent, accurate reflections that mirrored Mateo’s language and affect, showing strong perspective-taking and emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on safe, manageable physical activity and pacing, keeping questions and reflections action-oriented and goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt largely collaborative with inclusive language and follow-up, but the clinician often supplied options rather than inviting Mateo to generate solutions, limiting full mutuality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Mateo’s choice and control by asking preferences and offering experiments he could accept or decline, with no coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intrinsic reasons and concrete indicators of success and secured a first test, but missed deeper probing of values, confidence, and stronger amplification of change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaved collaboratively and invited preferences, yet the rapid presentation of clinician-generated options subtly steered the agenda instead of co-creating alternatives with Mateo.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out specific, doable steps and affirmed intentions, but did not consistently amplify or deepen change talk into stronger commitment or probe confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reframed sustain talk and avoided arguing, but relied mainly on neutral reflections rather than strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to tip toward change.
