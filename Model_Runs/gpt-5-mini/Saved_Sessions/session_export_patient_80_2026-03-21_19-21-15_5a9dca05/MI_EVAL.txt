# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental, strengths-focused language and normalizing statements, creating warmth and respect throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening and acknowledged both content and emotion (e.g., chest tightness, anxiety), but could use deeper, more complex reflections to explore the emotional meaning of Luka's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained clearly focused on reducing drinking and preventing blow-ups, with interventions and safety guidance directly linked to those target behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as a team effort, inviting Luka's input and co-constructing plans so decisions were negotiated rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Luka's choice and control by honoring harm-reduction goals, offering options, and affirming that he chooses what to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation by highlighting progress and prompting action planning, but relied more on offering strategies than drawing out Luka's values, reasons, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance throughout—asking permission, offering options, and allowing Luka's preferences to shape the plan, demonstrating authentic power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (acknowledging progress, co-creating steps), yet did not consistently deepen or amplify importance, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and supported ambivalence with harm-reduction options, but could more actively soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections and selective emphasis on change momentum.
