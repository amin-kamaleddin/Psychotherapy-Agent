# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opens with explicit unconditional positive regard and sustains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, offering choices and validating the patient's preparation. No labeling or confrontation occurred, supporting a safe atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing values and worries about side effects and energy. Could deepen emotional reflection with more complex reflections to fully explore anxiety or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on starting and adhering to cholesterol medication, tying each intervention back to that goal. The session is efficient and goal-directed without being pressuring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as true partnership: the clinician solicits input, co-creates the checklist, and tailors format and content to the patient’s preferences. The clinician invites the patient to shape the plan rather than dominating the interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's choice by offering alternatives, asking permission, and framing decisions as the patient's to make. Avoids directives and reinforces ownership of the decision to start medication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits intrinsic motivation linked to valued goals (retirement, hiking) and asks a confidence rating to anchor readiness. Could strengthen by prompting the patient to articulate his own arguments for change and exploring how to raise confidence further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invites patient preferences and co-constructs the plan (one-page list, script), sharing power and decision-making. This fosters genuine shared decision-making and recognizes the patient's expertise about his priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk well, reflecting commitment language and translating it into concrete steps (checklist, script). Could more systematically amplify spontaneous change talk and elicit explicit commitment and implementation intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and does not reinforce it, but misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or reframes to strategically soften minimizations. The clinician tends to move past sustain talk into planning rather than using it to explore ambivalence and tip the balance toward change.
