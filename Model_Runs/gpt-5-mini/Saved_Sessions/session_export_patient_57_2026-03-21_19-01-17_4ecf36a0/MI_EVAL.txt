# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing Jamal's experience and thanking him for trust. Blame and moralizing language are avoided, creating steady unconditional support.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows clear perspective-taking and reflective listening with concrete follow-ups, demonstrating understanding of Jamal's situation. The clinician moves fairly quickly into solution-focused work and could deepen affective exploration and more complex reflections on belonging and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and team-related risks while developing a concrete harm-reduction plan. Conversation stays action-oriented with specific next steps and no drifting from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the session as a joint project through role-plays, asking Jamal to choose practice targets, and checking his comfort. Clinician and client input are balanced and respectful, supporting genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces Jamal's sense of choice by offering a menu of options and asking which feels easiest to try. Language and practice invitations support ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from Jamal by asking him to specify actions and reasons, leading to his own change language and commitments. Prompts elicit concrete choices and rehearsal of preferred strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative with invitations to roleplay and shared planning, but the clinician occasionally supplies specific scripts and corrective tweaks that shift expertise toward the clinician. More consistent privileging of Jamal's formulations would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk by offering options, asking which feels easiest, and having Jamal name exact steps and rehearse them. The clinician strengthens commitment with concrete, client-selected actions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk and redirects toward safer alternatives and harm-reduction strategies. However, responses are mostly neutral redirections without systematic double-sided reflections or explicit elicitation of discrepancy.
