# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating language that normalizes difficulty and thanks the client, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate, and tailored reflections and summaries that capture the client’s experience and keep the conversation moving, helping the client feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on starting homework/managing deadlines throughout, moving stepwise to a measurable action plan and concrete follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently invites client input, offers options rather than mandates, and integrates the client’s ideas into the plan, reflecting a genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, asks permission before suggesting, and respects the client’s preferred ways of marking success, fostering a strong sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out ideas and motivation using confidence rulers and past-success questions, but could probe deeper into values and elicit stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power-sharing is clear: the clinician solicits preferences, co-creates the plan, and lets the client shape decisions at each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical change talk and concrete experiments with confidence ratings, though would benefit from more amplification, linking to values, and explicit commitment prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing reasons to stay the same and offers neutral problem-solving responses, but could more strategically use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk further.
