# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with affirmations and normalizing statements; explicitly encouraged self-kindness and avoided shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and pragmatic summaries that showed understanding, but missed deeper, affect-focused probes and more complex reflections to explore ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence with practical strategies, cues, backups, and a planned follow-up; no drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a genuine collaboration—clinician solicited preferences, incorporated patient ideas, and co-constructed the implementation plan and check-in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by asking permission, offering a menu of options, and reinforcing that the plan and timing were the patient's decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's ideas and used a confidence ruler to elicit motivation, but could have probed personal values and longer-term reasons to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated invitations to choose and joint problem-solving demonstrated strong power-sharing and mutual planning, including a mutually agreed check-in.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk via options, specific planning, and the confidence ruler, but could further amplify by asking for examples of past successes or deeper personal motives.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to problem-solving and planning, though used few double-sided reflections that could explicitly acknowledge ambivalence while guiding toward change.
