# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently expressed unconditional positive regard, normalized Marco’s experience, and affirmed both pride and frustration without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and timely summaries accurately captured Marco’s feelings and perspective, helping him feel understood and encouraging open disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on reducing orange juice intake, with each question and suggestion advancing toward concrete, time‑bound actions like a two‑week trial and a planned text.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the work as joint problem‑solving, solicited Marco’s preferences, and integrated them into tangible supports (texts, fridge note, seltzer/fruit choices).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were offered rather than directives, the clinician checked confidence and preferences repeatedly, and decision‑making remained with Marco to preserve his ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited from Marco through open questions, scaling, and reflections, which drew out his reasons, confidence, and concrete commitments rather than supplying impetus.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interactions were co‑constructed with frequent invitations for Marco’s input and offers of help only as requested, demonstrating shared power in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly elicited and amplified change talk, translated it into specific steps, and reinforced Marco’s commitment with targeted planning and confidence checks.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged nonjudgmentally and not amplified, with the clinician shifting attention to workable substitutions and coping strategies to reduce its pull.
