# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used consistently nonjudgmental, respectful language and affirmed Jamal's priorities (e.g., reflections and offers to help). No shaming or lecturing; unconditional positive regard was sustained throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated good perspective-taking with accurate reflections of content and priorities, reducing defensiveness. There were missed opportunities to reflect Jamal's affective tone (impatience, sarcasm) to deepen emotional rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained clear focus on possession/use-related risk and guided the interaction toward concrete risk-reduction steps. Each turn returned to actionable planning and avoided topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-created with frequent invitations to design the plan and the clinician deferring to Jamal's judgment. The clinician avoided a directive expert stance and offered support based on Jamal's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported Jamal's choice by asking which options felt doable and offering help only as requested. Language emphasized Jamal's control over the plan and avoided coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited Jamal's practical solutions and motivations (avoid trouble, keep friendships) and generated specific plans. Deeper probing of personal values or longer-term goals could increase intrinsic motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician framed the conversation as shared problem-solving with repeated invitations for Jamal's input. Offers of support and final framing reinforced joint responsibility for the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician frequently elicited concrete change language and steps, producing several change statements from Jamal. The clinician could more actively amplify and reflect these statements to strengthen commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing with sustain talk and reframed toward practical risk reduction, which redirected resistance. More use of explicit double-sided reflections or strategic summaries could better tip sustain talk toward change.
