# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validated the patient's concerns and barriers (e.g., balancing health/family and time). Avoided blaming or pressuring language, conveying unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking and reflective listening that captured ambivalence and skepticism. Could have deepened affect-focused reflections to explore feelings like frustration or burden more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on one specific target behavior (a single post-lunch glucose test) and tied every intervention to making that step doable. Progressively narrowed options into a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently solicited the patient's preferences, offered choices, and integrated responses into the plan. The clinician and patient functioned as partners solving a practical problem together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored the patient's choice and avoided coercion, offering options rather than directives. Validated the patient's preference for privacy and reinforced ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited useful, patient-generated ideas and logistical solutions for behavior change. However, the clinician focused mainly on implementation and could have probed intrinsic motivations, values, or used importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power-sharing was clear: the clinician asked for the patient's judgment at every step and co-constructed the plan from the patient's ideas. The interaction reflected a strong collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and summarized the plan to amplify 'I'll try' statements. To improve, the clinician could have drawn out deeper motives and stronger commitment language beyond logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and often reframed or neutralized resistance, pivoting to practical supports. More use of double-sided reflections or strategic summaries explicitly naming both sustain and change talk would help resolve ambivalence.
