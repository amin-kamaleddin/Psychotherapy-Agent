# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, offers control to the patient, and validates her choices without judgment. Suggestions are offered gently and respectfully, supporting a warm, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflections and perspective-taking, summarizes core concerns, and notices tone/physiology. Affirms feelings and constraints, showing consistently accurate empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on parenting/coping and co-parenting throughout the session. Keeps the conversation goal-directed with concrete planning, practice, and stepwise problem solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in true co-creation by soliciting priorities, offering options, and editing materials per the patient's preferences. Role-play and reciprocal turns reinforce a joint, mutually shaped plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports patient choice with repeated offers of options and invitations to modify suggestions. Honors the patient's preferences and defers to her expertise, reinforcing ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits values-based motivation and links practical steps to those values. Could deepen evocation by systematically drawing out reasons, confidence, and a specific commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the work as collaborative and defers to the patient's expertise, co-constructing scripts and materials with her wording. Demonstrates strong power sharing and client influence on content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk with evocative questions and support for small, doable steps. Misses some opportunities to amplify desire/ability/reason/need/confidence language and to elicit scaling or stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk and redirects toward practical solutions with neutral scripts for the father. Could further reduce sustain-talk momentum by using more double-sided reflections linking back to the patient's values.
