# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizes hesitation, and avoids judgment; honors the patient's constraints (short texts, low-cost options) throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both practical stressors and affect in ways the patient affirms and expands on.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused direction on smoking reduction/cessation with a specific, time-limited behavioral plan (one-week post-meal gum + breaths) and concrete relapse-prevention steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works largely as a collaborative partner with shared planning and choices, but sometimes shifts to offering clinician-driven implementation details before eliciting the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes the patient's choice and control, offers options rather than directives, and respects follow-up preferences and low-cost limits to support ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation via scaling and small steps but misses opportunities to probe values or personally meaningful reasons for change, leaving evocation relatively superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong power-sharing by offering options, inviting the patient to choose triggers and backups, and co-constructing the plan around the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to elicit change talk (readiness scale, first-step options) but does not sufficiently deepen or amplify desire/reason/need/confidence language to build stronger momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validates ambivalence and avoids reinforcing sustain talk, but rarely uses strategic softening techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or reframing minimizations) to pivot sustain talk toward change.
