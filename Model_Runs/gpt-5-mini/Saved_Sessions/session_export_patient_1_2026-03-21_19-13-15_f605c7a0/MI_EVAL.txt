# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, honoring the patient's preferences (e.g., declining meds/counseling) and normalizing slips; created a warm, supportive stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid empathic listening and occasional reflections that acknowledged the patient's perspective, but missed opportunities for deeper affective or experiential reflections about fatigue, ritual, and emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on mini‑cigar use and oral irritation with progressive problem solving, concrete harm‑reduction steps, and specific action timelines.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—offered choices, asked permission, and co‑designed the if‑then rule and tracking plan—but at times moved into instructive lists rather than eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient autonomy by offering options, reinforcing choice, and accepting the patient's decision against medications or formal counseling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some of the patient's solutions (e.g., tracking template, gum/water replacements) but did not systematically draw out deeper motives, values, or importance/confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision‑making and inclusive language while co‑creating strategies, though could better center the patient's language and solicit his ideas earlier and more often.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced change‑oriented statements and helped convert intentions into concrete plans, but missed systematic use of evocative questions and summaries to amplify change talk and elicit commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and did not amplify sustain talk, but relied on factual corrections rather than strategic MI reflections (e.g., double‑sided or values reflections) to tip ambivalence toward change.
