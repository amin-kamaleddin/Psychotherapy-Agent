# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful stance, normalized options, and framed steps as low‑pressure without shaming, showing clear positive regard for Tyler's position.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate paraphrase that captured Tyler's perspective, but some reflections were a bit formulaic and missed deeper emotions like belonging or fear of conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Stayed narrowly focused on peer-related substance use and alternatives, consistently tying dialogue to an actionable next step with concrete implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong co-creation throughout the session: clinician solicited input, negotiated choices, and worked alongside Tyler (e.g., drafting the text) rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized Tyler's choice and control, offering an optional, low‑pressure test run and allowing him to select and shape the plan and bail rules.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited doable steps and generated change talk through questions about preferences, but leaned toward logistics rather than exploring values or longer‑term reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned Tyler as a partner by inviting preferences, asking him to choose actions, and sharing power in developing the plan and wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully moved Tyler to concrete change statements and a test plan, though the clinician could have amplified and selectively reflected pro‑change language and probed importance/confidence more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed the goal as a low‑pressure test to reduce defensiveness, but relied mainly on neutral reflections rather than double‑sided reflections or strategic reframing.
