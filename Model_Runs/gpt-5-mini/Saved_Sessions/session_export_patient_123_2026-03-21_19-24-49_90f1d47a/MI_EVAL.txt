# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, using affirmations and normalizing skepticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated timely validation and brief reflections of Laura's perspective, but relied more on solution-focused guidance than deeper reflective listening of emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on time management and task initiation with concrete, actionable planning that moved the session toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Behaved as an equal partner by co-creating the schedule, inviting modifications, and framing the work as joint problem-solving rather than top-down instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by offering choices, checking permission, normalizing slips, and emphasizing Laura's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete plans and commitments that produced change talk, but did less to explore intrinsic reasons, values, or confidence to deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—asked permission, adjusted plans to fit Laura's availability, and deferred to her preferences when constructing the schedule.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked commitment language through planning and decisional prompts, though could have more explicitly amplified Laura's own reasons, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed skepticism into low-pressure experiments, but could strengthen use of double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence further.
