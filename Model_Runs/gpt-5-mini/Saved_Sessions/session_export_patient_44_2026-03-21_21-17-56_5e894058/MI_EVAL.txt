# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys unconditional positive regard with repeated, specific affirmations and a strengths-focused, nonjudgmental stance that normalizes progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid perspective-taking and reflective listening, summarizing progress and validating barriers. Missed some opportunities for deeper, complex reflections about the emotional tone of self-comparison.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a tight, appropriate focus on target behaviors with concrete, goal‑directed questions and plans tied to values and benefits.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co‑constructed: the clinician proposes options, asks preferences, and incorporates the client's language into a shared stepwise plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, asks what feels doable, and affirms the client's ownership of decisions without applying pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations, reasons, and resources with scaling and planning prompts but at times moves to solution suggestions rather than drawing out deeper intrinsic values or meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the client as the expert and invites decision-making, sustaining power‑sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces change talk with scaling and concrete commitments, though could more often amplify spontaneous desire/ability language and probe for stronger commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk toward problem‑solving and backups, but would benefit from more double‑sided reflections to acknowledge ambivalence while highlighting change reasons.
