# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, warmth, and validation without blaming or minimizing Daniela's struggles. Responses thanked and affirmed her and normalized difficulties throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries captured both practical and emotional elements, highlighted strengths, and included a safety check, showing strong perspective-taking. The clinician checked understanding before moving to solutions, demonstrating attentive reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior (reliable childcare) with a clear stepwise plan (choose neighbor, draft text, practice replies, confirmation). The clinician balanced goal-directed guidance with client choice and avoided drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The encounter functioned as joint problem-solving: the clinician co-created messages, role-played, and adapted wording to Daniela's preferences. Decisions and wording were shaped by Daniela's input, with the clinician acting as partner and coach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly asked permission, offered multiple options, and let Daniela choose which supports to pursue, avoiding pressuring language. This consistently reinforced Daniela's sense of choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited preferences, options, and past successes and used them toward action, drawing motivation from Daniela. However, there was less elicitation of internal motives, values, and confidence (e.g., scaling questions) to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative approach: the clinician invited Daniela to direct choices, co-constructed texts, and role-played to practice responses. The clinician served as a facilitator rather than an authority, honoring Daniela's input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk around childcare and supported concrete intentions by drafting texts and scripts. Missed opportunities included deeper exploration of reasons, values, confidence, and eliciting stronger commitment language for broader behavior changes like smoking/drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers and sustain talk were acknowledged and not reinforced, with redirection toward feasible strategies like drafting texts and practicing scripts. The clinician could have used more explicit MI techniques (double-sided reflections, pros/cons exploration) to further soften sustain talk.
