# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validated barriers, and normalized concerns (e.g., foggy mornings), showing unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking acknowledged low energy and practical concerns, but reflections were brief and leaned toward solutions rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on increasing activity with a concrete, measurable 2‑week plan, clear backups, and practical implementation strategies to support action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a team effort with choices offered and patient preferences shaping content, though the clinician often provided ready-made solutions instead of eliciting the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently offered options, honored the patient's selections, and framed recommendations as choices rather than prescriptions, supporting the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some preference questions and invitations were used, but the clinician relied largely on clinician-generated solutions and missed opportunities to draw out personal reasons, values, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative stance with co-creation of tools and choice of wording, but at times shifted to an expert-driven delivery without first soliciting the patient's own strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced some change language and small-step options, but did not strategically probe for personal reasons, importance/confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers and sustain talk were acknowledged and reframed nonjudgmentally with practical backups offered, though more double-sided reflections or pros/cons exploration could further tip ambivalence.
