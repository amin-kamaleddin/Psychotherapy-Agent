### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (normalizing avoidance, affirming courage), creating safety in the interaction; no further change needed in this brief session.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking that likely made the patient feel heard, with accurate multi-faceted reflections and validation; could deepen by adding more evocative reflections (e.g., underlying shame or helplessness) and occasional simple reflections to pace hesitation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly cooperative and co‑constructed—patient and clinician jointly generate texts, role‑play, and backup plans, with the clinician inviting patient choices at each turn.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supports choice and control by offering options, multiple text choices, and respecting readiness; alternatives are provided without pressure, reinforcing autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits the patient's own motivations and actionable steps (e.g., choosing support, drafting messages) and converts them into behavior; could be strengthened by explicitly eliciting personal reasons/values and using importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear power‑sharing and joint decision‑making throughout—clinician asks which help would be most useful and follows the patient's lead in drafting and role‑playing messages.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and scaffolds change‑oriented language into concrete first steps (drafting and planning to send a message); could amplify and summarize change statements more often and use scaling to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing avoidance and redirecting to manageable alternatives, thereby reducing its influence; improvement could come from using double‑sided reflections and strategic contrasts with the patient's values/goals.
