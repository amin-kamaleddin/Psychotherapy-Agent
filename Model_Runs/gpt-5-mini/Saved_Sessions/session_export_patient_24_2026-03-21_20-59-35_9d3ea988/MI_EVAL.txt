# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard, validated the patient's perspective, and avoided shaming or labeling. Warm, respectful language and autonomy-affirming statements were used throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with routine permission-giving, acknowledging concerns about training and surprise at the survey. Could deepen empathic connection by reflecting emotional ambivalence and the patient's guardedness or identity-related feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use in relation to marathon training, gathered specifics, provided targeted psychoeducation, and set concrete short-term goals tied to the patient's priorities. The sustained focus helped translate ambivalence into actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and egalitarian, with co-construction of plans, frequent invitations for patient input, and joint rehearsal of strategies. Decisions and troubleshooting were done together rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice through permission checks, offering multiple strategy options, and asking which plan the patient preferred. Suggestions were framed as choices, reinforcing patient ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's reasons for change and practical motivations using scaling and strategy elicitation. Could create more space for the patient to elaborate values and meaning, and amplify change talk to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered clear power-sharing, positioned the patient as the expert, and invited input at every step, including planning and role-play. The clinician encouraged the patient's contributions and shaped the session collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked and strengthened change talk with goal-setting and strategy generation, including SMART-style planning. Missed some opportunities to consistently reflect/amplify change statements and to press for stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and did not reinforce sustain talk, using permission to offer information respectfully. However, the clinician rarely used double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence exploration to strategically soften sustain talk before moving to advice.
