# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone, affirmed Daniela's efforts, and normalized her feelings throughout the interaction. Unconditional positive regard was consistently conveyed via thanks and affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflected Daniela’s concerns and affect, summarizing the childcare impact and validating nervousness. Reflective listening and perspective-taking were consistent and precise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear, appropriate focus on childcare and smoking/drinking and guided the session toward concrete, time-sensitive steps. The agenda moved logically from problem identification to actionable plans (messages, Head Start checklist).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-created the message and confirmation text with Daniela, soliciting and incorporating her preferences at each step. Interaction demonstrated genuine power-sharing and joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirmed Daniela’s choice and control, offered multiple options, and used no-pressure language. Daniela’s preferences determined the focus and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited practical motivation and immediate intentions (neighbor trade, Head Start) and noted progress, but did not deeply explore values, longer-term reasons, or scale confidence to strengthen internal motivation. Evocation focused more on concrete planning than on eliciting strong commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked as a partner by asking open questions, offering options, and inviting Daniela’s decisions and preferences throughout. Power-sharing was evident in drafting and revising messages together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk and translated motivation into concrete steps, but missed opportunities to probe confidence, commitment, and values-based reasons for change. More scaling or commitment questions would have strengthened change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirected to problem-solving with practical strategies, normalizing difficulty. A higher score would require more explicit double-sided reflections that acknowledge ambivalence while steering toward change.
