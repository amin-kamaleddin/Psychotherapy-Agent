# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed warm, nonjudgmental support from the start, normalized ambivalence, and affirmed the patient's perspective throughout with no instances of blaming or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections that tracked concerns and affect, though reflections were often summary-level and could probe deeper into emotional experience driving ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on phone/social media use and progressed clearly from exploration to specific, time-bound planning and contingency strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with frequent co‑creation of solutions, joint drafting of texts/notes, and ready acceptance of the patient's modifications to the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored the patient's choice by offering options, asking what feels doable, and framing the experiment as low-stakes rather than directive or pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited the patient's own motivations and solutions using open prompts and scaling, turning internal reasons into feasible experiments and concrete steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as a shared effort, deferred to the patient's expertise about feasibility, and ensured the plan was shaped substantially by the patient's preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced specific change statements and planning (night, DND, journaling), but could more consistently amplify values-based reasons and strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and used double-sided reflections and harm-minimizing options to redirect toward experiments, though could more strategically tilt reflections to amplify change talk.
