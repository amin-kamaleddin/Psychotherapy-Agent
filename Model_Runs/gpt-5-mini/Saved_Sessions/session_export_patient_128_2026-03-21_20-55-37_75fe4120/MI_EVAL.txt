# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician opened nonjudgmentally and maintained a consistently validating, warm stance, normalizing ambivalence and using affirmations throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that conveyed understanding, but tended to stay at content/practical level rather than exploring deeper emotional nuances like shame or belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on reducing risky drinking while preserving social life and consistently directed the conversation toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed: clinician solicited Tyler's goals and preferences, adapted suggestions to his comfort, and invited joint decision-making and practice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered options rather than directives, checked which choices felt doable, and repeatedly reinforced Tyler's control and ownership without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations and resources (importance/confidence, first steps) that produced change talk, but could use more open, evocative questions to deepen Tyler's own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Tyler as the expert and collaborator by asking preferences, checking comfort before suggestions, and letting him set the plan and order of steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with scales and concrete planning, though could more systematically amplify desire, ability, reasons, and need language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk (social pressure, fun) but mainly shifted to planning without using double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to soften ambivalence.
