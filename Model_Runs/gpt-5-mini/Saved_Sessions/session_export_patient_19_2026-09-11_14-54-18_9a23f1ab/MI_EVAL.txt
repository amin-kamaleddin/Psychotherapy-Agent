# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance and validated the client's agency (e.g., double-sided reflection, praising practical steps). This warm, respectful stance supported openness and avoided shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking were used throughout, with timely, nuanced reflections of ambivalence and tone. The clinician followed the client's lead and checked what felt realistic, showing high empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on protecting money and reducing gambling triggers, with each exchange tied to concrete, time-bound steps. The clinician consistently refocused toward implementation and avoided drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed the plan using inclusive language, offered to role-play and write checklists, and solicited the client's preferences. This mutual approach supported shared ownership of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were routinely offered and the clinician affirmed the client's choice and testing stance rather than prescribing actions. Questions about what 'feels' doable reinforced client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out the client's own solutions and practical motivations, eliciting concrete steps to try. Missed opportunities included more explicit linking of steps to deeper values and probes for confidence/commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated the client as the expert, invited choices, and supported implementation rather than directing it. Frequent invitations to lead the plan showed strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, reflections, and affirmations successfully elicited concrete change talk (e.g., shredding a card, routing pay). To strengthen motivation, the clinician could have amplified values (travel/parenting) and used scaling or commitment questions to consolidate change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ambivalence was acknowledged with double-sided reflections and the clinician redirected quickly to protective strategies, avoiding reinforcement of sustain talk. The clinician could more often use reframing reflections or selectively highlight client statements favoring change to tip the balance further.
