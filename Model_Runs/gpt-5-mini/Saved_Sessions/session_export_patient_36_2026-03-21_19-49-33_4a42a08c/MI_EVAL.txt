# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, normalizing slips and expressing appreciation for the patient’s experience. Language and tone convey clear respect for the patient’s choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows accurate, respectful reflections of the patient’s progress and practical concerns but relies mainly on factual/logistical reflections. Could deepen empathic connection with more complex reflections tying feelings to values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (no email after 7 PM) and orients all planning and problem-solving toward that goal. Concrete steps and scheduling keep the session on track.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions and materials are co-constructed with the patient, with repeated invitations for input and adjustment. The clinician offers supports only after eliciting preferences, demonstrating mutual influence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly emphasizes choice and control, offering options, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language. Repeatedly invites the patient to modify plans and choose timing, reinforcing ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete commitments and logistical reasons for change but focuses more on problem-solving than on internal motives or values. Could strengthen evocation by asking about core values and confidence (e.g., scaling questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently adopts a collaborative stance, asking permission, incorporating patient edits, and sharing planning power. The clinician treats the patient as the expert and invites ongoing joint review.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively amplifies and operationalizes change talk with specific goals and implementation steps but misses opportunities to elicit more desire, ability, reasons, and need statements. Using scaling or prompting for personal reasons would deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Minimizes sustain talk by redirecting to practical strategies and normalizing slips as data rather than failure. The clinician avoids reinforcing reasons to maintain the status quo and reduces defensiveness.
