# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalization with no-blame language and repeated reassurance. Warmth and validation met the highest standard for Acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated high-quality, timely reflections that captured both content and affect and deepened emotional material. Checked pacing and sensitivity to the client's needs throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained appropriate focus on diabetes-related problems and asked targeted questions about overwhelm, but deferred toward affect-focused listening after the client declined change. Limited movement toward concrete, manageable steps given the client's preference to only be listened to.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint endeavor by repeatedly offering options and seeking the client's preference. Language and tone positioned both parties as contributors and reinforced power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the client's right to choose and avoided pressure, offering to listen or explore only if the client wished. Never pushed for a plan, strongly supporting autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made a limited attempt to evoke motivation (offering a choice to focus on change or feelings) but mostly relied on reflections and did not elicit the client's own reasons, values, or past successes. Missed opportunities for gentle, nonpressuring prompts to draw out intrinsic motivators.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently adopted a collaborative stance, checking preferences and offering choices about how to proceed. Positioned the client as the authority on what would feel supportive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made a brief directional prompt early on but largely honored the client's request to only be listened to and did not persistently or strategically probe for change talk. Missed a few gentle prompts that could have elicited small expressions of desire, ability, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing or arguing with sustain talk and used reflective acceptance to prevent escalation. Could have more often used double-sided reflections or tilted summaries to gently nudge toward change.
