# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing and validating the patient's distress while avoiding pathologizing language. Repeatedly supports the patient's request for investigations in a warm, unconditional manner.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening with accurate summaries and recognition of situational patterns. Would benefit from more frequent deeper affect reflections and explicit naming of emotions to convey fuller emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, practical focus on obtaining investigations and managing symptoms, consistently returning to the agreed agenda. The session advances the patient's priority effectively while offering ancillary symptom-management options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑produces the email and phone script and repeatedly asks for the patient's exact wording and preferences. Frames actions as joint and defers final decisions to the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invites choice and honors the patient's preferences, offering options rather than directives. Explicitly refrains from pressuring the patient toward a psychological explanation and supports patient control throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does little to draw out the patient's intrinsic motivations or values beyond securing medical certainty. Missed opportunities for evocative questions about returning to work, reducing isolation, or small steps toward symptom relief.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shares power and works as an equal partner, inviting the patient to shape content and format of communications. Co‑creation of the message and scripts demonstrates strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opportunities to elicit and amplify change talk were limited and often treated as clinical data rather than explored for desire, ability, or reasons to change. Few evocative questions or reflections were used to strengthen pro‑change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk (requests for tests and rejection of psychological explanations) but does not strategically soften it with double‑sided reflections or discrepancy highlighting. Missed chances to subtly shift toward change‑oriented language while still respecting concerns.
