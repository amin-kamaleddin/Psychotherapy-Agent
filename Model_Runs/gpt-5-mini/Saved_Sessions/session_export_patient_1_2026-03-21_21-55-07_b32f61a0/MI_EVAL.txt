# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed respectful, nonjudgmental regard—asked permission, normalized ambivalence, and affirmed the patient’s choices and efforts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and summaries that demonstrated understanding of the patient’s perspective, validated concerns about slipping, and mirrored family motivation and practical constraints.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on mini‑cigar use and reducing indoor smoking, progressing efficiently from assessment to specific, measurable planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a partnership—clinician solicited input at each step, practiced with the patient, and co‑wrote the checklist and plan rather than imposing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient’s sense of choice by offering options, asking which tactics felt doable, and emphasizing patient ownership of the plan without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation and reasons for change from the patient using the importance ruler and targeted questions, then had the patient articulate and rehearse concrete change language and plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative stance—asked permission, offered options, invited the patient to choose tactics, and collaboratively constructed the one‑week checklist and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk by asking readiness/importance, reinforcing chosen steps, encouraging concrete commitments, and rehearsing the one‑week checklist.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately avoided confrontation and offered brief facts with permission, but missed opportunities to more explicitly explore and reflect minimization (e.g., non‑inhaling belief) with double‑sided reflections to deepen discrepancy.
