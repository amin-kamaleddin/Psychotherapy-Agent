# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and nonjudgmental support, offering frequent affirmations and normalizing ambivalence without labeling or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflective listening and summaries of ambivalence and practical concerns, but could probe more deeply into affective material (loneliness, shame, fear).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on avoiding repeat DUIs and protecting license/job, tying questions and suggestions to concrete plans and outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-constructs the plan by soliciting patient preferences, rehearsing language together, and endorsing patient-led choices throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms choice, offers multiple options, and frames help as optional rather than directive, reinforcing the patient's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical motivations and change talk (plans, supports, confidence ratings) but could deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring values and personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a collaborative stance, inviting the patient's voice in wording and planning and functioning as coach rather than director.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits and reinforces change talk with open questions, specific action planning, rehearsal, and commitment prompts that produced concrete steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilfully avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to harm-reduction strategies, though could more explicitly reflect and normalize minimizations before pivoting.
