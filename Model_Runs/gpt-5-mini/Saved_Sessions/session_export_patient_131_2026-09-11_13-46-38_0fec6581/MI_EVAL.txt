### Acceptance

**Comments:** Clinician is nonjudgmental and affirms Wiremu's efforts and difficulties, conveying respect. To reach top score they could have more explicit normalization of setbacks and more individualized (cultural/whānau) affirmations.

### Empathy

**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of ambivalence and key motivations (family, independence), showing good listening. Empathy stayed at content/practical level and could be deepened by reflecting underlying emotions (shame, fear) and cultural context.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol use and reunification, consistently linking questions to actionable steps. Repeated narrowing to a single concrete first step (text + alarm) shows excellent directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is highly collaborative with the psychiatrist offering options and following Wiremu’s preferences, co‑constructing the plan. Language and planning share power without taking over, enabling client-driven choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supports choice by offering options, asking what feels doable, and avoiding pressure. This validates Wiremu’s independence while helping him plan practical steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is pulled from Wiremu’s own values and prior steps, with questions that elicit concrete reasons and plans. The clinician resists supplying reasons and instead amplifies and organizes the client's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Wiremu is treated as the expert in his life, with co-created planning and frequent checks about what he trusts to follow through. The clinician shares power and decision-making throughout the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and amplifies change talk and gets concrete commitment language (texting mum, booze‑free nights). To reach 5 they could further summarize and strengthen commitment language (e.g., scaling confidence or asking for specific follow‑through plans).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ambivalence is acknowledged and not argued with, and the clinician moves quickly to elicit small steps toward change. There were missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections and strategic reframing to explicitly redirect sustain talk toward values and goals.
