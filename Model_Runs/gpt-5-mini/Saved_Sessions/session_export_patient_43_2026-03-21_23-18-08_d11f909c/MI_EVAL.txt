# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizes ambivalence, and affirms the client’s priorities which reduces defensiveness and supports autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections captured content and ambivalence well, but the clinician could probe deeper into underlying feelings (frustration, guilt, worry) and the emotional meaning of pressures to increase empathic resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking and steadily moved the session toward a specific, measurable experiment with concrete planning and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed: the clinician summarized, invited client choices, and refined the plan with client input, demonstrating reciprocity and shared ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently endorsed client choice and avoided pressure, repeatedly inviting the client to choose supports, timing, and cues to preserve voluntary action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited the client’s own reasons and motivations and had the client generate a specific experiment and possible next steps rather than supplying change arguments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered power-sharing and positioned the client as the expert by asking for preferences and letting the client select the specifics of the test.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used open invitations and targeted probes to draw out and amplify change talk, then reflected and reinforced it into a clear, small-step plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk via double-sided and neutral reflections and reframed 'not quitting' as a manageable test to reduce defensiveness and keep focus forward.
