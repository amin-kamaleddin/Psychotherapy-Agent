# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing symptoms and validating Naomi's efforts (e.g., praising the grounding attempt). These behaviors created a safe, supportive tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated clear understanding by reflecting symptoms and normalizing panic, but empathy could have been deeper and more frequent. There were missed opportunities to reflect Naomi's underlying feelings (frustration, embarrassment, fear of professional consequences).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a strong, appropriate focus on managing social/performance anxiety with practical, job-focused goals. The clinician consistently linked discussion to a concrete action plan and measurable follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician practiced with Naomi and co-created the routine, inviting her input on wording and use. Collaboration fell short when the clinician often presented sequences of interventions rather than eliciting Naomi's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly honored Naomi's choices, offered options, and avoided pressuring language (e.g., choices about grounding now vs. planning and check-in timing). Control was clearly framed as Naomi's to accept, adapt, or decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was drawn out (agreement to try the routine and log), but much of the interaction relied on offering strategies rather than eliciting Naomi's own reasons, values, or confidence. The clinician did not consistently use targeted evocation techniques like importance/confidence ratings or exploring past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally fostered partnership by tailoring the routine and practicing together, treating Naomi as an expert in her experience. Partnership waned at times when the clinician shifted toward an expert role by providing multiple concrete strategies without first asking Naomi's preference.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some effective elicitation of change talk (Naomi voiced wanting shoots to be less awful and agreed to try), but the clinician did not systematically amplify or probe for more desire, reasons, need, ability, or commitment. Follow-up evoking questions to deepen change language were often missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to small, doable steps and alternatives when Naomi expressed doubt. Opportunities to use MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or balanced pros/cons exploration) were sometimes missed.
