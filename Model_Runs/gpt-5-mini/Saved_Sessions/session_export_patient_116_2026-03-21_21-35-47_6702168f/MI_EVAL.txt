# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating Samir’s choice and honoring his limits (e.g., not sending messages without approval). Suggestions were framed as optional and respectful, reinforcing his autonomy throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective listening and concise summaries that preserved rapport and accurately reflected Samir’s stance. Could deepen empathic responding with more complex or emotion-focused reflections to capture underlying frustration or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on medication adherence and translated resistance into concrete, manageable steps (alarm, pill placement, phone placement) with a specific 3-day follow-up. The clinician prevented drift by summarizing and confirming agreed actions and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively solicited Samir’s input at each step and adapted suggestions to his preferences, co-creating the plan (weekday alarm, no family nudges). The clinician validated and recorded Samir’s choices, reflecting a mutual, equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Samir’s sense of choice and control, explicitly agreeing not to act without his permission and offering options rather than directives. Language reinforced Samir’s ownership of decisions and respected his boundaries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used open questions and a confidence ruler to elicit practical motivators and specific steps, producing modest change talk and a plan. Missed opportunities to probe deeper intrinsic values or long-term reasons that might strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the interaction as collaborative by repeatedly asking preference questions and negotiating boundaries, with decisions co-created (alarm time, label, family involvement). The clinician treated Samir as the expert on what would fit his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete, change-oriented language (alarm, phone placement, prep meds) and followed up with targeted questions that produced an actionable plan. Could have done more to amplify and elaborate Samir’s small commitments to strengthen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or pushing and used rolling-with-resistance language, accepting Samir’s reluctance and redirecting to pragmatic, low-effort options. Neutral summaries and focus on feasible steps minimized reinforcement of sustain talk.
