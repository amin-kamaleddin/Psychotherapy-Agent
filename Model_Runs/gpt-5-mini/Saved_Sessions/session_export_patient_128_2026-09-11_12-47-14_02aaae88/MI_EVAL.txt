# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is warm and nonjudgmental throughout, normalizing ambivalence and affirming Tyler's strengths and choices. Acceptance is consistently demonstrated in language that avoids blame and validates competing priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate content-level reflections and validation of ambivalence. Could deepen emotional reflections to capture feelings (worry, fear, belonging) to reach a top rating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on risky drinking and moves logically toward a concrete harm-reduction plan. Questions consistently advance planning and commitment rather than allowing topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist treats Tyler as an equal partner, inviting ideas and co-constructing the plan (e.g., drafting texts, choosing supports). Tyler's suggestions clearly shape the intervention, reflecting genuine collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly honors Tyler's choice by offering options, asking which feels doable, and seeking permission before offering help. Language is non-directive and supports Tyler's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Tyler's reasons for change (protecting school, avoiding police) and links them to specific strategies. Could more consistently probe deeper intrinsic motives and assess confidence/importance to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exchange is framed as joint problem-solving with frequent invitations to choose and shape the plan, demonstrating relinquished expert authority. Decisions and details are co-created with Tyler throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and develops change talk via targeted open questions, asks for specifics, and secures a small commitment (phone reminder). Could further amplify and summarize change statements and use scaling questions to increase commitment strength.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses double-sided reflection early to acknowledge reasons for continued drinking while pivoting to harm-reduction. Would benefit from more explicit reframing and repeated double-sided reflections when sustain talk appears.
