# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician used a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone, acknowledged the patient's concerns and choices, and normalized withdrawal—demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and validation were present, but reflections were often brief and quickly followed by information-giving rather than deeper exploration of the patient's felt experience and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear and appropriate focus on caffeine behavior with concrete goals, a 2-week tracking plan, specific strategies, and repeated returns to the behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reflected true co‑creation: the clinician solicited input at key decisions, adapted recommendations to the patient's routine, and used co-design language to build the plan together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered multiple options, asked which felt doable, respected the patient's choices (e.g., preserving morning cups), and emphasized voluntary experimentation and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete commitments (swap, 60/40, delay morning cup) but relied heavily on information provision rather than systematically drawing out the patient's intrinsic reasons, values, or readiness scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently fostered a collaborative stance by inviting the patient's preferences, offering to co-create tracking/reminders, and shaping recommendations around the patient's routines and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully prompted and affirmed some change talk (asking which change felt doable, translating choice into a plan) but missed chances to amplify and deepen the patient's own change language with follow‑ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and offered low‑effort alternatives when the patient expressed sustain talk, though could more strategically use double‑sided reflections and summaries to resolve ambivalence.
