# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a warm, nonjudgmental tone while normalizing struggle. Could add more personalized normalization (e.g., naming repeated attempts) but overall acceptance was strong and consistent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening; reflections and summaries captured Linda's conflict and affect and made her feel understood. Questions were open and well-paced, and the patient explicitly noted that talking helped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking cessation and consistently moved the session toward concrete, actionable steps (check-ins, texts, call agenda, social scripts). The session avoided drifting and stayed purposefully goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-created materials and next steps with Linda, soliciting her input on wording, timing, and priorities and integrating her feedback. Interaction modeled an equal, co-creative partnership rather than an expert-driven plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Linda's choices, offered multiple options, and asked permission before drafting materials, demonstrating respect for agency. The clinician consistently deferred to her preferences and supported self-directed decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Linda's motivations, resources, and reasons for change with evocative questions and a confidence ruler, drawing motivation from within. Would benefit from more consistent amplification and summarizing of change talk to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative, asking which option to focus on and offering to co-create texts, agendas, and scripts while following Linda's lead. Power was shared and decisions were jointly constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently evoked and reinforced change talk with targeted questions and supportive reflections, and highlighted practical next steps. Missed some opportunities to amplify desire/ability/reason language and deepen commitment (e.g., double reflections or follow-up commitment questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk neutrally and redirected toward supports and specifics without arguing, using acceptance to avoid reinforcement. Could use more strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, summaries that end on change) to further tip ambivalence toward action.
