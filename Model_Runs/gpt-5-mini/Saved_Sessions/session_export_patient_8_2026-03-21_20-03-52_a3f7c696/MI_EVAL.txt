# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client's experience (e.g., warm openings, validating curiosity, avoiding blame). Statements repeatedly acknowledge the client's perspective without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, paraphrasing ambivalence and tracking both sustain and change talk. Reflections and normalizing language show a clear grasp of the client's internal state and barriers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on phone/social-media use with questions tied to triggers, consequences, and concrete experiments. Uses scaling and asks for specific small successes and fallback plans to keep momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in genuine joint problem-solving, checking preferences and negotiating experiments stepwise (sticky note wording, micro-behaviors). Offers brief coaching only after eliciting the client's choices, demonstrating two-way collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports client agency by offering options, asking which strategies feel doable, and inviting revisions rather than pressuring. Language consistently reinforces the client's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's reasons and readiness using open questions, scaling, and self-observation experiments but could more strongly link change to personal values and meaningful goals. Missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation through evocative follow‑ups.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters client expertise and choice throughout the session, asking permission and letting the client select strategies and wording. The session is co-created with the client making concrete decisions about next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and supports change language (scales, experiments, defining small successes) and reinforces incremental steps. Could more strategically amplify and link expressions about sleep/grades to valued goals to strengthen movement toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reframes sustain talk empathetically while offering alternatives, which reduces resistance. Would benefit from more double‑sided reflections and summaries that explicitly name both sides of ambivalence and pivot to change.
