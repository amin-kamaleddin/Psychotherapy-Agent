# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the client's perspective throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and accurate, double‑sided reflections but misses opportunities to probe deeper emotions (fears, shame) with more open invitations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on community participation and progresses logically to actionable, specific small‑step planning (timing, reminders, staff confirmation).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and integrates the client's responses into the plan, framing the work as joint problem‑solving rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces choice and control with repeated invitations and manageable options, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits preferences and tentative motivations through choices and experiments but could ask more evocative questions to uncover values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the client as decision‑maker by offering choices, though frequent structured options slightly limit elicitation of the client's spontaneous ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and shapes tentative change talk into concrete experiments (five‑minute log‑on, reminders), but could more often reflect/amplify pro‑change language and ask about importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and neutralizes sustain talk with double‑sided reflections, yet tends to neutralize rather than strategically redirect to discrepancy or the costs of staying home.
