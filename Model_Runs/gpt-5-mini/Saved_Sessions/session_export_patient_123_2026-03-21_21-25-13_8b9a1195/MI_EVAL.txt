# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental tone, validating stresses and honoring preferences (e.g., privacy limits and preferred name). There is no evidence of labeling, blame, or coercion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening are evident through summaries and validations of the client's routine and stressors. The clinician reinforces coping strategies, signaling understanding and strengthening rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on keeping grades steady and constructing a check-in plan. However, the original referral concern (alcohol screening) was not addressed, which would have improved directional completeness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is clearly collaborative, with the clinician soliciting preferences on frequency, medium, content, and reminders and confirming the plan. The clinician invites changes and gains consent for next steps, demonstrating shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms choice, offers multiple options (format, name, frequency), and respects the client's autonomy about intensity and privacy. Language is nonpressuring and lets the client set the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits the client's own strategies and reasons for wellbeing (e.g., coping behaviors and desire for check-ins) but uses few deeper evocative probes. More targeted questions linking values to goals or briefly exploring referral-related ambivalence would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is co-constructed with power shared; the clinician repeatedly asks for Laura's input and follows her lead on format and scheduling. Plans are restated and confirmed collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces realistic, small goals and coping strategies appropriate to the client's stated aims. There were missed opportunities to deepen change talk (e.g., exploring what maintaining grades would mean) and to briefly elicit any thoughts about the referral concern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is essentially absent and the clinician avoids eliciting or reinforcing reasons to maintain the status quo. Neutral, option-giving language and redirection to planning minimized the risk of strengthening sustain talk.
