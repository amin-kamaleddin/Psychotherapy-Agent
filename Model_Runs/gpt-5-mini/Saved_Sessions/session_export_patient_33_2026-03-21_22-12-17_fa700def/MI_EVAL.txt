# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance with supportive openings and affirmations. No labeling or moralizing, even when discussing safety and risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and accurate reflections that facilitated openness. Could use deeper, more frequent complex reflections to explore underlying shame, grief, or the emotional meaning of 'escape.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on harm-reduction targets and steered the session toward concrete, measurable steps (Narcan, role-play, supplies). Repeatedly brought the conversation back to action planning while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Behaved as an equal partner, co-constructing plans and adapting to the patient's preferences. Language and tone invited mutual problem-solving and positioned the patient as an active agent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported the patient's sense of choice and control by offering options and permission to decline. Advice and scripts were offered with permission and framed as optional rather than prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out concrete change intentions and readiness (patient rated 10/10) and elicited prior attempts and barriers. Could more consistently elicit intrinsic motives, broader values, and use summaries to amplify change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance—asking permission, inviting preferences, and sharing power in decision-making. Co-created plans rather than prescribing steps, demonstrating excellent partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, affirmations, and concrete planning that supported commitment. Missed opportunities to systematically amplify, summarize, and probe importance/confidence to further strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged the coping function of use, reframing toward safety and practical risk reduction. Could increase use of double-sided reflections and explicitly validate the desire to escape while pivoting to goals to further soften sustain talk.
