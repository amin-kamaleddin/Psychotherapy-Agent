# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence with a warm, nonjudgmental tone. Could further deepen acceptance by explicitly naming and normalizing common setbacks when slips occur.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, nuanced reflective listening and synthesizes the patient's ambivalence and priorities effectively. Could occasionally probe more into the affective quality of the patient's fear to deepen emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing weekday drinking and moves the session toward a specific, time-limited experiment with concrete coping strategies. Keeps momentum balanced with client-centered questions and operational details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is highly collaborative, inviting and honoring the patient's ideas and co-developing a concrete plan (timing, script, NA beer). Language positions the clinician as a teammate rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's choice and agency by framing the plan as a test and asking which options feel most doable. Validates hesitancy and respects the patient's right to change or revise the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits the patient's own motivations and reasons for change (protecting job, family, clearer mornings) and draws out personally relevant strategies. Uses open questions and reflections to pull motivation from the patient rather than imposing it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models genuine partnership by repeatedly inviting the patient's input and letting the patient own the plan. Co-constructs solutions and resists expert dominance throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces concrete change talk and uses summaries to strengthen it. Missed opportunities to deepen and amplify change talk by asking importance/confidence scales, soliciting more personal reasons, and eliciting commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to solutions while validating the patient's concerns about coping and belonging. Could more explicitly use softening techniques (double-sided reflections, selective reflections or strategic summaries) to reduce the persuasive weight of sustain talk.
