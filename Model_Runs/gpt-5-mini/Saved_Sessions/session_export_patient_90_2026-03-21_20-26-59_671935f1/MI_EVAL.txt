# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician set a clear nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly normalized Marcus's distress, avoiding labels or blame. Permission statements and validating reflections consistently communicated unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing both content and emotion and inviting elaboration. The clinician sustained empathic engagement instead of rushing to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on asthma management and linked barriers (costs, appointments, dog) throughout the visit. Conversation stayed on concrete, behavior-linked actions with actionable next steps and no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed: patient was invited to choose priorities and scheduling preferences and given options for next steps. Role-play and offers to search resources demonstrated shared responsibility for planning and skill-building.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Marcus's right to choose, sought permission before offering advice, and framed suggestions as options rather than prescriptions. This consistently reinforced his sense of control and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Marcus's motivations and concrete change statements by asking what he wanted to change and which options felt doable, and reinforced his selected steps. Could be more systematic in drawing out values links, pros/cons, and scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and deferred to Marcus's expertise and preferences, offering to support his chosen next steps. Language and offers (scripts, role-play, resource searches) positioned clinician as coach rather than authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk (med check, spacer use, bedroom changes) and offered a menu of options to support choice. Missed some opportunities to deepen and amplify change talk with elaboration prompts, scaling, or eliciting commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reframed options toward harm-reduction strategies that allow Marcus to keep his dog, offering practical resources. Relied more on information and brief reflections; more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries could have softened sustain talk further.
