# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, validated Marisol's choice, and avoided judgment throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking matched Marisol's concise style and accurately captured her views; more probing of emotional nuance could have deepened empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stayed tightly focused on oral care for the lip piercing with a clear stepwise plan, reminders, and troubleshooting without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician acted as an equal partner by offering options, asking preferences, and co-creating the practical plan and reminder wording with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reinforced Marisol's freedom to choose by asking permission, presenting options, and framing suggestions as choices made on her terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited doable commitments and used a confidence ruler, but relied more on information and pragmatic prompts than deeper evocative questions about values or personal reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interactional features demonstrated genuine partnership: permission, shared decision-making, and tailoring of reminders and supplies based on the patient's input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician amplified available change talk into concrete commitments (reminders, saline, brushing) and refined the plan with confidence scaling, though deeper evocation could have produced stronger change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk into harm-reduction strategies, but could have used more double-sided reflections to more actively shift ambivalence toward change talk.
