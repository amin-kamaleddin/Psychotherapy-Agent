# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established and maintained unconditional positive regard with validating, nonjudgmental language that reduced shame. Little to no blaming or coercive statements were used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with complex reflections capturing feelings, meaning, and function of smoking. The clinician repeatedly checked understanding and conveyed warmth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and its role while honoring patient choice, progressing from exploration to a specific, time-bound experiment and follow-up. The session stayed goal-directed without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative interaction: the clinician solicited priorities, offered options, and co-constructed the experiment and backup plan with the patient. The patient’s agenda shaped the session throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized autonomy with language like 'I won’t push' and framed the experiment as optional and patient-led. The clinician checked preferences for follow-up and respected the patient's readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's reasons, ideas, and a low-risk experiment which surfaced some intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy. Could have more deeply evoked values and amplified change talk tied to the role-model concern.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the interaction as a joint exploration, repeatedly deferring to the patient's expertise and choices about what to explore. The clinician invited co-creation of the plan and follow-up timing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced some change talk (concern for children, willingness to try an experiment) and asked for specifics. Missed opportunities to amplify role-model language and deepen desire/reasons/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized ambivalence, and pivoted to a no-pressure experiment and harm-reduction options. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge sustain talk while foregrounding change language.
