# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating frustration and normalizing ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that matched the patient's affect, helping him feel heard and creating safety for further dialogue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking and its interpersonal consequences, offering specific steps and rehearsals to keep the session goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a genuine partner by asking permission, offering alternatives, inviting role-play, and co-constructing scripts with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice, framed options as voluntary, and honored his right not to quit while suggesting modifiable tools.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out some motivation tied to the relationship and elicited concrete steps, but did not sufficiently probe deeper personal values, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated true partnership through shared decision-making, inviting the patient to choose options and shaping plans together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and strengthened limited change talk and concrete commitments (e.g., smoke outside, skip meals), but missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivations or assess readiness/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used harm-reduction strategies to reduce resistance, though some accommodations (e.g., preserving routines) risked reinforcing sustain talk instead of using more double-sided reflections.
