# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, using validating statements and affirmations that reinforced safety. The clinician avoided labeling or shaming when the patient minimized her drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated perspective-taking and reflective listening with supportive summaries, but often moved quickly to offering solutions. Deeper, more complex reflections of feelings and ambivalence would strengthen empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related risk and tied recommendations directly to that goal. The session moved logically from assessment to a concrete plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the session as a joint endeavor by co-creating the plan, soliciting patient input, and adapting strategies to her preferences. The clinician offered to draft messages and arrange a mutually agreed check-in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking permission, and accepting patient-selected smaller steps. Language and pacing honored the patient's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language through choices and planning, but many moves were directive and solution-focused rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons or confidence. Missed opportunities for open questions and scaling to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and shared decision-making, repeatedly positioning the patient as the agent of change. The clinician invited preferences and let the patient's choices shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created multiple opportunities for change talk and reinforced commitments with praise and follow-up planning. Additional prompts to elicit personal values, expected benefits, or confidence ratings would have amplified change talk further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and accepted the patient's perspective, using neutral-to-positive reframing to sidestep sustain talk. More strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) could have softened sustain language more actively.
