# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming throughout the session, normalized ambivalence and treated slips without blame, reinforcing unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and validated the patient's experience (e.g., smoking as calming vs. role‑model concern), but could have probed more deeply into the emotional meaning of being a role model.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking and guided the session logically toward a specific, measurable goal (no smoking in the car for a week) with actionable steps and follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co‑created the plan, adjusted language to the patient's requests, and engaged supports, but at times offered many ready‑made options instead of eliciting the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's right to choose, asked permission before offering help, and framed suggestions as optional while respecting noncommitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation around being a role model and used a confidence ruler to strengthen commitment, but could use more open questions to deepen values‑based reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displayed a collaborative stance by inviting choices and editing materials per request, though occasional unsolicited advice introduced a mildly expert‑driven element.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and amplified change talk with a concrete plan, confidence rating, and follow‑up, but missed opportunities to further explore why change matters to the patient.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and normalized ambivalence but often moved quickly to practical solutions rather than using double‑sided reflections or summaries to soften and resolve sustain talk.
