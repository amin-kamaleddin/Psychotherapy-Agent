# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment with affirmations and reassuring language. The clinician normalizes ambivalence and supports the patient's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, capturing ambivalence well. Missed opportunities to probe deeper emotion and use double-sided reflections to explore feelings more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy with targeted materials and concrete action items. The session stays goal-directed without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-produces materials and repeatedly checks the patient's preferences, ensuring the patient's ideas shape the work. The interaction models equal partnership rather than an expert lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, asks permission, and frames guidance as information to support patient decision-making. The clinician honors the patient's values and suggests small, patient-chosen steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical commitments and some change talk (e.g., putting wine away, swaps), but relies more on directive information than open questions to draw out deeper reasons and confidence. Could use scaling and reflective prompts to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters strong partnership and power-sharing through joint planning, rehearsal, and offering options. The clinician centers the patient's expertise and preferences throughout the encounter.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforces and elicits concrete commitments and small steps, turning ideas into practical plans. Could further amplify change talk by asking about importance and confidence and reflecting the patient's own motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (skepticism about evidence) but primarily responds with corrective information rather than MI strategies to soften resistance. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and strategic redirection to reduce sustain talk.
