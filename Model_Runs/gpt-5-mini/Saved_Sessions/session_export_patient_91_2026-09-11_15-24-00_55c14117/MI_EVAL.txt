# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent, nonjudgmental positive regard—validating Marcus's burden and respecting his perspective. Warmth and support are maintained throughout, signaling unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and accurate reflections capture Marcus's burden and specific worries about forgetting or dismissing alerts. Could deepen empathy by exploring underlying emotions (e.g., anxiety or embarrassment) more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on a concrete behavior (daily pre‑lunch glucose check) and a time‑bounded two‑week trial, with each question returning to making the plan doable. The session moves logically from broad invitation to a specific, rehearsed action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative—work framed as shared, patient input invited at each decision point, and clinician offers role‑play rather than dictating. Decisions are jointly made and the plan is co‑constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently offers options and asks Marcus to choose the tweak he trusts, avoiding pressure and reinforcing his right to try and evaluate. Ownership of the change process is emphasized throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits Marcus's practical solutions and multiple commitments (alarm, calendar alert, script, rehearsal). Could strengthen evocation by drawing out personal reasons for change, past successes, and confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treats Marcus as the expert on his life and shares power in decision‑making, repeatedly checking preferences and offering choices. The plan is driven by Marcus's selections and co‑created step‑by‑step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician actively elicits and reinforces change language and concrete planning, resulting in multiple verbal commitments. Would benefit from more explicit prompts about reasons, importance, and confidence to amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward workable strategies and small tests of change. Could further soften sustain talk using double‑sided reflections or explicit summaries of ambivalence.
