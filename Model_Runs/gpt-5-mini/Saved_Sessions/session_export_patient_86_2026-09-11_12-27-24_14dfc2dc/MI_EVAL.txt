# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently shows unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, avoids blame or moralizing and honors the patient's request for no lectures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate reflections and use of the patient's language, plus checks of preferences like SMS vs plain text.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking change throughout and consistently moves the conversation to concrete, achievable actions (GP call, delay first cigarette, reminder text).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine shared problem-solving: asks how the patient wants help and co-produces tools (script, reminder), letting the patient shape content and format.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirms the patient's control and offers options rather than directives, respecting boundaries around advice and lectures.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits motivation and converts it into concrete commitments (GP call, small steps) but could probe more for personal values and deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reflects true partnership with repeated checks, co-creation of materials, and clear power-sharing where the patient's choices drive the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change and commitment language, including a specific plan, but could deepen and broaden change talk by exploring underlying values and reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without confrontation and offers manageable alternatives, though could more often use double-sided reflections to explicitly name both sides and build discrepancy.
