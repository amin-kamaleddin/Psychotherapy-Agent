# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental—normalized Farid's experience and used affirmations and self-compassion prompts throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and validation were present, but the clinician missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections of Farid's discouragement and self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing brief exercise/resets and moved the session toward concrete, measurable planning and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as true joint problem-solving—clinician solicited input, adapted options to Farid's responses, and invited him to choose and refine plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Farid's choices, offered multiple options, and reinforced ownership while normalizing slips and inviting permission for supports.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete commitments and planning language, but relied more on offering solutions than on drawing out Farid's own motivations, values, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—clinician checked preferences, invited choice at each step, co-constructed the plan, and offered to support follow-up without imposing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk with commitments and micro-plans, though much of the language was clinician-led rather than elicited from Farid's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed concerns toward small steps (e.g., '2 minutes still counts'), but did not consistently use MI-specific double-sided reflections to tip toward change.
