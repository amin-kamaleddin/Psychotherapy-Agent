# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, naming concerns neutrally and validating the patient's priorities. No blaming or clinical distancing is apparent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses repeated accurate reflections and empathic summaries that capture the patient's feelings and perspective, and checks how the patient prefers information to be delivered. These moves support clear patient-centered understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on understanding and safely starting the statin, returning to concrete decision points throughout. The session results in a specific, actionable plan (gradual start, checklist, red‑flag language).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co-constructed—clinician asks for preferences at multiple steps and waits for patient direction while offering to draft materials. The patient's choices substantially shape the checklist and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options and affirms the patient's choices without pressuring, asking which format and plan would make the patient feel safest. Leaves final decisions to the patient, reinforcing a strong sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical reasons and preferences that point toward readiness (safety, gradual start, checklist), but focuses more on mechanics than deeper intrinsic motivations or values. Could further explore long-term health goals or confidence levels.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language and co-creates the checklist content and format with the patient, sharing power and expertise. The clinician repeatedly solicits the patient's input on what would work best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces the patient's change language and specific steps (e.g., every-other-night plan, checklist). Missed opportunities include amplifying commitment and using scaling questions to strengthen readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reframes the patient's skepticism without arguing, offering options that reduce sustain talk and emphasize safety. Could add more double-sided reflections to explicitly honor reluctance while gently steering toward change.
