# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, honored boundaries, and avoided moralizing throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking (e.g., acknowledged embarrassment and annoyance), validating the patient's feelings and reducing defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained focus on alcohol-related behavior and practical interventions, but missed some opportunities to deepen focus on longer-term consequences or specific goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—asked for preferences, co-created the exact boundary phrasing, and positioned the clinician as an ally rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice with repeated affirmations, offering options and asking permission rather than using pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used evocation-consistent moves (choice, concern scale) but did not sufficiently probe for intrinsic motivations or systematically explore ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was co-constructed with shared decision-making, frequent invitations for the patient's input, and acceptance of his final decisions and phrasing preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered a menu and asked a concern rating but did not follow up with targeted evocative questions to draw out or amplify the patient's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and shifted to harm-reduction and boundary skills, though could have used more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both benefits and costs.
