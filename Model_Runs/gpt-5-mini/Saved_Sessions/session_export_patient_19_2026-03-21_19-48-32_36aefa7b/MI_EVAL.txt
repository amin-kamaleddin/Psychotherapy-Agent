# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validating disclosures and normalizing slips. Warm, respectful language and avoidance of moralizing were evident throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic reflections and accurate summary of feelings, responding sensitively to relief and nervousness. Could have spent more time exploring emotional nuance behind minimizing humor and mixed pride/shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on gambling and related financial behaviours with concrete, actionable targets. Pacing moved logically from assessment to specific planning and rehearsal without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session functioned as a genuine collaboration with co-created scripts, role-play, and repeated invitations for patient input. Clinician adjusted plans to the patient’s preferences and practiced responses together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and respecting the patient’s readiness. Emphasized flexibility and normalized slips to reduce pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient’s motivations (travel, family) and linked them to change options, but did not systematically deepen exploration of importance, confidence, or past successes. More scaling or linking values to specific steps would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely co-constructed the plan with shared decision-making and role-play, empowering the patient’s expertise. At times the clinician provided detailed directives without first eliciting the patient’s usual approach, slightly reducing parity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk, asking for a specific first step and amplifying commitments through role-play and implementation details. Missed opportunities to more explicitly connect change talk to the patient’s core values to further strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and shifted focus to harm reduction with concrete barriers and safety planning. Could have used more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries to acknowledge sustain talk while pivoting to change.
