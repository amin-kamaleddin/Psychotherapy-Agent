# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, avoids judgment, and honors the patient's preferences throughout the visit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening are evident, with validations that capture both emotion and content of the patient's concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction/cessation and NRT use, tying discussion to concrete strategies and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative—options are offered and preferences queried—but could strengthen equal partnership by co-constructing a specific, patient-worded plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly supported through offering choices, asking permission, and reassuring the patient that low-commitment options are acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of willingness occurs, but the encounter is largely clinician-driven with fewer open questions and fewer prompts to draw out deeper personal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician fosters partnership by asking which options feel doable and offering shared problem-solving, yet could elicit the patient's agenda and summarize goals more explicitly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician creates opportunities for change talk and elicits concrete language (e.g., trying the patch), but missed chances to amplify reasons and deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed toward harm-reduction rather than argued with; adding double-sided reflections would better highlight discrepancy.
