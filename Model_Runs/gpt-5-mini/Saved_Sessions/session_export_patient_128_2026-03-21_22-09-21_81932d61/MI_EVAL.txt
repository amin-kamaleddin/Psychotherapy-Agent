# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating language throughout the session, acknowledging Tyler's efforts and normalizing his experience. Demonstrates clear unconditional positive regard that supported disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with timely paraphrases that captured ambivalence and frustration, helping Tyler feel understood. Could increase depth with more affective and complex reflections to explore emotional weight and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on risky drinking and harm reduction, linking discussion to Tyler's priorities and moving logically toward actionable steps. The session avoided drifting and progressed from ambivalence to concrete planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with shared language, co‑construction of plans, and in‑session practice of strategies. Clinician solicited Tyler's preferences and followed his lead in drafting messages and exit lines.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported Tyler's choice by offering options, asking permission, and honoring hedged commitments while encouraging trial changes. The clinician repeatedly framed suggestions as choices and asked which felt doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations, goals, and reasons for change using open questions and scaling, drawing motivation from Tyler. Could further maximize evocation by eliciting more of Tyler's own pros/cons and personally meaningful reasons before offering solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reflected a genuine partnership with power‑sharing, invitation of input, and joint decision‑making. The clinician repeatedly checked what felt doable and invited Tyler to select and commit to strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions, affirmations, and confidence scaling, leading to specific change statements. Would benefit from more frequent reflections and amplification to convert tentative desire/reason language into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and redirected toward harm‑reduction, acknowledging social benefits without judgment. Could add more double‑sided reflections that explicitly hold both the pull of socializing and the reasons for change to further soften sustain talk.
