# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized Jason's feelings without blame, even acknowledging his responsible actions. The clinician avoided moralizing or criticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used timely, accurate, and sometimes complex reflections that captured Jason's affect and concerns, eliciting further disclosure. Summaries tied feelings to actions and demonstrated clear understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the incident and its immediate consequences, consistently steering toward concrete next steps (call, email, written statement). The session avoided tangents and moved efficiently to action planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co‑created scripts and plans with Jason, invited his language and preferences, and engaged in role‑play as an equal partner. Decisions were made jointly and adapted to his priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options, asked permission, and checked readiness, affirming Jason's right to choose his approach. The clinician avoided pressuring language and respected his low‑key preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited practical, instrumental motivation tied to protecting housing and classes and recalled prior coping, but did not probe deeper values or longer‑term reasons for change. Evocation was present but limited and largely procedural.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Jason as a collaborator and decision‑maker by offering choices and soliciting his input on wording and priorities. Language and actions consistently shared power rather than adopting an expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited concrete commitment language and steps (calling housing, drafting statements) and reinforced those intentions with practice. There were missed opportunities to deepen affective or value‑based change talk beyond pragmatic goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully rolled with defensiveness and minimizations, reflecting concerns and redirecting to problem‑solving without confrontation. The clinician used affirmations to reduce defensiveness but rarely employed double‑sided reflections to contrast sustain and change talk.
