# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard—avoided judgment, normalized difficulty, and affirmed the patient’s perspective while honoring his preferences. Warmth and tailored support were present throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing both content and feeling and mirroring ambivalence. Reflections built rapport and validated the patient’s exhaustion and safety concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on smoking cessation and medication/patch use, tying questions and reflections back to actionable next steps. The session remained goal-directed toward deciding to start the patch safely.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged the patient as an equal partner by repeatedly inviting input, negotiating options, and agreeing to perform tasks (coach application, one‑pager, 3‑day check). Demonstrated genuine teamwork and shared problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by offering choices, checking comfort, and affirming that the patient makes the final decision. No pressure was applied; the clinician facilitated informed decision‑making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient’s motivations and concrete reasons for change (fewer cravings, sitting through dinners) and translated them into a plan. Missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation through values exploration, scaling, or eliciting a verbal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the patient as the primary decision‑maker and collaborator, asking preference questions and tailoring plans to his needs. Power was shared throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and guided the patient to concrete steps (patch+gum, follow‑up). Could have further amplified commitment by using scaling, eliciting past successes, or summarizing and reinforcing the patient’s own motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and reframed it toward problem‑solving with concrete safety offers (plain‑talk side effects, same‑day contact, 3‑day check). Would be stronger with explicit double‑sided reflections and transitional statements that shift emphasis to change.
