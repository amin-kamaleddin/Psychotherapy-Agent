# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful tone and normalized Marcus’s perspective while setting limits without moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries captured Marcus’s ambivalence, though reflections tended toward pragmatic content rather than deeper emotional or identity-level meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session kept a clear focus on reducing blackout risk and avoiding trouble, moving from readiness assessment to concrete harm-reduction steps and rehearsal of implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly two-way: the clinician invited preferences, co-created a plan, and practiced strategies with Marcus while centering his priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly affirmed Marcus’s choice and control, asked permission before advice, and framed recommendations as options rather than imposing abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited reasons for change and confidence ratings, producing change talk, but could probe deeper into intrinsic motives and values to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative stance with permission and options offered, although a rapid delivery of multiple harm-reduction steps briefly shifted toward an expert stance before returning to collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully evoked and amplified Marcus’s change talk (why a 3, doable steps) and asked for priorities, but could more consistently sequence and deepen change talk toward stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and acknowledged both sides, redirecting toward planning and harm reduction, though more double-sided or strategic reflections could further diminish sustain talk.
