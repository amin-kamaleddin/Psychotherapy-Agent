### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing ambivalence and affirming Lukas’s values. Language avoided labeling and supported warmth and respect throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong perspective-taking with accurate reflections and probes that mapped onto Lukas’s concerns about teasing, team fit, and performance. Reflections and paraphrases showed deep and consistent understanding of his experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, tight focus on declining alcohol/handling teammates and consistently moved the conversation toward actionable steps. Questions and prompts repeatedly narrowed choices to concrete scripting and fallback plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative and egalitarian—co-authored lines with Lukas, offered suggestions and immediately handed them back for his input. The clinician repeatedly solicited wording that sounded like him and let Lukas shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering options, emphasizing choice, and avoiding pressure; planning framed as voluntary practice. Repeated invitations to pick what fits reinforced Lukas’s ownership of the decision.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Effectively elicited motives tied to team connection and performance and drew out a preferred outcome, but could have used more open questions to surface deeper values, long-term goals, and confidence. Additional elicitation of importance/confidence or past successes would strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear power-sharing and co-creation throughout, positioning Lukas as the expert and repeatedly inviting him to choose and refine wording. The clinician’s approach supported joint planning and client-led decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk via choice, co-scripting, and asking what would feel like a win, producing explicit change statements. Could deepen change talk further by probing personal reasons/values and eliciting confidence or commitment strength.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized Lukas’s pull to fit in, and redirected to options rather than arguing for change. Could strengthen softening by using double-sided reflections and proactively reframing sustain talk into reasons for change.
