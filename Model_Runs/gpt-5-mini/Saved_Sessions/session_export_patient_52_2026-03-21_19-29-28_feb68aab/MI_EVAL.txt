# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing feelings and affirming small efforts throughout the session. The clinician maintained warmth and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and acknowledged affect (guilt, nervousness), showing solid perspective-taking. Could deepen empathic listening by exploring the emotional nuances (e.g., guilt, meaning of "only time for myself") more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on reducing evening alcohol use with interventions tied directly to that goal. No drift into unrelated topics and concrete plans and timelines were established.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally a joint problem-solving approach with client choices and follow-up planning, but the clinician often provided many prescriptive tips. Slightly fewer directives and more elicitation of the client's ideas would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice, offered multiple options, and affirmed the client's decisions, reinforcing ownership of change. Avoided coercive language and respected the client's limits for simplicity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation (asking what felt easiest, one-week experiment) but much of the session offered clinician-driven solutions. Missed opportunities to draw out intrinsic motivation by asking about importance, confidence, or having the client verbalize reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session read as a collaborative co‑creation of a plan with invitations for input, yet frequent detailed instructions sometimes shifted the dynamic toward expert advice. More explicit invitations to co-design the plan in the client's words would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change language and secured a one‑week commitment, supporting practical steps. Did not systematically amplify spontaneous change talk or probe importance/confidence to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and validated stressors that supported drinking while offering alternatives, which prevented reinforcement of sustain talk. Could more proactively use double‑sided reflections and selective amplification to further diminish sustain talk.
