# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently warm and nonjudgmental, normalized Julian’s experience, and validated practical constraints (transit, evenings). They offered help rather than criticism, supporting unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening (checked constraints, reflected stress) and tailored suggestions to Julian’s situation. Could deepen empathy by exploring underlying anxiety or emotional concerns rather than moving quickly to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remained tightly focused on the target behavior (completing court-mandated hours) with interventions directly tied to logistics and next steps. The clinician consistently moved the client toward concrete actions and contingency plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the clinician solicited Julian’s input at decision points and incorporated his preferences into the plan. The workflow framed the session as joint problem-solving with shared decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician offered meaningful choices, asked permission before acting, and honored Julian’s preferences (texts first, limited orgs, role‑play optional), reinforcing his sense of control. This consistently supported client autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited specific behavioral commitments but did little to draw out Julian’s internal reasons, values, or confidence about completing hours. More evocative questions and exploration of importance/confidence would strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Julian as an equal partner—asking permission, offering options, and co-creating the stepwise plan (texts → follow-up → calls). Power was shared and decisions reflected Julian’s choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician supported concrete, logistical commitments (texts, calendar blocks) but did not systematically elicit or amplify intrinsic reasons for change. Opportunities were missed to deepen desire, ability, reasons, or need language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively avoided reinforcing avoidance and offered lower-burden alternatives (text templates, emails, reserved orientations) that redirected toward action. Could add double-sided reflections and explicit empathic statements to better acknowledge and shift sustain talk.
