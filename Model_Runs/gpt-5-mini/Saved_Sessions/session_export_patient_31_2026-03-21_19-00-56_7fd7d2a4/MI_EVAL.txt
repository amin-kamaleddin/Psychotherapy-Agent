# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone and respectful responses that address practical concerns, but interaction leans toward problem‑solving expert mode with few individualized affirmations or normalizing reflections.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician tailors information and acknowledges concerns (sleep, gum, evening stress) but uses limited reflective listening or emotional exploration and misses chances to validate ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation tightly focused on smoking reduction and NRT strategies, steadily progressing to a concrete 2‑week plan with no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positions self as a helpful resource and asks brief questions, but much of the interaction is unilateral information delivery and power‑sharing is uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers multiple options and pragmatic framing that support choice, yet often provides instruction without explicitly checking preferences or asking permission before advising.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicits the patient's own motivations or values; most motivation arises from patient statements rather than clinician‑led evocative questioning or amplification.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (asking about a plan, health checks) but overall stance is expert/informational and the plan is not consistently co‑created with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits a concrete step and elicits some tentative change talk, but relies mainly on information provision and seldom uses open questions or reflections to draw out and amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids direct confrontation and offers harm‑reduction options which reduce resistance, but misses strategic techniques (double‑sided reflections, normalization) to actively soften sustain talk.
