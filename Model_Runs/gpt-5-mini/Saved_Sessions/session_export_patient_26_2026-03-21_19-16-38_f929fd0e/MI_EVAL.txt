# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental language, frequent affirmations, and validation of the patient's efforts. No disapproving or labeling statements were used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good understanding and some reflective listening (acknowledging burden and nervousness) but often moves quickly to problem-solving and misses deeper affective reflections. Greater paraphrase and emotional labeling would strengthen empathic resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors (childcare and coping with stress/cravings) and consistently returns to concrete, actionable next steps. Uses stepwise planning, scripts, and follow-up prompts to keep the session goal-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient as a partner by co-creating messages and checklists, inviting edits, and using language that frames the clinician as a helper. The patient’s preferences direct the flow and next actions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control through offering options, asking permission, and honoring the patient's decisions (e.g., preferring real call before role-play). Avoids pressure and emphasizes the patient's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some patient-driven planning for childcare but does not sufficiently draw out intrinsic motivation or reasons for health behavior change like smoking/drinking. Missed opportunities to amplify successes and explore values, confidence, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a genuine partnership by iteratively incorporating the patient's edits, soliciting preferences, and sharing power over decisions. The clinician consistently treats the patient as the expert on her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports some preparatory change talk and affirms small successes but does not probe or amplify reasons, desire, ability, or commitment for reducing smoking/drinking. Relies more on providing solutions than eliciting evoked change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to practical, nonjudgmental problem-solving for barriers like finances and distrust. Could strengthen impact by using more double-sided reflections or eliciting the patient to weigh pros and cons aloud.
