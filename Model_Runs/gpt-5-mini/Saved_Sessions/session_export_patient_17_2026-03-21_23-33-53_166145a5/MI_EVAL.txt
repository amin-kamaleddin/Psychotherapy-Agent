# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard—normalizes struggle, avoids blame, and affirms the patient’s efforts and constraints throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective‑taking, repeatedly paraphrasing both facts and emotions in the patient's own language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete first steps (no smoking inside, gum, delay first cigarette) and avoids topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative—elicits preferences, co‑constructs the plan, and offers practical supports (doctor message, joint check‑ins) that share decision‑making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and control by offering options, asking permission before giving information, and inviting the patient to choose what to try first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation tied to values (protecting daughter) and asks evocative questions, but sometimes moves quickly into technical advice rather than deepening the patient’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as a team effort, checks preferences, and repeatedly invites the patient's input to co‑create a realistic plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change talk with options and goal‑setting, but at times shifts to solution‑giving before fully amplifying or exploring the patient's own reasons and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes fear and skepticism and gently redirects toward small steps, though could use more double‑sided reflections and deeper exploration of sustain concerns before offering facts.
