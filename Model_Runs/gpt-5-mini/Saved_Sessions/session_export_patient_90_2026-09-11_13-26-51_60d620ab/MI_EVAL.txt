# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing Marcus’s ambivalence and validating constraints while affirming his choices rather than pressuring him.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that named overwhelm and practical concerns, but the clinician missed some opportunities to probe deeper into Marcus’s emotional attachment and affective distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, goal-directed focus on asthma adherence with purposeful sequencing from options to implementation and monitoring without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in genuine two-way problem solving by soliciting Marcus’s ideas, building on them, and checking feasibility so the plan was co-constructed rather than expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Marcus’s sense of choice by offering options, asking his preferences, and respecting his decisions without using pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations and reasons for change and asked about meaningful differences, but could have amplified internal motivation further using scaling, values, or stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Marcus’s input and positioned him as the expert on what would fit his routine, resulting in a collaboratively developed, feasible plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively evoked and reinforced change language, helped generate concrete, measurable steps (alarms, visible inhaler, tracking) and summarized commitments to strengthen intent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided amplifying sustain talk by reframing solutions into acceptable compromises and acknowledging financial concerns while offering pragmatic, non-shaming alternatives.
