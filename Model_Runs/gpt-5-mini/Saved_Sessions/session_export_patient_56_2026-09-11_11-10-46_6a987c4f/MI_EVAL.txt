# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizes the client's worries, and uses nonjudgmental language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent accurate reflections and clear attunement to values and concerns, but missed some opportunities to deepen emotional nuance (e.g., guilt, fear of disappointing family).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remains tightly focused on the agreed target (small predictable self-care) with clear steps, troubleshooting, and minimal drift toward other topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative and co-creative, continually soliciting the client's preferences and offering expertise only in response to requests.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently preserves client choice by offering multiple options, asking permission, and avoiding pressure, which supports the client's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully draws out the client's motivations and concrete plans, but at times narrows responses with preset options rather than open-ended prompts and could better link plans to stated values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes shared work, defers to the client's expertise, and offers choices about how to proceed, fostering a strong partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete change talk and planning (e.g., one-week trial, role‑play) but could more consistently amplify, summarize, and quantify commitment to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain concerns and redirects to practical solutions, though using double-sided reflections could more gently validate both sides while biasing toward change.
