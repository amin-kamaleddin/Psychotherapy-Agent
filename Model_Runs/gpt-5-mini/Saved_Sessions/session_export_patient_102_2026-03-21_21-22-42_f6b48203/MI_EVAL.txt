# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful—normalized awkwardness, asked permission before sensitive topics, and validated ambivalence and pragmatic needs.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that conveyed understanding, though reflections sometimes remained at the behavioral level rather than probing deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related risk reduction and linked strategies directly to Milan's goals (college, family, faith).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as joint problem-solving—clinician invited Milan to co-create plans, rehearse options, and repeatedly deferred to his preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported autonomy by asking permission, offering multiple options, framing suggestions as choices, and honoring Milan's limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivations and resources with open questions and a readiness ruler, but could have drawn out more personal 'why' statements and self-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—shared power, asked which options felt doable, and let Milan shape the plan, consistent with textbook MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited desire, reasons, and ability language and used readiness/confidence scaling, yet could have amplified and summarized change talk more and sought firmer commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and validated sustain talk while redirecting to risk-reduction, but did not consistently use double-sided reflections to explicitly name both sides of ambivalence.
