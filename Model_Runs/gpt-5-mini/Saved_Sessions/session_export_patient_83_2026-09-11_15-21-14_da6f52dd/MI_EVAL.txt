# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; named ambivalence, offered options, and normalized uncertainty to honor the patient's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing practical concerns; some reflections felt formulaic and missed chances to explicitly validate frustration or explore emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on initiating the statin with a safety-focused short trial and repeatedly returned to concrete, goal-relevant elements.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine collaborative exchange—solicited input before explaining, summarized choices, and co-constructed the trial plan with frequent tailoring to patient preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported patient choice by offering multiple options and asking which to do first, reinforcing that the patient controls the decision without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical motivations and planning language effectively, but did not sufficiently draw out personal reasons, values, or confidence to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the interaction as joint decision-making, inviting the patient to choose order and content and summarizing her chosen plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and invited planning, yet could have probed further to translate pragmatic statements into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and avoided arguing or pressuring, but would benefit from more double-sided reflections to validate caution while gently contrasting reasons to try the medication.
