# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing Rowan’s limits (e.g., accepting 'no promises') and never shaming. Resistance was treated as understandable rather than wrong.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, frequent reflections that captured Rowan’s practical constraints and stance, helping him open to planning. Missed opportunities to explore underlying emotions (frustration/overwhelm) and use deeper, feeling+meaning reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on charging behavior and small, testable steps throughout the session. Questions, reflections, and summaries all tied back to practical solutions for charging.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the exchange as joint problem-solving with inclusive, tentative language and built on Rowan’s own ideas. The plan was co-created rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Rowan’s choice by offering options, asking his preference, and accepting limited commitment without pressure. Decision-making and ownership remained with Rowan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Rowan’s solutions and elicited an internally generated experiment (tape charger, pair with coffee). Could have probed deeper reasons for monitoring and used confidence/importance rulers to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed decision-making as Rowan’s and solicited his ideas, integrating his suggestions into the plan. Language and approach reflected genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, producing concrete commitment language and a specific experiment. Could have amplified tentative 'I’ll try' language and asked scaling questions to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing but allowed it to recur and sometimes echoed resistant language with similar questions. More active techniques (double-sided reflections, shifting quickly to change reasons) would better soften sustain talk.
