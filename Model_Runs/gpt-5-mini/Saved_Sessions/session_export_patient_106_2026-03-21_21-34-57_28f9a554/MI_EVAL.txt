# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, normalizing Natalia’s context and affirming realistic goals (house smoke-free + gum). The clinician validates choices throughout and avoids shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures burden and ambivalence. Missed a few opportunities for deeper, affect-focused reflections (e.g., guilt or fear) to deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking and specific, achievable first steps, progressing logically from readiness to concrete implementation (gum dose, check-ins, scripts). The session shows a strong forward trajectory toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative joint problem-solving: co-creates texts, rehearses wording, negotiates details, and builds plans shaped by Natalia’s preferences. Power-sharing and co-construction are evident throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, using a readiness ruler, asking permission, and honoring limits (e.g., not pushing the patch). The clinician avoids pressure and reinforces Natalia’s agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out motivation with readiness measures and linking values (protecting her son) to behavior change. Could strengthen evocation by more targeted value-clarifying questions and eliciting past successes to build internal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Top-tier partnership: asks permission, invites choices, and positions Natalia as the expert while offering collaborative supports. The clinician consistently shares power in planning and decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk through readiness questions and concrete options resulting in specific change statements. Would benefit from amplifying and summarizing the patient’s change language to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and effectively redirects toward harm-reduction and doable steps. There were missed opportunities for double-sided reflections that explicitly acknowledge both sides of ambivalence to further diffuse sustain talk.
