# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, normalizing difficulty and validating Ryan's intentions and values to create a warm, accepting atmosphere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate paraphrases and perspective-taking but stays mostly at content level; could deepen emotional reflections to better capture ambivalence and shame/fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on preventing alcohol-related incidents and harm-reduction steps while balancing guidance with elicitation of Ryan's perspective.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the session as a joint enterprise—invites co-creation of plans, role-play, and relies on Ryan's ideas (Ethan, alarm, prepaid ride) to shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirms Ryan's choice and control by offering multiple options, asking what feels doable, and using scaling to let him self-rate confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively links change to Ryan's values and elicits personal reasons and plans, but could more explicitly deepen discrepancy and amplify his change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and invites Ryan to choose specifics, though occasionally offers concrete wording that subtly shifts power back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and reinforces desire, ability, reasons, and commitment language using options, confidence rulers, rehearsal, and specific action requests.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids confronting or reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward protection and planning, but uses few explicit double-sided reflections to strategicaly acknowledge pros and costs.
