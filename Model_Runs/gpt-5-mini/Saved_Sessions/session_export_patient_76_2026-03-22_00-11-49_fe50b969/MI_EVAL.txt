# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing the client’s struggles and affirming efforts (e.g., “you’re doing your best…”), creating a warm, accepting atmosphere throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated frequent, accurate empathic reflections and complex summaries that captured both concrete details and emotional tone, validating the client’s exhaustion, fear, and pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear and consistent focus on reducing immediate self-harm risk and creating safer coping alternatives, repeatedly returning to a concrete safety plan and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was strongly collaborative, framed as joint problem‑solving with client-generated options and negotiated steps (towel, playlist, timer), honoring the client’s limits and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before interventions, and explicitly reinforcing the client’s control over decisions without coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited practical resources and small motives for change (alternatives and commitments), but did less to draw out deeper personal reasons, values, or confidence statements to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely partnered with the client by asking preference, co-creating the plan, and inviting the client to lead decisions, demonstrating that expertise resides with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and shaped actionable change talk with specific alternatives and implementation details that led to commitments, but could more consistently amplify and selectively reflect emerging change language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk thoughtfully but sometimes reflected it in ways that risked reinforcing it; more strategic moves (double‑sided reflections, selective amplification of change language) would better shift momentum.
