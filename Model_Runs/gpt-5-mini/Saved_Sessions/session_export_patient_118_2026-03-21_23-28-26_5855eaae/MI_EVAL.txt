# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through validation, reframing, and affirmations, avoiding blame and honoring boundaries throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong empathic listening with accurate content and feeling reflections and by naming mixed feelings, which maintained rapport with a defensive client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on the target behavior (smoking) while respecting the patient's agenda, moving logically to a concrete, time-limited implementation plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as a joint effort by soliciting preferences and co‑constructing the plan from the patient's own suggestions, keeping the patient in the driver’s seat.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively reinforced the patient's control and choices, honored prior negative experiences with medication, offered options, and avoided pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete motivations and existing strategies and linked them to outcomes, but could probe deeper into intrinsic motives or broader values to strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly conveyed a partnership stance by repeatedly inviting the patient's preferences, asking permission implicitly, and jointly deciding check‑in logistics and strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with evocative questions and reflections, but missed opportunities to amplify, request elaboration, and assess confidence/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and normalized resistance while reframing caution positively, though could more actively use double‑sided reflections to highlight ambivalence and tip toward change.
