# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating language that normalizes ambivalence and highlights strengths, reducing shame and creating safety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and perspective-taking are used, but the clinician often moves quickly into problem-solving instead of lingering to deepen emotional exploration (e.g., shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on staying sober with every question and intervention tied to concrete relapse-prevention steps and immediate goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly a team effort: the clinician elicits preferences, offers options after asking, and jointly generates the plan while preserving the client's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice and control by offering options, checking preferences, and framing suggestions as offers, then honoring Elias’s selected plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations (kids, shame avoidance) and uses scaling to draw out reasons, but could ask deeper, value-focused questions to strengthen intrinsic commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance throughout, inviting co-creation of solutions, checking readiness, and treating Elias as the decision-maker and expert on his experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and affirms change talk using scaling and linking actions to values, though there were missed opportunities to amplify brief change statements into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is recognized and logistical barriers are addressed, but the clinician only moderately softens sustain talk and rarely uses double-sided reflections or deeper reframing.
