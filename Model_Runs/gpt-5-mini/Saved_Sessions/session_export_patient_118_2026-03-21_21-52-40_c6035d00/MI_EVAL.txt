# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, honoring the patient’s preference not to discuss quitting and avoiding blame or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate, specific reflections and summaries that matched the patient’s guarded, practical stance and de-escalated resistance, keeping him engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session appropriately focused on dental treatment, pain, and cost and corrected early misalignment after an initial quitting question, with a small loss for that opening move.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicited the patient’s preferences and incorporated them into a shared plan (sedation, pricing, contacting cardiology), treating him as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed choice, asked permission before offering information or contacting others, and presented options rather than directives, strengthening the patient’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some attempts to elicit motivation (asking about quitting and past attempts, linking to values) but generally honored the boundary rather than using brief, permission-based elicitation to draw out deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positioned the patient as an active collaborator by asking permission, honoring selections, and co-constructing decisions and the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened the door to change and linked smoking to outcomes the patient cares about, but missed opportunities to systematically evoke, amplify, or explore emergent change language (e.g., follow-up elicitations after small commitments).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used reflective summaries and reframing toward patient priorities to defuse sustain talk; could further benefit from strategic techniques like double-sided reflections to shift sustain talk more explicitly.
