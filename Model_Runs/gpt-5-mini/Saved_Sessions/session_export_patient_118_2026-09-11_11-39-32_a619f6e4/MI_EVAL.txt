# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, validating both sides of ambivalence and avoiding judgment. The clinician accepts reluctance without pressuring and honors the patient's concerns about medication side effects.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening with accurate reflections and summaries that match the patient's tone. These reflections track the patient closely and reduce adversarial pushback.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction and progressively narrows to a specific, manageable target (not smoking in the car). Questions and planning consistently move the conversation toward a concrete experiment without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative: the clinician checks preferences, solicits wording/timing, and positions themselves as a helper rather than an authority. The plan is co‑created and shaped by the patient's input at every step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by offering multiple options and accepting the patient's reluctance to promise change. The clinician invites choice and frames the patient as the decision‑maker while encouraging a small trial.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits concrete steps and supports from the patient, producing actionable change talk (e.g., car smoke‑free). Evocation could be strengthened by probing values, using scaling, and amplifying the patient's own motivations to build commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a genuine partnership by inviting input and co‑designing a realistic plan that fits the patient's context. The clinician repeatedly invites the patient's views and shares power in decision‑making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and shapes change talk that leads to a specific, doable experiment (not smoking in the car). Missed opportunities remain to more strongly amplify and affirm change statements or to elicit importance/confidence ratings to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and uses double‑sided reflections and neutral summaries to acknowledge sustain talk while steering toward options. Could more actively diminish sustain talk by highlighting discrepancies between short‑term comforts and longer‑term costs or using amplified reflections.
