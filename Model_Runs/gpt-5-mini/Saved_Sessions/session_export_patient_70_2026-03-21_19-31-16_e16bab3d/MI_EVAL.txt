# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician communicates respect and a nonjudgmental stance with warmth and normalization. To reach a 5 they could have offered more individualized affirmations of Lars's strengths and commitments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows solid perspective-taking via summaries and normalization of nervousness, indicating understanding of Lars's situation. Deeper, affect-focused reflections (e.g., about guilt, fear, or thrill-seeking) were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on avoiding reoffending, peer/alcohol pressure, forms, and job steps and ties interventions to those goals. Consistently steers toward actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a partnership: co-creates scripts, adapts language, and checks preferences. Some sections felt more clinician-driven and could have solicited more of Lars's own solution ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice by offering options and asking which approach Lars wants to try, avoiding pressure. Language and offers consistently reinforced Lars's control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates space for choices and confirms goals but relies more on clinician-provided solutions than on drawing out Lars's internal motives or past successes. More open, values- and confidence-eliciting questions would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats Lars as a collaborator through option-choice and acceptance of his phrasing, showing good power-sharing. Could improve by explicitly inviting his expertise and co-creating the plan elements step-by-step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-oriented statements and affirms plans, but generally moves quickly to solutions without amplifying Lars's reasons, confidence, or importance statements. Strategic reflections and scaling questions would bolster change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers practical alternatives and delay strategies. Missed opportunities to explicitly reflect ambivalence or use double-sided reflections to soften sustain language.
