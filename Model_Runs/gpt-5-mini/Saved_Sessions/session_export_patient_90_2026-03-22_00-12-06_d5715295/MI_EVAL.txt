# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard and respect, validated Marcus's priorities (especially keeping the dog), and avoided judgment throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, specific reflections captured both content and feeling (overwhelm, attachment to the dog), helping Marcus feel heard and prompting further disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained clear focus on asthma-management behaviors, repeatedly linking conversation to concrete actions like controller use, alarms, and symptom tracking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was structured as joint problem‑solving with repeated invitations for Marcus's input and co-construction of the plan (checklist, alarms, text template).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician emphasized choice and pace, offered options rather than directives, and reinforced Marcus's ownership of decisions (two‑week trial, option selection).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician elicited Marcus's own motivations and reasons for change with evocative questions about past success and what matters, and then built on Marcus's change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language and actions positioned the clinician as a helper rather than an expert, with Marcus's ideas consistently shaping the plan and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician skillfully elicited and amplified change talk by asking about past successes, asking preference‑based questions, and securing commitment to a two‑week trial.
