# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance

**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone; validated Tyler's concerns, accepted ambivalence, and accommodated his wording preferences to build rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective listening that acknowledged stress and ambivalence, but could use more complex reflections to deepen emotional nuance (e.g., shame, fun vs. consequences).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on the drinking incident, sanctions, and concrete next steps throughout the session with a logical progression of tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: co-developed emails, hearing statement, and supporting documents, asked permission before templates, and invited Tyler's edits and decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Tyler's choice by offering options, respecting reluctance to promise abstinence, and leaving final wording and decisions to him.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Elicited some motivation (protecting academics, interest in diversion) but leaned toward solution delivery rather than drawing out Tyler's values, reasons, or confidence with open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invited agenda-setting, co-creating materials and accommodating edits, though occasional directive template provision reduced shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted some actionable change talk but moved quickly into problem-solving without systematically eliciting or amplifying Tyler's own reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
