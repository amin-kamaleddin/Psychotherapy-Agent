# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing frustration and acknowledging effort throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately naming frustration and minimizing pressure; could use deeper, affect-focused or more complex reflections to elicit more emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, client-centered focus on a specific target behavior (submit one application) and sequences practical steps (checklist, timer, check-back) without being directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages the patient as an equal partner—offers options, co-creates the plan, and tailors involvement to the patient's preferences throughout the interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice and control by offering alternatives, emphasizing no long-term commitments, and asking permission before intervening or pasting wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits pragmatic, actionable motivations but relies more on offering solutions than drawing out deeper personal values or the patient's own language about reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates a strong partnership stance: shares power, solicits the patient's input on structure and wording, and lets the patient direct pace and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for change talk and elicits a concrete commitment (apply today), but sometimes supplies reasons and misses chances to have the patient elaborate on motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing and redirects sustain talk with reflections and reframes, though could use more double-sided reflections and strategic elicitation to further reduce sustain talk.
