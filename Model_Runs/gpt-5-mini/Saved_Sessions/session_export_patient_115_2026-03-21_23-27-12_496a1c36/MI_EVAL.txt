# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with normalizing statements and gratitude, creating a safe, nonjudgmental atmosphere that supports disclosure and engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and reflections that validate ambivalence and link values to outcomes, though some reflections are broad or repetitive rather than deepening emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on asthma-related behaviors and moves session logically from values to past successes to a concrete, measurable plan and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—offers options, asks the patient to choose among them, and co-creates a realistic plan rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports the patient's pace and decision-making by framing options, inviting choices, and reinforcing ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and links behavior to valued outcomes, but probes less for confidence, commitment strength, and anticipated barriers that would deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reflects an equal partnership with inclusive language and shared problem-solving; the clinician respects the patient's expertise about his life and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk, turning general desires into specific, actionable steps and securing concrete commitments (e.g., nicotine gum, smoke-free shifts).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without judgment and pivots to incremental goals effectively, though using double-sided reflections more often could better resolve ambivalence.
