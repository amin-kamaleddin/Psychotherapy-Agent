# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, supportive tone, affirmed the patient's priorities, and normalized difficulty; could briefly name ambivalence more explicitly to deepen unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong moment-to-moment reflections and a safety check validated the patient's stress and priorities; missed some opportunities to deepen empathic exploration of hesitancy and the tentative emotional language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on risky drinking and academic/legal consequences with concrete, goal-directed steps and agreed next actions throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like joint problem-solving—co-creating the plan and repeatedly revising the conduct-office message based on the patient's input showed genuine collaboration and flexibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently emphasized choice, offered options (role‑play vs plan), and framed suggestions as optional, supporting the patient's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change language was elicited, but the clinician relied more on information and solutions than on evocative questions to draw out the patient's values, reasons, confidence, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent invitations for input and incorporation of the patient's wording fostered partnership, though the clinician occasionally shifted into a more directive expert role with unsolicited step-by-step instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited change-oriented statements and offered practical plans, but did not consistently use questions or reflections to amplify tentative change talk or explore importance and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Harm-reduction responses avoided reinforcing sustain talk, but the clinician seldom used MI-specific strategies (double-sided reflections or cost/benefit reflections) to acknowledge and soften the patient's sustain talk about social norms.
