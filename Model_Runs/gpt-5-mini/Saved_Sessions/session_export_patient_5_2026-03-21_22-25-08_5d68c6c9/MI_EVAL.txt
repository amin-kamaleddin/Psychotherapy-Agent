# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and positive regard, normalized the patient's caution, and validated her decision-making without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid perspective-taking and accurate reflections of concerns, but emotional reflections were often logistical rather than explicitly naming feelings like worry or relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on safe medication initiation with concrete steps (washout, low start dose, EKG, monitoring) and steered the conversation toward a signed safety plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the encounter as joint problem-solving, co-created the safety bundle, and repeatedly invited the patient to shape wording and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's right to choose, offered options rather than directives, and honored her preferences (e.g., signature requirement, wording choice).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited practical motivations and readiness by asking what would make her feel safe, but could more strongly draw out personal values and amplify commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and shared decision-making, treating the patient as an equal partner in creating the safety plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change language about trying a small, monitored trial through targeted questions, but could amplify and summarize those statements to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing reasons against change, validated ambivalence, and redirected concerns into actionable safety planning rather than debating hesitations.
