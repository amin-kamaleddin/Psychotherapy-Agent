# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the client's preferences throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate understanding via reflective listening and paraphrase, capturing both practical benefits and emotional attachment to the ritual and using reflections to anchor the plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior and guided the conversation to a specific, measurable experiment with concrete monitoring and troubleshooting plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—solicited preferences at multiple points, adjusted the plan to fit the client's choices, and genuinely shared power in co‑constructing the change strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported client choice by offering options, respecting reversible decisions, and explicitly deferring to the client's control over check‑ins and follow‑up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the client's own reasons and criteria for change, using those internal motivations and practical knowledge to shape the experiment rather than supplying external reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint endeavor, repeatedly invited the client's input, and followed the client's lead in deciding wording, timing, and supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk by planning a small experiment and asking outcome signals, but missed chances to amplify change language, ask importance/confidence, and elicit a stronger commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and balanced sustain talk with double‑sided reflections, yet could more actively pivot away from sustain talk by emphasizing costs of continued behavior and using strategic summaries to increase discrepancy.
