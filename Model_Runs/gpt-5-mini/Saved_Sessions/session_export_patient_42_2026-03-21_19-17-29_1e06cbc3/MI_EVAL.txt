# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized Elise’s ambivalence, avoiding blame and validating social concerns throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate paraphrases and clarifying questions, but missed opportunities for deeper empathic reflections exploring fears of loneliness or the emotional meaning of belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation while preserving social connection and steered the conversation toward practical strategies and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as a joint effort by asking preferences and inviting role-play, but frequently supplied prescriptive scripts instead of co-creating language with Elise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly reinforced Elise’s sense of choice and agency by offering options, asking permission to role-play, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-related statements but relied more on offering solutions than drawing out Elise’s own values, reasons, past successes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance with invitations and checks for preference, yet tended to fill the space with clinician-generated scripts rather than eliciting Elise’s preferred wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Produced modest change talk (willingness to try scripts) but did not sufficiently amplify Elise’s desire, ability, reasons, or commitment through evocative questioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk and offered practical alternatives to preserve social connection, though more use of MI-specific techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) could better soften sustain talk.
