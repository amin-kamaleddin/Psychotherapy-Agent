# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with validating reflections, normalization, and nonjudgmental language while acknowledging supports and constraints.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate naming of stressors and worries, though tended toward concise, medical/problem‑solving frames rather than deeper emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on smoking cessation with clear actionable steps (med options, safety screening, dosing, quit date, and follow‑up schedule).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively sought patient input at each decision point, used inclusive language, and integrated the patient's supports and preferences into a negotiated plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Routinely supported choice by offering options, asking preferences for medications and check‑ins, and avoiding coercive language while proceeding only with patient agreement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited several expressions of motivation (why now, success, quit date, confidence) but relied partly on informational content and could have used more evocative open questions linking values and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as collaborative throughout, offering choices (A/B), soliciting preferences (pharmacy, contact method), and involving the spouse as requested.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created many opportunities for change talk and reinforced motivations (quit date, confidence rating), but missed some chances to more deliberately elicit and amplify specific change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk about stress and social cues but often pivoted quickly to medical/logistical solutions rather than exploring or using double‑sided reflections to soften it.
