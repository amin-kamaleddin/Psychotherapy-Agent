# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing feelings and affirming strengths throughout the interaction. No instances of judgment or minimization were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with timely summaries and emotion acknowledgement. Could deepen emotional reflections and avoid moving too quickly into information provision to draw out affect more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on alcohol use and contraceptive reliability with action-oriented planning and options. Occasional information-heavy segments risked a didactic tone and the session lacked a final patient-chosen, time-framed next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine collaboration by asking permission, following the patient’s agenda, and co-constructing scripts and backup plans. The back-and-forth and rehearsal offers demonstrated strong shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, invited preferences, and avoided pressuring recommendations while emphasizing reversible options. Language consistently supported the patient’s sense of ownership over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited practical reasons and motivations (e.g., avoiding family patterns, raising confidence) and asked for strategies to increase readiness. Could further strengthen by asking about past successes and selectively reflecting statements of desire to amplify commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed a collaborative stance and recognized the patient’s expertise while offering options and rehearsal. At times the clinician supplied expert information rather than co-constructing and could invite the patient to lead more agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk, asking for concrete steps to raise confidence and supporting patient-generated strategies. Missed opportunities to amplify change language, reflect commitment statements, and follow up with scaling questions to build ability and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward practical supports and planning, using implicit double-sided framing. Could improve by using more explicit double-sided reflections and strategic reflections that validate sustain talk while guiding toward change.
