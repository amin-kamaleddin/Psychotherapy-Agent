# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental regard, normalizing the patient’s constraints and honoring preferences throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking with accurate reflections about scheduling and practical barriers, but missed opportunities to probe deeper emotions or the patient’s minimization of stress/alcohol.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on hypertension self-management and progressed logically from options to commitment to implementation details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as genuine two-person problem-solving: the clinician elicited preferences, offered tailored tools, and followed the patient’s lead rather than adopting an authoritarian stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly honored patient choice by offering options, asking which approach was most doable, and negotiating help rather than prescribing a single solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited practical reasons and commitment for monitoring BP, but elicitation of deeper intrinsic motivation or values was limited and could be strengthened with importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as collaborative, using open choices and shared decision-making to co-create a realistic plan with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced concrete change talk and implementation details, though could more often amplify, summarize, and scale commitment and confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing but missed strategic opportunities to acknowledge and gently reframe the patient’s minimizations (e.g., double-sided reflections) to invite reconsideration.
