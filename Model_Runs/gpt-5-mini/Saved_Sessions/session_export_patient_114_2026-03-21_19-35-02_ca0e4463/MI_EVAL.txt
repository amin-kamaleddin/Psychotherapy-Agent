# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, with clear validation and normalization (e.g., opening support, validating stress, honoring wording edits). The clinician avoided shaming and honored the patient's preferences, reinforcing positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and brief accurate reflections that helped the patient disclose and feel understood. Reflections were often short and the clinician frequently shifted quickly into problem-solving rather than exploring emotional ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on the conduct response with concrete, prioritized action steps (advocate, evidence, draft response). However, the clinician did not develop a sustained exploration of alcohol use or longer-term change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑authored the email, invited edits, offered options, and frequently solicited the patient's preferences. Decisions were shaped by the patient's input rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's choice and control by offering options, asking which felt most helpful, and promptly implementing requested wording changes. The clinician emphasized the patient's ownership of decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited effort to elicit intrinsic motivation about changing drinking; the session focused mainly on procedural problem-solving. There were few targeted evocation strategies (no importance/confidence rulers, pros/cons, or selective amplification of change talk).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and shared decision making through menus of options and co‑creation of responses. To reach a top score, the clinician could have more explicitly framed the patient as the expert and co‑developed a longer‑term plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some invitations allowed expression of concern and readiness (e.g., asking about sending the email), but cultivation of change talk about alcohol was limited and largely procedural. The clinician did not systematically elicit or amplify reasons, importance, or confidence for reducing drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or reinforcing sustain talk and did not confront rationalizations, which prevented escalation. However, the clinician missed opportunities to use double‑sided reflections or reframing to actively pivot ambivalence toward change.
