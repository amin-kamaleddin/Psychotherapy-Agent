# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized Kenji’s ambivalence, avoiding judgment and validating priorities (job and health). The supportive tone felt genuine throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and reflective listening, capturing both content (job, health, unwinding) and ambivalent emotions (worry/relief). These reflections helped Kenji elaborate and move from avoidance to concrete ideas.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on reducing weeknight drinking and moved logically from awareness to a concrete, time-limited plan. The clinician guided the session toward specific goals without being controlling.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and invited Kenji’s input at each decision point, co-constructing the plan from Kenji’s own suggestions. Partnership and negotiation were evident throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Kenji’s sense of choice by offering options, asking what felt doable, and accepting uncertainty. The clinician avoided pressure and emphasized that the plan was Kenji’s to adopt and try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited specific reasons for change and concrete ideas (no weeknight drinks, substitutes, marker) but relied on clinician prompts more than deep spontaneous motivation. Could have probed values, confidence/importance, and past successes to strengthen intrinsic motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated Kenji as the expert, inviting choices like which change felt most doable and negotiating supports together. Power-sharing and mutual decision-making were clear throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk that produced concrete strategies and a one-week marker, but missed opportunities to amplify commitment language or elicit confidence/importance ratings. Summaries or targeted reflections could have consolidated commitment more strongly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk neutrally and redirected attention toward change with balanced reflections and planning, avoiding arguments. Could have used more double-sided reflections or explicit exploration of costs of the status quo to further weaken sustain language.
