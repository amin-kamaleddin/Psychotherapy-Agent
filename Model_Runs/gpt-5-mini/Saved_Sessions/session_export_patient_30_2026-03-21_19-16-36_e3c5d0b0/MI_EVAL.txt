# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalized concerns, and accepted the patient's decision to delay without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and tailored practical supports, but focused more on problem‑solving than deeper emotional exploration of the patient's worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the decision about cholesterol medication and the patient's muscle‑weakness concerns, linking each intervention to practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as joint problem‑solving, co‑created materials, solicited preferences, and positioned the patient as an active partner in decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's sense of choice and control, avoided directives, and offered options and tools to enable an informed decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and some invitations to discuss values but did not consistently elicit or deepen the patient's intrinsic motivations or use evocative questions to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fostered shared decision‑making, invited the patient's input on next steps, and respected their expertise about tolerances and daily functioning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provided practical supports and options but did not sufficiently elicit the patient's own reasons or confidence for change; change talk remained limited and was not consistently amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and used harm‑reduction strategies (low‑dose monitored trial, labs, pharmacist review) to redirect toward safer steps; could add more double‑sided reflections to further shift resistance.
