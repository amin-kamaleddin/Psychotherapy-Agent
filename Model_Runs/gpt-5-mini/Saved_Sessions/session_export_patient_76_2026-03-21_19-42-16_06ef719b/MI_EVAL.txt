# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, warm, and supportive stance with repeated validations and normalization of distress, demonstrating clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong empathic listening and accurate reflections matched the patient's somatic and emotional experience, checked safety compassionately, and helped the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed sharply focused on self-harm and immediate risk reduction with concrete stepwise safety planning, regular urge ratings, and no drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed a brief safety plan, invited choices, and adjusted to the patient's refusals, though the clinician led much of the pacing and provided specific instructions appropriate to the crisis.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options were repeatedly offered and patient choices were respected (e.g., refusing to move the razor), but several directive instructions reduced the felt autonomy when safety allowed for more collaborative phrasing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited immediate behavioral commitments (e.g., 10-minute delay, ice) but did little to draw out internal motivations, values, or past successes that would deepen change talk and sustain motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative stance with invitations and permission-seeking and shared planning, yet occasional directive language (start now, do X cycles) lessened the degree to which the patient's ideas drove the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective efforts produced small action language and commitments, but the clinician missed opportunities to ask open questions about reasons for change, pros/cons, and importance/confidence to amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and focused on alternatives, but rarely used strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring the patient's ambivalence) to soften sustain talk and highlight discrepancy.
