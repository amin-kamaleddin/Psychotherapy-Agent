# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental support, normalized ambivalence, and offered concrete help without blaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and reflective listening that kept Mateo engaged, but could use more complex reflections to explore emotions like 'feeling stuck' and resentment about mandated sessions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on finding work/attending an interview and linked each intervention to the behavioral target, producing a concrete action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was genuinely collaborative—clinician invited choices, practiced role‑play with Mateo, and involved social supports (cousin text) to share responsibility for follow‑through.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed Mateo's choice, offered options (resume vs script, role‑play timing), avoided pressure, and preserved his sense of control throughout planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Mateo’s motivations and specific plans (small goal, scripts, role‑play), but could probe deeper into intrinsic reasons and elicit stronger commitment language (e.g., importance/confidence ratings).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered power‑sharing and treated Mateo as the primary agent of change by offering options and co‑creating the plan (call time, check‑ins, resume edits).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and built on change talk with concrete planning and reinforcement (scripts, role‑play, supports), but missed opportunities to further amplify desire and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and redirected toward action, but did not consistently use double‑sided reflections or reframing to acknowledge barriers while reducing their pull.
