# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating—opens with permission, normalizes waiting, and affirms the patient's priorities, modeling unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate summaries that capture both concerns and priorities; could probe deeper into emotional meaning and fears to further deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation tightly focused on the medication decision, risks/benefits, and concrete next steps, steering toward actionable items like a checklist and doctor questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co‑constructed—offers options then follows the patient's preferences, adapts materials, and repeatedly solicits what the patient wants.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice through permission‑seeking, offering multiple options and formats, and explicit statements that it is okay to wait, reinforcing patient ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some evocation (open questions, readiness ruler, choice of next steps) but misses opportunities to draw out internal motives, values, and discrepancy to build stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong shared decision‑making stance—asks permission, offers options, invites patient choice, and positions the patient as the expert on their priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts (readiness ruler, concrete next steps) and brief patient statements about bringing questions, but the clinician does not consistently amplify or expand change talk with strategic reflections or evocative follow‑ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes sustain talk and provides factual risk/management info, but uses few double‑sided reflections, reframes, or discrepancy highlights to gently move ambivalence toward change.
