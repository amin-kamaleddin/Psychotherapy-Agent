# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, using validating language and a warm tone that reduced threat and normalized Ryan’s concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking were present, but the clinician missed opportunities for deeper, emotion-focused or complex reflections to fully explore ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use and the conduct meeting with clear, concrete goals and no drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt collaborative with frequent invitations for Ryan’s preferences and joint development of tools (script, log, refusal lines), reinforcing an equal-partners stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offered explicit choices, asked which option to pursue, and framed plans as negotiable, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit motivation were made (asking openness to change), but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than drawing out Ryan’s intrinsic reasons or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed that Ryan’s input mattered and invited choices, though the interaction became somewhat prescriptive when specific solutions were offered rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change statements and offered practical options, but moved quickly into expert solutions instead of strategically deepening or amplifying Ryan’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided confrontation and used harm-reduction strategies, but did not consistently use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to pivot sustain talk toward change talk.
