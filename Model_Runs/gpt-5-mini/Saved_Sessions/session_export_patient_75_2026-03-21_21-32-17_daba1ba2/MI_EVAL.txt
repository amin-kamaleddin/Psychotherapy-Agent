# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful (e.g., opening line and normalizing language) which conveys unconditional positive regard and supports the patient.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, empathic reflections that capture anxiety and ambivalence, though occasional deeper feeling-laden or complex reflections would strengthen rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on improving asthma self-management and moves the session toward concrete, measurable actions and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co-constructed—asks preferences, drafts the script together, and adapts to the patient's choices, showing strong teamwork throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, asking what feels doable, and only providing tools (role‑play, script) when the patient opts in, avoiding pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and practical concerns, but could more frequently use scaling or value-linked questions and amplify patient expressions to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invites the patient's input, though at times the clinician shifts to expert-driven explanations rather than leaving more space for patient-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits actionable change talk (PCP call, scripted questions, role‑play) but could more consistently reflect and amplify change statements to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and normalizes ambivalence, which prevents sustain talk from escalating, though more use of double-sided reflections could better pivot toward change.
