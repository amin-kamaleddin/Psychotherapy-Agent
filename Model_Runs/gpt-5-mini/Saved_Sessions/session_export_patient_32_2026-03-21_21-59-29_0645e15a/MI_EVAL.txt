# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, used nonjudgmental language and affirmed strengths (e.g., six months sober). The tone stayed warm and normalizing throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that acknowledged context and barriers, but missed some opportunities to deepen emotional reflections (e.g., probe shame, fear, loneliness). Strong summaries were present but could more consistently mirror underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on preventing DUI and preserving license/job, repeatedly returning to concrete steps tied to that goal. The session advanced specific action planning with timelines and contingencies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative with invitations for input and preference checks, but several clinician-offered scripts and step-by-step suggestions shifted some of the task away from the client. Overall respectful joint problem-solving with room to elicit more client-generated wording earlier.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Derrick's choice and control by asking permission, offering optional scripts, and presenting alternatives while leaving decisions to him. Language avoided pressure and reinforced client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within by eliciting personal reasons for change, past successes, and concrete coping strategies, leading to client-generated commitments. Scaling and question prompts successfully surfaced internal motivation and actionable steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and invited choices (e.g., which text fits), yet occasionally offered ready-made solutions rather than prompting the client to generate them first. Partnership was strong but could emphasize client expertise more.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk using targeted questions, scaling, and reinforcement of client language, resulting in concrete plans (stash money, sober buddy, rideshare). Clinician summarized and strengthened commitment effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward practical consequences and supports, but could have used more double-sided reflections or explicit acknowledgment of the pull of sustain talk. Additional reframing would further reduce resistance.
