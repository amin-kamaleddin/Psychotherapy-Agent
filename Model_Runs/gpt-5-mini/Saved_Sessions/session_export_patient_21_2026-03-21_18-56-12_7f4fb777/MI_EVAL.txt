# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, offering affirmations and normalizing concerns throughout the session. Avoids labels or criticism and conveys unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening with reflective, pragmatic statements and tailored alternatives for low-energy days. Missed some opportunities to explore deeper emotional nuance about inconsistency and the emotional benefits of walking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing short walks and repeatedly anchors the conversation to concrete, achievable steps and a follow-up plan. There is no drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative with co-creation of the plan, checklist, and self-talk options, and frequent solicitation of the patient's input. Positions the interaction as joint problem-solving with planned follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering multiple flexible options, permission to stop, and inviting the patient to choose scripts and scheduling. Encourages self-directed decision-making and self-kindness after misses.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete intentions and commitments effectively (days, starter walks, self-talk) but does not probe deeper internal motivators, values, or use scaling questions to strengthen intrinsic motivation. Evocation is solid around pragmatic planning but could be deeper.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters shared decision-making and repeatedly asks for preferences while co-constructing the plan. At times the clinician supplies many specific suggestions, making the process somewhat clinician-led rather than fully client-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk around concrete commitments, scheduling, and progress. Does not consistently deepen exploration of broader reasons/values for change or elicit confidence/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by normalizing concerns and offering gentle alternatives and contingency plans. Could increase use of double-sided reflections to explicitly link sustain and change talk and guide resolution of ambivalence.
