# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing the patient's feelings and thanking requests. Tone remained nonjudgmental throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely reflections and summaries that validated the patient's mixed feelings and priorities. Used reflections to check understanding before moving on.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the specific target behavior (safe transition between meds) and repeatedly returned to timelines, red-flag symptoms, tapering, and producing a concrete plan. Progressed the interaction toward a dated calendar and checklist.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner by inviting the patient's priorities, checking format preferences, and repeatedly offering to draft or prefill materials. Language and actions placed the patient in the driver’s seat while providing professional support.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirmed patient choice and control by offering options rather than directives and asking permission to draft the plan. Emphasized that the patient's preferences would guide timing and content of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicited the patient's internal motivations and contingencies (safety, exact timelines) with targeted questions and invitations to prioritize specifics. Built on these expressed values to shape next steps and plan content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated genuine power-sharing by asking which option felt acceptable and checking how the clinician could assist (e.g., drafting, pre-filling dates). Adjusted the plan based on the patient's stated priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and amplified preparatory change talk and concrete information needs, and offered actionable options. Could be strengthened by eliciting explicit commitment language or using scaling to capture readiness and next-step intent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and neutrally reframed ambivalence, focusing on structure and options rather than arguing. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections and summaries that juxtapose reasons for and against to further tilt toward change.
