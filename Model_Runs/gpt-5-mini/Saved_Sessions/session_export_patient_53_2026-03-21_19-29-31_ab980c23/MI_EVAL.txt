# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance with repeated normalizing and supportive phrases (e.g., “I won’t be mad,” “be gentle with yourself”). These moves clearly affirm the patient and remove blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening and validation (reflects anxiety, thanks patient for sharing), but sometimes moves quickly to planning and offers few deeper, complex reflections of internal experience. Empathy is high but not fully maximized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (stepping outside) with concrete steps and a precise check‑in time. The session stayed goal-directed and structured throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed—clinician solicited input on door, timing, reminders, and adapted the plan to patient hesitancy. Language framed the process as a team effort rather than an expert directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong use of choice language and explicit statements preserving patient control (e.g., offering options, assuring no judgment). The clinician consistently reinforced that the patient retains decision-making authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts to draw out motivation (offering planning help and check‑ins) but the clinician leaned toward giving solutions rather than eliciting the patient’s own reasons, values, or scaling of importance/confidence. Opportunities to surface stronger, self‑generated change talk were underused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician asked permission, offered options, and co-created the plan with the patient, positioning her as the expert on preferences. This fostered a genuine collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced small change language and commitments (e.g., planning, check‑ins) but relied heavily on proposing plans rather than systematically drawing out and amplifying the patient’s own reasons, desires, and confidence. Change talk was present but not consistently developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing returning inside and acknowledging fear rather than arguing with avoidance. Could improve by using more double‑sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge ambivalence.
