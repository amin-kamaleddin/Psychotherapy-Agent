# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated nonjudgmental support and positive regard (opening support, safety check, normalizing statements) and affirmed small steps like filling out the form and trying one day.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective listening and accurately summarized feelings and facts (anxiety, grogginess, drink patterns), but relied on brief validations and problem-solving rather than deeper, emotion-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus on alcohol reduction and translated the conversation into a measurable, concrete plan with safety contingencies and tracking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked cooperatively with the client by soliciting preferences, tailoring options (alarm/friend wording), and co-creating a shared, client-driven plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported client choice and control by asking permission, offering multiple options, endorsing small steps, and normalizing slips without pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation (importance/confidence scales, doable choices) but leaned toward offering strategies instead of drawing out the client's own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered an explicitly collaborative stance—asked permission, invited input on doable options, and checked preferences throughout the plan-building process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced tentative change talk (scaling, asking which option feels doable) but often moved quickly to offering solutions instead of amplifying the client's own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized ambivalence with harm-minimizing redirections, though could have used more double-sided reflections to explicitly contrast sustain and change language.
