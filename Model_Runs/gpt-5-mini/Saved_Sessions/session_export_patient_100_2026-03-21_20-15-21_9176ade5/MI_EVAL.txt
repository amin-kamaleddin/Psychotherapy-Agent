# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance throughout, validating ambivalence and avoiding blame while normalizing the teen’s perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displayed good reflective listening and perspective-taking, summarizing concerns and checking feelings, though some reflections were more paraphrase than deeper, affect-focused responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use in relation to grades and college, moving logically from eliciting concerns to readiness assessment, goal-setting, and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Modeled an equal partnership with frequent invitations to choose and co‑constructed plans, using inclusive language and seeking the client’s preferences for interventions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honored the client’s choice by offering a menu of options, asking which felt doable, and avoiding pressure for commitment beyond the client’s willingness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the client’s own motivations and resources using values exploration and scaling, and amplified emerging motivation to support internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt collaborative and power‑sharing: clinician repeatedly invited input, checked permission, and treated the teen as the expert in planning and decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk with scaling, concrete planning, and reflections, but could more deliberately amplify and sequence desire, reasons, and ability to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and gently redirected toward risk reduction and protective steps, though more frequent double‑sided reflections could better validate both sides of ambivalence earlier.
