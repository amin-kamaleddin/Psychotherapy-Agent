# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive with normalizing statements and validation that convey unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and timely, specific reflections that capture Jonathan’s overwhelm and pragmatic distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on diabetes self-management (one morning glucose check and obtaining strips) and keeps the conversation action-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High level of joint problem-solving—co-creates scripts and messages, asks preferences, and negotiates next steps rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces Jonathan’s choice and control, offers multiple options, and respectfully declines to do tasks for him to support ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some concrete action intentions but does little to draw out internal motives, personal values, or stronger self-endorsed reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly collaborative—asks permission and offers choices—but early prescriptive listing of steps reduced opportunities for jointly generating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively invites small, doable steps and elicits tentative change language, but misses opportunities to deepen or amplify intrinsic reasons, importance, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reframes sustain talk without arguing and offers neutral options, though could use more double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence.
