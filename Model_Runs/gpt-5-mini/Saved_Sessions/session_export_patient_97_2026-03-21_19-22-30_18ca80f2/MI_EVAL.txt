# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental support, normalizing Mateo's struggle and avoiding blame throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate understanding with specific reflections and summary work, follows Mateo's affect and checks safety, showing strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (triggers, coping actions, rehearsals) and organizes the session around concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites Mateo's input and tailors suggestions, but detailed technical instructions (e.g., phone shortcuts) sometimes shift power toward the clinician's expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offers options, checks preferences, and frames advice as suggestions, preserving Mateo's sense of choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change language and choices but relies heavily on offering solutions and misses opportunities to probe deeper values, reasons, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively to co-create plans and invites choices, although at times the clinician adopts an expert role that reduces the sense of client-led ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates multiple opportunities for commitment and concrete planning (choice of task, reinforcement, follow-up) but could more often elicit internal reasons and importance/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and normalizes ambivalence but often moves to solution-focused responses instead of using reflections or double-sided strategies to explore and reduce sustain talk.
