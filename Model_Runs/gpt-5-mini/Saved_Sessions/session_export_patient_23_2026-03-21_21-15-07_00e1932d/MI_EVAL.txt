# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support (opens with choice, frequent affirmations, normalizes slips). No shaming or labeling and clinician validates Claudia’s values throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good perspective-taking with accurate, timely reflections and summaries of concerns (mood, health, high-risk situations). Could deepen empathic responding with more complex/feeling-level reflections and probe the emotional link between alcohol and depression.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and moves the session toward concrete planning. Questions and summaries steer toward actionable steps (specific goal, strategies, start date, follow-up).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is highly collaborative with co-constructed decisions, solicitation of preferences, and incorporation of Claudia’s choices. Clinician checks back and negotiates the agenda rather than dictating it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering options, respecting Claudia’s pace, and using noncoercive language. Repeatedly reinforces choice and control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits plans, confidence ratings, supports, and barriers and turns them into actions. Could strengthen evocation by asking an importance ruler, eliciting underlying values/past successes, and amplifying spontaneous change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates an equal partnership by positioning Claudia as the expert and offering choices (nights, reminders, backups). Power is shared—clinician suggests but leaves the final choices to the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with open questions, affirmations, confidence scaling, and targeted planning. Clinician amplifies Claudia’s specific change language and operationalizes it into concrete steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirects toward change by validating ambivalence and supporting a patient-chosen goal. Could more intentionally use double-sided reflections or selectively reflect sustain talk to further diminish its influence.
