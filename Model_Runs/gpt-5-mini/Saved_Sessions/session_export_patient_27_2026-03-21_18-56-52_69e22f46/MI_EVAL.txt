# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with validating statements and an early safety check that created a supportive climate for adolescent disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good empathic listening and perspective-taking (validations, reflective questions) but misses some opportunities to deepen reflections on fear, vulnerability, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on disclosure throughout the session, moving logically from exploration to concrete planning, message selection, and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally acts as an equal partner—asking input and inviting role-play—but occasionally leans directive by offering ready-made scripts without first asking permission.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by offering multiple options, asking which feels safest, and avoiding pressuring language, reinforcing that disclosure is the patient's decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits values, preferences, and a concrete plan, activating internal motivation, but could use more evocative open questions and reflections to amplify client-generated reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as collaborative and invites co-creation, though offering scripts without permission at times reduces the sense of full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change language (choice of message, rehearsals) but could more deliberately amplify and elaborate the client's own change talk to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing reasons for not disclosing and offers incremental, safer options to shift focus, though double-sided reflections would better acknowledge ambivalence and tip toward change.
