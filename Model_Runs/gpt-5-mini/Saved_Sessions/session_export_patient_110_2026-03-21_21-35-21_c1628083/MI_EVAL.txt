# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, offering options rather than pressure. Warm, supportive stance is maintained throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and validation show clear understanding of Richard's perspective and priorities. Some reflections remain functional rather than exploring deeper emotions (e.g., fear of dependence), so more empathic probes would strengthen this area.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on cholesterol management with concrete, behavior-specific steps (low-dose trial, monitoring, walking calendar). Conversation consistently advances actionable goals tied to Richard's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reads as a joint project with co-created artifacts (doctor note, checklist, calendar) and frequent solicitation of patient input. Clinician adapts materials to Richard's preferences, demonstrating strong teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by presenting options, asking permission, and reassuring that stopping is allowed if problems occur. Avoids coercive language and checks before acting on the patient's behalf.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and change intentions and links values to plans, producing explicit commitments. Could further evoke internal motivation by asking more targeted follow-ups (e.g., exploring the 6/10 confidence rating) before moving to solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative with clear power-sharing: patient edits are incorporated and choices shape the plan. Shared decision-making is evident throughout the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk, translating values into concrete steps and obtaining a confidence rating. Missed opportunities to amplify and deepen Richard's own change language (e.g., reflective summaries to strengthen commitment).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reduces fears through reassurance and monitoring plans but rarely employs MI-specific techniques to roll with sustain talk. Limited use of double-sided reflections or pros/cons summaries to strategically redirect sustain talk toward change.
