# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, normalizing distress and avoiding blame. Warm, respectful statements and appreciation for the patient's actions maintained safety and rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate empathic listening and reflections tracked the patient's affect and somatic experience and validated ambivalence. Frequent, well-timed empathic statements built rapport and safety throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on immediate safety and self-harm risk, repeatedly steering to concrete safety steps. The clinician monitored urge levels and avoided drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner by soliciting preferences, co-developing the roommate text, and offering to stay while the patient tried coping strategies. Patient contributions clearly shaped the session's actions and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and agency by offering options and asking permission rather than issuing directives. The clinician affirmed control over decisions and avoided coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited immediate behavioral commitments (locking blade, texting roommate) but did little to draw out deeper internal motivations, values, or longer-term reasons for change. Missed opportunities to ask open-ended questions to amplify desire, reasons, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance through frequent permission requests and shared decision-making, positioning the patient as the decision-maker. Offers to help draft texts and to stay were supportive rather than directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited and reinforced practical steps that produced some change talk (urge reduction, safety behaviors). However, the clinician missed chances to deepen the patient's own reasons, values, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively acknowledged sustain talk without judgment and redirected to harm-reduction and coping alternatives. Could have used more double-sided reflections to explicitly highlight ambivalence and further soften sustain talk.
