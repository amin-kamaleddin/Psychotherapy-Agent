### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, normalizing ambivalence and respecting Evelyn's preferences. Warmth and repeated validation conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate synthesis of the patient's perspective, validating both enjoyment and concern. Could deepen emotional nuance with more complex reflections to mirror affect and explore feelings about the son's worry and the fall.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol-related safety and progressed logically from assessment to a concrete, measurable plan with follow-up. Repeatedly brought conversation back to implementation details and medical follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative tone using inclusive language and co-constructing messages and reminders with the patient. Repeatedly asked permission and incorporated Evelyn's wording and choices into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized patient choice and avoided pressure, offering options and leaving decisions to Evelyn. Medical suggestions were framed as choices rather than mandates, supporting her control.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical reasons for change (independence, son, dislike of falls) and used these to build a plan. Could further deepen intrinsic motivation by exploring values, confidence, and securing a stronger verbal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reflected a true partnership with shared decision-making and mutual problem-solving. Clinician honored Evelyn's expertise and preferences at each step, fostering joint ownership of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and built on change talk with open questions and converted ideas into actionable steps (smaller pours, alcohol-free nights, reminders). To improve, selectively amplify change talk and explicitly elicit confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and skillfully reframed minimizations, pivoting to acceptable small changes rather than confronting sustain talk. Could more often use double-sided reflections to validate ambivalence while gently highlighting reasons for change.
