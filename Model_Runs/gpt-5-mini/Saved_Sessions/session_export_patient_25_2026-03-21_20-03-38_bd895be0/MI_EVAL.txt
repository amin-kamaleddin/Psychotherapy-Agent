# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with clear unconditional positive regard from the opening line and repeated affirmations of accomplishments (e.g., quitting smoking).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate and often complex reflections linking social pressure and family motivations, demonstrating strong perspective-taking and reflective listening throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, sustained focus on managing alcohol in social situations, using goal questions, scaling, if‑then planning, and role‑play to move toward concrete change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session felt like genuine two-way work—invited Daniel to pick and tweak strategies, co‑constructed refusal lines, and encouraged joint ownership of rehearsals and plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice by asking permission before advice, accepting limits on what the client would try, and repeatedly reinforcing the client's agency in selecting strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated from an evocation stance—used open questions, scaling, and guided practice to draw out Daniel's own reasons, solutions, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Firmly collaborative stance with frequent invitations for input, checking feasibility, and co‑creating language and plans that centered the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited and strengthened change talk through evocative questions, affirmations, scaling, and prompting concrete, doable strategies that produced specific change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward coping strategies and role‑play, but could have lingered more on ambivalence and used double‑sided reflections to normalize the pull to drink.
