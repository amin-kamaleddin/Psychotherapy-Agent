# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently respectful and affirming, offering early and repeated praise and normalizing nervousness. Language remained nonjudgmental and conveyed unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated clear understanding with reflections and validations of Marco's experience, acknowledging pride and nervousness. More complex empathic reflections exploring the emotional meaning of regain or ambivalence would have strengthened this further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed sharply focused on reducing orange juice intake, repeatedly returning to concrete strategies and an implementation plan. Momentum was maintained toward specific behavioral change steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction felt like a joint effort—preferences were solicited and language was practiced together—but the clinician provided many scripted recommendations that sometimes shifted the session toward clinician-led problem solving. Greater elicitation before advising would enhance equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through offering choices, asking permission, framing slips as data, and proposing trial periods. The clinician consistently emphasized that change decisions and trials belonged to Marco.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited concrete motivational statements and plans (e.g., dilution, asking his wife, cues) and used questions that brought out Marco’s intentions. More probing of personal meaning, confidence/importance ratings, and amplification of pride would deepen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fostered a collaborative stance by asking preferences and practicing wording, creating an equal‑partner tone. However, supplying a substantial toolkit and scripted lines sometimes created an expert-led feel rather than fully co-creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully converted intent into concrete change language and implementation steps by asking which swap felt easiest and inviting practice. They could have used more reflections to amplify Marco's change statements and probed commitment/confidence further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but not strategically softened—there were missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or exploring the appeal of OJ. The clinician moved quickly to problem-solving, which redirected toward change but didn’t explicitly bridge ambivalence.
