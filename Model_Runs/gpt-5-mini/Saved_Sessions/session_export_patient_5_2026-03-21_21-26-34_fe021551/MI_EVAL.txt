# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates concerns while offering practical help (drafts/scripts). Could strengthen unconditional acceptance with more explicit normalization of uncertainty and less clinical/formulaic language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and tailors information to the patient’s concerns and context. Empathic reflections are present but sparse, with more emphasis on information and tools than on exploring feelings or burdens.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on safe medication management and communication, advancing concrete goals and action steps. Keeps the encounter outcome-focused and moves the patient from questions to planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: repeatedly elicits preferences, incorporates edits, and co-constructs messages and next steps with the patient. Language and behavior frame clinician and patient as partners throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respects and reinforces the patient’s control over medication decisions and offers options for contact and wording. Could add more explicit affirmations of competence and ask permission before offering advice to further bolster autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical actions and plans but does little to draw out the patient’s internal motivations, values, or confidence. Would benefit from more open, evocative questions about reasons and personal priorities.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters genuine power-sharing by inviting input, offering to draft and finalize messages, and letting the patient shape content and next steps. The clinician consistently treats the patient as a collaborator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Encourages actionable steps (drafting messages, scheduling urgent visits) that support movement toward change and planning. Missed opportunities to elicit and amplify intrinsic reasons or values that would deepen and sustain change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing the status quo and redirects toward safety and practical planning, which reduces ambivalence. Could more explicitly use strategies like double-sided reflections to acknowledge hesitations while highlighting change reasons.
