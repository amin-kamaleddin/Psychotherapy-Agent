# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established unconditional positive regard from the opening and maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, validating stance, normalizing ambivalence and thanking the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, concise reflections that captured ambivalence and practical concerns, but missed deeper affective mirroring of shame/embarrassment and opportunities for amplified empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on alcohol use linked to academic risk, using strategic questions to move from ambivalence to concrete, time-linked action steps and implementation intentions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a team effort—clinician solicited preferences, co-created scripts, and offered role-play and structure only after eliciting the client's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized the client's choice and control by offering options, asking which step felt most doable, and respecting the client's preferences without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivations, used scaling and planning questions effectively, but could have more fully developed intrinsic motivation and amplified tentative change talk to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently fostered a collaborative relationship, treated the client as the expert, and invited the client's input at decision points, demonstrating strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Desire/Reason/Ability talk and some commitment language through planning and scaling, yet did not consistently amplify or reflect the client's change statements to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and used rolling-with-resistance techniques and an early double-sided reflection, but could more proactively pair sustain reflections with change talk to shift momentum.
