# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, using normalization, praise, and encouragement; no shaming or minimizing was observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and validation of frustration and low confidence were used, but the clinician often moved quickly to planning and could have explored deeper felt experience more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with concrete steps and commitment questions, avoiding topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner by co-creating scripts, offering alternatives, and adapting suggestions to the patient's stated limitations and choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, checking preferences, avoiding pressure, and emphasizing kindness and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change-oriented statements and confidence ratings but relied mainly on practical strategies and did not sufficiently probe values or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited the patient to choose options, asked permission for follow-up, and ensured the plan reflected the patient's priorities and feasibility.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk through commitment choices and affirmations, though could more often elicit the patient's personal reasons and desired outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing barriers, reframing, and offering tiny-start plans; could further use double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence.
