# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician expressed unconditional positive regard early and repeatedly, validated Eric's priorities and coping, and avoided judgmental language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections and complex paraphrases captured emotional nuance and ambivalence, showing clear understanding of Eric's perspective without confrontation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, appropriate focus on smoking and related goals, consistently steering toward a concrete, time‑bound experiment (truck‑only plan for a week).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acted as an equal partner, soliciting Eric's ideas and co‑constructing a realistic plan based on the patient's suggestions and supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reinforced Eric's choice and control, honored his pacing and decision not to quit immediately, and offered open invitations to choose manageable steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple internally generated reasons for change were drawn out and moved Eric to a concrete experiment, though deeper probing of values and confidence/commitment scaling would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-quality power sharing was evident: the clinician invited Eric's solutions, accepted his suggestions, and built the plan around his expertise and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and amplified specific change talk that produced a concrete plan, but could have more systematically amplified change language and asked for confidence/commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and reframed without argument, steering toward small changes; using more double‑sided reflections could further soften sustain talk and deepen discrepancy.
