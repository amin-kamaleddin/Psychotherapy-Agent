# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with affirmations and validation (e.g., normalizing difficulty and praising efforts), maintaining a nonjudgmental stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking (summaries of efforts and worries) but could have probed deeper into emotional tone and the personal meaning of flare‑ups to reach a top score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept clear clinical focus on inhaler technique, spacer use, meds, and smoking/drinking reduction and moved logically to concrete, actionable steps without overriding patient preference.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-created the plan by inviting input on priorities, offering options, and scheduling joint practice, positioning the clinician as a guide and partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice through permission language, offering options (e.g., practice vs. action plan), and respecting the patient's decision to cut back rather than quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation (fear of flare‑ups, importance rating) and used confidence-building offers, but could have more systematically drawn out the patient's own reasons and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated a consistent partnership model by asking permission, offering choices, inviting the patient's priorities, and framing steps as joint problem‑solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited change language (importance/confidence scale, asking what to try first) and amplified some statements, but missed targeted follow-ups (e.g., 'Why an 8?') to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and honored harm‑reduction (cutting back), which reduced sustain talk, but could have used more double‑sided reflections to gently shift ambivalence toward change.
