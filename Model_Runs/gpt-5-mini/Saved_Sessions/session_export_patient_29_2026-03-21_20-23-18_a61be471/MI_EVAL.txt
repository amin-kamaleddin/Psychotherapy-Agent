# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and normalized the patient’s experience, offering choices and validating concerns. Nonjudgmental tone and repeated affirmations supported a strong accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately restating readiness and stress-related worries. Missed a few opportunities to probe deeper into the emotional experience and meaning of quitting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with concrete steps (med choice, titration, quit date, pharmacy/provider tasks). Questions and interventions stayed on target with little drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like a joint enterprise: clinician drafted messages, asked for location and pharmacy, and incorporated the patient’s input into plans. Tools were offered while leaving implementation and choices to the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported patient choice by asking permission, offering multiple medication options, and framing offers as choices. Avoided pressuring language and fostered patient ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out readiness, confidence ratings, and discrete commitments (varenicline choice, quit date). Could increase open evocation questions to elicit intrinsic motives and personal values for quitting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was strongly collaborative with co-construction of the plan and clear power-sharing, asking for med preference and quit date. Patient’s ideas consistently shaped session content and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced several strands of change talk and helped move to actionable commitment (med selection, quit date). Could more frequently amplify and explore reasons for quitting to deepen desire and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk about stress and social triggers and immediately offered practical coping strategies to reduce their power. Could further neutralize sustain talk using double-sided reflections, reframing, or summaries that tip toward change.
