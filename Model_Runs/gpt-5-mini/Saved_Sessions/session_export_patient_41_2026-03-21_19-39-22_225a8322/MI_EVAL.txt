# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation, normalizing the patient’s experience and affirming efforts. Language is warm and supportive throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking that reduces judgment, but stops short of deeper emotional exploration. Could use more complex reflections to capture self-criticism and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on improving follow-through with concrete experiments and implementation intentions. Moves the conversation from exploration to specific, measurable steps while preserving the patient’s voice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative and egalitarian, inviting patient input and co-constructing wording and plans. Decisions are negotiated rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, avoiding pressure, and affirming the patient’s agency. Repeatedly invites the patient to select what feels doable, preserving ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation and intention through experiments and commitments, producing change talk and agreements. Could more actively explore intrinsic reasons, values, and desired identity changes to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters shared decision-making and conveys that the patient’s expertise matters, offering options and inviting phrasing tweaks. The clinician co-creates the plan with the patient, reflecting strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and strengthens change talk via the 5‑minute experiment and implementation-intention wording, producing plan language and intentions. Could better amplify the patient’s pro-change statements and explicitly probe importance and confidence to increase commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk by normalizing doubt and reframing tasks as low-pressure experiments, reducing reasons for avoidance. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections and selective amplification of fleeting change talk.
