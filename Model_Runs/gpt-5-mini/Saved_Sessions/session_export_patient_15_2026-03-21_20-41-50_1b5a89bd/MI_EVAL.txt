# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened and maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, normalized choices and affirmed the patient's plan throughout; no evidence of labeling or criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective statements and a clear summary that validated the patient's perspective, but could deepen empathy with more complex reflections of minimization and underlying feelings or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on blood pressure and modifiable contributors, advancing concrete, measurable actions (home monitoring, alarms, thresholds) tied to revisiting alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co‑designed the monitoring plan with the patient (alarm times, demo, sticker text) and consistently framed decisions as shared, incorporating the patient's preferences into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered choices, asked permission before providing information, avoided pressuring language, and repeatedly affirmed the patient's control over decisions and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited intentions, confidence ratings, and barriers which produced practical plans, but did not sufficiently draw out personal motivations, values, or past successes to strengthen intrinsic change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong partnership by treating the patient as the expert, offering options, and co-creating a specific, patient-centered monitoring plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prompted actionable change-related steps (monitoring, alarms) and asked about confidence/barriers, but missed opportunities to elicit stronger intrinsic reasons for changing alcohol use or to amplify past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and defused sustain talk with neutral information and a plan to revisit alcohol, though could more actively use double-sided reflections or summaries to highlight discrepancy.
