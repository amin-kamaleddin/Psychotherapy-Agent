# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes ambivalence, and avoids judgment; tone is supportive and makes the client feel respected and safe to disclose.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate reflections and summaries mirror both emotion and content and the clinician checks meaning with open questions; empathy is strong and consistently demonstrated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol use linked to grades/college, narrows to a single concrete step (not drinking on school nights / telling Jess), and works through implementation details without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as a genuine two‑person collaboration: clinician solicits preferences, role‑plays, invites tweaks, and jointly makes decisions rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the client's choice and control by offering options, checking permission, and avoiding directive language, preserving the client's agency throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation from the client by exploring values and past successes, prompting client‑generated reasons and practical solutions rather than supplying them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters partnership and shared power through collaborative language and joint planning, treating the client as the expert on her life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly elicits and reinforces change talk with evocative questions, reflections, and by narrowing to a doable first step and exploring implementation strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (peer pressure) and redirects toward supports and strategies, but relies mostly on neutral reflections; could more often use double‑sided reflections or reframing to explicitly reduce sustain talk.
