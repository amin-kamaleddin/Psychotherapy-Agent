# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys unconditional positive regard throughout, normalizes ambivalence, and avoids judgment (e.g., praising 22 days and validating feelings). Uses warm, validating language consistently to support the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing ambivalence and triggers. Could deepen affective reflections and explicitly explore identity/stigma statements like "I'm lazy" or feeling unaccepted.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on relapse prevention and pro‑social first steps, repeatedly steering toward concrete actions (9am call, blocking numbers, alarms). Keeps session content tightly linked to target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: rehearses scripts, asks permission, and jointly constructs implementation details with the client. Demonstrates strong co‑construction of feasible plans drawing on both clinician skill and client experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's right to choose, offers options rather than directives, and affirms incremental commitments ('trying it for now'). Fosters client ownership and avoids pressuring decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation by highlighting values (Fiona, porch moments) and using scaling/confidence questions to draw out reasons for change. Could further probe deeper values and identity-based motives before moving to strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the work as a shared enterprise, asking what feels most helpful and letting the client lead choices. Demonstrates strong power‑sharing and a client‑centered stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk, affirms successes, and prompts concrete commitments (calling agency, blocking numbers, delay plan). Could more often reflect and amplify client‑generated change language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to change but tends to move quickly to problem‑solving. Missed opportunities to use double‑sided reflections, rolling with resistance, and to explore self‑labels/stigma to more effectively soften sustain talk.
