# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys respect and nonjudgmental support, including a safety check and validating language that normalizes the client's strain. No blaming or labeling occurred, demonstrating strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and timely reflections/summaries capture both emotion and goals throughout the exchange. The clinician shows clear understanding and curiosity about the client's context.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, appropriate focus on co-parenting communication and routines from start to finish. The clinician consistently brings the conversation back to concrete steps that serve the stated aim.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frames work as a joint effort and iterates drafts with the client, co-creating messages and offering tips. At times the clinician supplies many ready-made templates, which makes the interaction somewhat clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options are offered and permission is requested before drafting, supporting client choice. Some directive recommendations and specific scripts reduce the emphasis on the client's own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician recognizes the client's motivation (love for her son) but does little to draw out deeper personal reasons, confidence, or values. Interventions favor problem-solving and advice rather than eliciting the client's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely collaborative with solicitation of the client's preferences and joint message editing. However, frequent provision of concrete solutions and templates shifts some control away from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician creates openings for change talk but relies more on offering solutions than on eliciting the client's own reasons, strengths, or confidence. Several missed opportunities to amplify and deepen the client's expressed change language limit cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and redirected without amplifying resistance, with helpful de‑escalation language and practical reframing. The clinician missed some double-sided reflections that would have more actively resolved ambivalence.
