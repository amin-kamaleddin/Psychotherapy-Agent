# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with repeated affirmations and normalization of awkwardness; conveyed unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good empathic listening and validated triggers and feelings, but could use deeper affective reflections and more summarizing of underlying emotions to reach a top rating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on drinking and anger with actionable steps and a concrete weekly plan, though offering many strategies at once slightly diluted the focus at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked jointly with the patient to choose strategies and role‑play, but occasional instructive, prescriptive listing reduced the equal‑partner feel somewhat.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by respecting readiness, offering choices, asking permission, and framing suggestions as optional rather than prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations (protecting the relationship, controlling temper) but tended to move quickly to solutions instead of deeply drawing out the patient's own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and co‑creative with plans and role‑play, though more explicit statements affirming the patient's expertise would strengthen the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and praised progress, but missed opportunities to ask open questions that would deepen the patient's own statements about why change matters and how life would differ.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected ambivalence and avoided amplifying sustain talk by recommending incremental strategies, but could more explicitly use double‑sided reflections to help resolve ambivalence.
