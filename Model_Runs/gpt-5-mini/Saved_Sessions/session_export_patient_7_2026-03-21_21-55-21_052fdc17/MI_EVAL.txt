# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalized Amina's feelings, and respected boundaries (e.g., honoring requests not to discuss family). The clinician affirmed choices and avoided criticism throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Delivered accurate, repeated reflections that captured both content and emotional meaning (invasiveness, need for privacy, value of music). The clinician showed genuine perspective-taking and named Amina's boundaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, appropriate focus on engagement and rapport rather than pushing behavior change, promptly redirecting to an agreed topic (music). The clinician sustained that focus through collaborative playlist work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—invited co-creation of the playlist, solicited specific preferences, and followed the client's lead. The session felt like joint problem-solving rather than top-down direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Amina's control with explicit choice language and honored requests (e.g., keeping the playlist private). The clinician avoided pressure and checked in with options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited what matters to Amina (music, writing) and the functions music serves, drawing out personal meaning. However, the clinician did not strongly link those values to the presenting concern or potential coping strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a partnership stance through offers of choice, soliciting preferences, and co-creating the playlist, treating Amina as the expert. Decision-making power was consistently placed with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Efforts to elicit change talk about home/well‑being were minimal—appropriate given resistance but representing missed micro-opportunities. The clinician did elicit change-relevant language around music, which could be a pathway for future elicitation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk without argument, using acceptance, reframing, and offering alternatives to reduce defensiveness and redirect to music. Could be strengthened with occasional double-sided reflections acknowledging privacy and potential small costs.
