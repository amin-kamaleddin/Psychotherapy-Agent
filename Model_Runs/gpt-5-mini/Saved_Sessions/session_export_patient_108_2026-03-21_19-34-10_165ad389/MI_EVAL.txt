# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental; normalizes feelings and avoids blaming, conveying clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regular empathic acknowledgements and validations are present, but deeper reflective listening and exploration of emotional meaning are limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on getting the flatmate to move out with every intervention tied to that objective.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments (role‑plays, choices), but the clinician often drives content and agenda with prescriptive scripts rather than co‑construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and respects volition through options and invitations, though could more explicitly emphasize client ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on didactic scripts and logistics with few open, evocative questions to draw out the client's intrinsic motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invitations to practice support joint work, but clinician frequently assumes an expert/prescribing role that limits equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides language and opportunities to practice change statements, yet misses chances to elicit the client's own reasons, values, and confidence more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and validates ambivalence, but uses few strategic MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections) to consistently soften or redirect it.
