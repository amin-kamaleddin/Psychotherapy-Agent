# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful and nonjudgmental, repeatedly acknowledging Julian's feelings and guardedness. Acceptance could be stronger with more explicit normalization and affirmations of strengths to sound warmer and less clinical.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking captured both content and emotion and validated ambivalence. Reflections were timely and matched the client's level of hesitancy throughout the interaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on completing the court-mandated community service with concrete, practical steps (list → text → call). Questions and offers consistently tied back to actionable next moves.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician operated as an equal partner, co-constructing the plan and repeatedly soliciting Julian's input and preferences. Decisions were shaped jointly rather than imposed, reinforcing mutual problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician offered clear choices (list vs call, tone, anonymity) and honored Julian's preferences, supporting his sense of control. Language avoided pressure and explicitly left decisions to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out internal reasons and benefits for change (less stress, more confidence) and used questions to elicit motivation. There were missed opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation, use scaling, or convert change talk into concrete commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative language positioned Julian as co-author of the plan and shared power in decision-making. The clinician repeatedly invited input and adjusted the approach to fit Julian's comfort and needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several clear instances of change talk were elicited and supported through small-step options, producing statements of intent. The clinician could more consistently reflect, amplify, and link desire/ability/reasons to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged accurately but mostly restated rather than strategically softened. The clinician missed chances for double-sided reflections or reframing to reduce the power of avoidance and pivot toward change.
