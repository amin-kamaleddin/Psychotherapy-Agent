# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician established clear unconditional positive regard with warm language, affirmations, and normalization of slips, consistently respecting Evelyn's preferences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated a solid empathic stance with validations, reflections, and safety checks, but reflections were relatively surface-level and did not deeply explore the emotional meaning of Evelyn's ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session kept a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol with targeted safety assessment, concrete strategies, and a scheduled follow-up, resisting drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative: the plan was co-created, materials were tailored to Evelyn's requests, and the clinician positioned themselves as a helper rather than a director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician strongly supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, preserving valued behaviors (keeping the pub), and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivations and commitments were elicited (specific plan and follow-up), but the clinician relied more on offering solutions than on open questions to draw out Evelyn's deeper reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly invited collaboration and shared decision-making, checking preferences and co-constructing the plan with Evelyn throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician elicited concrete change language (limits, AF days, follow-up) but missed opportunities to deepen and amplify change talk via open questions about importance, confidence, and past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and the clinician accommodated social concerns, but MI-specific techniques (double-sided reflections, amplifying downsides) were rarely used to reframe ambivalence.
