# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance (e.g., normalizing ambivalence and offering affirmation). Validated feelings and offered support like rehearsal to reduce shame and nervousness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately summarizing Elias's internal conflict and emotional state. Responses consistently matched the client's words about shame, family motivation, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on sobriety and immediate risk reduction, moving the conversation toward specific actionable steps. Progressed purposefully from reflection to concrete planning (who, when, supports, rehearsal).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Built a genuine partnership by soliciting Elias's preferences and following his choices, co-constructing wording and supports. Offered suggestions but deferred to the client to shape the plan and language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly preserved choice and control by offering multiple options and asking which felt most helpful. Language consistently reinforced Elias's agency without pressuring him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focused on eliciting Elias's own motivations and resources, asking for small doable steps and amplifying his reasons (kids, avoiding shame). Avoided supplying motivation and repeatedly drew out client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and deferred to Elias's expertise about what would work, asking him to choose and tweak scripts. Followed his preferences for rehearsal and role-play, reinforcing shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and strengthened change talk by asking for one or two small steps, drilling into specifics, and inviting elaboration and commitment language. Helped craft scripts and rehearsal to amplify intention and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and quickly redirected toward change by acknowledging both sides and pivoting to steps. Missed a few opportunities for more strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or explicit discrepancy to deepen movement away from sustain talk).
