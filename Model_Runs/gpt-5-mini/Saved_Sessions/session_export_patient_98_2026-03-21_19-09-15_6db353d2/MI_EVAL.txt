# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently showed unconditional positive regard, validated Mateo's distress, and used respectful, nonjudgmental language while explicitly checking safety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and timely, acknowledging skepticism and discouragement, though there were a few missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on rehab progress and pacing, offering concrete, stepwise guidance tied to safety and behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction felt joint and respectful—preferences were solicited and practical supports were offered as options—yet the clinician could have more fully co-created solutions by eliciting Mateo's own strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choices, asked permission before giving advice, and emphasized Mateo's control over pacing and tracking, supporting self-directed decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician assumed motivation resided in Mateo and provided tools, but did limited direct elicitation of his intrinsic motives, values, or past successes and did not use scaling or evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructed concrete steps and invited follow-up, creating a collaborative tone, but could better incorporate Mateo's own wording and ideas into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were openings for change talk via small goals and a 7-day plan, but the clinician did not consistently elicit or amplify Mateo's change statements with follow-up questions or scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician normalized ambivalence and avoided endorsing sustain talk, but often moved quickly to advice without using double-sided reflections or eliciting pros and cons to gently redirect sustain talk.
