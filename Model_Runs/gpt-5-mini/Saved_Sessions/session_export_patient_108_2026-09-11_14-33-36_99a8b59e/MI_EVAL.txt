# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard through validating reflections and explicit affirmations, maintaining a warm, nonjudgmental tone that normalizes ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with frequent content-and-feeling reflections and paraphrases that convey understanding; there were no clear misunderstandings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (setting a move-out date and boundary-setting), using targeted prompts to move from ambivalence to concrete planning and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functions as a partnership: the clinician offers structure and options while inviting the client to shape choices, language, and rehearsal, showing mutual contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client autonomy by offering multiple options, asking which feels most doable, and avoiding pressure toward a specific solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's own motivation and commitment through open questions and invitations to choose a date, craft language, and rehearse, reinforcing internally generated reasons to act.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the work as collaborative and defers to the client's expertise, repeatedly soliciting preferences and inviting shared decision-making about next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and strengthens change talk by prompting commitment, planning (dates, scripts), and rehearsal, which produced concrete change statements and choices from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (guilt, desire to protect the friendship) and pivots to planning, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and deeper exploration of guilt before redirecting.
