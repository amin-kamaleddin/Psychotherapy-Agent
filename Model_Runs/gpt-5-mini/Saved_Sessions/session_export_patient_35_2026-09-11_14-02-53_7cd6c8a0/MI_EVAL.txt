# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveys unconditional positive regard through explicit validation, nonjudgmental language, and acceptance statements while praising Elena's practical efforts to protect her child.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking capture ambivalence and make Elena feel heard, though deeper probing of emotional burden and more complex reflections would strengthen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on reducing the child's smoke exposure and steps toward quitting, moving from ideas to specific, time-limited actions without being prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is collaborative problem-solving: the clinician solicits Elena's ideas, integrates them into a feasible plan, and checks for barriers and backups.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supports choice by offering options, accepting hedged commitments, and reinforcing that decisions belong to Elena, which preserves her sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicits and mobilizes Elena's motivations into concrete steps (patch, reminders, backups), but could probe deeper into underlying values and use scaling or commitment language to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician treats Elena as the expert and co-creates the plan through open invitations and negotiation, demonstrating strong power-sharing and mutual decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits and amplifies change talk and moves Elena to a specific plan, though further reinforcement, summaries of change talk, and elicitation of commitment language would increase potency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward protective behaviors, but could use more explicit double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while highlighting costs to more strategically shift ambivalence.
