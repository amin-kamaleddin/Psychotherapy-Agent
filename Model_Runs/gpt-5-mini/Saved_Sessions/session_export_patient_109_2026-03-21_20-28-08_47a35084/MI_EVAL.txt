# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard with a nonjudgmental opening, normalization, and validation throughout. The psychiatrist avoided blame and respected Rafael's experience and concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High-level reflective listening and accurate understanding were shown through summaries and reflections of barriers, values, and skepticism. Empathic attunement moved the conversation forward while acknowledging Rafael's feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, consistent focus on returning to the gym with every question tied to that behavior. Scaling and concrete, time-bound planning (buddy, backup, reminders) kept the work goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative, framed as joint problem-solving with options offered and decisions co-created. The clinician checked readiness and invited Rafael to choose and draft messages himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported Rafael's sense of choice by offering options, asking preferences, and affirming fallback plans. Language emphasized Rafael's ownership over the plan without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited Rafael's own motivations and resources using open questions, scaling, and drawing out past facilitators. Solutions came from Rafael rather than being prescribed, reflecting strong evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned Rafael as the expert, solicited input, and shared power in decision-making. Choices and phrasing were offered with permission and Rafael's preferences shaped the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation of change talk occurred via scaling, identifying facilitators, and creating a concrete plan, but opportunities to amplify and expand Rafael's own change statements were missed. The clinician could have more explicitly evoked stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled nonconfrontationally with reflections, normalization, and if‑then fallbacks that redirected toward change. Improvement could come from more consistent use of double‑sided reflections and selectively minimizing sustain language while amplifying change reasons.
