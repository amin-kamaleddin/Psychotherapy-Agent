# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, with frequent validation and an early safety check. Accepted the patient's concerns about side effects and offered practical support rather than criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and reflective statements with normalizing language and calming strategies. Some reflections remained at a surface level and were often followed quickly by clinical instructions rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and medication adherence, linking education to specific behavioral targets. Offered concrete thresholds and a practical follow-up plan to guide change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction largely felt like a joint effort with co-writing, role‑play, and shared decision-making. At times the clinician offered a lengthy set of steps without first eliciting which options the patient wanted to prioritize.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respected patient choice, asked permission before interventions, and offered multiple options including second opinion and educator referral. Avoided coercive language and reinforced the patient's right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client-generated motivation (drafting message, willingness to try lowest dose) but relied heavily on clinician-provided solutions. Missed opportunities for evocative questions about values, benefits, or scaling to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently adopted a collaborative stance by asking what the patient wanted help with and offering to co-create plans and role‑play. Could improve by negotiating the session agenda earlier and explicitly eliciting the patient's priorities before presenting options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created multiple opportunities for change talk through planning, drafting messages, and role‑play which elicited willingness to try a low dose. Could have amplified and summarized the patient's change statements more and asked about importance/confidence to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and did not dismiss concerns, but often moved quickly to medical reassurance and directive advice. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or rolling with fear while gently eliciting reasons for change.
