# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language (e.g., reflective validation and offers of support), creating a clearly accepting stance throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries convey steady empathy, though the clinician could deepen this further by exploring the emotional meaning of 'escape' and underlying fear or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear focus on harm reduction and safer injecting, with every prompt tied to concrete actionable steps (script, practice, printed note, needle exchange) that keep momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently operates as an equal partner, inviting co‑construction of the plan, offering help while deferring to Diego's choices, and framing actions as joint efforts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers options and asks Diego's preference at each decision point, avoids pressure, and affirms that Diego controls pace and content of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses evocative open questions to draw out Diego's internal motivation and specific commitments, then builds on that motivation by translating it into immediate, feasible actions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is consistently framed as collaborative with repeated choices and preference checks, demonstrating genuine power‑sharing and respect for the client's expertise about what feels doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk with targeted questions about what feels doable and concrete planning, converting readiness into specific next steps (role‑play, note, exchange).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician skilfully avoids debating sustain talk and redirects to harm‑reduction planning, though could more explicitly use double‑sided reflections to normalize ambivalence while guiding toward change.
