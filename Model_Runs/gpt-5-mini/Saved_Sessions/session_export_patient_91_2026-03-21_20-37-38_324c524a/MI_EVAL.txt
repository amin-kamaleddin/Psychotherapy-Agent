# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth, validating and normalizing the patient's experience. Frequently invited preferences and avoided criticism, creating a safe tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate paraphrase and reflective listening, picking up key concerns and clarifying specifics. Reflections remained pragmatic and task-focused with missed opportunities for deeper emotional or ambivalence-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on blood glucose monitoring and actionable responses, with questions and plan elements tied to the target behavior. Effectively moved the conversation toward concrete next steps and a practical plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Solicited the patient's input at every stage, revised materials to match preferences, and treated the patient as an expert on their routine. Decisions were co‑constructed rather than imposed, demonstrating genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered multiple options, asked permission before intervening, and framed actions as choices the patient could accept or modify. Avoided pressuring language and respected the patient's role in decision‑making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out practical ideas and small commitments (alarms, supplies, scripts) and asked for a next step. Could deepen by eliciting internal motives, values, and using scaling to strengthen intrinsic motivation and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked permission, incorporated the patient's wording, and invited revisions, demonstrating shared power in planning. The clinician checked feasibility and accepted patient edits, reinforcing a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk well, asked for concrete next steps, and amplified small commitments. To improve, more systematic sequencing of desire/reason/ability/confidence language and occasional scaling would strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized worry, and redirected ambivalence toward problem‑solving with practical backups. A few moments moved quickly to solutions without explicitly reflecting sustain‑talk content; double‑sided reflections could further soften sustain talk.
