# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and individualized affirmations, avoids judgmental or confrontational responses. Frequent, specific validations support the client's perspective and readiness to test change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, specific reflective listening and perspective-taking throughout the encounter. Reflections mirror both feelings and concrete details, building rapport and encouraging disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking and a concrete, manageable goal (skipping the first cigarette). Questions and reflections continually return to the target behavior without notable drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—seeks Eric's input at decision points, follows his lead, and co-constructs a realistic plan. Clinician integrates Eric's prior strategies and balances expertise with lived experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by honoring Eric's stance, offering options, and avoiding pressuring language. Repeatedly frames the experiment as voluntary and low-stakes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations, past strategies, and actionable ideas (skip first smoke, gum, alarm), but could probe deeper into personally meaningful values and strength/confidence. Further exploration of values and importance/confidence scaling would deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the session as a shared enterprise and positions Eric as the expert on his life. Uses inclusive language and checks preferences, demonstrating genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces concrete change talk and specific implementation details, producing a feasible experiment. Could more often amplify change statements and elicit stronger commitment or confidence (e.g., scaling or commitment questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and responds nonconfrontationally with empathic reflections, but relies mostly on neutral reflections. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections, reframing, or actively redirecting sustain talk toward discrepancy.
