# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys unconditional positive regard throughout, validating progress and ambivalence without judgment and normalizing Erik’s experience. No shaming or minimization; choices treated as legitimate and reasonable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong reflective listening and perspective-taking about concrete progress and barriers, showing understanding of benefits and concerns. Could deepen emotional reflection (e.g., name shame or discouragement) to reach a fuller empathic level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on regular, doable exercise and concrete implementation steps, with no drift into unrelated topics. Questions and reflections repeatedly narrow toward actionable planning tied to Erik’s values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner by offering options, reflecting, and co-constructing a plan while inviting input. Occasionally leads with clinician-generated strategies that steer the session more than eliciting all ideas from Erik first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, asking Erik to select what feels right, and affirming his preferences without pressure. Repeatedly reinforces Erik’s ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation from Erik’s own values and experiences, reinforcing internally generated reasons for change. Questions draw out specific commitments that Erik then details and owns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and inclusive, asking Erik to choose and refining plans together. Sometimes the clinician’s proposals shape the agenda more than pure shared discovery, modestly limiting equal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk by inviting concrete intentions and detailed planning, leading to multiple spontaneous commitments. Reflects and reinforces Erik’s own reasons and translates motivation into actionable steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and validates sustain talk and avoids arguing, then pivots to practical strategies to reduce its impact. Could use more deliberate softening techniques (double-sided reflections or summaries) to further diminish sustain talk’s influence.
