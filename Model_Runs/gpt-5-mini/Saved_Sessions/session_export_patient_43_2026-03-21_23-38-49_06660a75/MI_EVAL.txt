# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizing tension and avoiding judgement. Language like “I hear you” and explicit acceptance of the patient’s priorities reinforced respect throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections mirrored the patient’s words and priorities, deepening rapport and understanding. The clinician captured feelings (tired of nagging, wanting calm) and practical concerns effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed sharply focused on smoking location/frequency and moved logically from options to commitment and supports. The clinician kept the conversation action-oriented with concrete steps and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed, with the clinician eliciting the patient’s ideas and framing suggestions as joint problem‑solving. The final plan directly reflected the patient’s priorities and input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and framed changes as experiments the patient could try or modify. Offers of realistic backups and invitations to choose preserved the patient’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the patient’s own values (quieter home, preserving the unwind ritual) using open questions and amplification. The clinician avoided external persuasion and anchored change in intrinsic reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as collaborative and deferred to the patient’s expertise about what fits his life. Repeated invitations to choose between options and co-plan supports reinforced shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections skillfully elicited and strengthened change talk, moving the patient from vague ideas to a concrete commitment. Planning prompts and support brainstorming amplified commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and effectively redirected sustain talk toward practical steps, but sometimes echoed the patient’s minimizing language. More strategic use of double-sided reflections or selectively amplifying small change talk could further tip ambivalence toward change.
