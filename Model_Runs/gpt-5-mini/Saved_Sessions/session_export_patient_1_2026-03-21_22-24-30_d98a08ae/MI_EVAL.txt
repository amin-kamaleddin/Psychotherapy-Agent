# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation throughout the session, normalizing Ahmed’s perspective and respecting his values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely reflections that captured both content and meaning, building strong rapport and understanding of Ahmed’s concerns about work and family.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on mini‑cigar use and its impact on throat/work, guiding the conversation from values to concrete, time‑bound action steps without losing a patient‑centered stance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a partnership by asking open questions, eliciting Ahmed’s implementation ideas, and integrating his solutions into the plan rather than prescribing actions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Ahmed’s sense of choice by offering options, asking which felt doable, and honoring his uncertainty while helping him design a fitting plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out intrinsic reasons tied to family and work and linked them to change, but could have probed deeper into personal meaning and long‑term values to strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative—solicited Ahmed’s input at each step and co‑constructed the plan (alarms, not inhaling, rinsing/gum) so the patient’s expertise guided the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete change talk and doable steps, but sometimes moved quickly to planning without amplifying or reflecting the strongest change statements to build momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argument and used double‑sided reflections that honored sustain talk while redirecting toward change, though more explicit exploration and reframing of perceived benefits would further diminish sustain talk.
