# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, normalizing ambivalence and offering support; repeatedly affirms the patient's choices and preferences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong reflective listening and accurate summaries that validate ambivalence, but stays largely at a pragmatic level with few deeper emotion-focused reflections exploring fear or loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol use and consistently steers the conversation toward specific, achievable actions and an implementation plan while balancing empathy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly co-constructed: the clinician invites input, adapts to the patient's preferences, and uses joint problem-solving, positioning themselves as a helpful partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly emphasizes choice and preserves the patient's control by offering multiple options and asking which approach she prefers, avoiding coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivations and resources and prompts client-generated change talk, but much of the motivation is clinician-prompted and could be deepened by probing personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance throughout, offering options and deferring to the patient so decisions are made together, reinforcing shared problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies multiple change statements and reinforces commitment with focused questions and concrete planning to strengthen intention.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and skillfully reframes ambivalence with double-sided reflections, though there were missed opportunities to explicitly name minimizing statements alongside consequences.
