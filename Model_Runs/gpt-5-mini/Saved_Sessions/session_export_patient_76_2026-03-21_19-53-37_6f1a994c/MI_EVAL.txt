# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation (e.g., opening support statements and repeated affirmations), avoiding blaming or minimizing language throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections of emotion and physical sensations, tracked urge changes, and offered succinct validations that demonstrated strong empathic understanding of the patient's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on self-harm and immediate safety, repeatedly tying interventions to concrete tasks and safety thresholds without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative—invited co‑construction of the safety plan and offered to stay—though the clinician at times shifted to more directive coaching and scripted suggestions for safety.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported the patient's choice and control by offering options, asking permission, and affirming decisions, avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited several behavioral commitments and tracked progress but did not sufficiently explore internal motivations, values, or long‑term reasons for change with evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and invited input on the plan, but often led the agenda with specific suggestions that reduced some client ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced small instances of change talk and praised urge reductions, but did not systematically elicit the patient's own reasons, values, or hopes for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk nonjudgmentally by acknowledging it and pivoting to alternatives and safety planning, though missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections to strategically name ambivalence.
