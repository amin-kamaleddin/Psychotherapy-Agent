# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, explicitly invited disclosure and normalized the patient’s distress, conveying strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and validation that likely reduced shame, but reflections were often high-level and quickly followed by solutions rather than deeper, complex reflections of emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on weight, eating/exercise patterns, and safety, moving logically from assessment to concrete next steps and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solicited the teenager’s perspective and offered choices and drafts, but frequently shifted into an expert-advice stance with directive instructions instead of co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered options and avoided coercion, but autonomy was undermined at times by prescriptive lists; prefacing suggestions with permission and inviting choice would strengthen this area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some efforts to draw out the patient’s perspective (typical day, goal, tracking preferences) but the clinician mainly offered solutions and did not systematically elicit motivations, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and choices which supported partnership, yet frequent unsolicited recommendations reduced the sense that solutions were being co-created with the adolescent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked about goals and affirmed efforts, producing modest change talk, but moved quickly to offering solutions instead of eliciting, amplifying, or strengthening the patient’s own motivation and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalized plateaus, offered lower-threat alternatives, and assessed safety nonjudgmentally, though occasional rapid medical/behavioral instructions risked validating sustain talk; more double-sided reflections would help.
