# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, with repeated validation and respectful language that created a safe environment. No minimizing or critical statements were observed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good perspective-taking and practical reflections, validating concerns and time pressure. Reflections were more informational than deeply emotional and the patient's anxiety was not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, focused agenda on safe medication switching with accurate, actionable information and repeated summaries of the plan. Maintained goal-directedness despite time constraints.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative and framed as joint problem-solving, with co‑created wording and tailored materials. The clinician invited input and adjusted plans to the patient’s needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported patient choice by offering options, asking preferences, and avoiding coercion. Language reinforced ownership and allowed the patient to decide next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions invited the patient's perspective, but the clinician largely provided solutions rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations, importance, or confidence. Missed opportunities to draw out intrinsic reasons and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a strong partnership through inclusive language, shared decision-making, and honoring the patient’s preferences. The clinician demonstrated genuine power‑sharing in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change-related statements (plans to show note, follow washout), but did not intentionally probe reasons, readiness, or confidence to amplify change talk. Could have asked follow-up questions to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and reframed caution as reasonable while offering practical alternatives given time limits. Opportunities were missed to use double‑sided reflections to explicitly address ambivalence.
