# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated nonjudgmental support and respect, using validating language and calm safety checks without blame or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, timely reflections and paraphrases matched the patient's affect and conveyed understanding, helping maintain engagement despite low energy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear focus on addressing bills and developed a concrete, stepwise plan with repeated summaries that maintained momentum toward specific behavioral tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was a true partnered problem‑solving effort—role‑plays, co‑written emails, and joint planning reflected genuine two‑way collaboration driven by the patient’s choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were offered rather than imposed, the clinician honored hesitance and preferences, and affirmed the patient's ownership of decisions without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some internal motivation and reinforced small change talk but often supplied solutions instead of using more evocative open questions or scaling to draw out deeper client‑generated reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** High‑quality partnership was evident through invitations for input, deferring to the patient’s sequence of actions, and co‑constructing scripts and follow‑up plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several pieces of change talk were elicited and supported, but the clinician could have amplified and deepened them with more reflections, summaries, and questions tying talk to personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validated futility and offered low‑burden options to counter hopelessness, but missed opportunities for double‑sided reflections and sometimes moved to problem‑solving before resolving ambivalence.
