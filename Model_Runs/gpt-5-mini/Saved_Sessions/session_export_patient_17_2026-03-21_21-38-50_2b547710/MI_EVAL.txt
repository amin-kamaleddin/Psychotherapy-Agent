# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed warm, nonjudgmental regard, normalized struggles and slips, and explicitly honored Anisa's difficult circumstances. This demonstrated clear unconditional positive regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflective listening captured both content and affect (shame, stuckness, determination) and the clinician validated Anisa's emotions. These empathic moves were sustained across the interaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking cessation using scaling, eliciting what would move her toward action, a concrete week plan, and a scheduled follow-up. These actions provided consistent direction toward behavior change while staying person-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the interaction as joint problem-solving, invited Anisa's preferences, and integrated her input into the pocket card and follow-up plan. Information and recommendations were shared consultatively and with permission.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported Anisa's control and choice by asking permission, offering options, and respecting her preferences (e.g., lozenge vs gum, text reminder). Normalizing slips and framing options without coercion reinforced autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Anisa's own motivations and resources with open questions, scaling probes, and by building the plan from her stated reasons for change. Motivation was consistently drawn from Anisa's perspective rather than being prescribed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician treated Anisa as an equal partner, co-creating the week plan and asking for her input on reminders and wording. Power-sharing was evident throughout decision points.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and reinforced change talk with evocative questions and scaling, affirmed specific steps Anisa offered, and turned them into a concrete, co-created plan. This consistently encouraged language favoring change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk, acknowledged barriers and offered problem-solving, and normalized slips to reduce self-judgment. There were missed opportunities for more deliberate MI moves (e.g., double-sided reflections or explicitly eliciting the costs of not changing) to further soften sustain talk.
