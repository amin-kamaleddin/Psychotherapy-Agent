# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence without judgment, acknowledging the patient's ideas and choices. No admonishment or shaming language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated accurate, frequent empathic reflections that captured the patient's internal conflict and practical concerns. Questions invited the patient's perspective and validated her realistic confidence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on reducing vaping during basketball season with concrete, actionable steps (alarms, peer check‑ins, leaving pen at home). The clinician consistently steered from brainstorming to a specific implementation plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative, with the clinician soliciting and building on the patient's ideas and co‑constructing a tailored plan. Minimal structure was added only to support the patient's solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and the patient's agency, asking which options felt easiest and what would move confidence higher. There was no coercive or directive pressure, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician elicited the patient's own motivations and resources, drawing out intrinsic reasons to change and past successes (the Maya routine). Open questions required the patient to generate solutions and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician framed the work as a joint enterprise and shared decision‑making, using inclusive language and inviting the patient to choose and shape the plan. The patient's plan clearly guided the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change talk through choices, confidence ratings, and summaries, strengthening desire and ability language. To improve, they could more often reflect change statements verbatim and invite an explicit commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and steered toward solutions rather than confronting resistance. Opportunities were missed for double‑sided reflections or explicit acknowledgment of vaping's positive functions to more gently resolve ambivalence.
