# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with normalizing statements and affirmations that validated the patient's feelings and decisions; no dismissive or blaming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate, timely empathic reflections that acknowledged emotions and specific fears, and paired understanding with practical reassurance and safety steps.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and lifestyle change with concrete, action-oriented planning (one-week plan, baseline tests, follow-up).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the interaction as a joint effort by soliciting preferences, integrating the patient's values, and offering shared follow-up and supports like involving the wife.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported patient choice by offering options (dose/start choices, planning help) and reinforcing that decisions and plans were up to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out the patient's motivations and linked them to values, but at times reverted to giving information rather than using evocative questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and inviting of input, though the clinician sometimes led with educational content and specific prescriptions instead of co-constructing solutions from the patient's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (plans, willingness) but could have more strategically amplified and reflected the patient's own change statements to elicit stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected to safety planning and small steps, though could use more double-sided reflections or explicit summaries of ambivalence to help the patient weigh options.
