# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with warm, validating language and no judgment. Acceptance is thorough throughout opening, reflections, and planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and reflective listening, validating disappointment and resilience. Could deepen affective reflection and explore the emotional impact of missed opportunities more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, persistent focus on securing work while managing childcare and moves from assessment to concrete action steps. Keeps conversation tightly linked to practical barriers and solutions without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a genuine two-way collaboration, with the clinician inviting patient input, adjusting language, and role-playing as a partner. The clinician honors patient edits and readiness, co-creating the plan together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's sense of choice by offering options and asking permission before offering help. Language reinforces that the patient retains control and there is no pressuring or directive insistence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation and resources through strength-highlight and concrete steps, prompting commitment-oriented responses. Missed opportunities to ask deeper evocative questions (e.g., probing what makes confidence a 10) to tie motivation to personal reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters a strong collaborative stance using inclusive language and regularly inviting the patient's input. Power is clearly shared and the patient meaningfully directs choices and pacing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change-oriented language through strengths, options, and asking which step feels doable, successfully drawing out commitment. Could further amplify change talk by probing reasons, needs, and specifics behind the patient's high confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and quickly pivots to practical problem solving, which prevents amplifying barriers. However, there is limited use of MI strategies like double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence acknowledgment to gently reframe sustain talk.
