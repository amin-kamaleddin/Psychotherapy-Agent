# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming—validates frustration and praises concrete efforts throughout the exchange. Communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes difficulties without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate perspective-taking with frequent, precise reflections that capture both content and affect. Names feelings (frustration, urgency) and tracks the patient's language, showing strong reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on attending job interviews and moves logically from barriers to specific, small, testable actions. Repeatedly narrows options and secures a concrete, manageable rehearsal plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Co-constructs the rehearsal plan and invites the patient's ideas to shape decisions, negotiating timing, offers, and scripts together. The interaction reads as a reciprocal, co‑creative process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers options, asks permission, and avoids pressuring language so the patient selects the steps to try. Honors the patient's control and choices at every turn.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits logistical motivators and builds on the patient's ideas, but rarely probes deeper values or longer-term reasons for employment. Missed a brief opportunity to explore what the job would mean for her and her family.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as a collaboration and defers to the patient's expertise about family logistics, asking which option feels most realistic. Offers to practice only with permission and follows the patient's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits, reinforces, and amplifies change talk through open questions, reflections, and summaries that highlight commitment. Translates the patient's '10' desire into concrete planning and actionable steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk and pivots to problem-solving, but often repeats barriers verbatim before moving on. Could further soften sustain talk with more double‑sided reflections and strategic summaries that contrast desire and obstacles.
