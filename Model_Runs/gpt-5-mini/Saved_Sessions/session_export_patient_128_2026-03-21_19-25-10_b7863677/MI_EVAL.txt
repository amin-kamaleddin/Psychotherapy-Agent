# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently used a supportive, nonjudgmental stance and validated Tyler’s feelings, demonstrating unconditional positive regard; one mildly sharper phrase briefly challenged the patient but did not undermine overall respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic reflections and safety checks that validated ambivalence and concerns about school, but reflections were mostly content-level and could probe deeper feelings like shame or fear for greater precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on alcohol-related consequences and actionable goals (protecting classes, preparing for the hearing, harm-reduction steps) with targeted questions and roleplay to move toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative: the clinician co-created the conduct statement, solicited preferences, and adapted wording and plans to match Tyler’s voice rather than imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly offered choices, asked permission before interventions, and respected Tyler’s preferences (e.g., wanting to keep friends), reinforcing that decisions belong to him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited Tyler’s reasons for change and used roleplay to draw out commitments, but often moved quickly into providing drafts and strategies instead of first amplifying Tyler’s own motivations and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong sense of partnership with shared problem-solving, invitations to roleplay, and mutual decision-making about wording and plans, showing genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and supported change-oriented statements and concrete steps, but could more consistently reflect and amplify tentative change talk and link values (education) to specific commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and redirected sustain talk toward specifics and harm reduction, though there were missed opportunities to explicitly reflect social pulls and use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk further.
