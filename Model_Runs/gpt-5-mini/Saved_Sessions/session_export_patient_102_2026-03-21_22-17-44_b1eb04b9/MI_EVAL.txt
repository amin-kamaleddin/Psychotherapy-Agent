# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician expresses consistent unconditional positive regard and normalizes Milan’s perspective without moralizing. Concerns are framed in terms of Milan’s own values (college, team), reinforcing a nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate empathic listening with reflections that capture both content and feeling (social belonging, fear of embarrassment). Responses follow with open questions that show understanding rather than correction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear focus on reducing alcohol use, with questions and exercises consistently tied to cutting back. The clinician balances open exploration with purposeful steering toward a realistic plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a genuine team effort, inviting Milan to set the agenda and choosing which skills to practice. Inclusive language and negotiated pacing signal equal partnership rather than an expert lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician explicitly supports autonomy, offers concrete choices (practice lines, texts, role‑play), and avoids pressuring language. Milan’s right to choose is reinforced by acknowledging he isn’t promising anything.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits Milan’s own motivations, resources, and past successes (e.g., dry period during finals) instead of supplying reasons for change. Clinician’s questions draw out client-generated strategies and builds on them with rehearsal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly invites Milan’s input and co-constructs decisions (e.g., choosing one sober weekend/month and a check‑in person). Offers options and supports rather than prescribing a single course of action.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies change talk using scaling questions, evocative prompts, and highlighting past successes to strengthen ability language. Invites specific, doable steps and affirms Milan’s change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and uses reflective, double-sided approaches to validate concerns while gently redirecting toward change planning. Exploration of potential consequences is nonconfrontational and guides movement toward action.
