# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows unconditional positive regard, normalizes Isabella's nerves, and honors her pace without judgment. Offers small steps and options rather than pressuring change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures preferences and ambivalence (e.g., safety vs. wanting connection). Could include deeper, more complex reflections about underlying emotions or past influences to reach a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on the concrete target behavior (increasing low‑pressure contact) with readiness scaling, specific barriers/supports, and a clear timeline. The clinician repeatedly returns to actionable next steps and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly collaborative, soliciting Isabella's input at each step and offering to help in ways she prefers (draft, role‑play, be Anna). Frequent invitations for choice shaped the plan and reflected shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by providing options, asking permission, and framing actions as optional and up to Isabella. Avoids pressuring language and emphasizes her control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out motivations, coping ideas, and readiness steps while eliciting personally fitting supports and rehearsals. Could further amplify personally meaningful reasons for connection and strengthen change language to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fosters a partnership by inviting choices and input and offering help collaboratively. At times the clinician provided fairly detailed scripted texts which slightly shifted control away from co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses readiness scaling, a menu of small steps, and prompts to elicit and strengthen change talk effectively. Missed some opportunities to consistently reflect and amplify tentative change language and link it to values/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and does not reinforce sustain talk but rarely uses double‑sided reflections or explicitly validates reasons for staying home before moving to planning. More strategic reflections of sustain reasons could help resolve ambivalence.
