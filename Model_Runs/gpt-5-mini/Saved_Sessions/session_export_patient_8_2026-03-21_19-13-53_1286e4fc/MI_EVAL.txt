# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician is consistently nonjudgmental and supportive, normalizing ambivalence and affirming Amina's choices, which creates a safe, positive atmosphere for disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic listening with accurate reflections of sleep, focus, and grades, but misses some opportunities to probe deeper emotional nuance and meaning behind ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing problematic phone use and links all suggestions (DND, Pomodoro, alarms) to that behavioral goal, ending with a concrete checklist and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as joint problem-solving—asks permission, offers options, and co-creates a checklist—but often supplies technical solutions before eliciting Amina's own strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports client choice by asking what feels doable, offering help rather than imposing solutions, and framing slips as data to preserve ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits small commitments and doable steps but frequently shifts to advising and technical instruction instead of systematically drawing out Amina's reasons, values, confidence, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance by inviting input and offering assistance, yet the clinician often leads with solutions rather than co-generating alternatives with Amina.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective prompts and affirmations produced commitment to concrete actions, but the clinician underuses questions to amplify Amina's own change language and long-term reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about connection and relaxation is acknowledged but not validated or reframed; the clinician moves quickly to problem-solve instead of using double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk.
