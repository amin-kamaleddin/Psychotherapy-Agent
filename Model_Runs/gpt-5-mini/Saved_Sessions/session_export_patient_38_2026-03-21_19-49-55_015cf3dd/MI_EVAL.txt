# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone throughout, using validating language and normalizing Elena's mixed feelings. This warmth and respect were consistently conveyed in phrasing and safety checks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were clear empathic reflections and accurate summaries that likely helped Elena feel heard, including normalizing her anxiety and shutdown. However, opportunities to deepen exploration of the emotional nuance (e.g., what 'shutting down' feels like or specific fears) were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on contraception and the pregnancy-scare problem, with all questions and plans tied to decision-making and safety. The clinician consistently oriented the conversation toward concrete next steps and follow-ups.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was a joint enterprise: the clinician solicited input, offered to co-write scripts, and tailored recommendations to Elena's concerns. At times the clinician shifted into an educator role with dense information without explicitly inviting Elena's interpretation, which reduced mutual exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly honored Elena's choice and control—asking permission, offering low-commitment trials, and emphasizing she can stop a pill anytime. Options were presented without pressure and decisions were left to Elena's timing and comfort.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation occurred (asking what makes deciding hard, inviting choices, drafting language), but the clinician relied heavily on information and scripting rather than systematically drawing out Elena's own reasons, values, or confidence. Evocation was present but not consistently applied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician adopted a collaborative stance, co-creating plans and checking before giving details, and offering to help draft language. Occasionally the clinician moved into a more expert/informational role without pausing to elicit Elena's preferences, limiting full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change statements and offered practical supports, but did not consistently follow up to amplify or explore Elena's expressed motivations. Much of the interaction shifted to information-giving and scripting rather than deliberately strengthening commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not confronted, with normalization that avoided escalation. However, there were few strategic attempts (e.g., double-sided or shifting reflections) to pivot sustain talk toward change or resolve ambivalence more actively.
