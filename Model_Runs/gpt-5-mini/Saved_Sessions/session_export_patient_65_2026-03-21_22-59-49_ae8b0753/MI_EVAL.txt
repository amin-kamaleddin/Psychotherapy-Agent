# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed nonjudgmental regard and normalized Julian’s feelings, validating anxiety without blame and supporting engagement. This warm acceptance lowered defensiveness and facilitated cooperation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and summaries demonstrated perspective-taking and frequent paraphrasing of Julian’s experience. Several empathic phrases were somewhat formulaic and the clinician did not probe deeper into underlying emotions like shame or past experiences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on completing community service hours and on a concrete first step (sending a one‑sentence text and setting an alarm). The clinician repeatedly steered the conversation back to logistical planning and immediate implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly collaborative: the clinician drafted the text with Julian, solicited edits, and adjusted the plan based on his preferences. Decision-making and planning were co‑constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently honored Julian’s choices by offering options, asking which felt doable, and only offering help when invited. Phrasing like “Which of those sounds doable?” reinforced the patient’s sense of control throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited specific intentions and implementation details (preference for texting, timing, alarm), strengthening action-oriented motivation. Evocation remained focused on logistics and consequences rather than eliciting internal motives or personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician positioned Julian as an active participant by offering choices and inviting wording preferences, accepting his edits. At times the clinician proposed wording before eliciting Julian’s own phrasing, which reduced opportunities for earlier ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicited and amplified change‑leaning language, turning vague willingness into a specific plan with timeline and reminders. Further exploration of internal motives or values would have deepened and broadened change talk beyond logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and acknowledged reluctance, preventing escalation of resistance. However, they did not actively employ techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or pros/cons exploration) to resolve or reframe lingering sustain talk.
