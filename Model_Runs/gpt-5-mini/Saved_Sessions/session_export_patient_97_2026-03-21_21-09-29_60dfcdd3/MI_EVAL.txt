# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth throughout the session, validating Mateo's experience and affirming his efforts. The clinician avoided labeling and offered options rather than directives, demonstrating steady acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with accurate reflections and emotional attunement to Mateo's ambivalence and goals. Could deepen engagement with more complex or affect-labeled reflections to draw out underlying pain or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on concrete actions toward employment and reentry, guiding Mateo to specific steps, timing, and contingency plans. Balanced exploration of ambivalence with progressive momentum toward a workable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was an equal, co-constructed exchange using inclusive language and frequent invitations for Mateo's input. The clinician jointly designed the plan, scripts, and backups, reflecting strong collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported Mateo's sense of choice by offering options, asking permission, and accepting hesitance without pressure. Repeatedly emphasized that actions were Mateo's decision, reinforcing autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited Mateo's motivations (family, stability) and used scaling to draw out internal reasons for change. Could further amplify values-consistent motives and elicit stronger verbal commitments to build momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the work as a partnership, asking permission and letting Mateo choose supports, times, and follow-up. Power-sharing was evident in offering options and shaping the plan together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk with scaling, vision questions, and prompting specific, doable steps that produced actionable language. Could amplify and summarize change statements more aggressively and elicit firmer commitment beyond 'I'll try.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and doubt but did not consistently use targeted techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring the function of sustain talk) to soften it. The clinician often moved quickly into planning without fully shifting sustain language.
