# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental tone (e.g., normalizing ambivalence and praising small steps). Validation language and acceptance of uncertainty supported the client's experience and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with multiple attuned reflections and summaries capturing both emotion and ambivalence. Clarifying, empathic questions reinforced understanding of the client's stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on reducing alcohol-related risk through a specific, measurable trial (one sober night with a check-in). Every turn tied back to actionable steps and kept momentum toward the behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged the client as an equal partner by brainstorming, offering options, and co-creating the plan (including drafting the text together). Decisions were led by the client's preferences and feasibility concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking permission before suggesting, and avoiding pressure. Language reinforced the client's right to accept or decline and emphasized personal decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited practical reasons and some intrinsic motivation by linking social values to academic concerns and asking what would help sustain the step. Could deepen evocation by drawing out richer value-based meaning, past successes, or confidence/importance ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated a strong collaborative stance with real co-construction of the plan and shared decision-making. The clinician repeatedly invited the client's input and respected her expertise on feasibility.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change-oriented language, producing concrete commitments (asking Leah, plan for one sober night). Could be more strategic in amplifying and chaining change talk (e.g., confidence/importance scaling and reflective amplification).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and pivoted to low-risk plans, acknowledging doubts without escalation. Opportunities remained to use more double-sided reflections or link hesitancy to past successes to further reduce sustain language.
