# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated clear unconditional positive regard and a consistently nonjudgmental, validating tone that normalized ambivalence and avoided lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections captured both facts and feelings, showing strong perspective-taking and sustained reflective listening throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on behavior linked to smoking and produced a concrete plan (three walks), but could more explicitly link the plan to long-term smoking cessation goals and tobacco-specific follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed: the clinician solicited Marco's ideas and integrated his preferences into the plan, demonstrating shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician repeatedly emphasized choice and pacing, avoided pressure, and reinforced that any change is Marco's decision, supporting autonomy strongly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician drew out past successes and elicited concrete commitments, but could further elicit personally meaningful reasons for reducing smoking and use rulers or summaries to amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language invited collaboration and treated Marco as the expert on his life, co-creating realistic steps rather than prescribing a plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited preparatory and some commitment language (past successes and specific walks), yet missed opportunities to more directly elicit desire/ability/reasons/confidence about smoking reduction.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician rolled with resistance, validated sustain talk, and reframed toward small changes; additional double-sided reflections and strategic exploration of costs of staying the same would strengthen this further.
