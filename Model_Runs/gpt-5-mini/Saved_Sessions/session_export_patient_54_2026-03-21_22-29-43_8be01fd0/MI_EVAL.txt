# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard by validating the family ritual and normalizing ambivalence without judgment. The clinician repeatedly affirmed the patient's priorities and choices throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated highly accurate, responsive understanding with complex reflections that captured both feelings and values. Reflections deepened the patient's elaboration and made her feel heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, behaviorally specific focus on pausing wine and guided the conversation toward concrete, achievable steps. Repeatedly narrowed options and checked priorities to keep momentum toward a plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly two-way and co-constructed, offering tools and participation while deferring final decisions to the patient. Questions invited input and the clinician integrated patient responses into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported the patient's right to choose by inviting her to set start dates, success criteria, and terms for the trial. The clinician framed options to preserve her ownership and avoided coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew motivation from the patient using evocative questions that prompted her to articulate values and concrete reasons for change. The patient progressed from ambivalence to concrete preparatory change talk and plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance, positioning the patient as the expert on her life and offering to co-create materials and role-play. Language consistently invited her ownership and participation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk with open questions and reflections that brought out desire, reasons, and preparatory commitment. The patient moved to a concrete one-month trial and specific supports.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and offered neutral resources, which is MI-consistent, but missed some opportunities for explicit double-sided reflections and targeted summarizing of ambivalence. Could more strategically name both sides to further soften sustain talk.
