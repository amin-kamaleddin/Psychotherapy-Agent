# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, using validating language and reassurance. Avoided labeling or criticism and normalized the client's experience throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good reflective listening and occasional deeper empathy (e.g., reflections about being torn and responses in role‑play). At times the clinician shifted quickly to problem‑solving instead of exploring the client's guilt and self‑judgment more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the target behavior with concrete steps (deadline, scripts, role‑plays, viewing plan). Balanced exploration with forward movement toward an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as a collaborative problem‑solving partner, co‑creating tasks, schedules, messages, and checklists with the client. Asked about preferences and shared decisions rather than dictating next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supported client autonomy from the outset by offering choices, asking permission, and avoiding pressuring language. Reinforced the client's sense of control over decisions and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation with questions about ideal follow‑through and used a confidence ruler to surface readiness, leading to concrete commitments. Missed some opportunities to link change to deeper values or to further expand the client's intrinsic reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered partnership and power‑sharing through repeated offers of choice, permission‑seeking, and co‑creation of the action plan. Treated the client as the expert on his life and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk via planning questions, scripts, and the confidence scale, supporting movement toward commitment. Could more often amplify and reflect the client's own change statements to strengthen desire/ability/need language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk with double‑sided framing and normalization and redirected toward problem‑solving without reinforcing reluctance. Would benefit from more frequent strategic double‑sided reflections to gently erode guilt and sustain‑oriented arguments.
