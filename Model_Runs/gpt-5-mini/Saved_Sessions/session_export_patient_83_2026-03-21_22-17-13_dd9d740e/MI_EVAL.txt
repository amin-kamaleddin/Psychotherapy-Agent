# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validated Mai’s concerns without judgment, normalizing confusion and affirming her choices. No dismissive or corrective language was used.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and accurate paraphrasing of worries and goals, with occasional reassurance. Could deepen emotional nuance with more complex or amplified reflections to fully capture feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, appropriate focus on understanding and safely starting the statin and progressed logically to concrete adherence steps. Resisted topic drift and consistently tied discussion back to Mai’s stated concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicited Mai’s input and worked from her suggested script and plans, framing the interaction as a team effort. Patient contributions directly shaped the plan, showing high-quality collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices and asked permission before providing information or setting reminders, reinforcing Mai’s control over decisions. No coercive language appeared and conditional plans respected her preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited concrete motivations and commitments (nightly plan, calling Dr. Ferris, switching grapefruit), and reinforced those commitments with planning. Could use more open-ended, evocative questions to draw out deeper personal reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered shared decision-making by co-creating scripts, checklists, and reminders and polishing Mai’s wording rather than replacing it. Demonstrated genuine power-sharing throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully encouraged multiple change statements and operationalized them into concrete steps (checklist, pharmacy script, reminders). Could further strengthen change talk by eliciting more personal importance and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, normalized uncertainty, and redirected skepticism into neutral information and collaborative planning. Using more explicit double-sided reflections could better acknowledge ambivalence while tipping toward change.
