# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgmental regard (apologized for wait, validated frustrations) and normalized constraints, demonstrating unconditional positive regard throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking to summarize concerns and priorities, though could probe emotional content more deeply and use more complex reflections to access feelings and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on target behaviors and moved the conversation to a concrete, time‑sensitive plan with safety boundaries, avoiding drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem-solving and co-created the plan with the patient, offering options and adapting recommendations to fit the patient's constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, asking preferences about follow‑up, and honoring the patient's request for brief responses and limited messages.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited internal motivations and linked change to valued goals (protecting work/health), but could further probe desire, reasons, need, confidence, and strengths to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered power-sharing by inviting the patient's expertise, asking what support would help, and implementing the patient's choices rather than imposing a plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with open questions and affirmations and offered doable options, but could amplify the patient's own motivations and confidence language more deliberately.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated sustain talk about time constraints and redirected to low‑effort alternatives without arguing, though could employ more strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to help resolve ambivalence.
