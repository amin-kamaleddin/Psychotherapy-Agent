# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, using affirmations and respectful language without blaming or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and validated feelings, but could deepen emotional exploration (e.g., probe guilt, frustration, or the protective role of humor).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on medication adherence, set concrete behavioral steps, and arranged a specific follow-up time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was strongly collaborative with negotiated steps and invitations for input, though the clinician could solicit the patient's ideas before offering strategy lists.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored choice and control with statements like 'you get to decide,' offered options rather than directives, and emphasized nonjudgmental noticing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some behavioral commitment and noticing but did not sufficiently draw out intrinsic reasons, values, or hopes that would build longer-term motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance and co-constructed plans, yet could strengthen power-sharing by inviting the patient to set the agenda more often.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and encouraged small change talk (opening bottle, moving a pill) but missed opportunities to probe internal reasons, desires, or amplify hints of concern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively rolled with resistance and reframed goals into smaller steps without arguing, though could use more double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain reasons while nudging toward change.
