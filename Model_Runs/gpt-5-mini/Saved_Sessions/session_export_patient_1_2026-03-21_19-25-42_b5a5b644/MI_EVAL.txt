# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, offering options and validating the patient's choices. Tone sometimes feels functional and informational rather than warm and individualized, with few explicit affirmations of strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate perspective-taking and concise reflections, and information is tailored to the patient's preferences. Limited exploration of the patient's emotions, values, or deeper reasons for smoking beyond cognitive reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on mini‑cigar use, risks, harm‑reduction, and concrete monitoring/action steps throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks about patient preferences, adapts information, and offers practical options like checklists and links. However, the clinician still largely drives the content with statistics and prescriptive recommendations rather than fully co‑creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices, respects refusals (no meds/quitline), and tailors information to the patient's requests, consistently reinforcing that change is the patient's decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session is primarily informational with limited elicitation of the patient's own motivations or values. Missed opportunities to use open, evocative questions to draw out and develop change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites preferences and offers choices, showing some collaborative elements. The interaction leans toward an expert/educator stance rather than fully co‑designing monitoring or harm‑reduction steps with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Creates openings for change by offering options and asking which the patient wants, and the patient hints at cutting back. Does not systematically elicit, reflect, or amplify that tentative change talk when it appears.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges minimization and avoids confrontation but relies on corrective facts that may act as counterarguments. Limited use of MI strategies like double‑sided reflections or rolling with resistance to soften sustain talk.
