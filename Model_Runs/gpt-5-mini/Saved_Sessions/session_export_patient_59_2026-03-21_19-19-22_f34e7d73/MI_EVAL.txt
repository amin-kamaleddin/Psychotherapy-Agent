# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive from opening to close, normalizing feelings and affirming responsible behavior. No shaming or punitive language and acceptance of the client's stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and safety checks that showed attunement to Jamal's state. Reflections stayed at a practical/surface level and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions, ambivalence, or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, appropriate focus on the target behavior with concrete goals, scripts, rehearsals, and a concise plan and follow-up. Minimal topic drift and strong steering toward actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed tasks as joint, solicited preferences, and adjusted to the client's style, conveying collaboration. At times the clinician became more directive when offering concrete scripts and instructions, though usually with permission.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering multiple options, asking permission, and respecting the client's desire for brevity. Reinforced client ownership throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited brief statements about worries and readiness but primarily supplied tools and language rather than drawing out internal motivations, values, or future-oriented reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited input and co‑constructed tasks which communicated shared control. Frequent provision of scripted language sometimes made the interaction more clinician-led despite being offered with permission.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reinforced compliance-oriented change talk and rehearsals, but did not systematically elicit Jamal's internal reasons, values, or questions to amplify desire, ability, reasons, or need. Change talk was present but pragmatic and limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and rolled with sustain talk, redirecting to consequences and problem‑solving which helped soften resistance. Missed some opportunities for double‑sided reflections to more actively shift sustain talk toward change.
