# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, validating the patient's perspective and avoiding blame. Language like “You’re the expert on your life” reinforced respect for the patient’s choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent empathic reflections and paraphrases matched the patient's minimal, defensive style and kept the door open for disclosure. Summaries captured both content and feeling without pressuring the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear, immediate target behavior—creating one hour of privacy—and the clinician steadily steered toward concrete planning. Momentum was maintained with specific steps (sign wording, placement, headphones, contingency plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interactions were clearly co-constructed: the clinician elicited the patient's ideas, offered limited options, and invited choice and refinement. The tone and process conveyed equal partnership rather than an expert-driven lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strongly supported the patient's sense of choice and control by deferring to her preferences and offering planning only if she wanted it. Statements honoring limits and asking which option she would try reinforced autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions consistently drew out the patient’s own motivations, strategies, and concrete plans (sign wording, headphones, leaving if escalated). The clinician evoked internally generated reasons and solutions rather than imposing them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the work as collaborative, inviting co-creation of a low‑drama plan and sharing decision-making power. The patient proposed the visual sign and the clinician helped refine it rather than dictating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively elicited and deepened change talk by asking for specific, doable steps, realism checks, and what success would look like, which produced clear client change language. Follow-ups amplified the patient’s commitment to try the plan once.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and normalized the patient’s skepticism while redirecting to small experiments. There were missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or selectively exploring the costs of the status quo to more actively reduce sustain talk.
