# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental stance, repeatedly validating the client's perspective and avoiding labeling or moralizing language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening and accurate reflections captured ambivalence and stress, but the clinician stayed mostly at content/planning level and missed opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear focus on risky drinking and practical harm-reduction steps, culminating in a concrete Party Smart Plan and follow-up arrangement to keep momentum toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative approach: the clinician co-created the plan, invited preferences, practiced scripts with the client, and deferred to the client's knowledge of his social context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician actively supported choice and control by offering options, acknowledging the client's readiness level, avoiding pressure, and asking which strategies felt doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of motivation occurred (e.g., linking drinking to education), but the clinician relied more on offering strategies than systematically drawing out the client's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong partnership evidenced by joint decision-making, asking which elements to prioritize, and collaboratively drafting and rehearsing the harm-reduction plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were moments of change talk and invitation, but the clinician did not strategically follow up to amplify or explore importance/confidence, instead moving quickly to solutions and advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and effectively redirected sustain talk toward harm-reduction options, though could have used more strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, selective emphasis of costs) to further diminish sustain talk.
