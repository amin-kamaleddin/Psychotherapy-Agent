# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician demonstrated consistent, nonjudgmental regard and frequent validation, affirming the patient's ideas without criticism. Tone was warm and supportive with no signs of labeling or dismissiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening captured both content and feeling and used the patient's language to move the conversation forward. Questions built on the patient's statements rather than introducing assumptions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, concrete focus on scheduling hangouts earlier to protect homework/time and tied each question/reflection to implementation steps. The clinician kept the discussion goal-directed while remaining patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as a partnership between clinician and patient, with the clinician soliciting ideas and inviting patient preferences. Plans and options were jointly developed, showing shared responsibility for next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options and asking which approach the patient preferred. Language affirmed the patient's control and avoided pressuring or prescribing a single course.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections drew out practical intentions and some internal motivation, but the session emphasized logistics over exploring deeper values. Could be strengthened by eliciting importance/confidence and firmer commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the interaction as collaborative, deferring to the patient's expertise and inviting preferences at each step. Decisions and plans were co-constructed with clear patient engagement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk with targeted questions and affirmations, producing concrete change intentions. Improvement could come from more systematically deepening change talk (importance/confidence, past successes, and commitment language).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validated sustain concerns and avoided amplifying resistance, but primarily normalized ambivalence rather than strategically shifting it. Opportunities were missed to use double-sided reflections or problem-solving to reframe sustain talk toward change.
