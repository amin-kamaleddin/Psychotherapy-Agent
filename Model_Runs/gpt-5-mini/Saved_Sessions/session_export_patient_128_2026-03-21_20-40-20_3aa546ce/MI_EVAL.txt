# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, normalizing ambivalence and avoiding blame. Validations and affirmations supported a warm, supportive stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate perspective-taking with timely reflections and summaries that captured both content and emotion. Frequent paraphrasing helped Tyler feel understood and kept him engaged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on risky drinking and avoiding academic/legal consequences throughout the session. Guided conversation toward concrete planning and specific action steps without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative interaction with co-creation of the plan; clinician solicited Tyler's ideas and invited personalization. Followed Tyler's lead and balanced input to reinforce an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and avoided coercion by asking permission and offering options rather than directives. Encouraged Tyler to pick what felt doable to preserve ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Tyler's own reasons for change using open questions and scaling, allowing motivation to emerge from his language. Plan elements arose from Tyler's preferences, indicating strong elicitation of internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as collaborative and deferred expertise to Tyler, inviting his input on wording and strategy choices. Co-constructed specific steps (buddy, signal, text) that reflected shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk through open questions, scaling, and affirmations, which produced concrete intentions and rising confidence. Clinician reinforced and built on Tyler's statements of value and tentative commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and redirected toward solutions, but missed some opportunities for deeper double-sided reflections to fully explore the appeal of drinking. Could more explicitly validate social rewards and contrast them with Tyler's values to strengthen discrepancy.
