# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard through affirmations, invitations, and nonjudgmental language, creating a warm, supportive stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking (accurately captured frustration and constraints), but at times moved quickly to problem-solving instead of exploring the emotional texture of the patient's hesitation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on re-establishing exercise and steered the conversation toward small, concrete, actionable steps and contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner by eliciting preferences, co-creating the plan, and offering to help draft messages, though occasionally could ask permission before offering multiple solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported the patient's choice and control by offering options, avoiding pressuring language, and reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the patient using questions about past success, a confidence ruler, and prompts to generate self-endorsed strategies and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative stance by inviting input and framing decisions as the patient's while co-constructing specifics, but could more consistently check permission before offering suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with concrete planning and confidence scaling, though could better amplify past successes and more directly elicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers without arguing, but did not consistently use strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections or exploring costs of the status quo) to soften sustain talk before offering solutions.
