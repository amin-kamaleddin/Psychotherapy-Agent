# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician showed consistent unconditional positive regard—warm, nonjudgmental language, validation, and normalization throughout the session. They avoided labeling or admonishment after the incident and affirmed the client's efforts to stay safe.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician used empathic statements, validation, and reflective summaries and asked safety questions sensitively. There were missed opportunities for deeper reflection of tentative language and emotional tone (e.g., shame/defensiveness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, sustained focus on risky drinking and safety, moving efficiently from assessment to a concrete behavior-change plan. The clinician repeatedly steered toward actionable steps (SMART goal, start date, tracking, reminders).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was highly collaborative with the client invited to co-create the plan, choose supports, and shape texts and technical solutions. Decisions were jointly made and the client's language guided final materials.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and normalizing imperfection. Phrases like 'tell me which parts you want to keep or change' and multiple text versions respected the client's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician elicited explicit change statements (start day, check-in person) and some motivation, but could have further evoked personal reasons, values–behavior discrepancy, and used scaling/confidence questions. More selective reflection of change talk would strengthen momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician clearly adopted a partnership stance, sharing power and inviting client input at every decision point. Co-creation of the plan and tools demonstrated mutual collaboration and respect for the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician effectively elicited and reinforced change language through goal choices, SMART planning, and commitment prompts. Additional probing for reasons, importance, and confidence (e.g., scaling) would have deepened desire and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician avoided arguing and normalized ambivalence, shifting to safety and planning rather than confronting minimization. They could have used more strategic MI techniques (double-sided reflections, reframes) to actively diminish sustain talk.
