# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician shows consistent respect and a nonjudgmental tone, validating the patient's struggles and affirming plans. To reach a 5, adding more personalized affirmations that normalize ambivalence and warmth when the patient expresses skepticism would help.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid perspective-taking and accurate reflections of the patient's conflict and concerns. Could deepen emotional empathy with more complex, affect-focused reflections (e.g., frustration or anxiety about performance).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol to support marathon training and steadily guides toward a specific, time-limited experiment. Questions and reflections consistently narrow toward practical, testable steps and success criteria.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as a genuine partnership with the clinician eliciting the patient's ideas and co-constructing decisions. Language is inclusive and nonauthoritarian, and options are offered after eliciting preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice by offering options, framing steps as experiments, and asking the patient to choose what feels doable. Leaves ownership with the patient and avoids pressure or prescribing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations, desired outcomes, and concrete commitments, drawing out what the patient hopes to notice. Could strengthen by probing intrinsic motivators, eliciting past successes, and using importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the session as a joint exploration and repeatedly solicits the patient's input before offering supports. Decisions (seltzer, reminders, buddy check-ins) are co-created, reinforcing equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces concrete change language and commitments, using targeted questions that invite planning. To improve, the clinician could amplify and summarize change talk more forcefully and ask for importance/confidence ratings or past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and redirects to low-risk experiments, avoiding validation of minimizations as fixed truths. Could be stronger with explicit double-sided reflections and strategic pivots from sustain talk to change talk.
