# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician conveys respect and validates the client's struggle with supportive language and normalizing statements. Acceptance is reduced by quick shifts to solutions without deeper, unconditional affirmations or normalization of relapse patterns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good perspective-taking and asks about feelings before/after the behavior, which invites the client's experience. Empathy could be strengthened with more complex reflections and deeper amplification of nuanced emotions like guilt and numbness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing shoplifting with specific goals, concrete tactics, and an accountability plan. The session stays on topic throughout and orients interventions toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites client input on wording and tactics and asks permission for role-play, showing some collaborative moves. However, the interaction is largely clinician-driven with prescriptive lists and rehearsals, limiting joint generation of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers meaningful choices (targets, tactics, permission-based offers) and respects client control in planning. Autonomy support is tempered by many directive recommendations presented in quick succession, which can feel prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some preferences, frequency, and feelings, and secures small commitments. Overall evocation is limited because the clinician relies more on offering solutions than drawing out the client's own reasons, values, strengths, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows collaborative intent by asking preferences and tailoring language, incorporating some client choices. Much of the session remains clinician-led with expert-driven plans, so partnership is present but inconsistent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates opportunities for change talk, elicits goals and small commitments, and reinforces doable tactics. Improvement would come from more strategic reflections and amplification of the client's own change statements to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges doubts and normalizes uncertainty but does not consistently use MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to reframe or soften sustain talk. The predominant prescriptive responses risk sidelining exploration of ongoing temptations and peer pressure.
