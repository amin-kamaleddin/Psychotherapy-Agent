# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician set a nonjudgmental, supportive tone and used respectful language throughout, validating concerns and offering practical help (e.g., scripts, contact assistance).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the patient's worry and provided reassuring, concrete information, but used limited reflective listening or emotional labeling to deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the safety and logistics of switching from phenelzine to citalopram, with relevant clinical details and concrete next steps throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as a partner by offering scripts and help contacting the prescriber, though more elicitation of the patient's agenda and priorities would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported patient choice—advising consultation with the prescriber, offering options, and avoiding pressuring language to leave decisions with the patient and clinician.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction prioritized information delivery and made minimal effort to elicit the patient's intrinsic motivations, values, or commitment language about switching or staying on medication.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited joint problem‑solving (e.g., scripts, offers to contact), creating a partnership feel, but did not fully co‑create a plan using the patient's stated priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Practical supports and options were offered that could prompt action, but the clinician rarely asked open questions to elicit or amplify the patient's own motivations, confidence, or desire to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validated the patient's reluctance to stop phenelzine, avoided confrontation, and softened sustain talk by normalizing caution and offering alternative safe options and plans.
