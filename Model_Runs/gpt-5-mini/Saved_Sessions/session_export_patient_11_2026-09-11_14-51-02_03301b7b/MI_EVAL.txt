# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with a warm, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client's struggle while inviting her to lead planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening and perspective-taking focused on practical barriers, but reflections are more cognitive/task-focused and do not fully capture the client’s underlying emotions (e.g., mild discouragement, body‑image concerns).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity with questions and planning tightly tied to the specific target behavior and concrete steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction reads as joint problem-solving with inclusive language and choice, yet the clinician offers many concrete suggestions rather than pausing to elicit more spontaneous ideas from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's sense of choice by offering options, avoiding pressure, and letting the client select plans she finds realistic.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's own motivations, values, and confidence (e.g., 8/10 ratings) and builds concrete commitments from those self‑generated reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and asks the client to choose specifics, supporting teamwork, but slightly clinician‑driven with many offered options instead of eliciting the client's brainstorm first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and amplifies change talk and commitment language, repeatedly prompting for specific reasons, confidence, and concrete commitments that the client articulates.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and appropriately redirects to activity, but misses opportunities for explicit double‑sided reflections to validate ambivalence about nutrition while guiding toward change.
