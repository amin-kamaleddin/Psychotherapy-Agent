# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental validation throughout the session, creating a warm, respectful tone that matched the patient's defensive affect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate, timely reflections and summarized ambivalence well, but reflections often stayed at surface level and could probe deeper emotional meaning (shame, fear, loyalty).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on tobacco use with logical progression to concrete planning, a precise start date, confidence/readiness ratings, and a scheduled midweek follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co‑created the plan, asked permission before offering suggestions, and negotiated logistics, though frequent practical tips sometimes made the exchange slightly more expert‑driven than purely co‑constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly framed options, invited preferences, affirmed the patient's right to choose and to fail without shame, and offered to adjust plans if the patient changed his mind.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used pros/cons and readiness/confidence rulers and elicited a plan, but largely left motivation externally anchored and did not sufficiently draw out internal motives or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Signaled shared decision‑making by offering choices and letting the patient select steps, though offering a detailed menu of strategies slightly reduced client‑led idea generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created opportunities for change talk and elicited some relational language, but did not adequately amplify or follow up to deepen the patient's internal motivations and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation and offered harm‑reduction alternatives, which prevented escalation, but missed strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections or value reframes) to further soften sustain talk.
