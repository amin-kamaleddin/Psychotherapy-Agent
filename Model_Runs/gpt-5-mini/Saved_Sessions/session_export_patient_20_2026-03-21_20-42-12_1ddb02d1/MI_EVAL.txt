# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, validates tension, and avoids labeling while affirming the client's preferences and limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking and accurate reflections that capture sustain and change sides, but could deepen affective reflections to name and explore underlying emotions like stress or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on alcohol use and targeted behavior change, repeatedly guiding toward specific implementation plans and contingencies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is clearly collaborative—clinician invites input, uses inclusive language, offers options, and builds on the client's ideas to co-create steps throughout the encounter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's sense of choice and control by offering options, avoiding pressure, and emphasizing incremental changes that respect the client's decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from the client by eliciting change talk, using confidence rulers, asking what would move confidence up, and building on the client's intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the client as the expert on their life, shares power in decision-making, and co-develops concrete steps with frequent invitations for the client to choose next actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk with future-oriented questions, specific goal-setting, confidence ratings, and concrete commitment-oriented planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and acknowledges the benefits the client values, but uses fewer strategic double-sided reflections or summaries that would more proactively shift sustain talk toward change.
