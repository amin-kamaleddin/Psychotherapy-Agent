# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with nonjudgmental reflections and normalization of ambivalence, creating a warm accepting tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, timely perspective-taking with complex reflections that pick up emotional nuance (shame, helplessness) and validate the patient's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing isolation and reaching out, repeatedly narrowing to concrete steps (draft/save text, choose contact) and avoiding tangents.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages in mutual, respectful partnership by offering options, asking permission, deferring to patient preference, and co-constructing the small-step plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the patient's freedom to choose, invites decisions, normalizes opting out, and avoids pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient's own small-step ideas and solutions but relies on pragmatic prompts and does not probe deeper values, motives, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly shares power and follows the patient's lead, offering to collaborate (e.g., drafting together) and repeatedly inviting the patient's choice of next step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies tentative change talk and actionable steps, but misses opportunities to deepen reasons for change or use scaling to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids argument, using neutral reflections and safe options, though it could use more strategic double-sided reflections and reframing to reduce sustain talk's potency.
