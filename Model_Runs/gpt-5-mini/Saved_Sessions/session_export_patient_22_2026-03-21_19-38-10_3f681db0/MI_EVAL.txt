# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, respecting Camila's goal of control over abstinence and tailoring suggestions without shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking that helped Camila feel heard, though the clinician moved quickly to solutions and missed opportunities for deeper, more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on reducing weeknight drinking with clear, concrete goals (pick two nights, scripts, exit plans) and minimal drift to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the process as a joint effort by inviting choices, soliciting which strategies felt doable, and using role-play to co‑construct the plan rather than imposing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respected Camila's autonomy by offering multiple options, asking permission, and supporting her right to decide how to handle social pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some motivations and commitment through choices and practice, but relied more on providing strategies than drawing out Camila's deeper personal reasons or values for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated a collaborative stance and invited client input, but sometimes shifted into advising with long lists instead of eliciting Camila's ideas first to strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited actionable change talk (selecting nights, choosing scripts), yet missed chances to amplify and deepen client-generated reasons, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and normalized sustain talk and redirected to practical strategies, but seldom used double-sided reflections or strategic reflections before moving to problem-solving.
