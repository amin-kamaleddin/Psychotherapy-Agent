# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened with explicit safety and nonjudgmental listening, normalized Elise’s conflict, and consistently validated her experience without labeling or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used good perspective-taking and reflective summaries that matched ambivalence, but reflections tended to stay cognitive/behavioral rather than deepening into emotional affect or personal meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking behavior with questions and suggestions tied to concrete behavioral experiments and a specific next-step plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem-solving, co-created plans and scripts with Elise, and adapted recommendations to her preferences, signaling an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and control by offering options, asking preferences, using noncoercive language, and framing slips as information rather than failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited change-oriented language with a readiness ruler, past-attempt questions, and a chosen experiment, but missed deeper probes into values and the personal meaning of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Elise as an active partner by asking which options felt doable, inviting input at each step, and co-creating the plan and follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used open questions, a readiness ruler, and low-effort experiments to generate change talk, but could have amplified and reflected brief change statements to strengthen them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged the social benefits of smoking and offered practical alternatives, yet relied more on problem-solving than MI-specific softening techniques like double-sided reflections or strategic pivots.
