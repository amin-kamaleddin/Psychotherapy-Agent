# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician was consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, normalizing feelings and affirming the patient’s choices throughout. No shaming or blaming statements appeared and acceptance was steady.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summary reflections and validation of anxiety and safety needs. Could deepen empathic resonance by reflecting more complex emotions (e.g., shame, fear of rejection) and underlying values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on disclosure from initial scaling through planning, scripting, and role-play without topic drift. Interventions progressed logically from ambivalence to concrete rehearsal and troubleshooting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative: the clinician co-created the plan, solicited the patient’s preferences, and used role-play to practice together. Decisions were shaped by the patient’s comfort and choices rather than prescriptive instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong support for patient autonomy with repeated permission-seeking, multiple options, and respect for pacing and privacy. The clinician emphasized the patient's control and never pressured action before readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient’s reasons, barriers, and solutions (scaling, choosing Ana, preferring a private text) and used questions about safety and confidence. Could strengthen evocation by linking disclosure to deeper values and eliciting firmer commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively, invited choices, and engaged in shared role-play, fostering a partnership stance. At times the clinician offered multiple scripted texts before sufficiently eliciting the patient's own wording, which slightly reduced patient ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out change language via scaling, asking why the number was chosen, and building concrete plans and rehearsals to bolster ability and activation. Missed opportunities to consistently reinforce accumulated change statements and to elicit explicit near-term commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged sustain talk and avoided confrontation, then moved into problem-solving and planning. More deliberate use of MI strategies (double-sided reflections, exploring pros of status quo) could better reduce sustain talk before action planning.
