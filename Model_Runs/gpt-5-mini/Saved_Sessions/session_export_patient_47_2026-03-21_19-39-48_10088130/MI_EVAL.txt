# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support, affirming strengths and normalizing slips throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and accurate summaries of ambivalence, but missed opportunities for deeper affective reflections about the fall, son’s reaction, and underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus on alcohol risk and concrete risk-reduction steps, safety guidance, and goal-setting throughout the encounter.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged in shared decision-making by inviting preferences, co-creating reminders and messages, and repeatedly checking in so the patient’s ideas shaped the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice and respected the patient’s decisions (e.g., keeping wine at home), offering options and permission language without pressuring for abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited reasons for change and prompted planning and intentions, though could have used more targeted open questions and strategic reflections to deepen intrinsic motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt collaborative with co-creation of plans and invitations for input, but the clinician sometimes offered ready-made solutions instead of eliciting more of the patient’s own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced change talk leading to a specific week plan, but could amplify and chain the patient’s change statements more to strengthen commitment and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered alternatives, but did not consistently use double-sided or reflective strategies to roll with and redirect minimising remarks.
