# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, normalizing ambivalence and avoiding labeling. Warm, respectful language (e.g., 'no lecture here') is used throughout the interaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries accurately capture the patient's behavior and ambivalence and the clinician asks permission before offering information. Opportunities remain to reflect deeper emotions and the patient's values or affective reactions more often.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on weekend drinking with specific questions about frequency, quantity, consequences, and concrete next steps. A clear, time-bound plan (two-week tracking and review) was set with little topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician co-creates tools and plans with the patient (phone-note template, conversion offers) and repeatedly solicits the patient's preferences. The interaction is clearly teamwork rather than a top-down approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports choice by offering options, asking permission, and framing steps as the patient's decision. Language reinforces ownership of the plan and respects the patient's control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (tracking commitment and the patient's remark about health concerns), but the clinician relied more on information and strategies than on drawing out intrinsic motives. Missed opportunities to ask values-based or importance/confidence questions limited deeper evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician frames the encounter as a joint exploration and defers to the patient's expertise when choosing strategies and review timing. Mutual decision-making is evident in the co-created tracking plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change talk through questions about consequences and offering small steps, securing a concrete two-week tracking commitment. However, follow-up prompts to deepen desire, reasons, or confidence were limited, reducing potential amplification of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids argumentation and uses neutral, validating responses when the patient minimizes drinking, and suggests low-effort steps rather than insisting on abstinence. Greater use of double-sided or strategic selective reflections could further shift the balance away from sustain talk.
