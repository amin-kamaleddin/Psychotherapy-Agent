# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed respect and positive regard, validating Tariq's feelings and honoring his boundary about not adding complaints. Offers to help without blaming demonstrated stable, unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both content and feeling (e.g., mirroring his line and naming injustice), and empathy was used repeatedly to check understanding. The clinician’s tone and summaries showed high-quality perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior—getting a message to Tariq's mom tonight—and moved toward a concrete plan (exact wording, delivery, staff person). Questions and reflections repeatedly returned to that goal without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician and Tariq co-constructed the message and plan, soliciting his wording and delivery preferences and sharing decisions. The exchange honored Tariq's expertise and led to a jointly owned solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options, checked for consent, and respected Tariq’s instruction not to add complaints, supporting his sense of choice and control. There was no pressure to accept a particular option.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited personally meaningful reasons to act (stay connected, feel less frantic, sleep better) but missed opportunities to deepen motivation by linking to longer-term goals or eliciting confidence and commitment language. More probing for commitment/readiness would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was clearly collaborative, with the psychiatrist repeatedly asking Tariq’s preferences and offering help while leaving final decisions to him. Power-sharing was evident throughout the exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited change talk about using a note to stay connected and calm, asking which option felt most likely to work. Opportunities were missed to amplify and summarize those statements or to elicit a specific commitment or confidence rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician validated the unfairness but promptly redirected to problem-solving, which softened sustain talk, yet sometimes repeated complaint language rather than using double-sided reflections to pivot fully toward change. More explicit contrast reflections could further reduce sustain talk.
