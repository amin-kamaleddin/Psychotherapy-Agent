# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, warmth, and nonjudgmental language with multiple affirmations and normalization. Validates efforts and avoids labeling, meeting a high standard for acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate, supportive reflections and summaries. Could use a few more complex, feeling-laden reflections to mirror deeper anxiety about family history and the emotional meaning of ‘not caring.’

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing drinking and improving contraceptive reliability, consistently steering to actionable steps and a concrete plan. Session moves logically from exploration to specific implementation intentions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly a joint enterprise: clinician solicits the patient's ideas, checks wording, and co-constructs implementation steps while positioning themselves as a helper. Offers drafts only after eliciting needs and invites shared responsibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supports choice and control by asking permission, offering options, and affirming the patient's preferences. Avoids pressuring language and consistently respects Mei’s decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits the patient's own motivations, options, and confidence ratings and uses evocative questions to draw out change talk. Could probe deeper into values, past successes, and elicit an explicit commitment statement to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with repeated invitations for preference and co-construction of plans, including role-play and shared problem-solving. Slight tendency to offer clinician-crafted scripts before fully eliciting the patient's own phrasing reduced optimal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicits and reinforces change talk, asks for concrete strategies, and checks readiness/confidence repeatedly. Missed opportunities to amplify and summarize key change statements and to follow up with deeper questions about reasons and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids reinforcing it but makes fewer strategic moves to soften or reframe minimizations. Could use double-sided reflections and explicit reframing of statements like 'I don't really care' to reduce sustain talk's influence.
