# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently communicates nonjudgmental, warm support and normalizes relapse, conveying unconditional positive regard. Language avoids blame and repeatedly reassures the patient's value while acknowledging difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist shows accurate, attuned reflective listening and perspective-taking, mirroring core themes of shame, helplessness, and isolation. Targeted safety checks and validating responses demonstrate strong empathic attunement throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus on reducing drug use and immediate safety with concrete, practical steps (7-day goal, SMART template, scheduling therapy). Clinician repeatedly steers toward actionable next steps and measurable milestones.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician behaves as an equal partner, co-creating the plan and soliciting Amira's input and preferences (choice of goal, message tone, role-play). Decisions and tools are offered as adaptable options, supporting joint decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively supported through offering multiple options, inviting choice, and framing suggestions as optional and adaptable. Clinician avoids pressuring language and reinforces patient control over pacing and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Some internal motivation is elicited (choice of goal, commitments) but deeper intrinsic reasons, values, and confidence are not consistently explored. More open, evocative questions about importance and confidence would strengthen internal motivation and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction strongly invites the patient's expertise and choice, with offers to adapt templates and select preferred steps. The clinician consistently frames suggestions as collaborative offers rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician successfully elicits and reinforces concrete change talk (7-day abstinence, scheduling therapy) using options and affirmations. Opportunities were missed to deepen change talk with more open, evocative questions to draw out personal reasons and strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist avoids reinforcing sustain talk, normalizes relapse, and offers small-step options to reduce overwhelm. Greater use of double-sided reflections, strategic reframing, and summaries highlighting discrepancy could further weaken sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
