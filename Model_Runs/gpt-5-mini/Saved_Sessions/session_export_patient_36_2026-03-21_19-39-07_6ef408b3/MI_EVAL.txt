# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated respect and positive regard, normalizing planning and avoiding criticism. Opening statements and repeated affirmations conveyed unconditional acceptance of the patient's goals and experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated accurate perspective-taking and used reflective statements, asking about the patient's experience. Reflections were often brief and task-focused; more complex emotional reflections would deepen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on translating values into concrete behaviors with a practical action map and repeated return to the morning check-in target. Questions about timing, scripts, and rehearsal kept the conversation oriented to the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly co-constructed: clinician offered structure and the patient supplied preferences, wording, and scheduling choices. The clinician invited revisions, accepted patient wording, and refined plans jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported the patient's sense of choice by offering options, asking preferences, and inviting selection of values and actions. Language emphasized agency and avoided directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation and concrete commitments were elicited (values, time, scripts), but the clinician provided detailed solutions early rather than first drawing out more patient-generated ideas. Using more open, evocative questions and scaling would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician adopted a collaborative stance, treating the patient as the primary decision-maker and offering options rather than prescriptions. Permission was sought for rehearsal and plans were co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicited and amplified change talk through value selection, commitment timing, and rehearsal, repeatedly drawing out the patient's reasons and plans. Reinforcements of present-tense commitment helped strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** There was minimal sustain talk and the clinician avoided reinforcing status-quo arguments, reframing obstacles into if-then contingencies and flexible fallbacks. Resistance was strategically redirected into manageable plans without confrontation.
