# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently expressed unconditional positive regard and normalized the client's stance, avoided judgment, and validated bluntness while affirming the effort to attend.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking that captured feelings and somatic signs, but reflections grew somewhat formulaic and the clinician did not consistently probe deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing reactivity and creating 'breathing room,' moving systematically to a specific, time-bound action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the session as a joint enterprise, co-creating the plan and soliciting the client's preferences so decisions were shaped by the client's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored the client's choice, avoided pressuring language, framed steps as tests, and checked that the plan felt like the right first step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivation and strengths (e.g., stubbornness, desire for breathing room) and elicited concrete steps, but could deepen evocation with scaling questions and more exploration of past successes and explicit client-stated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Invited the client's input, deferred to their expertise, and co-constructed options (e.g., backup strategies) demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively elicited and amplified change talk through strategic questions and reflections, moving the client from terse sustain-talk to a concrete commitment and plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged and reflected sustain talk using double-sided reflections, but missed opportunities to more explicitly validate resistance while summarizing costs of staying the same or reframing resignation into ambivalence.
