# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and validated Elias’s priorities and limits without shaming, using supportive praise and normalization throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking were evident with tailored adaptations and safety language, though the clinician could have probed more deeply into Elias’s emotional experience (sadness/resignation) for fuller empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on increasing safe physical activity with a specific, measurable plan and contingencies, avoiding drift and ending with time‑bound steps and a scheduled check‑in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co‑constructed with Elias, inviting his input on options, timing, and involvement of his wife, creating a genuine team approach rather than an authoritative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choices, asked permission, and respected Elias’s preferences (e.g., timing, rest length, demo vs. sheet), reinforcing his sense of control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited motives (independence, helping his wife) and used scaling/elicitation techniques, but could have probed the deeper emotional meaning of those values and amplified positive memories to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Throughout the interaction the clinician emphasized shared decision‑making, deferred to Elias’s expertise, and negotiated roles and follow‑up in a collaborative manner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and reinforced change language and secured a concrete plan with scaling, but missed opportunities to amplify Elias’s positive statements (e.g., ‘very, very beautiful’) and to elicit a clear verbal commitment in his own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without confrontation and redirected with harm‑minimizing adaptations and safety steps, though use of double‑sided reflections and reframing (e.g., worried about bothering the doctor) could further reduce sustain language.
