# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect and nonjudgmental regard through validating reflections and normalizing language (e.g., acknowledging skepticism). They avoided critical or directive language and affirmed the client's experience throughout the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated high-quality reflective listening and perspective-taking, using complex reflections that accurately captured the client's feelings and concerns. Responses were tied to the client's lived experience, showing consistent and accurate understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing evening stress and improving sleep, consistently funneling toward a concrete, small change and implementation details. The clinician progressively clarified a measurable plan (five-minute breathing routine for three nights, using a timer).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange was genuinely collaborative, inviting input and building on the client's ideas and past strategies to co-create practical steps. Language and questions supported mutual problem-solving and teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician reinforced the client's choice and control by emphasizing preferences and validating decision-making rather than prescribing. Questions and reflections supported the client's autonomy in selecting an option that fit her routine.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client's own values, past successes, and anticipated outcomes, which the clinician elicited and used to shape the plan. The plan aligned with the client's internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly invited the client's expertise and choices, avoiding an expert-dominant stance and sharing power in decision-making. They built on the client's preferences and past successes to co-construct the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk through open questions and reflections that amplified desire, ability, and anticipated benefits. This evocation led directly to a concrete, actionable plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and not reinforced, with validation of skepticism and redirection toward small, testable steps. However, there were missed opportunities for more strategic erosion of sustain talk (e.g., double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of main obstacles).
