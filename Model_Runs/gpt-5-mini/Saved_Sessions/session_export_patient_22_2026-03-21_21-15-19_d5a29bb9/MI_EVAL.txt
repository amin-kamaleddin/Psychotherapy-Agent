# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support from the opening through affirmations, normalizing and validating Camila's priorities. No shaming or minimizing statements were used, supporting a top rating for acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries of Camila's concerns and ambivalence. Could improve by more frequent complex/emotional reflections and explicitly naming underlying feelings like anxiety or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol and moved efficiently from assessment to concrete planning and practice (e.g., role-play, decline lines). The clinician avoided major topic drift and sustained momentum toward small, actionable change steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly bidirectional and collegial, with the clinician soliciting Camila's input, offering options, and co-constructing the plan. Role‑plays and choices (NA drink, supportive friend, timing) reinforced a fully collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported autonomy through permission‑seeking, offering multiple options, and respecting Camila's wish not to quit entirely. Advice was presented as choices, preserving her sense of control and ownership over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Camila's own motivations, values, and past successes (e.g., exam breaks) and used those to build practical strategies. Frequent prompts for what matters and confidence ratings fostered intrinsic motivation and resource mobilization.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered a collaborative partnership by repeatedly asking permission, inviting choices, and deferring to Camila's lived experience in tailoring the plan. The clinician and patient worked as co‑problem‑solvers throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced concrete change talk (realistic Mon–Wed sober goal, energy and routine gains) and used confidence scaling to explore readiness. Could more deliberately amplify, summarize, and elicit specific commitment language to strengthen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it and offered respectful, practical alternatives to preserve social connection (NA drinks, decline lines). Would be stronger with more double‑sided reflections that explicitly name both sides of ambivalence to gently guide toward change.
