# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized Adrian’s pace and mixed feelings, creating a safe, nonjudgmental atmosphere. Affirmations and validation were used throughout to support the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries accurately captured both content and emotion (pride, embarrassment, worry) and invited correction, demonstrating strong perspective-taking. The clinician listened closely and paraphrased Adrian’s strengths and concerns effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing infection risk with concrete, time-bound risk-reduction behaviors (early-shift line, leaving cash, leaving by 10, 11 pm check-in). The clinician consistently steered toward an actionable weekend plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was co-constructed, with the clinician soliciting Adrian’s preferences and letting him choose supports and strategies. Decisions were negotiated and shaped by the client’s input throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician reinforced choice and ownership by offering options, deferring to Adrian’s expertise, and avoiding pressure to commit. Statements like “you’re allowed to move at your own pace” sustained client autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew on Adrian’s past successes and elicited concrete steps tied to his goals, but could have probed deeper into personal values and used scaling to strengthen commitment. Evocation was effective yet could be further deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician framed the session as collaborative and treated Adrian as the expert, co-constructing the plan and inviting his input on realistic options. Power-sharing was evident in planning and support choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and strengthened change talk through questions about strengths and past success, yielding specific change statements and actions. There were missed opportunities to more often amplify and reflect change statements to consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing and redirected ambivalence toward practical mitigations, which limited sustain talk’s influence. More strategic techniques (double-sided reflections, eliciting drawbacks of use) could have been used to further soften sustain talk.
