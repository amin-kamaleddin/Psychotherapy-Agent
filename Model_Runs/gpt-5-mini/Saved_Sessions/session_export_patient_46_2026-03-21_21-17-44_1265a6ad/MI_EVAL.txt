# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with explicit positive regard; asked permission, normalized ambivalence, and offered to explore options together, appropriate for an adolescent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening, perspective-taking, and validation of concerns, but reflections often stayed at the content level and could probe deeper emotions like shame or fear of exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on adolescent alcohol use with goal-oriented tasks (confidence ruler, role‑plays) and concrete harm‑reduction planning throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt like teamwork with joint exploration and rehearsal, but frequent provision of ready-made lines sometimes reduced opportunities for co-created solutions and client-generated phrasing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported choice by asking permission, offering options, and framing suggestions as choices the patient could accept or reject, including pragmatic safety advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the patient’s reasons and plans using questions and scaling and moved to practice, but could better amplify spontaneous change statements and use more evocative follow-ups to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance by inviting choice and practicing together, though providing many concrete scripts occasionally shifted decision-making toward the clinician rather than co‑creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk and used planning and scaling, but missed opportunities to reflect and amplify the patient’s own change language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and acknowledged minimizations, but used limited strategic techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, rolling with resistance) to deflate sustain talk about belonging.
