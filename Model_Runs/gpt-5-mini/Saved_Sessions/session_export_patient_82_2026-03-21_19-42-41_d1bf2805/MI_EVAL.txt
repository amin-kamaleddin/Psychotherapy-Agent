# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and accepting (e.g., explicit normalization and praise for honesty), creating a clear accepting tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good empathic listening and validation of feelings but could use deeper, more frequent reflections and more complex reflections that capture ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use and short-term harm reduction, steering the session toward a concrete one-week plan and commitment check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt like a joint project with adaptation to Lukas's language, but frequent provision of ready-made lists and scripted solutions reduced opportunities for Lukas to generate his own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice and control by offering options, honoring decisions (e.g., not involving coach/parents), and phrasing suggestions as choices rather than mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out intentions and a commitment (one-week plan, check-in text) but often supplied solutions instead of exploring Lukas's personal motivations and reasons in greater depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Took a collaborative stance by asking preferences and co-creating plans, yet frequently led with clinician-generated strategies rather than first eliciting Lukas's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and strengthened change talk and planning (produced explicit commitment language), though there were missed opportunities to amplify spontaneous change talk and probe for reasons/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided shaming and accepted ambivalence but used few double-sided or reframing reflections and relied more on directive advice, which limited active softening of sustain talk.
