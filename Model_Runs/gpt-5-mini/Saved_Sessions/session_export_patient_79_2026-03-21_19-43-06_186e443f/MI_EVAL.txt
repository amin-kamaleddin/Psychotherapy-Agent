# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful; validated the patient's frustration and offered support and resources without lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and summaries that opened space for clarification, but responses remained pragmatic and missed deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on simplifying asthma management with every question and suggestion tied to actionable options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as genuine two-way problem-solving by soliciting priorities, checking preferences, and co-creating scripts and plans with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered options, honored the patient's limits, and provided caveats to avoid pressure while reinforcing patient choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of willingness and practical commitments occurred, but the clinician relied mostly on solutions rather than drawing out the patient's values or deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked collaboratively by asking which approach the patient preferred and co-creating language, though at times recommendations were delivered in a more expert than co-constructed voice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced practical change talk with scripts and options, but did not consistently probe for importance, confidence, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and reflected sustain talk while offering compromises and alternatives, though more double-sided reflections could have further softened ambivalence.
