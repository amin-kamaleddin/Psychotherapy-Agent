# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent validations and normalizing language; avoided shaming and offered support, conveying clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections and perspective-taking showed strong understanding of Sofia's fear, guilt, and conflict; timely empathic listening throughout, with only minor room for deeper affect-focused reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy, safety, and assessment while translating motivation into concrete, sequential action steps and referrals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was framed as cooperative—invited preferences, co-drafted language, and offered to do tasks together—treating Sofia as an active partner rather than a passive recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported Sofia's choice by asking permission, offering options, and emphasizing her control over decisions while still providing guidance and suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited some internal motivation (readiness ruler, affirmations, planning) but tended to offer solutions rather than systematically drawing out values, reasons, strengths, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered co-creation of plans and scripts and solicited preferences, yet at times shifted to an expert-driven approach by offering many specific solutions before eliciting Sofia's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced change language and invited concrete planning and referrals, but missed opportunities to probe more openly for reasons, values, and confidence to further amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue and provided practical strategies to reduce risk, but used limited MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) and included a directive safety statement that moved toward advice rather than softening sustain talk.
