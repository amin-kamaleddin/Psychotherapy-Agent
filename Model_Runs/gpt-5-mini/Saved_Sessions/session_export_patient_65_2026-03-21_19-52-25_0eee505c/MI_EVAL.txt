# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with validating statements and reassurance. Could increase individualized affirmations that name strengths and explicitly normalize mixed feelings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Showed good empathic listening and accurate reflections early in the session. Empathy was uneven afterward as the clinician moved into solution-focused planning and did not explore the client's guarded or anxious responses more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on completing the court-ordered hours and related logistics. Every intervention tied back to that specific target behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Displayed a collaborative tone by asking preferences and tailoring materials to the client. Interaction still leaned toward clinician-driven solutions rather than co-constructed problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported autonomy by asking what the client wanted and offering choices while avoiding pressure. Some communications were prescriptive (detailed plans/scripts) without first inviting the client to choose or modify options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied mainly on providing information, scripts, and options instead of eliciting the client's own reasons, values, or confidence. Missed opportunities for open, evocative questions to surface internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative, asking permission and tailoring suggestions to the client's preferences. At times the clinician assumed the expert role by delivering plans without first eliciting the client's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Focused on logistical planning which produced agreement but did not elicit or amplify the client's own motives beyond avoiding legal consequences. Lacked strategic questions to draw out desire, reasons, ability, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and avoided reinforcing sustain talk but missed chances to strategically soften resistance. Could use double-sided reflections or reframing to acknowledge concerns while highlighting benefits of compliance.
