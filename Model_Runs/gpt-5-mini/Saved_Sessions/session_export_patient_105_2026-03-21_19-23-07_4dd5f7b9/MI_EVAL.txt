# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and supportive with validating language and affirmations (e.g., safety check, normalizing symptoms, praising effort). Tone avoided labeling or blame and communicated unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good perspective-taking and brief reflective statements that helped Naomi feel understood (breathing felt calmer). Reflections were often short and the clinician moved quickly to problem-solving, missing chances to deepen emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on social anxiety and linked all interventions to that target behavior. Session included concrete strategies and measurable homework with an explicit follow‑up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt cooperative with Naomi shaping choices (sticker wording, log template, option selection) and negotiated homework. However, much content was clinician-driven with scripted phrasing rather than jointly generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering options, asking permission, and inviting Naomi to choose wording and reminders, reinforcing ownership of decisions. Avoided pressure and respected Naomi's control over implementation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation through choice and committing to try the routine, but overall relied more on providing strategies than eliciting Naomi’s intrinsic motivations or values. Missed open, evocative questions to draw out personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership with co-creation of materials and regular checks about what felt doable, reflecting collaborative problem-solving. At times the clinician adopted a more expert stance, reducing equal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change talk (e.g., 'I’ll give it a real go') and supported implementation planning, but did not consistently amplify internal motivations, values, or confidence language. Change talk cultivation was present but moderate and skill-driven.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Normalized difficulties and avoided reinforcing sustain talk, which helped reduce resistance. However, the clinician rarely used double-sided reflections, ambivalence summaries, or explicit reframing and often shifted straight to problem-solving.
