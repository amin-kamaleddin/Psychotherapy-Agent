# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard through validations, labeling feelings, and normalizing ambivalence, creating a warm, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking with complex reflections that capture both content and affect, building clear understanding of the client's inner world.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a tight focus on family routines and moves logically from values to a specific, feasible plan, keeping the session goal-directed throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co‑constructed—clinician solicits preferences, co-designs the plan with the client, and positions themselves as a helper rather than an expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's choice and control by offering options, inviting rehearsal rather than prescribing, and avoiding pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out motivations tied to core values and elicits concrete reasons for change, but could more consistently deepen and amplify change language (e.g., scaling/confidence questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance that signals the client's expertise, repeatedly inviting input and sharing power in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits desire, reasons, and a concrete plan, yet would benefit from more systematic amplification and summarizing of the client's change language and explicit commitment/confidence queries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges and redirects sustain talk toward problem-solving effectively, though additional MI techniques (e.g., double‑sided reflections, paired affirmations) could further soften resistance.
