# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, explicitly respecting Eric’s choices. Avoided blame and normalized ambivalence throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and paraphrase that kept Eric engaged. Many reflections remained factual rather than exploring deeper emotions (e.g., defensiveness or embarrassment), so more complex feeling reflections are needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and moved toward a concrete 2-week trial with follow-up. Could tighten momentum by more explicitly linking Eric’s values or long-term goals to the smoking target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction was clearly mutual problem-solving with Eric’s preferences shaping the plan. The clinician avoided dominating and integrated Eric’s practical constraints and ideas into the trial.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Eric’s freedom to choose and sought permission before offering options, normalizing a trial without pressure to quit. Options were offered and selected based on Eric’s preferences, honoring his agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used the readiness ruler and targeted questions to elicit reasons and mild motivations (wife/doctor concerns, cough, desire to quiet pressures). Could probe more deeply into intrinsic values and elicit stronger confidence and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance—asked permission, invited Eric’s input, and co-created the plan together. Decisions about goals, barriers, and check-in timing were negotiated rather than imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk (readiness ruler, follow-up questions, praise for a practical trial) and supported a concrete plan. Missed some opportunities to more aggressively reflect, amplify, and convert change language into stronger ability/confidence and commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged ambivalence and used at least one double-sided reflection, avoiding reinforcement of sustain talk. However, many rationalizations were mirrored or tolerated rather than strategically reframed or challenged to reduce sustain language.
