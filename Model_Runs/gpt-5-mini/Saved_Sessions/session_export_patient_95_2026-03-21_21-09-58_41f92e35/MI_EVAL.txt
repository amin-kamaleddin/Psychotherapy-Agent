# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance, honors requests to listen, and avoids pathologizing language throughout the encounter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong perspective-taking with accurate, timely reflections that validate Maria's pain and build safety for disclosure of intent and plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on imminent suicidal risk and immediate safety (plan, means, timing, protective steps) without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an authentic equal partner—invites preferences, offers options, and co-constructs a concrete, feasible safety plan while respecting patient limits.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently preserves choice by asking permission, offering options rather than mandates, and framing escalation thresholds as voluntary decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits some motivations and resources with evocative questions, but could deepen exploration of the 'tiny part' and the values underlying protective reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly shares power and decision-making, repeatedly checks what feels doable, and accepts patient limits while offering structured supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and small commitments (check‑ins, moving bottles), but misses opportunities to amplify and deepen desire/ability/reason/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately validates and redirects sustain talk toward safety, but at times echoes despair verbatim rather than using double‑sided reflections or actively amplifying protective reasons.
