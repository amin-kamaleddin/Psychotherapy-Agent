# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support with validating language and normalization, maintaining a respectful tone throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening and captured frustration and practical concerns, but reflections were mostly surface-level and could more explicitly name loneliness and self-judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on the target behavior and moved the session toward a concrete, time-limited plan with agreed follow-up without getting sidetracked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint problem-solving, solicited the patient's ideas and contingencies, and negotiated a mutually acceptable plan and check-in, deferring to the patient's preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission before advising, and building the plan from the patient's preferences while affirming her control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited behavior-specific motivation and increased confidence, but explored intrinsic reasons and values only minimally and could probe deeper personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a genuinely collaborative stance, asking permission to suggest options and repeatedly inviting the patient to choose specifics, sharing power in plan development.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively and skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk, using scaling questions and prompting specific commitments that moved the patient from tentative to more committed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected toward small, manageable changes, though could have used more strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to resolve ambivalence.
