# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician adopts an explicit nonjudgmental stance, validates goals and progress, and consistently uses respectful, affirming language throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate, timely summaries; could deepen affective reflections and use more complex reflections to explore guilt/shame more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation tightly focused on people-pleasing and protecting evenings, moving steadily toward concrete, actionable steps and skill practice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong joint work—scripts are co-created, rehearsed, and iteratively refined with the patient’s preferences guiding the coaching and feedback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice by asking permission, offering multiple options, and presenting advice tentatively while inviting the patient to select what feels natural.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivations and a concrete plan (10-minute pause) but at times supplies solutions rather than drawing out additional personal reasons or eliciting a clear commitment statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative stance with repeated permission-seeking, power-sharing, and client-led decisions about wording, tone, and practice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and amplifies change talk through concrete questions, scaling, and rehearsal, but could more consistently summarize and amplify the patient’s own reasons for change and seek a commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal and acknowledged neutrally with pivoting to strategies; could be strengthened by using double-sided reflections to more explicitly resolve ambivalence.
