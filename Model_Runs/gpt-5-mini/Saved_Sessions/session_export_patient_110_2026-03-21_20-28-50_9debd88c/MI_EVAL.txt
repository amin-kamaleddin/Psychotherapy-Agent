# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated consistent respectful, nonjudgmental regard and normalized the patient's ownership of the agenda. Supportive language and incorporation of the patient's values were evident with no blaming or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and reflective listening—priorities and concerns were accurately summarized and worries were validated. Reflections were timely and helped clarify the patient's needs for reassurance and numbers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, goal-directed focus on cholesterol management and treatment decisions throughout the session. The clinician moved the conversation purposefully with concrete next steps (risk calculation, printout, time-limited trial).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was clearly co-constructed: the clinician repeatedly solicited Richard's input, integrated his preferences into the printout, and checked for edits. Decisions and materials were created jointly rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly supported with options and framing that the patient decides what to do. The clinician respected Richard's preference for trying lifestyle changes first and offered a time-limited trial if needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited motives linked to staying active and drew out some reasons for action, but relied more on information provision than on eliciting intrinsic motivation. More use of scaling or questions about why staying active matters would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reflected a strong partnership: the clinician asked what would be most helpful, offered tailored options, and co-created a one-page printout with the patient. Power was shared and decisions were collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and supported change-oriented language and linked it to values (staying active), and offered options that highlighted benefits. Missed opportunities to amplify change talk (e.g., asking what being active in retirement looks like or using importance/confidence scaling) to build toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoided reinforcing sustain talk and gently redirected toward problem-solving with neutral responses and options. However, more strategic use of double-sided reflections or summaries could have more actively softened ambivalence.
