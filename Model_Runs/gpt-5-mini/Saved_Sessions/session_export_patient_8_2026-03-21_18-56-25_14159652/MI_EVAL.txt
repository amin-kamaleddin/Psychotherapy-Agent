# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient’s experience, using nonjudgmental language and framing actions as experiments to reduce moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows steady empathic listening and validation and paraphrases concerns well, but misses opportunities for deeper, complex reflections of ambivalence and emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing problematic phone/social media use with behaviorally specific strategies, a time‑limited experiment, and planned follow‑up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction reads as joint problem‑solving with tailored suggestions and invitations to choose, and the clinician solicits and incorporates the patient’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering multiple options, emphasizing choice, and framing tracking as a nonjudgmental experiment rather than a directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitment through choices and planning but relies more on offering solutions than drawing out the patient’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fosters a collaborative stance with many invitations to choose, but supplying numerous clinician‑crafted scripts slightly shifts power away from co‑creating language with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces change‑oriented statements with choices and small experiments, yet misses chances to deepen and amplify change talk with open questions or scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk and offers practical compromises tied to the patient’s reasons, though double‑sided reflections to highlight ambivalence are used infrequently.
