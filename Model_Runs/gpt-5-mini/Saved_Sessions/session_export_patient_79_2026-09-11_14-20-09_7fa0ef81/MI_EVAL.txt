# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, respecting the patient’s limits and pragmatic orientation without shaming or lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong, accurate reflective listening and validation of ambivalence, though emotional content beneath sarcasm/overwhelm could be explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, focused aim on improving inhaler access and refill behavior and skillfully guides the patient to a specific, doable action without taking over.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is genuinely two‑way with the clinician inviting patient input, offering options, and co-constructing a plan that fits the patient’s constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes choice and personal control, offering options and adapting to the patient’s boundaries without pressure or judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the patient’s own plans and practical reasons for change, but tends to focus on logistics rather than deeper values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the work as collaborative, asking permission to brainstorm and repeatedly deferring to what the patient finds most doable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits and amplifies concrete implementation talk and commitments, though could further draw out desire, importance, and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects pragmatically to options, but could more actively soften ambivalence with double‑sided reflections or brief summaries of sustain talk.
