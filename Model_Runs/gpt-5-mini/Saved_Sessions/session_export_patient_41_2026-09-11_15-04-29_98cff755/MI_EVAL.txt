# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence, avoids blaming or pressuring language, and consistently affirms the client's pace and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and validates fear, accurately capturing internal conflict, though reflections are often surface-level with missed chances to deepen emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, appropriate focus on the ten-minute attendance experiment and repeatedly steers the conversation toward concrete next steps while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Firmly collaborative—solicits preferences at each step, offers practical help only when invited, and co-constructs the plan with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and control by offering options, validating the client's pace, and avoiding pressuring language, fostering ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully draws out the client's ideas and preparatory actions (plan, supports), but could more often elicit underlying values and intrinsic motives to strengthen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as an equal partner throughout—asks what the client wants, tailors support to those choices, and frames the work as joint problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits specific, doable change talk and a concrete plan, but could do more to amplify and summarize the client's strengths and reasons to convert preparatory talk into firmer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and avoids confrontation but often moves quickly to solutions; earlier use of double-sided reflections and linking avoidance to values would better soften sustain talk.
