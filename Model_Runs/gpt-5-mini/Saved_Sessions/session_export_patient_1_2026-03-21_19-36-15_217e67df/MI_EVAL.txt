# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental tone, validating the patient's perspective and offering options. There was no shaming or labeling and choices were respected throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good empathic listening with accurate, brief reflections and perspective-taking. Could deepen empathy by naming underlying feelings/concerns and exploring the patient's viewpoint more before offering solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on occasional mini-cigar use and steered the session toward concrete, actionable harm-reduction steps. The visit ended with a specific two-week tracking plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was collaborative—preferences were solicited and a plan was co-constructed. At times the clinician offered many specific solutions, which shifted the balance slightly toward clinician-led problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's choice, asked permission before giving information, and framed suggestions as optional. The clinician supported autonomy by letting the patient pick what felt most helpful.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's preference and some reasons for change, creating opportunities for future self-motivation. Could strengthen evocation by probing importance/confidence and amplifying personal motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally adopted a collaborative stance and invited input while co-creating the plan. Occasionally shifted toward an expert role by offering step-by-step instructions instead of eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change talk and helped translate it into practical steps and limits. Missed opportunities to probe importance/confidence and to amplify small change statements into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk and provided corrective facts, but did not consistently use MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflections) to soften minimization and rationalizations. The clinician tended to move quickly into problem-solving rather than lingering on ambivalence.
