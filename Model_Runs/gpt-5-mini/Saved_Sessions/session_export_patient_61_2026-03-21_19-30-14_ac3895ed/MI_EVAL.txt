# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance through explicit statements and validations, avoiding blame and reducing defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections and perspective-taking were accurate and consistent, capturing overwhelm and skepticism, though opportunities were missed to probe deeper emotions like resentment, shame, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on medication adherence and moved to a concrete, time-bound plan (8:30 AM alarm, one-week trial, Friday check-in) with practical implementation steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction felt partner-oriented with joint decision-making (patient chose alarm time/label and check-in), and future adjustments framed as shared work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly honored choice and control by offering options, framing the plan as a one-week test, and inviting honest reporting without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was an attempt to elicit internal motivation (e.g., the label 'energy'), but much of the session relied on clinician-provided solutions and missed evocative questions about values or meaningful outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance was fostered through offering options and co-creating the plan, though at times the clinician led with specific instructions which slightly increased clinician-driven content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some small change talk was elicited by offering doable steps, but the clinician did not systematically evoke the patient's own desire, reasons, or confidence for change to amplify motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively normalized ambivalence and reframed the task as a low-effort test to defuse resistance, but missed opportunities for double-sided reflections to more explicitly shift sustain talk.
