# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Begins with an explicit nonjudgmental invitation and uses supportive, validating language throughout; avoids labeling and consistently conveys warmth and respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good understanding with accurate reflections of strengths and barriers and useful normalizations, but misses opportunities to explore the emotional meaning of change and deepen empathic reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on restarting activity and moves the session toward concrete actions (calls, trial passes, scheduling, role‑play), keeping momentum from exploration to planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites participation (role‑play, scheduling) and checks preferences, but occasionally leads content by providing scripted language rather than co‑constructing plans with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms Leila's choice and control by offering options and asking permission, though at times gives prescriptive scripts without clearly prefacing them with permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some commitments and acknowledges reasons, but moves quickly into advice and rehearsal instead of systematically drawing out the patient's own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operates largely in partnership—inviting choices and practice—but sometimes shifts to an instructional stance when providing detailed scripts rather than co‑creating them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforces and elicits change talk through praise, scheduling, and role‑play, yet could deepen and amplify importance, reasons, and commitment language with more open eliciting questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk and reframes barriers into practical solutions (trial passes, backup plans, scripts), successfully redirecting toward problem‑solving while missing some double‑sided reflections.
