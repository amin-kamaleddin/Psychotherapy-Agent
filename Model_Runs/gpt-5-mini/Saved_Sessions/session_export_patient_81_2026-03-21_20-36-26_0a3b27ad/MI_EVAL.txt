# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and normalized ambivalence without shaming; a couple of approving phrases appeared but remained respectful and supportive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections and perspective-taking to capture ambivalence and priorities, but missed deeper exploration of identity, belonging, and underlying shame that would deepen empathic resonance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on change-relevant goals with concrete, time-framed steps (auto-transfer, friend check-ins, checklist) and kept momentum toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction felt like joint problem-solving with frequent invitations for Lukas’s input and adjustments to his preferences, shaping a true team approach to planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, respected Lukas’s pace and decisions, and framed supports as optional, which reinforced client autonomy throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Lukas’s own motivations and practical resources (concern about rent, desire to keep connections) and translated them into planned actions and timelines.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and deferential to Lukas’s preferences, though at times the clinician offered specific solutions before inviting Lukas to generate his own options first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced change language using scaling and small-step planning, but could better amplify client statements and more directly elicit stronger preparatory and commitment talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used double-sided reflections to redirect toward change, yet did not fully explore sustain-talk tied to identity/belonging or link those concerns to values and costs.
