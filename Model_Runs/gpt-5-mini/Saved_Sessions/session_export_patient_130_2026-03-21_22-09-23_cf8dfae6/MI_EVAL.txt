# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician was consistently nonjudgmental and validating, affirmed strengths and normalized awkwardness throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate summaries, though there were missed opportunities for deeper affect-focused reflections to deepen emotional connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on reducing risky use while protecting work and marriage, guiding the conversation toward specific plans and contingency strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly collaborative with co-created plans and role-plays, but at times offered multiple scripts without always explicitly checking Viktor’s desire for that level of detail.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed suggestions as options and asked Viktor what he was willing to try, clearly respecting his choice and control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Viktor’s own reasons and commitments for change and a concrete plan, but could have further deepened intrinsic motives and elicited more 'why' language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopted a collaborative stance and invited Viktor’s input on wording and plans, with minor drift toward offering directive suggestions at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited pros/cons, concrete steps, and a confidence rating, but could more frequently amplify spontaneous change talk and probe reasons behind confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and used reflective redirection to neutralize sustain talk, though more double-sided reflections or strategic summaries could further reduce resistance.
