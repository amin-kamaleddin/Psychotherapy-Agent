# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently showed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental language, normalizing ambivalence and validating guilt and friendship worries to create a safe space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective‑taking with timely reflections and supportive prompts, but missed some opportunities for deeper emotional exploration before moving to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with concrete steps, timelines, and repeated redirection to actionable next steps and rehearsals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fostered genuine teamwork by inviting preferences, co‑constructing the plan, and using role‑play with real‑time feedback to support shared problem‑solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice by offering multiple options, asking permission to practice, and inviting the client to set deadlines and articulate readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited motivation and moved to planning using scaling and probes, but could have drawn out more client‑generated reasons for change and past successes to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative and invitational, though occasional directive scripting and immediate role‑play prompts slightly reduced shared power compared with fully client‑led co‑construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk with questions, scaling, and planning, but missed chances to systematically reflect, amplify, and deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without confrontation and normalized ambivalence, but used few MI‑specific strategies (e.g., double‑sided reflections or exploring costs) to shift sustain talk toward change.
