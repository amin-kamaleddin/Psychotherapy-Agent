# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized Julian’s stress; could add more explicit unconditional positive regard and tailoring to his age/legal stressors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Gave empathic statements and brief reflections acknowledging stress and logistics, but did not deepen exploration of emotional ambivalence or use more complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on completing court-ordered service and documentation and moved the session toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicited preferences and adapted materials, yet often led with solutions rather than eliciting Julian’s own ideas first; more explicit co-construction would help.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices and avoided pressuring language, supporting Julian’s decision-making; could more explicitly emphasize his control and invite weighing trade-offs to strengthen ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited limited internal motivation and relied on supplying solutions; missed opportunities to probe personal reasons, values, or confidence to sustain change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included some shared tasks (availability, drafting scripts) but frequently adopted an expert/problem-solver role instead of asking Julian to lead planning or offer ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered tools and affirmed a tentative plan, yet did not systematically elicit Julian’s reasons, values, or confidence or amplify his tentative change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided confronting sustain talk and reframed toward solutions while offering alternatives and coping strategies; could further use double-sided reflections and explicitly validate reluctance.
