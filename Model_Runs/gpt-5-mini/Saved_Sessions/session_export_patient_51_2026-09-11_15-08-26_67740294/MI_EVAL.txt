# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and respect, normalizing time constraints and offering practical, nonjudgmental support. Avoids blame and offers help rather than criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflective listening and acknowledges the patient's time pressures and practical needs, but reflections focus more on logistics than emotional nuance. Naming feelings or ambivalence more explicitly would deepen the empathic stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, sustained focus on a single concrete target behavior (the three-line phone note) and guides planning toward doable next steps. Narrows options appropriately to fit the patient's time constraints.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Models equal partnership by soliciting patient input at every decision point and adapting proposals to the patient's responses. Shared problem-solving is evident as the patient’s choices substantially shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and leaves final decisions to the patient, framing help as optional and preserving the patient's control. Invitations to choose format and wording support autonomy throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits practical motivation, specific plans, and indicators of success, but stops short of probing underlying values or asking about importance and confidence. More questions about reasons for change and confidence would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively partners with the patient by co-creating the template and repeatedly checking preferences, demonstrating power-sharing and respect for the patient's expertise. The clinician defers to the patient's choice rather than prescribing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reflects concrete change statements and commitment (e.g., 'I’ll copy that into my phone'), but could amplify change talk more by asking for reasons and confidence to consolidate commitment. Reflections and summaries reinforce the patient's stated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates time-related barriers and pivots to feasible options without reinforcing sustain talk, reframing obstacles into practical solutions. Could further use strategic techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections) to explicitly soften sustain talk when it appears.
