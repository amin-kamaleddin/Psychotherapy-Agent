# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance with frequent validations and normalization, explicitly honoring Maria's wishes and distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong, accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking that helped Maria feel understood and facilitated safety cooperation, with only minor missed opportunities to probe deeper feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on immediate safety throughout the encounter, repeatedly steering the session toward specific, actionable steps to reduce risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative—co‑constructed practical safety steps, solicited Maria's preferences, offered scripts and stayed alongside her to implement the plan, creating genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices, honored preferences (e.g., avoiding police), and asked permission, while at times using necessary directive requests for immediate safety that slightly limited autonomy emphasis.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited pragmatic, immediate commitments for safety but did not systematically draw out or amplify Maria's internal reasons for living (the 'tiny part' that cares for her sister) or broader DARN‑C language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally fostered a collaborative stance by inviting choices and helping craft messages, though safety imperatives required more directive moments that constrained full power‑sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some change talk around immediate safety behaviors and affirmed risk‑reduction, but missed deeper follow‑up to amplify fleeting change language and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and respectfully redirected despair toward safety actions, though could more actively soften sustain talk with double‑sided reflections that highlight small change language.
