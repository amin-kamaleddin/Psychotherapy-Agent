# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental acceptance (e.g., summarizing both sides, explicitly stating "I accept that") and avoids confrontation or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate, timely reflective listening and perspective-taking that captures ambivalence and Viktor's goals, deepening rapport rather than merely restating facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on a specific behavior change (the one-month 'no drugs at home' test) and steers toward actionable supports and operationalized plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is clearly co‑constructed: the clinician solicits Viktor's priorities, preferred language, and choices, offering options and inviting him to shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Viktor's sense of choice by offering multiple options, asking which feels doable, and inviting him to modify wording and plans to fit his preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits practical reasons and concrete plans (reduce fights, keep house drug‑free) but misses deeper probing of intrinsic values and confidence scaling to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly frames the work as a collaboration, privileging Viktor's expertise about what will work and repeatedly asking how the clinician can support his chosen approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces useful change talk and planning (e.g., 'not bringing anything back,' exit lines) but does not systematically strengthen confidence or elicit a firm commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and respectfully accepts ambivalence while pivoting to pragmatic steps, though could more actively use double‑sided reflections or explore costs of staying the same to further reduce sustain talk.
