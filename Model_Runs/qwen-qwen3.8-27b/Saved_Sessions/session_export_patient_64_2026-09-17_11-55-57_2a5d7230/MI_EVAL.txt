# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Respectful and non-judgmental opening with a few warm moments, but the four consecutive mechanical repetitions of "I understand. Can you tell me more about that?" undermine genuine warmth and individualized positive regard. No affirmations of courage, normalization of ambivalence, or explicit respect for autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One strong complex reflection early on effectively captures both cognitive and emotional content. However, this is followed by four vague, non-specific prompts that fail to reflect back specific content, and the psychiatrist misses key emotional breakthroughs such as Jordan's admission that his logical argument "falls apart."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the flu vaccine decision without drifting into unrelated areas. However, it lacks strategic progression through a clear arc and does not use summaries or guide the session toward a concrete goal or action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction follows a strictly one-directional pattern of generic prompt → response → generic prompt, with no shared problem-solving or mutual influence. There is no evidence of the clinician framing the conversation as a joint exploration or adapting based on Jordan's specific contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not pressure, advise, or make decisions for Jordan, meeting a baseline level of autonomy support. However, there are no explicit statements affirming Jordan's ownership of the decision, his capacity to choose, or his freedom to decide without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriate open-ended questions in the first half elicit some of Jordan's own motivations and concerns. However, the repeated generic prompt in the second half fails to draw out values, importance, or confidence, and the psychiatrist misses opportunities for scaling questions or future-focused evocative techniques.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No advice-giving or expert assumptions (baseline met), but the session does not demonstrate genuine partnership through co-creation, agenda-setting, or acknowledging Jordan as the expert on his own life. The repeated generic prompts further erode the sense of collaborative engagement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan produces significant and increasingly strong change talk throughout the session, progressing from doubt to genuine reconsideration. However, the psychiatrist does not actively reinforce, amplify, or deepen this change talk with strategic reflections, scaling questions, or exploration of next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify Jordan's sustain talk, and it naturally diminishes over the course of the session. However, no MI-consistent strategies such as double-sided reflections or reframing are used to actively soften or redirect sustain talk when it appears.
