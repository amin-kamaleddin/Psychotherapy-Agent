# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance without labeling, shaming, or lecturing. Acceptance could be strengthened with warmer, more individualized affirmations of Lucía’s frustration and effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is adequate and improves later with strong reflections of ambivalence, but it is inconsistent and sometimes formulaic. More emotionally specific reflections of shame, worry, and fear would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally remains focused on smoking and moves toward a concrete, manageable pause behavior. Direction is limited by repetitive early questions and a slower initial move toward a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as joint exploration and uses Lucía’s ideas to shape the next steps. Collaboration could be improved by reducing repeated frames and more closely following her shifting concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by avoiding direct advice, pressure, or prescriptive language. More explicit statements about Lucía’s freedom to choose how or whether to change would further strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes useful client-generated coping strategies such as walking, water, staring, and pen chewing. Evocation is limited because it does not consistently develop intrinsic motivation or importance/confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Lucía as the expert on her own life and invites her to define what would be useful. A stronger partnership stance would include even more responsive follow-up to her emerging ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are effective efforts to evoke change talk, especially around existing coping strategies and health concerns. Change talk remains underdeveloped because importance, confidence, and personal reasons for change are not fully explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and uses double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk about needing a break. More consistent redirection from sustain talk toward health, daughter, or work-related change goals would strengthen this criterion.
