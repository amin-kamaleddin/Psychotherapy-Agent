# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed warm, nonjudgmental regard and accepted Sofia’s hurt, fear, and defensive responses without moralizing. Even when a brief inference was corrected, the therapist remained respectful and stayed focused on validating Sofia’s experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated strong reflective listening, often capturing Sofia’s ambivalence between wanting connection and fearing exploitation or being misread. A notable miss was introducing “power” when Sofia had said “risky,” and the therapist did not fully repair or dwell in the deeper emotional pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the referral concerns—peer conflict, school avoidance, and safety at school—while gradually narrowing to a manageable next step. Each question moved the conversation toward a concrete, client-controlled action without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with Sofia’s ideas driving the session rather than the therapist imposing an agenda. The therapist co-constructed meaning and next steps with Sofia, creating a clear sense of partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist consistently emphasized Sofia’s choice, ownership, and readiness, respecting her “done chasing” boundary without pressure. However, occasional specific suggestions and positive appraisal slightly reduced the purity of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicited motivation from within Sofia instead of providing reasons for change. Questions drew out Sofia’s own values and goals, such as wanting control, safety, dignity, and a real test of the friendship.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Sofia as the expert on her own experience and followed her contributions rather than leading from an expert agenda. Phrases like “in your own words” and “what part feels most like ‘me’?” reinforced a genuinely collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist frequently evoked and expanded Sofia’s change talk, especially around stopping so much skipping, testing the friendship in a low-pressure way, and not chasing. The main limitation was not explicitly eliciting confidence or importance language to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by acknowledging fear and risk without arguing, then redirecting toward smaller, client-controlled actions. The main limitation was occasional continued use of “risk” and “gamble” language, which could have been softened further with more balanced or double-sided reflections.
