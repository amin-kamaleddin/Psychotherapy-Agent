# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with specific, individualized affirmations and normalization of the client's discomfort. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming her identity as a musician and student rather than just her session behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled reflective listening that accurately captures emotional patterns, particularly the 'whatever' coping mechanism and its automaticity. Reflections remain somewhat surface-level and do not deeply explore the emotional weight of being flagged at 17 or the shame/fear underlying it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The target behavior (alcohol use) is never addressed despite being the reason for referral. The session drifts through tangential topics without ever connecting back to the change goal, representing a fundamental failure of direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance in managing session logistics and structure, with genuine invitations for client input. Collaboration is limited to session-level decisions rather than co-creating a shared understanding or therapeutic direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit support for the client's choice and pace, including accommodation of time constraints without guilt or pressure. Support is somewhat passive in scope, limited to her autonomy about the session rather than extending to her choices about the target behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Well-crafted evocative questions that draw out self-reflection about coping and experience. However, evocation is not directed at the change goal — questions target the session and general coping rather than alcohol use or motivation to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong non-expert stance that consistently positions the client as the authority on her own experience. Partnership remains somewhat one-directional, with the psychiatrist managing the interaction rather than fully sharing power in deciding where to go.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No evocation is directed at the target behavior (alcohol use), and the client never produces change talk regarding the referral reason. The session ends without any movement toward change talk about the specific behavior of concern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles present sustain talk (reluctance, desire to leave) competently by validating without reinforcing. The absence of any engagement with alcohol use means there is no opportunity to demonstrate this skill at the level of the target behavior.
