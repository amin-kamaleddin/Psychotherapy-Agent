# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms Amina’s efforts and values. Acceptance is strong but sometimes tied to change-oriented framing, and the final fear is not explicitly validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate perspective-taking, especially with double-sided reflections of ambivalence. Some deeper emotional material is underexplored, and the session ends without a final empathic response to her lingering concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, appropriate focus on a concrete target behavior—calm bedtime boundaries. Direction is purposeful but could be strengthened by more explicit connection to values and a concrete next step or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with Amina’s ideas and language shaping the conversation. The psychiatrist still guides the agenda noticeably toward bedtime boundaries and calmer phrasing, limiting full co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist keeps decision-making with Amina and reinforces her ownership of choices in her home. Autonomy support could be stronger with more explicit autonomy-affirming language and less steering toward a specific boundary.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits motivation from within Amina rather than providing reasons for change. Evocation is strong but not fully expert because it does not consistently deepen by exploring ability, importance, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and invites Amina’s perspective throughout. Partnership is strong but not fully transparent in power-sharing because of a clear agenda toward bedtime boundaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits meaningful change talk through open-ended, client-centered questions. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, summarized, or strategically developed toward confidence or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges ambivalence with reflections. However, sustain talk remains central at times, and the final fear of being seen as “the bad guy” is left unresolved without a strategic pivot toward values or strengths.
