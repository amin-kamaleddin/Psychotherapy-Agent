# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids challenging the client’s habit framing or defensiveness. It is strong but somewhat procedural, with less explicit warmth or personal validation of the client’s effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, exhaustion, and the client’s desire for autonomy. The empathy is consistent but could be more emotionally nuanced by exploring underlying fears, relief, or anxiety more directly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on after-work drinking and steadily moves toward a concrete first step. The psychiatrist effectively narrows ambivalence into a testable behavior and later evaluation plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on client-generated ideas and frames suggestions as something to evaluate rather than prescribe. It would be stronger with more explicit invitations for the client to critique or modify the suggested plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes that the choice belongs to the client and avoids imposing rules or surveillance. This strongly supports self-direction, especially given the client’s concern about external control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively draw out the client’s own motivation, plans, and success signals. The evocation is strong but more behavior-focused than values, importance, confidence, and future-oriented motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to define the plan and treats him as the source of expertise. Some clinician-led framing and structuring suggest the partnership is strong but not fully maximized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently reflects and amplifies the client’s own change language about avoiding autopilot and making a real choice. It would be stronger if it more explicitly explored values, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids debating labels and reframes the experiment as nonjudgmental to reduce defensiveness. It could more fully reflect the legitimate function of drinking, such as stress relief, using a double-sided reflection.
