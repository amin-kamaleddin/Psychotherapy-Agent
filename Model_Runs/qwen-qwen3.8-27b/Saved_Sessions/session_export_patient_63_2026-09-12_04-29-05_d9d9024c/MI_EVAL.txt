# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly normalizes Jordan's struggle without blaming him. The stance is generally supportive but can feel somewhat task-focused and formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a strong early reflection and adequate validation of Jordan's main concerns. However, later reflections become more surface-level and do not consistently explore the deeper emotional weight of his ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on shoplifting urges, peer pressure, and in-the-moment refusal strategies. The main limitation is that the focus remains somewhat diffuse and plan-driven rather than strategically MI-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan's input and adjusts suggestions based on his concerns. However, the session becomes partly "here are my options, which one do you pick?" rather than a fully co-created exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often asks Jordan to choose what feels doable and avoids overt pressure. Autonomy support would be stronger if it more explicitly emphasized that Jordan is in charge and can choose not to use any strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with appropriate evocative questions about change goals, past success, readiness, and confidence. Much of the session then shifts toward advising and adding plan elements rather than helping Jordan generate his own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on the surface by asking Jordan to choose among options. However, the session is heavily shaped by clinician-generated tactics and plan components, which limits true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan offers useful change talk early on, such as being done with the trouble and wanting a short refusal. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or develop this change talk into a stronger, client-generated commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing, normalizing, or amplifying it. The work is mostly neutral and could be more strategic by using double-sided reflections to pivot Jordan's concerns toward change.
