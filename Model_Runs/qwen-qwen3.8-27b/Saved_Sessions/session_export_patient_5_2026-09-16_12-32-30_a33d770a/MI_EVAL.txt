# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect and nonjudgment, validating Alessia’s concerns without blame or criticism. The warmth remains professional and somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized, which keeps it below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are strong perspective-taking statements that accurately capture Alessia’s fears about safety, work, and avoiding another crisis. The psychiatrist often shifts quickly from emotional validation into information and problem-solving, limiting the depth of empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior: safely transitioning from phenelzine to citalopram, including timing, side effects, monitoring, and dietary precautions. The focus is maintained more through informational teaching than client-led exploration, preventing a higher rating.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration because Alessia chooses which questions to use and raises specific concerns. However, much of the session is driven by the psychiatrist supplying scripts, statistics, and lists, leaving less space for shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking what Alessia wants to focus on and whether she wants help wording questions. It would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement that the decisions are hers and fewer ready-made scripts supplied.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is an initial evocative opening, but the session quickly becomes informational and directive. The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit Alessia’s values, confidence, or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alessia’s concerns guide the agenda, and she is invited to choose or tweak some scripts. The psychiatrist remains largely in an expert/educator role, which limits a fully collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to cultivate change talk, with most of the session focused on medical information and practical preparation. Alessia does express some change-related language, but it is not explicitly reflected, amplified, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing resistance and validates concerns about medication, burden, and restrictions. The softening relies mostly on neutral validation and redirection rather than stronger MI strategies that reframe ambivalence toward change.
