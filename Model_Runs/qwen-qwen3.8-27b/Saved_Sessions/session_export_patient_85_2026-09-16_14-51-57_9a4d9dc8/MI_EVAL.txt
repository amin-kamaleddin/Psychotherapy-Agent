# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently normalizes Marco's mixed feelings and avoids judgment. It is warm but somewhat formulaic, with fewer explicit affirmations of courage or resilience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture pride, frustration, and relational concerns. Deeper shame, disappointment, or the meaning of juice is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing fruit juice and moves logically from awareness to practical home planning. This is a clear strength.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco's household ideas shape the plan, but some frames are therapist-generated. More explicit joint language would strengthen equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the decision-maker without pressure. Explicit statements that whether to try the plan is entirely his choice would be stronger.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions elicit useful behavior plans and self-monitoring. Deeper values, meaning, and confidence are not fully evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist treats Marco as expert on his rhythms and avoids imposing advice. The agenda is still somewhat therapist-led rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Many change statements emerge naturally, such as keeping one breakfast glass and using sparkling water. Desire, need, ability, and confidence are not systematically reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Desire to enjoy juice is acknowledged and reframed without debate. It could more explicitly link keeping some juice to broader health and family values.
