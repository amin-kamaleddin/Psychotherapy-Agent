# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honored Tyler’s request to avoid a lecture. Acceptance was strong but somewhat task-oriented rather than consistently warm and affirming of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Tyler’s central conflict between social drinking and protecting his degree. However, important emotional experiences, such as fear and social anxiety, were not fully explored before moving into planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing alcohol-related risk while preserving social connection and academic goals. The progression from ambivalence to scaling to a concrete Friday plan was coherent, though the focus occasionally shifted toward problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well by co-constructing the plan and adjusting it based on Tyler’s preferences. Collaboration was strong, but the counselor remained the primary generator of many tactics and the written script.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invited choice and respected Tyler’s preferences, such as keeping the plan short and casual. Autonomy support was good, though the detailed recommendations slightly overshadowed explicit reinforcement of Tyler’s ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used scaling and asked about realistic changes, eliciting some of Tyler’s own motivations. However, the session moved quickly into advice and planning, limiting deeper evocation of Tyler’s values, confidence, and future self-image.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tyler was treated as the expert on his own situation, and his contributions clearly shaped the plan. Partnership was effective, though the counselor’s substantial menu of tactics gave the interaction a somewhat expert/problem-solving flavor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited change talk through scaling and questions about what would increase readiness. However, promising readiness statements were not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided reinforcing the social benefits of drinking and used balanced reflections such as protecting his degree while keeping social time. Sustain talk was acknowledged but not actively reframed or strategically reduced.
