# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or labeling. The score is limited by a somewhat corrective, risk-focused framing that keeps the interaction more clinical than fully affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledge the ritual and dislike of the word “addiction,” but they remain at the behavior level. The clinician misses deeper emotional themes such as feeling over-controlled, pathologized, or disrespected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has clear practical direction toward cleaning and hygiene steps. The MI focus on reducing mini-cigar use is inconsistent, with the conversation shifting toward scheduling and pre-visit logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is shaped by Ahmed’s input, including flossing cues and a focused hygienist question. It is not fully collaborative because the clinician still supplies most of the structure, content, and prescriptive plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician strongly affirms Ahmed’s freedom to choose and respects his decision not to quit. Directive instructions, such as “try not to smoke for a few hours,” slightly reduce the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open and choice-based prompts elicit some patient-driven actions. Evocation is limited because the clinician provides reasons for change rather than drawing out Ahmed’s internal motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites preferences and some patient choices, but the session remains largely expert-driven. A stronger approach would more clearly defer to Ahmed’s knowledge of his routine and co-create the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits practical action statements like flossing and rinsing. Change talk is limited because it focuses on compliance rather than Ahmed’s personal values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician accepts Ahmed’s perspective without arguing directly, but repeated risk education can sound corrective and may strengthen sustain talk. More double-sided reflections and roll-with-resistance would better soften his resistance.
