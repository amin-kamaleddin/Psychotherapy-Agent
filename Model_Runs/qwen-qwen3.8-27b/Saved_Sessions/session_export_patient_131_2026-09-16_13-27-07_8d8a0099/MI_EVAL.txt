# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the client without pressure. Repeated evaluative praise and an intrusive offer to stay made the stance less fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's ambivalence, priorities, and fear. It stops short of deeper emotional exploration around shame, grief, and cultural family weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol reduction, violence recovery, and rebuilding family trust. The conversation moves clearly from priorities to a concrete, time-bound plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes key details, but the psychiatrist largely provides the structure and recaps. More client-led summarizing and shared agenda-setting would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, respects client decisions, and builds on client-generated ideas. Over-monitoring suggestions and therapist-generated reminders slightly reduce the sense of self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions effectively elicit priorities, readiness, and coping ideas. Deeper evocation of values, identity, discrepancy, and future motivation becomes less consistent during planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts client pushback and offers choices, but still leads the agenda and structure. More explicit power-sharing and agenda negotiation would better support equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces the client's commitments and concrete steps. Change talk is front-loaded and could be more consistently amplified as the session becomes task-focused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and addressed with flexible, autonomy-respecting options. More classic MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, would further soften ambivalence.
