# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and affirming while honoring the client's time constraints. The main limitation is repetitive affirmations after the client says goodbye, which slightly undermines her stated boundary.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Empathy is strong in recognizing the client's time pressure and need for a brief, usable answer. Some reflections assume emotional difficulty that the client did not clearly express.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on medication safety and practical next steps. The opening drifts briefly toward broader emotional support, and post-farewell comments create a minor pacing inconsistency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively by using the client's stated constraints to shape a concise safety note. Continuing to drive closure after the client repeatedly says goodbye slightly weakens the collaborative balance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering options, checking wording, and respecting the client's pace. It would be stronger with more explicit affirmation that any follow-up is entirely the client's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions draw out the client's priorities and practical preferences. Evocation is not sustained after the safety information is provided, as the psychiatrist mostly affirms rather than deepening the client's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are good partnership moves, such as asking what would be most helpful and checking whether the wording works. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary expert and continues driving the session even after the client signals she is leaving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms and reframes some of the client's change talk as responsible self-care. There are few follow-up questions to further elicit the client's reasons, values, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively avoids arguing with the client's time pressure and adapts to a concise safety response. A stronger move would be a double-sided reflection that validates the time constraint while highlighting the value of the safety decision.
