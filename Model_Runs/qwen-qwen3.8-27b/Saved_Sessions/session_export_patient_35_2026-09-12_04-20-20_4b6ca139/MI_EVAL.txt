# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, normalizing, and affirming, but did not fully repair Elena’s feeling of being blamed near the end. Stronger acceptance would have more explicitly validated her as a caring parent and normalized the slip.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Elena’s ambivalence, fatigue, and the quiet-home trigger, but the responses often shifted quickly toward advice or planning. The final emotional burden of feeling blamed and “just a mom” was not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session mostly stayed focused on stress, triggers, and the son’s exposure, progressing toward a small concrete plan. The ending drifted by adding medical evidence, multiple options, and a follow-up question, slightly diluting focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena clearly shaped the session through permissions, constraints, and choosing the keys/urge step, creating a realistic tiny experiment. The main limitation is that the psychiatrist still supplied many options and information, making it somewhat clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was strongly supported, including saying it would be okay to smoke if needed and framing the plan as nonjudgmental. Some directive menus and the prompt to choose a risk-reduction step could still feel pressuring given Elena’s exhaustion and defensiveness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open and scaling questions elicited a small plan, but often interrupted deeper evocation before drawing out Elena’s own values, confidence, or motivations. The final shift to medical information was more informational than evocative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The process was collaborative because Elena identified the trigger, rejected impractical options, and selected a doable cue. However, repeated clinician-generated lists kept the interaction somewhat expert-led rather than fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged when Elena agreed to try a small cue, but the psychiatrist did not consistently ask about importance, desire, ability, or commitment. More client-centered elicitation of her own reasons would strengthen this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena’s sustain talk was acknowledged, but often followed by advice, options, or medical information rather than being gently redirected through double-sided reflections. The final thirdhand-smoke message risked increasing defensiveness and reinforcing a failure narrative.
