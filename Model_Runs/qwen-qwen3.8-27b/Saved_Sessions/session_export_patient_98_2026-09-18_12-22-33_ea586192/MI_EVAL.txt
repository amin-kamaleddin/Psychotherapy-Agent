# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with frequent validation and normalization throughout the session. Slightly limited by a structured, professional delivery and lack of an explicit statement of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy capturing both cognitive and emotional dimensions of the client's experience, including the ambivalence of hope and fear coexisting. Reflections are nuanced and track emotional shifts without rushing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical, progressive arc from exploring ambivalence to a concrete, client-generated action plan. Each question builds on the previous answer with well-calibrated pacing and no drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully egalitarian dynamic where the psychiatrist consistently invites input, builds on the client's contributions, and explicitly credits the client with his own insights. The final plan is entirely the client's creation, facilitated but not directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforces client ownership throughout with phrases like 'that's your plan, not theirs' and 'you just built your own case for it.' The commitment to try tomorrow is the client's own statement, elicited through questions rather than directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every insight in the session is the client's own, facilitated by skillful questioning and reflection rather than external reasoning. Results in a genuine, internally-motivated commitment that emerged organically from the conversation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on his own body and life, asking rather than telling and never imposing solutions. The session structure follows the client's lead and builds on what he offers rather than steering toward a predetermined agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically cultivates change talk by picking up and amplifying the client's own language, asking questions that elicit hope, and reflecting change talk with meaning. The progression from ambivalence to a concrete commitment was achieved entirely through evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never challenges or dismisses the client's concerns; instead validates them, reframes to preserve dignity, and uses double-sided reflections to hold both sides. The client generates his own reframe ('the gotcha's smaller too'), demonstrating genuine softening.
