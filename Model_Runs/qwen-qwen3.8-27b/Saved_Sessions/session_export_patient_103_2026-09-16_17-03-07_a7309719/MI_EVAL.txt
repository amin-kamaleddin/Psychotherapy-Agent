# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Nabil’s frustration. The score is limited because some responses subtly steered toward problem-solving before fully containing the emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, with accurate reflections of Nabil’s values, ambivalence, and the meaning of punctuality as respect. It fell short of a 5 because the deeper sense of injustice, powerlessness, and being treated as “in the way” was not fully captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on appointment delays, feeling overlooked, and the impact on work and family. It was not a 5 because the specific target for change was not clearly agreed upon or narrowed collaboratively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treated Nabil as the expert on his own life and invited him to define what would feel respectful. Collaboration was limited by continued use of a “fair ask” or boundary framework that Nabil resisted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were several autonomy-supportive statements, and the psychiatrist avoided directly telling Nabil what to do. However, repeated emphasis on “asking” or making a “fair ask” began to feel subtly directive after Nabil repeatedly rejected that framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used frequent open-ended questions to draw out Nabil’s values, standards, and preferences. Evocation was solid but not fully skilled in deepening Nabil’s limited change talk into stronger internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The stance was largely collaborative, with frequent invitations for Nabil to define the problem and desired visit. The partnership was slightly weakened by the therapist’s persistent focus on a particular “fair ask” framework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made reasonable efforts to evoke change-relevant language and values, such as caring for family and wanting to be seen. However, explicit change talk was limited and was not consistently built on or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Nabil’s sustain talk and generally did not contradict his statements. The score is limited because sustain talk was not consistently softened or redirected; the conversation remained anchored in Nabil’s reasons for not making a request.
