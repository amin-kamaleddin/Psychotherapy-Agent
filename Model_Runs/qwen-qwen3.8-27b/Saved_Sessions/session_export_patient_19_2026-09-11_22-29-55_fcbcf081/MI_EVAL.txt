# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone while normalizing stress and shame. However, the interaction can feel procedural when it repeatedly shifts into solution-mode and checklists.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate surface-level reflections about financial stress, the pull of the cards, and discomfort around handing them to her sister. Deeper emotional and existential ambivalence, such as the tension between pub-based social freedom and wanting a stable future, is underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing gambling-related financial harm without significant drift. The direction is somewhat overly action-focused and could more deliberately connect behavior to values, readiness, and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is meaningful collaboration in parts of the session, particularly when the psychiatrist builds on Anya’s ideas such as the $50 prepaid card and keeping cards in a drawer. That said, the psychiatrist often drives the agenda with practical recommendations, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options and asking which steps feel doable, and generally respects Anya’s refusal to hand over her cards. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated suggestions, especially continuing to offer to draft a text after Anya has declined.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with some open and evocative questions, but much of the interaction becomes advice-heavy rather than client-led. Important opportunities are missed to draw out Anya’s own values, motivations, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and follows some of Anya’s contributions, such as the prepaid card and drawer/envelope ideas. However, the overall stance remains fairly expert-driven, with repeated structured plans and suggestions that can feel more managing than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some practical change talk, including trying the $50 prepaid card, keeping cards in a drawer, and considering staying home. These ideas are not consistently deepened or connected to Anya’s values, ability, desire, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and acknowledges concerns about needing cards for groceries and the social role of the pub. Key sustain talk remains underexplored, with limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to help Anya integrate the appeal of the status quo with the value of change.
