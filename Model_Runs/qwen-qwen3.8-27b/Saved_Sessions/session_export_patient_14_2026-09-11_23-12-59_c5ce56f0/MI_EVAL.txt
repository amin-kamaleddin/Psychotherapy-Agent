# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance but quickly shifts to practical and medical instructions, which limits warmth. The acceptance is adequate but not strongly affirming or empathically rich.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate early reflections on frustration and stress, but empathy remains largely surface-level. The therapist misses opportunities to explore the client's deeper ambivalence and practical constraints.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on weight, blood pressure, breathlessness, and practical next steps, and it narrows over time. However, direction is often driven by the clinician's medical agenda rather than collaborative MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative gestures, such as asking what the client wants, but the interaction remains expert-driven. The therapist continues to offer lists and scripts despite the client's preference to lead with his own constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist uses some choice language but also gives many directives and prescriptive instructions. Autonomy support is weakened by continued suggestions after the client says he will handle things himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist mostly provides information and advice rather than eliciting the client's own values, goals, and motivations. There are missed opportunities to build on the client's spontaneous change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely led by the psychiatrist through lists, scripts, and specific instructions. Collaborative gestures are present but do not substantially shift the power balance toward the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers clear change talk, but the therapist rarely reflects, amplifies, or builds on it. Responses tend toward advice and logistics instead of evoking further client-driven reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist does not argue with the client's barriers and generally acknowledges them. However, he does not consistently use MI-consistent techniques to redirect sustain talk into change talk.
