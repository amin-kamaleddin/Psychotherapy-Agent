# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout; never labels or dismisses the patient's ambivalence. Occasionally reads as slightly formulaic rather than deeply individualized, and lacks explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions with remarkable precision, including sophisticated complex reflections that validate multiple perspectives simultaneously. Deep attunement to underlying values (fear of loss, love for wife, desire for agency) without overreaching.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and consistent focus on medication adherence with a logical, purposeful progression from ambivalence exploration to action planning. Never becomes rigid or pushy; direction emerges naturally from the patient's own values and concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative structure with inclusive language and co-construction of the action plan. The psychiatrist functions as a facilitator and equal partner, inviting the patient to be an active participant in problem-solving rather than an expert authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces patient ownership through open questions and never uses directive language or imposes decisions. The final plan is entirely the patient's own, supported without any added prescriptive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the patient's own motivations without providing external reasons, using his values (mountain trip, being the one saying 'let's go') as anchors. Could be slightly more systematic in eliciting confidence and ability language to build a stronger confidence narrative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise about change resides within the patient, using shared exploration language rather than expert guidance. The power dynamic is genuinely equal throughout; the psychiatrist never prescribes or assumes to know what the patient needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently picks up on and amplifies the patient's change talk, effectively using his own values as anchors for motivation. Could be slightly more deliberate in deepening change talk when it appears, such as explicitly asking about confidence on a scale.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully through validation without reinforcement and double-sided reflections that keep the door open for change. Could be more active in strategically reframing certain sustain talk moments, such as connecting 'feeling fine' back to the father's story.
