

# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 / 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone throughout. Normalizes patient's anxiety and fear of freezing; explicitly removes shame (e.g., "That's not stupid at all," "That's not a character flaw"). Minor limitation: occasional slightly cheerleader/affirming tone (e.g., "That's not a small step," "The good news is…") that leans toward conditional encouragement rather than fully embodied unconditional positive regard.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 / 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking. Captures nuanced distinctions (pills vs. monitoring, "no time to think" as the core difference, "two things stacked on top of each other"). Occasionally remains at the cognitive level rather than fully accessing the emotional undercurrent (e.g., the vulnerability and identity threat beneath "asking for help makes it feel like I'm admitting I can't handle it").

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 / 5
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the target behavior (blood sugar monitoring / diabetes self-management). Logical trajectory: identifying the overwhelming part → exploring what would help → concrete action step → reducing barriers. Somewhat linear progression; occasional use of summarizing to consolidate progress and reinforce the patient's own narrative of change would strengthen the directional flow.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 / 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative session. Co-constructed dialogue with inclusive language ("I'd like to understand," "just for us"). The psychiatrist actively seeks input at every decision point and builds on the patient's contributions. The final move of practicing the script "just for us" is a strong embodiment of shared effort. Power dynamic is genuinely equal.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 / 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit, and well-integrated. The opening statement ("I want to work alongside you, not tell you what to do") sets the tone. No prescriptive or directive language at any point. All suggestions framed as exploratory questions. Explicitly affirms ownership ("The framing is yours to use in whatever way gets you through the door"). The final invitation to practice the script is an invitation, not a directive.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 / 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws out the patient's own motivations, ideas, and solutions rather than providing them. Never gives advice or reasons for change. Trusts the patient to generate solutions. Could have gone deeper at key moments (e.g., when the patient says "I can probably do that," a follow-up like "What in your experience makes you feel like you can do that?" would further develop ability self-statements). More systematic evocation across all DARN categories would elevate to a 5.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 / 5
**Comments:** Exceptional and textbook example of MI partnership. Opening explicitly positions the patient as the expert on his own life. The session is clearly shaped by the patient's responses, following wherever the patient leads (monitoring → learning style → wife/educator → phone call → framing → script). Never assumes, never prescribes. Consistently frames the work as joint exploration.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 / 5
**Comments:** Skillfully cultivates change talk throughout. Patient produces multiple instances of desire ("I'd probably go"), ability ("I can probably do that"), and commitment ("I think if I actually said that on Friday, I could keep going"). The final evocative question ("Would you be willing to try it here, just for us?") effectively elicits commitment language. Could have more explicitly amplified and reflected back the patient's own confidence statements, and a confidence ruler or full commitment summary would have further solidified the change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 / 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively. Normalizes rather than minimizes concerns (e.g., "That's not a character flaw"). Holds multiple paths without dismissing (e.g., "You're actually holding two paths at the same time"). Redirects toward solutions without invalidating. Sustain talk was relatively mild in this session, limiting opportunities for advanced techniques. More creative use of double-sided reflections or evoking underlying values (e.g., "I wonder if the part of you that's worried about the phone is actually trying to protect you from…") would demonstrate a higher level of technique.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 4.4 / 5**
