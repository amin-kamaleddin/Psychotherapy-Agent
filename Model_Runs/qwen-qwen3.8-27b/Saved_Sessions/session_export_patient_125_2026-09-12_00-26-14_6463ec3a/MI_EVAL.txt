# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, calm, and nonjudgmental, validating Tariq’s frustration and desire for family contact. Acceptance could be stronger by explicitly affirming Tariq’s strengths, such as his care for family and prior compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician captures frustration, unfairness, and the importance of family contact, but reflections remain somewhat surface-level. Deeper emotions, such as fear of missing his father’s birthday or feeling punished, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the immediate issue of phone privileges and family contact. However, the direction is overly narrow and task-focused, with less connection to broader goals such as managing frustration, maintaining compliance, or preparing for release.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and asks Tariq to choose, showing some collaboration. However, the content is largely clinician-generated, making the interaction more like a menu of solutions than a true co-created partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally acknowledges choice, but repeated scripts, plans, and safety checks can feel controlling. Tariq is not consistently encouraged to identify his own preferred path or fully own his decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited evocation of Tariq’s own motivations, values, or confidence in change. The session relies more on providing practical scripts and steps than drawing out his internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes an expert-driven, problem-solving stance rather than a collaborative one. Tariq is not consistently treated as the primary expert on his own experience, values, or solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq expresses some change-relevant language, such as wanting to connect with family before release, but the psychiatrist does not sufficiently reflect, amplify, or expand this. More skillful follow-up questions could have strengthened his motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq’s sustain talk about unfairness and not admitting fault is acknowledged, but not strategically softened. The clinician does not use balanced or double-sided reflections to redirect attention toward broader goals or consequences.
