# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens with genuine warmth acknowledging court-mandated attendance and never judges or labels. Acceptance is limited in scope with few explicit affirmations of Jordan's worth beyond his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are notably weak; the phrase 'I understand. Can you tell me more about that?' is repeated verbatim three times as a generic prompt. Misses multiple opportunities to reflect emotional depth (mother's quiet sadness, fear of social exclusion, guilt).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains focus on the target behavior without drifting, but the session circles between topics without building progressive momentum toward a concrete focus or next step. The repeated generic prompts create a lack of forward movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language ('I'm curious,' 'what would that look like for you?') but the repeated prompts position the psychiatrist as driving the conversation rather than co-exploring. No explicit agenda negotiation or power-sharing is evident.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong opening directly addressing external pressure and affirming Jordan's agency; questions are framed as exploratory rather than directive. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Jordan's right to make any decision, including to continue.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes several reasonable evocative moves (imagining a day without shoplifting, past moments of saying no, smallest version of change) that do elicit some change talk. However, evocation is inconsistent and lacks strategic depth in following up on emergent change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a generally non-authoritarian stance with no imposed advice or solutions, and invites client-led direction. However, the repeated generic prompts undermine true partnership by positioning the psychiatrist as controlling the pace rather than following Jordan's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk through evocative questions (ability-based and values-based), but fails to consistently reinforce, amplify, or build on the change talk that emerges. Misses opportunities to reflect statements like 'it's not that I can't do it' and the weights/lifting response.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does not actively reinforce sustain talk (a minimal positive), but also does not strategically soften or redirect it using double-sided reflections or reframing. Prominent sustain talk around peer influence, temptation, and lack of alternatives remains largely unaddressed.
