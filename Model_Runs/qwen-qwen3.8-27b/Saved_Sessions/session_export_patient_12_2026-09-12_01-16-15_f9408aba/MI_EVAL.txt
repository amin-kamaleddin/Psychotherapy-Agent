# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, especially in affirming small steps. Some procedural or compliance-oriented moments slightly reduce the depth of warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers helpful reflections, such as naming pain, helplessness, and common ambivalence. These reflections remain somewhat surface-level and do not consistently capture deeper shame, fear, or isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safety, substance use, and immediate coping strategies. It does not consistently build toward a clear, client-driven change focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and responds to the client's preferences in a collaborative way. The interaction remains mostly clinician-led, with the client selecting from the psychiatrist's options rather than generating her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by offering options, allowing the client to stop anytime, and avoiding pressure for a large plan. Some compliance-oriented questions slightly shift focus away from ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some attempts to elicit preference and motivation, but the psychiatrist mostly provides information and skills. The client's own reasons, values, and hopes for change are not consistently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a partially collaborative stance by offering choices and inviting preference. The client is not fully positioned as the expert on her own experience and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or explore it. There are missed opportunities to ask what matters to her or what she would like to be different.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client's sustain talk. It would be stronger with more double-sided reflections that hold ambivalence while pointing toward capability.
