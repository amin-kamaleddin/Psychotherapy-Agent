# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent, warm unconditional positive regard throughout, never judging Aisha's smoking and validating her feelings even when she expresses doubt. Language is affirming and free of disapproval or labeling, normalizing her experience at every turn.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking is demonstrated through complex reflections that capture both content and feeling, such as identifying the functional role of smoking in filling a daily gap. The psychiatrist skillfully moves between simple and complex reflections while honoring Aisha's ambivalence without rushing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from understanding the pattern to a concrete plan (three instead of six, pack moved), staying focused on the target behavior throughout. A brief consolidating summary or explicit movement toward commitment would have further strengthened the sense of direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a model of collaborative interaction, with the plan entirely co-constructed from Aisha's own ideas and the psychiatrist building on her contributions with genuine engagement. The tone is conversational and mutual, with the psychiatrist sharing curiosity rather than operating from professional distance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Aisha's ownership and right to choose are consistently reinforced through language that never pressures or implies obligation, centering her agency naturally throughout. Phrases like 'You don't owe anyone a dramatic change' and 'You don't have to solve it tonight' communicate that this is entirely her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never introduces external reasons for change (no health risks, cancer, or arguments for quitting), instead drawing out Aisha's own motivations around sleep, time with her son, and the image of sitting with tea. Aisha arrives at her change talk entirely through her own reasoning, building on her own metaphors and language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Aisha as the expert on her own life, explicitly acknowledging that she knows her day, evenings, and moments better than anyone. The session is shaped entirely by Aisha's contributions, with the psychiatrist acting as a guide rather than a director.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions and reflections effectively evoke substantial change talk from Aisha, including her own concrete plan and recognition that 'three instead of six is big enough.' The psychiatrist could have more explicitly amplified or reinforced Aisha's change talk with targeted reflections or confidence-rating questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha's ambivalence and self-doubt are handled skillfully through double-sided reflections that validate the negative while gently holding the positive, without ever arguing or dismissing her concerns. The opportunity to demonstrate this skill is somewhat limited as Aisha does not strongly argue for maintaining smoking.
