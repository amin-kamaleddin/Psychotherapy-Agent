# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor was respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout the session, validating the client's choices and concerns. Some warmth felt procedural, with fewer individualized affirmations of the client's values or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and practical, summarizing the client's goals and preferences well. However, responses often stayed cognitive and logistical, missing deeper emotional or value-based reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained strong, focused direction toward a concrete behavior-change plan. The session moved clearly from goals to specific steps, barriers, backups, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor worked with the client as a partner and allowed the client's preferences to shape the plan. Collaboration would be stronger if more solutions were generated by the client rather than selected from clinician-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently framed the client as the decision-maker and respected her preference for a simple, low-pressure plan. Some directive practical suggestions slightly reduced the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited useful information about past success and confidence, but did not consistently deepen or amplify the client's internal motivation. More scaling, meaning, or change-talk-focused questions would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and avoided an expert-driven stance, supporting shared decision-making. A stronger partnership would begin more with client-generated ideas before offering multiple structured options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked good MI-consistent questions that evoked confidence and readiness. However, strong change talk was not consistently reinforced, expanded, or connected to the client's values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handled barriers and sustain talk effectively by normalizing concerns and converting them into manageable problem-solving. Occasionally the response remained too focused on barriers rather than pivoting back to motivation or autonomy.
