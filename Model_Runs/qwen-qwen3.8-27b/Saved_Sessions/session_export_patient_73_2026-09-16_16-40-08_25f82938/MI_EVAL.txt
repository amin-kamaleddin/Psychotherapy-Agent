# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts Linda’s ambivalence and fear without pressure. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with less explicit affirmation of Linda’s worth independent of her choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture mixed motives, social worry, and self-protection. Empathy could deepen further by exploring underlying shame, fear of failure, or repeated quit-attempt distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on smoking cessation and moves logically from ambivalence to a concrete after-dinner action. There is no drift into irrelevant or vague territory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner in constructing a client-preferred safety plan. The final plan and wording clearly emerge from Linda’s choices and language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Linda’s ownership, freedom, and self-direction without directives or pressure. The client repeatedly expresses that the plan feels safe, easy, and like her own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws motivation from Linda rather than giving advice or reasons for change. Evocation could be strengthened by exploring the deeper meaning of quitting and her confidence in follow-through.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows Linda to shape the plan, including the contact method and note wording. The score is not a perfect 5 because the psychiatrist still controls the question sequence and does not explicitly center Linda as her own expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits client change talk, including “My health. My bills.” and “I’m doing this for me.” The key gap is less explicit amplification and reinforcement of that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed as self-protective rather than dismissed or reinforced. More strategic double-sided reflections could better turn social smoking and the six-month delay into stronger change-oriented language.
