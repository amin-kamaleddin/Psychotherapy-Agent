# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirms Matteo’s control and concerns. Acceptance is solid, though it could be warmer and more individualized in normalizing Matteo’s social worries.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, with clear summaries of Matteo’s ambivalence about injury, social connection, and drinking. Empathy could deepen further by exploring underlying emotions such as embarrassment, isolation, or fear of losing his place with friends.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session follows Matteo’s priorities, but the specific alcohol-related change target becomes somewhat diffuse. Direction would improve by agreeing on a clearer goal, such as limiting drinks or understanding how alcohol may affect recovery.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a practical plan and invites Matteo’s input throughout. Collaboration could become more fully equal by reducing therapist-led menus and explicitly treating Matteo as the expert on his own social world.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated emphasis on Matteo’s choice, control, and self-monitoring. The final plan clearly reflects Matteo’s own values and preferred level of practicality.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Matteo’s own ideas for staying social and protecting his ankle. Evocation is strong for practical planning but less focused on deeper motivational reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with Matteo treated as the primary decision-maker. Partnership would be stronger if the psychiatrist more explicitly acknowledged Matteo’s expertise and reduced leading questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Matteo offers useful change talk, such as limiting drinks and monitoring his ankle. The psychiatrist does not fully amplify or develop this change talk with deeper importance, confidence, or values-based exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or moralizing when Matteo minimizes drinking. Sustain talk is tolerated but not meaningfully softened through double-sided reflections or exploration of what the minimization protects.
