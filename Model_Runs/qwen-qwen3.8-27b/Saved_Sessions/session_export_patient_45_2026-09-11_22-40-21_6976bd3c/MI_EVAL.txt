# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided lecturing or moralizing. Acceptance could be strengthened with warmer validation of ambivalence and greater affirmation of effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered helpful situational reflections, such as acknowledging shift work and feeling fine. Empathy was limited in exploring deeper fear, identity conflict, or ambivalence behind Erik’s resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on diabetes management, diet, alcohol, medication adherence, monitoring, and physical activity. Direction was practical but could better connect concrete actions to Erik’s personal goals and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporated Erik’s medication, shifts, and preference for simplicity into the plan. Collaboration was limited by frequent counselor-led recommendations and direct medical answers instead of co-created understanding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options and asked about small changes Erik might try. Autonomy was undermined by many prescriptive recommendations without explicit affirmation of Erik’s choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few questions invited Erik to identify a small change and barriers, but motivation was not deeply evoked. The psychiatrist often supplied reasons for change instead of eliciting Erik’s own values, fears, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were moments of input-seeking and tailored planning, but the psychiatrist often led as the expert. Partnership would improve if Erik’s own priorities and constraints were explored before presenting a full plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Erik expressed some willingness to try walking, a beer swap, and a weekly check. These statements were not consistently amplified, reinforced, or explored with scaling or values-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some normalizing statements and offered lower-effort alternatives to reduce resistance. However, direct corrective information and risk explanations sometimes maintained Erik’s defensive stance.
