# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, repeatedly normalizing Sofia's concerns and avoiding shame about eating or past failures. Tone is supportive but somewhat task-focused and rarely offers deeply warm, individualized affirmations of her strengths or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides adequate surface-level reflections and notices practical obstacles, but does not consistently track her deeper emotional experience such as fear of spirals, self-criticism, or overwhelm. Responds more with problem-solving and options than with deeper emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally maintains clear focus on the target behavior of improving afternoon energy through a sustainable, low-effort strategy. Effectively narrows from initial broad menu to a concrete plan tied to her presenting concern, ending with a specific behavioral goal and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows collaborative moments toward the end when Sofia's preferences meaningfully shape the final minimal plan. However, the early part is counselor-driven with multiple structured options and expert recommendations, resulting in uneven power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly invites Sofia to choose, avoids coercive language, and explicitly affirms permission for no streak or guilt if she skips. Still introduces some structured self-monitoring (silent one-word note) despite her stated dislike of tracking, though this is softened and accepted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session relies heavily on the psychiatrist offering options, rationales, and practical problem-solving rather than drawing out Sofia's own reasons or values for change. Only a few narrow, practical questions are asked; no deeper exploration of her motivation, confidence, or what would make the step feel meaningful.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early stance is expert-driven, presenting multiple structured options that do not strongly center Sofia's lived experience as the source of knowledge. Later adapts to her feedback and honors her constraints, but continues to supply many suggestions and refinements, keeping the interaction more guided than fully equal-partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Contains very little active elicitation of desire, need, ability, or importance; no confidence scaling or amplification of her own reasons for change. Much of the session is the clinician offering options rather than helping Sofia articulate her own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles her sustain talk reasonably well by validating concerns about diets, guilt, and spiraling without arguing or reinforcing. Could have more skillfully used double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to reflect both her fear and willingness, then elicit what would make the small step feel safer.
