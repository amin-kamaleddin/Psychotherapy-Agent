# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Daniel's perspective without labeling or minimizing concerns. Acceptance is strong but could include warmer, more individualized affirmation of Daniel's effort and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and useful, especially in summarizing Daniel's mixed stance and clinical picture. Empathy remains more cognitive than emotional and could better name Daniel's defensiveness, fear of labeling, and internal conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and its relationship to training, sleep, and recovery, with a clear logical progression. A higher score would require more consistent strategic guidance toward the core motivational target of changing drinking rather than a narrow sleep-performance test.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively, and the final plan is clearly co-created around Daniel's choices. Collaboration could be strengthened by inviting more open-ended, client-generated ideas rather than structuring the options primarily through the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, with permission-seeking language, multiple options, respect for Daniel's boundaries, and emphasis on a self-directed "quick data test." The psychiatrist avoids pressure and reinforces Daniel's ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some effort to evoke Daniel's own motivations by asking how drinking may affect training and what he wants to focus on. However, evocation remains somewhat superficial and gives way to a practical plan before deeply exploring values, confidence, or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and repeatedly asks for Daniel's preferences, allowing his choices to shape the session. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly positioning Daniel as the expert on his own life, goals, and body.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel offers some change talk, and the psychiatrist creates space for it through open-ended questions and a low-effort experiment. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or build on this change talk using importance, confidence, values, or future-outcome questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Daniel's sustain talk without argument and uses autonomy-supportive framing. However, sustain talk is mainly tolerated rather than actively softened with double-sided reflections, strategic reframing, or gentle pivoting to resolve ambivalence.
