# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client’s ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but slightly limited by formulaic language and fewer explicit affirmations of the client’s strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor shows reflective listening and understands the client’s conflicting feelings well. Deeper emotional pain and underlying fears are not explored consistently enough for a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on reducing recidivism and building a stable, prosocial life. The conversation moves clearly from broader tension to a concrete, realistic next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas drive much of the session, and the counselor follows his lead. Collaboration is strong but not fully equal because the counselor still steers toward a chosen frame and does not co-create the plan more explicitly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client’s choice, respects ambivalence, and avoids pressure. The client is clearly positioned as the expert on what would help him move forward.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out the client’s own ideas and solutions. Evocation could be stronger by asking more about values, identity, confidence, and deeper meaning rather than providing interpretive frames.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys a collaborative, walking-with-you stance and follows the client’s ideas. Partnership is strong but limited by the session still feeling somewhat guided by the counselor’s chosen focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicits several client-generated change statements, such as the hardware store, texting Fiona, and talking to Dave. Change talk is not systematically amplified using DARC, confidence scaling, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully without arguing, moralizing, or challenging the client. Statements about staying where it is familiar or being “lazy” are acknowledged, explored, and gently reframed without reinforcing resistance.
