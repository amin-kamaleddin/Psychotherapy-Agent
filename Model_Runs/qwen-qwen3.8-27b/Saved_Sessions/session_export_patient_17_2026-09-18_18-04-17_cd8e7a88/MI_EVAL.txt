# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with multiple genuine affirmations and explicit validation of the client's experiences. While consistently warm, the affirmations don't quite reach the level of deep, individualized, embodied acceptance that would warrant a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional reflective listening with accurate, nuanced reflections that capture the emotional core of the client's experience (e.g., 'the cigarette isn't really about the nicotine — it's about having a moment that's just yours'). Reflections consistently go beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture the existential meaning behind the client's words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows a clear, strategic progression from understanding the surface goal to exploring underlying meaning, identifying alternatives, and addressing barriers without drifting into unrelated territory. Each question builds coherently on the previous one, creating a logical narrative arc from exploration to emerging action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses inclusive language and frames the interaction as a joint exploration, treating the client's insights as equally valuable. The session feels like a genuine partnership where both parties contribute to direction and content, with no lecturing or prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist masterfully removes pressure throughout, explicitly stating things like 'I'm not asking you to commit to a routine' and 'you don't have to perform recovery here.' Suggestions are consistently framed as explorations rather than recommendations, and the client's right to make her own choices is repeatedly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out the client's own motivations and insights through open-ended questions rather than providing external reasons for change. Questions like 'what's the smallest version of nobody that might still exist?' effectively evoke the client's own creativity and self-direction.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The collaborative, client-led stance is established immediately and maintained throughout, with explicit rejection of an expert-driven approach. The statement 'someone who gets that it's about the silence, not just the habit — that's what I want to be for you' powerfully positions the psychiatrist as a collaborator rather than an authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes change talk through open-ended questions and by reflecting and amplifying the client's own statements of hope. However, the session could have more explicitly elicited confidence and ability language, working more systematically through the DARN-CAT framework.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively through exploration and double-sided reflections rather than dismissal or counter-argument. The psychiatrist could have more explicitly helped the client resolve ambivalence about whether quitting would enhance or diminish her access to the valued 'silence.'
