# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a nonjudgmental and validating stance, normalizing Marcus's confusion and fear. However, the acceptance is somewhat procedural and could be warmer or more affirming of Marcus as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Marcus's confusion, fear of pain, and fear of failure, helping him feel understood. Empathy is strong but often moves quickly into problem-solving, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on diabetes self-management, particularly blood glucose monitoring, logging, and a brief post-meal walk. The conversation moves logically toward a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by asking for preferences, adapting the plan to Marcus's constraints, and modifying targets and tracking. However, many options are still supplied by the psychiatrist rather than elicited first from Marcus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is repeatedly given choices and is not pressured into a more demanding plan. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming his ownership and right to modify or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evoking techniques, such as an importance scale and questions about doable steps. However, the session often shifts to selecting from clinician-generated options rather than drawing out Marcus's deeper values or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is clear collaboration, including asking permission before offering a plan and letting Marcus choose components. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure, making the interaction somewhat expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through questions about importance, doability, and first steps. It does not consistently deepen, amplify, or reflect Marcus's own motivation and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps reframe missed tests as information rather than failure and offers smaller, manageable options. However, the conversation remains somewhat focused on barriers and failure, with limited use of double-sided reflections.
