# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental acceptance, even when the client describes temptation to return to crime. The language normalizes struggle and avoids labels, reflecting the client’s experience with dignity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate understanding of the client’s perspective, including underlying shame, identity concerns, and ambivalence. Reflections capture emotional nuance and track the client’s inner experience effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of reducing recidivism and building a stable, pro-social life. The conversation moves strategically from broad stability to concrete, actionable steps such as texting Fiona and exploring support.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the client’s ideas shaping the content and the therapist reflecting and building on his statements. However, the therapist remains somewhat structured and question-led, occasionally offering multiple options rather than fully allowing the client to lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects the client’s pace and supports agency through low-pressure language and attention to boundaries. Autonomy is mainly supported implicitly; more explicit choice language would strengthen this area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own motivations by asking what matters, what feels reachable, and what change would mean. The client’s intrinsic values—relationship, dignity, identity, and not being alone—are drawn out rather than supplied by the therapist.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on his own life, and his choices shape much of the session. However, the therapist still drives the agenda through leading questions and occasionally offers menus of options rather than more explicitly inviting the client to set the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and strengthens the client’s own change-oriented language, moving from abstract stability to concrete actions like texting Fiona and practicing asking for help. The client’s own words are reflected and amplified throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled in an MI-consistent way by acknowledging barriers without arguing or dismissing them. A 5 could be achieved by using more explicit double-sided reflections that directly connect the pull of the old life with the client’s emerging pro-social choices.
