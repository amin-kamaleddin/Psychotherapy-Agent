# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental regard while acknowledging the client's time pressure. However, moments of over-interpretation slightly reduce unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures the client's priorities and evidence-based stance. Empathy is somewhat cognitive and task-focused rather than deeply emotional, and the final over-interpretation missed the client's exact framing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on blood pressure and a practical, doable next step. The counselor consistently guides the conversation without drifting into generic advice or unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as an expert on his own routines, data, and professional habits. The main limitation is occasional reliance on counselor-provided frameworks rather than fully client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supports client choice by asking open questions and affirming client control. Introducing specific suggestions and brief interpretations slightly reduces the autonomy emphasis.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client's own language, systems thinking, and data-based plan. Evocation is strong but not fully client-generated because the counselor contributes specific structures and anchors.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positions the client as the expert and allows the client's preferences to shape the plan. Some expert-led suggestions and an inaccurate final summary reduce the consistency of partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor successfully draws out commitment, ability, and control-based change talk. The work focuses mainly on practical action rather than broader desire, values, or identity-level motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's barriers and respectfully redirects toward feasible options. The use of double-sided reflections is limited, and some phrasing preserves negative framing.
