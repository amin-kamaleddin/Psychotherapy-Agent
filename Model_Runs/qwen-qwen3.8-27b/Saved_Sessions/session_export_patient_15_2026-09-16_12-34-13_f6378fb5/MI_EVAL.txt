# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client's effort and concerns. It is not higher because the warmth can feel somewhat formulaic and tied more to the action plan than to Andrei as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses several accurate content reflections but remains mostly cognitive and surface-level. Stronger empathy would include more feeling reflections and follow-up questions exploring doubt, annoyance, awkwardness, or the desire to keep things simple.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on one concrete blood-pressure-related behavior: a short evening walk. However, the direction is incomplete because alcohol use, a key clinical target in the case, is never addressed or connected to blood pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and client co-construct a concrete plan, with Andrei choosing important details such as timing, duration, and environmental cues. Collaboration is not fully equal, however, because the psychiatrist frequently drives the agenda and provides many options and suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through open questions, preference-based options, and a low-pressure check-in. The score is not higher because some messages are quite structured and offer multiple options in rapid succession, which can feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some client-generated goals, readiness, confidence, and a concrete step. Evocation remains superficial because there is limited exploration of Andrei's values, internal motivations, or skepticism before moving into practical suggestions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and invites Andrei's input, and the final plan reflects his choices. It is not higher because the initial multi-question prompt and frequent suggestions can make the conversation feel more guided than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including Andrei's goals and a concrete first step. However, there are missed opportunities to deepen or expand this change talk with follow-up questions before moving quickly into practical problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and accepts Andrei's barriers without challenge, which is appropriate. The score is limited because sustain talk is mostly acknowledged and then shifted into practical solutions rather than strategically softened to help Andrei resolve ambivalence.
