# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without shaming or moralizing. Acceptance was strong but sometimes procedural, moving quickly into options or planning before fully affirming the client’s broader strengths and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used clear reflective listening and accurately captured the client’s social tension and discomfort with feeling “off.” Some reflections were accurate but slightly surface-level, missing deeper emotional meaning such as feeling absent from her own life.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior and the client’s specific concerns about memory, fog, and social fit. The progression from exploration to values, confidence scaling, and planning was clear and well managed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited client input and incorporated her preferences into the final plan, including her own wording for the text to Sophie. Collaboration was strong, though the counselor often generated options before fully eliciting the client’s own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor emphasized choice, avoided pressure, and allowed the client to set her own confidence level and plan. Autonomy support was generally strong, with only occasional subtle steering toward preferred options or mildly directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited the client’s own values, confidence, and motivation through open questions and scaling. Evocation could be stronger by waiting longer and allowing the client to generate more of her own reasons and solutions before offering information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treated the client as an active partner, inviting her to choose topics, options, and wording. Partnership was strong, though moments of expert-driven advice or psychoeducation slightly reduced the balance toward client-led direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor helped the client express important change talk around staying sharp, being present, and avoiding fog. Change talk could be further strengthened by reflecting and expanding the client’s own reasons before moving into additional options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with the client and did not reinforce minimization of the behavior. However, sustain talk was often acknowledged and moved past rather than fully softened using double-sided reflections, autonomy focus, or redirection toward change.
