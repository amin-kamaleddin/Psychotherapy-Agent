# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and reduces the client's fear of being judged. Some statements are mildly evaluative or procedural, which keeps acceptance slightly below a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects the client's practical burdens and frustration, such as feeling overwhelmed and annoyed by reminders. Deeper emotional themes such as shame, fear, and self-image are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on diabetes self-management and successfully narrows a broad goal into a specific, manageable behavior. Pacing could be cleaner, as the initial message includes multiple questions and options that may feel overwhelming.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The final plan is clearly co-created, with the client's preferences shaping the chosen behavior, timing, duration, and check-in method. The psychiatrist builds on the client's contributions rather than imposing a predetermined plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding controlling language. Statements such as not assuming or reading into non-response strongly reduce pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses scaling questions to evoke some motivation, but the session becomes solution-focused and clinician-led. There are missed opportunities to draw out the client's own values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites input and adapts the plan based on the client's responses. However, the session remains somewhat agenda-driven and could better frame the client as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some change-relevant language, especially around importance and the need for something simple and helpful. These statements are not consistently amplified or deepened into stronger DARN-C change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and acknowledges sustain talk without judgment. More strategic use of double-sided reflections and value-based reframing would help soften resistance more effectively.
