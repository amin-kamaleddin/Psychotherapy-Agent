# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids lecturing or blaming, supporting the client’s stated need not to be scolded. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, affirming the plan more than the client’s deeper worth or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures several practical worries and conflicts, such as not feeling robbed, being boring, or fearing labels. Empathy remains mostly surface-level, with less exploration of shame, social identity, or deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is highly focused on the target behavior and consistently converts vague intentions into concrete, time-limited steps. Direction is clear, purposeful, and ends with a specific follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client actively shapes the plan by choosing the amount, exit, script, and coping steps, indicating collaborative engagement. Collaboration is uneven because the counselor supplies much of the plan content and problem-solving structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently offers choices, avoids coercion, and accepts the client’s solo attempt and potential failure without judgment. Autonomy support is strong but slightly reduced by directive suggestions and immediate decision prompts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions about seriousness and readiness help elicit the client’s goals, but deeper values, confidence, and personal reasons for change are not consistently explored. The session remains more behavioral problem-solving than motivational evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input and the client contributes key plan choices, reflecting genuine collaboration. Partnership is limited by counselor-generated scripts, rules, and structure, making it somewhat expert-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions elicit early intentions and readiness, but the counselor does not consistently amplify or expand the client’s own change talk. Change talk stays practical rather than being developed into stronger personal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing or amplifying the client’s doubts, normalizing concerns and reframing potential failure as information. It would benefit from more active double-sided reflections to pivot ambivalence toward change.
