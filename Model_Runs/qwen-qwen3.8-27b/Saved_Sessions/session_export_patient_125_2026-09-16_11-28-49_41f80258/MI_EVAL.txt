# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and communicates support without blaming or scolding. The stance is strong but somewhat procedural, with frequent focus on logistics rather than deeper positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers accurate reflective listening for frustration, urgency, and feeling targeted, but remains mostly surface-level. Deeper emotional themes around family connection, fear of disappointing his dad, and powerlessness are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the immediate goal of restoring the phone call, with little drift. However, it lacks broader strategic direction toward compliance, frustration management, or values-based change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Tariq’s input and incorporates his preferences into the request. Collaboration is present but uneven, as the counselor becomes the main advocate rather than a true equal partner in problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Tariq’s choices and asks permission before acting. Autonomy is undermined by the counselor taking on a strong advocacy role and not consistently highlighting what Tariq can control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is an initial evocative question about what matters most, but the session quickly shifts to logistical problem-solving. The counselor does not consistently elicit Tariq’s own reasons, values, strengths, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids a purely expert stance and frequently asks Tariq for input. Partnership is limited by repeated permission-seeking and a counselor-led advocacy role rather than equal collaboration on change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening question elicits meaningful values about family and doing the right thing, but the counselor does not amplify or build on this change talk. Evocation becomes weak as the focus turns to logistics and staff requests.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue with Tariq’s blame toward staff or his frustration, which is appropriate. However, there is little use of double-sided reflection or redirection to soften sustain talk and explore agency.
