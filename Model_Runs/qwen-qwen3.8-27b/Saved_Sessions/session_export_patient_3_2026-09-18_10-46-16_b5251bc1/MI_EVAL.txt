# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, warm unconditional positive regard with individualized affirmations tied to Aisha's specific words and actions. Never labels, judges, or minimizes concerns, even when Aisha uses self-critical language like 'gross' or 'failure.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shows highly accurate complex reflections that capture both logistical overwhelm and emotional weight, including the all-or-nothing pattern and the inner critic. The 'bouncer at the door' metaphor and the final reflection on predicting reluctance fading demonstrate precise emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, progressive momentum from exploring barriers through identifying patterns to a concrete, time-bound commitment (shoes by the door Monday, 10-minute walk Tuesday). Questions are strategically sequenced with no topic drift or loss of focus on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine power balance with Aisha setting the agenda, co-constructing the analysis of the all-or-nothing pattern, and generating the shoe strategy in response to the psychiatrist's questions. The psychiatrist builds on Aisha's contributions rather than redirecting, and the final commitment is entirely Aisha's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Aisha's ownership through question formats ('what would it look like to try...') and by affirming her choices rather than adding recommendations. The psychiatrist never prescribes or instructs, and the final plan is articulated entirely by Aisha.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully draws out Aisha's own motivations, insights, and plans through targeted questions rather than providing reasons for change or advice. The session ends with Aisha generating her own commitment language about wanting to see if she's wrong, the hallmark of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Aisha as the holder of expertise and wisdom through attribution language ('that's you stepping back,' 'you already know the answer'). Functions as a mirror and amplifier rather than an expert delivering content, with Aisha's contributions directly shaping the session's trajectory.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully amplifies Aisha's own change talk through reflections and strategically designed questions that elicit DARN-C talk (desire, ability, reason, need, confidence). Explicitly reinforces shift language and the session ends with Aisha generating her own confidence and desire talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk by validating the feeling while reframing context and inviting Aisha to evaluate it herself, never arguing or contradicting directly. The sustain talk naturally diminishes across the session as Aisha softens it on her own ('why do I need to justify ten minutes to myself?').
