# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the patient's caution. Acceptance is appropriate but feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate perspective-taking, but it is inconsistent. Important emotions such as frustration, fatigue, and difficulty waiting are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safe citalopram timing, prescriber communication, and waiting-period management. Direction is clear, though more medical-management than MI-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates practically, especially in editing the patient's portal message. The power balance remains uneven because the counselor supplies most of the medical guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient is given some choices, such as how to contact the prescriber and whether to include a phone number. However, much of the session is directive, with less explicit support for the patient as the final decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies mainly on information, templates, and behavioral instructions. There is little use of open questions to evoke the patient's own values, concerns, or reasons for choosing safety.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are brief partnership moments, such as asking whether the patient wants help wording a message. Overall, the psychiatrist becomes the expert and advisor, limiting collaborative involvement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient naturally produces strong change talk about being safe, careful, and doing the right thing. The psychiatrist does not consistently summarize, amplify, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk, such as uncertainty and discomfort with waiting, is normalized and redirected toward action. However, it is not strategically softened with reflective or double-sided MI responses.
