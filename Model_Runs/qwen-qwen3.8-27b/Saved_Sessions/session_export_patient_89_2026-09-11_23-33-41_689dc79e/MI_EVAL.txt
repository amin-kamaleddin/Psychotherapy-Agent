# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, often normalizing Marcus’s experience. The warmth is professional but somewhat formulaic at times, and the opening crisis statement may briefly interrupt rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections and validations are present but generally surface-level. The psychiatrist does not fully explore the emotional complexity of Marcus’s ambivalence, past failures, shame, or fear of repeating the same cycle.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear behavioral target: a short, doable walk. There is minor early broadening with multiple options, but the psychiatrist quickly narrows to a concrete plan and maintains good direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus contributes to choices such as timing, route, and fallback plan, showing some collaboration. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure and plan, making the interaction feel more clinician-led than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Marcus’s choice and allows flexibility in the plan. However, frequent directive advice and detailed implementation steps reduce the sense of self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocative questioning is limited, with only an initial open question and a preference check. The session quickly shifts to advice and planning rather than drawing out Marcus’s own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to Marcus’s preferences, supporting some partnership. Yet the psychiatrist carries much of the agenda-setting and expertise, limiting a fully collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not actively elicit, amplify, or deepen it. More scaling, importance/confidence, or follow-up questions would better strengthen the patient’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes sustain talk and avoids arguing or reinforcing the patient’s concerns. However, more MI-consistent strategies, such as double-sided reflections or active ambivalence work, are not consistently used.
