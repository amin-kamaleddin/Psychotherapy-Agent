# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental. Some responses feel slightly formulaic or a bit leading before the client fully explores the emotion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both emotional and relational concerns, especially around being judged or being a burden. Some reflections summarize before fully sitting with deeper feelings such as futility or fear of taking up space.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a coherent flow, but it drifts toward family dynamics and emotional reassurance. It does not consistently return to the specific target behavior or broader health goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s ideas clearly shape the conversation, and the psychiatrist is responsive to her concerns. The collaboration remains mostly implicit, with limited explicit equal-partner language or shared agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids giving direct advice or pressuring the client to decide. However, explicit statements affirming the client’s freedom to choose are limited, and some questions feel somewhat leading.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of the client’s own values and change-oriented language. Evocation is sometimes replaced by interpretation, limiting deeper exploration of intrinsic motivation and readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on her own experience and lets her responses guide content. The session is still partly agenda-driven by the psychiatrist’s sequence of questions rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk, such as wanting less mirror stress, more energy, and a normal interaction with her mother. However, this change talk is not consistently amplified, summarized, or connected to values, ability, or readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Self-critical and doubt-based statements are mostly met with normalization rather than argument or dismissal. The response could be stronger with more double-sided reflections or explicit pivots toward the client’s own reasons for change.
