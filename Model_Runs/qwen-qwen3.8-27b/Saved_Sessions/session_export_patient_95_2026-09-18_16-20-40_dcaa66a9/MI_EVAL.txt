# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with powerful normalizing statements like 'that's not a weakness, that's a survival strategy.' Occasionally reads as technically precise rather than deeply personally invested.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with reflections that capture the emotional and existential depth of Maria's experience. Complex reflections like 'having to pre-decide you'll be abandoned so the actual abandonment doesn't catch you off guard' go beyond the literal content to name unspoken pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and logical progression from validation to exploring ambivalence to belief systems, with appropriate pacing for a contemplation-stage patient. Could include a brief safety planning check-in or explicit transition toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative frame with consistent 'we' language and open questions that invite joint exploration. Slightly limited by occasional interpretation of Maria's experience for her rather than asking her to name it herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Maria's right to hold her beliefs and avoids directive language, with explicit statements like 'I'm not asking you to drop it today.' Could more explicitly affirm Maria's ownership of her choices with language like 'It's your call.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks open questions that invite Maria to explore her own experience, but lacks MI-specific techniques like scaling or importance questions. Could more explicitly amplify Maria's change talk when it appears rather than relying solely on reflection.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong collaborative stance and consistently invites Maria's perspective rather than directing. Occasionally takes the lead in interpreting Maria's experience, which slightly reduces the fully collaborative quality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maria offers change talk spontaneously and the psychiatrist reflects some of it, but there are missed opportunities to deepen or draw out more change language. Could have asked follow-up questions like 'What would that look like?' or explicitly amplified Maria's engagement as a sign of wanting change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively uses double-sided reflections and validates struggle without endorsing hopelessness. Could more actively pivot away from repeated sustain talk, such as when Maria circles back to 'I just need the pain to stop.'
