# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the patient’s ambivalence and coping needs. The stance is accepting but occasionally feels slightly goal-focused rather than fully embracing the patient’s experience as a whole.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, with accurate capture of the patient’s practical worries, fear of failure, and need for low pressure. Deeper emotional themes such as exhaustion, fear of disappointment, and self-criticism are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a small, concrete behavior: the five-minute car break. Direction is effective but slightly weakened by repetition and some circularity in the questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by building on the patient’s ideas, such as the car break, gas gauge check, and exception for a screaming child. Some questions are slightly leading or repetitive, which reduces the sense of fully client-led exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with consistent emphasis on choice, optionality, and avoiding a pass/fail frame. The patient is supported to develop a self-directed, low-pressure plan without pressure or obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes the patient’s own practical change talk and concrete solutions. Evocation could be stronger by connecting the plan to deeper values, identity, and long-term hopes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient’s ideas shape much of the conversation, and the psychiatrist treats her as the expert on her own life. The partnership is occasionally reduced by repeated steering questions and a noticeable agenda toward a specific trial step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is consistently elicited and reinforced, especially around a small, boring, self-defined plan. The change talk is frequent but not always amplified by linking it to the patient’s values or broader motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully, with the psychiatrist avoiding confrontation and reframing statements like not letting lunch rewrite the morning. Ambivalence could be softened further with more double-sided reflections that honor both sides of the conflict.
