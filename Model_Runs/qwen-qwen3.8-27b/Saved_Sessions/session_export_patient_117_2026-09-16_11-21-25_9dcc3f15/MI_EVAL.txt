# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally used respectful, nonjudgmental language and acknowledged Samuel’s effort. However, acceptance felt procedural and limited in deep affirmation or normalization of ambivalence and shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some perspective-taking around the break-room awkwardness and hesitation with nicotine replacement. Reflections were often brief and cognitive rather than emotionally deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a concrete smoking-reduction goal, especially not smoking at lunch for five workdays. The main weakness was that the direction became somewhat formulaic and plan-heavy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samuel was invited to choose options such as the exit line, target duration, and follow-up time. Much of the collaboration, however, was limited to selecting from psychiatrist-generated suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respected some of Samuel’s preferences, such as not role-playing and choosing the “quick call” excuse. Frequent provision of specific plans and strategies still limited stronger cultivation of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions evoked some useful change talk about reducing smoking and trying the lunch plan. The psychiatrist also supplied many strategies and reasons, which reduced the focus on eliciting Samuel’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for input and collaborative choices, but took a strong expert/drive role in proposing goals, scripts, and coping plans. The interaction often felt more like coaching a plan than co-creating direction as equal partners.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samuel offered change talk, including willingness to try the five-day lunch plan and a realistic slip response. The psychiatrist elicited change talk but did not consistently deepen or amplify Samuel’s own values, benefits, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not directly challenge Samuel and reasonably normalized some concerns about the break room and NRT. The session still remained barrier-focused rather than using more skillful double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
