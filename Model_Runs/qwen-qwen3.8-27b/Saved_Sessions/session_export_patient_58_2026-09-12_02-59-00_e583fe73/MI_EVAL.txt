# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout the session. Acceptance was strong, though warmth remained somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate but tended to stay at a surface level of frustration and practical barriers. Deeper ambivalence, autonomy concerns, and the emotional meaning behind Jamal’s behavior were underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was highly focused and consistently guided toward a concrete, actionable plan for improving punctuality. There was no meaningful drift, and the target behavior remained central throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some meaningful collaboration, especially once the plan became more concrete and client-generated. However, the psychiatrist supplied much of the structure early on, making the interaction more guided than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was well supported through privacy, choice, and avoidance of rigid rules. The score falls short of 5 because the psychiatrist still introduced several prescriptive tweaks that subtly shifted control back to the clinician.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively used scaling and exception-finding to draw out some client-driven motivation and planning. However, deeper intrinsic reasons, values, and personal meaning behind change were not fully evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session showed genuine attempts to invite input and respect the client’s preferences. It remained somewhat expert-driven, as the psychiatrist generated many options and drove much of the problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was present, particularly around ability, action, and concrete commitments. The psychiatrist could have strengthened and amplified this change talk by connecting it more deeply to values and personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by validating concerns and redirecting them into acceptable, low-pressure constraints. The clinician did not fully leverage these concerns to build deeper intrinsic motivation.
