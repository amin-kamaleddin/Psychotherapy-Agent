# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirming reflections of ambivalence and constraints. This is not a 5 due to less explicit, broad-based positive regard for the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s practical reasoning and ambivalence around breaks, stress, and energy. The reflection is stronger cognitively and behaviorally than emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on smoking cessation, moving from cues to a practical alternative break plan without meaningful drift. Each provider turn advances the target behavior toward client-generated action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed in real time from the client’s own ideas, such as the perimeter walk, standing pause, and wrist marker. The client’s lived retail experience materially shapes the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, ownership, and the client’s right to modify or drop the plan. Autonomy is actively strengthened rather than merely acknowledged.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions elicit the client’s own motivations, standards, and solution ideas without advice-giving. Evocation is frequent, natural, and central to the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert, with questions keeping her central to the plan. The score is limited because the psychiatrist still directs the conversational sequence and agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The provider effectively elicits client-generated change language and reinforces self-directed choices. It falls short of a 5 due to limited amplification, confidence exploration, or readiness rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and ambivalence are validated rather than challenged, with useful reframing toward practical standards and ownership. It is not a 5 because some sustain talk is not fully transformed into stronger change talk.
