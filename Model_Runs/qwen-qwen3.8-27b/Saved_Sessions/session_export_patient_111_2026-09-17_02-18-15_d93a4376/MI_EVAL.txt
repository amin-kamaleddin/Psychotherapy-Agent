# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is largely nonjudgmental, validating, and respectful of Richard as a person rather than a problem. However, occasional interpretive statements, such as suggesting he is “leaving room,” slightly over-attribute meaning and reduce the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong complex reflections that capture Richard’s dignity, boundaries, routine, and concern about being reduced to his wife’s worry. Some reflections miss or stay too shallow, and there is less exploration of underlying anger, resentment, or autonomy-versus-care conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains relevant to smoking, health, and the wife’s pressure but lacks a clear target behavior and agenda. It would benefit from collaboratively specifying whether the goal is quitting, cutting down, delaying smoking, or a specific health concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the conversation as a collaboration between two adults and follows Richard’s topics rather than imposing an expert agenda. This is one of the strongest aspects of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Richard’s freedom to choose, avoids pressure or advice, and respects his stated boundary that he is not quitting. Autonomy is strongly supported throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Richard to define what would make the session useful and respects his self-defined threshold. However, evocation is mainly directed at alliance and process rather than drawing out Richard’s own reasons for smoking-related change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys a collaborative stance and acknowledges Richard’s authority over his own life. A few therapist-imposed interpretations, especially the “leaving room” comment, slightly weaken the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are a few useful openings, such as asking what would make the hour serve Richard and noting “that’s not nothing” when he mentions a possible health threshold. These moments are not consistently developed into stronger desire, ability, need, or benefit statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and accepts Richard’s “not quitting” stance without confrontation. However, sustain talk remains dominant, and the session does not sufficiently use double-sided reflections or redirection to move the conversation toward change.
