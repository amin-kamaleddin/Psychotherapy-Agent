# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, often normalizing the client’s discomfort. However, repeated corrective directives and the poorly received "reward" framing weaken unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective statements are present but remain surface-level and formulaic. The psychiatrist misses the deeper meaning behind the client’s obligation, loss of control, and resentment toward the daily ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on medication nonadherence and moves toward a clear next step: sending a portal message to the prescriber. The main limitation is that the focus is more behavioral and practical than values-based.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration, as the psychiatrist incorporates the client’s preference for brevity and the portal message. Still, most of the content, structure, and plan are clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks what the client wants to discuss. Autonomy is undercut by repeated instructions, especially around medication use, breathing exercises, and sending the message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides advice, scripts, and behavior strategies rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations. Values, fears, and internal reasons for change are not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some client contributions, such as message tone and portal use. However, the interaction remains largely expert-driven, with uneven power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are few efforts to strengthen the client’s own motivations for change. The session moves quickly into practical advice without exploring desire, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is normalized, but not actively softened or redirected toward autonomy-respecting change. The response remains focused on compliance planning rather than resolving ambivalence.
