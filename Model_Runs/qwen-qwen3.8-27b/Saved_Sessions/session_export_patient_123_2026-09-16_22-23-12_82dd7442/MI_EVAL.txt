# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor communicates respectful, nonjudgmental support by normalizing the screening experience and reflecting without criticism. It is slightly formulaic and does not fully affirm broader worth or autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor uses reflective listening to capture Sofia's values, tensions, and practical concerns. Reflections remain mostly cognitive and do not fully explore deeper emotional meaning, such as loneliness, pressure, or alcohol-related concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts into general topics like school, friends, phone use, and quiet time rather than maintaining focus on the alcohol screening result or a related target behavior. A clearer, gentle direction toward the primary concern is needed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor treats Sofia as the person who knows her own life and builds on her ideas. Collaboration is somewhat procedural because the counselor still leads most turns and does not explicitly negotiate the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor consistently avoids pressure and invites Sofia to generate her own ideas and choices. It would be stronger with more explicit affirmation of her right to decide and ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions draw out Sofia's ideas and small plans. However, evocation is repetitive and superficial, with limited exploration of values, ambivalence, confidence, or motivation related to alcohol use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor maintains a collaborative stance and invites Sofia's perspective throughout the conversation. It is not fully strong because the counselor does not explicitly acknowledge Sofia's expertise in her own preferences and path.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor notices some client-generated change ideas, such as texting friends, choosing a book, or trying a short break. These are not consistently amplified or connected to the primary alcohol-use target.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor responds to sustain talk without judgment and acknowledges both sides of the experience. The session could better resolve ambivalence and shift focus toward change rather than lingering on difficulties.
