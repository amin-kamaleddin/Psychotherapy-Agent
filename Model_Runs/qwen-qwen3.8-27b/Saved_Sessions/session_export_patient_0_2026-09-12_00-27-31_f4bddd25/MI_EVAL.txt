# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor shows basic respect and tolerance for the client's need for privacy, but prescriptive framing and evaluative guidance reduce the sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor makes some reflective comments about being judged and exposed, but misses deeper shame, defensiveness, and stigma-related fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a coherent focus on responding to social prying, but drifts from the referral concern involving methamphetamine use and infection-related risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses menus of options and inclusive language, but often supplies preferred scripts and follow-up steps rather than building from the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and avoids overt coercion, but repeated behavioral instructions and a coaching tone limit the client's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are initial evocative attempts, but the counselor quickly shifts to advice, role-play, and closed questions rather than drawing out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites choice and uses collaborative language at times, but often assumes an expert role by directing specific scripts, tone, and behavior.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with a reasonable MI question, but focuses on boundary scripts rather than eliciting the client's own values, reasons, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not reinforce minimizing statements, but does not skillfully soften or explore ambivalence around the client's substance use and risk.
