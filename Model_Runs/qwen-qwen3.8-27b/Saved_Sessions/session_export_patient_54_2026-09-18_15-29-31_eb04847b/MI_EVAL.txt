# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm unconditional positive regard throughout with no moments of judgment or disapproval. Acceptance is individualized and specific, connecting to Isabella's particular values (family tradition, partnership, not being dramatic) rather than offering generic reassurance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and nuanced, capturing underlying meaning and emotion beyond literal content. Standout examples include 'You don't want permission, you want partnership' and gently naming the vulnerability beneath Isabella's autonomous exterior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on the target behavior while following the client's lead, with a clear progression from rapport through exploration to concrete planning. Each exchange is purposefully directed toward deepening engagement without tangential drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is structured as a genuine exchange between equals, with every significant element (plan, timeline, emotional needs, contingency) co-constructed from Isabella's own responses. The psychiatrist never lectures or provides information unprompted, consistently asking for Isabella's input to shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly reinforced, with the entire session structurally organized around Isabella's choices, criteria, and timeline. The psychiatrist never pressures, uses directive language, or implies obligation — instead reframing change as self-directed (e.g., 'not a surrender, a choice that fits who you are').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful use of strategic questions to draw out Isabella's own motivations and solutions without providing advice or opinions. The psychiatrist's restraint allows Isabella to generate the pizza plan, partner involvement, and coping strategy entirely on her own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions self as a supporter rather than an authority, guiding the session by Isabella's needs rather than own agenda. The psychiatrist's expertise is used only in service of drawing out Isabella's own wisdom, and the session is genuinely co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully facilitates Isabella's progression from 'I'm not here because I have a problem' to a concrete, partner-involving plan with a timeline. All change talk is in Isabella's own language, drawn out through strategic questions and reflections that evoke her own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk appropriately by validating the reasoning without agreeing or arguing, and avoids reinforcing it. Misses a couple of opportunities for more strategic double-sided reflections, particularly when Isabella asserts 'I'm not a kid, I make my own choices.'
