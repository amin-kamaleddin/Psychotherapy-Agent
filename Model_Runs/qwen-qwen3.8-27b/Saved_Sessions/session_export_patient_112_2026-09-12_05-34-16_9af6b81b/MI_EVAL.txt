# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful and nonjudgmental, using normalizing statements to support Rosa. However, acceptance remained somewhat generic and could have more deeply affirmed her identity concerns and emotional struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, especially in accurately summarizing ambivalence and specific worries. The main limitation was that reflections were often followed quickly by practical information, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking cessation and moved from ambivalence toward a concrete, manageable action step. The main limitation was an overemphasis on technical planning, which narrowed the MI process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Rosa’s input and offered choices, creating a cooperative atmosphere. However, collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist often shifted into an expert role with detailed advice and problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by offering options, normalizing privacy, and emphasizing low pressure. Repeated recommendations and structured plans may have subtly reduced Rosa’s sense of full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was limited evocation of Rosa’s internal motivation, values, and confidence for change. The session focused more on practical strategies than on eliciting Rosa’s own reasons for quitting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered choices, supporting collaboration. However, the session became expert-driven, with the psychiatrist largely guiding the agenda and supplying solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were occasional evocative questions, but Rosa’s change talk was not consistently identified, reflected, or expanded. The session shifted more toward problem-solving than cultivating change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was often acknowledged without direct reinforcement, and there were some attempts to redirect toward experimentation. However, direct counterarguments and practical fixes reduced opportunities for deeper ambivalence exploration.
