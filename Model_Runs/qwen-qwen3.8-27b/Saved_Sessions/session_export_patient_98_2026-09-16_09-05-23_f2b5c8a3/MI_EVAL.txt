# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided blaming or shaming. Some praise felt mildly performance-contingent, which limited the rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured the patient’s goals, pain, and frustration effectively. They could have gone deeper into the emotional meaning of needing help and fear of setbacks.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the rehabilitation goal and practical next steps. Some responses bundled multiple questions, making the pacing feel slightly task-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-created with the patient’s preferences and choices. The psychiatrist supplied many options, which kept it from feeling fully patient-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasized the patient’s choice, trust, and ownership. Occasional evaluative suggestions and praise slightly directed the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and small-step questions elicited some motivation. The conversation moved quickly to logistics, limiting deeper exploration of values, hopes, and internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient’s goals and choices shaped the plan, and his boundaries were respected. The psychiatrist still led much of the structure and offered many specific options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient generated some practical change talk, such as trying lifts and tracking pain. The psychiatrist did not consistently deepen or expand the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns and ambivalence were acknowledged without debate or judgment. The response could have used more MI-specific softening techniques, such as double-sided reflections.
