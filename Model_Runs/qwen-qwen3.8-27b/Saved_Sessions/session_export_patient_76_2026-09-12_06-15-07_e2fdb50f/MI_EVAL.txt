# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard without judgment despite the client’s disclosure of cutting. The response normalized the experience and affirmed courage, which supported safety and openness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used accurate reflections around exhaustion, overwhelm, and doubt, which helped maintain rapport. However, some deeper emotional themes, such as the “noise,” burden, and helplessness, were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing self-harm and building a safer, lower-effort coping plan. The clear safety checks and next steps kept the work relevant, though they slightly interrupted flow at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor worked with the client rather than imposing a plan, offering small choices and letting client preferences shape the intervention. The final plan was co-created and grounded in what the client found doable.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supported the client’s autonomy by offering choices, respecting refusals, and emphasizing that the client was in charge. Language such as “would you be willing” and “you can skip a phrase entirely” made choice central.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited the client’s own goals, triggers, and willingness to try small steps. Deeper exploration of the client’s values, confidence, and meaning behind change would have strengthened evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and allowed client preferences to guide the session, showing a collaborative stance. The partnership was strong, though the clinical safety agenda remained somewhat counselor-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor encouraged and reinforced the client’s change talk, such as wanting to be less tired and trying something less damaging. There was an opportunity to deepen this talk by exploring what those choices meant to the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handled sustain talk well by validating doubt and redirecting toward small options rather than arguing. More explicit double-sided reflections could have helped resolve ambivalence around cutting more fully.
