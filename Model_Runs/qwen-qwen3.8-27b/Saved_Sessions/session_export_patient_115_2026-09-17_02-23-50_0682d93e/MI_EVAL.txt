# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and warm throughout the conversation. Acceptance is slightly limited because agreement with a potentially risky partial-quit plan is not fully separated from positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence, practical constraints, and fear of attacks. Deeper emotions such as guilt, loss of control, and identity tied to smoking are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma, breathing, smoking reduction, and inhaler use. It does not adequately address drinking and broader exposure management, and the behavioral target remains somewhat vague.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed around the client’s practical needs, such as a simple phone alarm. The psychiatrist still leads much of the structure through summaries and interpretive framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure, respects the client’s need for simplicity, and supports choice without imposing a complicated plan. The support could be stronger with more explicit language reinforcing that the client owns the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions invite the client to generate reasons, constraints, and practical solutions. Evocation remains limited because it focuses mainly on feasibility rather than values, fear, hope, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and lets the client’s perspective shape the plan. Partnership is not maximal because there is no explicit agenda negotiation or clear statement that the client is the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk about sleep, breathing, driving, and protecting his children. This talk is not consistently amplified, deepened, or developed into stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly normalizes the client’s sustain talk about keeping the night smoke and avoiding full quitting. There is little effective softening, double-sided reflection, or discrepancy work to move the client toward stronger change consideration.
