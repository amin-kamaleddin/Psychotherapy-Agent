# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, validating the client's parenting efforts and stress without judgment. The tone remains warm, respectful, and non-critical throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects the client's ambivalence, worry about her son's health, and need for relief. Perspective-taking is consistent and helps name the tension between protecting her child and managing stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking in the context of stress, child health, and a small next step. However, the direction becomes somewhat repetitive and could more strategically build toward deeper values or goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is genuinely co-created, incorporating the client's ideas of a hot drink and standing by the car. The stance is collaborative, though the clinician still leads by offering multiple options throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client's sense of choice by emphasizing 'no pressure' and respecting her boundaries regarding reminders and follow-up. The plan is framed as a voluntary, low-pressure experiment that the client owns.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses effective open questions and scaling to elicit the client's own motivations and willingness to try a small step. Evocation could be more skillful if the clinician explored the client's underlying values and reasons for change before offering options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance by inviting the client's input on what feels possible. The client's suggestions clearly shaped the final plan, though the clinician still supplied several specific intervention ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively evokes change language by asking what would make quitting or cutting back feel more doable. The client's emerging change talk, such as willingness to try a hot drink, was identified but not fully amplified or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument and acknowledging the client's efforts to keep her son safe. The clinician could further soften resistance by more consistently linking the client's protective values to potential reasons for change.
