# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates a non-judgmental stance and avoids judgment effectively, but responses feel somewhat clinical and formulaic rather than deeply warm and individualized. Lacks consistent communication of deep, personal acceptance of the client as a whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with vivid, accurate reflections that capture experiential quality (e.g., the foggy feeling and hour slipping away). Some reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional undercurrents such as the client's subtle pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with logical progression and good strategic pacing that never wanders. The session trails off at the end without a clear collaborative next step or summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language, open-ended questions, and 'I'm curious' framings to avoid lecturing. However, the session feels more guided than truly equal, as the counselor structures the flow without explicitly inviting the client to co-set the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy with strong statements like 'I'm not here to convince you' and 'you don't have to manufacture a reason to care.' Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client's right to decide what to do with any information gained.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Excellent evoking questions that successfully draw out the client's own motivations and move from abstract to concrete without pushing. Could be stronger by following up on the client's 'maybe it's not nothing' with a deeper question about what that realization means to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with clear statements like 'I'm not going to force one' and frequently asks for the client's perspective. The power dynamic still feels somewhat counselor-led, with the psychiatrist doing most of the guiding and structuring.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evokes some change talk through well-crafted questions, but does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or deepen it when it appears. When the client says 'maybe it's not nothing,' the response validates but doesn't push further into exploring implications or desired actions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Correctly avoids arguing against or reinforcing sustain talk, but relies mainly on neutral reflections rather than active softening techniques. Lacks double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to acknowledge validity while gently exploring the other side.
