# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is consistently demonstrated with no judgment or pressure. The clinician validates Marisol's autonomy, boundaries, and frustration without any moments of disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate and nuanced reflections capture emotional complexity, such as the duality of the 'tooth thing sticking in your head' while feeling far away. However, the reflections could probe deeper into the emotional quality and underlying anxiety behind the dismissal.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and logical progression from opening through closure while maintaining focus on the target behavior. The session moved somewhat quickly to closure rather than sustaining the 'tooth thing' as a deeper focal point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with language like 'in your own terms' and 'I'm curious.' The interactional dynamic remains somewhat clinician-led, with Marisol in a brief-response role rather than actively shaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook MI autonomy support with explicit language affirming the client's ownership, timeline, and boundaries. The closing is exemplary, explicitly respecting her choice to disengage without making the relationship contingent on change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriate evocative questions are asked, but there is limited follow-through when Marisol identifies the tooth concern as sticking in her head. The clinician could have been more persistent and creative in drawing out underlying values and motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently positions Marisol as the expert in her own life and uses collaborative language. However, partnership is somewhat one-directional in practice, with the clinician doing most of the guiding and framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-relevant language is elicited, but the clinician does not actively amplify or deepen it. There is no explicit reflection of Marisol's values, no exploration of confidence language, and limited follow-up on seeds of motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by acknowledging validity while subtly redirecting, never arguing or reinforcing the dismissal. Could be strengthened with more explicit double-sided reflections affirming the positive within the sustain talk.
