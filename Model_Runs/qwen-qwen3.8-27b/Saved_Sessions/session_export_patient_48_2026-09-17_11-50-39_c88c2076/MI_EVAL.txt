# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, normalizing and validating Farid's self-doubt without reinforcing self-judgments. Language is warm, non-judgmental, and treats Farid as a whole person rather than someone who needs to be fixed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate, nuanced reflective listening that captures the emotional and cognitive texture of Farid's experience beyond surface-level paraphrasing. Reflections deepen over the session, including sophisticated complex reflections that validate Farid's ambivalent position.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and coherent arc from exploring barriers to identifying a concrete strategy, with each step building logically on the previous one. Could benefit from a brief collaborative summary or gentle exploration of readiness/commitment level at the end of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration where the psychiatrist brings MI skill and Farid brings all content, insight, and self-knowledge. The session direction is consistently shaped by Farid's responses, with the psychiatrist never overriding his contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Farid's ownership and freedom to choose through 'if' framings and open questions that leave all decision-making with the client. Never uses directive language such as 'you should' or 'you need to,' even when suggesting ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never states a single reason why Farid should exercise; every benefit and motivation is elicited from within Farid through carefully designed evocative questions. The 'message to yourself' exercise is a sophisticated technique that produces a powerful self-generated change statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom about change reside within Farid, functioning as a facilitator of his own thinking rather than an expert dispensing advice. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with the psychiatrist having no agenda beyond helping Farid find his own answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully cultivates change talk throughout without ever making the change talk for Farid, using evocative questions that lead him to articulate his own reasons for change. The session progresses from vague interest to a specific, self-generated value statement: 'This is the one thing that makes the tired feel different.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively by exploring it with curiosity rather than argument, particularly the 'earning it' threshold and the on/off pattern. Could more deeply explore the underlying all-or-nothing thinking and shame-based response to missed days for a full score.
