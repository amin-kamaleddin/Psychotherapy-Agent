# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and validates ambivalence. However, the client's worth is not as explicitly affirmed independent of outcomes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistently captures the client's meaning, emotion, and ambivalence. The counselor demonstrates strong perspective-taking and dual awareness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on small, sustainable movement changes and avoids wandering. The conversation progresses coherently from broad concerns to a concrete behavioral plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint language and invites the client's input throughout. The plan is co-created, with the client proposing key ideas such as the car-locking cue and recharging rest.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice and freedom are strongly emphasized without pressure or directive advice. The plan is framed as a personal option rather than a strict obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client's own values, self-efficacy, and meaning. The counselor asks effective questions rather than providing reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert and shares power. The client's contributions substantially shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor successfully elicits change talk, including desire, reason, and confidence. It could be strengthened by more actively amplifying change talk and evoking commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed without judgment or guilt. The session could benefit from more consistently redirecting from obstacles toward change-focused language.
