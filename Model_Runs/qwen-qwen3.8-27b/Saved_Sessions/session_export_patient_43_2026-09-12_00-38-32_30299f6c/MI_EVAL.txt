# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids direct criticism of Eric. However, acceptance becomes formulaic and is weakened when the session shifts into advice-giving and scripts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflective listening captures Eric’s frustration, comfort with smoking, and low urgency to change. Later reflections become more surface-level and do not deeply explore ambivalence, identity, or underlying concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a reasonable focus on smoking and health-related change. However, direction becomes inconsistent when the conversation shifts to scripts for managing the wife and an abrupt safety statement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some initial collaboration through choices and open invitations. However, the psychiatrist frequently becomes expert-driven by offering lists of strategies and scripts despite Eric’s repeated rejections.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive language is used, such as asking preference and respecting Eric’s choice. Overall, the psychiatrist often undermines autonomy by prescribing actions, substitutions, and specific scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes early evocative moves, including a readiness ruler and exploring pros and cons. However, evocation is not sustained and is replaced by directive advice and problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially invites Eric’s input and offers choices, but later becomes more counselor-driven. Repeatedly offering therapist-generated tactics reduces the sense that Eric is the expert in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited early effort to evoke change-related thinking, such as asking about readiness and situations linked to smoking. The psychiatrist does not consistently develop or amplify Eric’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Eric’s sustain talk without arguing, but does not effectively soften or redirect it. The suggested scripts may reinforce Eric’s current position rather than open the possibility of change.
