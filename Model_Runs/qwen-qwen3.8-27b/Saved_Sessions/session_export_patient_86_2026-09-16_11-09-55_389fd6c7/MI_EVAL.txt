# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of Marco’s right to refuse change. Warmth is strong but occasionally formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Marco’s stated concerns and desire for autonomy. Reflections often remain content-level and miss the deeper emotional experience underneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking and related dental/health concerns, and appropriately shifts toward respecting autonomy. The repeated sequence of small behavioral options makes the direction feel somewhat narrow and less strategically guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses collaborative language and adjusts options based on Marco’s feedback. However, much of the interaction remains selection from clinician-generated menus rather than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician strongly emphasizes Marco’s freedom, ownership, and right to decide, and ultimately respects his boundary. Repeated invitations to try tiny experiments before fully settling into his refusal can feel subtly pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session offers limited exploration of Marco’s internal values, reasons, fears, or confidence for change. The clinician frequently supplies solutions instead of eliciting and expanding client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites Marco’s perspective and adapts the conversation to his feedback, showing a collaborative tone. The structure remains uneven because the clinician supplies most of the content and drives the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only occasional, weak attempts to invite client-generated change talk. The clinician misses opportunities to elicit, amplify, or develop Marco’s own reasons, desire, need, ability, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing or reinforcing resistance and validates Marco’s reasons for not changing. Softening is not consistently strategic, as repeated new options keep the focus on behavioral experiments rather than reframing the resistance.
