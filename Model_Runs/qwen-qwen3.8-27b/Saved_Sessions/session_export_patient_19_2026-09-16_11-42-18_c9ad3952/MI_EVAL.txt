# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses affirming language. Acceptance is strong but somewhat task-focused, with room for warmer individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflective listening to track ambivalence and practical concerns. The main limitation is that empathy remains more cognitive and strategic than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing gambling-related financial risk. The progression from ambivalence to triggers, confidence, and a concrete plan is clear and consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician and client co-construct a practical plan, with the client contributing key choices and adjustments. Collaboration is strong, though frequent therapist-provided options make it slightly less equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently uses choice language and avoids imposing a plan. Autonomy support is strong, but could be reinforced more explicitly during moments of self-doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits the client’s own reasons, strategies, and confidence. Some reliance on scripts and examples slightly limits fully client-generated evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client meaningfully shapes the session and chooses key elements of the plan. The interaction is collaborative, though the clinician provides many structured options that make it somewhat therapist-shaped.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits and reinforces substantial action-oriented change talk and concrete commitments. It would be stronger if it more fully elicited values, identity, and long-term goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges ambivalence. However, the response often shifts quickly into planning rather than using reflective, double-sided softening strategies.
