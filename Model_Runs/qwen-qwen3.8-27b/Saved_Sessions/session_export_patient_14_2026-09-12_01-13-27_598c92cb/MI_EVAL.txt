# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and practical stance, accepting the client's frustrations and constraints. The tone is supportive but somewhat brisk and efficiency-focused rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects frustration and acknowledges the client's perspective, but empathy remains surface-level and task-centered. There is limited exploration of the emotional meaning of the client's time pressure, ambivalence, or health concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is strongly focused on a specific, practical blood pressure and weight-related plan. The psychiatrist keeps the conversation on task with minimal irrelevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adjusts the plan based on the client's preferences, creating a realistic and co-created approach. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still supplies much of the content and decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and accepts the client's modifications, supporting some autonomy. However, the session remains directive in content, reducing the sense that the client is generating his own solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some preferences and commitments, but the approach is more informational and problem-solving than evocative. Internal motivations, values, and confidence are not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to the client's constraints, supporting a collaborative tone. However, the psychiatrist still functions primarily as the expert providing solutions rather than fully affirming the client's authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps the client articulate a workable action and some commitment language. Change talk is elicited but not consistently deepened through questions about values, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce resistance, acknowledging barriers without judgment. However, MI-consistent strategies such as double-sided reflections or reframing are not consistently used to soften sustain talk.
