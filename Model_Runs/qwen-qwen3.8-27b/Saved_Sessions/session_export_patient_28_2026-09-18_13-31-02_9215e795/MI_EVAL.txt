# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with normalizing and non-judgmental language (e.g., 'It's not like you gave up,' 'Those are real competing priorities, not laziness'). The psychiatrist never labels, disapproves, or implies the client should feel ashamed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate, specific, and go beyond surface paraphrasing to capture subtle emotional nuances (e.g., distinguishing 'I don't know how' from 'I don't want to'). The psychiatrist consistently names underlying feelings like the fear of repeating past patterns of routines dissolving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and logical progression from understanding the loss through exploring values to building toward intention, all directly connected to the target behavior. The session feels slightly open-ended at its conclusion, as the emerging intention ('try it tomorrow?') is not consolidated into a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The power dynamic is genuinely balanced, with Dante's contributions (the ten-block walk, the 'quiet' framing, the realization about scale) substantially shaping the session's direction and outcome. The psychiatrist follows the client's lead while providing strategic reflections and questions that deepen exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No directive language is used; the psychiatrist explicitly removes the prescriptive stance ('not to prescribe it, but to figure it out with you'). The client's final realization and intention emerge entirely from within, which is the hallmark of effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits motivation and solutions from within the client rather than providing external reasons for change. The progression from 'I don't know how' to 'I could just... try it tomorrow?' is entirely Dante's own insight, facilitated but not provided by the psychiatrist.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly states the partnership model and consistently positions expertise as residing within Dante ('You already know what your quiet looks like'). The session direction is substantially shaped by the client's own contributions and insights.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits desire ('I miss that'), capability ('Maybe it's not as far as I thought'), and intention ('I could just... try it tomorrow?') through strategic questions and affirmations. The session could benefit from further exploration of commitment level, confidence, and multiple dimensions of change talk before concluding.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with validation and gentle reframing rather than argument or dismissal (e.g., 'That's not a character flaw. That's someone who's learned, the hard way, how fragile these things can be'). The psychiatrist acknowledges the logic in sustain talk while removing self-critical framing and redirecting toward change.
