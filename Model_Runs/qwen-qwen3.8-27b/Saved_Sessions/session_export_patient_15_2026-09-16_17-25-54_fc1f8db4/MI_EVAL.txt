# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoids blaming or criticizing Andrei. The tone is cognitively respectful, though it could include more explicit warmth and positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening and accurately reflects ambivalence about health, work, and daily life. Some reflections become repetitive and miss deeper emotional meaning, such as the worry behind Andrei’s comments about beer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on blood pressure management, alcohol reduction, tracking, and small next steps. The focus becomes too narrow when it centers on proving that beer is not the issue rather than moving toward broader change motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Andrei to help shape the plan and co-constructs a tracking approach with him. The interaction remains somewhat therapist-led, and repeated questions may reduce the sense of true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice by asking what would be doable, realistic, or meaningful for Andrei. It would be stronger if the decision were more explicitly framed as fully Andrei’s to make rather than implied through the beer experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help Andrei articulate reasons for tracking, such as certainty and relief from worry. The exploration remains somewhat surface-level and does not deeply connect the plan to broader values, fears, or internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Andrei as the expert on his own life and invites his perspective on what would be manageable. The session is still largely led by the psychiatrist, which slightly limits a fully equal partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Andreis expresses some action-oriented language, such as tracking blood pressure, easing beer, and reducing salt. The psychiatrist does not consistently affirm or deepen Andrei’s own agency, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Andrei’s doubts without arguing or directly confronting him. However, the conversation remains heavily focused on proving that beer is not the cause, rather than consistently softening sustain talk and linking it to change talk.
