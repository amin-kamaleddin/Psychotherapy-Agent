# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Jordan’s nervousness and court-related pressure. Acceptance is strong but sometimes tied to effort rather than clearly separating Jordan’s behavior from his worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Jordan’s ambivalence about stopping shoplifting while keeping his friends. Empathy remains largely at the content level and does not fully explore deeper feelings such as shame, fear of rejection, or need to belong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and practical next steps, such as the envelope plan and a refusal line. Direction is clear, though it can feel task-driven and checklist-like at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s input and offers role-play, supporting a collaborative stance. However, the counselor often supplies options and scripts, making the interaction more counselor-driven than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan is given choices and selects the envelope plan and phone-based backup. Autonomy support is weakened by the detailed, prescriptive planning and exact phrases the psychiatrist provides.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions, such as what stopping would change and confidence scaling. However, too many solutions and scripts are offered before Jordan generates his own motivation and change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership because Jordan contributes decisions within the session. The psychiatrist’s repeated lists and scripted guidance limit the sense that Jordan is the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including Jordan’s desire to avoid court trouble and try the envelope plan. Change talk is not deeply expanded or connected to Jordan’s values, identity, or longer-term goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges peer influence, temptation, and fear of teasing without arguing against Jordan’s sustain talk. The session does not consistently use sustain talk as a springboard to redirect toward change.
