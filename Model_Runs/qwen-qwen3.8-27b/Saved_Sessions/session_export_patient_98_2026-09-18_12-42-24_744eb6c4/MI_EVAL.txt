# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, validating Mateo's experience without judgment throughout the session. There is no labeling, disapproval, or minimization; the warm, respectful tone affirms Mateo's worth and effort even in moments of discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both the content and emotional texture of Mateo's experience, evolving in complexity as the conversation deepens. The psychiatrist tracks Mateo's emotional shifts from resignation to curiosity to a small spark of hope with precise attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a remarkably clear focus on the target behavior (increasing physical activity/rehab progress) with a logical arc from exploring ambivalence to generating a concrete action plan. The psychiatrist never drifts off-topic and consistently uses reflections to anchor the conversation toward the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is structured as a genuine partnership between equals, with the psychiatrist building on Mateo's language and ideas rather than redirecting to their own agenda. The final plan is entirely co-created, with the psychiatrist adding nothing to the client-generated solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Mateo's ownership by inviting his own solutions, using no prescriptive language, and explicitly acknowledging his self-generated ideas. Mateo's right to uncertainty is respected without being pushed past his comfort level.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never states external reasons for change; every question is designed to draw out Mateo's own motivations and values. The final action plan is entirely Mateo-generated, demonstrating that both motivation and solution reside within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Mateo as the expert on his own experience, using questions that invite his insights rather than offering the clinician's answers. The stance is that of a guide, not a director, with Mateo's contributions substantially shaping the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully amplifies, reflects, and builds on every instance of change talk, from the subtle 'I'd like to feel something' to the concrete morning plan. Open questions at key moments consistently invite Mateo to generate his own reasons and solutions for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively acknowledges sustain talk without reinforcing it, using double-sided reflections that subtly weight toward change. However, transitions from sustain talk to change-oriented exploration could have been slightly more explicit in their strategic redirection.
