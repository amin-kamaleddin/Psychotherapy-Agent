# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, normalizing, and affirming language without judgment about drinking, inactivity, or minimization. The stance is warm but not fully individualized or deep enough for a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s content but remain largely surface-level. There is limited exploration of the underlying feelings, ambivalence, or fear behind his minimization and resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on elevated blood pressure and moves clearly toward a concrete behavior-change plan. Momentum is occasionally diluted by multiple options, and the alcohol concern is not fully resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and co-creates a plan based on his preferences and barriers. It is still somewhat provider-led because he structures many options and agenda points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, respects the client’s decisions, and avoids directive pressure. It could be stronger by more explicitly affirming that the client fully owns the choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative moves are present, such as asking about confidence and barriers, but the session often shifts to counselor-generated planning. There is limited drawing out of the client’s own deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction treats the client as a contributor, with repeated invitations for input and a client-informed plan. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still sets much of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers mild change talk, and the psychiatrist asks questions that invite small commitments. The evocation is procedural and plan-focused rather than systematically developing the client’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and responds nonjudgmentally. He does not consistently use more advanced MI strategies to redirect ambivalence toward change.
