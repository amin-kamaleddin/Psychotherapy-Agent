# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating the client’s stress. The acceptance is somewhat task-focused and could more explicitly affirm the client’s effort and right to feel ambivalent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are adequate but mostly content-focused, capturing deadlines and documentation pressure. Deeper reflection of underlying worry, avoidance, or fear would improve emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on completing the court-mandated hours and related documentation. The direction is largely logistical, with limited connection to the client’s own goals, values, or sense of agency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to client preferences, but frequently supplies options, scripts, and templates rather than co-creating them. More balanced power-sharing would allow the client’s ideas to drive the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong through nonjudgment, permission-seeking, and respect for the client’s preference for simplicity. It could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing the client’s ownership of choices and consequences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is driven mostly by practical suggestions and drafts rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations, values, or confidence. More evocative questions about what matters to the client would be needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client choices and allows them to shape the session, but often assumes an expert role by drafting tools. More explicit affirmation of the client’s own judgment would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some prompts invite ownership, but the session does not consistently amplify or expand the client’s own change talk. The client’s language remains mostly compliance-driven rather than motivational.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes the client’s avoidance and simplifies concerns without arguing or escalating resistance. However, the response lacks strategic reframing or double-sided reflections that could pivot toward change.
