# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client's frustration without blame. Acceptance was strong but became repetitive near the end and did not deepen beyond brief acknowledgments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several reflections accurately captured the client's frustration, ambivalence, and sense that the process feels pointless. Empathy remained mostly surface-level and did not fully explore underlying feelings such as loss of control or hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a specific, manageable behavior change: asking whether Kieran needs to stay or can leave. The ending became somewhat repetitive and administrative, which slightly diluted the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and asked for the client's preference in several moments. Collaboration weakened toward the end when the clinician repeatedly restated the plan after the client signaled a desire to leave.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized the client's choice, including the option to simply note that he was done for the day. The directive statement about choosing one tiny option slightly reduced the sense of client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made an MI-style evocation attempt by asking what Kieran might change about the process. However, the conversation shifted quickly to a clinician-generated solution rather than drawing out the client's own motivations, values, or goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed partnership by asking for the client's preference and using a low-pressure, collaborative tone. The balance was uneven because the psychiatrist also took a directive role and the closing became repetitive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked a small step of change and reflected the client's willingness to try once. The change talk was not further developed through questions about reasons, confidence, benefits, or past experiences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument, which avoided reinforcing resistance. The psychiatrist did not consistently redirect sustain talk toward agency, benefits, or smaller change goals.
