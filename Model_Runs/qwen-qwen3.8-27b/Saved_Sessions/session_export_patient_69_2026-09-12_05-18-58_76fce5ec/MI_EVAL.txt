# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect, patience, and nonjudgment toward the client’s irritation and disengagement. Acceptance remains somewhat surface-level, with less acknowledgment of the client’s likely sense of being mandated or powerless.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, respectful reflections of the client’s impatience, boredom, and resistance. Empathy could go deeper beneath the client’s flat affect, resignation, and underlying ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, practical focus on a small, manageable action that matches the client’s preference for brevity. The direction is somewhat task-focused and does not consistently connect the small step to broader motivation or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and adapts the log to the client’s request for brevity. Collaboration is uneven because the therapist largely generates the intervention options rather than co-creating the session agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides clear choice language, avoids pressure, and respects the client’s decision to end the session. Autonomy support is limited by the therapist’s structured menu of tasks and options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some open-ended attempts to elicit the client’s own motivations. These efforts are not consistently developed, with limited use of evocative follow-up questions or deeper exploration of change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and states that the client is “in charge,” supporting a sense of partnership. The session remains relatively expert-driven, with the therapist introducing most interventions and the client mainly selecting from those options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only weak attempts to cultivate the client’s own reasons for change. The psychiatrist moves quickly to practical options rather than amplifying the client’s own language or exploring values and preferences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it. The counselor does not strategically redirect or reframe sustain talk toward smaller, more tolerable changes.
