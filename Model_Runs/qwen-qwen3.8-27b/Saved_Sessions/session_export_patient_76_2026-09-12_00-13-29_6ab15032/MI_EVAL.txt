# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental stance, validating Liora’s feelings and actions. Acceptance is slightly limited by a scripted, crisis-management focus on safety compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers clear reflections that validate the functional role of cutting and Liora’s exhaustion. Empathy is limited by less exploration of deeper shame, loneliness, or the conflict between needing relief and being exhausted by the only coping tool.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the relevant target behavior of self-harm, with a clear through-line from safety assessment to coping and rest. Direction is more crisis-management than MI-guided change, with less emphasis on Liora’s own goals and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Liora to choose among coping options and incorporates her selections, creating some shared effort. However, the agenda remains largely shaped by the psychiatrist’s repeated lists and safety protocol rather than Liora’s own plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices and respects refusals, avoiding overt coercion. Autonomy support is limited because the session remains directive, with repeated prompts, lists, and instructions that may reduce Liora’s ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few initial evocative openings, but the session quickly becomes prescriptive rather than eliciting Liora’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change. Key opportunities to evoke confidence, ability, or protected values are missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and adapts to some choices, which supports collaboration. However, the interaction is uneven because the psychiatrist leads much of the session through structured safety and coping steps rather than treating Liora as the primary decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Liora offers several relevant statements of intention, such as not cutting right now and trying gum, but the psychiatrist does not consistently expand or reinforce them. More skillful MI would invite Liora to articulate her own reasons, confidence, and values for safer coping.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges some sustain talk without arguing, but does not consistently soften or redirect it toward change. Adding more coping lists after ambivalence can unintentionally reinforce the belief that nothing works, whereas double-sided reflections would be more MI-consistent.
