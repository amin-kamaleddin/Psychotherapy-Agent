# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed Claudia's perspective and effort. Warmth was solid but remained somewhat procedural and plan-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used accurate reflections of Claudia's perspective and ambivalence, especially early in the session. Deeper empathy was missed when responding to emotional nuances such as her mood, irritability, and fear of slipping.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing evening wine use, moving from ambivalence to a concrete plan. There was no meaningful drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Claudia as an active partner by inviting input on steps, wording, timing, and follow-up. Collaboration was strong, though the clinician still led the structure and provided many specific suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a major strength, with repeated invitations, permission-seeking language, and respect for Claudia's choices. The final alarm wording clearly preserved her ability to decide rather than imposing abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk through open questions, scaling, and preference-based planning. Evocation remained somewhat superficial because values, identity, and personal meaning of change were not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited Claudia's input and allowed her preferences to shape the plan. Partnership was evident, though the interaction sometimes felt like selecting from clinician-offered options rather than fully co-constructing direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generated moderate change talk, including willingness to try a one-week experiment and confidence increases. The psychiatrist did not consistently deepen change talk by exploring values, benefits, or self-image.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by accepting Claudia's limits and preserving choice without arguing or pushing abstinence. Deeper ambivalence was not fully explored with more advanced double-sided reflections.
