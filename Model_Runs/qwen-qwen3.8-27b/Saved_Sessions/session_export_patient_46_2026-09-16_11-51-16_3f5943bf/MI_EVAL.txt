# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and reassurance while avoiding moralizing or shaming. The acceptance is strong but slightly formulaic and could more explicitly affirm Ethan’s strengths, resilience, and individualized worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses reflective listening to capture surface-level content and concerns about judgment. However, reflections often remain behavioral and miss deeper emotional or identity-based themes such as belonging, dignity, and autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing risky alcohol use while preserving social connection. The direction is strong but could be tightened further with a clearer consolidation of the specific plan and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and invitation for Ethan to choose among options, but the psychiatrist often supplies the strategies and scripts. A more collaborative approach would elicit Ethan’s own ideas first and build on them more explicitly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Ethan’s boundaries, asks permission, and supports his choice to slow down rather than stop. This could be strengthened with more explicit language emphasizing that Ethan owns the decisions and plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocation questions around importance, confidence, and feasibility, and Ethan offers several useful change statements. The session would be stronger if it more consistently drew out Ethan’s values, goals, and discrepancies rather than shifting into advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and asks which options feel doable, which supports some partnership. However, the interaction becomes more expert-driven because the psychiatrist supplies much of the agenda, strategies, and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through importance and confidence questions and by asking which strategies feel doable. These client-generated ideas are often not fully amplified, deepened, or connected to values and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by normalizing social drinking and accepting harm reduction without confrontation. This could be improved by using more double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to connect sustain talk with change talk.
