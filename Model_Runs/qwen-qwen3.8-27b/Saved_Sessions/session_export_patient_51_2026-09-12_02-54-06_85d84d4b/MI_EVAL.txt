# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and affirming, but repeatedly adding warnings and offers after the client says she must leave undercuts acceptance of her stated time boundary.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The responses accurately reflect the client's plan and urgency, but the empathy remains largely cognitive and does not deepen into the emotional tension behind her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on medication safety, the 14-day washout, warning signs, and prescriber follow-up. The main limitation is that this focus becomes repetitive rather than efficiently guiding a concise client-owned close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative gestures, but the interaction becomes expert-driven with repeated information and offers that do not fully integrate the client's clear preference for brevity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and offers choices, but continued detailed warnings after the client repeatedly needs to leave make the approach feel more prescriptive than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides safety information and instructions more than eliciting the client's own motivations, concerns, or confidence about the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening invites collaboration, but the interaction quickly shifts to the psychiatrist leading with repeated safety details rather than treating the client as the expert on her time and needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers a clear plan and the psychiatrist affirms it, but there is little strategic effort to expand, deepen, or amplify her own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client's time constraint and acknowledges her need to leave, but does not use double-sided reflections or other MI skills to help resolve the ambivalence between safety and time pressure.
