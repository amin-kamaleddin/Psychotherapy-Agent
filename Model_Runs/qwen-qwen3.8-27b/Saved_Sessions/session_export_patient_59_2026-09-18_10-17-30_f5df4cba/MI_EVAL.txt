# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with strong validation of the client's experience, including the powerful 'real need, not a flaw' statement. Slightly held back from a 5 by a somewhat measured warmth that doesn't fully normalize the client's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and nuanced reflective listening that captures the emotional essence more precisely than the client articulated it. Consistently identifies and reflects the undercurrent beneath surface-level dismissal with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear strategic progression from rapport through goals to barriers, keeping focus on the target behavior. Could more explicitly connect the autonomy/mom theme back to the original clinical goal of marijuana use and avoidance patterns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative dialogue with a genuinely balanced power dynamic; the psychiatrist follows the client's lead and meets resistance with curiosity rather than correction. The client is treated as an equal partner with expertise about his own life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally strong throughout—never uses 'should' or 'need to,' consistently frames choices as the client's own, and removes external motivation framing. The job question is a model of autonomy support, focusing entirely on the client's own values.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the client's own motivations through open questions like 'what made those stick in your head?' Could be stronger by more deeply evoking the discrepancy between stated values (independence, not being stuck) and current avoidance patterns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the session as a joint exploration between equals, never assuming answers or positioning as authority. The client's responses genuinely shape the direction of the conversation, making it fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk, particularly around future goals and the 'I don't wanna be stuck' statement. Could push further by more actively deepening and expanding change talk throughout, especially connecting it to the target behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively acknowledges and softens sustain talk without arguing or challenging, as seen with the weed dismissal. Could more actively explore the cost of avoidance patterns and redirect sustain talk toward change.
