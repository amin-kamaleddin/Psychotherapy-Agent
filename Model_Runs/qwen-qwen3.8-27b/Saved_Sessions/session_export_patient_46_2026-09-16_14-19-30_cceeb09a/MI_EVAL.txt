# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout. Warmth is strong but sometimes formulaic and could be more individualized during moments of defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Ethan's ambivalence between belonging and health. Deeper emotional attunement to shame, fear, or embarrassment would strengthen the work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on drinking, friendships, and the soccer game with a clear logical flow. It would be stronger if it consolidated a specific next step or change focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively and lets Ethan's ideas guide the conversation. More visible client-led agenda-setting or real-time co-creation would improve power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and affirms Ethan's control over his choices. Explicitly normalizing his right to choose, including the right to drink, would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Ethan's own motivations, values, and possible solutions effectively. Deeper exploration of confidence, readiness, or personally compelling change language would be needed for a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly partners with Ethan rather than directing him. The process remains counselor-led in structure, limiting full client-driven partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws out change talk and options from Ethan. More deliberate amplification, scaling, or DARN-focused exploration would strengthen the score.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without being reinforced, often through balanced reflections. More strategic double-sided reflections or cost-focused language could reduce its dominance.
