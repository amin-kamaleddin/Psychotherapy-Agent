# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, nonjudgmental, and explicitly creates an unjudged space, including affirming Jamal’s request to keep things clear and simple. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and could more consistently affirm Jamal’s strengths and worth independent of the legal situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture Jamal’s ambivalence and practical concerns about being overburdened by the process. The reflections remain mostly cognitive and logistical, with limited exploration of underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and legal consequences, especially protecting Jamal’s record and staying out of trouble. Direction could more deliberately move from compliance to intrinsic motivation and broader change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Jamal to shape a brief, practical plan and incorporates his preference for a short, low-pressure check-in. The collaboration is effective but remains within a narrow compliance framework rather than exploring change more broadly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions, avoids pressure, and respects Jamal’s request for a brief no-homework check-in. Autonomy support could be strengthened by explicitly affirming that Jamal is the expert and in control of his own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical preferences about making the check-in useful and fair, but mostly stays at the logistical level. There are missed opportunities to evoke deeper change talk related to values, confidence, future goals, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Jamal to define what would be useful or fair. Partnership is strong on session logistics but limited in exploring broader goals, concerns, and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-relevant statements are elicited, such as protecting his record and not being the same person who gets caught again. The psychiatrist could better amplify, summarize, or expand these statements into stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Jamal’s sustain talk and gently reflects it while shifting toward practical consequences. However, accepting “not using, not getting caught” as sufficient risks reinforcing compliance focused on avoiding detection rather than deeper behavioral change.
