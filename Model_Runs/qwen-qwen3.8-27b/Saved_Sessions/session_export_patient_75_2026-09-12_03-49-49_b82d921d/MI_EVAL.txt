# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, normalizing Linda's guilt, fear, and freezing. Acceptance is slightly weakened when approval and directives become intertwined with problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflection of Linda's ambivalence, shame, and conflicting feelings about inhaler use. At times, empathy quickly shifts to practical problem-solving before fully exploring the emotional weight of her concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safe inhaler use and preparing for the clinician conversation, with clear structure and manageable steps. The direction occasionally becomes checklist-like, introducing many options that may feel overwhelming.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language and joint role-play are used, and the psychiatrist adapts options to Linda's comfort level. However, the session remains expert-driven, with the psychiatrist introducing much of the agenda and medical content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda is given low-burden options and choices, including handing a note instead of speaking. Autonomy support is weakened by directive statements such as continuing the inhaler as prescribed and relying on psychiatrist-provided scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions allow Linda to express concerns, but the psychiatrist often moves quickly into information-giving and scripts. There is less opportunity for Linda to generate and deepen her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and involves Linda in selecting next steps, creating a collaborative format. The partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently assumes the expert role and drives the content of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked through questions about what feels doable and which script feels easier. These statements are not consistently amplified or deepened, and the psychiatrist often supplies the language rather than eliciting it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda's concerns about side effects and clinician reactions are acknowledged as understandable. The psychiatrist does not fully use MI techniques to pivot this sustain talk, instead offering reassurance and directives that maintain the current regimen.
