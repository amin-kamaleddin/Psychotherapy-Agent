# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional and consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with no instances of judgment or subtle pressure. The psychiatrist validates Richard's reluctance, his defensiveness, and even the frightening layby incident with warmth and curiosity rather than alarm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding empathic attunement, with reflections that consistently capture the emotional function behind the behavior rather than surface content. The reflection about the cigarette keeping 'the quiet from getting loud' is a profoundly accurate complex reflection that demonstrates deep understanding of the client's inner world.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear and coherent focus on smoking cessation, progressing logically from resistance to routine to health concern. However, forward momentum could be sharper — the session ends at increased awareness without a collaborative trajectory toward a concrete next step or plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative interaction where Richard's contributions substantially shape the session direction. The psychiatrist consistently follows Richard's lead (e.g., the reading pack), uses inclusive joint language, and the session functions as a joint exploration rather than a clinical interview.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support, with explicit respect for the client's choices and boundaries throughout. The psychiatrist never uses directive language and repeatedly affirms Richard's authority over his own decisions ('That's your call,' 'You know your life better than I do').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation consistently embodying the principle that 'the answer is in the client.' The psychiatrist never provides a single statistic or reason for change; all movement comes from Richard's own words, elicited through varied and strategically timed questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent genuine partnership with an explicitly equal power dynamic. The psychiatrist clearly rejects the expert role ('I'm not here to be another voice telling you what to do') and structures the entire session around Richard's own language, priorities, and pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of evocative questions and noticing subtle language differences (e.g., 'I don't see what quitting looks like' vs. 'I'll never do it') to open space for change talk. However, the psychiatrist could more explicitly amplify and reflect Richard's emerging change talk to deepen and consolidate it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful rolling with resistance, validating the client's perspective without endorsing it as final (e.g., 'I'm not here to argue with your track record'). Could be strengthened by more deliberate use of double-sided reflections to honor both the routine's value and the body's emerging signals, creating productive cognitive dissonance.
