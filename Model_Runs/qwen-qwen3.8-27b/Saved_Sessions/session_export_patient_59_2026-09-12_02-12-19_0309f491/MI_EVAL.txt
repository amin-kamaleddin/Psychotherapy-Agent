# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician is nonjudgmental and uses permission-based language, but warmth remains somewhat mechanical. Repeated offers of a script/checklist create mild pressure and limit individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reflects the client's surface-level concerns accurately, but responses stay mostly cognitive and content-focused. Missed opportunities to acknowledge frustration, fear, or loss of control prevent deeper empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has clear logistical direction around court requirements, but it lacks an MI direction toward behavior change or discrepancy. The conversation remains focused on procedural details rather than connecting the situation to Jamal's values or goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses some collaborative language and follows the client's lead on factual questions. However, much of the session is clinician-driven information giving rather than genuine co-creation of a therapeutic focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through phrases like “your call” and “you’re free to leave,” but repeated offers of tools and expert advice create subtle pressure. A stronger approach would elicit what Jamal wants to handle independently rather than repeatedly offering external supports.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician provides information but does little to evoke Jamal's own reasons for change or concerns about future consequences. Missed opportunities to explore values, goals, confidence, and motivation keep evocation weak.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership is evident in permission-based questions and respect for the client's request for facts. The clinician still takes an expert role on court process, limiting the sense of Jamal as the expert on his own life and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only brief, weak attempts to elicit change talk, and the client's statements about returning to his life are not built upon. The session lacks DARN-C strategies, confidence questions, or summarization of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with minimization, which is a strength, but does not actively soften or reframe the client's sustain talk. Statements about the situation being “a little weed” are left unexamined rather than pivoted toward consequences or discrepancy.
