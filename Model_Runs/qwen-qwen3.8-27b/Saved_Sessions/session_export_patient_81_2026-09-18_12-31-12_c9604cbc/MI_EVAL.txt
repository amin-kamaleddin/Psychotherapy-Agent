# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally respectful and non-judgmental, but the 'alcohol' error reveals a lack of attentiveness to the client's specific context. Affirmations tend to focus on the client's statements rather than deeply affirming the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures ambivalence and emotional experience, including a well-executed complex reflection. The alcohol/meth error and some slightly formulaic reflections prevent a higher score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from ambivalence exploration to concrete change ideas to deeper self-awareness, with good pacing. The alcohol error is a minor directional lapse, but overall focus on methamphetamine use is consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as an equal partner through open-ended questions and exploratory framing, avoiding any lecturing or advice. A slightly polished, professional distance prevents it from feeling like a true partnership between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's right to choose, framing ideas as options and explicitly crediting the client's own insights. Could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing self-direction in moments of client doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent throughout — every insight in the session comes from the client himself, drawn out through well-timed open-ended questions without any advice or reasons for change. The session ends with the client articulating a deeper self-understanding they had not previously expressed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with strong partnership language, explicitly crediting the client and avoiding an expert role. Could be elevated by more explicitly validating the client's expertise and negotiating the agenda at the outset.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates change talk through open-ended questions and skillful reflection/amplification of the client's own language. The psychiatrist explicitly names the client's shift in language, highlighting their own movement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk reasonably but often simply acknowledges it rather than strategically redirecting with double-sided reflections or gentle pivots. The distinction between 'impossible' and 'doesn't fix the money' is a good softening move, but several moments are missed.
