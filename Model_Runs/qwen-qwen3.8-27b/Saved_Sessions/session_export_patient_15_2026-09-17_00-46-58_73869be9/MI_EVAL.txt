# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Andrei’s perspective without criticism or blame. Some slightly positivity-biased interpretations subtly define the client’s motives rather than letting his own meaning fully emerge.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures Andrei’s preference for simplicity, routine, and preserving family time. The empathy remains somewhat cognitive and task-focused, not deeply exploring the emotional weight of surprise or underlying ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on making medication use part of the evening routine, showing clear practical momentum. Direction is limited because broader case targets, especially alcohol reduction and values-based health change, are not addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting Andrei to choose routines and placements rather than deciding for him. Collaboration is strong but not fully equal-partner because the psychiatrist still leads much of the process and agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Andrei is frequently treated as the decision-maker, with options presented and choices respected. Some language subtly leads toward preferred interpretations, such as suggesting he is ready or framing the plan as a simple habit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Andrei’s own practical ideas, such as taking medication with dinner and placing the bottle visibly. Evocation remains surface-level and does not consistently draw out deeper values, motivation, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Andrei’s input and avoids an authoritarian stance, treating him as the expert on his routine. The session remains somewhat clinician-led, with the psychiatrist structuring questions and occasionally interpreting for the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Andrei expresses practical change talk about taking medication and incorporating it into his routine. The psychiatrist does not systematically elicit deeper change talk themes such as desire, ability, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Andrei’s minimizing statements are not reinforced or challenged, which is appropriate. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently use MI-specific strategies, such as double-sided reflections, to actively redirect sustain talk toward change.
