# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates Tyler’s ambivalence without blame or moralizing. Positive regard remains somewhat procedural, as it does not strongly affirm Tyler’s strengths, courage, or worth as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, complex reflections that capture Tyler’s conflict between social participation and avoiding negative consequences. The empathy is strong but often more cognitive than emotionally deep, with less exploration of shame, embarrassment, or identity threat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and relevant focus on alcohol use, social consequences, citation risk, and implementation. The direction becomes somewhat narrow and repetitive as it repeatedly returns to the same implementation-focused questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Tyler’s own ideas, such as a check-in buddy, a ride home, and keeping the app visible. The collaboration is solid but not fully equal, because the psychiatrist maintains a steady agenda and guides the conversation toward a specific plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong autonomy-supportive language, emphasizing choice, ownership, and avoiding a rule-like approach. The score is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still guides Tyler toward a specific ride and texting strategy rather than repeatedly affirming full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to help Tyler generate ideas and some self-awareness. However, evocation remains somewhat superficial and problem-solving focused, with less exploration of values, discrepancy, confidence, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates that Tyler is the expert on his own life and builds on his contributions throughout. The partnership is collaborative but still somewhat guided, as the psychiatrist maintains a clear internal trajectory toward a specific plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some meaningful change talk, including Tyler’s concern about risking his degree or record. The change talk is not consistently amplified, summarized, or deepened, and standard evocative moves such as importance/confidence rulers are not used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by not arguing with or reinforcing Tyler’s reasons for continued drinking. The score is not a 5 because the psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or explicit reframing to pivot sustain talk toward change.
