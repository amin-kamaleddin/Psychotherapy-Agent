# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout, validating Elias's struggle without minimizing it or pressuring resolution. Minor tendency to veer toward agreement rather than pure acceptance in a few moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally sophisticated complex reflections that precisely capture the dual nature of Elias's ambivalence, such as distinguishing 'can't' from 'don't want to' and reflecting the emotional weight of nightly counting without being sycophantic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear through-line around the central question of choice, with appropriate pacing from relapse reflection toward tentative ownership. Could benefit from slightly more concrete anchoring to specific behavioral goals or next steps in the recovery plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Cedes agenda-setting to Elias and consistently uses co-investigator language, positioning both parties as equals. The closing framing of 'watching it with you rather than watching you fight it' is a perfect example of balanced power dynamics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Elias's ownership and pace, explicitly respecting his timeline ('You don't have to stop counting tonight'). Never pushes for a decision or prescribes an outcome, allowing 'I don't know yet' to remain a complete answer.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Primarily draws motivation from within Elias through strategic questions that elicit his own reasons and evidence (e.g., the kids, three months of progress). A few reflections lean slightly toward interpretation rather than pure evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the work as residing within Elias, using curious, open language that invites his perspective rather than supplying answers. The psychiatrist serves as a skilled guide rather than an authority throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Elias's own change talk through strategic questioning about moments of genuine choice and the evidence of three months of progress. A few reflections could have stayed more open to allow Elias to generate additional change language independently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk with double-sided reflections that acknowledge the fear of ownership while gently opening a door toward agency. Minor gap: could have engaged slightly more with the 'if I slip' fear that Elias introduces but which is largely left to sit.
