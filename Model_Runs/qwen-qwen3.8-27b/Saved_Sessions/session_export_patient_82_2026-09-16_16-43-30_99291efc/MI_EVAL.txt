# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Lukas’s experience without labeling or blaming. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with room to more concretely affirm his courage in disclosing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate reflective listening and captures the tension between friendships, identity, and performance. Empathy could deepen by exploring the underlying fear, shame, and self-critical loop more consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol experimentation, peer pressure, and Lukas’s competing identities. At times, the focus narrows into practical scripting and could better emphasize values, safety, and a clearly client-owned change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Lukas’s perspective and treats him as the expert on his friendships. Collaboration is evident, though the interaction sometimes feels guided by counselor-generated problem-solving rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids judgment, directives, and pressure, reinforcing that the decision is Lukas’s. A stronger 5 would more explicitly affirm his freedom to continue, reduce, or decide later.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions, but they tend to focus on practical options and conditions rather than deeper motivation. There is less exploration of Lukas’s values, identity, fear, confidence, and importance of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative posture and positions Lukas as the expert on his own life. Partnership is strong, though the session still moves somewhat toward counselor-led behavioral solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk by inviting alternatives and reflecting Lukas’s goals. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, deepened, or reinforced through desire, ability, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and generally redirects toward values and alternatives. Softening is inconsistent, especially when sustaining talk such as staying with the group is addressed through surface-level strategies rather than deeper reflective pivots.
