# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes help-seeking without blame or shame. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth is somewhat professional and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some perspective-taking regarding pride, independence, and realism, but empathy often remains shallow and quickly shifts to practical problem-solving. Missed opportunities include deeper reflection on shame, fear, and identity as a father.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on managing anger, reducing alcohol-related risk, and increasing accountability for reunification. It could be stronger by more consistently using MI-guided prioritization and values-focused summarization.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with Wiremu choosing the scenario, rejecting formal language, and selecting his own short phrase. It is not a 5 because the early detailed plan feels somewhat counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by inviting choice, respecting Wiremu’s decision not to send the text today, and encouraging language that sounds like him. The directive six-step plan and specific scripts limit full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Wiremu does generate some of his own change language, such as “I’m out. Talk later.” However, the clinician frequently moves from brief questions into scripts, strategies, and safety planning rather than fully evoking his own motivations and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Wiremu rather than at him, asking for his preferences and following his lead in rehearsal. It is not a 5 because the session begins with a fairly structured plan that can reduce the sense of co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms existing steps and connects them to goals, such as protecting his children and showing change to the court. However, change talk is not consistently evoked or expanded across desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced or argued against, and some concerns are acknowledged as legitimate. The responses are mainly neutral or practical, with limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots toward change.
