# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, normalization, and nonjudgment without shaming or labeling. The warmth is somewhat procedural, with fewer deeply individualized affirmations of Anisa’s strengths and resilience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures Anisa’s stress burden and fear of appearing like a “fraud” or “hypocrite.” Empathy could be stronger by more fully exploring the shame, stigma, and self-criticism in her language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation, stress triggers, and a small next step. It becomes somewhat mechanical in places, relying on repeated binary choices and logistics rather than a more strategic MI focus on ambivalence and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and adapts the plan to Anisa’s preference for something discreet and low-effort. The collaboration is not perfect because the counselor still drives much of the structure and content, and the transcript ends before final co-construction is complete.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong emphasis on Anisa’s choice, low pressure, and the idea that she can carry the lozenge without promising to use it. Mild directive moments, such as suggesting a three-day trial and asking when she will pick it up, slightly reduce the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some initial evocation of Anisa’s thoughts about quitting and what would make change workable. Much of the session, however, focuses on providing options and information rather than drawing out her deeper motivations, values, fears, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses supportive language such as “I’m here to support whatever you decide.” The interaction often shifts into an expert or educator role, limiting the sense that Anisa is the primary expert on her own change process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked early, and the psychiatrist elicits a small commitment to consider the lozenge. The session frequently moves into problem-solving and option selection, missing opportunities to deepen Anisa’s own reasons, hopes, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anisa’s fears of failure and feeling worse are handled without argument, with slips reframed as learning moments. The score is limited by repeated focus on failure scenarios, which can maintain a problem-centered frame rather than consistently redirecting toward values or self-compassion.
