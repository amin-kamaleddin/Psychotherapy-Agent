# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and nonjudgment, validating Mai’s confusion, privacy concerns, and preference for simplicity. The acceptance is somewhat task-focused, with a missed opportunity to fully normalize anxiety about the blood draw.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflections but often moves quickly into education and problem-solving. Greater depth would involve exploring Mai’s frustration, vulnerability in managing care alone, and fear of future blood draws.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on safe initiation, understanding, adherence, and safety planning for the new cholesterol medication. The trajectory is clear, practical, and goal-directed from confusion to a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and Mai collaboratively build a useful one-page fridge plan, with Mai’s preferences shaping the final checklist. Collaboration could be stronger by inviting Mai to generate more of the safety rationale before the clinician offers options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly asks permission, offers options, and respects Mai’s privacy and preferences. Autonomy support could be further strengthened by explicitly emphasizing Mai’s full decision-making authority over starting the medication, scheduling labs, and selecting an emergency contact.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Mai’s own statements, but often provides reasons and practical information instead of drawing out her values, confidence, or future goals. More MI-specific evocation would include exploring what protecting her heart means to her and her confidence in taking the medication nightly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting Mai’s preferences and allowing her routines to shape the plan. The partnership could be strengthened by explicitly conveying that Mai is the expert on her own life, values, and readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai expresses several change-talk statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen them through targeted questions about importance, ability, desire, or commitment. More systematic elicitation would help amplify and strengthen Mai’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Mai’s concerns and redirects them toward information and safety planning. The session would benefit from more MI-specific softening, such as double-sided reflections that acknowledge both safety and simplicity.
