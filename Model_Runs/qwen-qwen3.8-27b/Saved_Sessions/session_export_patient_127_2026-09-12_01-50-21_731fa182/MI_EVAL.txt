# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and nonjudgment while affirming progress and avoiding labels such as “lazy.” Acceptance is strong but sometimes generic rather than deeply individualized around Timur’s shame, record, and sense of exclusion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist understands Timur’s practical tension between quick money and stable work and reflects some of his concerns accurately. Empathy is mostly cognitive rather than emotionally deep, especially around fear, shame, and the meaning of coming home empty-handed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on reentry, relapse prevention, work engagement, and communication with Fiona. There is some drift into broad advice, but the discussion moves toward concrete behavior-level steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments, especially when Timur’s preferred wording for Fiona is accepted and the “binder” idea is dropped. Overall, the session remains unevenly expert-led, with the psychiatrist supplying many plans, scripts, and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and accepts Timur’s preferences, which supports autonomy in places. However, repeated directive plans and specific scripts can feel prescriptive and do not consistently emphasize Timur’s freedom to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful logistical questions but provides relatively few evocative questions to draw out Timur’s own values, motivations, and reasons for change. As a result, Timur’s internal change talk is not consistently elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and adapts to Timur’s contributions. However, the frequent expert-driven suggestions and detailed planning position the psychiatrist as problem-solver rather than fully equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces progress and some emerging change talk, such as trying a simpler text and considering site-hand work. He does not consistently deepen or summarize change talk into a stronger personal rationale for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Timur’s concerns about money, work, and the difficulty of his record. Some responses risk reinforcing high-risk sustain talk, such as encouraging a walk or calling a mate without fully addressing the associated triggers.
