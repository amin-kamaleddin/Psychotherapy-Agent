# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist never judges, labels, or minimizes the client's experiences. Normalizes vulnerability (e.g., aloneness) and reinforces worth without performance expectations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that goes beyond surface content to capture emotional and existential meaning. Complex reflections (e.g., 'the silence isn't just boring, it's where aloneness lives') demonstrate deep attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, coherent progression from values clarification to deeper self-awareness with well-calibrated pacing. Could have more explicitly connected the deeper exploration back to her core value of family happiness at the close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructive questions and building on the client's own language create a genuine exploratory partnership. Occasionally the psychiatrist takes a slightly more leading role in shaping the direction rather than using explicit mutual framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary throughout — the psychiatrist consistently reinforces the client's right to choose and never uses directive language. Statements like 'You don't owe anyone a plan for Sunday' explicitly remove external pressure while affirming agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's arc is entirely driven by the client's own evoked insights, with the psychiatrist serving as a facilitator of self-discovery. Questions are designed to draw out understanding rather than provide external reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on her own life and avoids giving advice. Could be strengthened with more explicit co-creation language (e.g., 'How would you like to proceed?') and slightly less guiding framing in the closing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk, amplifying the client's own insights and framing. Could have pushed slightly further on ability language (e.g., reflecting 'you already know a first step that feels doable').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles minimal sustain talk skillfully by holding ambivalence without collapsing it (e.g., 'Those two things can live at the same time'). A 5 would require more active strategic redirection, though the context limited opportunities.
