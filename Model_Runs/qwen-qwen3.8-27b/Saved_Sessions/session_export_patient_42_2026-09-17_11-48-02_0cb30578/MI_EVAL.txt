# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgmental respect throughout, with multiple affirmations embedded in reflections (e.g., "That sounds like a genuine social space"). Acceptance is strong but slightly functional, channeled primarily through curiosity rather than explicit statements of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening capturing both content and emotion, including subtle shifts like the fear in "a little scared?" and the distinction between "polite" versus "easy." Could have gone deeper on the social anxiety component of Elise's fear of "making it weird," which deserves more empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a coherent thematic arc from description to possibility to self-assessment, with each question building logically on the last. Lacks a clear closing or consolidation — the session ends with Elise still saying "I don't know what I'd actually do" without bridging toward even a small next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative throughout, with Elise treated as a co-investigator rather than a patient receiving treatment. The psychiatrist builds on Elise's own metaphors and language, offers reflections as hypotheses for her to confirm, and asks for her interpretation of her own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support — no directive language, no implicit coercion, and explicit validation that Elise's social attachment is legitimate. Every question is framed as an invitation, and the psychiatrist explicitly removes the expectation of change ("not to fix it, just to understand").

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All change-relevant insights emerge from Elise herself through open, curious questions — the psychiatrist never provides reasons for change or offers advice. Could have been pushed further in the final exchange to bridge from insight to intention (e.g., asking about the smallest possible step).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary partnership — the opening question cedes agenda-setting to the client, and the session is shaped entirely by Elise's contributions. High-level partnership moves like "What do you make of that?" and asking her to assess her own shift demonstrate a genuinely equal interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through strategic elicitation questions ("What would need to be true for that 'maybe' to become a 'yes'?"), helping Elise recognize her own shift. However, the session doesn't fully consolidate this into a confidence statement or commitment, leaving the ending slightly open.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively honors sustain talk without challenging it, using nuanced double-sided reflections (e.g., noting she said "I don't know if it IS dependent" rather than "it IS dependent"). Could have been more strategically active when Elise says she'd "just go with it" about yoga — a missed opportunity to explore what that costs her over time.
