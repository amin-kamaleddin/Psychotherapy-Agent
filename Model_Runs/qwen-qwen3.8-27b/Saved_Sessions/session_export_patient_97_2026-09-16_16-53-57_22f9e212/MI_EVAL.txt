# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, patience, and nonjudgmental understanding without moralizing or blaming. It is strong but somewhat structured and professional rather than deeply individualized or explicitly warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Mateo’s ambivalence, fear, and protective attachment to the crew. It could go deeper into the emotional core of identity, shame, isolation, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target concern of reducing reoffending risk through work, money, and staying away from the crew. It moves purposefully from a realistic next step to a concrete, manageable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Mateo’s ideas rather than prescribing solutions, and Mateo’s concerns directly shape the session. The interaction feels like a working partnership between two contributors.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressuring Mateo to cut off the crew and validates his own chosen low-key approach. It does not repeatedly and explicitly affirm his freedom to choose whether, when, or how much to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to draw out Mateo’s own reasons, plans, and self-monitoring signals. It becomes somewhat task-focused and could more deeply evoke values, identity, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a partner stance and invites Mateo’s perspective, allowing his ideas to shape the plan. The agenda remains fairly structured, so power sharing is strong but not fully balanced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk about wanting a real job, family, and a small step toward work. It builds toward a concrete test but does not explicitly amplify change talk with stronger confidence or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and reframes it as protective and understandable. It remains fairly focused on the sustain-talk concerns rather than fully pivoting toward strengthening the change side.
