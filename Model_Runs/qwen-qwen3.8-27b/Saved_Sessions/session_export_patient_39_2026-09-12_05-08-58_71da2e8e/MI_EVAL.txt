# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys warmth, respect, and nonjudgment. The final “report back” question slightly made the client feel evaluated, reducing acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture the client’s values and ambivalence effectively. The empathic response could go deeper into the emotional weight of feeling useless, limited, or guilty.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on increasing physical activity within realistic limits. The plan is practical, though it could more consistently connect to deeper values and medical goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is strongly co-constructed, with the client contributing key ideas such as the shoes-by-the-coffee-maker cue. Some clinician-led structure and the accountability question slightly reduce the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is generally well supported through invitations to choose and permission to stop if it hurts. Prescriptive suggestions and the final reporting-back question slightly weaken the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits the client’s own motivations, ideas, and confidence level. Evocation is weakened by occasional provision of options/external rationale and a less evocative, more evaluative final question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a collaborative stance by inviting the client to select options and contribute to the plan. Repeated summaries and the final accountability question make the relationship feel somewhat more coach-like or evaluative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as wanting to feel capable and not stop completely. There is less consistent amplification or reflection of the client’s own reasons, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is often met with understanding rather than argument, and the response about stopping is MI-consistent. The session remains somewhat barrier-focused, and the final question increased guilt and sustain talk without clear repair.
