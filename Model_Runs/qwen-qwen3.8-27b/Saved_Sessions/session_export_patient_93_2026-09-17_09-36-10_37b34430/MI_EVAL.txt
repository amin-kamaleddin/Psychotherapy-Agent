# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, explicitly countering stigma about drinking. There were no shaming, moralizing, or dismissive statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate, reflective listening to capture Margaret’s ambivalence, fear of monitoring, and desire to keep her pub life. Some reflections remained more cognitive than emotional, missing deeper shame, identity, and family-related pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of making drinking safer while preserving independence and pub life. The focus became somewhat repetitive later, and a concise summary could have re-anchored progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Margaret to co-create the plan rather than prescribing rules, and her ideas shaped the discussion. The process was still somewhat counselor-led in sequencing, with less explicit joint agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported Margaret’s freedom to choose by avoiding advice, pressure, or directive language. The plan was framed as self-directed and owned by Margaret rather than imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked Margaret’s own motivations through open, client-centered questions and allowed her to generate reasons for change. Evocation was strongest at the practical level but could have explored deeper values, independence, and future self more explicitly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and treated Margaret as the expert in her own life. Some guidance and affirming interpretations still positioned the psychiatrist as somewhat leading the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited change talk about reducing drinking and using safer self-checks. The change talk could have been more explicitly summarized and consolidated into a stronger internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk without arguing, lecturing, or pushing abstinence, reframing it as a need for autonomy and practicality. More deliberate double-sided reflections or discrepancy language could have further softened ambivalence.
