# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist was respectful, affirming, and nonjudgmental, validating progress and normalizing concerns. It falls short of 5 because warmth was tied somewhat to performance and plan adherence rather than being consistently unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Samuel's perspective, including worries about stress, coworker pressure, and delay habits. Deeper emotional exploration of ambivalence, fear of failure, or the weight of stress would have strengthened empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior of reducing smoking, especially around work and lunch. The therapist moved logically from progress review to a specific plan, backup strategy, and a concrete same-day setup step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Samuel's contributions shaped the session, including the drive strategy, keyboard cue, phrase, gum backup, and self-compassion response. The therapist built on Samuel's ideas rather than imposing a preselected plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently supported autonomy with preference-based questions, non-directive framing, and emphasis on Samuel's choice. Samuel's decisions drove the plan, with advice offered as options rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist evoked internal motivation by exploring strengths, past successes, confidence, and what would increase confidence. It falls short of 5 because reliance on option menus limited deeper elicitation of personal values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist maintained a collaborative stance by inviting Samuel to choose the focus and framing decisions as his. Frequent structured option menus made the interaction somewhat guided rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively elicited change talk through questions about strengths, confidence, and what would make the plan more doable. It could be stronger by more consistently amplifying importance, ability, need, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about stress, coworker pressure, and possible slips was normalized and redirected toward practical coping without argument. More double-sided reflections or explicit reframing of negative talk into change-oriented problem-solving would improve this criterion.
