# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with clear affirmations and no shaming. However, warmth sometimes feels procedural, and corrective framing like "not lazy" limits deeper individualized empathy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges practical barriers and all-or-nothing thinking but often stays at a factual level. The client's fear, dread, and self-doubt are not consistently reflected with complex, emotionally attuned reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear, concrete target behavior: increasing physical activity through a small realistic movement goal. The direction is effective but is often maintained through prescriptive planning rather than client-led exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone, and the psychiatrist adapts when the client rejects reminders, alarms, or rigid schedules. However, the psychiatrist still functions as the primary planner, supplying much of the structure and plan components.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, low-pressure options, and the client's preference to avoid alarms or rigid commitments. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated additions of tracking, rewards, cues, and check-in structures.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers many suggestions and plans rather than drawing out the client's own reasons, values, hopes, or confidence. As a result, the client's internal motivation remains underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and avoids a fully authoritarian stance. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently controls the structure, content, and progression of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms some emerging change talk but does not consistently deepen it with questions about reasons, benefits, values, or confidence. The client's change talk remains relatively thin and is not systematically developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds well to barriers and sustain talk without challenging, arguing, or reinforcing negative self-views. A stronger approach would include more strategic double-sided reflections or questions that explore the costs of staying the same.
