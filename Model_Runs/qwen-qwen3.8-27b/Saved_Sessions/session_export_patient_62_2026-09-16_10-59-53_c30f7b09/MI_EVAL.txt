# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard. They normalize Jordan's experiences and counter self-blame without dismissing his struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician provides strong, specific reflections of Jordan's feelings and concerns. However, the empathy is sometimes more cognitive than affective and could more deeply validate his ongoing helplessness and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on financial strain, employment instability, and the recurring job-loss pattern. The discussion moves purposefully from identifying urgent stressors to choosing and rehearsing a concrete, actionable step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and allows Jordan's choices to shape the next step. The interaction remains therapist-guided at times because the clinician frequently supplies structured options, scripts, and role-play directions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported throughout, with repeated invitations for Jordan to choose among options. The language avoids pressure and treats suggestions as options the client can accept or reject.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some effective early questions that invite reflection on goals and past coping. However, the session shifts toward problem-solving and skill rehearsal without consistently drawing out Jordan's deeper values, hopes, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan is clearly positioned as the expert on his own life and is invited to choose among options. The partnership would be stronger with more explicit power sharing and greater client-led co-creation of the next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes and reinforces Jordan's emerging motivation, especially around small, doable actions. The clinician could further amplify the client's own change talk by reflecting and expanding statements about wanting change or trying tomorrow.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by normalizing Jordan's self-critical and hopeless statements rather than arguing with them. The clinician could more skillfully soften the entrenched belief that 'I can't get a break' using deeper double-sided reflections.
