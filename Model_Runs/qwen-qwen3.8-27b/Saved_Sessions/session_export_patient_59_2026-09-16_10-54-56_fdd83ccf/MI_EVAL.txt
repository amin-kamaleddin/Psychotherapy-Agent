# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental, validating, and respectful, accepting Jamal’s impatience and minimization without shaming. However, the acceptance feels procedural rather than deeply warm or individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture Jamal’s stated goals and frustrations, but remain at a surface level. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as anxiety, unfairness, family pressure, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is organized and consistently focused on completing court-ordered requirements. However, it does not fully guide the conversation toward the broader MI goal of developing motivation to change marijuana use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and offers choices, showing partial collaboration. Much of the session is still driven by a preformed counselor plan rather than genuine co-creation with Jamal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are offered and refusals are accepted, supporting Jamal’s sense of autonomy. Repeated directive suggestions and a preset plan, however, make the stance more advisor-like than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions appear, but they are narrowly focused on administrative compliance rather than Jamal’s values, concerns, or reasons for changing marijuana use. The confidence ruler is used for compliance, not deeper change motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, collaborative language and offers options, demonstrating partial partnership. The power dynamic remains uneven because the counselor supplies much of the plan and structure rather than building it with Jamal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is minimal and mostly compliance-oriented, with little effort to evoke internal motivation to reduce or stop marijuana use. The psychiatrist does not effectively use Jamal’s values to explore benefits or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal’s repeated minimization and sustain talk about marijuana use are largely acknowledged and moved past without deeper engagement. There is no strategic rolling with or double-sided reflection to explore ambivalence and consequences.
