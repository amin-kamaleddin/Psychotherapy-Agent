# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations such as “Thanks for being direct” and “I hear you.” However, the warmth felt procedural, and repeated summaries/safety checks after the client said he was done limited the sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledged the surface frustration of being referred against his will, such as “that sounds frustrating and unwanted.” Deeper meanings—autonomy, identity, health fear, and marriage significance—were not sufficiently reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began broadly and then drifted into safety checks, coping tips, marital scripts, and boundary-setting. There was not a consistent strategic focus on Richard’s ambivalence about smoking or his personal motivation to change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some collaborative language and offered choices, but the agenda remained counselor-led through experiments, scripts, and practical steps. Richard’s expertise about his routine and marriage was not fully used to shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor included autonomy-supportive statements like “I won’t push” and “You’re in charge of this.” However, continued directives and advice after Richard explicitly rejected them undermined true autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly provided solutions rather than evoking Richard’s own reasons, values, or motivation to change. Key opportunities, such as his request for something that makes him want to quit “for me” and his father’s lung cancer death, were not explored deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership language was present, but the overall stance was expert-driven and plan-focused. The counselor did not fully follow Richard’s agenda or co-create next steps after he repeatedly said he was done.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Richard offered meaningful change talk around “no smoking in the house” and a “weekly check-in,” which was affirmed. The counselor did not expand this into deeper client-based motivation, values, confidence, or expected benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing but did not effectively soften Richard’s sustain talk with double-sided reflections or gentle redirection. Responses often reinforced problem-solving around his wife’s nagging rather than exploring his ambivalence.
