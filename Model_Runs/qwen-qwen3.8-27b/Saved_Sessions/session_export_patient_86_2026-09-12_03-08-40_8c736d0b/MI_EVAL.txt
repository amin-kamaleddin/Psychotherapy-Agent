# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of the client's choices. Warmth is strong but sometimes feels procedural or administrative rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects annoyance, fatigue, and skepticism about quitting. However, empathy remains fairly surface-level and does not fully engage deeper ambivalence or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking-related dental harm reduction and a one-week experiment. Focus is generally useful, though introducing multiple options before exploring the client's perspective reduces strategic efficiency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as an equal partner in choosing the session focus and plan. The client's constraints substantially shape the session, including no logs, no check-ins, and a brief future agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated affirmations of choice, ownership, and freedom from pressure. The clinician avoids imposing monitoring, lectures, or mandatory follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some useful open questions about preferences and next-visit focus. However, the session is more advice- and plan-driven than deeply evocative of the client's own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites client input and allows the client to choose the session direction. The strong collaboration is slightly undercut by the expert-guided structure of options and summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some practical change talk about trying rinsing, gum, and an outside-only rule. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk with questions that expand desire, ability, reasons, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids confrontation and responds to resistance with low-effort options and normalization of slips. However, the session does not actively use double-sided reflections or strategic exploration to soften sustain talk.
