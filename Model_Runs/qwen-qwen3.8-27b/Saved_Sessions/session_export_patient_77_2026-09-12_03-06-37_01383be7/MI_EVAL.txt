# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, offering reassurance that missing a day is okay and consistency matters. Acceptance is somewhat task-focused and does not fully affirm the client’s worth independent of weight or plan success.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledge frustration, effort, low confidence, and barriers, but often remain at the behavioral surface. Deeper feelings of shame, loneliness, and fear of continuing to feel stuck are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting concern and moves from distress to a small, workable weekly plan. Pacing occasionally bypasses deeper ambivalence and values before moving into concrete behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a collaborative, non-prescriptive tone and the client values being included in planning. Collaboration is somewhat scaffolded by therapist-generated options rather than fully client-led agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission-based language, choices, and permission to simplify or miss a day strongly support autonomy. Slight limits remain because the therapist still provides a structured menu of solutions and some rationale.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions invite goals, confidence, and ease, but the therapist sometimes substitutes their own explanations for eliciting client change talk. Deeper evocation of values and reasons for change would strengthen the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influences choices such as snack, jeans check, walk fallback, and reminder wording. Still, the therapist generates much of the agenda and solution structure, limiting a fully client-led partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and questions elicit some client-generated ideas such as checking jeans, eating a protein bar, and trying a walk. However, the therapist frequently supplies options and plans rather than amplifying the client’s own reasons, desires, and abilities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist normalizes fear of failure and does not reinforce sustain talk aggressively. Redirection often uses rationale or reframing rather than MI-consistent reflections that help the client resolve ambivalence.
