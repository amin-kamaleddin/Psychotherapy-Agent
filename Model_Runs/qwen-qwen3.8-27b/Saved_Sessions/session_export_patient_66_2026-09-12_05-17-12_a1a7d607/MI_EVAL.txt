# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone and clearly supports the client's autonomy and right to choose. However, the acceptance is somewhat generic and transactional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects key ideas and frustration, but the reflections remain largely surface-level and factual. There is limited exploration of deeper emotions, values, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and progresses logically to a concrete, practical plan. The direction is clear, though somewhat narrow and repetitive in confidence ratings.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and involves them in selecting plan components. However, much of the content and options are counselor-generated, limiting genuine co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, low pressure, and non-commitment. Autonomy support is strong, though could be strengthened by more explicit affirmation of client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical plans, barriers, and confidence levels effectively. However, the evocation is limited to practical planning rather than deeper motivational exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is used, but the psychiatrist often provides menus of options and strategies. The interaction leans somewhat expert-driven rather than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is present but narrow and mostly behavioral. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or deepen the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against the client's smoking preference and does not reinforce sustain talk. However, the sustain talk is not actively explored with MI techniques such as double-sided reflections.
