# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone and skillfully counters self-doubt without dismissing. Affirmations could be more deeply personalized to Alessia's specific identity, values, and circumstances to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate capture of ambivalence and relational dynamics. Reflections sometimes land on the cognitive level rather than fully accessing the emotional undercurrent (e.g., the pharmacist's dismissive look, the recurring anxiety of opening the bottle).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook direction maintenance with a logical, momentum-driven progression from clarification through to a concrete, actionable plan. No drift; every step builds on the previous one toward a specific outcome.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing with shared problem-solving language and consistent invitation of client input. The psychiatrist still does the heavier lifting in framing and guiding rather than truly sharing the cognitive work equally (e.g., co-constructing the call script).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's right to choose and never pressures for immediate action. One mild directive ('I'd encourage you to reach out as soon as you can') slightly shifts toward advice-giving; a purely evocative framing would strengthen this further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Excellent evocation — the client generates her own plan, strategy, and specific questions rather than being told what to do. Could be pushed further with confidence/ability elicitation (e.g., a readiness ruler) and more explicit amplification of change talk to build additional momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance, honestly acknowledges own limits, and credits the client's strategies. Could be deeper by more explicitly inviting co-construction of solutions (e.g., 'Would it help if we worked together on what you might say?').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk, probing deeper into commitment language and reflecting back the client's own words to strengthen them. Could have more consistently summarized and amplified change talk, and more explicitly elicited ability statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles self-doubt with double-sided reflections and gentle reframing ('That's not overreacting — that's being thorough') without confrontation. Sustain talk was relatively mild in this session; more explicit normalization of the self-doubt would have added depth.
