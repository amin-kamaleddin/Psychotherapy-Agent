# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Mateo’s pain, fatigue, and limits. The tone was generally accepting but sometimes more procedural than deeply warm or affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and supportive, especially around ambivalence and frustration with body limits. Empathy was limited by a tendency to move quickly into advice without fully exploring emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed generally relevant to pain, movement, and rehab, but the focus drifted across many topics. There was no clear agenda-setting or consolidation around a specific activity or rehab goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered choices, showing some collaboration. However, the interaction remained largely expert-driven, with many techniques introduced rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly honored Mateo’s ability to stop, choose, and limit activity without pressure. Autonomy support was slightly weakened by the amount of prescriptive advice and routine suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relied more on external explanations and strategies than on eliciting Mateo’s own reasons for change. There were few questions that explored values, confidence, hope, or motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist recognized Mateo’s expertise about his body and offered some options. The session, however, felt more clinician-led than a balanced, co-created partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo offered some change-oriented statements, such as recognizing that moving helps and openness to trying shoulder rolls later. These opportunities were not consistently noticed, amplified, or built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Mateo’s concerns rather than arguing or dismissing them. There was limited use of double-sided reflection or strategic reframing to move sustain talk toward change.
