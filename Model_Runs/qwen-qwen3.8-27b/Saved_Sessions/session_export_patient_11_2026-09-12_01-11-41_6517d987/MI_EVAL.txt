# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and supportive, but responses were more task-oriented than deeply person-centered. Acceptance was present but not consistently warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was limited and mostly factual, with little exploration of Amira’s emotions, ambivalence, or deeper concerns. Empathic understanding of her internal experience was weak.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on physical activity and moved toward a clear, actionable plan. Direction was strong, though more behavioral than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira’s preferences were repeatedly incorporated into the final plan, showing a collaborative stance. However, the psychiatrist remained the primary source of the exercise strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was meaningful support for choice, flexibility, and self-regulation. Autonomy support was strong but occasionally reduced by prescriptive language and rule-setting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relied more on advice and explanations than on drawing out Amira’s own reasons and motivations for change. Client-generated change talk was not consistently elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction showed some shared decision-making, but the psychiatrist still held a dominant expert role. True partnership was present at a moderate level rather than a fully equal one.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were few attempts to elicit or amplify Amira’s own desires, values, or confidence in change. The psychiatrist mostly supplied the plan rather than strengthening Amira’s internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reduced pressure around failure and offered flexible, low-pressure options. Softening was practical but not consistently MI-based or reflective of ambivalence.
