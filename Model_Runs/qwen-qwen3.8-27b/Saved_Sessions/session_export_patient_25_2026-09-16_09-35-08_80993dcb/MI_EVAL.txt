# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment with frequent, specific affirmations. Praise is occasionally achievement-focused and repeated, but unconditional positive regard remains strong.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers good reflective listening, accurately capturing pride, annoyance, and concern about staying present for his child. Deeper emotions such as fear, guilt, loss of control, or social identity tied to drinking are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays strategically focused on alcohol use in social situations, moving from values to a concrete plan, rehearsal, and confidence checking. Direction is clear, though the therapist sometimes directs the process with provided options and scripts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the client’s ideas meaningfully shaping the plan, including the cue text, exit time, and leave-line. The therapist controls much of the agenda and introduces multiple strategies, so collaboration is strong but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice and supports the client’s final decisions about the text, leave-line, exit time, and backup plan. The frequent provision of specific scripts and plans can feel mildly prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions, confidence scaling, and reflection to draw out the client’s reasons, confidence, and commitment. However, the therapist often supplies options and scripts before the client fully generates his own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on his own life and incorporates the client’s preferences into the plan. The frequent offering of multiple suggestions and exact wording gives the session a somewhat expert-driven quality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively encourages desire, need, and ability statements through questions about goals, past success, and confidence. Change talk is strong but could be more client-generated before the therapist offers options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is mild, and the psychiatrist responds by redirecting toward practical, low-pressure strategies rather than arguing. More double-sided reflections could better explore the social pull of drinking alongside change motivation.
