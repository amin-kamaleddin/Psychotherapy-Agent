# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates Nadine’s feelings without disapproval. However, acceptance is diluted by frequent shifts into reassurance, safety monitoring, and practical advice before fully sitting with her distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some valid reflections of exhaustion, guilt, and resentment, showing reasonable understanding. Empathy remains mostly surface-level and often moves quickly from emotion to advice or medical reassurance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and helpful focus on infant sleep, feeding, exhaustion, and safety. The main limitation is that the conversation does not consistently move toward a clearly defined, client-owned change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of collaboration, especially when offering text options and asking for Nadine’s input. The overall style remains more expert-driven, with frequent recommendations that reduce equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice at times by offering options and respecting Nadine’s concerns. The approach is still fairly directive, relying heavily on specific instructions and problem-solving rather than emphasizing her own decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supplies much of the motivational content rather than drawing out Nadine’s own values, reasons, and capacities for change. Key emotional statements are not consistently reflected in ways that would elicit her own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and offers some choices, showing partial collaboration. The session remains uneven in power balance because of the psychiatrist’s expert-driven guidance and frequent practical advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nadine does express some willingness to try suggestions, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or strengthens this change talk. The therapist often responds by adding more options or instructions rather than expanding the client’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and sometimes reduces its intensity through validation and normalization. However, the session does not consistently use more advanced MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to resolve ambivalence.
