# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, affirming progress, and nonjudgmental toward urges or lapses. It falls short of 5 because the warmth is somewhat formulaic and tied to visible success rather than explicitly unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects ambivalence and practical concerns accurately, especially around stress and whether new strategies will hold up. It remains mostly intellectual and does not consistently capture the deeper emotional meaning of fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on reducing smoking in high-risk work contexts. It moves efficiently from urges to coping, if-then planning, confidence, tracking, and follow-up indicators with little drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction uses joint language and builds on Samuel's ideas, with his preferences clearly shaping the final plan. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still supplies many concrete options before or alongside Samuel's own problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices, reduces pressure, and respects Samuel's decisions about timing and disclosure. It is not a 5 because it does not consistently emphasize that Samuel is fully in charge and can change or abandon any step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful evocative questions are used about what has helped, what is doable, and confidence. However, many external suggestions shift the session away from consistently drawing out Samuel's deeper intrinsic motivation, values, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samuel's choices shape the plan, but the psychiatrist often assumes the expert role by presenting menus of options. More client-led idea generation and stronger emphasis on Samuel as the expert on his own life would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samuel offers clear change talk about goals, walking, if-then planning, and tracking, and the psychiatrist elicits some of this. The change talk is present but not deeply developed because the session often shifts to suggestive strategies rather than amplifying Samuel's own reasons and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist manages doubt without arguing, correcting, or minimizing, and uses noncombative statements such as 'Trying is progress, not failure.' Softening is limited because the response often moves quickly into problem-solving rather than using double-sided reflections or exploring the cost of staying the same.
