# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and offers appropriate affirmations, but the acceptance is undercut by repeatedly providing intervention menus after the client rejects them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable reflections of the client's stress and exhaustion, but remains somewhat surface-level and misses deeper emotional themes regarding her isolation and fear of losing her identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session maintains only a loose connection to the target behavior, frequently drifting into general stress management and practical support rather than a consistent MI focus on smoking cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through asking for client input, but the psychiatrist still controls much of the agenda by repeatedly presenting menus of interventions rather than co-constructing with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides helpful autonomy support by validating the client's pace, but inconsistently fails to fully honor her preference to just talk by continuing to offer new options after repeated rejections.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited attempts to evoke internal motivation, shifting mostly to advice and coping tools rather than using deeper questions to explore the client's values and hopes regarding quitting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite some invitations for input, the interaction remains largely expert-driven because the psychiatrist repeatedly supplies practical recommendations and carries the session's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fails to consistently reflect or amplify the client's meaningful change talk, instead shifting to external options that limit the client's role in generating her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** While the psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk, she fails to soften or redirect it, repeatedly offering new strategies that inadvertently reinforce her reasons against change.
