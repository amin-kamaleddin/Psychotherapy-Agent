# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, validating language and accepts skepticism without correction. Acceptance is slightly limited by attaching praise to actions rather than expressing unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture the client's frustration and exhaustion while contextualizing financial and work pressures. Deeper emotional themes such as shame, identity loss, or helplessness are less explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete employment goal and narrows effectively into a specific micro-step. Direction is strong but remains somewhat task-focused rather than linking the step to broader values or goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and checks preferences, but frequently supplies the substantive resume content and plan. The interaction feels more expert-driven than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options are presented, and the psychiatrist respects the client's decision to complete the step independently. Autonomy support is strong, though less evident when the psychiatrist controls how the task is framed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling helps the client express and track motivation over time. Deeper personal reasons for change are not fully drawn out, and much motivational content is supplied by the psychiatrist.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but the psychiatrist often takes on an expert role by drafting content and structuring the plan. This reduces the client's role as a co-creator of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some confidence and commitment statements through scaling and micro-steps. Change talk remains narrow and task-bound rather than exploring desire, need, or importance of change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and redirected toward small actions. More nuanced MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance are not consistently used.
