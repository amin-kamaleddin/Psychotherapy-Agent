### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5

- [x] Client's ownership and right to choice reinforced
- [x] Explicit protection against rule-based thinking
- [x] Pressure removed at decision points
- [x] Final plan is entirely client-generated
- [x] Autonomy woven into fabric of every interaction

**Comments:** Autonomy support is pervasive and structural. Key examples: "I'm not asking you to cook every night — that could turn into another rule," "You don't have to have an answer," "You don't have to pick one or make it big." The session is structured around Luka's own language about choice vs. control. The final plan is entirely Luka's idea, offered as a question, not a suggestion. No pressure or implication that Luka "should" do anything.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5

- [x] No external reasons or consequences imposed
- [x] Internal vision, values, and self-efficacy evoked
- [x] Motivation emerges organically from client
- [x] Open and evocative questions used consistently
- [x] Conditions created for self-motivation to surface

**Comments:** The psychiatrist never states "you should quit drinking" or provides external consequences. Evocation is achieved through questions like "What would 'more than okay' look like?" (internal vision), "What's the version where it feels like yours?" (internal values), "What do you think made Tuesday different?" (internal self-efficacy). Luka's motivation surfaces organically: "it was mine," "maybe it's not about willpower," "I put the shoes by the door that morning." This is the gold standard of MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5

- [x] Client sets the agenda
- [x] Client generates goals, insights, and solutions
- [x] Psychiatrist positioned as curious collaborator, not expert
- [x] No imposition of plans or directives
- [x] Session entirely shaped by client contributions

**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that expertise about Luka's life resides with Luka. The client sets the agenda ("What feels most present for you?"), generates the goal ("just easy"), defines the problem (rule vs. choice), analyzes his own experience (the "gap"), and generates the solution (walking, shoes by the door). The psychiatrist's role is to ask, reflect, and gently redirect — never to direct.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5

- [x] Change talk elicited consistently throughout
- [x] Multiple types of change talk evoked (desire, ability, values, past success)
- [x] Change talk reflected back and amplified
- [x] Progressive deepening of client's own motivation
- [x] Client produces rich, specific change talk

**Comments:** Skillful and consistent evocation of change talk at every turn. Techniques include: "What would 'more than okay' look like?" (desire/imagery), "What's one evening this week where you'd be proud?" (past success/self-efficacy), "What do you think made that Tuesday different?" (ability/self-awareness). Luka produces rich change talk: "it was mine," "I don't think about it the next morning," "maybe it's not about willpower." The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies these, connecting them to earlier statements.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5

- [x] Sustain talk validated before redirecting
- [x] Never argued with or dismissed sustain talk
- [x] Reframed from powerlessness to curiosity
- [x] Each instance handled gracefully
- [x] Underlying concern honored in every pivot

**Comments:** Sustain talk appears multiple times and is handled masterfully at each occurrence. Examples: "I don't know if I get there" → "You can feel far from something AND be genuinely moving toward it. Those aren't contradictions." "I'll just bounce back" → "I get why the bounce-back worries you. When you've been through a cycle before, of course you'd worry about it." "I can't just decide" → "So maybe the question isn't 'how do I control it' but… what was true about Tuesday?" The psychiatrist never reinforces or argues with sustain talk; consistently pivots while honoring the underlying concern. Textbook softening.

---

### **Overall Score: 5.0 / 5**

**Summary:** This is an exceptional demonstration of Motivational Interviewing. The psychiatrist maintains a consistent, skilled MI stance across all nine criteria without any notable lapses. The session flows naturally, the client generates rich change talk and a concrete action plan, sustain talk is handled gracefully at every appearance, and the overall arc moves from exploration to a client-owned next step. The integration of all MI processes is seamless and clinically sophisticated. This transcript could serve as a teaching model for MI training.
