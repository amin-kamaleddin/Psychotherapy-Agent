# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong nonjudgmental respect and positive regard throughout, consistently normalizing Farid's experience. No criticism or pressure was evident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Farid's ambivalence and validated both sides of his conflict. Deeper exploration of underlying emotions, such as guilt or fear of failure, would elevate the response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The focus narrowed effectively from a vague exercise goal to a concrete 4 PM movement experiment. The session would benefit from a clearer summary or collaboratively defined next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was collaborative, with Farid's ideas shaping the direction. Some therapist-introduced conceptual frames made parts of the conversation feel slightly less co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Action was repeatedly framed as Farid's choice rather than obligation. The psychiatrist avoided advice-giving, pressure, or implicit coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided giving reasons for change and elicited Farid's own ideas, including a self-relevant body cue. More deliberate use of MI-specific evocation tools, such as importance/confidence rulers, would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Farid was consistently treated as the expert on what feels useful and doable. The psychiatrist did not lecture, prescribe, or assume the expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful open questions elicited several change statements, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen them. More reflective reinforcement and readiness/confidence follow-up would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and reframed in a shame-reducing way. The session would improve by more consistently pivoting away from barriers and linking doubts to change.
