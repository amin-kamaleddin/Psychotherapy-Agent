# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using several affirming statements. However, acceptance feels somewhat task-oriented and performance-based rather than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few empathic statements, but the reflections are mostly factual or behavioral. Important emotional content, such as fear of failure and feelings of 'cheating,' is not deeply explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a strong, consistent focus on increasing physical activity and building a concrete plan. This is one of the clearest and strongest areas of the interview.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some input and incorporates Dante's preferences into the plan. However, most of the structure is clinician-driven, making the interaction feel more like guided advice than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy is supported through questions and acceptance of Dante's choices. Overall, the language is prescriptive and commitment-focused, which reduces the sense of autonomous choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides more advice and problem-solving than eliciting Dante's own motivations. There is limited exploration of values, past successes, importance, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, and Dante contributes specific details. Still, the psychiatrist frequently directs the conversation with recommendations rather than fully positioning Dante as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dante offers some change talk, such as past success and willingness to try specific nights. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this change talk to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or amplifying sustain talk, which is helpful. However, responses often shift into advice or planning rather than MI-consistent reflections that gently pivot toward change.
