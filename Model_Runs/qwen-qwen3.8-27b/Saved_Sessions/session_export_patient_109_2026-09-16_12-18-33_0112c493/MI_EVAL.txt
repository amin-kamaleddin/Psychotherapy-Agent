# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows steady respect, patience, and non-judgment while accepting Rafael's ambivalence and limited confidence. It does not reach 5 because warmth is often tied to the practical plan rather than explicitly affirming Rafael's worth independent of progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects surface themes such as work fatigue and the gym feeling overwhelming. Deeper emotional ambivalence, self-doubt, and fear of failure could have been explored more consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of returning to a small physical activity routine. Progress moves logically from barriers to confidence, a concrete first step, and relapse planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rafael's ideas strongly shape the final plan, including the timing, location, and cue-based structure. The collaboration could be stronger by more consistently inviting client-generated options before offering multiple choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and respects Rafael's limited commitment by reducing the goal to a manageable first step. It falls short of 5 due to moments that assume commitment, such as summarizing the plan as already decided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence and practical motivation are evoked effectively through scaling and small-step prompts. Deeper client-owned values, desires, and reasons for change were not explored consistently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a collaborative stance and treats Rafael as an active contributor to the plan. The stance could be more fully partner-like by reducing therapist-generated menus and using more explicit permission-seeking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rafael expresses increasing confidence and some change-oriented language across the session. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify and explore Rafael's own change talk rather than steering through specific options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and gently reframed into smaller, lower-pressure actions. The main limitation is that the session often moves quickly into problem-solving before exploring the sustain talk more deeply.
