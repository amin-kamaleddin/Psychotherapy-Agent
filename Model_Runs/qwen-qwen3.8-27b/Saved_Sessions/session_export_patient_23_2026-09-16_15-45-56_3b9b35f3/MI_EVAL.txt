# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental respect, warmth, and unconditional positive regard throughout; normalizes Claudia's ambivalence and reduces shame. Minor note: could be further elevated by explicitly affirming Claudia's worth independent of her drinking choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures ambivalence and emotional texture on both sides. Some reflections are slightly counselor-constructed (e.g., 'letting the light end it for you'); could explore more underlying feelings like fear or self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, focused progression from ambivalence to concrete options, a first step, and a plan for tired nights with little topic drift. Could have explored deeper consequences or values associated with continued drinking before moving into implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative in practice with Claudia's ideas consistently shaping the work and plan. Slightly therapist-guided through leading/funnel questions; could involve more visible co-creation by inviting Claudia to redirect the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** One of the strongest areas; consistently supports Claudia's freedom to choose with no pressure toward clinical guidelines. Normalizes her sense of permission ('stopping isn't losing the evening') without implying failure for not meeting stricter standards.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws out Claudia's own motivations, preferences, and strategies with evocative, client-centered questions. Some questions are slightly leading or impose counselor-constructed metaphors; more open-ended evocation would strengthen further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys Claudia as the expert on her own evenings and the work is collaborative ('what would be useful for us to look at together'). Still guides the conversation sequence with some structured options; could signal more explicitly that no preferred plan exists beyond Claudia's.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently evokes and affirms change talk through questions about small doable changes and preferred first steps. Could more explicitly reflect and expand the meaning of Claudia's change statements for fuller amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk reasonably by acknowledging both sides without reinforcing (e.g., reframe of 'organizing better'). Could make more consistent skillful pivots when Claudia states 'the wine makes it stop' or 'the quiet is really loud,' perhaps with stronger double-sided reflections.
