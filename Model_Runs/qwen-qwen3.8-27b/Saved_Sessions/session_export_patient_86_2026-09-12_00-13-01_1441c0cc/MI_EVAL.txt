# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or lecturing about smoking. Warmth is solid but somewhat procedural, with repetitive safety language that slightly reduces personal attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects tiredness, soreness, and frustration, but does not fully capture the deeper emotional meaning of Marco's repeated health crises. Empathy is present but not consistently deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts away from the smoking-cessation target into repetitive dental pain management and safety warnings. There is little sustained focus on linking dental concerns to motivation for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and invites input early in the session, showing some willingness to collaborate. However, the interaction becomes uneven when the psychiatrist repeatedly gives instructions rather than co-creating a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Marco's boundary against lecturing and avoids pressuring him to quit. Repeated directive advice and safety warnings somewhat dilute the noncontrolling stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is little effort to draw out Marco's own values, motivations, or reasons for change regarding smoking. The session relies more on logistical questions and advice than on evoking internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially invites Marco's input and offers choices, showing some partnership. After that, the interaction becomes more expert-directed, with less emphasis on Marco as the decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little effort to elicit reasons, values, confidence, or readiness related to smoking cessation. Marco's positive statements focus on pain management, not on smoking-related change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and does not amplify Marco's resistance. However, there is no strategic softening, reframing, or reflection that helps him explore ambivalence.
