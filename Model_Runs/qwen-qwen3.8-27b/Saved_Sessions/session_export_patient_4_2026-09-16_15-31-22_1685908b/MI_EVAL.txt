# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, validating Aizada’s need for certainty after four years of symptoms. Acceptance could be deepened by more individualized positive regard, such as explicitly normalizing her fear of cancer and affirming her beyond her symptoms.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture Aizada’s core concerns, including her distrust of stress explanations and the meaningfulness of the wedding detail. However, some emotionally important material, such as her isolation, prolonged frustration, and cancer fear, is not fully explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains generally relevant to Aizada’s symptoms and need for medical investigation, but the MI target behavior is not clearly defined or consistently pursued. After the referral is chosen, the session becomes more procedural, reducing MI momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner, co-constructing the plan and directly incorporating Aizada’s preferred language into the referral. Aizada’s ideas clearly shape the session’s direction and outcome, reflecting strong mutual partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Aizada’s freedom to choose, avoids pressuring her to accept a psychological explanation, and respects her decision to prioritize medical investigation. There is no coercive language or attempt to override her preference, and her ownership of the process is well maintained.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions and reflections to elicit Aizada’s priorities, but does not consistently draw out or develop her internal motivation for change. Key moments, such as the wedding example, are acknowledged but not further explored to deepen change motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and frequently invites Aizada’s input, with her contributions directly shaping the referral. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly positioning Aizada as the expert on her own experience and allowing greater agenda negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some effort to create room for change talk, and Aizada offers meaningful statements about the wedding and possible stress connection. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or build on these statements to develop stronger change motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Aizada’s sustain talk and uses reflections to reduce confrontation. However, there is limited use of skilled MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections, to soften her need for tests and reconcile it with emerging stress-related insight.
