# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Anika’s practical goals around basketball. It stops short of a 5 because the acceptance is more performance-based than deeply individualized or unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and tracks Anika’s ambivalence and priorities well. Empathy is somewhat cognitive/behavioral rather than emotionally deep, especially around embarrassment, shame, or stress triggers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing or pausing vaping to protect basketball eligibility and performance. The psychiatrist consistently links the conversation to concrete, relevant concerns and change steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the conversation as joint problem-solving and actively invites Anika’s input. Anika’s choices clearly shape the plan, making the interaction feel collaborative rather than advisory.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Anika’s autonomy by offering choices and respecting her boundaries. It falls just short of a 5 because the psychiatrist occasionally steers toward specific targets or options rather than consistently positioning Anika as the final decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Anika’s own motivations, ideas, and confidence. Evocation is occasionally undercut by frequent suggestions or structured options, limiting deeper exploration of identity, values, or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Anika as the expert on her own choices and repeatedly invites her input. At times the psychiatrist adopts a more advisory/expert stance by offering structured options before fully opening space for Anika’s independent ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicits and reinforces Anika’s change talk around ability, reasons, and concrete plans. It is strong but not a 5 because the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen the change talk by exploring broader values, identity, or future self.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing, moralizing, or reinforcing reasons for continued use. Softening is not perfect because some questions and suggestions could keep the option of continued use more prominent rather than more fully pivoting to Anika’s goals.
