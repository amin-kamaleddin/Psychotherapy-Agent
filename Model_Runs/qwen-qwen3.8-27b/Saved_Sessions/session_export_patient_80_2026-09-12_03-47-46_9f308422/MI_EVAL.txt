# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client’s fear and strengths. The session became somewhat task-focused, which slightly limited deeper acceptance of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate and supportive reflections of the client’s fear and concerns. Deeper emotional themes such as shame, resentment, and relational meaning were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol-related anger and safety planning. A stronger closing summary and more consistent arc toward a client-owned goal would improve coherence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked collaboratively, with the client contributing meaningfully to the final plan. However, the therapist often supplied the options, making the collaboration partly therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly supported choice and respected the client’s boundaries. Rapidly offering many options slightly reduced the space for client-led decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions helped elicit motivation, confidence, and barriers. The psychiatrist sometimes moved quickly into plans and scripts before fully exploring the client’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was a collaborative tone, but the psychiatrist frequently took a planner/coach role. More client-led direction and fewer therapist-generated interventions would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered useful change talk, and the psychiatrist evoked some importance and commitment. However, this change talk was not consistently amplified or expanded into a stronger rationale for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not push abstinence and accepted the client’s current readiness. More active double-sided reflections or exploration of ambivalence would strengthen this criterion.
