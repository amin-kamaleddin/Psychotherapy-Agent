# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided arguing with Marek’s minimization. The acceptance was strong but somewhat procedural, with fewer affirmations of Marek’s strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Marek’s confusion, defensiveness, surprise, and concern about losing his weekends. However, the empathy remained somewhat surface-level and did not deeply explore what weekends mean to him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use, liver risk, and Marek’s decision about change. The focus was clear but became overly data-focused, limiting forward movement toward a collaborative next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and generally honored Marek’s preference to see the labs first. The collaboration was undermined by repeated medical-expert language and directive lab instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized Marek’s right to choose and did not pressure immediate change. Repeated instructions to obtain specific lab values and reference ranges reduced the autonomy-supportive tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open questions invited Marek to share what mattered to him, but the session quickly shifted to factual and medical content. The psychiatrist did not consistently elicit or develop Marek’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supported a collaborative stance by inviting Marek’s input and following his agenda. The medical-expert role, especially around lab definitions and interpretation, reduced the sense of partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marek produced meaningful change talk, such as wanting to understand the risk and reconsidering if the liver was affected. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided challenging Marek’s sustain talk and respected his resistance. However, the responses were not consistently strategic in pivoting resistance toward change or using double-sided reflections.
