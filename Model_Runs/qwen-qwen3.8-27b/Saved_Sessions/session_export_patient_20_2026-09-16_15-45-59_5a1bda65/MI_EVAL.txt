# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance and does not argue with the client's use of alcohol. Minor early inferences about the client's feelings slightly reduce the accuracy of the acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflections are accurate and capture the function of the alcohol ritual, especially in the middle of the session. Empathy is inconsistent because early reflections are premature and the therapist often moves too quickly from feeling to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation remains focused on the target behavior of alcohol use and its consequences. It is somewhat diffuse because the therapist repeatedly shifts among several possible micro-solutions without fully consolidating around one client-identified target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and co-constructs some options, creating a collaborative feel. Collaboration is limited because the agenda and framing remain partly therapist-directed around reduction, transition, and small changes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through language about the client being the expert and choosing their pace. Many questions still imply that some change or adjustment is the expected next step, making autonomy support uneven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist elicits some client-generated ideas about the function of alcohol and the value of mornings. Evocation is limited because the therapist does not consistently deepen values, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses partnership language and avoids presenting himself as the sole authority. Partnership is uneven because questions frequently steer toward change planning before the client has fully chosen the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk about mornings, relationships, and possibly reducing drinks. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, reinforce, or expand that change talk, often responding with solution-focused questions instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without disapproval and sometimes held in double-sided reflection. The therapist does not consistently soften the hold of sustain talk or redirect the conversation toward the larger costs and meaning of maintaining the current routine.
