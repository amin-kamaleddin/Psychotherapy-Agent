# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client’s right to set boundaries. The frequent directive coaching and script-giving slightly reduces the purity of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client’s ambivalence well but remains somewhat surface-level. Deeper self-criticism and fear of being perceived negatively are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session has excellent momentum toward a concrete, targeted outcome. It progresses logically from exploring difficulty to role-play, drafting, and finally sending the message.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively in the later stage by offering options and incorporating the client’s feedback. The final text reflects the client’s voice and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and affirms the client’s right to choose when to act. However, repeatedly providing exact scripts may subtly undermine the client’s own autonomy and voice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions but rarely evokes the client’s internal motivation. The focus on external scripts and practical guidance limits deeper elicitation of change reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input at key points but often assumes an expert role. This reduces the sense of equal partnership and shifts the session toward guidance rather than collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client naturally generates meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist briefly affirms it. However, the psychiatrist often bypasses opportunities to deepen or expand this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges guilt and fear but quickly neutralizes them rather than exploring the ambivalence. This approach does not effectively soften sustain talk or help the client process it.
