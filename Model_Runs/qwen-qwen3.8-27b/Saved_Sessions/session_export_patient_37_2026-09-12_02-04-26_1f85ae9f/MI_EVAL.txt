# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming language, including normalization and brief affirmations. However, the acceptance is sometimes formulaic and can feel less individually warm due to the information-heavy delivery.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of the patient’s uncertainty, caution, and conflict about vaccination. The main limitation is that some emotionally important concerns, such as fear of harm and worry about being judged, are not reflected with deeper nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior of addressing vaccine hesitancy and moving toward a concrete next step. The progression from concern to sources, questions, script, and plan is clear and purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative style by offering options, co-creating questions and scripts, and respecting the patient’s preferences. It is not a 5 because much of the content is still generated by the clinician rather than primarily by the patient’s own language and ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice, avoids pressure, and frames information and support as optional. The patient is allowed to set priorities, choose materials, and decide on the next step without being told what decision to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions and elicits statements of intention, but the session relies heavily on clinician-provided information and structure. There are missed opportunities to deepen the patient’s own values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient to choose the focus and repeatedly asks for preferences, showing a clear collaborative posture. The partnership is not maximal because the psychiatrist supplies much of the substantive content, making the interaction more expert-guided.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including the patient’s intention to call the pediatrician and take a step. However, the change talk is not consistently amplified, expanded, or linked to importance and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the patient’s sustain talk and normalizes the concern about harm. The main limitation is that the response includes direct informational balancing rather than more deliberate MI-style redirection toward values and agency.
