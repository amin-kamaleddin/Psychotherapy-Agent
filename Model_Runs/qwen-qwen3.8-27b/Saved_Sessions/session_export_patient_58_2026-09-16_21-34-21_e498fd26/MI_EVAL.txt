# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Jamal’s need for autonomy and not being treated as a problem. It is less than perfect because the warmth remains somewhat task-focused and could include more explicit affirmation of Jamal’s broader strengths and worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Jamal’s ambivalence and desire for self-direction. The main limitation is that the empathy is more cognitive and practical than emotionally deep, missing fuller exploration of frustration, shame, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on punctuality, attendance, morning routine, and communication with Jamal’s mother. The psychiatrist moves purposefully from the broader concern to a concrete, actionable plan with little topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the session as a joint exploration and invites Jamal to co-construct the plan. Jamal’s ideas strongly shape the direction, and the psychiatrist builds on them rather than directing or advising.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Jamal’s autonomy, ownership, and right to control the process. He avoids directive advice and repeatedly reinforces choice, self-direction, and personal responsibility.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Jamal’s own practical ideas, such as the sticky note, no-check-in agreement, hard-day rule, and short text. It could be stronger by more consistently eliciting deeper internal motivation, values, confidence, and personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jamal to shape the focus and collaborates well on the emerging plan. It falls slightly short of a perfect score because the psychiatrist still structures much of the session through his own sequence of questions and offers less explicit agenda negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes concrete change talk and affirms Jamal’s ownership and responsibility. The evocation is stronger for behavioral planning than for eliciting deeper motivational language such as importance, desire, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Jamal’s sustain talk and reframes it in healthier autonomy terms. The score is limited because the session still leaves avoiding nagging and surveillance as central motivators rather than consistently linking change to intrinsic values, trust, or identity.
