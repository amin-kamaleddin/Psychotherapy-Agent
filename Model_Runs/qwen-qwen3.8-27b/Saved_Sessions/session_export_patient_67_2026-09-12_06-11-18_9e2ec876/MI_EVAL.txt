# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance throughout. Affirmations were frequent but occasionally generic and could better address Kenji's underlying loneliness and self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and often captured behavioral and logistical concerns. Reflections stayed largely factual and did not consistently explore underlying emotions such as loneliness, anxiety, or discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on alcohol reduction with clear momentum toward a concrete plan. There was no topic drift or aimless exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kenji's ideas clearly shaped the plan, and the psychiatrist offered choices rather than prescriptions. The interaction reflected a teammate rather than an authority figure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices, safety concerns, and pacing were consistently framed as Kenji's own decisions. The psychiatrist avoided pressure, persuasion, or coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions were mainly drawn from Kenji through open questions and choice prompts. Some counselor-provided structure and content made the evocation less purely client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and avoided an expert-driven approach. However, the counselor did much of the structural work, which slightly reduced co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicited desire, concern, ability, and commitment. Strong change talk could be further amplified, summarized, and developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ambivalence and self-minimization were acknowledged without argument and redirected toward small, doable steps. The psychiatrist relied more on neutral redirection than strategic double-sided reflections.
