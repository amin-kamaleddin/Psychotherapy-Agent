# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance throughout the session. Occasionally edges toward over-accommodation (e.g., over-correcting when client pushes back), preventing a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate and complex reflections that consistently capture meaning and emotion beneath surface content. Exemplary work in identifying identity threats, ambivalence, and subtle emotional shifts with varied, well-timed responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with a strong session arc from discomfort to emerging self-awareness. Could be strengthened with a brief consolidating summary before closing to help the client integrate key insights.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a strong collaborative stance with inclusive language and real-time adaptation to client feedback. Could be elevated by more explicit co-creative language and visible incorporation of client framings into session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the owner of their own process, explicitly removing pressure to commit or perform. The closing attribution of insight to the client ('You got there yourself') is a textbook demonstration at the highest level.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively uses open questions and reflections to draw out the client's own language rather than providing reasons for change. Could be stronger by pushing the client's strong change talk markers ('I want to stay good') toward more concrete self-motivated reasoning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance with open invitations rather than directives. Slightly one-directional in that the psychiatrist rarely explicitly shares their own perspective or demonstrates full power-sharing language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates change talk through strategic reflections, affirmations, and attribution of insight to the client. Could have pushed slightly more on strong change talk markers like 'I want to stay good' to develop them further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully validates sustain talk without dismissing, arguing, or minimizing the client's ambivalence. Could use more explicit double-sided reflections to strategically hold both sides of the tension simultaneously.
