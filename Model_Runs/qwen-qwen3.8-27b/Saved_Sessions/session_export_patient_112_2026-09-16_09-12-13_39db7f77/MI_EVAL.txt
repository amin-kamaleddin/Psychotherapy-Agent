# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and non-judgmental, normalizing ambivalence and avoiding criticism. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and brief, limiting deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture key conflicts, including the symbolic meaning of smoking. Empathy remains mostly cognitive and lacks deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and concrete target behaviors. It could strengthen the connection between the client's values and the action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client has some influence and can push back on suggestions. The interaction often feels like the counselor offers options and the client evaluates them rather than true co-exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms choice and avoids pressuring the client. The volume of suggested options can feel subtly directive despite collaborative intent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses rulers and open questions but does not deeply elicit the client's internal reasons for change. There is a tendency to provide options and reasons rather than draw them out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to choose and modify ideas, supporting a collaborative tone. However, frequent lengthy lists of strategies make the session feel more expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through scaling and small-step questions. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on the client's own change motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about weight, cost, and identity is met with reassurance or counterarguments rather than MI-consistent responses. Double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of ambivalence would improve this area.
