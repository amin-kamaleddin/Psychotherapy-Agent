# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and acknowledges some of the patient's frustration, but repeatedly offers unsolicited advice and scripts. Acceptance is weakened by pushing the counselor's agenda despite the patient's clear preference for autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections stay at the surface level and miss deeper feelings such as pain, fatigue, shame, and resentment about being lectured. The counselor does not fully validate the patient's relational experience of being managed rather than seen.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the immediate dental visit but lacks a clear MI direction toward the smoking-related target behavior. There is no sustained focus on the patient's ambivalence about smoking and oral health.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some nominal invitations, such as offering to draft a message or a shorter version, but the session remains counselor-led. The patient's preferences and objections do not meaningfully shape the direction of the interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly gives instructions, scripts, and behavioral directions despite the patient's repeated statements that he does not want them. The client's right to choose and ownership of decisions are not consistently honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor supplies advice and reasons for action rather than drawing out the patient's own values, goals, and motivations. There is little effort to elicit internal change talk or explore what the patient personally wants to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts an expert-driven role and positions the patient as a follower in the problem-solving process. There is little genuine co-creation of the agenda or mutual decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is almost no effort to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment related to change. The counselor misses opportunities to explore and amplify the patient's own change-related language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not explicitly reinforce sustain talk, but also does not skillfully redirect it toward exploration or collaboration. The patient's resistance is met with more instructions rather than reflective or motivational responses.
