# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental and respectful stance toward Diego’s heroin use and ambivalence, avoiding pressure to quit. Some task-oriented and repetitive language limited a deeper sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately reflected ambivalence but often stayed at the level of content summary rather than emotional nuance. Missed opportunities to reflect the pain, fear, loneliness, or overwhelm behind statements like “the silence gets too loud.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on heroin harm reduction and overdose safety, especially ensuring the housemate knew about and how to use Narcan. The progression from concern to concrete first step was logical, efficient, and aligned with the presenting issue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative problem-solving occurred, with Diego’s choices and edits incorporated into the final plan. The psychiatrist remained the primary generator of options and wording, making the collaboration somewhat consultant-like.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported Diego’s autonomy by offering choices, respecting his boundaries, and avoiding abstinence pressure. The early provision of a detailed list of safety recommendations was somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client-generated motivation, confidence, and a clear first step. Heavy use of practical advice and scripting limited deeper evocation of Diego’s own values, reasons, and capabilities.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated a collaborative stance by allowing Diego to select the first step, communication method, and wording. At times, providing many options and scripts positioned the psychiatrist as the problem-solver rather than fully sharing the work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked clear change talk related to harm reduction through questions about doability, readiness, and confidence. The psychiatrist did not consistently expand or deepen Diego’s change talk by exploring reasons, ability, need, or desire.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and accurately reflected the function of heroin use in reducing emotional “noise.” The sustain talk was tolerated but not actively softened or processed with complex or double-sided reflections.
