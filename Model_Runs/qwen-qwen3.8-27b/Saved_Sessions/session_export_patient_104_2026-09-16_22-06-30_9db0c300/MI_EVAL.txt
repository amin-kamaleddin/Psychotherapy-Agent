# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using affirmations that reduce shame around nighttime worry. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and could more explicitly normalize the emotional burden of new-parent fear and comparison with her mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections frequently capture Nadine’s exhaustion, panic, and self-doubt, including ambivalence about reassurance and safety. The empathy could be deeper by more explicitly acknowledging shame, fear, vulnerability, and feeling judged by her mother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on infant sleep worry, exhaustion, and building a practical reassurance anchor. There is minor drift toward checklist, backup contact, and early planning, which slightly broadens the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nadine’s ideas clearly shape the session, especially in co-creating and refining the sticky-note phrase. The psychiatrist still provides a structured sequence of topics, which limits fully spontaneous collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended and choice-based questions support Nadine’s sense of agency and ownership. Some multiple-choice framing and limited explicit emphasis on her final decision-making reduce the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated motivation, such as the sticky-note idea and phrase. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist often introduces frameworks or options instead of consistently drawing out Nadine’s own values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Nadine as the expert on her baby and invites her observations to guide the conversation. The therapist still sets a fairly clear structure, limiting full power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nadine expresses change talk around trusting her gut and creating a small anchor for the night. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or expand this change talk before shifting toward practical planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Nadine’s doubts without arguing, which helps preserve the therapeutic alliance. There are few skillful double-sided reflections that validate her caution while gently redirecting toward self-trust.
