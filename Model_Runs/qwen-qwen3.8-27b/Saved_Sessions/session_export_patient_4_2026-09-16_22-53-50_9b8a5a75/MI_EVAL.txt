# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Aizada's distress. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicitly validating the long-term emotional burden and family-related fears.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking were evident, especially in capturing both the practical and emotional meaning of her concerns. Deeper exploration of underlying fears, loss, and social withdrawal would further strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed relevant to her gastrointestinal concerns and cancer fear, but the direction became narrow and administrative around obtaining a letter or test result. A clearer MI-focused behavioral change goal was needed to maintain effective direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist engaged collaboratively and allowed Aizada to shape the wording, content, and next steps. Collaboration could improve by more explicitly sharing power over the overall direction and purpose of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through respect for her choices, preferences, and need to “hold the pen.” Support could be further strengthened by emphasizing her freedom to decide about tests, uncertainty, and coping strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked Aizada’s own language, metaphors, and motivations, such as “steadier floor” and “No cancer was found.” Evocation would be more effective if it more consistently connected to internal motivation for change rather than reassurance or proof.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative, non-authoritative partnership was established, with Aizada positioned as the expert on her own experience. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly co-creating the session agenda and reinforcing her decision-making role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited around comfort, sleep, walking, and reduced checking, but it was not consistently developed or amplified. More systematic use of DARC/CRAF themes would strengthen this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and respectfully acknowledged the need for safety and proof. However, the session mostly accommodated the “test first” stance rather than actively softening sustain talk through double-sided reflections or values-based pivots.
