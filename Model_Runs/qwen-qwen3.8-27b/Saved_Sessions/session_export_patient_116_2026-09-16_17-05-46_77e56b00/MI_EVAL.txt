# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of the client’s ambivalence. Some statements feel slightly scripted and could more fully affirm the client as a person beyond medication adherence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses several good complex reflections that capture the client’s concerns about feeling controlled. Empathy could be deeper by reflecting emotional undertones such as frustration, resentment, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on medication adherence and related concerns. However, the conversation becomes somewhat circular and could better move toward a concrete, actionable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and treats his ideas as useful. The session would become stronger if it moved further from therapist-led guidance toward true shared agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is consistently strong, with repeated emphasis on the client’s pace, choice, and ownership. The counselor avoids pressure and supports the client’s right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client’s own ideas, such as using breakfast as an anchor and a text nudge. Evocation could be strengthened by using scaling, values, or confidence questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally positions the client as the expert and allows his contributions to shape the conversation. Partnership could improve with more explicit co-creation of goals and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generates several client-driven change statements and useful ideas. The counselor could more strategically amplify and expand the strongest change talk into commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but remains heavily focused on the client’s reasons against change. More double-sided reflections and gentle links between autonomy and health goals would help soften sustain talk.
