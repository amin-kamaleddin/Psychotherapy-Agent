# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming Marcus for missed doses, financial strain, or attachment to the dog. Warmth is somewhat repetitive and occasionally projects interpretations Marcus has not fully expressed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Marcus’s ambivalence, fatigue, and fear, but the reflections become repetitive and somewhat leading. The emotional meaning of the dog, financial shame, and impact on relationship or identity is not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on asthma self-care and moves from a small step to routine, reminder, and crisis-call planning. Momentum is limited by repeated similar questions and limited agenda consolidation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely co-constructs the plan with Marcus and responds to his generated ideas rather than imposing advice. Collaboration is uneven because the clinician repeatedly returns to similar questions and offers interpretive summaries that feel clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by avoiding directives, affirming that the plan must fit Marcus’s life, and respecting the boundary around the dog. Explicit affirmation of his right to choose or not change could be stronger, and repeated steering toward a “small step” limits consistency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions successfully evoke concrete, self-generated strategies such as the toothbrush reminder, nightly inhaler use, and visible doctor number. The evocation remains somewhat surface-level by not deeply accessing values, identity, confidence, or future-oriented motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Marcus as the expert on his own life and allows his ideas to shape the session. Full power-sharing is limited by repeated similar questions and occasional interpretive assumptions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and affirms concrete change talk, including tracking inhaler use, a nightly routine, a reminder system, and calling the doctor before going to the ER. This change talk is not actively amplified, expanded, or deepened through scaling, future imagery, past success, or strategic summarizing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Marcus’s concerns about the dog, finances, overwhelm, and fear without arguing against them. He mostly acknowledges sustain talk rather than actively softening it through double-sided reflection, reframing, or redirection toward Marcus’s own reasons for change.
