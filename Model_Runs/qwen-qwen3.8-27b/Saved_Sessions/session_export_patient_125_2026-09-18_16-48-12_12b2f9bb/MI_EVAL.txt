# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and unconditional positive regard throughout; validates Tariq's experience without judgment or conditions. Holds both truths simultaneously when Tariq acknowledges his own part while maintaining the punishment was disproportionate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface content to capture emotional meaning (e.g., 'Like your efforts don't count'). Consistently tracks Tariq's emotional shifts and responds to the feeling underneath the words rather than just the content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from validating pain to exploring worthlessness to evoking a vivid vision of release. Could have been stronger if it ended with a concrete commitment or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong shared exploration that moves organically based on Tariq's contributions, with open and inviting questions. Could be deepened with more bidirectional co-construction moments (e.g., 'What do you make of that?').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports agency by inviting input rather than prescribing solutions; Tariq arrives at his own conclusion naturally. Could have been more explicit in affirming his ownership of the decision process at key moments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions consistently draw out Tariq's own motivations, with the evocative question about walking out leading directly to vivid, self-generated change talk. The client's own words become the engine of change — the hallmark of excellent evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with open questions; the conversation is clearly driven by Tariq's own words and contributions. Could have more explicitly acknowledged Tariq's expertise in his own life and used more power-sharing language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Tariq's own motivation through well-timed reflections and the powerful question about imagining walking out in three weeks. Could have been stronger by more explicitly eliciting commitment language or exploring confidence levels.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully acknowledges and redirects blame without challenging or reinforcing, using the 'both sides' technique effectively. Could have used more double-sided reflections to gently explore the impact of Tariq's beliefs on his experience.
