# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the client's move-related stress and fear of slipping. A somewhat clinical, formulaic tone (early safety check, procedural warmth) keeps it from being fully deep and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides accurate but brief reflections that are quickly followed by practical advice, limiting reflective depth. Misses opportunities to explore the disappointment, body-confidence themes, and fear of failure implied in her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on small-movement routines and honors the client's stated boundary to exclude nutrition. Main limitation is that direction is maintained through concrete guidance rather than a broader MI process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client makes meaningful selections (activity, reminder label, coping plan) but the psychiatrist generates nearly all ideas, structure, and problem-solving strategies beforehand. Interaction feels more like a clinician-provided menu than an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently affirms the client's freedom ('You don't have to hit exact numbers,' 'skip without shame') and validates her desire to keep things simple. Slightly undercut by the high volume of specific behavioral suggestions still offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Moves quickly into external rationale and practical problem-solving rather than using MI questions to draw out the client's personal values, importance, or confidence for change. No scaling, importance, confidence, or value-based explorations are present.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites client input on activity selection and reminder wording, and the client's choices shape the final plan. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary architect, creating an uneven power dynamic where the client selects from pre-made options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Uses supportive affirming language ('nice choice,' 'you've got this') but makes only minimal MI-specific efforts to elicit or expand the client's own change talk. No strategic questions to deepen her expressed motivation or past-success reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmentally acknowledges barriers and offers useful reframes ('misses as data, not failure,' 'skip without shame'). Tends to respond to sustain talk with additional problem-solving strategies rather than soft reflections or questions to help her resolve ambivalence.
