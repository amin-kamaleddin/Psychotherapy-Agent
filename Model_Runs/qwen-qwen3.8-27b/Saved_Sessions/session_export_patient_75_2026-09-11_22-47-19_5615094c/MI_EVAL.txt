# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes the patient’s concerns. Warmth is appropriate but remains more procedural than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers basic reflections of stress and fear. Reflections are adequate but often shift into explanation or problem-solving rather than deepening emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on inhaler use, asthma control, and medication concerns. The direction is strong but more task-oriented than MI-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers options and adjusts to the patient’s preferences. Collaboration is present, but the therapist often remains the primary driver of content and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician supports choice by offering options and avoiding overt pressure. Autonomy support is limited by frequent scripts, instructions, and clinician-supplied solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician asks questions, but they mainly serve task selection rather than drawing out the patient’s values and motivations. The role is more informative than evocative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and offers choices, showing partial collaboration. However, the session often becomes clinician-led, reducing true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expresses some change-oriented language, but the clinician rarely amplifies or expands it. Responses often add information or next steps instead of deepening motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expresses significant sustain talk around side effects, dependence, and long-term use. The clinician acknowledges concerns but does not consistently soften or strategically redirect them toward change.
