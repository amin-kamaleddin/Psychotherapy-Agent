# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and reduces shame around smoking and past relapse. Acceptance is solid, though it could be warmer and more individualized when addressing stigma and family-related distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist accurately reflects the client’s push–pull experience of craving, temporary calm, shame, and fear of failure. Some reflections remain somewhat repetitive and could go deeper into the client’s values, identity, or relational pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and urge management, with clear clinical relevance. Direction could be stronger by summarizing the emerging focus and connecting the exercise more explicitly to the client’s broader cessation goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses collaborative language and co-constructs a brief urge-management experiment with the client. However, the frequent clinician-led options and psychoeducation make the collaboration less fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by inviting permission, framing the exercise as an experiment, and avoiding pressure to accept medications or coping strategies. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client’s ultimate ownership of the decision to quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit useful client-generated material about cravings, triggers, and previous quit attempts. The evocation could be deeper by exploring the client’s values, reasons, confidence, and ability related to quitting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist invites the client’s preferences and adapts when the client declines medical options. The session remains collaborative, though the multiple structured options make it feel somewhat clinician-guided.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist acknowledges the client’s desire to quit and asks questions that invite change-related language. However, strong change talk from the client is not consistently amplified or systematically developed through desire, ability, reasons, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and normalizes the difficulty of a prior quit attempt. More strategic softening is needed, such as double-sided reflections that turn concern or resistance toward change.
