# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows unconditional positive regard and avoids moralizing, blaming, or pressuring the patient. Responses remain respectful and validating even when the patient is skeptical about change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate complex reflections that capture both the practical and emotional dimensions of the patient's ambivalence. Reflections validate the patient's stress, work pressure, and the meaningful role of the cigarette break.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation within the workplace policy while addressing underlying stress management. The conversation moves smoothly from ambivalence to a concrete, patient-defined action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the patient as an equal partner by inviting input and co-constructing the plan. Patient ideas such as the stairwell, shoulders, and thirty-second check-in are incorporated into a cohesive strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly reinforces the patient's freedom to choose, stop, or avoid feeling graded. The patient's sense of ownership and control is strongly supported throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the patient's own motivations, resources, and ideas rather than providing advice or reasons for change. Open questions help the patient generate solutions and express self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that the patient knows their life and workday best. The patient's contributions substantially shape the direction and content of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and amplifies the patient's emerging agency, safety net, and desire to avoid feeling stuck. The patient ends with a clear statement of ownership over the proposed thirty-second check-in.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without challenging it, validating concerns about losing the cigarette's function. The response is strong, though it could use more explicit double-sided reflections to pivot further toward change.
