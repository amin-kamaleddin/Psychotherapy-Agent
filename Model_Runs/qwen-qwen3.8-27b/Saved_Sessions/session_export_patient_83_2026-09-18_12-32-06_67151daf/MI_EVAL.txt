# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates warmth, respect, and validation without judgment. Occasionally feels formulaic with repeated phrases like 'that makes total sense,' limiting the depth of genuine connection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections that capture both situational and emotional dimensions of Mai's experience. Reflections sometimes remain at the cognitive/content level rather than accessing the deeper emotional undercurrent, such as the vulnerability of feeling unheard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression from rapport-building to medication education with appropriate pacing. Could strengthen by stating a collaborative session goal more explicitly early on (e.g., 'So that you walk out feeling confident about this medication...').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language, invites Mai's perspective, and negotiates what to cover and in what order. Shifts toward an instructive mode with 'I'm going to walk you through it,' creating a slight power imbalance during the educational portion.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy through permission-asking, letting Mai choose priorities, and giving her control over pace. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Mai's right to make her own decision at key decision points.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Mai's own definition of good care, her conditions for change, and her preferred next steps through open questions. Could go deeper by connecting the medication to Mai's broader health values and long-term identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance by inviting input, asking for permission, and positioning as a resource rather than authority. The framing 'I'm going to walk you through it' shifts the dynamic toward a more directive, less equal-partner model.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some organic change talk (values, capacity, desire) but does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or strategically build on Mai's own change-favorable language. Lacks systematic use of DARN-CAT evocation strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Mai's mild sustain talk (confusion, frustration) well by validating concerns without arguing or minimizing, and reframes resistance as intentionality. Could employ more explicit double-sided reflections to further soften the tension.
