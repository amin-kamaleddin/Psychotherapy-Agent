# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a supportive tone and several affirmations, but the opening is somewhat didactic and the later script reintroduces declined nicotine options. Acceptance is present but not consistent enough for a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's concerns about driving, children, late shifts, and progress. It is not a 5 because the emotional weight behind ambivalence is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on nighttime asthma control, inhaler technique, spacer use, and a concrete plan. Occasional drift into declined nicotine alternatives prevents a perfect score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client preferences clearly shape the plan, including the short visor note and technique-first focus. Collaboration is occasionally disrupted by expert-driven delivery and reintroduction of declined nicotine options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers choices, and acknowledges the client is not ready to quit. Autonomy support is weakened by continuing to include nicotine options the client has declined.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful 0-10 questions elicit the client's own motivation and readiness, especially for inhaler technique. Evocation is present but not consistently deep enough to amplify the client's reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking, options, and co-creation show partnership, but the interaction is more expert-led than equal in places. The client is not consistently positioned as the primary expert on his own life and work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces meaningful change talk about technique, flare-ups, and reduced smoking need, but the psychiatrist does not consistently expand it. More systematic amplification of change talk would be needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often redirects toward smaller, workable steps and avoids direct argument. Repeatedly introducing nicotine options can re-energize resistance, so softening sustain talk is not consistently skillful.
