# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s values and boundaries. Acceptance is slightly procedural and affected by repeated menu-style questions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s practical concerns and the tiredness-versus-drift distinction. The empathy does not always deepen into the stronger emotional fear of rejection, burden, or being misunderstood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the client’s values, barriers, signal, and small protective behavior. There is some redundancy in questions and limited concise summarization to consolidate the emerging plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well and uses the client’s expertise in shaping the behavior and phrase. The client’s role in setting the next direction could be made more visible.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy through open questions and client-chosen language. It would be stronger if the client’s freedom to modify, delay, or abandon the plan were stated more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes practical signals and small steps rather than deeper internal motivation. More evocation of values, confidence, consequences, or meaning would strengthen the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The stance is collaborative, with the client positioned as the expert on her family and experience. The structure still feels somewhat guided by repeated prompts rather than fully negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces useful change talk and a concrete protective phrase. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify, summarize, and deepen the client’s change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s worries without arguing and keeps the behavior small and safe. More explicit double-sided reflections would better balance the client’s competing concerns.
