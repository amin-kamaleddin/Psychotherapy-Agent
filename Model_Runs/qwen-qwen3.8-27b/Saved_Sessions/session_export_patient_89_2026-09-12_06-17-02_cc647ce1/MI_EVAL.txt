# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, including clear statements such as “No judgment — just curiosity.” Acceptance is practical and formulaic, and does not fully affirm Marcus’ worthiness despite prior dieting setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers adequate reflections of Marcus’ frustration and barriers, but the empathy remains largely cognitive and problem-focused. There is limited exploration of deeper emotions such as shame, exhaustion, or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on increasing physical activity in a way that fits Marcus’ schedule. The counselor moves consistently from concerns to a specific, organized plan, though occasional health education can make it feel more advisory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative, with Marcus selecting plan elements that fit his life. The counselor still leads by providing lists and shaping the plan, acting more like a helpful consultant than a fully equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice by asking permission, offering options, and letting Marcus select cues and follow-up timing. However, the specific repetitions and two-week trial feel somewhat prescriptive without explicitly affirming Marcus as the final decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some evocative questions about importance, doability, and confidence, but also provides substantial advice and education. This limits deeper elicitation of Marcus’ own values, motivations, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Marcus’ input and uses his preferences in the final plan, but often functions as a practical expert rather than a true partner. More partnership would involve inviting Marcus to generate his own ideas before offering a menu of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some change talk, including Marcus choosing the family walk and raising his confidence from six to seven. There are missed opportunities to reflect, amplify, and summarize Marcus’ own statements in favor of change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges Marcus’ sustain talk respectfully without arguing or reinforcing it. However, the session spends considerable time problem-solving rather than strategically softening sustain talk through double-sided reflections or redirection to values.
