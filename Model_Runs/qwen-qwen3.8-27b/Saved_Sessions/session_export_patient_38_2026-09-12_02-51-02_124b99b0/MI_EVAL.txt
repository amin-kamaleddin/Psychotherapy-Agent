# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Elena’s distress without criticizing her inconsistent contraception use. The main limitation is that the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and repetitive rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately tracks Elena’s fear, exhaustion, and mixed relief. However, empathy often remains at a summary level and shifts quickly toward offering choices or scripts rather than deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a loose connection to the referral target of contraception but frequently shifts toward anxiety management and coping. It avoids aimlessness but lacks consistent strategic direction toward the intended behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, especially when Elena modifies the suggested language and the psychiatrist accepts it without overriding. The psychiatrist still supplies much of the structure, options, and language, limiting more fully client-generated collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Elena’s sense of choice and avoids pressuring her to decide or commit to a future appointment. The main gap is that the autonomy support is mostly about avoiding pressure rather than actively affirming Elena’s authority over her own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to draw motivation from Elena, especially by asking what “steady” would feel like and inviting her own coping phrase. However, the evocation is limited in depth and does not consistently elicit stronger change talk around contraception.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally collaborative stance and repeatedly invites Elena’s preferences, allowing her input to influence the session. The partnership is less full because the clinician still controls much of the structure and agenda through scripted options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are effective moments when Elena expresses a need for change, such as wanting to stop guessing and reduce stress. The psychiatrist does not consistently develop or amplify this change talk through deeper questions about importance, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing, judging, or reinforcing Elena’s reasons for postponement, which helps reduce defensiveness. However, the sustain talk is not consistently or skillfully redirected toward the target change around contraception.
