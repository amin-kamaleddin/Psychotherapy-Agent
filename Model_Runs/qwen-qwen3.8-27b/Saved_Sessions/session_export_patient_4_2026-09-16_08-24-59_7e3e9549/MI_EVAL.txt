# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, honoring Aizada’s preferences for wording and urgency. It falls short of a 5 because some validation is procedural and does not fully normalize her fear of cancer.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Aizada’s distress, sense of invalidation, and practical impact of symptoms. Empathy remains mainly content-level and does not fully track deeper emotions such as fear, vulnerability, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Aizada’s primary concern, symptoms, and next medical steps. Direction is less strategic in guiding broader MI-relevant change, such as coping with stress-related symptoms or uncertainty.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively in constructing the referral letter, incorporating Aizada’s edits and preferences. Collaboration is strong for the medical task but limited in broader MI areas such as values, goals, or ambivalence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aizada’s preferences are consistently invited and respected, especially regarding wording, timing, and the plan for follow-up. Autonomy support is strong but mainly expressed through choosing among clinician-generated options rather than client-led exploration of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions, but these mostly elicit factual or preference-based information. There is limited effort to evoke Aizada’s internal motivation, values, hopes, or confidence about change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is clear partnership in problem-solving around the referral and practical next steps. The interaction is less clearly MI-like because the psychiatrist remains the medical expert rather than explicitly positioning Aizada as the expert on her own body and change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that could elicit change talk but does not consistently amplify or build on Aizada’s positive statements, such as getting back to work or symptoms easing during the wedding. The session remains task-focused rather than motivation-focused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Aizada’s need for tests without arguing, which is appropriate. However, the sustain talk is not effectively softened or redirected using reflections that acknowledge both sides and move toward change.
