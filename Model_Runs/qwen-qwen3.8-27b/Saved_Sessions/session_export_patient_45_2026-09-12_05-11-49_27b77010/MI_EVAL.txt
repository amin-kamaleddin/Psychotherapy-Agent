# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, without scolding or moralizing. Acceptance is slightly limited because the warmth is brief and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures Erik's frustration, ambivalence, and social concerns well. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and procedural, with limited deeper exploration of underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is practical and focused, but the psychiatrist often supplies concrete options and scripts rather than fully drawing out Erik's own solutions. Direction is supported by frequent choices and respect for Erik's decisions, though it remains moderately directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a joint problem-solving tone, and Erik's preferences clearly shape the plan. Collaboration could be stronger by eliciting more of Erik's own ideas before offering detailed strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses choice-based language and respects Erik's decision not to involve a friend in advance. Autonomy support is reduced slightly by the amount of specific tactics and rules offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions elicit readiness, confidence, and planning, but motivation remains largely external, such as avoiding lifelong medication. There is limited deeper exploration of Erik's values, identity, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik is invited to choose among options, but the counselor remains fairly expert-driven by supplying many tactics, scripts, and rules. More emphasis on Erik as the expert on his own life would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently prompts change talk and confidence, leading to specific action statements. Change talk could be further amplified by reflecting and deepening its connection to Erik's values and independence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and uses nonjudgmental language, which is a strength. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened or redirected using more advanced MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or reframing.
