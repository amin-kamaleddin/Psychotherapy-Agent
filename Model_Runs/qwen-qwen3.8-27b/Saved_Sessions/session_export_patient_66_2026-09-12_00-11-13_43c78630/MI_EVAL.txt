# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout, especially in accepting Kareem’s stance of coping rather than quitting. The main limitation is that the session becomes quite advice-driven, which slightly reduces the sense of deep affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers useful cognitive reflections of Kareem’s practical concerns about the smoke-free policy. However, several emotional undercurrents—irritation at being managed, dignity, embarrassment, and resistance to being treated as a “project”—are not reflected deeply enough.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on smoking, workplace triggers, coping strategies, and the immediate plan for the coming week. The main weakness is that the direction remains more procedural and problem-management focused than collaboratively change-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kareem contributes meaningfully to the plan, such as choosing the lozenge over gum and identifying the printer as a better cover. However, the clinician frequently supplies multiple lists of options, which makes the interaction feel more like expert advising than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician respects Kareem’s choice to focus on coping rather than quitting and uses autonomy-affirming statements such as “you’re in charge.” The repeated advice and structured tactics, however, subtly steer the client’s choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician asks a few helpful choice-oriented questions at the start, but then shifts quickly into advice mode. There are many missed opportunities to evoke Kareem’s own values, motivations, concerns about dignity, and confidence about getting through the week.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative effort, including checking whether Kareem wants help quitting or coping and incorporating his preferences. The session still feels uneven because the clinician occupies an expert role and supplies much of the plan rather than co-creating it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Kareem offers some forward-looking coping language, such as trying the lozenge, using the printer, and making it to Friday without sneaking outside. The clinician affirms this but does not actively expand or deepen change talk with questions about desire, ability, reasons, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician accepts Kareem’s sustain talk without arguing, which is appropriate. However, the session does not effectively soften or explore the ambivalence behind statements like “I’m not quitting” or “it’s my reset button,” and instead remains anchored in maintaining the current habit.
