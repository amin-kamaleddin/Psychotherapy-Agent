# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the patient’s concerns without criticizing her drinking or proposed excuse. The warmth is appropriate but somewhat formulaic, with room for deeper, more individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects the patient’s ambivalence, practical concerns, and fear of being misread by friends. Empathy is strong but remains mostly cognitive and plan-focused rather than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on reducing alcohol use while preserving social connection and moves logically toward a concrete plan. Direction could be stronger by tying the plan more explicitly to a measurable alcohol-reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is highly collaborative, with the psychiatrist following the patient’s ideas and helping refine them rather than imposing solutions. The patient’s contributions clearly shape the direction and content of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the patient’s ability to choose and avoids pressuring her to commit. Some evaluative language is slightly leading, so explicit autonomy support could be strengthened.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits the patient’s own priorities, ideas, and plan components rather than providing external reasons for change. Evocation could be deeper by exploring values, identity, confidence, or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveys a non-expert stance and treats the patient as the expert on her own life. The patient’s choices and ideas drive the session, reflecting a genuine collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient generates substantial change talk about protecting sleep, studies, relationships, and using smaller drinking or low-alcohol alternatives. The therapist could further strengthen motivation by exploring values, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about social fear and feeling “weird” is handled without argument or dismissal, keeping the patient engaged. More advanced reflections could better defuse sustain talk and connect it back to deeper values.
