# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts the client’s ambivalence. The acceptance is professional but could be slightly deeper and more explicitly normalizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s conflict and trust standards. Empathic accuracy is strong, though some reflections are repetitive and could explore deeper feelings such as shame or fear of social loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol-related ambivalence, school, friendships, and safer next steps. It would benefit from periodically returning more explicitly to the drinking-specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas about safety, privacy, and trust become the central content of the session. The interaction is collaborative, though it remains somewhat counselor-led in pacing and agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly emphasizes the client’s choice and avoids pressuring immediate commitment. At times, proposed scripts or specific first steps may subtly lead the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own ideas, boundaries, and safety criteria. Evocation could be stronger in drawing out deeper change talk, confidence, and intrinsic values related to reducing drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and builds on her contributions, creating a collaborative stance. The session would be stronger if the agenda were more fully co-created rather than partially counselor-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, especially around wanting school to feel more real and taking a smaller safer step. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify, summarize, and strengthen the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without argument or reinforcement of resistance. The psychiatrist could use more active strategies such as double-sided reflections or linking sustain talk to the client’s values.
