# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, validating stance throughout the interaction. Statements such as 'That makes sense' and 'I respect you handling this on your own' supported unconditional positive regard without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected resignation, repetition, and the sense of pointlessness associated with mandated attendance. However, the reflections remained somewhat surface-level and did not deeply explore underlying frustration or powerlessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on administrative tasks and form completion rather than a clear therapeutic target. There was limited momentum toward engagement, compliance, or a concrete change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by inviting input on how to proceed and checking the preferred wording for documentation. Collaboration was strong but mainly directed toward logistics rather than jointly shaping a change plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported choice by offering options, respecting the decision to take forms later, and explicitly stating the patient was in control. There was no coercion, persuasion, or pressure to sign or engage further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some open-ended questions, such as asking what felt most important or useful today. These attempts were present but limited and were not followed up in a way that drew out motivations, values, or readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed a collaborative stance by following the patient's lead and asking preferences. The partnership was evident but remained focused on administrative decisions rather than deeper co-created treatment direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was little active effort to elicit reasons for engagement, desired outcomes, or confidence in change. The patient's change talk remained minimal and was not further developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the patient's sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it. However, the statements were rarely redirected toward ambivalence or examined in a way that might soften resistance.
