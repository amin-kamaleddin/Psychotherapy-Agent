# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and respect for Aisha as a person. There is no judgment, blame, or shame about body image, weight, or past attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses specific, individualized reflections that accurately capture Aisha’s emotional burden and perspective. However, some responses remain more cognitive or behavioral rather than fully exploring the deeper shame, fear, and self-judgment underneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the target behavior and moves toward a small, manageable action. The main limitation is that it does not end with a clear, consolidated next step linking Aisha’s goals, chosen action, and follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and invites Aisha to co-construct meaning and planning. Aisha’s own ideas guide the direction of the session rather than the psychiatrist imposing an expert-driven plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Aisha’s sense of choice and avoids controlling or directive language. Change is framed as Aisha’s own decision, with no rules, pressure, or imposed standards.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Aisha’s own motivations through open, evocative questions. The session could strengthen evocation by more explicitly eliciting confidence, commitment, or higher-level change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly shares power with Aisha and positions her as the expert on her own life. Aisha substantially influences the direction and content of the session through her generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha expresses meaningful change talk, such as wanting to feel lighter, okay, and less stuck. The psychiatrist could strengthen this by explicitly consolidating or amplifying that change talk through rulers, summaries, or commitment questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reframes sustain talk in a way that reduces its power without reinforcing it. Some failure or skipping language remains fairly central before being redirected, so the softening could be more strategic.
