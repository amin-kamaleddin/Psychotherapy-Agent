# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained nonjudgmental, validating, and respectful throughout. Some validation felt formulaic, and the client's hesitation around mirror work was not explicitly acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic statements were present but often stayed at the content level. The core ambivalence about weight loss, fear of wasted effort, and guilt around her mother were not consistently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on concrete targets such as non-diet habits, thought records, weighing, and boundaries. Direction was strong but somewhat agenda-driven rather than fully co-created.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some joint activity, including role-play and preference choices. However, the psychiatrist remained the primary knowledge-holder, making the interaction feel more coach-like than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were regularly offered and the client's decisions were honored without pressure. Ownership was slightly weakened by the clinician-authored plan and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generated useful change talk on her own, and there was some initial elicitation. The clinician did not consistently deepen her values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The stance included preference-seeking and some shared decision-making. Power was uneven, as the psychiatrist supplied most of the framework, plan, and language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client change talk was present but mainly self-generated. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, develop, or strategically reflect that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with sustain talk but often accommodated or redirected it instead of resolving the ambivalence. The fear of wasting time and desire for weight loss were left underprocessed.
