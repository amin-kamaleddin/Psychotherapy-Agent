# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and respectful, but acceptance is mostly task-focused approval. Deeper validation of Jamal’s emotional burden and person, rather than only his compliance, is needed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The response stays mostly on factual/behavioral details with limited perspective-taking. It misses key emotional undercurrents such as independence, self-image, frustration, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on school punctuality and concrete morning actions, with little topic drift. However, the direction is more logistical than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal contributes to the final plan, but the psychiatrist supplies most options, content, and troubleshooting. The interaction feels more expert-directed than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by asking Jamal to choose options and acknowledge his preferences. However, rapid specific instructions can feel controlling and should be softened with more open questions and permission-seeking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to elicit Jamal’s own values, reasons, hopes, or confidence for change. Key opportunities to explore what punctuality means to him beyond avoiding consequences are missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session includes some collaborative prompts but remains largely expert-driven and coaching-focused. The psychiatrist should more explicitly position Jamal as the expert on his own life and experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are occasional efforts to involve Jamal in choices and capture some commitment language. The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit or expand intrinsic reasons, desire, ability, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue against or reinforce sustain talk and often accepts reluctance. However, the conversation could better use double-sided reflections or gentle reframing to preserve autonomy while expanding intrinsic motivation.
