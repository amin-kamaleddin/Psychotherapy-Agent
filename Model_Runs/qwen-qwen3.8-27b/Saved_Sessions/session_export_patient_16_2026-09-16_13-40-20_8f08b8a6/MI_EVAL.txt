### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Ambivalence is validated without moralizing, labeling, or expressing disappointment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and captures Anika's ambivalence, stress, and competing goals. It could be deeper by exploring underlying fears, identity stakes, and emotional meaning more precisely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on vaping use, stress, and basketball performance. It would be stronger with clearer naming of the target behavior and brief consolidation of the broader goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative because it builds on Anika's ideas rather than imposing expert advice. However, agenda-setting and equal-partner collaboration are not always made explicit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Anika's ownership is consistently supported through open questions, client-generated plans, and emphasis on her own choices. The therapist avoids pressure, directives, or imposed timelines.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Anika's own reasons for change and coping plans. It would improve with deeper exploration of values, confidence, and stronger amplification of motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist frequently asks for Anika's perspective and follows her suggestions. More explicit language about Anika being the expert on her own life would strengthen the partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anika generates multiple change statements and practical ideas, including the no-vaping streak and drawer plan. The therapist could more consistently amplify, scale, and consolidate this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is reflected without arguing or amplifying it, and the therapist redirects toward change goals. More explicit double-sided reflections would make the softening stronger.
