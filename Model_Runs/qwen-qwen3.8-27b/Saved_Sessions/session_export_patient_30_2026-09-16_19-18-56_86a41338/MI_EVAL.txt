# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, validating stance throughout, accepting both sides of David's ambivalence without judgment or pressure. Falls short of a 5 because the acceptance is somewhat procedural and lacks deeper, individualized positive regard (e.g., affirming David's courage or values).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate, coherent reflections that correctly capture the core tension between medication concern and respect for his doctor. Remains mostly cognitive and repetitive, missing opportunities to reflect the emotional depth of David's fear about losing independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stays appropriately focused on the medication decision with relevant questions about evidence, trial criteria, and risk. Does not consolidate the lifestyle trial (diet, walking, blood recheck) into a specific, client-owned behavioral target or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as joint exploration ('figure this out together'), and David's concerns shape the session's content. Still somewhat structured by the psychiatrist's line of inquiry; less evidence of David visibly directing the agenda or next direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly affirms David's right and ability to make his own decision, with no pressuring or medical authority imposed. Directly addresses his fear of being a 'guinea pig' and reinforces that any choice is made 'with eyes open.' One of the strongest areas of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks useful open questions about evidence criteria and a fair plan without lecturing about cholesterol risk. Focuses mainly on logistical/evidentiary decision criteria rather than evoking deeper values, motivations, confidence, or future-self imagery for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and invites David's own ideas, criteria, and judgments. Still drives the session with a series of structured questions; limited evidence of explicit agenda negotiation or power sharing where David co-creates the conversation's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David offers several instances of change talk (willing to try lifestyle trial, would reconsider with specific evidence), and the psychiatrist asks follow-up questions that invite further development. Does not consistently reflect, amplify, or summarize the change talk, and misses opportunities to evoke desire, confidence, or ability for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meets David's sustain talk (medication concerns, waiting for doctor to push harder) with balanced validation rather than confrontation. The session remains heavily focused on reasons for delay without skillful double-sided reflections or redirection toward client-identified benefits of change or a future self.
