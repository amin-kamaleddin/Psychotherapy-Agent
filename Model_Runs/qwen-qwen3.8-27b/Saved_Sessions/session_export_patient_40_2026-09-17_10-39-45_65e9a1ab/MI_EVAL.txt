# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates unconditional positive regard, validating Elias without judgment even when he doubts his plan. There is no moralizing, correction, or minimization of his fears.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures both motivation and ambivalence, though some reflections become slightly interpretive. Greater depth is needed on fear, shame, and the emotional cost of forgetting the plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on relapse prevention and urge management. The pacing moves logically from a broader goal to one hour, one signal, and one physical anchor.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist functions as an equal partner, building directly on Elias's ideas rather than imposing a therapist-made plan. The interaction feels like a joint exploration of his own recovery concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently keeps choice with Elias and avoids directives or pressure. Language supports self-authorship and personal coping design throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist primarily draws out Elias's own motivations, values, and coping ideas. This evocation stance strongly elicits client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats Elias as the expert on his own life and body, inviting his input at each step. Occasional therapist-supplied examples are slightly leading but do not undermine the collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, values-based questions reliably evoke meaningful change talk about dignity, fatherhood, and self-control. The counselor could more explicitly deepen confidence, importance, and commitment with scaling or commitment questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubt and ambivalence are acknowledged respectfully and redirected toward problem-solving without arguing. The score is limited by less consistent use of double-sided reflections or explicit pivots from sustain talk to change.
