# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Marcus's concerns about his dog, fatigue, and need for simplicity. Slightly deeper normalization of Marcus's stress and ambivalence would strengthen unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated accurate reflective listening around Marcus's ambivalence, exhaustion, and practical barriers. More complex reflections exploring the emotional meaning of "losing the dog" would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on improving asthma management and making the inhaler easier to access. The conversation progressed logically from identifying the concern to forming a concrete if-then plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructed a plan with Marcus, incorporating his preferences for a keyring/carabiner, after-dinner timing, and a one-word cue. The collaboration could be stronger by allowing more client-generated solutions before introducing options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized choice, respected Marcus's boundaries, and avoided pressure for a more complex plan. Some mildly directive questions about timing and confidence could subtly imply an expectation to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some client-generated motivation around confidence, small steps, and barriers. However, the session relied heavily on clinician-generated options and did not deeply evoke Marcus's values or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited Marcus's input throughout the session. Offering multiple external options at times made the interaction feel more guided than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently prompted Marcus to articulate his own change-oriented language and reinforced his proposed step. Change talk could be deepened by exploring the values and meaning behind easier breathing and being present for his wife and dog.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Marcus's sustain talk without arguing or minimizing his concerns. Some statements, such as "it's not the end of the world," could be more skillfully reframed toward the benefits of the plan.
