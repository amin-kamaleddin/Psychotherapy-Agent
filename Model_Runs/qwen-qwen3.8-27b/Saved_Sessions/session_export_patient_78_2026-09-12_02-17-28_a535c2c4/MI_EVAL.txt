# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently expresses respect, nonjudgment, and validation of the client’s frustration and willingness. Affirmations are sometimes formulaic and tied to participation rather than individualized personal worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s exhaustion, repetitive advice, and smoking as a quiet manageable break. They do not fully explore deeper emotions, values, or the meaning behind the behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and narrows to a small after-lunch cigarette experiment. It could better link the target behavior to the client’s values and build toward broader change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed, and the psychiatrist accepts the client’s refusal of gum and follow-up. However, offering multiple clinician-generated options can feel like selecting from a menu rather than client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes choice, no pressure, no homework, and no judgment, accepting the client’s limits. It could more explicitly affirm that doing nothing or not trying is also fully acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions that elicit some client perspective and a small intention to try. It moves too quickly to options without deeply exploring what the quiet time means or the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows the client’s lead, accepts the client’s agenda, and adapts to the minimal plan. The collaboration is stronger when the client’s expertise is honored rather than the clinician supplying options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, including willingness to try a small step and stepping outside. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or follow up on that change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or judging statements like needing the other cigarettes or fearing forgetfulness. It misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections or explore the function of the sustain talk.
