# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone without blaming Jamal for alcohol use or peer pressure. Acceptance is somewhat generic, however, and could be stronger by explicitly affirming Jamal’s worth, strengths, and full right to decide his own pace.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects several key conflicts, including torn feelings about team belonging, fear of looking weak, and worry about being mocked or pulled back. Empathy is helpful but remains somewhat surface-level, especially in missing deeper emotional meaning and not reflecting Jamal’s later idea about skipping the first drink.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of alcohol games and how Jamal might avoid or exit the situation. The main limitation is that direction becomes overly tactical, focusing on scripts and exit maneuvers rather than periodically returning to motivation, values, or a smaller client-owned change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jamal to choose among options and incorporates some of his ideas, such as the study excuse, ally idea, and skipping the first drink. Collaboration is limited by the counselor’s repeated presentation of prepackaged strategies, which makes the session feel more like a toolkit than a fully co-created plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is sometimes supported through questions about preference and what feels doable, but it is weakened by directive behavioral instructions such as using a line twice, walking without pausing, and freeing his arm. More explicit autonomy language about Jamal’s choice and control would strengthen this criterion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes useful client-centered statements about not wanting to seem weak, not wanting a speech, and considering skipping the first drink. Evocation is inconsistent because the counselor often supplies solutions rather than exploring Jamal’s own values, reasons, benefits, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A collaborative tone is established early, and the psychiatrist invites Jamal to explore options together and choose what feels doable. Partnership is undermined when the session shifts toward counselor-led problem-solving with many specific strategies and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked early through questions about desired outcomes and past attempts, and Jamal eventually mentions skipping the first drink. Change talk is not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened, and there is little use of importance, confidence, or commitment scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally acknowledges sustain talk rather than arguing, including Jamal’s fear of looking weak and wanting to keep his spot on the team. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened or redirected toward change, and the counselor often responds with tactical exit strategies instead of resolving the underlying ambivalence.
