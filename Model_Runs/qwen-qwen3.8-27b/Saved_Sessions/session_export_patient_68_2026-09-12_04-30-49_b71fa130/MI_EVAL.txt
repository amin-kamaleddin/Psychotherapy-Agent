# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed Kevin’s ambivalence and harm-reduction goals. Acceptance was strong, though occasionally procedural as the session moved quickly into practical problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used balanced reflections that captured Kevin’s enjoyment of social life and concern about losing control or being injured. Empathy could be deeper by exploring the emotional weight of his embarrassment and self-image.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing alcohol-related risk while preserving Kevin’s social life. The final plan was specific and behaviorally relevant, though the focus sometimes shifted toward logistics before motivation was fully developed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin’s ideas increasingly shaped the plan, including the buddy system, simple cue, and coat-grab strategy. Collaboration was limited early on when the psychiatrist presented a list of options before fully exploring Kevin’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Kevin’s freedom to choose and respected his boundaries, such as not requiring abstinence or strict planning. Autonomy support would be stronger with more emphasis on Kevin’s own decisions and less clinician-drafted language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used evocative techniques such as open-ended questions and a confidence ruler, but much of the session became advising rather than eliciting Kevin’s own motivations. Evocation would improve by drawing out Kevin’s values, discrepancy, and reasons for change before offering solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally took a collaborative stance by asking permission and inviting Kevin’s input. Partnership was weakened when the psychiatrist supplied many options early, creating a more advisor-like role than co-exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin expressed useful change talk about wanting to avoid another injury, protect his social life, and take control of risky nights. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify, summarize, and develop Kevin’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Kevin’s resistance to strict plans or quitting drinking. Softening could be stronger through double-sided reflections that help Kevin hold the value of social fun alongside reducing risky drinking.
