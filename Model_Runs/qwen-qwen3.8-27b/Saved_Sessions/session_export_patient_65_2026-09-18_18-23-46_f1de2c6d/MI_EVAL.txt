# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with specific, non-generic validation tied to Julian's actual words. However, the 'That makes sense / That's not nothing' pattern becomes somewhat formulaic by mid-session, and the lack of explicit acknowledgment of Julian's courage in engaging despite court mandates limits the depth to a 4.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate identification of emotional subtext, such as connecting 'stuck again' to a pattern of things happening to him and capturing his underlying need for agency and proof. The empathic depth could be stronger by exploring the shame or fear of judgment embedded in 'I don't want them to think I'm not serious,' as reflections occasionally prioritize cognitive content over emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus throughout, with every question and reflection building toward the concrete target of making the phone call. The progression from identifying what's stuck to scripting the opener to anchoring a specific time is clear, strategic, and free of topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The dialogue is genuinely two-sided, with Julian providing the content and the psychiatrist providing structure, and questions are exploratory rather than leading. Collaboration could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging Julian's expertise in his own situation and stating a working agreement about how they'll proceed together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Julian's input, offers choice of timing, and never imposes a timeline or uses the 'or else' as pressure. The approach is strong but could be further strengthened by more explicitly affirming Julian's right to NOT make the call, which would deepen the autonomy-affirming stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest aspect of the session, with the psychiatrist consistently eliciting Julian's own reasons, script, and plan rather than providing any external justification for change. Julian generates all of the change talk himself, culminating in a concrete plan articulated entirely in his own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the work as Julian's ('You just built a plan,' 'You wrote yourself a script') and avoids directive language or the expert role. However, the session direction remains somewhat counselor-led in its structuring (breaking the call into parts, asking for a specific time), which would benefit from more explicit negotiation of focus or acknowledgment that Julian could set the pace differently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Julian moves meaningfully from passive waiting to active planning, and the psychiatrist skillfully amplifies his own language to reinforce this shift. The gap is that a confidence ruler question (e.g., 'On a scale of 1-10, how confident do you feel about making that call?') would have further solidified ability talk and deepened the commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never argued against or dismissed; the psychiatrist acknowledges 'or else' as a heavy reason while immediately redirecting to Julian's agency, and second-guessing is reframed as evidence of care. The area for improvement is the moment Julian says 'it's not like I'm gonna do it right this second,' where the psychiatrist could have more actively pivoted to what IS happening in the present moment to reduce the weight of that statement.
