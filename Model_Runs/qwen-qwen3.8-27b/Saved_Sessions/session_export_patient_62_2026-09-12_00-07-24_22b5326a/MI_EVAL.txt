# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respectful, nonjudgmental support and validates Jordan’s difficulty. Acceptance is clear but somewhat brief and could include stronger unconditional positive regard for Jordan as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening for exhaustion and stuckness. Deeper reflections of shame, fear, self-punishment, and ambivalence were often missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, concrete focus on financial stress and a manageable next step. Direction is somewhat task-oriented and could be more clearly linked to broader values or goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choice and acknowledges preferences, but still controls much of the structure and task planning. More equal co-creation and use of Jordan’s own ideas would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses noncoercive language, offers choices, and supports Jordan’s pace. Autonomy support could be further strengthened by inviting Jordan to generate more of his own options and affirming his ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to evoke Jordan’s own motivations, values, or hope. The session is more solution-focused and supplies scripts/options rather than eliciting internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a partially collaborative stance by offering options and asking Jordan to choose. The interaction remains somewhat expert-driven and task-led rather than a fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand it. More values-focused, importance, confidence, or future-focused evocation would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is frequent, and the psychiatrist often responds with validation without strategically softening it. More double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or discrepancy work would be needed.
