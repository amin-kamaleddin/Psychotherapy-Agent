# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing with the patient's resistance. It stops short of a 5 because the warmth feels somewhat procedural and the frame remains oriented toward making medication more acceptable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflective listening to accurately track the patient's stated concerns. It is limited by being largely cognitive and problem-focused, with less reflection of the emotional texture behind the patient's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the target behavior of medication adherence for a chronic condition. There is coherent movement from abstract acceptability to a concrete daily routine, making this a clear strength.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is collaborative, using open questions and building on the patient's ideas such as breakfast, avoiding visual cues, and using a drawer. It is less than fully co-created because the psychiatrist still controls the flow through a fairly linear problem-solving sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by framing medication as something the patient can live with rather than a demand. It is not a 5 because the session still repeatedly assumes the goal is making the medication more acceptable or automatic.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes the patient's own practical preferences by asking how medication could be more acceptable, easier, or automatic. However, the evocation stays at a surface level and does not deeply elicit values, hopes, or stronger intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as a source of insight, especially by reflecting that the patient found the key himself. Partnership is slightly limited by a linear question structure and a prescribed path toward making medication more manageable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk by inviting the patient to consider a medication approach that fits his life and reflects back useful ideas. Cultivation is inconsistent and superficial, with little deliberate development of desire, ability, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and reframes concerns in terms of fit and long-term condition. However, the softening is not consistently active, and ambivalence is often acknowledged but left unresolved.
