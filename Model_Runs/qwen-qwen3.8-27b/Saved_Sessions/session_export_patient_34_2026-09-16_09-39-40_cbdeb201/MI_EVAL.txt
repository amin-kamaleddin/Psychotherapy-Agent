# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Dylan's frustration, low readiness, and boundaries without pressing. Warmth was somewhat procedural and did not consistently affirm strengths or self-worth beyond the immediate behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Dylan's perspective about age, wanting a warning, avoiding a record, and not wanting to be the buzzkill. Empathy remained mostly surface-level and did not fully explore deeper values, fears, or emotional tensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed largely focused on the party incident, future risk, and a practical behavioral plan. Some drift toward the conduct process reduced the focus on Dylan's own motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Dylan's input and co-constructed a plan around checking the host and using a roommate pickup. Collaboration was somewhat counselor-led because the psychiatrist supplied many concrete options and framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by asking Dylan's preferences, accepting his low readiness, and respecting his boundary against check-ins. Some early directive language and frequent option-giving limited fuller client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence scales elicited some change talk and a small step. Evocation was often superficial and option-driven rather than deeply drawing out Dylan's values, reasons, or commitment to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Dylan as a partner by seeking his perspective and letting his ideas shape the final plan. The interaction sometimes felt counselor-led due to clinical framing and many offered options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Dylan offered useful change talk, such as asking who is hosting, staying home if unfamiliar, and arranging a roommate pickup. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, deepen, or connect this change talk to Dylan's values and motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and used reflections that kept the conversation moving despite Dylan's minimizing statements. Sustain talk remained prominent, and the counselor sometimes mirrored it rather than using double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy.
