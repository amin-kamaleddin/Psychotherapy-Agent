# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Amina's self-awareness and struggle without judgment, affirming her effort and maintaining respect even when she doubts whether her plan is enough. Minor evaluative phrases like "real courage" could feel slightly over-invested but did not become pressuring or corrective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the emotional meaning of phone use, including loneliness, connection, and the difficulty of the gap between wanting sleep and being on screen. Strong perspective-taking names the dual function of scrolling without pathologizing it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear focus on nighttime phone use and its effects on sleep and coursework. Progression is coherent, moving from broad ambivalence to a specific, testable plan without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the counselor building on Amina's visual preference, show idea, and lock-screen collage rather than imposing alternatives. Client-generated ideas actively shape the next steps of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes choice, ownership, and a self-defined plan, accepting a small commitment as meaningful. This reduces pressure and supports Amina's perception that change comes from her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes Amina's own goals, values, and solutions, producing a client-owned plan. Some gentle solution framing and limited explicit elicitation of a full range of change talk prevent a perfect score.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session positions Amina as the expert on her own experience and invites her to define boundaries, routines, and success. Her ideas, rather than counselor prescriptions, drive the direction and content of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Amina's own change language and concrete plan, but sometimes uses suggestive framing and does not fully elicit broader change talk such as ability or confidence. Strong but not maximized.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and softened rather than argued with, preserving the legitimacy of reasons to continue scrolling. The main limitation is that the conversation sometimes stays with barriers without more consistently redirecting to values and change goals.
