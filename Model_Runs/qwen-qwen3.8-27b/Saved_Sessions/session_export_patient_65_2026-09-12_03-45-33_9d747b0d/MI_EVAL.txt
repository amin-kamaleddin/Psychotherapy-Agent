# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with normalizing and affirming statements. However, the warmth remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer reflects the patient’s factual and logistical perspective adequately. Emotional concerns such as fear of being judged and anxiety about making a mistake are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on completing the court-mandated hours and identifying practical barriers. The direction is strong but more logistical than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer offers multiple options and adapts to the patient’s stated preferences. However, he still does much of the intellectual work, and one email template is incomplete.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer repeatedly offers choices and respects the patient’s preferences without pressure. It could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing the patient’s freedom and ownership of follow-through.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interviewer asks one initial open question but then shifts mainly to practical planning. He does not deeply evoke the patient’s own reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer invites input and offers choices, creating a moderately collaborative stance. He does not fully position the patient as the expert driving the agenda or change process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to elicit or amplify change talk, as patient statements are mostly treated as logistical information. Importance, confidence, and commitment questions are largely absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer generally avoids arguing with the patient’s avoidance of calls and paperwork. At times, he accommodates avoidance rather than consistently reframing it toward commitment and change.
