# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, validating the client as a whole person without judgment or conditional regard. The opening framing and statements like 'you're not broken, you don't need saving' set a tone of acceptance that never wavers.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, nuanced reflections that capture emotional patterns (exhaustion, pride, guilt, feeling cornered) rather than simple paraphrasing. Each reflection builds on the last, progressively deepening the exploration of the client's inner experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear logical progression from rapport to exploring the trade-off to evoking small steps. The connection between the daughter tension and the smoking cessation goal could have been made more explicit at a few intermediate points.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine dialogue between equals with no imposed agenda or hierarchy. Content is entirely shaped by the client's contributions, and the psychiatrist actively seeks input before moving forward.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering reinforcement of client ownership and choice throughout; the psychiatrist never prescribes, advises, or pushes. Respects the client's pace by allowing her to sit with her own realizations without rushing toward action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every insight in the session emerges from the client herself — the trade-off, the 'bad deal,' and the small step of reducing. The psychiatrist never provides reasons for change, only asks questions that create conditions for motivation to surface.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within the client, explicitly attributing key insights to her ('That's not me telling you, that's you seeing it clearly'). Questions invite the client to generate her own answers rather than providing information.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully moves the client from resistance through contemplation to early preparation without pressure. The final question about 'what would you be doing instead' directly evokes readiness and leads to concrete, client-generated action talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with exceptional skill — never argues, dismisses, or minimizes, but uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge the value while gently pivoting toward change. The 'five minutes of quiet' reflection is a masterful example of rolling with resistance.
