# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance toward Kieran’s caution and desire for simple treatment. The warmth is consistent, though it can feel somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Kieran’s conflict between compliance, autonomy, and frustration. Empathy is strong but occasionally moves to problem-solving before fully exploring deeper emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on disengagement, compliance frustration, and making sessions feel less like an obligation. Progression could be stronger by consolidating needs into a specific, agreed-upon change behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kieran’s ideas shape the session topics, and the psychiatrist avoids an authoritarian stance. However, collaboration is uneven, with limited explicit co-creation of a plan or joint decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, ownership, and low pressure while avoiding direct advice. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly separating mandated requirements from personal values.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out important values such as autonomy, respect, privacy, and being treated as a person. However, the evocation does not consistently develop internal motivation or confidence for concrete change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Kieran as the expert on his own life and asks what would make sessions useful and more like his choice. Partnership is present but not fully realized because the psychiatrist still drives pace through repeated similar questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions often elicit complaints, constraints, or sustain talk rather than clear change talk about the target behavior. Small change-oriented preferences are acknowledged but not amplified or developed into stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and acknowledges Kieran’s realistic constraints. However, reflections often remain anchored in rules and constraints without stronger strategic reframing toward change.
