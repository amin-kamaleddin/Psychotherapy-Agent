# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids pressuring the patient to remove the piercing. It could be stronger by adding more individualized positive regard beyond validating the decision.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The reflections accurately capture the patient's perspective about aesthetics, autonomy, and not missing a real problem. Empathy would be stronger if it explored the deeper emotional or identity-related meaning of the piercing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the piercing and oral health concerns without drifting into unrelated topics. It would be more effective if the direction also included clearer movement toward risk reduction or possible change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well in building a simple monitoring plan using the patient's own criteria. It would be stronger by giving the patient even more ownership over setting and prioritizing the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects the patient's freedom to choose and avoids persuasion or imposed advice. The conversation is strongly centered on the patient's decision-making authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The questions draw out the patient's own monitoring plan and criteria. They would be more therapeutically effective if they evoked deeper motivation for change rather than mainly reinforcing the status quo.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and recognizes the patient as the expert on her own experience. The partnership would be stronger if the patient had even more control over the session structure and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session does not strongly elicit or develop statements supporting change. More deliberate reflection and development of the patient's ambivalence about protecting her smile would be needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tends to affirm the patient's decision to keep the piercing rather than gently balancing it with oral health concerns. A stronger approach would use double-sided reflections to acknowledge the piercing while exploring possible change.
