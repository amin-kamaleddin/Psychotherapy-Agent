# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, using supportive statements to reduce shame. However, the warmth is somewhat generic and the early risk information feels more didactic than relationally accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor makes reasonable efforts to understand the client’s perspective and uses some accurate reflections. However, empathy remains surface-level, especially when the client identifies smoking as her only meaningful break.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and moves toward a concrete, manageable next step. Repetition near the end slightly reduces momentum, but the overall direction is strong.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor incorporates some of the client’s practical ideas and adjusts the plan based on her feedback. However, the session still feels more clinician-driven than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and uses low-pressure language, but also repeatedly gives a fairly prescriptive plan. Stronger autonomy support would include more explicit reminders that the client is in charge of her decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks a few questions but does not consistently draw out the client’s own reasons, values, or motivation for change. Much of the session relies on advice rather than evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input and adapts to her concerns, which supports partnership. However, the overall stance remains expert-driven rather than truly equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, but it is not consistently deepened, amplified, or reinforced. The counselor often moves quickly into advice and plan-making instead of exploring the client’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or shaming the client’s sustain talk and uses normalizing language. However, she does not consistently pivot sustain talk into change talk or use double-sided reflections.
