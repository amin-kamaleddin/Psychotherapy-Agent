# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and non-judgment, validating Maria's corrections without defensiveness. However, it occasionally shifts focus toward the psychiatrist's own emotional state (e.g., "I'm really scared for you") rather than maintaining pure acceptance of Maria's experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate capture of ambivalence and somatic experience (e.g., "the fear that you can't stop, and the evidence that you're still here"). However, the empathy occasionally remains at the level of paraphrasing rather than accessing the deeper emotional texture in key moments.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session follows a logical progression from exploring suicidal ideation through body sensations to ambivalence and safety check. However, the direction lacks strategic purposefulness, sometimes circling without clear momentum toward a specific change goal or collaboratively established focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a genuinely collaborative stance, building on Maria's contributions and responding well to her pushback. However, it could be stronger in explicitly co-constructing understanding (e.g., "So together we're seeing that...").

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy support is present through non-directive questions and avoidance of advice-giving. However, it lacks explicit affirmations of Maria's right to make her own choices or her capacity for self-direction, and the safety check could have been framed in a more autonomy-supporting way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several effective evocative questions (e.g., "What's its job?") successfully draw out meaningful content from Maria. However, the evocation is not consistently developed—when Maria offers change talk, the psychiatrist acknowledges it but doesn't immediately deepen it with scaling or follow-up questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance using language that positions Maria as the expert on her own experience, without imposing an agenda. However, it occasionally drives the conversation with consecutive questions without pausing to check if Maria wants to go in a different direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some efforts to evoke change talk, particularly through the "What's its job?" question and highlighting "I keep showing up here." However, it doesn't consistently build on or amplify Maria's change talk statements, and uses few scaling or importance questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One good example of softening ("when the pain feels like it'll last forever, 'at least' starts to make a certain sense") normalizes the logic without endorsing it. However, the psychiatrist generally handles sustain talk neutrally rather than actively redirecting with double-sided reflections or strategic reframing.
