# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepted Jonathan’s concerns without challenging resistance. Validation was solid but somewhat procedural, lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, accurately capturing Jonathan’s desire to manage diabetes while protecting time and avoiding burden. The final empathic response was generic and missed a chance to reflect his frustration about being managed or graded.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on diabetes self-management and helped narrow the problem into a small, specific step. However, toward the end the focus drifted toward Jonathan’s overwhelm without redirecting back to the identified action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction largely felt collaborative, with Jonathan’s ideas shaping the plan and the psychiatrist asking before proposing solutions. Collaboration was slightly reduced by occasional clinician-led proposals and a final generic question instead of deeper co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by asking what would work, what would feel doable, and normalizing low-commitment options. Specific structure proposals were offered as options, but autonomy support could have been stronger in reinforcing Jonathan’s freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out some of Jonathan’s own preferences for a small, easy trial. Evocation was limited by superficial questioning and a failure to access deeper values, especially after his strong disclosure about feeling merely compliant.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jonathan was treated as the expert on his own life, and the session was largely shaped by his ideas and priorities. Partnership was slightly reduced when the psychiatrist introduced structured suggestions rather than fully eliciting Jonathan’s own plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited, particularly around small steps such as weekends only or a one-week trial. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk to strengthen Jonathan’s motivation and sense of capability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged rather than argued with sustain talk and occasionally reframed it in a neutral way. However, the session remained anchored in Jonathan’s reasons against change, with limited use of double-sided reflections or pivots linking his values to change.
