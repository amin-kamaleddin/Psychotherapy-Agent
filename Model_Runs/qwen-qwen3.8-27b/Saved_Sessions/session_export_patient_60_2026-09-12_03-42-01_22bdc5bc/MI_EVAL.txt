# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoids shaming Jason. Warmth is generally good but somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Jason’s main concerns about normalcy, school, and feeling like a suspect. Empathy could go deeper by exploring embarrassment, fear, anger, or the tension between wanting normalcy and being taken seriously.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete alcohol-related next step for the upcoming event. Direction is clear and efficient, though somewhat narrow and procedure-focused rather than linked to broader values or long-term motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some input and offers options, but largely supplies the plan and tactics. Jason’s own ideas and agenda-setting are underused, making the session more expert-guided than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes choice, non-coercion, and Jason’s ability to decide. There is still some structure and mild closure tied to performance, which slightly limits full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions elicit practical next steps and confidence scaling. Evocation remains shallow because deeper values, identity, and intrinsic reasons for change are not sufficiently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership through questions and invitations to choose doable options. However, the psychiatrist frequently sets the agenda and provides a ready-made strategy, limiting a true equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jason offers concrete change talk, such as not drinking at the next event and using a filled cup. The change talk stays narrow and focused on avoiding consequences rather than deeper values or self-identity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Jason’s minimization and redirects to practical planning. Sustain talk is not actively softened or explored, and some statements may inadvertently validate the avoidant framing.
