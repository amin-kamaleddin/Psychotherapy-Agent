# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes slipping without pressure. Affirmations are consistently positive but could be more individualized and emotionally attuned rather than somewhat formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track the client's practical ambivalence and concrete preferences. Deeper empathy would more explicitly name the underlying fear, guilt, exhaustion, or self-judgment behind the client's caveats.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on one target behavior and quickly narrows to a specific, doable experiment. Guidance is clear and purposeful without drifting or over-directing the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist offers options while letting the client decide the specifics, resulting in a jointly constructed plan. The final plan is a co-created product rather than something imposed by the therapist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, avoids controlling language, and validates the client's right to avoid rigid rules. This reinforces ownership of the process rather than compliance with an external prescription.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist repeatedly asks the client to identify doable changes, confidence, fallbacks, and signs of success, producing a largely client-generated plan. Even brief psychoeducation quickly returns to the client's own preferences and motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the expert on her own life, and the final plan emerges from her choices. Closing language such as 'we'll troubleshoot' reinforces an equal, collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, scaling, and plan-focused prompts effectively elicit change talk and increased confidence. The response could be stronger by more consistently amplifying and deepening specific change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about low energy, skipping, and possible failure is met with normalizing, nonargumentative responses. A stronger response would more consistently pivot that sustain talk back to the client's values and motivation for change.
