# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone but repeatedly re-offers plans after Adrian declines them. This limits warmth and can feel controlling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some empathic reflections of annoyance and the need for control, but they remain superficial. Important emotions such as shame, fear of hospitalization, and ambivalence are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on methamphetamine use and nasal health without wandering. However, the direction is mainly medical risk reduction rather than MI-focused exploration toward a client-defined goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some early collaboration in inviting Adrian's perspective, but the conversation quickly becomes expert-driven. The psychiatrist's clinical agenda dominates over Adrian's stated preference to wait, use spray, and see how it goes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy-supportive language is present, such as "if you want" and "I respect that." Repeated plan offers after refusal may feel directive and fail to fully emphasize that no plan is required.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions create some space for evocation, but the session mainly shifts to advice rather than eliciting Adrian's own motivations. There is limited exploration of values, fears, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially invites input but becomes increasingly directive with repeated medical instructions. Adrian's concern about being advised or labeled is not used to reshape the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Adrian offers several change-related statements about healing his nose, avoiding hospitalization, and taking a week off. These are acknowledged but not expanded, reinforced, or turned into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Adrian's sustain talk, which is appropriate, but also does not effectively soften it. There are no double-sided reflections or gentle explorations of the gap between control and health consequences.
