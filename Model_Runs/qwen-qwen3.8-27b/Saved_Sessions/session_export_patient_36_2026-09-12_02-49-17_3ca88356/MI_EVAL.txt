# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed warmth, validation, and nonjudgment when affirming hesitation and a low-pressure plan. A perfect score would include even more explicit unconditional positive regard independent of whether the client follows through.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used good reflective listening to capture the client’s motivation, clarity, and concern about pressure. Reflections could be deepened around fear of self-criticism and people-pleasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session kept a clear focus on developing a small, realistic self-care ritual with practical follow-through. It could more consistently connect logistical details to deeper values and reduce focus on mechanics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was interactive and the client actively shaped the ritual, timing, cues, and check-in. More client-generated agenda-setting and fewer initial options would make it feel more equal-partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported choice and ownership by offering optional supports and accepting declines without pressure. Language reinforced client agency, such as “you picked” and “trust your gut.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out the client’s own motivations, confidence, and preference for a low-pressure, self-directed approach. It could more consistently evoke and amplify values-based motivation related to self-respect and self-worth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and repeatedly invited the client’s input, allowing her preferences to shape the plan. Some counselor-generated options made the interaction somewhat guided rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited client-generated change talk through open-ended questions about the plan, confidence, barriers, and success cues. More strategic amplification of values-based change talk would strengthen this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was limited and appropriately handled without reinforcing the client’s worry that the plan would become an obligation. More double-sided reflections or gentle pivots could move sustain talk more skillfully toward change.
