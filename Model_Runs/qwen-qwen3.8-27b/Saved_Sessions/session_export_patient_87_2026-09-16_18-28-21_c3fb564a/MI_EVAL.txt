# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment while validating the client's effort and small steps. Acceptance is slightly limited by occasional interpretive inferences and incomplete normalization of past failed attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflections that capture the client's ambivalence and burden accurately. The reflections could be more effective if they more directly deepened the underlying shame, fear, and self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior of mindful eating at the family dinner table. The psychiatrist guides the conversation toward a concrete and achievable change without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the client's ideas shaping the session rather than a therapist-imposed plan. The final step feels co-created and grounded in the client's own language and concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's sense of choice and reduces pressure by emphasizing small, self-selected steps. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by occasional sample phrases and framing tied to external standards such as health and family.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly elicits the client's own motivation through open questions and reflections. The process is somewhat limited by a leading question offering specific phrase options and less explicit exploration of values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's perspective and allows the client's contributions to shape the discussion. Partnership is not fully optimal because the therapist still sets the agenda and uses a sequence of leading questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes concrete change talk and reinforces the client's small, workable steps. Cultivating change talk could be stronger by more systematically amplifying confidence, commitment, and the client's reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by reframing barriers in a less threatening way rather than arguing against them. Softening sustain talk is not perfect because some questions keep exploring obstacles, and the client's final sustain question remains unresolved.
