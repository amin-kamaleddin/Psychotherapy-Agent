# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect and nonjudgment, especially around Linda’s ambivalence about smoking. However, some reflections feel generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and often accurately captures Linda’s conflict and emotions. The score is limited by an early inaccurate reflection and some repetitive responses that do not always deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally remains focused on smoking, stress management, and the impact on her daughter. However, the conversation occasionally drifts into abstract feelings and multiple competing options, weakening a clear collaborative focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting Linda’s input and following her preferences. Still, repeated use of therapist-provided options and interventions can make the interaction feel less equally co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda’s choice and lack of pressure are frequently emphasized. The score is not higher because several structured suggestions subtly steer the session away from full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some important values and motivations from Linda. However, there is less consistent follow-through to expand Linda’s own change talk rather than introducing therapist-driven ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Linda’s lead and treats her as the expert on her own experience. The partnership is slightly weakened by moments where the therapist introduces structured options that can feel agenda-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some values-based change talk is evoked, especially around being a role model for her daughter. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or consolidate this change talk to build stronger momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Linda’s desire to keep smoking. However, repeated focus on the benefits of smoking can unintentionally reinforce sustain talk rather than soften it.
