# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician was warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, affirming the client for reaching out and validating frustration. It fell short of a 5 because the acceptance was somewhat procedural and did not deeply affirm the client's worth or self-direction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used good reflective listening to capture the client's frustration and feeling judged. It was not a 5 because the reflections sometimes repeated similar themes without exploring deeper meaning, autonomy, or belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session began with a relevant focus on drinking, safety, and responses to others, but often drifted toward interpersonal frustration. Direction was inconsistent because the clinician did not consistently consolidate themes or rebuild momentum toward the alcohol-related goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used inclusive language and presented options rather than instructions, creating a generally collaborative tone. It was not a 5 because the collaboration remained somewhat structured around clinician-generated options, with the client mainly influencing the decision to end.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support was strong, with frequent choices, respect for the client's decision to stop, and no pressure to practice scripts. It was not a 5 because the focus was mainly on respecting the client's desire to stop rather than reinforcing her broader capacity for change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asked some open evocative questions, but the work often became assessment- or problem-solving-focused. Deeper exploration of the client's values, confidence, and intrinsic reasons for change was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a non-expert, collaborative stance and invited client input on next steps. It was not a 5 because the client was not explicitly positioned as the expert in her own life or fully co-creating the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered some spontaneous confidence statements, but the clinician did not consistently reflect, amplify, or develop them into stronger change talk. Scaling, values exploration, and deeper evocation of motivation were largely absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician validated the client's status quo statements without arguing, which was appropriate for the session. However, there was limited use of double-sided reflections or broader evocation to soften sustain talk and explore ambivalence more deeply.
