# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with multiple affirmations of Samuel's honesty and effort. Lacks the depth of genuine individualized positive regard and explicit normalization of struggle for a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides some reflective listening and accurate summaries of ambivalence, but reflections are sometimes repetitive and don't delve into deeper emotional nuances. Misses opportunities to explore the emotional pain of repeated failure and depth of social loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on smoking cessation and related ambivalence throughout. Occasionally drifts into exploratory dialogue without consistent strategic guidance toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good collaborative stance with consistent framing as joint exploration and active seeking of Samuel's input. Collaboration is somewhat superficial and lacks the depth of genuine individualized partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently acknowledges Samuel's right to make his own choices and avoids imposing advice or coercive language. Could be strengthened by explicitly emphasizing Samuel's ownership of both successes and setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally asks about reasons for quitting and possible next steps, but evocation is limited in depth and frequency. Does not consistently elicit and develop client change talk throughout the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive language and frequent invitations for Samuel's input. Partnership is somewhat superficial without explicit power-sharing or co-created plan demonstrating full equality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally evokes change talk through questions about reasons and benefits, and Samuel offers some desire/reason/need statements. Does not consistently reinforce or deepen change talk, nor explicitly elicit commitment or ability language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (attachment to smoking, social aspects) without reinforcing it, but responses are mostly neutral reflections. Lacks double-sided reflections or strategic redirection that would actively soften sustain talk toward change.
