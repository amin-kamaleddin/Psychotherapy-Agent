# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, validating Yasmin's frustration and affirming her honesty without labeling. Affirmations are well-crafted but could be slightly more individualized and personal to push to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with complex, layered reflections that track the emotional undercurrent rather than surface content. Effectively extends the client's own metaphors (e.g., 'the finish line keeps moving') to deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from body dissatisfaction through identification of patterns to exploring a non-diet approach. Could benefit from more explicit summarization of themes and directional checkpoints as the session progresses.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates fully collaborative MI practice with a tone of mutual curiosity rather than expert-to-patient. Picks up on the client's language and extends it, signaling willingness to be guided by her experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — never pressures or implies a correct answer, consistently frames all choices as Yasmin's to make. Key questions place the definition of effort and 'a real try' entirely in her hands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within the client rather than providing external reasons for change. The progression from 'I don't know the alternative' to a client-generated vision of what a 'real try' could look like is entirely facilitated through skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with open-ended questions and avoids imposing a framework or agenda. Could be slightly stronger by more explicitly acknowledging the client's expertise in her own experience (e.g., 'You know yourself best').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategically asks questions that elicit rich change talk and skillfully amplifies it when it appears (e.g., 'That's not settling. That's you saying this is what I actually like'). Could more explicitly draw out ability and confidence statements and consolidate the change talk before the session ends.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by acknowledging it without arguing and reframing it as a fear-based response rather than a true motivation. The strongest sustain talk ('a nicer way to fail') could have been addressed with a more explicit double-sided reflection that honors both sides while tilting toward change.
