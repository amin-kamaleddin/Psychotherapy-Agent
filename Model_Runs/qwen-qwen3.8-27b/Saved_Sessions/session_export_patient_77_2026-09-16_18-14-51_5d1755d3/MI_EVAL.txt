# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while honoring the client's boundaries. Acceptance is strong but somewhat structured, with less explicit affirmation of the client's worth independent of progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and nuanced empathic understanding of the client's emotional experience. Reflections capture subtle themes of feeling misunderstood, burdened, unseen, and cautious about being perceived as dramatic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general thread around the presenting concern but becomes somewhat meta-focused on how to speak with the client. There is limited summarizing, linking of effort to values, or collaborative identification of a concrete target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and co-constructs the therapeutic process with the client. The client's feedback directly shapes the counselor's language and approach in real time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's sense of choice and avoids pressure, advice-giving, or evaluation. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement of the client's ownership over decisions, successes, and setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions effectively draw out the client's needs, preferences, and language. Evocation could be stronger by more explicitly eliciting change talk related to values, confidence, self-efficacy, or a preferred future.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert on her own experience and effort. The client's contributions substantially shape the session, including the therapeutic approach and specific language used.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, but it remains mainly about effort, willingness, and persistence. The session does not systematically cultivate deeper change talk around reasons, benefits, values, confidence, or readiness for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively avoids challenging or reinforcing the client's skepticism and instead honors her boundaries. A slightly stronger approach would use double-sided reflections to more actively transform sustain talk into change language.
