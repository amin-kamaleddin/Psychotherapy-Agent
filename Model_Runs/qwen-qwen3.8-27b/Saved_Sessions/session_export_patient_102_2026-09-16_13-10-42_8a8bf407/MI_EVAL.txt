# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepted Milan’s choices without shaming or pressure. Acceptance was slightly limited by a practical risk-management agenda that occasionally felt procedural rather than fully emotionally normalizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used good reflective listening and accurately captured Milan’s social priorities and ambivalence. Empathy remained somewhat surface-level, missing deeper emotions such as fear of judgment, shame, or frustration about being controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed connected to drinking and socializing but repeatedly shifted into practical planning, scripts, and check-in logistics. It did not consistently guide Milan toward clarifying his own goals, values, and reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language, offered choices, and respected Milan’s refusal to practice or check in. Collaboration was effective but narrow, focusing mainly on practical strategies rather than Milan’s deeper values and concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support was strong, with repeated affirmations that Milan was in charge and that no choice was being forced. The repeated introduction of specific scripts and options slightly undercut autonomy by making the counselor more solution-oriented.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some client-driven thinking early with open questions about likes, worries, and values. However, the session shifted too quickly to supplying strategies and scripts rather than consistently drawing out Milan’s own motivations and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveyed partnership by asking permission, offering options, and returning decisions to Milan. The session was still largely agenda-driven by the psychiatrist, especially around strategies and follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some effective attempts to evoke change talk, including Milan’s statement that he does not want to be the guy who is buzzed all the time. The psychiatrist did not sufficiently amplify or deepen this change talk before moving into practical problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and responded to sustain talk with acceptance rather than confrontation. However, Milan’s minimization was not consistently softened or used as an opportunity to explore tension between his values and behaviors.
