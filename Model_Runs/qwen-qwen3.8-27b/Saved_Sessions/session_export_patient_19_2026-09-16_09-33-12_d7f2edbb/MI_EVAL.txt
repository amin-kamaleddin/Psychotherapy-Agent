# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, while also honoring Anya’s preference for simplicity. It is not a 5/5 because warmth is often tied to task completion rather than fully validating her identity, ambivalence, and shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Anya’s ambivalence, financial worries, and discomfort with performing or being “boring.” Deeper empathy would require more nuanced, double-sided reflections of her underlying values, social concerns, and emotional conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior: reducing gambling, protecting money, and making a concrete plan. It can become somewhat task-driven, moving to problem-solving before fully clarifying what matters most to Anya.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anya’s ideas meaningfully shape the final plan, including the cash jar, “saving for a trip” line, Sasha, and alarm phrase. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist often supplies options and scripts before fully exploring what Anya has already tried or would prefer.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking what feels doable, respecting boundaries, and allowing Anya to decide the amount, day, support friend, and reminder. It falls short of a 5/5 because some directive language still makes the plan feel partly therapist-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and scaling questions successfully elicit motivation, confidence, and client-generated ideas. Evocation is not consistently developed, as the psychiatrist often shifts from Anya’s change talk to advice and practical steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites choices and allows Anya to shape the plan, showing a collaborative stance. However, the psychiatrist often remains in the expert role by offering options and solutions before first drawing out Anya’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk around travel, avoiding long-term debt, and confidence increases. It would be stronger if change talk were more consistently amplified, summarized, and expanded before introducing new strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and occasionally acknowledges the social pull of the pub and friends. More effective softening would use double-sided reflections to connect fun, connection, and autonomy to long-term goals without debate.
