# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating concern about smoking and lapses without moralizing. Warmth is strong but somewhat task-focused rather than deeply affirming the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture her tension between social connection and health worry, fear of awkwardness, and tendency to give in. They often remain surface-level and do not fully explore belonging, rejection, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing smoking in social situations and narrows to concrete behaviors such as stepping inside, using a brief script, and checking the phone. Focus is clear, though more behaviorally tactical than motivationally deep.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise chooses several elements of the plan, including stepping inside and using her phone. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, making it more clinician-led problem-solving than true MI collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and avoids direct pressure, including self-compassion if a drag occurs. Slight push toward committing and many detailed suggestions can make the plan feel somewhat predetermined.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to draw out Elise's internal motivations, values, or confidence for change, with the session shifting quickly to strategies. Change talk is not consistently elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise's input shapes the final plan, showing some power sharing and influence. The psychiatrist remains the primary source of ideas and strategies, so partnership is uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening questions invite some discussion, but there is little systematic use of importance/confidence rulers, DARN-CAT, or values exploration. Valuable change-related statements are not developed further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully without arguing or judgment. It is not strategically softened or redirected toward change through double-sided reflections or curiosity about the function of smoking.
