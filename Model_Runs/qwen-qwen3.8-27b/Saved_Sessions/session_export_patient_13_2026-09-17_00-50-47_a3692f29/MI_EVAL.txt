# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental warmth. Validating statements reduce shame and make the client feel safely heard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and emotional attunement to the client’s fear and ambivalence. Some reflections are slightly leading or formulaic, which limits depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear health-related target but shifts toward emotional safety and relational pacing. More consistent reorientation toward a concrete step in medical care is needed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint language and invites the client to co-create a safer process for the next visit. The collaboration is strong, though the clinician still organizes and leads the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and respects the client’s pace and choice. It would be stronger with more explicit statements that the decision about care is entirely the client’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite the client to express concerns and preferences. The evocation focuses more on emotional safety than on eliciting the client’s own values, reasons, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to direct the session and uses inclusive, non-directive language. A perfect score would include more explicit recognition of the client as the expert on her own life and agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers several possible change steps, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or consolidate them. More systematic development of DARN-C change talk is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to barriers and avoidance without arguing, dismissing, or reinforcing them. The session could be stronger by more actively connecting the client’s fears to her own reasons for moving toward care.
