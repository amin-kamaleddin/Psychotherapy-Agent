# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session. The psychiatrist never labels or disapproves, and affirmations like 'That's not nothing' communicate genuine respect for Mateo's internal resources.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex, accurate, and progressive—capturing emotional meaning beneath surface content (e.g., 'It's not just the joints that are hurting you; it's the hope that got worn down along with them'). Mateo's ambivalence is consistently honored without being reduced to resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows a clear, progressive arc from exploring pain and discouragement through evoking values, identifying the self-critical voice as a barrier, to a concrete, client-generated action plan. Every question and reflection ties back to the target behavior of increasing physical activity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The power dynamic is genuinely balanced; the psychiatrist uses 'I'm curious' and 'I'm wondering' to signal joint exploration rather than leading. Mateo's ideas consistently shape the session's direction, with the psychiatrist adding value through observation rather than steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never prescribes or tells Mateo what to do, explicitly removing pressure ('Not which is better, just which feels more like something you'd actually do'). Ideas are consistently attributed back to Mateo, and the final decision is entirely his.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change are elicited from within Mateo—his values, his insight about the self-critical voice, and his own solutions. The evocation is progressive and layered, moving from values to insight to strategy to a concrete, self-owned commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a collaborative exploration where expertise and wisdom reside within Mateo, as shown by questions like 'What do you think?' and explicit acknowledgment of his self-generated ideas. The psychiatrist adds almost no information or advice of his own.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mateo generates substantial change talk across values, insight, and concrete action, which the psychiatrist skillfully reflects and amplifies. The evocation is progressive, moving from the walk with his wife to identifying the voice as the barrier to a specific, self-generated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk and self-criticism are gently reframed without invalidation (e.g., 'You're not refusing to keep going — you're asking why it has to feel this way'). The self-critical voice is isolated as separate from Mateo's actual capacity, making it something he can work with rather than an identity.
