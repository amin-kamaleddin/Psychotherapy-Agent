# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone with clear normalizing responses to the patient's concerns about missing doses. To reach a 5, the counselor could add more explicit, person-centered affirmations of the patient's worth and effort beyond the medication task.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was adequate and helped clarify the patient's ambivalence, but it remained largely content-based rather than fully emotionally attuned. The counselor could have more deeply named feelings such as fear of failure, overwhelm, and the tension between wanting to protect health and feeling burdened by demands.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on medication adherence for diabetes and remained practical without drifting into unrelated topics. A higher score would come from more explicitly linking each planning step back to the patient's broader long-term health goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative, with the patient's preferences shaping the final plan, including the alarm, nut packs, no formal tracking, and check-in time. It was not a 5 because the psychiatrist remained the primary problem-solver by offering specific options before fully eliciting the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported choice, framed the plan as a low-pressure one-week experiment, and respected the patient's preference to avoid tracking. The slight limitation was the somewhat prescriptive advice about medication timing and implementation steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked some evocative questions but often moved quickly into practical problem-solving. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, and internal reasons for change would strengthen the MI process.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited patient input and allowed her preferences to influence the plan, but also adopted a fairly expert role by offering a list of solutions. To strengthen partnership, the counselor could more explicitly treat the patient as the expert and invite her to generate options first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited some change talk and reinforced it with positive statements, but did not consistently deepen it by exploring importance, confidence, values, or commitment. The session could better expand the patient's own reasons for change before shifting to practical solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalized sustain talk and avoided arguing or confrontation, which helped keep the session safe. More skillful softening would involve double-sided reflections that acknowledge the difficulty while gently reconnecting adherence to the patient's health goals.
