# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes the patient's fear and self-doubt. However, some praise ties worth to effort and progress rather than expressing fully unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects ambivalence, fear, and the core dilemma of wanting to change while fearing failure. The main limitation is moving too quickly into problem-solving before fully exploring shame and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear focus on a concrete, observable behavior: starting a 10-minute practice session with a hard stop. It is somewhat narrow in its strategic focus on deeper motivational issues like self-trust and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is meaningfully co-created using the patient's ideas, such as the 7:50 alarm and 10-minute stop. However, the counselor still supplies many scripts, tools, and decision frameworks rather than more explicitly sharing power.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices, avoids pressure, and normalizes that the patient might close the laptop. Some affirmations still subtly reinforce the preferred direction and could more explicitly state that the patient owns the decision either way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some evocative questions about readiness, barriers, and confidence. However, the evocation is mostly practical and misses deeper opportunities to explore values, self-efficacy, and the meaning of the certification.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites the patient's input and incorporates her contributions into the plan. The interaction is collaborative but remains somewhat expert-guided in setting the agenda and offering options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor affirms the patient's change-related statements but does not consistently deepen or amplify them. There are missed opportunities to evoke the patient's own reasons, needs, and commitment more fully.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor responds nonconfrontationally to resistance and avoids arguing with the patient's fear of failure. However, the session does not strategically redirect sustain talk and final ambivalence toward change.
