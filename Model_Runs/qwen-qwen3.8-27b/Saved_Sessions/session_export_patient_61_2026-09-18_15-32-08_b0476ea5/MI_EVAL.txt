# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent respect and non-judgment throughout, never lecturing or minimizing Jonathan's frustration. However, the warmth remains somewhat professional and measured, and affirmations feel slightly formulaic rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy throughout, with reflections that capture not just content but the emotional undercurrent with remarkable precision. Reflections consistently go beyond simple paraphrase into complex, accurate summaries of Jonathan's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on diabetes self-management with a logical progression from understanding overwhelm to exploring specific strategies. Could benefit from a clearer sense of what session success looks like or a co-created roadmap.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a collaborative interaction, following Jonathan's lead when he names specific strategies and building on those rather than redirecting. The collaboration is more implicit than explicit—could ask Jonathan to evaluate reflections or suggest next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy with appropriate language around choice and never prescribes or tells Jonathan what he should do. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Jonathan's right to choose differently and naming his decision-making capacity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Makes strong evocative moves, consistently drawing out Jonathan's own ideas rather than providing advice or information. However, stays at the surface level of practical strategies without tapping into deeper motivational drivers like values or long-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and avoids an expert-driven approach, never providing unsolicited advice. Could more explicitly invite Jonathan to co-create the session agenda or share what he'd want from the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes efforts to elicit change talk and notices shifts, but the evocation is limited in scope with no systematic exploration of desires, abilities, needs, or values. No work on confidence/efficacy or explicit elicitation of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating and gently pivoting toward exploration rather than arguing or pushing back. Could be more strategic with double-sided reflections that simultaneously acknowledge legitimate reasons for staying the same and open a door toward change.
