# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, directly countering self-critical statements without scolding the client for his choices. The stance is supportive, though it could be deeper and less formulaic in affirming the client’s worth and struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several accurate summaries of the client’s situation, but empathy remains mostly content-level. The psychiatrist missed opportunities to reflect deeper feelings related to identity, belonging, exclusion, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and keeps the discussion linked to concrete change steps. The strong action orientation can reduce space for exploring the client’s core ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas shape much of the plan, and his language is effectively incorporated to make the plan feel owned. However, the interaction remains partly expert-driven due to frequent structured options and implementation details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking permission, offering options, and respecting the client’s limits. The final phase becomes more directive in specifying exact steps, which slightly weakens the emphasis on client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes motivation through scaling and evocative questions, and much of the plan emerges from the client. Evocation would be stronger if it more deeply connected to the client’s values, identity, and long-term self-image.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows the client to make several choices, showing partial partnership. The interaction remains somewhat counselor-led because the psychiatrist repeatedly introduces ideas, options, and detailed steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and reinforces the client’s change talk, especially using readiness and confidence scales. The change talk could be consolidated more thoroughly and connected to values, identity, and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client’s barriers and reframes several of them in a workable way. However, the central ambivalence around identity, belonging, and social acceptance is not fully processed or explored.
