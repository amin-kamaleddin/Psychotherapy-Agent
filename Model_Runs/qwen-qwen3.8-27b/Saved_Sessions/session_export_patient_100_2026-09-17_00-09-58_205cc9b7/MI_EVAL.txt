# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming, labeling, or arguing with Maya’s minimization of alcohol use. Minor reliance on repetitive acceptance language and one lightly counselor-driven characterization keeps it from a perfect 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately mirroring exhaustion, embarrassment, guilt, and the function of drinking. Some reflections are slightly interpretive and do not consistently explore deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and stress, narrowing a broad concern into a specific moment and possible self-monitoring marker. It remains somewhat exploratory at the end and does not clearly consolidate a concrete, client-owned next step or follow-up target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is framed as a partnership, with Maya’s preferences shaping the focus, pace, and method of exploration. The psychiatrist acts as a sounding board rather than an expert and adapts well to the client’s stated boundaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong and consistent, with repeated emphasis on Maya’s choice, pacing, and right to define the work. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, persuasion, and fixing, while respecting boundaries against a lecture or formal recovery plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn primarily from Maya through open questions and client-led exploration rather than through supplied reasons to change. The psychiatrist could strengthen evocation by using more affirmations, summaries, and scaling questions to deepen the client’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Maya as the expert on her own life and choices, inviting input rather than directing the session. Client-led collaboration is clear, with Maya’s concerns and boundaries directly shaping the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps Maya generate useful change talk by exploring first steps, warning signs, and possible pause strategies. However, the client’s change talk is not consistently amplified, affirmed, or developed through stronger strategic reflections or systematic DARN-C evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by accepting Maya’s perspective without arguing, reinforcing, or making it the focus. The response could be stronger with more explicit double-sided reflections that hold both the benefit of drinking and the possible costs.
