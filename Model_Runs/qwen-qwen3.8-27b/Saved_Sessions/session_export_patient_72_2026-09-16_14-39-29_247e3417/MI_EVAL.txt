# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance, normalizing the client’s experience without pressure or disapproval. The positive regard is strong but somewhat professional and plan-focused rather than deeply warm or explicitly unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections show accurate understanding of the client’s internal negotiation, planning, and self-sabotage patterns. Empathy could be deepened by exploring more of the emotional texture behind statements such as feeling “heavy” when moving again.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target behavior: building a realistic exercise plan centered on walking and low-pressure gym exploration. Each question advances the client toward a more concrete, actionable plan without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist functions as a coach and co-planner rather than an expert-driven authority. The client’s own ideas about walking, timing, cues, and barriers substantially shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice and ownership are strongly reinforced throughout the interaction. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, directives, or ultimatums while supporting the client in defining what feels possible.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the client’s internal values, self-trust, and confidence rather than supplied by the psychiatrist. The client generates strong language about proving capability and building energy for future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is clearly treated as the expert on her own life, preferences, and capacity. The plan emerges from her suggestions rather than being imposed by the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions effectively elicit concrete change talk and personal motivators. The psychiatrist could more explicitly amplify, summarize, or expand the client’s change talk to reach full mastery.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers are skillfully reframed as manageable design problems rather than reasons to stop. The psychiatrist could pivot a little more decisively from each barrier back to change goals after it has been addressed.
