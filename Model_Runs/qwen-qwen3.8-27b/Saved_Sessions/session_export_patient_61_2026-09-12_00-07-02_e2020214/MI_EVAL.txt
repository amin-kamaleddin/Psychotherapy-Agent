# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone throughout, often validating Jonathan's feelings. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and procedural, with repeated technique offers that can feel more like coaching than deep, individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Jonathan's burden, unfairness, fear, and feeling judged at work. The main limitation is that empathy often stays at surface-level frustration without fully exploring deeper themes such as autonomy, identity, loss of control, or the meaning behind the boss's reaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session initially targets diabetes self-management, but the focus drifts into venting, work stress, and repeated multiple-choice prompts. This creates some structure but prevents a coherent movement toward a specific, manageable change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites choices, but often supplies the agenda, interventions, and coping strategies. A more collaborative approach would rely more on Jonathan's own language and ideas rather than repeatedly introducing external techniques.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently emphasizes choice, avoids overt pressure, and respects Jonathan's rejections of scripts or tiny steps. The main limitation is that the repeated suggestions of specific techniques and early directive prompts can still feel somewhat guiding rather than fully client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the weakest area, with limited exploration of Jonathan's own motivations, values, hopes, or reasons for change. The psychiatrist offers more suggestions and interpretations than open-ended questions that draw out Jonathan's own change-relevant language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a partially collaborative stance, with questions inviting input and choices. However, the psychiatrist frequently introduces specific interventions, which makes the interaction feel more like a counselor directing solutions than truly sharing power with Jonathan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes only minimal efforts to elicit or amplify change talk, and emerging desire or ability language is not consistently followed up. The session remains focused on burden and venting rather than exploring Jonathan's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce Jonathan's sustain talk, which is a strength. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or balanced responses to help Jonathan hold ambivalence and create space for change.
