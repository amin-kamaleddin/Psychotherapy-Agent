# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a polite, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes being flagged. However, the warmth feels procedural rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some surface-level reflections about stress and school/band demands. The psychiatrist does not sufficiently explore the client’s uncertainty, pressure, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session lacks clear focus on the referral concern of elevated alcohol-related risk. The conversation drifts toward study, band, and general coping topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices and collaborative language, but the agenda is dominated by concrete advice and plans. The client’s preferences are acknowledged but not fully used to co-create direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Statements such as “no pressure” and respect for the client’s routine support autonomy. Repeated suggestions after the client indicated readiness to leave weaken the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few open questions are asked, but they are quickly followed by advice and problem-solving. The client’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change are not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist sometimes invites the client’s input but frequently assumes an expert role. Repeated recommendations and planning limit power sharing and client-led direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is essentially no effort to cultivate change talk related to the target alcohol-related risk. The client’s statements about studying or band are not connected to motivation for changing alcohol use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the client’s resistance to new strategies and says “trust your routine,” which may reinforce the status quo. There is no use of double-sided reflection or strategic redirection toward the client’s values or goals.
