# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming about the patient’s progress and concerns. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and task-centered rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate and captures practical and safety-related concerns. Empathy remains more cognitive and logistical than emotional, with missed opportunities to reflect deeper ambivalence and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on asthma management, triggers, and a one-week plan. Direction is strong but often becomes task-oriented, moving quickly from reflection to problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan and incorporates the patient’s preferences and constraints. Collaboration is strong, but many plan details remain counselor-generated rather than fully client-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice, avoids coercion, and supports a non-all-or-nothing mindset. Autonomy support is good but can be weakened by frequent specific suggestions and behavioral instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and a confidence ruler to elicit motivation, but often shifts quickly to concrete problem-solving. Evocation is present but superficial, with limited exploration of values, fears, or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative, with the patient choosing key plan components and timing. Partnership is strong, but the psychiatrist often functions as the primary architect of the plan details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including wanting better sleep and feeling more ready to try. These moments are not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened before moving to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully, including partial cutbacks and worry about stress or failure. The psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to resolve ambivalence and strengthen motivation.
