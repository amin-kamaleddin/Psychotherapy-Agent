# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, affirming language and validates the client without criticizing her ambivalence. The stance is strong but somewhat structured, with less individualized emotional affirmation of the difficulty of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's ambivalence, practical caution, and physical cues. The empathy is mainly cognitive and would benefit from deeper exploration of underlying feelings such as fear, frustration, or social concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation. The conversation moves logically from ambivalence to a concrete, manageable experiment without major topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client's own ideas, creating a sense that the session is being co-constructed. However, the process is still fairly clinician-led and could include more explicit invitations for the client to direct the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through open choice language, respect for the client's limits, and avoidance of pressure or moral urgency. The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively draw out the client's own values, strategies, and emerging confidence. Evocation would be stronger with more scaling questions and greater use of the client's own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positions the client as the expert on her own life and asks rather than instructs. Partnership would be more fully realized with clearer equal power-sharing and joint agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to articulate values, goals, and small steps, eliciting useful change statements. It would be stronger with more explicit summarizing and amplifying of the client's change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled appropriately without arguing, using normalizing and autonomy-supportive reflections. More explicit double-sided reflections or values-based reframing would strengthen this skill.
