# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive without shaming the client. Acceptance is somewhat generic and does not fully reach unconditional regard for the client’s ambivalence and identity concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence, fear of quiet, and social pull toward gambling. The empathy is usually cognitive and problem-focused rather than deeply emotionally nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally remains focused on gambling, debt, and a concrete behavioral experiment. Direction is sometimes diluted by broad financial options and advice-giving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on some client-generated ideas, but repeatedly supplies lists, scripts, and rules. This makes the interaction feel more expert-driven than genuinely collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through options and choice questions, but not consistently and explicitly. At times, repeated scripts and behavioral rules subtly pressure the client toward the clinician’s preferred plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurs through scaling questions and the Egypt goal, but advice frequently interrupts the client’s own motivational language. The clinician misses opportunities to deepen values, identity, and future self.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influences the session, but the psychiatrist often drives the content with lengthy option lists. The session does not strongly communicate that the client is the primary expert in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates useful change talk, especially around leaving cards at home and saving for Egypt. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk, often substituting practical advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally normalized rather than contradicted, which avoids argument. However, the session keeps gambling-related plans active instead of consistently softening the reasons for maintaining the status quo.
