### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect, affirmation, and nonjudgmental support for Amira’s goals and self-doubts. It falls short of 5 because moments of embarrassment or vulnerability are not always met with explicit, unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflective listening and accurate understanding of Amira’s concerns about consistency, low-friction habits, and rebuilding routines. It does not quite reach 5 because the emotional undertones of worry, embarrassment, and low self-trust are not always fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on increasing physical activity and efficiently moves from broad goals to a specific, testable plan. The target behavior becomes progressively more concrete without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is clearly co-created, with Amira’s preferences shaping the activity, frequency, cue, and level of effort. It falls short of 5 because the psychiatrist still provides noticeable structure and examples, keeping the interaction somewhat therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses open questions and noncoercive language that preserves Amira’s sense of choice and ownership. It does not quite reach 5 because occasional agenda-setting and offered options slightly reduce the pure autonomy emphasis.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Amira’s own reasons for change, such as wanting proof of consistency, showing up for herself, and breaking autopilot. It falls short of 5 because deeper confidence, readiness, and values-based change talk are not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction conveys that Amira is the expert on her own body, routines, and realistic next steps. It does not quite reach 5 because the psychiatrist still contributes examples and shapes the conversation’s progression.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws out meaningful change talk related to desire, ability, and need for change. It falls short of 5 because broader DARN/CRAFT language and explicit confidence or readiness scaling are not fully developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers such as fear of overpromising, tired mornings, and difficulty with food changes are reframed as useful constraints rather than challenged. It does not quite reach 5 because the protective function of the sustain talk is not explicitly acknowledged.
