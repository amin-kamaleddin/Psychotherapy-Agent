# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the patient's irritation and honesty. However, repeated practical reminders and instructions can make the interaction feel more managed than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some surface-level reflection of the patient's feelings, but the core ambivalence, social threat, and autonomy concerns are not deeply explored. The counseling tends to focus on behavior management rather than accurately understanding the patient's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use and moves toward a concrete behavioral plan for a specific Friday night. The main limitation is that the focus is heavily action-oriented for a patient who appears to be in the contemplation stage.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributes meaningful decisions, such as the refusal line, soda choice, and text message wording. However, the counselor frequently supplies ready-made options and scripts, making the collaboration uneven and counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient's choices and avoids overt coercion, using nonjudgmental language about failure and troubleshooting. Autonomy is undermined by frequent prescriptive suggestions and continued reminders after the patient indicates he has enough.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist rarely elicits the patient's own motivations, values, or concerns, instead offering practical strategies and implementation-focused questions. There is minimal use of scaling, importance/confidence questions, or reflective follow-up to develop change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are occasional collaborative prompts, but the overall stance is expert-driven and problem-solving rather than partnership-based. The counselor's repeated introduction of scripts and plan steps positions them as the director rather than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient produces some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not actively elicit or amplify the patient's own reasons, values, or confidence. The counselor mainly provides strategies rather than helping the patient articulate and deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or shaming and uses some reassuring language to reduce defensiveness. However, the patient's sustain talk around alcohol's social and emotional functions is not carefully explored or reframed.
