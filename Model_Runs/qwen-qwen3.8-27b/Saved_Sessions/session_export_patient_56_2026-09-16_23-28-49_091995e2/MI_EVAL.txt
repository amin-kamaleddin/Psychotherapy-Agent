# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses warm, specific, and nonjudgmental affirmations to validate the client’s strengths and struggles. The client’s concerns are accepted without disapproval, supporting dignity and self-respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and nuanced, often capturing the client’s meaning and emotional significance. Sometimes the response moves toward thematic summaries before fully exploring underlying feelings, which limits maximal depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on authentic family presence. It steadily moves from values to a concrete, doable behavior while keeping new material connected to the central goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas clearly shape the conversation, and the psychiatrist avoids imposing a plan. Collaboration could become stronger with more explicit co-creation language and invitations for the client to set or evaluate next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes the client’s choice, feasibility, and permission to be calm rather than always efficient. There is no advice-giving, pressure, or controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids providing external reasons for change and instead elicits motivation from the client’s own values and goals. The client generates the rationale for change, making the change talk fully client-owned.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on her own life and frames contributions as observations and questions rather than directives. The session is guided by the client’s interpretations and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and affirmations effectively generate desire, need, and commitment-like change talk. The skill could be strengthened by deepening the client’s change talk further, such as through elaboration or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers are normalized and redirected toward change without arguing or reinforcing the status quo. The response could be stronger by using more explicit double-sided reflections to validate ambivalence while softening sustain talk.
