# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout. Affirmations are specific and individualized, referencing Aisha's particular circumstances rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections progressively deepen from surface-level acknowledgment to existential understanding, culminating in a masterful insight about the cigarette as the one moment Aisha is not serving someone else. Tracks both content and underlying feeling with remarkable accuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent, and progressively narrowing focus from broad opening to the specific kitchen moment. Zero topic drift with patient but purposeful pacing throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint exploration where the client's ideas (tea, kitchen scene, identity insight) fundamentally shape the session. The psychiatrist builds on Aisha's contributions rather than imposing their own framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit affirmations of choice woven into every phase of the session. Validates Aisha's readiness level without disappointment, pressure, or implied obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client generates all therapeutic content—plans, insights, and emotional processing—while the psychiatrist provides almost no external information. Questions are designed to draw out the client's own motivations rather than provide them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Aisha chooses the topic, target, goal, strategy, and specific moment, with the psychiatrist never assuming the expert role. Power sharing is genuine and the client's contributions fundamentally drive the therapeutic direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Well-crafted evoking questions elicit desire, ability, need, and commitment language from Aisha. Strategic amplification of the client's own positive language (e.g., 'that's something') reinforces her motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk appropriately through validation and double-sided reflections that prevent it from dominating. However, the approach is primarily holding and balancing rather than actively redirecting to diminish the weight of the client's minimization.
