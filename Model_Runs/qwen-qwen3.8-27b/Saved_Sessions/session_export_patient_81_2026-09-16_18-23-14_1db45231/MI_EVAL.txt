# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental acceptance. The counselor validated the client's perspective and preserved dignity even when the client minimized the problem or expressed ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated highly accurate and emotionally nuanced empathic understanding. Reflections captured the client's ambivalence, fear of isolation, and conflicting needs, using the client's language skillfully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a generally clear focus on methamphetamine use, rent, weekday functioning, and social environment. The direction was useful but could become more strategically focused on a specific behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was highly collaborative and client-led. The psychiatrist built on the client's own ideas rather than imposing solutions, creating a genuine co-constructed process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized the client's freedom to choose and avoided pressure, advice-giving, or controlling language. Suggestions were framed as options, reinforcing client ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited the client's own values and motivations. The evocation could be strengthened by more deliberately deepening and amplifying the client's change talk once it emerged.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the client as the authority on his own life and repeatedly invited him to define what matters. The client's contributions substantially shaped the direction and content of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evoked change talk through open, future-oriented questions. The score is lower because the change talk was not always amplified, summarized, or deepened after it appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by avoiding argument and gently opening doors toward change. Some sustain talk around belonging, identity, and missing out remained prominent and could be softened more consistently.
