# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout the session. However, the acceptance is more procedural than deeply affirming of Lukas’s personhood and identity-related concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate perspective-taking and understands Lukas’s surface concerns, such as rent, auditions, and belonging. The empathy remains somewhat superficial and is quickly followed by problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains some focus on a practical target related to meth use, such as protecting rent money and creating a pause before buying. However, the specific behavioral target is not explicitly clarified, and the direction is not fully strategic or consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as if the interview is a joint problem-solving process and allows Lukas to select and modify the plan. The collaboration remains partly one-directional because the psychiatrist repeatedly presents structured options and a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Lukas’s autonomy and avoids coercive language. The score is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still frequently introduces lists of options and suggested steps, which reflect a degree of clinician-led direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative techniques, particularly importance/confidence rulers and questions about what would raise confidence. However, the evocation is mostly procedural rather than deeply motivational and does not consistently draw out Lukas’s own reasons for change, hopes, values, or preferred future.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a generally collaborative stance and often invites Lukas’s input. The partnership is more tactical than strategic and does not deeply explore Lukas’s own priorities, values, or long-term goals as an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some evocative moves, including asking about importance and confidence, and what would raise confidence. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify Lukas’s own change talk, and the client’s change talk remains narrow and task-focused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Lukas’s sustain talk and reframes the conversation toward realistic, non-all-or-nothing steps. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently soften or resolve the ambivalence, and at times accepts the sustain-talk frame rather than exploring both sides of the ambivalence.
