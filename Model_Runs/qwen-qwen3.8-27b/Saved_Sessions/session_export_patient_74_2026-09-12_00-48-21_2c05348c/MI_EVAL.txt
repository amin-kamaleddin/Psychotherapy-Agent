# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, offering low-pressure language and permission-based exploration. Warmth is somewhat mechanical and task-focused rather than fully embracing Linda’s ambivalence and emotional concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture the main content, such as the tension between smoking and modeling behavior for her children. However, they remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional meanings like guilt, fear, pride, and loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking reduction and related behavior. The direction is organized and on topic, but more behavior-planning oriented than motivation-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda is invited to choose a first step, and her preferences shape the coffee-and-gum plan. However, much of the session content is provided by the psychiatrist rather than co-created, limiting true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, permission, and low-pressure experimentation. Repeated instructions and fallback suggestions partially undermine the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit some client-generated information and choices. Evocation remains superficial because important statements are often followed by advice rather than further exploration of values, abilities, and motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative invitation to choose a doable first step. However, the psychiatrist often assumes an expert role by repeatedly listing strategies, which makes the partnership uneven and somewhat prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-relevant material, especially Linda’s concern about her children. There is limited amplification, reflection, or deepening of her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation and normalizes Linda’s perspective. Sustain talk is not actively softened or explored through double-sided reflection, and suggestions often replace exploration of ambivalence.
