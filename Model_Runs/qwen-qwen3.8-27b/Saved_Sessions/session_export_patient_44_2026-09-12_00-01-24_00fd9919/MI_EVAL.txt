# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and nonjudgmental tone with frequent validation. However, acceptance often feels transactional because it quickly transitions into advice and prescriptive planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and identifies surface-level feelings and patterns. However, reflections are brief and primarily function as bridges to solutions rather than deepening emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on gym consistency and post-workout dietary cravings. However, this direction is achieved through directive pacing and behavioral prescription rather than MI guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction lacks an equal-partner dynamic, with the psychiatrist holding and delivering all the practical expertise. The patient's contributions are limited to stating constraints and preferences, with little reliance on the patient's own wisdom.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects the patient's right to refuse interventions without pressure. However, autonomy is moderate because the choices are heavily bounded by the psychiatrist's prescriptive framework.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is a distinct failure to evoke the patient's internal motivation, values, or intrinsic reasons for change. The session relies heavily on providing external information and prescriptive solutions instead of drawing out the patient's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely adopts an expert-driven, advisory stance rather than a true partnership. The patient is positioned as a consumer of behavioral prescriptions rather than a co-creator of the treatment direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes very limited efforts to cultivate or amplify the patient's change talk and emerging motivation. Instead of reinforcing the patient's reasons for change, the focus quickly shifts to problem-solving and behavioral compliance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** When the patient expresses self-critical sustain talk, the psychiatrist acknowledges it but fails to actively soften it using MI-specific techniques. The response tends toward labeling the behavior and immediately offering a structured behavioral fix.
