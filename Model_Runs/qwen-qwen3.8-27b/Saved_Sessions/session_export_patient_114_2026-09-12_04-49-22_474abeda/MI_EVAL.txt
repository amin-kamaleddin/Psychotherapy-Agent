# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts Ryan's framing without arguing or labeling him. Acceptance is solid but could be warmer and more explicitly affirming of Ryan's values and emotional difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening, accurately capturing Ryan's ambivalence, social concerns, and practical worries. Empathy could be stronger by exploring deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or the conflict between peer belonging and self-protection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing the likelihood of another alcohol-related incident and moving toward a concrete next step. Direction is clear, though it remains somewhat narrow and task-driven rather than integrating broader values and discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options, asking for preferences, and responding to Ryan's pushback about scripts and timing. The interaction is generally collaborative, though the counselor still generates much of the plan and may feel slightly counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Ryan's sense of choice by inviting his preferences and avoiding imposed directives. Autonomy support is strong, though repeated prompts about follow-through and confidence begin to introduce subtle accountability pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some change talk through open questions, scaling, and reflective prompts. However, the session is driven by many counselor-generated options, limiting deeper evocation of Ryan's own values, reasons, and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that Ryan is the expert on his own life and choices, and Ryan meaningfully shapes the plan. Partnership is good, though it could be stronger by inviting Ryan to set the agenda and develop his own ideas before presenting options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively evokes and expands Ryan's desire, ability, and commitment through questions, scaling, and reinforcement. Change talk is strong behaviorally, though it could be more strategic in amplifying identity-level motivation and deeper values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing Ryan's sustain talk and accepts it without conflict. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or strategic ambiguity to transform sustain talk into change talk.
