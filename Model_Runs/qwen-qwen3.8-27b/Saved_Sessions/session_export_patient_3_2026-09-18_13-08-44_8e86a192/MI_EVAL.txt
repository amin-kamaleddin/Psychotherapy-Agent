# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and unconditional positive regard throughout, with specific affirmations that feel genuine rather than formulaic. Never judges or labels; explicitly rejects self-blame ('You're not lazy, and you're not broken').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with complex reflections that capture both content and emotional meaning beneath Aisha's words. The identification of the all-or-nothing bind and the attunement to developmental history without over-interpreting demonstrate deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on the target behavior while allowing organic exploration of related themes. Clear arc from identifying the goal to a concrete, actionable plan (Wednesday morning, 20 minutes) without letting the session drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as a collaboration between equals using inclusive 'we' language and following Aisha's lead. Aisha's contributions (20-minute idea, all-or-nothing identification, Wednesday choice) substantially shape where the session goes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy from the opening ('I'm here to walk alongside you, not to tell you what to do') and never makes decisions for Aisha. When Aisha self-identifies as 'performative' about Monday, the psychiatrist opens the door rather than pushing back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Aisha rather than providing external reasons for change. The final exchange, where Aisha independently identifies Wednesday as the practical choice, demonstrates the full evocation process.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within Aisha ('You know your own life better than anyone,' 'You defined it yourself'). Never positions herself as the one who knows what's best, treating Aisha as the expert on her own patterns and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates change talk through strategic evocative questions that draw out Aisha's own solutions and success criteria. Could more explicitly amplify and reflect back Aisha's powerful change talk statements (e.g., 'I'm tired of getting nowhere').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully handles Aisha's sustain talk by validating fear ('I hear that fear underneath too') while simultaneously redirecting to her own stated motivation. Avoids reinforcing reasons to maintain the status quo while honoring the validity of Aisha's concerns.
