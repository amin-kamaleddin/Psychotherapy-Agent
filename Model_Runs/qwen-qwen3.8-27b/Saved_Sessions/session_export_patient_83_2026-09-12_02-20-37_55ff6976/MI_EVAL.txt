# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, validating Mai’s concerns and decisions. Acceptance was strong but slightly procedural, with less personal affirmation of her uncertainty and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected some of Mai’s frustration and uncertainty, especially early in the session. However, many reflections were cognitive rather than emotionally attuned, missing deeper exploration of anxiety, ambivalence, or relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on understanding and adhering to the new statin medication. The conversation moved clearly from uncertainty to a concrete, behaviorally specific adherence plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Mai’s input and built the plan around her choices, such as the orange juice swap and checklist. The interaction was collaborative, though the educational role of the psychiatrist made moments less fully power-balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Mai’s choice and ownership, offering options rather than directives and accepting her declines without pressure. The final readiness check reinforced her role as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful change talk and practical readiness, particularly regarding the medication plan and confidence level. However, evocation remained more superficial, with less exploration of Mai’s personal values, motivations, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Mai to lead focus areas and offered multiple options for how to proceed, and her contributions shaped the final plan. The partnership was strong, though the psychiatrist’s expert-educator role created a slightly directive edge at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai was able to clearly voice her own plans and readiness, including specific steps for taking the medication and monitoring symptoms. The psychiatrist could have amplified or deepened this change talk further with stronger reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled Mai’s mild sustain talk by validating her concern about adding another medication rather than debating it. The response reduced resistance effectively, though more strategic double-sided reflections could have strengthened the pivot.
