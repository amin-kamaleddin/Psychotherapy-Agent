# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates warmth, respect, and normalization of the client's protective behavior. Minor conditional affirmations prevent a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's feelings and meanings, such as guilt, fear, and exhaustion. This helps the client feel understood rather than judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session moves toward a concrete, client-chosen action involving school signups and reduced checking. The psychiatrist supports the client's own direction without imposing a plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language such as “we” and positions the client as the expert on her life. The interaction feels partnership-based rather than directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's values, choices, and sense of agency. Change is framed as the client's own decision and action, not as a requirement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes the client's own reasons for change, including stepping away from anger and testing the fear that the house will fall apart. Change talk is reflected and strengthened throughout.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and avoids positioning as the sole authority. The client is invited to examine fear and evidence alongside the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change statements such as “I need to remember the times it went fine” are noticed and reinforced. The psychiatrist helps the client connect these insights to a manageable next step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fear, guilt, and failure is validated without argument or minimization. The psychiatrist helps the client explore both sides of the change dilemma.
