# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally respectful and non-judgmental with consistent normalization of Laura's experience. However, the warmth felt somewhat formulaic and quickly shifted into problem-solving rather than sustaining unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but largely cognitive, missing opportunities to name deeper feelings like shame, fear of disappointment, and self-worth tied to performance. Deeper emotional reflections that connect to underlying values conflict would have been more skillful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on consistent yoga practice as a value-aligned health behavior with a concrete final plan. Some mild drift occurred due to repeated introduction of alternative options before narrowing to Laura's choices.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed with Laura making meaningful choices about components like the phrase, ritual, and timing. Could improve with more client-led idea generation rather than counselor-provided menus of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura's right to choose was consistently honored and she was able to discard elements that felt inauthentic like numeric tracking. Still slightly counselor-directed in framing choices through multiple offered alternatives rather than fully eliciting Laura's own options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort to draw out internal motivations and Laura's confidence increased from 5 to 7. The counselor often filled space with suggestions and measurement tools instead of using open questions to help Laura deepen articulation of her core values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura's preferences materially shaped the plan and she was invited to choose throughout the session. The counselor still functioned somewhat as agenda-setter by frequently introducing structured options and menus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura offered several change-related statements and confidence grew, but the psychiatrist often moved quickly to planning rather than reflecting or amplifying her motivation. More strategic evocation of values and reflective amplification of her change language would have been stronger.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged respectfully with normalization but not consistently softened using more active MI strategies. The psychiatrist sometimes pivoted to offering options rather than using double-sided reflections or values-based reframes to reframe sustain talk toward change-compatible language.
