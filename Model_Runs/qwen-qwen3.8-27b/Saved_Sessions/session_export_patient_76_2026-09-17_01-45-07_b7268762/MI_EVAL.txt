# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent, nonjudgmental positive regard without blaming, shaming, or minimizing Liora’s self-harm. Statements such as “That’s not your fault” and “I’m not going to ask you to throw that away” strongly honor Liora’s experience and effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate and sensitive reflections that capture ambivalence and the function of self-harm. However, some reflections are inferential and could deepen the emotional meaning beneath exhaustion, shame, and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on stress, emotional overload, and self-harm as a coping response without drifting into unrelated topics. It would benefit from more explicit naming of the target behavior, a brief safety-focused direction, and a clearer consolidated next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and responds directly to Liora’s ideas, positioning the work as shared rather than directive. Collaboration could be strengthened by more explicit co-construction, such as inviting Liora to set the agenda or choose the next focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated emphasis on Liora’s choice, self-direction, and freedom from being managed. The psychiatrist avoids coercive, directive, or pressure-based language, especially around self-harm.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative open-ended questions that invite Liora to generate her own understanding and motivations. Evocation could be stronger if it more consistently expanded change talk by exploring values, past successes, and confidence in safer options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that Liora is the expert on her own experience and follows her lead through several concrete topics. Partnership could improve with more explicit agenda negotiation and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, such as Liora’s tiredness of holding it in and desire for relief. The psychiatrist could more systematically cultivate and consolidate change talk by amplifying desire, ability, reasons, need, and confidence for safer coping.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or punishing Liora for sustain talk and validates the function of self-harm. More effective softening would include double-sided reflections that honor the reason for self-harm while redirecting toward exceptions, values, or safer alternatives.
