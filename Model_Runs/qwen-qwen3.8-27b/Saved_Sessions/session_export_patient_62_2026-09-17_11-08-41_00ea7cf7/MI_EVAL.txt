# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, validating Jordan's uncertainty and reframing self-judgment as a protective mechanism rather than accepting it. There is no moment of disapproval or conditional regard throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and consistent reflective listening that adds meaning and emotional depth rather than simply paraphrasing. Reflections capture the complex emotional logic behind Jordan's behavior with remarkable precision, including somatic awareness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on Jordan's target behavior with a logical, purposeful trajectory from opening through closing. The middle section exploring 'good enough' could have been slightly tightened to maintain momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of collaboration between equals—the psychiatrist consistently frames the work as joint exploration and follows Jordan's leads without imposing an agenda. When Jordan shifts from employment to the plumbing example, the psychiatrist immediately follows that thread.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Zero directive language throughout; the psychiatrist never uses 'you should,' 'I recommend,' or implies obligation. Every intervention reinforces Jordan's ownership, with the final question leaving the ball entirely in his court.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation—virtually all meaningful content (the plumbing example, the smile, the 'light' feeling) comes from Jordan himself. The psychiatrist acts as a skilled elicitor who draws out what is already present rather than introducing external content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions themselves as a partner rather than an expert, explicitly stating 'I'm here to walk through this with you, not to tell you what to do.' Every intervention is framed as curiosity or invitation, with power-sharing that is genuine throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evokes multiple instances of change talk across desire, imagery, ability, and emotional domains. Could have more explicitly amplified or reinforced these moments with stronger reflections to deepen the client's own commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by reframing avoidance as protective rather than as evidence of failure. Could have been more explicit in pivoting away from the 'I'm not good enough' sustain talk with a double-sided reflection acknowledging both the fear and evidence of capability.
