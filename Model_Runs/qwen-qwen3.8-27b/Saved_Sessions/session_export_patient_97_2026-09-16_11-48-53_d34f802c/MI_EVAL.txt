# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Mateo’s pride, boundaries, and choices. Acceptance could be deeper by normalizing the emotional fear and guardedness beneath the plan rather than mainly approving the practical steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Mateo’s ambivalence, need for autonomy, and concern about being monitored. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level when Mateo expresses doubt, fear of failure, or the weight of his own guardedness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on a concrete, low-risk weekend plan that supports the target behavior. The trajectory is clear, practical, and goal-congruent without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Mateo’s input and lets him choose the plan’s key elements, creating a collaborative process. It becomes slightly less equal when the psychiatrist offers multiple practical options and tips rather than consistently inviting Mateo to generate his own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with the psychiatrist consistently honoring Mateo’s need to choose his own plan and avoid scripts, monitoring, or pressure. Boundaries around check-ins and contact are respected clearly and consistently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful client-generated steps and some internal motivation, especially when Mateo frames the plan as doing something for himself. Evocation is limited by frequent practical suggestions and less exploration of deeper values, identity, or fear of failure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo is treated as the expert on his own life and his choices shape the session. The psychiatrist occasionally shifts toward a consultant role by offering menus of options, practical tips, and structured supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces concrete change talk, such as the library plan, job form, charger, and low-key text from Leo. Change talk could be deepened by exploring importance, confidence, and personal meaning more fully.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and accepts Mateo’s concerns about being managed. There are missed opportunities to more actively reflect both sides of his ambivalence and gently pivot doubt toward self-efficacy or future goals.
