# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but acceptance is weakened by repeatedly adding safety information after the patient asks to leave. The response does not fully validate the patient's frustration, rush, or decision to decline further guidance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reflects some surface-level preferences, such as wanting clear reasons and to move quickly. However, the empathy remains mostly cognitive and does not deeply capture the patient's frustration, feeling unheard, or ambivalence about medication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on asthma management and inhaler use rather than unrelated topics. However, the direction is inconsistent in an MI sense, cycling among medical education, safety warnings, and repeated script offers without a clear collaborative change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician asks a few questions and offers choices, but the interaction does not feel like a partnership between equals. The patient's concerns and priorities do not meaningfully shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses autonomy-supportive language such as respecting the patient's choice and avoiding overt coercion. Autonomy support is undercut by continued advice-giving, repeated safety lists, and repeated offers of a script.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician begins with some evocative questions but quickly shifts to education and repeated reasons for change. There is limited effort to draw out the patient's own values, concerns, goals, or confidence about asthma management.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The clinician largely assumes the expert role and dominates the interaction with brief facts, safety criteria, and script offers. The patient's stated need for a quick exit and refusal of further planning is acknowledged but does not change the clinician's approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk appears, such as the patient researching inhalers and agreeing to watch for worsening symptoms. The clinician does not consistently notice, reflect, amplify, or build on these moments of openness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expresses strong sustain talk, including not having time, distrust of daily steroids, and refusal of scripts or rehearsals. The clinician does not effectively soften this resistance with double-sided reflections or strategic pivots, and instead continues providing medical facts.
