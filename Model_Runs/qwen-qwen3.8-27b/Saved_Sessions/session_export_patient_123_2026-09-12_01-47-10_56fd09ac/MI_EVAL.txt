# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses a respectful, normalizing, and supportive tone that reduces blame. However, acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and does not deeply acknowledge the client's ambivalence or referral reason.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic engagement is limited, with little reflection of the client's uncertainty, feelings, or perspective. The counselor focuses more on symptoms and problem-solving than on emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session narrows toward a concrete after-lunch walk plan, providing some clear focus. Direction is inconsistent and drifts across topics without directly addressing the alcohol risk screening concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input and builds a plan around stated preferences, such as choosing the walk option. The interaction remains more counselor-directed than truly collaborative because of repeated suggestions and a structured plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice by asking which option feels doable and normalizing imperfect follow-through. Autonomy support is weakened by the high level of prescriptive advice, specific instructions, and checklists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are minimal attempts to elicit the client's own reasons for change or explore personal values and motivations. The session relies heavily on counselor-provided reasons and strategies rather than client-led motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks for client input and maintains a collaborative tone at a surface level. The partnership is uneven because the counselor takes a directive role and repeatedly offers structured plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few weak attempts encourage client choice, such as asking what feels doable. Overall, the counselor does not consistently elicit or amplify the client's reasons, ability, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses mild sustain talk about energy drinks, phone use, and not needing to change. The counselor does not reinforce this sustain talk but also does not skillfully soften or redirect it.
