# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with appropriate affirmations, but the tone can feel procedural and less emotionally attuned. Acceptance is generally present but sometimes undercut by rapid safety checks and planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers reasonable perspective-taking on feelings such as frustration, ambivalence, and fear. However, empathy often remains brief before moving into advice, planning, or safety language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to the target concerns of relapse prevention, work, and rebuilding with Fiona. However, direction is uneven because it frequently shifts into safety checks and concrete planning rather than MI-guided exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist engages in some collaborative dialogue and adjusts after the client resists being micromanaged. Still, the counselor remains the primary planner and frequently introduces new ideas and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive moments, such as asking whether a plan sounds right and acknowledging the client's dislike of micromanagement. The psychiatrist repeatedly adds external structure, which can reduce the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with a useful open question, but the psychiatrist mostly provides external guidance and specific plans. The client's own motivations and change talk are not consistently drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input at times, but much of the session is expert-driven and agenda-setting. The client often responds to the counselor's plan rather than leading the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the client's 22-day progress and elicits a few goal-related statements. There are limited opportunities to skillfully evoke or deepen the client's reasons, abilities, and desires for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directly arguing with the client's status-quo reasons, which is a strength. However, responses tend toward practical problem-solving rather than MI-consistent reflections, reframing, or redirection toward values.
