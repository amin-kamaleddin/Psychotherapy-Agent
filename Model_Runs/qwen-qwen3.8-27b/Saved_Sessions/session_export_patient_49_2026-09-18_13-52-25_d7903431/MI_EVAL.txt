# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout; every moment of shame or embarrassment is met with warmth and normalization (e.g., 'That doesn't feel embarrassing at all'). No instances of judgment, correction, or labeling are present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that capture emotional and identity-level meaning beyond surface content, such as the double-sided reflection on ambivalence and the insight that 'stupidly small' actually 'protects' the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear arc from context through values connection to a specific, measurable action step (water check-in before smoking). Focus remains consistently on the target behavior without wandering, with each question building naturally on the prior answer.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue rather than structured interview; the psychiatrist consistently uses the patient's own language and metaphors back into the conversation. The power dynamic is equal, with the psychiatrist showing curiosity rather than authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan explicitly preserves the option to still smoke ('even if you still have one after') and is framed as 'not a test, just a check-in.' No directive language is used; the entire session is structured around the patient discovering her own reasons and plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is entirely client-generated—no statistics, health consequences, or external motivators are offered. Questions are specifically designed to elicit rather than inform, and every insight is attributed back to Hana.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions herself as a curious listener, never offering advice, information, or external frameworks. Every insight is attributed to Hana, and the session is entirely shaped by her contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions are specifically designed to elicit change-oriented language, and the psychiatrist skillfully amplifies spontaneous commitment language (e.g., 'I'll try that') by asking what made it feel doable. The session moves Hana from vague ambivalence to a specific, self-generated action plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful reframing that removes the 'or else' threat—e.g., suggesting water 'even if you still have one after' completely neutralizes the sustain talk about loss. Sustain thoughts are validated as information rather than obstacles to overcome.
