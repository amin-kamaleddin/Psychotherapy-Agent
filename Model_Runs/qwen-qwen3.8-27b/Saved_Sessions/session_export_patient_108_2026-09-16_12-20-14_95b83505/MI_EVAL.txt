# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance while normalizing Oliver’s discomfort. Acceptance is slightly limited when warmth becomes linked to doing the “right move.”

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Oliver’s ambivalence, fear, and discomfort. The counselor often moves too quickly from emotional reflection into planning or scripting, which limits depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior: setting a boundary and choosing a date and communication method. There is no significant topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The message is co-constructed, and Oliver’s preferences clearly shape the wording. Collaboration is strong, but the counselor still provides much of the structure and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Oliver is regularly offered choices and reminded that he can decide the timing. Some directive language and prescriptive after-send planning slightly reduce autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through importance/confidence ratings and preference questions. However, the counselor frequently supplies scripts and plans instead of evoking Oliver’s own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites Oliver’s input and adjusts based on his feedback. The stance is collaborative, but the counselor sometimes functions more as a coach or scriptwriter than a full partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver expresses desire, intention, and some ability to act. Change talk could be deepened further by asking more about what the boundary means to him rather than moving quickly into scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or shame. The counselor sometimes responds in a corrective or overly action-focused way rather than using deeper double-sided reflections.
