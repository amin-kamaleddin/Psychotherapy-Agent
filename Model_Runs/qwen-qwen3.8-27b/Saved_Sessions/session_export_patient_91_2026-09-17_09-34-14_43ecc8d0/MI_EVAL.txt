# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance and avoids blaming the client for ambivalence or missed checks. It falls just short of a 5 because the affirmation is sometimes formulaic and not always individually person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially in capturing the client's tension between caring about diabetes and not wanting it to become self-punishing. Some reflections are repetitive or overly interpretive, limiting precision and nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target behavior and narrows from general concern to a concrete after-dinner check routine. It could be stronger by explicitly stating the goal, checking agreement, and moving more directly toward a collaborative action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor builds on the client's ideas and engages in cooperative problem-solving around timing, backups, and how to mark missed checks. Occasional counselor-driven framing slightly reduces the sense of equal co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice with open questions, avoids coercion, and respects the client's right to his own experience. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit affirmations that the client is the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some practical client-generated ideas and coping statements, but does not consistently draw out intrinsic motivation, values, or confidence. Standard evocation techniques, such as importance or confidence scaling, are absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally treats the client as a collaborator and invites his preferences and self-reflection. Partnership is not fully expert-averse because the counselor occasionally leads with their own preferred framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses meaningful change talk, but the counselor does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or summarize it. The evocation is useful but limited in depth and frequency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor responds to sustain talk nonjudgmentally and normalizes the client's fear of falling behind. The session could be stronger by more actively redirecting from worry language toward strengths, values, and change talk.
