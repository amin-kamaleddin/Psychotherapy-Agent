# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, immediately neutralizing the 'in trouble' framing from the athletic director's referral. There is zero judgment throughout, and Lukas's self-reflection is reframed as a strength rather than a problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking is evident throughout, including catching the hedging in Lukas's language and accurately capturing dual experiences (fun night vs. difficult morning) without collapsing them. Reflects the ongoing processing with precision and warmth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, progressive arc from disclosure through reflection to commitment, with a logical focus on the target behavior. However, it could have been more strategic in its final minutes by collaboratively setting a follow-up plan or tying back to the referral context.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely co-constructed, with the psychiatrist using language like 'I'm curious' and 'I'm wondering if' to position themselves as a thinking partner. The psychiatrist never introduces their own agenda or interpretations that Lukas hasn't already generated, and the power-sharing is authentic rather than performative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a standout feature — the psychiatrist consistently reinforces Lukas's ownership, never gives advice, and lets him resolve his own uncertainties. The 'just say no' plan is entirely Lukas's, and the psychiatrist simply reflects it back without adding strategies or pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never provides reasons for change externally; all motivation is elicitated from within Lukas through well-calibrated questions like 'where's the line for you?' The resulting change talk spans desire, ability, need, and commitment language, all self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently operates from an egalitarian stance, with Lukas holding the agenda from the opening ('your space, your call'). Questions are exploratory and open, and the psychiatrist's expertise is deployed only in service of asking better questions, never in providing answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lukas generates substantial change talk spontaneously, and the psychiatrist's questions effectively open space for it. The score is limited by the absence of confidence ratings, more detailed strategy exploration, or a specific commitment to a next step or follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Lukas's fear of losing his friend group well by validating the concern and redirecting toward the core value underneath ('What would you lose, beyond the drinks?'). The score is limited by not more explicitly stating the discrepancy between Lukas's values and the fear of social loss.
