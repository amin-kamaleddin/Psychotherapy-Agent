# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and validates the client, but the acceptance is weakened by repeated detailed medical warnings. The initial self-harm screening also feels somewhat disproportionate to the session context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are adequate but remain surface-level and sometimes assume the client’s emotional state. Deeper identity, frustration, and meaning-related dimensions were not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the lip piercing but lacks a clear agenda and strategic arc. It moves from validation to options to advice without a coherent progression toward a concrete goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and asks for preferences, but the options and agenda are mostly clinician-driven. The client’s preferred frame is not fully integrated into the session structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently affirms the client’s choice and autonomy, but repeatedly provides detailed medical advice despite the client declining it. This mixed behavior undermines the stated respect for autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with generic questions and no effective use of scaling, values, decisional balance, or future-oriented exploration. After limited attempts, the psychiatrist shifts to didactic information instead of eliciting the client’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative gestures, but the psychiatrist largely retains control over the session direction. The client’s contributions do not substantially reshape the interaction, making the partnership feel uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Attempts to cultivate change talk are rare and weak, relying mostly on clinician-generated suggestions that the client rejects. No meaningful elicitation of ambivalence or reasons for reconsideration occurs.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s position without arguing, but does not effectively soften or redirect the sustain talk. The client’s ambivalence is not explored through double-sided or nuanced reflections.
