# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated nonjudgmental acceptance, validation, and positive regard. Avoidance was understood as protective rather than criticized or moralized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate, nuanced reflections that captured Hana’s ambivalence, fear of judgment, and the meaning behind avoidance. The empathy deepened understanding without reducing Hana’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and moved logically from avoidance toward a concrete, manageable plan. There was no drift away from the exam-related goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-created, with Hana generating the timer, five questions, text check-in, and cue. The psychiatrist reflected, refined, and supported Hana’s ideas rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language was non-coercive, and Hana was allowed to define the level of accountability and plan. The psychiatrist avoided pressure, advice-giving, or insisting on commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out Hana’s own motivations, concerns, and solutions. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, ability, or commitment would have strengthened this dimension.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Hana was positioned as the expert on her own patterns and experience. The psychiatrist avoided positioning herself as the problem-solver and instead built on Hana’s contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful change talk, including Hana’s desire for progress, her plan, and her intention to try. The session could have further expanded Hana’s confidence, values, and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and reframed without argument or dismissal. More active pivoting or double-sided reflections could further reduce the weight of barriers.
