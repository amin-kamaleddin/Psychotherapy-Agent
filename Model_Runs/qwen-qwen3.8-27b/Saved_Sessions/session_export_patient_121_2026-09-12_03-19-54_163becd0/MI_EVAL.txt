# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and often normalized Sofia's reactions. It fell short of full excellence because warmth and affirmations were sometimes brief and not consistently individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used reflective listening to accurately capture several of Sofia's core emotions, such as feeling blamed, attacked, and torn. However, some reflections became repetitive and did not always explore deeper hurt, shame, or fear of rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session had an initial focus on the presenting peer-conflict concern but quickly narrowed into practical phone-use troubleshooting. This made the direction feel somewhat procedural rather than progressively tied to broader motivational goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally worked with Sofia rather than for her, incorporating her language, preferences, and suggested options. At times, however, the collaboration became more expert-guided through structured lists and detailed phone-setting instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offered choices, honored refusals, and avoided pressure or persuasion. This support could be strengthened by more explicitly connecting Sofia's choices to her own ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some MI-consistent evocative questions were used, particularly early in the session. Much of the interview shifted toward practical problem-solving rather than drawing out deeper values, reasons for change, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Sofia's input and allowed her preferences to shape the session, including message wording and app-limit selection. The partnership was less fully balanced when the clinician generated multiple structured options and technical steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia offered meaningful change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist did not consistently follow up on, amplify, or expand them. There was limited use of importance/confidence ratings or explicit exploration of reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Sofia's sustain talk and generally accepted her stated barriers. However, there was limited use of MI-consistent softening techniques such as double-sided reflections or gentle pivoting.
