# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, validation, and nonjudgment. The warmth is strong but sometimes more cognitive than deeply unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Amina's conflict, values, and ambivalence. Some emotionally intense moments are reflected more thematically than through deeper affective acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session moves toward a concrete plan, but the target behavior and context remain vague. A clearer behavior focus and summary would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amina's ideas substantially shape the session, and the therapist builds on her contributions. Explicit co-creation and shared agenda-setting would make the partnership more fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist avoids advising and repeatedly invites Amina to identify her own values and solutions. Some leading questions and strong affirmations could subtly increase pressure to follow through.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amina generates meaningful change talk, values, and concrete self-identified steps. The therapist could deepen evocation with more complex reflections and explicit elicitation of confidence, commitment, or need.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Amina as the expert on her son and her needs. The overall flow remains somewhat therapist-guided, with occasional leading language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session elicits useful change talk, including the pause, script, and follow-through plan. The therapist could further amplify and expand this change talk with stronger reflections and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or dismissal, and the therapist gently balances ambivalence. A more active pivot toward strengths or benefits of change would make softening more consistent.
