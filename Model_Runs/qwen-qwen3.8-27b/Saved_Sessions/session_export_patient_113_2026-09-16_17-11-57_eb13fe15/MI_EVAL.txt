# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental stance and validates ambivalence, resistance, and desire for control. Warmth is consistent but can feel formulaic, and could be strengthened with more explicit normalization and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures mixed feelings about autonomy, stability, and burden. The empathy is largely cognitive and misses opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as shame, fear of blame, or resentment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and avoids major drift. Direction is somewhat exploratory and circular, and would benefit from a clearer summary, narrowing of options, or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Rowan into joint problem-solving. True co-construction of agenda, goals, or the final plan could be stronger by letting Rowan prioritize options or choose the focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is respected through non-coercive language, acceptance of hesitation, and avoidance of pressure to commit. It could be slightly stronger by more explicitly affirming that the decision is entirely Rowan’s and reducing subtle implementation focus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions effectively evoke Rowan’s own practical ideas about routines and device use. Evocation remains mostly problem-solving rather than motivational, with less exploration of personal values, self-efficacy, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Rowan as the expert on his own life and allows his ideas to shape the session. The partnership could become more explicit by negotiating the agenda and clearly inviting Rowan to co-lead the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated change ideas, especially practical charging solutions. However, these are not consistently developed into fuller change talk, confidence/important scaling, or explicit reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled reasonably by validating concerns without arguing. Responses often remain acceptance plus logistical follow-up rather than strategic reframing or double-sided reflections that help move Rowan toward change.
