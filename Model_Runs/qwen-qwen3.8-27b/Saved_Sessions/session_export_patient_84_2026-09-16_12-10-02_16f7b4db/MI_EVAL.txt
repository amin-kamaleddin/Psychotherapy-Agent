# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Malik’s feelings and context. Acceptance is slightly limited by repeated "not to judge" language and limited explicit affirmation of strengths or capacity for change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Malik’s perspective on peer belonging, autonomy, and frustration with being policed. Deeper emotional undertones and longer staying-with feeling moments could strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on cannabis use, peer pressure, and conflict with his mother. The direction becomes overly tactical, emphasizing scripts and avoidance strategies rather than values or discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses Malik’s language and invites him to choose among options, showing some co-construction. However, frequent provision of scripts and role-play makes the interaction feel more expert-driven than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Malik’s right to choose and resists controlling language. Repeated offers of options and role-play can create subtle pressure, though autonomy is generally respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks meaningful early questions about values, identity, and what would need to happen for change. These are not fully developed, as the session quickly shifts to advice and behavior planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership language is present, and Malik’s preferences influence the plan. The counselor’s frequent problem-solving role and menu of options make the partnership feel less balanced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, including willingness to try subtle strategies if it reduces conflict with his mother. These reasons are not sufficiently amplified, reflected back, or connected to Malik’s own values and motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids direct confrontation, which is appropriate. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflection, rolling with resistance, or discrepancy-building to soften Malik’s strong reasons against change.
