# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard, respect, and nonjudgment. The stance was validating even when Marcus was defensive, creating a safe and accepting environment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both Marcus’s surface statements and underlying concerns about judgment, fitting in, and autonomy. The empathy was nuanced, age-appropriate, and consistently aligned with his perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on Marcus’s alcohol use in peer contexts and safer decision-making at parties. The conversation followed a clear trajectory from values and concerns to a concrete behavioral plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaved as an equal partner, inviting Marcus’s perspective and building on his ideas. The plan was co-created rather than imposed by the therapist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marcus was repeatedly framed as the person in charge of his choices and decisions. The therapist avoided pressure or directive advice, reinforcing Marcus’s sense of self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn out from within Marcus through open-ended questions and reflections. He generated his own reasons, strategies, and peer-support plan rather than being told what to do.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positioned Marcus as the expert on his own social life and choices. The interaction was collaborative, nonhierarchical, and centered on shared problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist effectively elicited and reinforced Marcus’s own language in favor of change, such as staying in charge and making safer choices. Marcus produced specific plans, abilities, and self-direction related to reducing alcohol-related risk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by avoiding confrontation and redirecting toward agency and choice. The psychiatrist could further strengthen this by using more explicit double-sided reflections on ideas like “everyone does it” or “it’s not a big deal.”
