# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, with multiple affirmations and normalizations. This was slightly formulaic and lacked a more explicit statement of unconditional positive regard, which would have made it a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The reflections were accurate and validating at a physical/sensory level, such as the client’s stomach upset, pounding heart, and shaky hands. However, the psychiatrist did not deeply explore or reflect the underlying fear, shame, ambivalence, or sense of entrapment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the concrete door exercise and kept the discussion anchored to the target behavior. However, it remained overly procedural and did not connect the small step to the broader goal of community engagement or social connection.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist framed the work as a joint effort and adapted the plan based on the client’s input. However, the collaboration occurred within a psychiatrist-designed structure, so the client was choosing how to execute the plan rather than co-designing the approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honored the client’s pace, choices, and limits, without pressure or disappointment. A stronger 5 would have included more explicit language reinforcing the client’s ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some open-ended questions, but they were mostly procedural and focused on logistics or preferences. There was little exploration of the client’s values, motivations, hopes, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited the client’s input throughout the session. However, the overall structure remained therapist-led, which prevented the interaction from feeling fully peer-equal or co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the client’s change talk supportively and affirmatively. However, the psychiatrist did not ask follow-up questions to expand or deepen desire, ability, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk reasonably by normalizing and reframing rather than challenging it. However, the client’s final and significant expression of doubt was not addressed, and the sustain talk was not actively redirected toward change.
