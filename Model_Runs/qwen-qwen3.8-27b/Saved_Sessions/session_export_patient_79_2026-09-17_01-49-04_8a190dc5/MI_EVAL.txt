# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and used validating statements that reduced defensiveness. Minor improvement was needed to fully affirm the client as a person beyond asthma-related problem language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the client’s ambivalence, exhaustion, work constraints, and fear of chest tightness. It could have been stronger by reflecting the protective role of sarcasm, anger at being labeled, and deeper fear of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma management and moved from barriers to a concrete inhaler plan. Direction could be stronger by integrating follow-up appointments and adding a brief collaborative closure around next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and built the plan around the client’s routines and preferences. It remained somewhat therapist-guided rather than fully co-creating the agenda, follow-up format, and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized the client’s choice, ownership, and privacy while avoiding controlling language. This was the strongest aspect of the session and clearly supported self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited the client’s own solutions, motivations, and success indicator rather than giving advice. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, importance, and readiness for change would strengthen the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s lived experience and ideas shaped the plan, especially the coffee-linked inhaler routine. Partnership could be stronger with more explicit agenda negotiation and shared decision-making about the clinician’s role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited concrete change talk through questions and affirmations. It could be stronger by amplifying change talk using importance/confidence ratings, values, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided direct confrontation and used helpful reframes, but occasionally reinforced avoidant communication such as “silence means it’s working.” More consistent double-sided reflections or strategic redirection would improve this area.
