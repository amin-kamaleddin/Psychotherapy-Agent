# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized the client's fatigue and past weight cycling. Slightly less than optimal because positive regard was often tied to plan completion and performance rather than unconditional value.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client's exhaustion, realistic needs, and value of family involvement. Deeper emotional reflections of disappointment, shame, or fear of repeated failure would have strengthened the empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity in a realistic, sustainable way. The conversation moved clearly from motivation to a concrete, actionable after-dinner walking plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist built the plan from the client's preferences, constraints, and suggestions, creating a joint problem-solving process. Minor limits occurred because the psychiatrist frequently initiated the next implementation step and offered several structured options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was repeatedly offered choices, respected in declining a next-day check-in, and encouraged to select the step and follow-up that felt most doable. The high number of specific clinician-generated suggestions made the process somewhat more directive than fully client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions, open questions, and implementation prompts helped the client articulate his own reasons, preferences, and growing confidence. Stronger use of silence and follow-up questions before offering concrete ideas would have made the evocation more client-driven.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the client as the expert on his life, schedule, and family constraints, and incorporated his choices into the final plan. However, the psychiatrist still controlled much of the agenda and introduced many concrete options, creating a subtle expert-led dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited change talk through importance/confidence scales, open questions, and prompts about meaningful cues. Some change talk was not fully amplified or connected back to the client's values and past strengths.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with barriers and responded to past failure with acceptance and realism. The session moved quickly from ambivalence to planning, and lacked more skillful double-sided reflections or deeper normalizing responses to the client's discouragement.
