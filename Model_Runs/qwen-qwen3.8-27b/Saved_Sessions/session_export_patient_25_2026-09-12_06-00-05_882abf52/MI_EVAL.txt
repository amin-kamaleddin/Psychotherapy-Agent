# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental support throughout the session. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with less explicit validation of the patient's emotional and identity-based ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and helps summarize the patient's mixed feelings about drinking, health, and family. The empathy is practical and summary-oriented rather than deeply exploring the emotional meaning behind his statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on reducing problematic social drinking. It moves strategically from concern to exploration, planning, confidence-building, barriers, and a concrete trial night.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with the patient's preferences clearly shaping the final plan. However, the frequent use of structured options, summaries, and checklists makes the interaction slightly more guided than fully client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports the patient's choice, flexibility, and willingness without using pressuring language. The high number of clinician-generated strategies and options slightly limits a fuller sense of patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits practical change talk, including specific steps, confidence ratings, and a trial plan. Evocation could be stronger by more consistently drawing out the patient's deeper values, identity, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributes choices and helps shape the plan, supporting a collaborative style. Partnership is limited by the psychiatrist's frequent advisor-like role, including multiple concrete strategies and checklists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generates many client statements supporting change, such as spacing drinks, using an alarm, and choosing a trial night. Change talk could be amplified further by reflecting and affirming the patient's values and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without arguing or reinforcing resistance. The psychiatrist reflects and reframes the patient's concerns while preserving his autonomy and identity.
