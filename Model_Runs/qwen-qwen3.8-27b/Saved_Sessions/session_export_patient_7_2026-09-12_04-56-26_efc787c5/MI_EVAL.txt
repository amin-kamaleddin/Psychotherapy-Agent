# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and respect for the client’s limits. Warmth is strong but somewhat procedural, with fewer deeper affirmations of the client’s personhood.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, perspective-taking, and help reduce resistance. They remain mostly surface-level and do not deeply probe underlying feelings of being controlled or disrespected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on immediate concerns, time limits, safety, and a practical boundary statement. The specific target behavior remains vague and is not consistently clarified collaboratively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and lets the client steer time and focus. Collaboration is somewhat menu-based because the therapist supplies the main content and scripts rather than co-creating the client’s own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice, control, and refusal are strongly honored, especially around session time and content. Autonomy support is less explicit around owning the change itself because the therapist still supplies the boundary language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions at the opening and about practical preparation. The psychiatrist then relies on closed questions and therapist-provided options rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a non-authoritarian stance and frequently asks which option the client prefers. Partnership is strong but not fully equalized because the therapist still structures much of the content and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change-related statements appear, especially when the client selects a boundary line. The psychiatrist does not actively expand these into stronger change talk by exploring what the line means or the client’s ability to use it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk and resistance without arguing, pressing, or becoming adversarial. It is not a 5 because resistance is mostly sidestepped or redirected rather than actively softened through evocative reflection.
