# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm unconditional positive regard throughout — normalizing Alessia's experience, affirming her approach, and challenging self-deprecating framing with warmth. Never labels, judges, or minimizes client concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that capture the underlying emotional and cognitive experience, not merely restating content. Skillfully tracks and reflects Alessia's shift from uncertainty to concrete momentum.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target concern with logical progression from concern identification to action plan. Minor deduction for a tangential question about why phenelzine was stopped and not revisiting the side effects topic Alessia initially raised.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal power dynamic with the psychiatrist contributing clinical knowledge as input for Alessia's decision-making rather than expert directive. Client ideas consistently shape session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All language framed as invitations and choices — never 'you should' or 'you need to.' Agency explicitly attributed to the client ('that momentum is yours'), and every decision point offers choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation and solutions from within Alessia rather than providing reasons for change. The final summary explicitly attributes all progress to her own agency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client directs agenda, chooses focus, selects the action, and sets the timeline — the psychiatrist functions as a facilitator who helps Alessia access her own knowledge. Textbook MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reflects and amplifies Alessia's change talk, evoking ability language and reinforcing momentum. Rated 4 because change talk is somewhat limited in scope, primarily focused on the phone call rather than broader health management.
