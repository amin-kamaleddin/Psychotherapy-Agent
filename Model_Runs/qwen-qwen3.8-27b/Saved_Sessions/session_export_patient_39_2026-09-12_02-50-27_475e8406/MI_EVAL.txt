# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client's limitations. The warmth is sometimes formulaic and mildly evaluative, such as describing choices as "wise" or "sensible."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture both positive experience and ambivalence, such as wanting to move while fearing pain or failure. Empathy is solid but often remains cognitive and does not fully explore deeper feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear goal and moves logically toward a concrete plan. Direction could be stronger by linking the plan more explicitly to the client's values and medical goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is collaborative and invites the client to choose among options. The psychiatrist still provides substantial structure and suggestions, which may reduce client-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the client's freedom to choose and respects his low-key preference. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly emphasizing client ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as trying shoulder rolls, but does not deeply explore values, importance, or confidence. The psychiatrist often supplies reasons for change rather than drawing out the client's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids being fully directive. However, the psychiatrist takes on an expert problem-solving role by offering a list of options and specific movement cues.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports action and helps the client identify a manageable step. Change talk is elicited but not consistently expanded, amplified, or connected to deeper motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk rather than arguing against it. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflection or turning sustain talk, and the final concern about not doing it again is not fully addressed.
