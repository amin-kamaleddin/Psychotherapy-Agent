# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive of Jamal’s ambivalence. The warmth was somewhat functional and did not consistently affirm his strengths or worth beyond the problem area.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledged the tension between peer belonging and avoiding alcohol, but they remained somewhat surface-level. Deeper feelings, identity concerns, or the emotional cost of fitting in were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior: reducing alcohol use while managing team and social concerns. There was minimal topic drift, with questions and suggestions consistently tied to the specific issue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for Jamal’s preferences and allowed him to choose a plan, showing some collaboration. However, most of the plan content came from the counselor, limiting the extent to which Jamal’s own ideas drove the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged Jamal’s choice and avoided pressuring him into full abstinence. Autonomy support was weakened by frequent provision of specific scripts and compliance-oriented options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some open questions early in the session, but the counselor quickly shifted to providing solutions. Evocative techniques to draw out Jamal’s own values, motivations, or confidence were limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began with an attempt to invite Jamal’s perspective, but it became largely expert-driven. The counselor acted more as a problem-solver than a partner, with Jamal selecting from predefined options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was present but thin and not consistently developed. The counselor did not frequently reflect, amplify, or build on Jamal’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged reasons for staying in the group without alcohol, which is preferable to challenging Jamal. However, double-sided reflections and skillful redirection to change were not consistently used.
