# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, even when the patient is resistant or disengaged. There is no blame, shame, or pressure related to nonadherence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate, specific, and emotionally attuned to the patient’s concerns about control and treatment. However, some responses remain surface-level and do not fully explore underlying ambivalence, guilt, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with some focus on making treatment feel more like a choice, but quickly drifts toward breaks, coffee, quiet, and future contact. The specific target behavior of medication adherence is not consistently maintained as the session focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows the patient’s preferences and collaborates on a simple, low-pressure contact plan. However, the patient’s direct request for factual information is not addressed, and a shared agenda is not fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with strong emphasis on the patient’s freedom, choice, and ownership of decisions. There is no chasing, pressure, or controlling language, even as the patient moves toward disengagement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions that invite the patient to reflect on comfort, space, and ways to reconnect. However, these questions mostly evoke autonomy and engagement rather than intrinsic motivation for medication change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The stance is strongly collaborative, treating the patient as the expert on his own choices. The main gap is bypassing the patient’s stated request for direct information and not fully equalizing agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited active elicitation or expansion of change talk related to medication adherence or treatment engagement. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore the patient’s values, health-related goals, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or lecturing and uses some autonomy-focused reframing, which helps reduce resistance. However, sustain talk is not consistently redirected toward change or explored through balanced, double-sided reflections.
