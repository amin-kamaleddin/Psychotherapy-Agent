# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and could more explicitly acknowledge the stress of being mandated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate but often surface-level reflections of the client's concerns. Deeper emotions such as fear of being judged and worry about legal consequences are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on completing the court-mandated hours before the deadline. The focus is primarily logistical rather than strategically guiding the client toward internal motivation and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and adjusts the plan based on his preferences. However, the interaction remains largely clinician-led rather than a truly equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and avoids pressuring the client to follow a specific plan. Frequent prescriptive solutions, scripts, and checklists reduce the emphasis on the client's ownership and freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions about preferences and schedules. However, the client's intrinsic motivation, confidence, and personal reasons for change are not consistently elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for client preferences and incorporates some of his ideas into the plan. Yet the psychiatrist often assumes the expert problem-solver role by introducing detailed scripts, messages, and fallback plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are moments when the client expresses intention to complete the hours, but these are not consistently reflected or amplified. The psychiatrist often shifts to logistics instead of strengthening change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges obstacles without arguing or reinforcing avoidance. However, repeated focus on potential barriers is not consistently redirected toward the client's motivation, ability, or next step.
