# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Elise’s ambivalence without blame. Acceptance is solid but somewhat procedural, with limited individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Elise’s fear of social loss, difference, and uncertainty about remaining “fun.” Empathy is strong but repetitive and does not fully explore deeper self-image concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation within the context of friendship and social connection. Direction is clear but can feel circular without stronger summarizing or consolidation of next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Elise’s concerns and ideas, creating a generally collaborative interaction. Collaboration is limited by repeated framing and little explicit power-sharing or agenda negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise is repeatedly invited to generate her own answers without direct advice or pressure. Autonomy support is strong but could include more explicit language that the decision is entirely hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite Elise’s ideas and some change talk. Evocation is limited because questions focus more on problem-solving than drawing out values, hope, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a collaborative stance and allows Elise’s contributions to shape the session. Partnership is somewhat guided by the psychiatrist’s repeated framing and question structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise offers some change talk about wanting to be a non-smoking version of herself and trying a small step. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or systematically expand this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Elise’s sustain talk and uses some rolling-with responses. However, it does not consistently soften or reframe sustain talk toward change, relying repeatedly on health-protection framing.
