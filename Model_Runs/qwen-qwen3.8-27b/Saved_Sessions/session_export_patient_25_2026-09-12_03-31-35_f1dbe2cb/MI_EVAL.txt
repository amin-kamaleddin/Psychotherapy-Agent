# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, supportive stance with clear affirmations and nonjudgmental tone. Acceptance is slightly formulaic and more tied to progress than to explicit unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of relief, pride, and ambivalence. Empathy is limited by frequent shifts into advice-giving and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use at parties and moves logically from concerns to a practical plan. Direction could be strengthened by tying movement more consistently to the client’s own goals and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes to the final plan, and there is visible co-creation in parts of the session. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist often provides structured options and scripts before eliciting the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses optional, non-pressuring language and respects the client’s decisions. Support could be stronger by reducing directive script suggestions and more explicitly affirming the client’s full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions, a confidence scale, and some invitation to identify strengths. Evocation is limited because the session is often driven by clinician suggestions rather than by the client’s own motivational language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear partnership elements, including asking the client to choose options and co-create a plan. The psychiatrist frequently shifts into an expert/advisor role, which weakens the sense that the client is the primary source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates meaningful change talk about avoiding the fuzzy feeling and saying no without making it a big deal. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or strategically expand that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk and generally keeps the session moving toward a workable plan. The approach lacks more skillful double-sided reflections to help the client hold and work with ambivalence.
