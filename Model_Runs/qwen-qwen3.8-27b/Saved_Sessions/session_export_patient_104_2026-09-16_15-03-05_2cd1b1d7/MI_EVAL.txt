# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental regard, affirming the client's care and effort. There is no blaming, labeling, or discouragement, and the client's fatigue is met with validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's confusion, fatigue, and ambivalence, often with strong complex reflections. It could go deeper into underlying shame, fear of being a bad parent, and the need to feel not crazy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, clinically appropriate focus on distinguishing hunger from comfort and reducing 3 a.m. guessing. It skillfully moves from broad worry to a specific, manageable, client-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly frames the work as joint, using language like we and inviting the client to co-create the review and tracking method. The final star system is a shared construction rather than a prescribed intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers choices and avoids directive control, allowing the client to select a low-effort option such as a star. It could more explicitly affirm the client's ownership and freedom to choose or stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively draw out the client's own observations, interpretations, and emerging plan, including the star idea. It does not fully explore deeper values, identity, or self-trust as a responsive parent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as an expert on her baby and contributes ideas that shape the session. The psychiatrist still sets much of the framework and could transfer more agenda ownership to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates conditions for the client to voice goals, preferences, and a concrete self-monitoring idea. Change talk is elicited but not always amplified, summarized, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, normalizing the client's fear of guessing wrong or being judged. It could use stronger double-sided reflections to pivot fear toward low-pressure experimentation.
