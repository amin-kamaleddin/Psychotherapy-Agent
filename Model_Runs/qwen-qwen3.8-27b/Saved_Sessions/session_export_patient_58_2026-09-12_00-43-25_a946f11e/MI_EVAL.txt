# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and generally supports the patient’s preferences and boundaries. Acceptance remains somewhat procedural rather than fully individualized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some early perspective-taking about frustration and pressure, but reflections often remain surface-level. Meaningful feelings about being nagged, tiredness, and wanting autonomy are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session consistently stays focused on school punctuality and attendance from the opening concern through the final plan. The structure and next steps keep the interaction purposeful and on target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and the psychiatrist incorporates the patient’s chosen steps, but much of the plan is clinician-led. The interaction feels more like a structured consultant offering systems than a fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, respects refusal to message the mother, and supports the patient’s chosen plan. However, repeated reminders, templates, and directives can feel over-managing and may limit ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly supplies practical strategies and tracking rather than eliciting the patient’s own values, reasons, and confidence for change. Important evocative questions about why punctuality matters personally are largely missing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows the patient’s choices to shape the plan, which supports some partnership. The session remains somewhat expert-driven, with long lists of strategies and reminders rather than fully equal co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers several change-related statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen them. Questions and responses focus more on action steps than internal motivation or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tolerates sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it, including acknowledgment of pressure from the mother. It does not consistently redirect sustain talk toward autonomy, values, or change.
