# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing fatigue, cost concerns, and limits on change. The stance is strong but somewhat procedural and less individualized in its warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's burden, ambivalence, and practical constraints. The empathy remains mostly cognitive and practical rather than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma-related nighttime symptoms and moves toward a concrete, small experiment. It occasionally broadens into multiple options, which slightly diffuses the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts repeatedly to the client's preferences and helps shape a doable plan. The collaboration is effective, but the therapist remains the main source of ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choice, fatigue, and boundaries are strongly respected, including the option to skip or stop. Support is strong but not maximally non-directive because several specific tips are offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful questions elicit timing, cues, confidence, and a success criterion. Deeper client-generated values, reasons, and motivations for change are not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's preferences clearly shape the final plan, such as the chair cue and 30-second task. However, the psychiatrist often supplies many solutions before fully eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, especially willingness to try a small experiment and define what success would feel like. The psychiatrist rarely amplifies or deepens the client's change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Resistance about the dog, cost, noise, and extra chores is validated rather than challenged. This sustain talk is used helpfully to narrow the plan, though with fewer advanced double-sided reflections.
