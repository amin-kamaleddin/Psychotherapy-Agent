# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful tone but uses conditional praise and frequent medical warnings. The paternalistic advice and repeated safety rules limit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses are brief and often followed immediately by practical advice. The psychiatrist misses key emotions related to fear, independence, pride, and social anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on alcohol reduction, fall risk, and medication safety. Recaps help maintain a clear, goal-oriented direction, though it is somewhat rigid.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction feels more like a clinician delivering a structured intervention than a collaborative exploration. Unilateral provision of practical tips limits true partner-like involvement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices and affirms the client's right to decide. However, the volume of prescriptive advice and repeated instructions undermines autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies mainly on providing reasons for change and specific strategies. The client's own motivations and subtle shifts toward change are under-explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a directive, expert-driven stance throughout the session. The client's input does not substantially influence the plan or agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few attempts to elicit change talk, but they are often followed by provider-generated reasons. The client's own change talk is not effectively amplified or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk and responds neutrally. However, there is little use of double-sided reflections or redirection to resolve ambivalence.
