# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client’s frustration without blame. This is not a 5 because the acceptance can feel formulaic and overly aligned with the client’s externalizing narrative.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s anger and practical stress, showing strong perspective-taking. The empathy stops short of fully addressing the ambivalence and deeper affect beneath the anger.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the appointment delay and the client’s immediate need for a decision. The direction remains too logistical and does not consistently guide the client toward broader goals or change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts collaboration through open questions and shared language, but often steps into a problem-solving or managerial role. The repeated use of structured options makes the interaction feel counselor-driven rather than genuinely co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear attempts to avoid coercion and acknowledge the client’s choice. These efforts are undermined by repeated option menus and scripts, which can feel autonomy-undermining given the client’s repeated refusals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some questions that elicit preferences, but they remain focused on immediate action rather than deeper motivation. The session does not consistently draw out the client’s own values, reasons for change, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows intention to collaborate by asking for the client’s input and preferences. The partnership is weakened by repeated structured choices, which the client explicitly rejects as feeling directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers statements that could be developed into change talk, such as needing their time back or knowing their boundary. The psychiatrist rarely expands on these statements, instead shifting back to logistical options and action scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client engages in considerable sustain talk by externalizing responsibility, and the psychiatrist often agrees in ways that reinforce this stance. There is little use of MI strategies to broaden focus, reflect ambivalence, or encourage personal agency.
