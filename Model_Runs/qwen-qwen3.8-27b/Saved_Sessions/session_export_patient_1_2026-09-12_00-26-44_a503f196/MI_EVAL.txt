# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a generally polite and non-judgmental tone, but comments like “minimizing” can feel labeling. Validation is present, though it does not fully capture the patient’s emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are frequent but often formulaic, especially with “I hear you.” The provider addresses practical concerns more than the underlying frustration, ambivalence, or health-related anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation lacks a clear MI-aligned direction after the patient rejects change talk. The provider repeatedly returns to advice and warning signs instead of pivoting to values, goals, or other areas of change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is more expert-driven than collaborative. The provider presents a plan rather than co-creating one with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient’s choice and time constraints, which supports autonomy. However, repeated directive advice undermines the sense of self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is little genuine evocation of the patient’s own motivations, values, or concerns. The provider relies mainly on medical information rather than drawing out intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The dynamic quickly shifts from a listening stance to a clinician-advisory model. The patient’s stated goal of completing the dental exam is not integrated into a shared process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is only one brief attempt to explore harm reduction or cutting down. When the patient refuses, the provider does not further elicit, amplify, or reinforce any change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The provider directly challenges the patient’s minimization with medical facts rather than reflecting ambivalence. This confrontational approach increases resistance, as shown by the patient’s reaction.
