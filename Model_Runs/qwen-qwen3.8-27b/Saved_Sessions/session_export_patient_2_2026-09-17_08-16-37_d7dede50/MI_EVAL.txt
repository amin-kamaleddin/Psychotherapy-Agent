# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, warmth, and unconditional positive regard without judgment or criticism. Aisha’s minimization of smoking is met with validation rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, with accurate reflections of Aisha’s ambivalence, exhaustion, and need for a pause. Some phrasing is interpretive rather than fully grounded in Aisha’s own words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation remains tightly focused on evening smoking and the specific goal of managing the behavior. There is no drift, and questions keep Aisha centered on the target behavior and next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as joint exploration and invites Aisha’s perspective throughout. Aisha’s ideas clearly shape the emerging plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Aisha’s choice and ownership are consistently supported without pressure or imposed commitments. The psychiatrist accepts a small, tentative plan and respects Aisha’s pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Aisha’s own motivation and practical ideas, especially around protecting her son. Deeper change talk around importance, values, or long-term identity is less fully developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally positions Aisha as the expert on her own evening and lets her drive much of the session. A few interpretive statements and suggestions make the interaction slightly less balanced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha generates concrete change talk and a small, meaningful plan. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify and deepen change talk across importance, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Aisha and reframes smoking as related to the pause rather than the behavior itself. The response is effective, though sustain talk remains fairly present and is not consistently redirected with more skillful double-sided reflections.
