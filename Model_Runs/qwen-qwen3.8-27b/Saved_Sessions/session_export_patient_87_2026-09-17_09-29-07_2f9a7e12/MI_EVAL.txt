# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming while normalizing the patient's difficulty. It reaches only 4 because the warmth is somewhat professional and occasionally evaluative, with no explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture the patient's ambivalence, exhaustion, and family-related tension. It does not reach 5 because the reflections become repetitive and do not fully explore underlying emotions such as shame, fear, or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing evening overeating and moves toward concrete, behaviorally specific strategies. It is less than 5 because the conversation becomes somewhat circular and lacks a clear consolidation or closing commitment step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports collaborative planning and allows the patient's ideas to drive the proposed steps. It does not reach 5 because several leading questions and prompts create a slightly consultant-driven dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasizes a "good enough" routine, avoids pressure, and preserves the patient's ownership of decisions. It does not reach 5 because it lacks more explicit language about the patient's choice, control, and self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out practical change ideas through open-ended questions but does not deeply evoke the patient's values, motivation, or confidence. The final opportunity to strengthen commitment and confidence is missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats the patient as the expert on his own life and invites him to define what matters and what is feasible. It is limited from 5 because some leading suggestions and consecutive questions reduce the sense of full equality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change-oriented ideas and tentative commitment from the patient. It does not reach 4 because it does not consistently amplify, reinforce, or strategically expand that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds respectfully to sustain talk without arguing or dismissing the patient's concerns. It does not reach 4 because it relies more on normalization than on double-sided reflections or effective redirection toward change.
