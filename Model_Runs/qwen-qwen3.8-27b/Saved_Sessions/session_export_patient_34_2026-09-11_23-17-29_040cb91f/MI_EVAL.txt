# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some affirming language but also aligns with the client’s minimization and defensiveness. This limits genuine unconditional positive regard because the client’s role and choices are not acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses reflect surface emotions such as frustration and anger, but they remain shallow and one-sided. The psychiatrist does not explore the deeper ambivalence, shame, or conflict between social identity and academic concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has a clear procedural focus on penalties, statements, and next steps. However, the direction is more legal/advisory than MI, with little movement toward the client’s drinking behavior or motivation for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language but takes an expert-advisor role in setting the agenda and proposing solutions. The client’s input is acknowledged, but it does not meaningfully shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not pressure the client directly, but repeatedly recommends specific actions and offers to complete them. This advisory stance can undermine the client’s autonomy and self-directed decision making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is little effort to evoke the client’s own values, goals, or reasons for change. Most questions are closed or procedural, and the client’s few change-adjacent statements are not explored or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s preferences but behaves more like an advocate or problem-solver than an equal partner. The session is service-oriented rather than driven by the client’s own perspective and wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not actively evoke or cultivate desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. The session focuses on external consequences rather than the client’s internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk is strongly reinforced through agreement with the client’s blame avoidance and minimization. Instead of softening resistance, the psychiatrist validates the client’s externalized framing.
