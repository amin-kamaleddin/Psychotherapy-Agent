# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Samir's progress without becoming defensive. Acceptance is competent but somewhat procedural, with limited warm affirmation of his ambivalence and identity tied to smoking, work, and coping.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Samir's fear, practical constraints, and chosen strategies. Empathy is mainly content-focused and could more fully name deeper emotions such as fear of incapacitation, loss of independence, and resignation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on asthma control, smoking and dust reduction, driving stress, and avoiding the ER. It is well structured and moves efficiently toward concrete, target-behavior steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint language and adapts to Samir's reworded cues, creating a collaborative plan. However, he still supplies many options and scripts, keeping the interaction more clinician-guided than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly uses choice language, respects Samir's partial change stance, and avoids demanding abstinence. Repeated strategy suggestions and the directive emergency statement leave less explicit ownership and some mild pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions that elicit Samir's goals, confidence level, and practical self-monitoring ideas. Deeper evocation of values, meaning, and identity-based motivation would strengthen the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Samir as the expert, invites input, and lets Samir shape the plan with practical cues and his wife's accountability. The structured menu of options and scripts remains somewhat clinician-directed rather than fully power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence scaling successfully evoke meaningful change talk focused on protecting his kids and avoiding the ER. The score is limited by less systematic exploration of DARN change talk and less amplification or elaboration of that talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts sustain talk without arguing or judging, which avoids confrontation. He does not sufficiently use double-sided reflections or explore the meaning behind Samir's resistance, so ambivalence remains less resolved.
