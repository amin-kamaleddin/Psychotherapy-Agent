# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, validating Jordan’s feelings and ambivalence. However, the warmth is somewhat generic and often shifts quickly into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes a genuine effort to understand Jordan’s perspective and reflects on the social conflict. Empathy is limited by surface-level validation and frequent moves toward practical advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on shoplifting, urges, social triggers, and concrete behavior management. The direction is strong but more behaviorally directive than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to some of Jordan’s concerns and preferences, such as the car/timer idea. However, the session often feels driven by the interviewer presenting a menu of strategies rather than joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few invitations for choice, but the psychiatrist frequently offers specific actions, scripts, and plans. This directive style undermines Jordan’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with open questions and asks about antecedents, but much of the session shifts to external advice. There is limited eliciting of Jordan’s own values, motivations, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently position Jordan as the expert on his own life. Many moments are expert-driven, with the counselor supplying scripts, strategies, and treatment suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan expresses several clear statements of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen them. Responses often move to logistics instead of strengthening motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or amplify Jordan’s sustain talk and validates the difficulty of his concerns. However, the counselor does not consistently soften ambivalence through double-sided reflections or pivoting to change motives.
