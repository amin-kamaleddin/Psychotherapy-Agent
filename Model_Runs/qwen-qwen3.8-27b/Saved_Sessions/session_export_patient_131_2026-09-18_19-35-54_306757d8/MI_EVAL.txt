# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard through multiple validations that normalize and reframe Wiremu's experiences (e.g., 'that's not weakness, that's a value you hold'). Sustained warmth is individualized and never formulaic, treating self-doubt and ambivalence with genuine respect rather than attempting to override it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface-level to capture identity-level meaning, such as identifying the 'whole identity you've built around it' when Wiremu names his 'I can handle it' framework. The psychiatrist accurately tracks the emotional undercurrent (nervousness, pride, identity conflict) and reflects it with progressive depth rather than repetition.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear arc from establishing motivation (kids) through identifying barriers, resolving ambivalence, and arriving at a concrete client-generated plan. Each step builds logically on the previous one without wandering, and the psychiatrist quickly refocuses when Wiremu brings up tangential decisions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration where Wiremu's contributions directly shape direction, from his choice of gym over the aunty to his sequencing of confidence before openness with kids. The psychiatrist uses collaborative language ('I'm curious,' 'I'm wondering') and never corrects, overrides, or redirects based on clinical judgment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision point in the session is a client decision, with the psychiatrist never using directive language like 'you should' or 'you need to.' Autonomy is supported structurally — the entire session is organized around Wiremu's own choices, values, and problem-solving rather than the psychiatrist's recommendations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out Wiremu's own reasons, values, and motivations through questions rather than providing them externally. Change talk is amplified as it appears ('You just named something that's been holding you back'), and the session produces rich, client-generated language across all DARN-C categories.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never provides information, advice, or recommendations — every clinical move is framed as a question back to Wiremu. The psychiatrist acts as a mirror and amplifier, explicitly honoring Wiremu's knowledge of his own life ('You know exactly where the trap is and you're designing a way out of it').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are skillfully designed to draw out Wiremu's own language for change, producing substantial change talk across desire, ability, reasons, and commitment. Each question builds on the previous change talk rather than starting from scratch, creating a progressive deepening of motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged, validated, and gently redirected toward change without the client ever feeling argued with or dismissed. The psychiatrist never reinforces reluctance (e.g., about the aunty/uncle) but also never dismisses it, instead using double-sided reflections followed by resolving questions.
