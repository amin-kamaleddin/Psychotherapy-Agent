# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental tone with strong affirmations like 'that's not nothing.' Warmth remains somewhat measured and professional rather than deeply personal, and the closing question shifts to cognitive content at the most vulnerable moment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with effective captures of the felt sense, particularly around the waiting and dignity themes. Could have gone deeper emotionally at moments, such as when Tariq described counting weeks and worrying about the reunion conversation with his mom.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session maintains a general focus on the presenting concern but lacks a clear target behavior or through-line toward a specific change goal. The conversation moves between the phone issue, officer relationship, waiting period, and family connection without progressively narrowing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with 'I wonder' framing and open-ended questions that invite Tariq's perspective and use his own language. Could be deeper through explicit co-creation of a plan or joint evaluation of session direction rather than remaining at the level of tone and question style.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and agency, affirming Tariq's right to set boundaries without pressing for specific actions. Does not address the inherent tension between Tariq's desire for autonomy and the institutional constraints of detention.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes several reasonable evocation attempts but remains at a single level of depth without progressively drawing out values, capability, or future vision. Lacks scaling questions, confidence exploration, or structured elicitation of specific behavioral commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with Tariq positioned as expert on his own experience and no lecturing or assumed expertise. Occasionally steers in a somewhat directed manner and does not explicitly negotiate the agenda or invite Tariq to set session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some reasonable evocation questions and Tariq provides forward-looking language, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or build on his change talk. Misses opportunities to reflect statements like 'I'd wait until they talked to me first' as a source of strength or agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tariq's sustain talk around blaming the officer and feeling unfairly targeted is neither reinforced nor effectively softened — mostly allowed to sit without active work. No use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to gently explore Tariq's own perspective on the situation.
