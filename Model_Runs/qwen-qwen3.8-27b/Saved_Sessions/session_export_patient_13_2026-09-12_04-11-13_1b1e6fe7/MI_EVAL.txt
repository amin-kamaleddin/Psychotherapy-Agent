# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses warm, nonjudgmental, validating language and normalizes the client's fear without criticizing the delay. Slightly generic affirmations and a minor misattunement to the client's feeling of being “cornered” keep it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate, validating reflections of feeling torn, overwhelmed, and afraid of waiting, testing, and being judged. Does not fully explore the deeper personal meaning of “getting bigger,” feeling stuck, or her identity/exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stays focused on the target behavior of taking a small step toward medical care without drifting off topic. Could be stronger by more explicitly framing the session goal and using less problem-solving, more MI-guided momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively collaborates on a text-only message and respects the client’s preferences to save rather than send. Frequent clinician-generated options and scripts keep the clinician in the lead, preventing a 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice, control, and no pressure by emphasizing the client can wait and that saving the message counts as progress. The client explicitly experiences this as autonomy-supportive, which is a major strength.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some open and evocative questions early and affirms emerging change talk. Quickly shifts to concrete options and scripts, with limited exploration of values, confidence, or deeper motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites the client’s choices and positions her as the decision-maker for what is doable. The clinician still frequently leads the structure and generates options/scripts, so partnership is good but not fully client-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some low-commitment change talk, especially around saving and copying the message. Does not systematically develop reasons, importance, confidence, or values, so change talk is present but underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and validates the client’s fears and reasons for avoiding care. Could do more with double-sided reflections, affirming the protective function of avoidance, and reframing sustain talk toward change.
