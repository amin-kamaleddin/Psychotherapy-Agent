# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistent nonjudgmental stance, validating Luis's frustration and acknowledging his capabilities throughout. Acceptance could be deepened with warmer affirmations of Luis's worth beyond his compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captures both surface content and underlying emotional meaning, including the car metaphor and the weight of managing alone. Some reflections are somewhat formulaic and could delve deeper into fear, helplessness, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, coherent focus on asthma management with a logical trajectory from frustration to identifying the personal line to understanding barriers. A 5 would require more strategic momentum toward a concrete next step or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Luis's perspective and avoids dominating the conversation or imposing an agenda. Collaboration could be strengthened by more explicitly co-creating the session's direction, such as asking where Luis wants to go with the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong non-directive stance with consistent positioning of Luis as the decision-maker and no advice-giving or pressure. Could be more explicit in affirming Luis's ownership of the change process, particularly in moments where he feels pressured by the medical system.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective at drawing out Luis's own values, metaphors, and reasons for change rather than providing them. Evocation could be more sustained and deliberate, with greater amplification and building on the change talk that emerges.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear collaborative language that conveys expertise about Luis's life resides with him, while avoiding the expert role. The approach is somewhat passive in its partnership rather than actively inviting Luis to shape the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evokes change talk including Luis's own line, his car metaphor, and his recognition that 'slowing down isn't really an option anymore.' The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or deepen these statements when they emerge.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by acknowledging it without arguing or dismissing, and uses a skillful double-sided reflection distinguishing 'I don't want to fix it' from 'I don't understand why this is the fix.' Could do more to actively reframe sustain talk or help Luis articulate the cost of the status quo in his own words.
