# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while affirming the client’s culture, values, and autonomy. There is no shaming, lecturing, or moralizing, making the client feel safely respected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong, accurate reflections of the client’s practical concerns, cultural meaning, and desire for control. Empathy is high but not perfect, partly because the repeated partner cue question suggests a brief lapse in real-time tracking and less depth in emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and moves from ambivalence to a clear, actionable plan. The direction is consistent, purposeful, and clinically appropriate.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the session as a joint process and allows the client and partner to shape the final plan. The plan emerges collaboratively rather than being imposed by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes that the decision remains the client’s own and avoids directive or coercive language. The client is supported in making a plan that feels self-directed and under her control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes the client’s own reasons, values, and practical solutions for change. However, the use of structured options and a somewhat prescriptive format limits how fully client-led the evocation is.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a strong collaborative stance and lets the client’s preferences shape the session and plan. Partnership is not a 5 because the repeated question and structured agenda suggest some clinician-led elements.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits change talk and helps the client move toward concrete, self-directed commitment. It falls short of a 5 because the change talk could be deepened further with scaling, explicit summarization, and fuller value exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by validating the client’s perspective without arguing or reinforcing minimization. Sustain talk is acknowledged and then gently redirected toward the client’s values, autonomy, and plan.
