# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client's intentions. Acceptance was somewhat generic and occasionally paired with corrective information, limiting fully individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered adequate empathy and normalized the client's stress. Deeper ambivalence, values, and emotional conflict were not consistently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and produced a clear practical plan. Momentum toward a client-driven decision tied to values and readiness was less consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was meaningful practical collaboration in creating questions, a card, and a script. The psychiatrist often supplied the content and structure, while the client's values guided less.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and direct pressure was avoided. Autonomy was undercut by frequent expert-driven information, scripts, and guidance rather than explicit decision ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions began the session and invited the client to identify her needs. The session often shifted to education and problem-solving rather than eliciting the client's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session included choices, input, and tailoring, showing some partnership. The overall stance remained expert-driven, reducing the client's role as the primary decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged and was sometimes supported. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen the client's own motivation, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was often redirected toward practical engagement. Responses tended to be informational or corrective rather than reflectively processing the resistance.
