# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect and nonjudgment without shaming or lecturing. Acceptance is slightly formulaic at times and occasionally competes with advice-giving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good perspective-taking is present, especially later in the session. Empathy is inconsistent early on, with more factual responses than reflections of Jordan’s ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the flu vaccine and related risk to Jordan’s grandparents. It could be stronger if the target behavior and MI focus were more explicitly guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s input and allows him to shape the plan and scripts. Collaboration is sometimes uneven because the psychiatrist also offers expert advice and prewritten options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported by respecting Jordan’s choice and avoiding pressure. Some prescriptive suggestions, such as scripts and specific steps, slightly reduce autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some open-ended questions and opportunities for change talk. Evocation is limited by too much information-giving and not enough use of scaling, values, or confidence questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a collaborative atmosphere and invites Jordan to choose. A fully collaborative stance would involve less of an expert or advisory role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan produces change talk about protecting his grandparents, and the psychiatrist acknowledges this. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk with evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct argument and later responds well to Jordan’s desire not to feel pushed. Some early responses use a “yes, but” pattern that can feel counterargumentative.
