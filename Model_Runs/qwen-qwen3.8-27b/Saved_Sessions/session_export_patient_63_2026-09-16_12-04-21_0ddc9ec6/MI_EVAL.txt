# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician treats Jordan with basic respect and avoids judgment, especially early in the session. However, acceptance becomes more procedural and less warmly affirming when the conversation shifts to problem-solving and tactics.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several strong reflective statements that capture Jordan’s ambivalence, shame, and fear of being cornered. The main weakness is that empathy often gives way quickly to advice or behavioral problem-solving rather than staying fully with the emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on shoplifting and relapse prevention, with little drift into unrelated topics. The direction is somewhat too narrow and tactical, focusing heavily on exit scripts and in-the-moment strategies rather than broader motivational goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan does make choices and reject some suggestions, so the interaction is not entirely one-directional. However, the clinician supplies most of the content and structure, making the collaboration feel more like selecting from a menu than co-creating a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks which options feel doable and respects Jordan’s rejections of some tactics. Autonomy support is weakened by frequent prescriptive instructions and scripts that imply the clinician is directing the behavioral plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some change talk early on, but does not consistently draw out Jordan’s values, intrinsic reasons, confidence, or vision for change. The session quickly shifts from exploration to external strategies, which limits evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with a collaborative stance but quickly becomes unbalanced, with the clinician taking on the expert role by offering many scripts and tactics. Jordan’s expertise about his friends and social environment is not fully used to shape the direction of the work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, such as Jordan’s desire to stop and frustration with court involvement, but it is not developed or amplified. The clinician does not systematically explore desire, ability, reasons, or need for change before moving to tactics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk around peer influence, embarrassment, and fear of a scene is present but not effectively softened or redirected toward change. Instead of reflecting ambivalence or exploring what Jordan is trying to protect, the clinician mainly offers additional behavioral options.
