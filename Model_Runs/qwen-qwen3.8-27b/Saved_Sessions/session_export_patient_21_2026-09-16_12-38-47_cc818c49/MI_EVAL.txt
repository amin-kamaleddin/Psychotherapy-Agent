# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Camila's limits without shaming ambivalence. It stopped short of a 5 because some affirmations emphasized planning and high confidence rather than consistently deepening individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Camila's main concerns, such as wanting more energy without overdoing it and worries about walking alone or feeling awkward. Empathy could be stronger by connecting more deeply to the emotional meaning of her fatigue, fear of failure, or relationship concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior, a realistic after-dinner walk, and moved logically from importance and readiness to barriers, planning, and a concrete next step. Minor redundancy occurred, but the counselor kept the session well structured and goal-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor worked with Camila by using her preferences, constraints, and ideas to shape the walking plan. It was not a 5 because the counselor supplied many options and problem-solving structures rather than consistently inviting more client-generated alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by using phrases like "which feels best" and "no worries if you'd rather not," while avoiding coercion. It fell short of a 5 because some normative suggestions and concrete planning language made the session somewhat more counselor-guided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently drew out Camila's own motivations, confidence, and solutions, helping her articulate a workable plan. It was not a 5 because the therapist often offered specific scripts, options, and strategies before fully eliciting more client-generated reasons or solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction showed a collaborative stance, with Camila's choices meaningfully shaping the session's plan. It stopped short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still provided many options, scripts, and specific suggestions, making the session less fully client-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited change-relevant language by asking about importance, readiness, confidence, and small actions that would make the plan more likely. It was not a 5 because the focus remained more practical and confidence-based rather than deeply exploring values, desire, or personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers were acknowledged without arguing or minimizing, and the counselor helped reframe them into manageable fallbacks such as walking whether or not her husband joins. It was not a 5 because some responses continued to focus on the source of worry rather than more consistently pivoting toward values, confidence, or preferred action.
