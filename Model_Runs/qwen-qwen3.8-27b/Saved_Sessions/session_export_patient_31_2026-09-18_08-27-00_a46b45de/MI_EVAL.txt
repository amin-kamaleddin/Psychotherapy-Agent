# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and never labels the client as a 'bad smoker.' The warmth is genuine but somewhat functional and strategic rather than deeply emotionally present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional empathic accuracy across cognitive, emotional, somatic, and social levels. Reflections are specific, precise, and build directly on what the client actually said, never generic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on the target behavior with a clear, logical progression from understanding function to a concrete implementation plan. No aimless exploration or topic drift at any point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conducted as a true collaboration where every key decision—time window, method, strength, and routine hook—is co-constructed by the client. The psychiatrist consistently positions as facilitator rather than authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirms client choice without pressure throughout (e.g., 'That's your call, not a promise, not a line crossed'). Never implies consequences of not changing and reinforces autonomy even when faced with resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every piece of information in the client's final plan is generated by the client himself, elicited purely through open questions. The psychiatrist provides zero reasons for change—no statistics, no health risks, no moral arguments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside with the client through questions like 'What's your gut telling you right now?' and 'What do you think would feel manageable?' Never prescribes or dictates a course of action.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits change talk through well-crafted questions that invite the client to articulate his own vision and criteria. Slightly less organic than it could be, as the psychiatrist clearly steers toward the patch solution through a strategic sequence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally handles sustain talk by reframing, honoring boundaries, and using resistant statements as springboards for exploration rather than correction. The 'I just need air' moment is particularly masterful—validating while simultaneously eliciting change-relevant information.
