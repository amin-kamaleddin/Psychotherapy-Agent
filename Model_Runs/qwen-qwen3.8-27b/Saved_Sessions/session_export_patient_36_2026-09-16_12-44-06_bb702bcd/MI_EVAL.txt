# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Elena’s guilt, exhaustion, and worry. The warmth is solid but can feel procedural, with some language that emphasizes performance rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening and perspective-taking are strong, especially around ambivalence about rest and guilt. The session could have explored the emotional meaning of needing to “earn” rest and past cycles in more depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on a specific after-work boundary tied to Elena’s values and self-respect. Some time is spent on wording details and multiple supports, but the overall trajectory remains relevant and purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena contributes meaningful changes, such as the 8:00 start time, coffee, and pre-scheduling Monday tasks. However, many options and polished drafts are therapist-generated, so Elena is not fully the primary source of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choice and accepts some rejections, which supports Elena’s autonomy. Repeated drafts, scripts, and suggested supports can create subtle pressure to adopt the therapist’s plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions effectively elicit change talk, self-respect, and doable next steps. Later, the session becomes more directive, and the question “What’s different this time?” is missed as an opportunity to elicit Elena’s own strengths and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is genuine partnership in that Elena shapes parts of the plan, including timing, wording, and routines. The interaction becomes more therapist-led when the psychiatrist offers many concrete options and drafts, which makes power sharing uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generates useful change talk, such as standing up for herself and making a values-aligned choice. The psychiatrist often supplies options and scripts instead of further amplifying Elena’s own reasons, confidence, and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk, including guilt and fear of failure, is met with normalization and nonjudgmental lapse language rather than argument. The score is limited because the session spends considerable time detailing failure modes and added controls, which can keep attention on risk.
