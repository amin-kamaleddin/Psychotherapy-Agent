# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, validating the patient’s concerns about medication and retirement identity. It is strong but occasionally formulaic, without fully individualizing the deeper fear of becoming useless or broken.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides good reflections of ambivalence, especially around wanting to lead while protecting health. It could be deeper by more directly naming the emotional fear of loss of identity and usefulness behind the sustain talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on cholesterol, medication, and the patient’s goal of staying active in retirement. The session consistently returns to the target behavior and guides toward a practical plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly frames the work as joint and builds on the patient’s own ideas for a simple trial. It remains slightly facilitator-driven in shaping summaries and agenda rather than fully co-constructing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes that the decision is the patient’s to make and avoids pressure or invalidation of hesitation. This explicit support for choice and self-determination is a major strength.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the patient’s own criteria, such as trust, proof, energy, and simplicity. It could deepen by eliciting more values, confidence, and hope behind his motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative and patient-led, with much of the plan articulated by the patient. The psychiatrist still does considerable framing and summarizing, which slightly limits fully equal power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Helps the patient move from hesitation to viewing the medication as a doable, owned safety net. It would strengthen 5-level practice by more explicitly amplifying values-based and confidence-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk respectfully without arguing or minimizing the patient’s concerns. It could better pivot from acknowledged barriers back to values and change talk with more momentum.
