# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard from the first utterance, normalizing the referral and affirming Jamal without judgment. Even when disagreeing with Jamal's self-diminishing statements, the psychiatrist honors his perspective and avoids disapproval or conditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional empathic accuracy, going beyond surface-level paraphrase to capture the emotional logic beneath Jamal's terse statements. Standout complex reflections include the snooze as 'the only small win you've got' and identifying the metacognitive process of 'questioning your own motivation, checking if it counts.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an exceptionally clear and progressive focus on the target behavior (school attendance/punctuality) with zero topic drift. The conversation follows a logical arc from referral through ambivalence, identifying change, exploring the snooze's function, generating a concrete step, and exploring intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration with inclusive framing ('I'm curious,' 'I'm hearing something really specific') and no expert-driven dynamic. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the collaborative nature of Jamal's discovery: 'You just came up with that on your own.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Jamal's ownership and choice, never imposing or prescribing, and directly addresses autonomy with questions like 'What do you think would make this feel less like something being done to you?' The final pushback on 'that's not for me' actually reinforces Jamal's sense of agency by helping him recognize his own desire.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never tells Jamal why he should change; instead uses a series of evocative questions that draw out his own reasons and values. The result is a clear progression from extrinsic motivation ('so people will get off my back') to intrinsic motivation ('a seat that's yours,' 'actually getting what you want').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Jamal, explicitly rejecting the expert role ('I'm not here to fix the alarm') and inviting his own perspective. Uses Jamal's own words to challenge his self-diminishing statements, positioning him as the authority on his own motivations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully guides Jamal from compliance ('stopped the whole thing') through intrinsic desire ('a seat that's yours') to concrete positive vision ('not sprinting, out the door') to intrinsic motivation language ('actually getting what you want'). Each shift is amplified with affirmations like 'That's a real starting point' and 'That's different from where we were a few minutes ago.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well, particularly the effective 'gently push back' on 'that's not for me' and the refusal to reinforce external framing as the primary driver. However, the 'what's the point of fighting the snooze' sustain talk could have been more actively dismantled before moving forward to the solution phase.
