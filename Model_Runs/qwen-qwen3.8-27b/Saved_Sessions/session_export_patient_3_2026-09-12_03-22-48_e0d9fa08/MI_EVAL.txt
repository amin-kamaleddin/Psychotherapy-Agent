# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly normalizes the client’s concerns. It could be stronger by affirming the client’s worth and resilience beyond specific choices and efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and supportive but remain more content-focused than emotionally resonant. There are missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings such as shame, fear, and frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of restarting exercise in a realistic, personalized way. It moves efficiently from motivation to practical planning with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative planning elements, and the client’s preferences shape the final plan. However, the psychiatrist provides much of the content before fully eliciting the client’s own ideas and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice and avoids commanding the client to follow a specific plan. The detailed prescriptions may slightly reduce the client’s sense of ownership if not explicitly framed as modifiable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-driven motivation is drawn out through questions about past success, preferences, and commitment. The psychiatrist could more consistently evoke the client’s own values, capabilities, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses questions and invites client input, creating some collaborative space. However, the session becomes more clinician-directed when detailed exercise advice and routines are introduced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change talk by asking what is doable and what tracking matters most. It would be stronger if the client’s existing motivation and change-oriented statements were reflected and expanded more consistently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not challenged or dismissed, and skepticism is validated. The response is more problem-solving-oriented than MI-consistent, with limited use of reflections or value-based reframing.
