# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment, affirming the client without criticizing his alcohol use, ambivalence, or past behavior. The acceptance is somewhat procedural and could be warmer or more individually focused on emotional and cultural tensions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate reflective listening and good understanding of the client’s ambivalence, independence concerns, and desire to be present for his children. The empathy is mainly content-focused and could more deeply explore fear, shame, grief, or identity-related meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behaviors: reducing alcohol use, engaging in Stopping Violence, and choosing a supportive living environment. The direction is strong, though at times the pacing feels task-driven and more structured than fluid.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a collaborative partner by inviting input, offering options, and adapting to the client’s preferences. The plan is co-created rather than imposed, though the therapist remains the primary organizer of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client’s sense of choice, allowing him to select his focus, steps, and simple coping plan. Some repeated suggestions and confirmation questions could subtly introduce a directive tone if the client felt less confident.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws motivation from within the client, linking change to his values as a father and his desire to be “actually there.” Deeper elaboration on core motivations after key statements would further strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting the client’s input and adapting when he prefers simplicity over scripts or rehearsal. The partnership is strong, but the therapist still holds much of the agenda and asks several sequential logistical questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes and reinforces change talk, helping the client articulate reasons, plans, and confidence for change. The clinician could benefit from slower pacing after strong change talk and deeper scaling or identity-based follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s reasons for maintaining the status quo without reinforcing them, such as valuing independence and concerns about living with whānau. However, more MI-consistent strategies, such as double-sided or paradoxical reflections, are not consistently used to soften sustain talk.
