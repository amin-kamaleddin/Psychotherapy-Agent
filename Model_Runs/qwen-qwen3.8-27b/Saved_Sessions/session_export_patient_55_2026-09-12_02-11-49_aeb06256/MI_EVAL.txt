# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone without pressuring the client. The warmth is somewhat procedural and could more fully normalize the client’s fear of judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s perspective on school, band, and uncertainty about the referral. Empathy is content-focused and could explore emotional undercurrents and ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session briefly addresses alcohol-risk questions but quickly shifts to IB workload and remains focused on studying. This drifts from the presenting alcohol-risk target and lacks a sustained alcohol-related goal or clear connection back to risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative in building a concrete study plan with client input on timing, task, and backup. It is not fully client-led because the clinician supplies most strategies and leads the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, avoids controlling language, and respects the client’s preference not to limit drinks. This is the strongest criterion, clearly reinforcing ownership over compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocative questions draw out the client’s study plan and confidence. Evocation regarding alcohol is more protocol-like and superficial, with limited elicitation of values, fears, or motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s choices shape the session, particularly the decision to focus on IB and the study plan. The clinician could more consistently invite the client’s own solutions before introducing strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked around the study plan, leading to readiness and confidence statements. Change talk is not consistently elicited regarding alcohol use, missing opportunities to connect drinking to values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with sustain talk and uses brief nonjudgmental reflections. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to keep alcohol-related change alive.
