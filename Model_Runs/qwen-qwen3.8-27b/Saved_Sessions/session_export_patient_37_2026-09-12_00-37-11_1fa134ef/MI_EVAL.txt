# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone and helped the patient feel safe. Affirmations were sincere but somewhat formulaic and could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the patient's protective concerns but missed deeper emotional cues such as stomach tension and fear of being the 'panicking mom.' The empathic work remained adequate but surface-level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the varicella decision and moved logically toward an actionable plan. Direction was strong but driven more by the psychiatrist's structure than collaborative goal-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited choices and responded to the patient's request for a message. However, most substantive content was provider-generated rather than truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's caution was respected and options were offered without pressure. Autonomy support was reduced by the provider supplying exact questions, message wording, and breathing steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relied heavily on advice, lists, and scripts rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations. Few open-ended questions were used to explore her values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited patient input and the session felt interactive. A stronger partnership would have involved co-creating the questions, message, and plan with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expressed progress and readiness, but these moments were not expanded or amplified. The psychiatrist pivoted to practical next steps rather than exploring the meaning of the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without judgment through brief validation and reassurance. The psychiatrist did not sufficiently use double-sided reflections to explore and resolve ambivalence.
