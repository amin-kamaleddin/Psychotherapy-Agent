# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, but acceptance is more neutral and task-oriented than deeply individualized. It does not consistently affirm the client’s emotional weight, caring identity, or ambivalence with warm positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and supportive but remain brief and more cognitive than emotionally exploratory. Stronger feelings of fear, guilt, and concern about being a good parent are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the varicella vaccine decision and moves logically toward a concrete plan. The main limitation is that the direction is somewhat driven by informational content rather than the client’s own change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers options and allows the client’s choices to influence parts of the session. However, the counselor remains the primary source of clinical information and structure, limiting the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is regularly supported through choices, nonjudgmental language, and avoiding direct pressure. It could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client’s freedom and ownership at moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks open questions and invites the client to name concerns, but much of the session is driven by information delivery. The client’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change are only partially elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moves, but the counselor frequently shifts into an expert or educator role. The client is not consistently positioned as the expert on her son, values, or readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses change talk, but the counselor only acknowledges it rather than actively amplifying or expanding it. Value-based statements about being a careful parent could have been reflected more deeply to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor validates worry but mostly responds with factual reassurance rather than MI-consistent softening techniques. More use of double-sided reflections or values-based pivots would help transform sustain talk.
