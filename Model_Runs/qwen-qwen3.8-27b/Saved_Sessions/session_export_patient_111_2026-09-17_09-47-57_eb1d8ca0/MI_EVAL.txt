# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids blaming Richard for smoking or attending because of his wife. Warmth is strong, though the opening interpretation of possible “relief” slightly imposes an emotional label.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Richard’s concerns about autonomy, routine, irritation, and protecting his sense of choice. Some reflections remain more cognitive than emotional and miss deeper ambivalence, guilt, or anger.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly related to smoking and health, but it often drifts into abstract medical-data or risk discussions. The target behavior of smoking cessation is not consistently clarified or consolidated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses non-authoritarian, collaborative language and follows Richard’s agenda rather than imposing a fixed plan. The partnership could be stronger with more explicit agenda-setting and invitations for Richard to steer the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is consistently strong, with clear respect for Richard’s need to make any decision in his own way. The counselor avoids pressure, moralizing, or framing health action as compliance with his wife.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open, process-based questions that draw out Richard’s own decision criteria and conditions for change. Evocation is generally good, but it is not consistently deepened into smoking-specific change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys collaboration and treats Richard as the expert on his own experience. The session is still interviewer-led through sequential questions, limiting full reciprocal co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, such as quitting on his own terms or trying stopping after breakfast. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, elaborate, or consolidate Richard’s change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and refrains from reinforcing Richard’s “I’m not sick” or “just a habit” positions. More consistent pivoting away from the medical-data frame toward values-based change talk would strengthen this area.
