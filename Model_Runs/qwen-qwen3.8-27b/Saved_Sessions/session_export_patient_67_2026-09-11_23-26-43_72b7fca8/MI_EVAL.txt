# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and normalizes Kenji’s ambivalence without judgment. Warmth is solid, though it remains somewhat procedural and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate simple reflections, but they often remain surface-level. The psychiatrist does not consistently capture deeper emotions such as shame, defensiveness, or loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing after-work drinking and builds a concrete target behavior. Direction is maintained, though the approach is more directive than evocative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kenji is invited to choose options and contributes details to the plan. However, the psychiatrist provides most of the structure, scripts, and content, so it feels more clinician-led than mutually created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice is offered and pressure is reduced with phrases like “if you like.” Autonomy support is limited by the many prescriptive steps, scripts, and reminders introduced by the psychiatrist.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist gives reasons and options rather than consistently drawing out Kenji’s own motivations. Important moments of doubt and hope are not fully explored to elicit change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and invites choices, showing some power sharing. Overall, the interaction remains largely driven by the clinician rather than co-led by the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited opportunities to elicit desire, reasons, or needs for change. The psychiatrist mostly provides practical steps instead of helping Kenji articulate his own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced or argued against, which is appropriate. The psychiatrist could do more by reflecting ambivalence and gently redirecting doubt toward exploration.
