# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, affirming the patient's choice and reducing blame. Acceptance is somewhat procedural because it is paired with repeated advice-giving, scripts, and safety warnings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate early reflections of frustration, but the empathy often remains at a surface level and does not fully track deeper fears. The self-harm question also feels checklist-like rather than embedded in empathic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to medication and treatment concerns, but it moves unevenly among bloodwork, coping, scripts, and safety. It does not consistently guide the conversation toward ambivalence, values, or readiness for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes collaborative moves by asking for preferences and inviting input, but continues to supply scripts, timelines, and cautions. The patient's own ideas do not fully shape the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is repeatedly affirmed with language like you're in charge and you can decide, but it is undermined by repeated practical suggestions. The psychiatrist keeps layering options and safety guidance after the patient asks to wrap up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are asked, but they are often followed by advice, menus, or scripts rather than reflective follow-up. There is little elicitation of the patient's own reasons, values, or possible change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and asks which option feels more useful, but repeatedly assumes the expert role. When the patient objects to scripts, the psychiatrist offers more scripted lines rather than fully letting the patient lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not strategically create conditions for the patient to express desire, ability, need, or commitment language. Most of the session focuses on options, medication history, and practical scripts rather than evoking the patient's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation, but does not effectively soften the patient's sustain talk or pivot it toward change. Responses such as totally valid are acceptable but are followed by scripts and medical warnings that can reinforce resistance.
