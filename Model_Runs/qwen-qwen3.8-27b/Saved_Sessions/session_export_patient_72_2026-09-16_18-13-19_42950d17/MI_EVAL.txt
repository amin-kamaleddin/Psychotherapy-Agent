# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance by normalizing Leila’s struggle and respecting her boundaries. It could be stronger by adding more individualized affirmation of her resilience, insight, or right to choose.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and frequent, faithfully capturing Leila’s goals, preferences, and specific details. It could be deeper by reflecting more underlying emotions such as frustration, fear of failure, relief, or pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior and moved purposefully from general concern to a concrete, actionable plan. There was little drift, and each response guided Leila toward a specific next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked with Leila using shared language and helped her design the plan from her own inputs. It could be more equal by reducing rapid clinician-generated options and giving Leila more control over agenda and pacing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was strongly supported through validating her limits, avoiding pressure, and framing the plan as low-demand. It could be stronger by adding more explicit autonomy statements and pauses asking whether she wants to continue narrowing the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicited Leila’s own reasons, preferences, and practical commitments rather than imposing advice. It could be stronger by explicitly evoking deeper values, confidence, ability, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a collaborative stance and positioned Leila as the expert on her own patterns. It could feel more equal by pausing more often to invite Leila to direct the next focus rather than moving through a structured sequence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections effectively elicited Leila’s own change-oriented language and concrete plan. It could be strengthened by using broader DARN-C or confidence-focused questions to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled barriers well by validating Leila’s concerns and reducing threat without arguing. It could be stronger by more consistently using double-sided reflections to hold both her desire for movement and her need to avoid rigidity.
