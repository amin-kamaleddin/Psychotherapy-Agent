# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while affirming Tariq’s values and intentions. No moralizing or arguing is evident, even when Tariq expresses frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Tariq’s ambivalence, sense of unfairness, and urgency around family contact and release. The empathic responses could go deeper into underlying emotions such as fear, sadness, or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on the target issues of managing the next three weeks, protecting his record, and finding allowed ways to stay connected. The conversation moves logically toward a concrete, behavior-relevant plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses shared problem-solving language and invites Tariq to generate and refine solutions. The resulting plan is largely Tariq-driven, with the psychiatrist providing MI-consistent structure rather than control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tariq is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, and the psychiatrist respects his preferences without pressure. His choices about the review, witness, and timeline are actively supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Tariq’s own concerns, goals, and proposed steps through open, client-centered questions. Evocation could be deeper by drawing out more personal values, motivation, and confidence before moving into problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Tariq as the expert on his own goals and preferences rather than imposing an agenda. Tariq’s contributions substantially shape the direction and content of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reflects and builds on Tariq’s language about responsibility, compliance, and protecting his release. More deliberate elicitation of desire, need, ability, and confidence language could further amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tariq’s frustration and blame-related statements are acknowledged without argument and redirected toward fairness, process, and his own goals. More frequent double-sided reflections could more fully honor frustration while strengthening his sense of agency.
