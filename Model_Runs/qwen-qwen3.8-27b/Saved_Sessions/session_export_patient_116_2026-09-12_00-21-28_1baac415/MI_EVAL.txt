# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a nonjudgmental, validating tone and avoids scolding Samir for nonadherence. Acceptance is somewhat generic and quickly shifts toward practical closing rather than deeper individualized emotional acknowledgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects surface-level feelings such as tiredness, stomach discomfort, and annoyance with messages. Deeper exploration of the meaning behind his “twisty stomach” reaction and ambivalence is limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts heavily toward text-message management rather than medication nonadherence. The central target behavior is not consistently addressed with a clear, collaborative focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the psychiatrist offers options and asks for preference, the interaction remains largely provider-driven. Samir’s main concern about not taking medication does not meaningfully reshape the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids coercion and offers choices such as which option to pursue. However, frequent practical directives and problem-solving weaken full support for Samir’s self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to evoke Samir’s own reasons for change, values, or concerns about medication. The psychiatrist offers solutions and information more than drawing out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership is shown through offering options and asking which would help. The overall style is counselor-led, with limited invitation for Samir to define the agenda or lead the discussion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist briefly asks why Samir stopped taking medication but does not pursue it effectively. There is little effort to draw out, amplify, or reinforce Samir’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Samir, but also does not skillfully soften or redirect his sustain talk. Responses repeatedly return to practical steps rather than reflecting or gently exploring his resistance.
