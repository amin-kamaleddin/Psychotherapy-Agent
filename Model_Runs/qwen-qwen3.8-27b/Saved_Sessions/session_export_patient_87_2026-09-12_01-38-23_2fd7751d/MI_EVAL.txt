# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes Marco's struggle without shame. However, the warmth is sometimes formulaic and shifts quickly into advice or problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is present and acknowledges fatigue and difficulty, but often stays at a surface level. Deeper reflection of underlying shame, self-doubt, or meaning would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on evening cravings and narrows toward a specific behavioral plan. There is some drift into general stress-management ideas, but they are generally tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts the plan to Marco's constraints, showing some collaboration. However, most ideas are still introduced by the psychiatrist rather than emerging from Marco.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice is supported by offering options and allowing Marco to simplify the plan. Frequent directive instructions and structured tasks somewhat reduce the sense that change is fully client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area, with the psychiatrist supplying many strategies and reasons rather than deeply eliciting Marco's own motivations and values. More open questions and client-generated reasons for change are needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a partial collaborative stance, with Marco choosing among options and adapting the plan to his constraints. The interaction remains fairly expert-driven, though, rather than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes a few attempts to evoke change talk, such as asking about confidence and what feels doable. Marco's change talk is not consistently reflected, amplified, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not simply reinforce barriers and redirects toward small, doable actions. However, responses are more advisory than MI-consistent, with limited use of double-sided reflections or ambivalence exploration.
