# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgment throughout, with effective validation of the client's self-doubt and experiences. Affirmations occasionally feel slightly formulaic (e.g., repeated use of 'I love that you just said' and 'I really appreciate'), and the psychiatrist could deepen normalization of the client's inner critic struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and nuanced reflections that capture both content and emotional texture, including ambivalence, self-doubt, and the 'worry underneath the hope.' Reflections go well beyond paraphrase to capture the client's lived experience with precision and attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent and consistent focus on the target behavior (lunch improvement for afternoon energy) with a clear, logical progression from rapport through exploration, planning, and practical implementation. The session never drifts, and the concrete ending plan (phone note, 1:30 alarm) is directly tied to the target outcome.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative stance with co-constructed decisions throughout — the client chooses the focus, designs the plan, defines success, and identifies her own tracking strategy. Inclusive language ('I'm wondering,' 'I'm curious') and consistent attribution of insights to the client maintain a genuinely balanced power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent and explicit reinforcement of the client's choice, ownership, and self-determination, with invitational language used throughout and no prescriptive 'you should' statements. The psychiatrist validates the client's caution against overpromising as wisdom rather than correcting it, and final strategies are framed as the client's own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question is carefully calibrated to draw out the client's own motivations, plans, and standards rather than providing external reasons for change. The psychiatrist amplifies and reflects back the client's change talk, making it more salient, and by session's end the client is articulating her own commitment in her own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with consistent invitations for the client's input and explicit attribution of insights to her. However, the psychiatrist still largely steers the session along a predetermined path (lunch → plan → tracking → fair trial), and could be deepened by more explicitly following the client's leads when they diverge from the planned trajectory.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of evocative questions that progressively elicit stronger, more specific change talk from the client, including her own goal articulation, success metrics, and setback strategies. The psychiatrist consistently amplifies and builds on the client's own language rather than introducing external reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective reframing of self-doubt and sustain talk (e.g., 'those aren't excuses, those are real things') with validation without amplification. The psychiatrist could be more strategically sophisticated by using double-sided reflections to hold both sustain and change talk simultaneously, rather than simply validating and waiting for the client to self-correct.
