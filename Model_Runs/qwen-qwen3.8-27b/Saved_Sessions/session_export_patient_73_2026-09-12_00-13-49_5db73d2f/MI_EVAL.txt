# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized the client’s experience without blame or moralizing. Acceptance was present but more task-oriented, with some affirmations sounding formulaic rather than deeply emotionally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were accurate early reflections of the client’s ambivalence and social worries. However, when fear and discomfort emerged, the psychiatrist often shifted to practical advice rather than fully exploring the emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on smoking cessation and moved toward a concrete, manageable plan. Direction was strong but could be enhanced by linking the behavior more deeply to the client’s own values and motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributed meaningfully to the plan, such as choosing the 30-minute delay and coffee ritual. Collaboration was present but uneven, as the psychiatrist remained the main source of strategies, scripts, and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honored choice, avoided overt pressure, and respected the client’s boundary around not practicing. Frequent suggestions created only mild pressure, but autonomy was generally well supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions helped the client express goals, barriers, and readiness. However, the psychiatrist often moved into offering options and advice rather than consistently drawing out the client’s own motivations and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client was invited to choose and contribute to the session, and some collaboration was evident. The interaction often became advisor-led, with the psychiatrist driving much of the content through lists and suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged naturally, and the psychiatrist reinforced it with positive affirmations. The session would be stronger with more follow-up questions that deepen the client’s desire, ability, and value statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was present and was generally normalized rather than reinforced. The psychiatrist did not always strategically explore or pivot the ambivalence, sometimes shifting to practical strategies before fully working with the client’s attachment to smoking.
