# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional, consistent unconditional positive regard throughout the session, normalizing Amina's minimal engagement without judgment. The immediate, genuine apology after being corrected ('You're right. I apologize — I jumped ahead') is a standout moment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows strong perspective-taking with accurate reflections of Amina's experience, though responses remain somewhat formulaic and safe. Could push further into deeper emotional territory (e.g., fear of judgment, powerlessness in the household) for a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a general focus on rapport-building but lacks strategic momentum — the session essentially circles without progressive movement toward a specific target. Could benefit from scaling questions, a gentle tie to the referral reason, or a clear next step for future sessions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with the psychiatrist consistently framing the interaction as a joint effort and adapting to the client's lead. Could be stronger by explicitly inviting Amina to set an agenda or co-create goals and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Arguably the strongest element of the session — every single utterance reinforces Amina's right to choose how much, how fast, and what to share. Never gives advice, never implies she 'should' change, and immediately yields when she pushes back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes several evocation attempts but they are limited in variety and effectiveness; the one moment of genuine client interest ('What do you want from me?') is answered rather than used as a springboard. Could employ more creative strategies such as scaling questions, hypotheticals, or indirect approaches suited to a non-communicative client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary power-sharing with explicit statements like 'You set the pace' and 'I'm willing to earn that trust, one small thing at a time.' The psychiatrist never assumes expertise or lectures, consistently positioning Amina as the one with the answers about her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes reasonable attempts to evoke client voice (open questions, dichotomous choices, asking about values), but none successfully elicit change talk and alternative strategies are not effectively deployed. The positive reinterpretation of Amina's question is a solid attempt, but she rejects it and the psychiatrist appropriately backs off without further creative alternatives.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Amina's resistance and defensive statements exceptionally well — never argues, never amplifies, and consistently normalizes her boundaries. A minor gap is the absence of double-sided reflections, which would represent a more sophisticated MI technique for this context.
