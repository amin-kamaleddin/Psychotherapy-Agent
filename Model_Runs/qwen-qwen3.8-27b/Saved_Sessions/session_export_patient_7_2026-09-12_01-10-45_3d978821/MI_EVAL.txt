# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and makes a strong repair after the client says “Stop asking.” Repeated safety language is clinically reasonable but can subtly interrupt the client’s expressed need for quiet.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately validate the client’s frustration and sense of invasiveness, including acknowledging the therapist’s role in the rupture. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not deeply explore underlying loss of control, shame, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a reasonable focus on the client’s concern but becomes procedural and drifts among silence, options, and safety. A clearer, small collaborative target would improve momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an expert stance and invites the client’s preferences, including allowing the client to ask for silence. Collaboration could be deeper by treating the client’s perspective as the central guide.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with nonpressuring language and repeated acknowledgment of the client’s right to choose. The safety question after “Stay quiet” briefly violates the client’s stated boundary.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is an initial evocative question, but the therapist does not consistently draw out values, confidence, agency, or reasons for engagement. Evocation is a major area for development in this session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses a collaborative stance by offering choices and returning control to the client. Partnership remains more procedural than deeply client-driven or co-creative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist makes a brief initial attempt to invite change-related focus but does not sustain efforts to elicit the client’s reasons, hopes, or values. No meaningful change talk or engagement talk is evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s resistance and reduces the power struggle. However, sustain talk is not strategically softened with double-sided reflections or collaborative reframing.
