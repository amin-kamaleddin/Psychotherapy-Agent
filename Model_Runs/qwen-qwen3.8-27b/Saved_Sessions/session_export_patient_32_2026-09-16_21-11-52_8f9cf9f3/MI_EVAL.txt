# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, reducing shame around the DUI and ambivalence. It falls short of a 5 because warmth is task-oriented and could more explicitly affirm Derrick's values and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Derrick's ambivalence, social pressure, and practical concerns. A 5 would require deeper reflection of underlying emotions such as shame, fear, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on avoiding another DUI and building a concrete, low-risk plan for a specific night. The progression is logical, targeted, and consistently guided toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a partner by summarizing, following Derrick's ideas, and inviting his input. It is not fully mutual because the agenda structure remains somewhat counselor-led and could use more shared co-creation language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces Derrick's choice, ownership, and capacity to decide what is realistic. This is a strength, with little directive pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Derrick's own goals, fears, and action steps rather than advising. It could be stronger by evoking deeper values, identity, and confidence beyond practical planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly conveys that Derrick is the expert on his own life and allows his contributions to shape the session. This demonstrates strong power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, forward-leaning questions help Derrick generate useful change talk around leaving early, using a taxi, limiting drinks, and texting in advance. A 5 would require more amplification of his strongest change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Derrick's desire to stay connected and avoids arguing or dismissing it. It could be improved by using more double-sided reflections and further exploring risk of 'one beer' becoming more.
