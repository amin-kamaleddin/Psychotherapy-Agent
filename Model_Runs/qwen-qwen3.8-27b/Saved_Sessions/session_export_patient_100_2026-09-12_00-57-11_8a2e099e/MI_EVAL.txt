# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client's plan without criticizing the drinking. Acceptance is somewhat task-focused and could include deeper personal regard for the difficulty of disclosing in a school setting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers appropriate but surface-level empathy for stress and social pressure. Misses opportunities to use complex reflections around fear of exclusion, belonging, and ambivalence about drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays focused on the client's immediate concerns and practical needs. Direction is partially diluted by a large section of structured study and sleep advice rather than staying centered on alcohol-related risk and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows moderate collaboration by asking for priorities, offering options, and ending with the client choosing a plan. The interaction remains therapist-led because most strategies, scripts, and structures are provided by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, respecting refusal of breathing exercises, and emphasizing that the client does not have to quit. Autonomy is somewhat undercut by specific therapist-generated limits, routines, and plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some evocative questions and the client generates useful solutions such as lime water and deflection lines. Evocation remains limited because the client's values, confidence, and own reasons for change are not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partnership by using the client's own ideas and inviting input on priorities. The stance is weakened by frequent expert problem-solving, detailed schedules, and therapist-supplied strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges, especially around not wanting to mess up the test or game and choosing a manageable plan. The therapist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or expand the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk and supports the client's autonomy around continuing to drink. Sustain talk is not actively softened through double-sided reflections or exploration of the costs relative to the client's values.
