# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing uncertainty and avoiding labels like “alcoholic.” Acceptance is solid but somewhat procedural and occasionally clinical, limiting deeper individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor reflects Daniel’s factual perspective on health and training but misses the emotional and relational dimensions of his defensiveness and stigma concerns. Key feelings such as discomfort and the function of “unwinding” are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and its possible impact on marathon training, moving toward a concrete next step. Direction is strong but somewhat agenda-driven rather than fully client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers options and co-constructs a phrase for Daniel to use with his doctor, showing some collaboration. However, the screening, risk framing, and plan remain largely counselor-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports Daniel’s choice by accepting a small change and emphasizing that he does not have to quit forever. Prescriptive advice and structured recommendations limit full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and offers choices, but relies heavily on providing expert reasons for change. More open-ended questions would better evoke Daniel’s own values, motivation, and preferences.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Daniel’s input and adjusts when tracking feels like homework, showing some partnership. Overall, the counselor takes the expert role, so Daniel has limited influence on the agenda and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel voices several statements supporting change, such as trying to drink less and watching his training. The counselor does not consistently deepen, amplify, or strategically reflect these change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some face-saving and non-confrontational responses, but heavy medical explanation can function like advice or counterargument. More complex double-sided reflections would better soften Daniel’s resistance.
