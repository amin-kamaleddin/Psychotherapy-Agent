# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates warmth, respect, and non-judgment throughout the session with no labeling or disapproval. Occasional formulaic repetition of 'you don't have to' constructions and one awkward verbatim repeat prevent a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening that accurately captures emotional undercurrents and extends the client's own metaphors. Falls slightly short by occasionally missing opportunities to explore deeper shame or fear with greater emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a natural thematic flow from behavior to identity to somatic experience but lacks a clear strategic direction toward a specific change goal. No periodic summaries or framework for next steps is provided, leaving the client without a concrete path forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth exploration where the client's contributions shape direction and his language is woven into reflections. Could be deepened by more explicit co-creation of the session's focus, such as asking the client which theme to prioritize.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently uses 'you don't have to' language to remove pressure and never imposes a timeline or prescribes action. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client's agency and capacity for change to reinforce ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong open questions that lead the client to his own key insights about identity, belonging, and the function of the behavior. Could have been more strategically targeted toward evoking ability, commitment, and taking-steps language rather than remaining in the exploratory phase.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong collaborative stance with curious, invitational language and a clearly client-led session. Could be deepened by more explicit power-sharing statements and active co-creation rather than the psychiatrist leading exploration with questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces meaningful change talk but it is not consistently reinforced, reflected back, or strategically amplified. The psychiatrist's questions are more exploratory than change-talk-specific, limiting systematic evocation of DARN-CAT language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled appropriately without reinforcing or arguing against it, but active softening is limited. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections to help the client see how needs and behaviors can be separated, leaving ambivalence unresolved.
