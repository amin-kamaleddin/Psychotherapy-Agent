# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and safety-focused stance while affirming the client’s caution. However, acceptance remains task-centered and tied to the client doing the “right” safety behavior, with less unconditional, person-centered warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges anxiety, relief, and the client’s need for accuracy with reassuring reflections. The empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore the underlying fear, unfamiliarity, or emotional complexity of the situation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong, consistent focus on medication safety and concrete next steps. Direction is clinically appropriate but is achieved mainly through directive instruction rather than MI-style, client-centered guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts to the client’s preferences and helps create phone/text scripts, showing some collaborative effort. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary source of medical knowledge and decision structure rather than a fully equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and validates the client’s independence in several moments. At the same time, frequent directive scripts and safety instructions create a somewhat managing tone despite the appropriate safety context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening question begins evocatively, but the session quickly shifts to information delivery and problem-solving. There is limited deliberate elicitation of the client’s internal motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction remains expert-driven, with the psychiatrist defining the problem, risk, scripts, and follow-up plan. There is limited power sharing and inconsistent use of the client’s perspective to guide the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client produces some change-oriented statements, but the psychiatrist mainly affirms rather than actively evokes or expands them. There are few questions that draw out the client’s own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing sustain talk and reduces the client’s fear of being a burden. However, the session often bypasses ambivalence rather than using reflections or reframing to fully soften the client’s internal conflict.
