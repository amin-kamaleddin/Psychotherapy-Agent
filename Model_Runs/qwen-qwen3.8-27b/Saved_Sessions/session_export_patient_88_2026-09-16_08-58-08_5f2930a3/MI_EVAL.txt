# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, validating language and avoids judgment or shaming. The repeated safety focus and scripted options make positive regard feel slightly formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist accurately reflects key adolescent concerns about awkwardness, belonging, and wanting to feel grown-up. Reflections are strong but could go deeper on fear of peer rejection and resentment of adult authority.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use, risks, safety, and a small next-party change. It sometimes repeats similar options and could move more efficiently toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist adapts to Marcus’s objections and builds the plan around his preferences. Frequent advising and menu-like suggestions make collaboration feel uneven rather than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is given choices and encouraged to select his own small experiment, such as water after two drinks. Repeated emphasis on trying it this weekend makes the support mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some client-generated motivation around not feeling sick and wanting to fit in. More of the session relies on the therapist supplying reasons, strategies, and scripts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus’s input meaningfully shapes the plan, including rejecting a buddy and scripted phrases. The counselor’s expert problem-solving role makes the partnership less balanced at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus offers some change talk, including trying water, eating beforehand, and wanting to avoid feeling sick. The therapist does not consistently amplify or develop these statements, and often shifts to practical options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist acknowledges that drinking helps Marcus fit in and avoid standing out. Sustain talk is not fully pivoted to broader values or double-sided reflections, keeping focus on avoiding social awkwardness.
