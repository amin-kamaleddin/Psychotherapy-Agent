# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Linda’s ambivalence about smoking and mothering. It could be stronger by explicitly normalizing her mixed feelings and affirming her as a person, not just her behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses complex, accurate reflections to balance Linda’s desire to protect her children and preserve her post-meal routine. Empathy could deepen by more fully exploring underlying shame, embarrassment, or fear of being seen as the “nervous” mom.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on after-dinner smoking but cycles through many small implementation options without clear momentum. It would benefit from concise summaries and a collaboratively chosen focus tied back to Linda’s values and stated goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Linda’s ideas rather than overriding them and builds on her suggestions to make starting the cigarette less automatic. Collaboration could be strengthened with more explicit “we” language, priority checks, and a co-created concise plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by emphasizing choice, no requirement to quit, and framing options as optional rather than rules. Some consecutive questions still gently push toward modifying the habit rather than fully honoring Linda’s right to choose no change at this time.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit some practical ideas, but the evocation is more procedural than motivational. It would be more effective if it explored Linda’s values, fears, hopes, and confidence, especially around being a role model and family life.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Linda as the expert on her own routine and invites her to choose workable options. Partnership could be stronger through more explicit power-sharing, such as negotiating the agenda and visibly centering Linda’s priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some change talk about not setting a bad example and changing timing, but the session remains focused on behavioral logistics. It would be stronger if the psychiatrist more consistently amplified Linda’s own reasons, importance, confidence, and values as a mother.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging Linda’s enjoyment of smoking and desire to keep the ritual easy without arguing or minimizing. It could be stronger by more consistently turning sustain talk into discrepancy or change talk rather than preserving the status quo.
