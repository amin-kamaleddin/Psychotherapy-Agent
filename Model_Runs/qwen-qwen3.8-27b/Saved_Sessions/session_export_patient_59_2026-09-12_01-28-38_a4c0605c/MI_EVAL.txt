# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and non-judgmental, using brief acknowledgments such as “Sounds fair” and “Okay.” The tone remains procedural and compliance-focused rather than consistently warm or affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic understanding is limited, with few reflections of Jamal’s frustration, defensiveness, or sense of being trapped. The session stays mostly factual and logistical rather than attending to his experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation stays connected to the court-ordered marijuana evaluation but lacks a clear MI focus on change. Much of the time is spent on logistics, scripts, and compliance rather than a collaborative change agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some invitations to collaborate, but the psychiatrist mainly supplies scripts, answers, and practical guidance. Jamal’s preferences and perspective do not meaningfully shape the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some autonomy-supportive phrases like “How would you like to start?” but frequently steers behavior with directive advice and scripted responses. The session emphasizes compliance with the court process more than Jamal’s ownership of his choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak after an initial open question; the psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Jamal’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change. Questions remain mostly closed, factual, and procedural.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes surface-level partnership moves but remains largely expert-driven and directive. The interaction does not feel like equal collaboration, especially when Jamal pushes back on scripted language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal use of the client’s own change-relevant language, such as wanting to “move on” or “get it done.” The psychiatrist does not reflect, amplify, or expand these statements into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Jamal’s minimizing and resistant language is not effectively softened, redirected, or explored. At times, the psychiatrist reinforces the minimizing frame by offering scripts such as “I’m not dealing” and “Not that I’m aware.”
