# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and reassuring, affirming Thomas’s choices without arguing. Acceptance is strong but somewhat repetitive and not always deeply normalizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Thomas’s surface message that he does not see a problem and does not want a lecture. They miss deeper feelings about being judged, labeled, or controlled, and repeat similar reflections near the end.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus remains on smoking, drinking, family, work, and a saved note without major drift. Direction lacks a clear concise client-centered next step and becomes repetitive toward closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, asks preferences, and respects Thomas’s decisions, creating a joint interaction. It is not maximal because the counselor still manages the risk note and closes repeatedly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through permission, no pressure, and options rather than directives. Final repeated closings could still feel like a persistent clinical push.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial questions and rating scales elicit Thomas’s perspective, but evocation becomes inconsistent after his low ratings. Missed opportunities include building on his values as a worker and parent or exploring a small step.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Thomas as the decision-maker and adapts to his request to wrap up. Partnership is not perfect because it does not fully establish Thomas as the expert on his own values or co-create a clear agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative moves and affirmation of quitting smoking, and Thomas offers slight future openness. These are underdeveloped and not consistently amplified into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Thomas’s minimization but does not effectively redirect or soften it toward change. Repeated offers of a risk note keep focus on problem information rather than Thomas’s own reasons.
