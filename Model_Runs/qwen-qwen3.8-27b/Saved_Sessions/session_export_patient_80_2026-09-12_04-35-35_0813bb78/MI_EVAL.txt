# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, including when the patient continues to drink rather than abstain. Warmth is strong but sometimes remains more focused on the practical quality of the plan than fully person-centered acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient's values and practical concerns, especially around safety and repairing what he has fixed. However, empathy is mostly cognitive and misses deeper reflections of ambivalence, fear of losing control, or worry about failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on reducing violence and reducing alcohol use, giving the conversation clear momentum. Direction becomes somewhat plan-driven and solution-focused earlier than a higher-fidelity MI approach would idealize.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the patient's ideas and collaborates on a concrete safety and coping plan. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently offers options, menus, and scaling questions that make the session partly therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the patient's choice to cut down rather than stop and offers choices rather than directives. Autonomy support is limited by the lack of consistent explicit language such as “this is your decision” or “you’re in charge,” and some prompts feel task-driven or leading.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits patient-generated plans, such as the porch pause, water by the fridge, texting the partner, and leaving if he might hurt someone. Evocation is strong for action planning but could deepen by more consistently eliciting the patient's values, identity, and internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is not authoritarian, and the patient's ideas shape the safety plan. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist repeatedly steers toward specific choices and confidence ratings, creating a more coach-and-client dynamic than a fully equal one.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, future-oriented questions and affirmations that draw out clear change talk about desire, ability, and commitment. The change talk is strong but somewhat behavior- and plan-focused rather than consistently amplified at a deeper motivational level.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding pressure for abstinence and reframing the plan around cutting down. It could be stronger by more consistently using double-sided reflections to validate both sides of the patient's ambivalence.
