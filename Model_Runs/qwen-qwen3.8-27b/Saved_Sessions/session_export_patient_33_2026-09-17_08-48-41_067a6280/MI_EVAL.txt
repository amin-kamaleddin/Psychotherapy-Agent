# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Diego’s autonomy, harm-reduction focus, and honesty without judgment. Some affirmations are slightly counselor-generated, which keeps this just below the highest level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Diego’s practical stance and boundaries, especially around privacy and low-pressure disclosure. Empathy is stronger on content than on deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or the meaning of using.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a concrete harm-reduction target: safer Narcan disclosure and a practical plan for clean supplies. The progression is strategically coherent while keeping Diego in charge of the process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego and the psychiatrist co-create specific scripts, cues, and timing choices, showing strong collaboration. The score is limited by occasional counselor-supplied interpretations that frame Diego’s experience rather than purely elicit it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Diego is consistently treated as the authority on his life, pace, household dynamics, and safety choices. There is no pressure toward abstinence or a fixed treatment path, and his preference for brief, private action is fully respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits Diego’s own ideas, scripts, and self-awareness, producing useful client-generated change language. Evocation could deepen by exploring values, confidence, hope, and fuller DARN-CAT change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly defers to Diego’s knowledge of his housemate, home environment, and safety needs. Diego’s contributions substantially shape the session content and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego generates practical change talk through concrete scripts, cues, and confidence that the plan is doable. The clinician could better elicit and amplify broader motivational categories such as desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully by avoiding confrontation, abstinence pressure, or moralizing. The clinician could more consistently link accepted status-quo language to Diego’s own safety and relational motivations.
