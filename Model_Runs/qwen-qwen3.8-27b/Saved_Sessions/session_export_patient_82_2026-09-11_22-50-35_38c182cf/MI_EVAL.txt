# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated the client, but acceptance was undercut by frequent problem-solving and a corrective stance. Affirming the client’s strengths and difficulty would have made positive regard clearer.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some meaningful reflections, particularly around the client’s sense of mismatch and replaying. However, the therapist missed several deeper concerns, including fear of losing the coach’s trust and concern about being excluded or labeled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed largely focused on the target behavior of alcohol use and next-day functioning. Although somewhat prescriptive, the conversation remained relevant and progressively narrowed toward practical steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist accepted and built on some of the client’s preferred solutions, such as taking photos and eating beforehand. Collaboration was uneven because much of the content, structure, and strategy came from the therapist rather than the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist respected the client’s boundaries, such as not wanting a written plan or scripted line. However, repeated offers of options and rules made autonomy support less consistent overall.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few open questions elicited important self-assessment, but the therapist often shifted into advice-giving instead of evoking the client’s own motivation. The client’s values and reasons for change were not consistently drawn out and used as resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stance was frequently expert-driven, with the therapist supplying lists, scripts, and solutions. This reduced the impression that the client held expertise and wisdom about change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client produced useful change talk about wanting to function better and have fun without the crash. The therapist did not consistently amplify, consolidate, or follow up on this language with MI-style questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoided challenging the client’s sustain talk and acknowledged the value of social connection. The response was acceptable but relied more on harm-reduction advice than on nuanced reflections or double-sided reflections to soften resistance.
