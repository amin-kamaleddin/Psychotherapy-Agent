# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor conveys consistent respect, warmth, and nonjudgment without labeling or blaming the client. Falls short of a 5 due to some formulaic language and limited personalized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong reflections that capture ambivalence, especially the conflict between belonging and self-protection. Could deepen emotional tracking of fear, shame, and vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally stays focused on alcohol use, team respect, and season performance. Becomes circular through repeated similar questions and lacks strategic narrowing to a specific behavior, situation, or change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative, nonimposing language and invites the client’s perspective without lecturing or imposing solutions. Could be more co-constructive by having the client help set priorities or shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids advice, pressure, and controlling language while emphasizing the client’s own definitions and choices. Could strengthen by explicitly affirming the client’s final decision, especially when questions subtly steer toward abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes reasonable efforts to evoke motivation from the client’s values rather than providing reasons for change. Needs more strategic follow-up, such as scaling, ability, or confidence questions, especially when the client becomes stuck.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a collaborative, nonexpert stance by inviting the client’s perspective and avoiding directives. Could share power more by explicitly letting the client set or adjust the session focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Attempts to elicit change-oriented language but does not effectively amplify or build on the client’s own statements. Lacks strategic use of DARN-C, scaling, or importance/confidence exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflects sustain talk without arguing or moralizing, which helps maintain a nonjudgmental stance. Does not consistently reduce the grip of sustain talk through double-sided reflections or balanced cost exploration.
