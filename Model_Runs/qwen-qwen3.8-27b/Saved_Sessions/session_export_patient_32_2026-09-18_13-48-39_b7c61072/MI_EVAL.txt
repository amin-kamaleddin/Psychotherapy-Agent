# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard with zero judgment or labeling throughout. Statements like 'I'm not here to argue with you' and affirming Derrick's friendships and feelings simultaneously exemplify textbook acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses highly accurate reflective listening that goes beyond paraphrasing to capture emotional and identity-level meaning. The precise reflection of Derrick's cognitive dissonance ('the distance between I just took a few shots and how am I behind the wheel?') demonstrates exceptional empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from rapport-building through exploring the DUI, identifying the shift, to generating strategies. However, it lacks a final consolidation step such as a readiness ruler or explicit action commitment to close the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a model of genuine collaboration with no sense of hierarchy or expert-client power differential. The psychiatrist follows Derrick's lead on topics, uses collaborative language ('I'm curious,' 'I'm wondering'), and invites correction throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never prescribes, lectures, or imposes a timeline, consistently framing all insights and strategies as Derrick's own. Key statements like 'you're the one who generated them, not me' and 'this is your space' exemplify strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All significant shifts in Derrick's thinking are self-generated in response to evocative questions like 'What's your own take on that?' and 'What would success feel like to you?' The psychiatrist never externally supplies reasons for change, allowing Derrick's intrinsic motivation to surface naturally.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Derrick as the expert on his own life and explicitly credits him for generating ideas. The power sharing is genuine and sustained, with no lecturing, correcting, or assumptions that the psychiatrist knows better.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully highlights and amplifies Derrick's own change language, moving him from 'I don't have a problem' to articulating concrete strategies and a vision of success. Reflections like 'That's a shift I don't want to minimize' strengthen and validate emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully by acknowledging it without arguing and using it as a bridge to deeper exploration. However, some instances like 'they'd think I'm getting preachy' could have been more actively reframed or explored for the underlying values conflict.
