# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the patient's concerns and choices. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not fully normalize the patient's anxiety or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening for frustration and confusion, but remains somewhat surface-level. Deeper exploration of underlying worry about health and control is needed for a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on blood pressure and medication, but lacks strategic direction on alcohol use. It drifts into logistical task completion rather than exploring lifestyle factors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive language and co-created next steps create a collaborative feel. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary source of content, limiting the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options are consistently offered and the patient's choice to keep the plan simple is respected. Explicit reinforcement of the patient's capacity for self-direction could make it stronger.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reliance on closed questions and directive problem-solving limits the patient's own voice. The psychiatrist provides reasons for the plan rather than helping the patient articulate personal motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes the patient in choices, but often takes a directive expert role by drafting messages and medical information. It lacks an explicit equal-partner stance where the patient is the expert on their own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited effort to amplify the patient's own motivation to improve blood pressure or health. The session shifts quickly to logistical options rather than deepening change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient's resistance to tracking without arguing, but does not effectively redirect sustain talk toward change. Repeated focus on tracking options keeps attention on the patient's stated barriers.
