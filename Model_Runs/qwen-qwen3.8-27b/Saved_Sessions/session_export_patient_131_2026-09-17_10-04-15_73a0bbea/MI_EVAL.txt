# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, with language that normalizes ambivalence rather than criticizing drinking or past violence. The counselor validates both sides of the client's conflict and treats him as a capable person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex and accurate, capturing both the emotional and relational meaning behind the client's statements. Minor refinement is possible in explicitly reflecting the fear of “blowing up” before moving into boundary formulation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear logical progression from ambivalence to a concrete plan for drinking, housing, and fathering. It is less fully aligned with the broader violence-accountability and Stopping Violence context, which is addressed only indirectly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions, reflections, and invitations to the client's perspective, creating a genuinely equal-partner stance. The client's ideas actively shape the session's content and direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the author of his own choices, with repeated affirmations of independence and self-knowledge. There is no pressure to move faster, stop drinking immediately, or give up autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited from the client's own values, hopes, and coping ideas rather than supplied by the counselor. The client generates concrete change statements and a workable boundary, which the counselor then reinforces.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly conveys that the client is the expert on his own life, relationships, and practical options. Clinical advice is avoided in favor of shared exploration and reflection.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully draws out and strengthens the client's desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment language. Change talk is expanded through reflection and questions, not imposed through persuasion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids confrontation and validates the client's reasons for maintaining independence and the current flat. Some of the strongest relational sustain talk about Tristan could be softened further with deeper double-sided reflections.
