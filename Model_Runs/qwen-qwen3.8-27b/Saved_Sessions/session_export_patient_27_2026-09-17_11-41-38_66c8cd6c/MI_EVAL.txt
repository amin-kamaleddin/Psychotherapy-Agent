# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizing Daniela's experience without pathologizing it and affirming her self-awareness throughout. The language is warm, specific, and individualized, referencing her storytelling and role as a junior, making the acceptance feel genuine rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate empathic understanding, capturing subtle cues such as Daniela's pauses and the internal conflict between what she wants to say and what her body actually does. Reflections go beyond paraphrase to capture the emotional texture of her experience, including her self-awareness about her own imagination building negative scenarios.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a coherent arc from ambivalence to understanding the fear to identifying a self-generated solution, with each question building logically on the last. However, the lack of a consolidating summary or clear closing that ties insights together and identifies a concrete next step slightly limits the strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine dialogue, with the psychiatrist positioning themselves as curious and learning from Daniela's perspective rather than as an authority. Daniela's metaphors and insights become the primary therapeutic material, and decisions about what to explore are guided by her responses rather than a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Daniela's ownership of the change process, asking rather than telling and never prescribing an action or implying she should disclose. The framing positions change as something Daniela already knows how to do and simply hasn't chosen to do yet, preserving her sense of agency throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and reflections to draw out Daniela's own motivations, resulting in her articulating what she wants, identifying her own solution, and discovering her own capacity. By the end of the session, the motivation for change has emerged entirely from within Daniela, which is the hallmark of skilled evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively fosters power sharing, with Daniela's contributions substantially influencing the session direction — her storytelling metaphor becomes the therapeutic framework and her insights drive the central conclusions. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with the psychiatrist curious rather than omniscient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes Daniela's own reasons for change through strategic questions that elicit language about feeling 'lighter' and 'more present,' and culminates in her self-generated insight about making diabetes 'part of the story instead of making it the story.' The evocation is natural, embedded in the conversational flow, and consistently draws from Daniela's own language and metaphors.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When Daniela expresses fear of freezing or being treated differently, the psychiatrist validates these concerns without dismissing or arguing against them, then gently orients toward her stated preference. Sustain talk is consistently held in the context of her larger narrative and pivoted toward change talk by asking what she would want rather than debating the fear directly.
