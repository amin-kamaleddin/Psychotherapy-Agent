# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Maria’s caution about the inhaler. The warmth is somewhat procedural, and the opening statement imposes an interpretation before Maria has fully framed her position.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist captures some of Maria’s core ambivalence, especially the tension between wanting protection and safety concern. Reflections remain surface-level and repetitive, and the session shifts to medical facts rather than tracking deeper fear and distrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on inhaler use, safety concerns, and discussing the medication with her primary care provider. However, repeatedly presenting multiple options can make the direction feel less focused on one concrete decision.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Maria’s input and asks which options feel helpful. The interaction often becomes more counselor-led, with the psychiatrist generating scripts, options, and medical summaries rather than co-creating with Maria.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms Maria’s freedom to choose and does not pressure her to accept the inhaler. The repeated offers of plans and materials can subtly imply a future adherence pathway, but the language remains respectful and noncoercive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist frequently shifts into advising, scripting, and medical information. Maria’s own agency, values, and reasons for change are not sufficiently explored or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moments, including asking Maria what would be helpful and acknowledging her research. The session often tilts toward the psychiatrist managing the agenda rather than inviting Maria to lead with her own knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited efforts to cultivate change talk and does not fully use Maria’s statements of possible agency or willingness. There is little use of scaling, importance/confidence questions, or value-based prompts to strengthen her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation and acknowledges Maria’s concerns without dismissing them. However, he sometimes stays in the sustain-talk zone by elaborating on risks and medical details, which can amplify her safety concerns.
