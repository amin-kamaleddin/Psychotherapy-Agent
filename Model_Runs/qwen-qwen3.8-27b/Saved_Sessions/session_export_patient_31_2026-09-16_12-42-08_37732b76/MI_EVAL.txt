# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained nonjudgmental and accepted David’s goal to reduce rather than quit. Some responses stayed practical rather than deeply affirming his person, effort, and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate content-focused summaries but often missed David’s underlying frustration, stress, and ambivalence. Reflections could have been more emotionally nuanced and exploratory.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing after-work smoking and a patch-based test. The focus sometimes drifted toward patch mechanics rather than broader motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David’s preferences shaped parts of the plan, but the psychiatrist still led the agenda and selected the intervention. More co-creation would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respected David’s choice to reduce smoking and allowed him to reject unwanted tasks. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming David’s right to decide and to lead.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited some change talk, but the psychiatrist also supplied considerable external reasons for change. Deeper exploration of David’s own values and motivations is needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and adjusted the plan, but the interaction remained largely expert-driven. More equal co-planning would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change talk but did not consistently reflect, expand, or follow up on it. Stronger evocation could build David’s confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with resistance but did little to soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections or reframing. More strategic pivots toward change would be needed.
