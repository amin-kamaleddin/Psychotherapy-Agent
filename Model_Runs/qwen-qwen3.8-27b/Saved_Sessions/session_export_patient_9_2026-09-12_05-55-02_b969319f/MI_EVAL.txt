# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental throughout the session. Acceptance was present but often procedural rather than deeply affirming, especially when the patient expressed concern about being a good mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were effective early reflections of the patient's stress and parenting values. However, empathy became more formulaic over time, with important emotions such as fear, guilt, and exhaustion not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear and practical focus on reducing conflict during co-parenting communication and handoffs. Direction was strong, though it could have been more explicitly tied to the patient's broader values and change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited the patient's input on choices and scripts. However, the interaction often remained expert-led, with many therapist-generated options limiting equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supported autonomy by offering choices and asking what felt doable for the patient. This was a strength, though some prescriptive suggestions and implied preferred actions slightly reduced openness to patient choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evocative questions early in the session to elicit goals, values, and past strengths. Evocation became less consistent after the session shifted into problem-solving and script-building.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated some partnership by inviting input and using the patient's own words. However, the session often moved quickly into therapist-generated solutions rather than fully honoring the patient as the expert in her own family.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some useful change talk, especially around peaceful handoffs and the importance of calmness for the child. The main limitation was not consistently amplifying or deepening the patient's own statements of motivation and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalized the patient's ambivalence but did not actively soften sustain talk. Responses often shifted back to practical options, which risked reinforcing the patient's concern that change would add burden.
