# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, validating, and nonjudgmental with effective reframing of self-critical language. Minor limitation: some affirming phrases ('very sensible,' 'great plan') felt evaluative rather than fully noncontingent acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of core concern, barriers, and emotional frame throughout. Did not consistently mirror the deeper shame/frustration/self-criticism embedded in vivid language like 'gross' and 'sick of feeling the way I do.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session progressed logically and efficiently from presenting concern through importance, barriers, specific behavior/time, backup, and follow-up. Minimal drift; emotional concerns were skillfully redirected into actionable MI-consistent processes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Aisha's ideas (tea, shoes, emoji, timing) actively integrated at every step. Clear equal-partner dynamic where her contributions substantially shaped session content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and permission language used consistently ('Would you like…', 'Which…', 'There's no obligation'). No coercive pressure; check-in explicitly offered multiple low-stakes formats including silence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent questions elicited useful client-owned solutions across the session. Could have more deeply drawn out values, identity, and confidence (e.g., 'How confident are you…?', 'What would sticking to 10 minutes say about how you treat yourself?').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple genuine invitations for client input, and her choices meaningfully shaped the session. Therapist still offered considerable specific suggestions (if-then plan, alarm, reward) that could be seen as slightly problem-solver-oriented.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Relevant scaling and planning questions elicited solid desire, ability, and commitment statements from the client. Often moved into structuring after strong change language rather than amplifying it with evocative follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All barriers (energy, time, embarrassment, burnout fear) were acknowledged with flexibility and no arguing or moralizing. Could have used more explicit double-sided reflections or reframed sustain talk as protective ('part of you is protecting yourself from burnout').
