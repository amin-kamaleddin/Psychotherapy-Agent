# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes Rowan’s experience without shaming or blaming. Acceptance is slightly limited by occasional emphasis on the importance of monitoring, which feels less like unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are strong reflections, especially around Rowan’s sense of losing control and conflicting feelings about the device. However, the empathy becomes repetitive and misses deeper emotional material such as guilt and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on making the device charging and use workable for Rowan. The direction is strong but becomes narrow and repetitive, staying focused on practical solutions rather than moving the MI process forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rowan’s perspective and incorporates his ideas, but the conversation remains largely clinician-led. Collaboration would be stronger with more true co-creation and less steering toward practical compliance planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasizes Rowan’s choice, self-direction, and doing things on his own terms. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by the repeated push to find a workable charging plan, which can feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often uses open questions that invite Rowan to generate ideas, and some client-originated solutions emerge. However, evocation stays mostly logistical and does not fully explore Rowan’s values, motivations, or emerging change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally takes a partnership stance by inviting Rowan’s perspective and treating him as the expert on his life. However, the session remains somewhat structured and agenda-driven around finding a practical solution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rowan offers several tentative change ideas, such as checking when bored or leaving the device plugged in. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen these ideas, so the change talk remains underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to Rowan’s resistance respectfully and avoids arguing against his reasons for not wanting to charge the device. However, sustain talk is often acknowledged but not strategically reframed or redirected toward change, especially around autonomy and control.
