# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, acknowledging exhaustion, fear, and burden without moralizing. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural and safety-driven rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening and accurately captures ambivalence, exhaustion, and fear of being alone. Some reflections are speculative or slightly minimize the client's hopeless state, preventing a higher score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on active suicidal ideation and immediate safety, which is appropriate for the crisis. The main limitation is that it does not consistently guide the client toward a broader or longer-term change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive, partner-like language and lets the client's choices shape the session. The safety framework remains somewhat clinician-led, especially with repeated crisis-line instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, acknowledging agency, and inviting the client to define what might make the next hour safer. Repeated direct safety statements could slightly reduce the sense of client-led choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks helpful open questions about immediate safety and comfort. Evocation is limited because it does not deeply target the client's intrinsic reasons to live or motivation to move away from suicidal behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting input, offering options, and acknowledging that they cannot control the client's decisions. Partnership is solid but constrained by the immediate safety focus and could more strongly position the client as the expert on their own needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client's statements about not wanting to die and choosing to put the pills away. These moments of change talk are not consistently deepened or expanded with more targeted evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client's hopeless statements and validates the pain. However, the response often returns to immediate safety steps rather than consistently using MI techniques to soften or pivot the sustain talk.
