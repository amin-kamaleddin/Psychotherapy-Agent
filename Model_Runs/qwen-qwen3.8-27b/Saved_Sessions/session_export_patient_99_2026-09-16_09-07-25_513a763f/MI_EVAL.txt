# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with frequent validation of Matteo’s perspective. Some warmth was formulaic and prescriptive medical limits briefly undercut the accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate at the content level but often stayed cognitive and factual. The psychiatrist missed opportunities to explore deeper social, identity, and emotional ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol reduction with a clear, well-paced progression from exploration to planning. Direction was strong, though it emphasized the mechanical “how” more than the personal “why.”

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Matteo’s preferences helped shape the session, and he selected specific strategies and scripts. The collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist generated most of the content and advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy was supported by asking for preferences and using a confidence scale. However, specific numerical limits, lists, and pre-written scripts created a more prescriptive than client-led dynamic.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supplied most reasons for change and strategies rather than eliciting Matteo’s own values and motivations. Change talk was present but thin and mainly practical rather than values-based.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used partnership language and invited Matteo’s input throughout. In practice, the psychiatrist remained the primary knowledge holder, limiting true power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited, especially around doable strategies and symptom reduction. The psychiatrist did not deeply amplify or explore values-based reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without judgment or direct argument. The psychiatrist did not sufficiently use MI-consistent techniques to explore or soften the resistance.
