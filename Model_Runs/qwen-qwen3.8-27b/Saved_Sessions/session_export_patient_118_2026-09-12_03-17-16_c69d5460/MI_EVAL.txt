# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts Seamus’s dental agenda without lecturing. Acceptance remains somewhat procedural and could more explicitly affirm his frustration with repeated healthcare advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and helpful for de-escalation but remain mostly surface-level. Deeper exploration of Seamus’s underlying irritation, fatigue, and ambivalence would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays focused on dental logistics but largely loses sight of the smoking-cessation target. More gentle, MI-consistent linking of dental care to smoking-related risks would improve direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects Seamus’s input, reducing the power differential. Collaboration is practical rather than deeply MI-centered and does not meaningfully include the smoking issue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly respected, especially when Seamus declines additional help. Repeated offers of assistance could begin to feel mildly persistent without explicit empowerment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited to the practical step of calling the dental office. The psychiatrist does not effectively elicit Seamus’s own motivations, values, or ambivalence about smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative, non-expert stance and avoids imposing a plan. The partnership remains transactional and does not extend to exploring smoking-related ambivalence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is only lightly supported for the dental call and is not cultivated around smoking cessation. More questions about what matters to Seamus could have evoked stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and respects Seamus’s sustain talk. The response is more avoidance than active softening, missing opportunities to use double-sided reflections around ambivalence.
