# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment, never labeling Jonathan as resistant or non-compliant. The warmth is genuine but remains somewhat measured and professional rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally strong reflections that capture emotional nuance and underlying meaning, such as reading 'I'm not stupid' as feeling told he doesn't get it. One minor over-interpretation of 'it doesn't stick' as 'what's the point,' but an otherwise excellent empathic performance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and consistent focus on diabetes self-management throughout, with every question and reflection tied to the presenting concern. A brief premature shift toward problem-solving is quickly corrected when the client resists.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Builds on Jonathan's own metaphors ('second job,' 'play patient') and uses inclusive, exploratory language. However, some heavy interpretive lifting (the 'identity' interpretation, 'mountain vs. path' metaphor) is presented as settled rather than as collaborative offerings for the client to refine.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly removes pressure ('You don't owe anyone a solution today'), validates his right not to have answers, and never imposes a plan or pushes for commitment. Strong and consistent performance across the entire session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One well-constructed evocative question ('what's the smallest piece that could fit?') but the session is largely reflective and validating rather than evocative. No exploration of values, DARN-C talk, scaling questions, or worst/best-case scenarios, and the client's partial response ('one small thing') is not followed up.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance with explicit deferral of expertise to the client ('you know your life better than anyone'). However, the psychiatrist occasionally carries moderate interpretive weight without checking whether reframes land before building on them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes one strong evocative move that opens the door to change talk, but Jonathan's partial response ('one small thing') is not amplified, reflected back, or deepened. The session ends with the client saying 'I don't know what to say next' without a gentle push toward change talk or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively by validating, normalizing, and gently reframing without arguing or minimizing. Could have been more strategic with double-sided reflections to actively pivot toward change more consistently.
