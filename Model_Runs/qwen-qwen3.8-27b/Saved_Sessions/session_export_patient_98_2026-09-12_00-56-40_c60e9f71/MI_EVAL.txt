# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful and safety-conscious tone, acknowledging pain and demoralization. Positive regard is sometimes tied to task completion rather than unconditional patient worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist names relevant emotional experiences like demoralization, stiffness, and tiredness. Reflections are often brief and do not fully capture ambivalence or emotional complexity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete, manageable rehabilitation behavior with little drift. Direction is more task-focused than MI-focused, with limited connection to motivation or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient contributes details such as logging format, time, and reminder phrase. The psychiatrist supplies most of the plan structure and direction, limiting true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Offers some choices and accepts flexibility, but frequently prescribes specific goals, timing, reminders, and steps. Autonomy is repeatedly undermined by directive instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some open or semi-open questions are used, but the session relies heavily on providing practical solutions. There is limited exploration of the patient’s own reasons, values, confidence, or hopes for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are moments of collaboration, such as asking which logging option is easiest. Overall, the psychiatrist maintains an expert-driven, instructional stance with limited patient control over direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expresses ability and hope, such as managing five minutes and feeling less stuck. These statements are often used to advance planning rather than amplified, reflected, or explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expresses substantial sustain talk about pain, stiffness, and the burden of activity. The psychiatrist avoids arguing but often responds with problem-solving rather than MI-based reflections or pivots.
