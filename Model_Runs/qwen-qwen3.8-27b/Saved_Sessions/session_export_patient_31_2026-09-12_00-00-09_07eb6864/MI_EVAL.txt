# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the patient's autonomy and choices. The delivery is supportive but somewhat formulaic and heavily informational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses reflective listening to capture ambivalence, practical concerns, and stated goals. Some deeper emotional themes, such as work stress, side-effect fears, and partner tension, receive less exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Stays consistently focused on smoking cessation and nicotine replacement. The session moves purposefully toward a concrete, actionable harm-reduction plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as joint problem-solving and actively incorporates the patient's preferences. The final plan is co-constructed rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the patient's ownership and freedom of choice. Avoids pressure to quit and supports the patient in defining his own success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk around reducing smell and feeling less wired. Relies more on information provision than open-ended exploration of deeper values or motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and invites input on plan details. The session remains somewhat structured by medical information, making the partnership slightly less fluid.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforces the patient's own reasons for change, such as being less of a nuisance and achieving a meaningful win. Could deepen further by evoking additional patient-defined reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by normalizing ambivalence without arguing. Uses double-sided reflections to reduce resistance and keep focus on harm reduction.
