# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while normalizing the difficulty of balancing work, family, and activity. However, there is limited evidence of sustained warmth or explicit unconditional positive regard across the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s burden and ambivalence by naming the competing demands of work, family, and wanting to feel active again. Empathy could be strengthened by more explicitly reflecting underlying emotions such as guilt, fatigue, or loss of identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a clear and appropriate focus on the target behavior of increasing physical activity and re-establishing a realistic routine. The question is directly tied to the referral reason and moves the conversation toward a concrete, near-term action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the conversation as a joint exploration by asking the client to participate in identifying a realistic step. However, the collaborative process has not yet been fully demonstrated through back-and-forth negotiation or co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s autonomy by asking what would feel realistic for the client rather than prescribing a specific action. The use of “for you” reinforces that the choice belongs to the client and avoids control or pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses an evocative question that invites the client to identify their own motivation and realistic next step. However, because this is only the opening question, it has not yet shown sustained evocation or strategic development of the client’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance by treating the client as the expert on what is realistic. There is not yet explicit language fully centering the client’s expertise or making the power-sharing stance unmistakable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a forward-looking, client-centered question that invites the client to generate their own change-oriented language. However, the response alone does not yet show active amplification, reflection, or strategic expansion of the client’s change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce reasons against change and gently acknowledges difficulty without amplifying it. A stronger response might explicitly pivot toward the client’s values, identity, or past exercise routine to further soften sustain talk.
