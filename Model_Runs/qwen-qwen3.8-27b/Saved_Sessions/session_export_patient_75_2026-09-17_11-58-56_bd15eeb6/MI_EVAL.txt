# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates without judgment, explicitly removing moral failing (e.g., 'not that you don't want to take care of yourself'). Warmth is specific and individualized rather than formulaic, with no shift toward pressure or implicit correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is precise and nuanced, capturing both feeling and meaning throughout (e.g., 'seeing both a shield and a potential harm'). Reflections distinguish between motivation and self-efficacy, demonstrating genuine understanding of the client's internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows a clear progressive arc from broad ambivalence to a concrete, self-generated action step with a client-defined timeline. Each step logically builds on the previous one while remaining guided by the client's own contributions rather than imposed direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All solutions (inhaler by coffee maker) and decisions (two-week timeline) are generated by the client, not the psychiatrist. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with the psychiatrist's role limited to asking, reflecting, and gently clarifying concepts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly reinforced at multiple points (e.g., 'you'd be the one deciding. Every single time.' 'You know your own timeline better than I do'). No language implies obligation, duty, or expectation; all suggestions are framed as options the client can accept or reject.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change are elicited from the client's own mouth through varied strategies including imagery, past success, self-design, and self-defined timelines. The psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change or uses prescriptive language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the expert on her own life and the primary agent of change (e.g., 'if you're the one designing a system for yourself — not him, not a doctor, you'). The psychiatrist never prescribes, recommends, or tells the client what to do.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully elicited, amplified, and built upon progressively through imagery questions, reflection of the client's own words, and validation of self-generated solutions. The psychiatrist strengthens the client's existing motivation rather than introducing new reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with full validation and gentle reframing that opens space for change talk without arguing or dismissing (e.g., 'That lose-lose feeling — it makes so much sense'). The cue vs. choice distinction softens the client's fear without invalidating her concern about overuse.
