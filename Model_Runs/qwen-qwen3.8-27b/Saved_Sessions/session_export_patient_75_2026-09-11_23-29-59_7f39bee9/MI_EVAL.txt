# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, but acceptance is often brief and quickly followed by medical education or directives. The stance is safe and supportive, though not consistently warm or deeply affirming of Linda’s experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate understanding of Linda’s fear and distress, particularly around nighttime symptoms and inhaler use. However, reflections are often surface-level and do not consistently capture her deeper ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management, inhaler use, safety, and practical next steps without drifting off-topic. The main limitation is that the direction is more medical and problem-solving-oriented than MI-guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and checks in about what Linda wants to work on, which shows partial collaboration. However, the session remains largely clinician-led, and Linda’s ideas are not fully integrated into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, and framing decisions as optional. Nonetheless, the frequent use of specific scripts, templates, and cautions can make the client feel less like the primary decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open or permission-based questions, but the session relies more on providing information and reassurance than eliciting Linda’s own motivations. There is limited exploration of what matters to her or her reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and offers options, which supports a partnership stance. However, the interaction remains expert-driven, with the clinician frequently supplying the agenda, rationale, and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only rare and weak attempts to evoke or amplify Linda’s change talk. When she offers positive statements, the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, deepen, or build on them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Linda’s concerns, but often responds with explanation rather than deeper softening. Her ambivalence is not fully held or explored through double-sided reflections or normalization.
