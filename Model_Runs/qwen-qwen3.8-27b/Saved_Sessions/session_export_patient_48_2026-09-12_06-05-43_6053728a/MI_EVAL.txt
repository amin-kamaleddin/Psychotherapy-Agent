# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and consistently normalizes Farid’s fatigue and self-blame. Affirmations are present but can feel somewhat generic in moments of stronger shame or self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and show good perspective-taking regarding exhaustion, ambivalence, and low confidence. However, some emotional cues are responded to in a more practical or normalizing way rather than explored with deeper empathic nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity. It moves efficiently from exploration to a concrete, manageable plan without significant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Farid’s input meaningfully shapes the final plan, including stretches, shoulder rolls, the sneaker cue, and no reminder. The psychiatrist remains the primary architect of the intervention, though the process is still collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports choice by framing the plan as an experiment and inviting tweaks. Autonomy is slightly weakened by prescriptive language and therapist-provided structures.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some evocation of confidence, doability, and practical cues, but limited exploration of Farid’s values, meaning, and intrinsic reasons for change. The process leans more toward structured behavior planning than deep MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Farid’s input and allows his choices to shape the plan. The session is still fairly therapist-guided, with the psychiatrist supplying many options and driving much of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Farid produces some change-related language around planning, ability, and commitment. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or build on this change talk, and deeper change talk is underexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk reasonably well without arguing or reinforcing resistance. There is room to use more double-sided reflections to help Farid explore both sides of his ambivalence.
