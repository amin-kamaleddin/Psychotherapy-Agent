# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout the session. Warmth is generally strong, though at times it feels more task-oriented than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers reasonable empathic responses, especially early in the session, but often stays at a surface or normalization level. Deeper reflections of shame, fear of rejection, loneliness, and helplessness are limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the most pressing stressor and moves toward a concrete, manageable next step. However, the direction becomes quite task-focused rather than consistently MI-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s preferences and adjusts the message based on his input, which shows some collaboration. Still, much of the interaction remains therapist-led, with collaboration mainly involving choices from therapist-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes choice, lack of pressure, and Jordan’s right to decide what feels doable. Autonomy could be stronger by more fully supporting Jordan’s own decision-making rather than presenting multiple therapist-provided options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to evoke Jordan’s own motivations, values, or hopes for change. The session shifts quickly into practical problem-solving rather than drawing out Jordan’s internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and invites Jordan’s input, but the overall interaction often becomes expert-driven. Jordan influences some details, yet the psychiatrist still shapes much of the direction and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan offers some change talk, such as wanting relief and not feeling helpless, but the psychiatrist does not build on it strategically. There are few evocative questions to deepen motivation, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and sometimes normalizes or redirects it with small steps. However, there is not enough consistent MI-consistent work to soften ambivalence or redirect toward values and past strengths.
