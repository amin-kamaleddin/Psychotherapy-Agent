# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows nonjudgmental respect and acceptance of the client’s preferences and limits. There is no moralizing, labeling, or pressure to adopt abstinence as a rule.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate reflective listening to capture the client’s ambivalence and concerns. However, empathy could be deeper by more fully validating the emotional texture of low mood, uncertainty, and fear of feeling judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing evening alcohol use around the specific trigger of pouring. The discussion moves purposefully toward concrete, client-centered strategies without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the client’s ideas shaping the plan. The psychiatrist offers options rather than directives and works with the client as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported by emphasizing the client’s freedom to choose and avoiding controlling language. The counselor helps the client frame the plan as personal rather than imposed by the doctor.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the client’s own values, goals, and self-efficacy rather than from external advice. The counselor skillfully amplifies the client’s own language about choice, contentment, and preserving the wind-down.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on her own life, goals, and evening routine. The session is shaped by the client’s priorities, preferences, and selected strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently evokes and builds on the client’s change talk, including decanting, water, pausing, and the remote cue. The client generates specific reasons and plans for change, which the psychiatrist reinforces and expands.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk such as not wanting to give up wine, avoid rules, or feel scolded is acknowledged without argument or dismissal. The counselor uses these statements as bridges toward change while respecting the client’s need for simplicity and authenticity.
