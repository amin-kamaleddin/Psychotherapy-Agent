# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, normalizing Laura's struggles without blame. Acceptance is strong but remains somewhat professional and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflective listening to capture Laura's ambivalence and emotional concerns. Some reflections feel slightly interpretive or repetitive, with missed opportunities to explore deeper meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on creating a concrete evening yoga routine. It moves efficiently from values to behavior planning, confidence, barriers, and follow-up with little wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and invites Laura to shape the plan, with her ideas clearly influencing the final routine. Collaboration is helpful but can feel slightly guided rather than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and choice-based planning support Laura's autonomy, but some language feels prescriptive or pressuring. Terms like “nonnegotiable” and “lock it in” may undermine the sense of voluntary choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes motivation by asking about values, confidence, and preferences. However, answer menus and frequent suggestions lead the client more than drawing out Laura's own change reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and frequently invites Laura's input on plan details. The session still feels somewhat agenda-driven because the psychiatrist introduces and structures many components.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura offers several useful change statements about health, grounding, and confidence. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes Laura's fear of slipping up and frames the plan as compassionate rather than punitive. It could be stronger by using more advanced MI techniques to fully resolve underlying fear of failure.
