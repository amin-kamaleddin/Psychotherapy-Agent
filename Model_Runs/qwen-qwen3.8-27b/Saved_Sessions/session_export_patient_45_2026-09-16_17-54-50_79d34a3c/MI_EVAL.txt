# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Erik's hesitation. It is not a 5 because the warmth is competent but procedural, with fewer individualized affirmations of his broader struggles and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, two-sided reflections that capture Erik's ambivalence and practical concerns. A 5 would require more consistent emotional reflection of underlying feelings such as fear, exhaustion, frustration, or concern about lifelong medication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on concrete target behaviors and moves logically toward a workable next step. Each question advances Erik toward a clearer, actionable plan without drifting into general advice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as a collaborative partner, building on Erik's supervisor identity and co-constructing the plan. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still shapes the structure and provides some framing rather than fully client-led direction at every point.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Erik's sense of choice, ownership, and self-determination. There is no pressure, controlling language, or imposition of a rigid plan, and decisions are repeatedly returned to Erik.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Erik's own ideas and change-oriented problem-solving. A 5 would include more explicit evocation of importance, confidence, values, need, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Erik as the expert in his own life and uses his supervisor role to guide problem-solving. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still retains a guiding role and occasionally provides framing or direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful client-generated change statements and supports a concrete plan. It is not a 5 because it does not systematically develop all forms of change talk, especially desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles resistance well by reflecting both sides and validating concerns instead of arguing. A 5 would involve more active pivoting and more consistent double-sided reflections that explicitly elicit the change side of ambivalence.
