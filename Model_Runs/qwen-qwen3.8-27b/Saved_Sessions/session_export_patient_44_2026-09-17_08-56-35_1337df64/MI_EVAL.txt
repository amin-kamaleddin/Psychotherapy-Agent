# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used warm, nonjudgmental language and validated Erik’s struggles without moralizing. Acceptance was strong but not fully unconditional, as it often tied to effort or progress, and the final boundary about journaling was not explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Erik’s ambivalence, self-criticism, and conflict about the gym, comfort, and consistency. Some reflections remained somewhat paraphrastic and did not fully explore deeper themes like shame, aging, or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on making gym attendance sustainable while reducing discouragement. The conversation moved logically from comparison and self-criticism to a concrete, client-owned plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Erik’s ideas shaped the plan, including 20-minute treadmill sessions, a Tuesday check-in, and monitoring back stiffness and fogginess. The psychiatrist built on Erik’s language rather than imposing a preselected solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized Erik’s choice, ownership, and right to adjust or stop the plan if it did not work. Erik’s uncertainty about changing gym times was respected without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions helped Erik generate his own reasons for change, plan details, and success measures. The psychiatrist effectively expanded Erik’s language, especially around “shifting the scoreboard.”

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Erik as the expert on his habits and followed his proposed solutions. However, some counselor-generated options were introduced, showing a slight tendency to shape Erik’s thinking rather than fully wait for his own next ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked strong MI-consistent questions that led Erik to express desire, ability, and commitment to a simpler gym plan. The score was not a 5 because emerging change talk was not always amplified, labeled, or consolidated more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalized and softened sustain talk, such as comparison, guilt about food, and distrust in willpower, without arguing or moralizing. Some sustain talk, especially food-related guilt and self-doubt about agency, could have been explored and reframed more deeply.
