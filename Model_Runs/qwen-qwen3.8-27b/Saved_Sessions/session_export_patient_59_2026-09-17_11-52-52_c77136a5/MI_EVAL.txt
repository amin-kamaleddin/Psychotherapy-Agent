# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone with multiple explicit affirmations and skillful reframing of minimization as clarity. However, affirmations tend to cluster around Jamal's compliance and practicality rather than extending to his personhood more broadly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are specific, accurate, and grounded in Jamal's actual words, with good tracking of his emotional shift during the session. The psychiatrist could have gone deeper on the unexplored emotional layer (shame, fear) beneath Jamal's 'it's not a big deal' presentation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session explores feelings and practicalities but does not converge on a specific focus area or concrete target behavior for future work. The conversation feels somewhat circular without a brief summary that ties themes together and collaboratively establishes a focus for next time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and positions Jamal as having knowledge to contribute, creating a collaborative framework. In practice, however, the interaction remains somewhat therapist-led, with Jamal in a minimal-response role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is woven naturally into every interaction — explicit invitations for Jamal's preferences, rejection of authority over his life, and consistent absence of directive language or implicit pressure to change. This meets the highest standard and feels genuine rather than scripted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses evocative questions that draw out Jamal's own thinking without providing external reasons for change or psychoeducation. The limitation is that evocation stays within the frame of compliance and practicality rather than probing deeper personal values or future identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Language like 'let's work from here' and questions about what Jamal wants positions the psychiatrist as a collaborator rather than an expert. The power dynamic still leans slightly toward the psychiatrist leading, who could have more explicitly invited Jamal to set the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and reflects Jamal's statements of agency but does not actively cultivate deeper change talk beyond compliance-driven motivation. Missed opportunities to probe what school, friends, or the future mean to him and how this situation might affect those areas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully without arguing or creating a righting reflex, and the reframe of minimization as 'getting clear' is a textbook double-sided reflection. Could have used more double-sided reflections to gently hold both sides of Jamal's ambivalence.
