# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes struggles such as comparison, slips, and aiming lower. It is generally supportive, though the affirmations can feel somewhat generic rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brief reflections capture some of Erik’s concerns, such as comparing himself and wanting less back stiffness. However, the responses often remain at acknowledgment and miss deeper emotional themes like feeling behind, small, or afraid of quitting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on exercise consistency, back pain reduction, and candy-related decisions without drifting. Each response advances a concrete, behavior-focused plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik’s choices shape the plan, including mobility work, the schedule, the podcast reward, and the calendar note. However, much of the plan is therapist-generated, making it less fully collaborative in the MI sense.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices, backup options, and non-coercive language about partial progress. Detailed instructions and prescriptive plans slightly reduce the sense of full client self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful evocative moves, especially validating that tying shoes without wincing would be a real win. The psychiatrist, however, provides answers and strategies more often than drawing out Erik’s own reasons and commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and adapts to Erik’s preferences, showing practical collaboration. The role remains more expert-driven than partner-like, with frequent problem-solving and detailed guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik offers relevant change statements, such as wanting function, reduced pain, and a fair approach to candy. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen these statements with stronger evocative questions or affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled reasonably by normalizing fear of failure, allowing slips, and avoiding rigid rules. It would be stronger with more double-sided reflections and rolling with resistance before moving into problem-solving.
