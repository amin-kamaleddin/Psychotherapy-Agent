# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, supportive, and largely nonjudgmental stance, including autonomy-respecting and normalizing language. However, some coach-like wording and the unaddressed final confidentiality concern prevented a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used adequate reflective listening and captured the client’s competing concerns about performance, friends, and stress. The reflections were more cognitive and behavior-focused than deeply emotionally nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior of reducing or avoiding vaping before basketball games and practices. The psychiatrist moved the session coherently toward a concrete, behaviorally specific plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well by inviting client input, honoring preferences for a short text check-in, and co-creating practical steps. The collaboration was limited by the psychiatrist introducing much of the solution space before fully co-shaping the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly sought permission, offered choices, and respected the client’s preference for a low-key approach. Some mildly directive or expectation-setting language kept the score below 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful client-driven language by asking about concerns, confidence, past attempts, and potential obstacles. Evocation was interrupted by an early shift toward advice, scripts, and concrete planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed some partnership by asking for input and offering options, but the intervention remained largely shaped by the psychiatrist. The client was not consistently positioned as the primary source of wisdom about her own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked questions that drew out change talk and affirmed statements such as wanting to play sharp and being able to say no. The development of change talk became inconsistent once the session shifted into concrete planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue against or reinforce the client’s sustain talk and acknowledged some positive aspects of vaping. However, the psychiatrist did not consistently use double-sided reflections or deeper exploration to help resolve ambivalence.
