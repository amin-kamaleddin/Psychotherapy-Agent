# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirming statements that validate the patient's honesty and self-awareness. Could go a step further in explicitly normalizing the difficulty of social anxiety or the courage it takes to discuss these feelings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening, particularly with the double-sided reflection on safety versus aloneness. Accurately captures the emotional weight of the patient's ambivalence and the painful avoidance cycle.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on community engagement with a logical progression from ambivalence to exploring barriers to future consequences. Consistently ties conversation back to the core issue without being pushy, maintaining clear momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the session as a joint exploration, frequently asking for the patient's perspective and building on her contributions rather than imposing a framework. Could be slightly more explicit in acknowledging the patient's expertise in her own experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by never pressuring the patient to commit to a specific action or timeline, and by offering easy outs. Could be strengthened with more explicit statements about the patient's right to choose her own pace and path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the patient's own motivations through progressive questioning, moving from initial ambivalence to the patient naming her own concerns about the future. The evocative arc builds self-awareness without the psychiatrist providing conclusions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance using inclusive language and inviting the patient's input at key moments. The conversation feels like a genuine dialogue rather than an interview, with the psychiatrist positioning herself as a curious partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates change talk by helping the patient articulate what would make engagement manageable and by drawing out her own concerns about the future trajectory of inaction. Could push slightly further in exploring confidence, ability, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating the safety function of avoidance without reinforcing it as the best path. Could be more active in the softening process rather than primarily validating the sustain talk.
