# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite the court-mandated context and shoplifting disclosure. Warmth is present but sometimes feels formulaic and lacks explicit affirmation of the client’s strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s ambivalence, practical tensions, and social risks. Empathy could be stronger by more deeply reflecting underlying guilt, shame, and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing shoplifting and developing a practical exit strategy without significant drift. Linking micro-strategies more explicitly to broader values and long-term goals would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as an equal partner and uses the client’s own language in planning. Collaboration could become more fully joint by explicitly framing the work as shared problem-solving and co-created next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and non-directive stance support the client’s decision-making and avoid imposed solutions. The psychiatrist could more explicitly reinforce the client’s freedom to choose and ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses effective open questions to elicit the client’s own ideas, motivations, and practical strategies. Evocation would be stronger if it more consistently drew out deeper values, self-respect, and intrinsic change motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on his life, friends, and triggers while inviting his own solutions. Partnership could be further strengthened by more explicit agenda negotiation and shared session goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces several change-related statements, and the psychiatrist elicits some motivation to stop. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, scale, or deepen this change talk with values-based follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustain talk about preserving peer status and avoiding “ditching” friends is prominent and often centered in the conversation. The psychiatrist could better soften this by using double-sided reflections that pivot toward change and alternative sources of belonging.
