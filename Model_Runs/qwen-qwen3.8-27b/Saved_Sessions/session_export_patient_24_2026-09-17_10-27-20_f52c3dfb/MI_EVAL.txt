# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session. The client’s alcohol use is treated without moralizing or pressure, while the client’s perspective and effort are consistently affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate and nuanced reflections that capture the client’s ambivalence, routine, and bodily experience. Strong empathic responses such as “holding two truths” and “closing the file” show deep understanding of the client’s meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on evening alcohol use and develops a coherent plan around a specific wind-down behavior. The target of reducing alcohol use could be more explicitly connected back to the initial health concern or reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to choose where to start and co-constructs the plan using the client’s own ideas and language. The final experiment is shaped by client contributions rather than imposed by the therapist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s right to choose, keeps the plan flexible, and avoids pressure, obligation, or controlling language. Options such as “beer wins this round” and skipping without guilt reinforce autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits the client’s own reasons, functions, and possible steps rather than providing advice. Questions help the client articulate the meaning of the beer and identify his own possible change ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on his own recovery and daily life. The session is organized around the client’s preferences and choices, reflecting genuine collaboration and shared authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk and affirms the client’s proposed small experiments. The session could be stronger by more explicitly developing deeper change talk such as desire, need, benefits, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by validating the function of the beer rather than arguing against it. However, the client’s sustain talk remains central and could be further transformed using more double-sided reflections or change-direction pivots.
