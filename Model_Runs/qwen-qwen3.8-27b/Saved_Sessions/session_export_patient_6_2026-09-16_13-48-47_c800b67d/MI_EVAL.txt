# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming. Acceptance could be strengthened with more explicit normalization of the mistake and positive screen as part of growth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and track the client’s goals, barriers, and concerns well. More explicit attunement to underlying emotions would strengthen the empathy score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior of avoiding alcohol in peer-pressure situations. Each step moves the client toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as a genuine partnership with the client co-creating the plan. The interaction consistently feels collaborative rather than prescriptive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice and ownership are emphasized throughout. The client is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker in developing and selecting the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own goals, preferences, and plan. More deliberate drawing out of deeper change talk would improve evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms that the client is the expert on their goals and realistic options. Client preferences directly shape the conversation and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client produces strong change talk and the psychiatrist affirms the client’s goals and confidence. The session could more explicitly develop and label the full range of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and redirects toward skill-building and goal protection. More active use of double-sided reflections would better soften ambivalence.
