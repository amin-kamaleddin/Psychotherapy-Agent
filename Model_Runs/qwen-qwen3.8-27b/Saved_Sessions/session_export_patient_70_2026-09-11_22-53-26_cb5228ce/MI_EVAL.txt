# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys respect and nonjudgment, avoiding labels such as lazy, failing, or irresponsible. The warmth is supportive but remains somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects practical barriers such as boredom, cost, nervousness, and paperwork overload. Deeper emotional themes, including fear of reoffending, shame, guilt, and identity concerns, are not consistently captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on high-risk Fridays, paperwork barriers, and car-related engagement. The main limitation is that the direction is more logistical than values-linked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Lars to choose among options and co-creates messages and practical steps. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist often supplies the plan rather than negotiating the agenda as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lars is offered choices and some boundaries are respected, such as around Donna. However, prescriptive planning and repeated focus on paperwork can reduce the sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly offers practical options rather than drawing out Lars’s own reasons for change. Values, importance, confidence, and the meaning of moving forward are not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership language is present, but the overall stance is more counselor-driven than client-led. The psychiatrist does not consistently frame Lars as the expert on his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lars offers useful change talk, and the psychiatrist supports some client-led choices. These statements are not consistently amplified, reflected back, or expanded into a stronger motivational rationale.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tolerates sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it. More advanced MI moves, such as double-sided reflections, are not consistently used to soften sustain talk toward change.
