# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, validating Richard’s concerns without arguing. It is somewhat formulaic and task-focused, with limited explicit normalization of his mixed feelings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures Richard’s ambivalence and values. It remains mostly content-level and does not deeply explore underlying emotions such as fear of failing his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cholesterol management and moves toward a concrete, realistic next step tied to retirement. Later logistics and an inaccurate medication timeline slightly reduce clarity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well and uses Richard’s ideas to build a manageable plan. However, the clinician often supplies structure, scripts, and details rather than letting Richard generate more of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard is regularly invited to choose preferences, including how to involve his wife, and pressure is kept low. Frequent provision of scripts and next steps still feels somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several questions draw out some motivation, but the psychiatrist often shifts to external suggestions before deeper exploration. More evocation is needed to elicit Richard’s values, confidence, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard’s choices clearly influence the session and the final plan feels shared. The psychiatrist still plays a fairly expert role by generating many options and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard expresses useful change talk and commitment, and the psychiatrist affirms it. More consistent skill is needed to expand his own reasons, ability, and commitment rather than moving quickly to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally met without argument and reframed in a low-pressure way. MI-specific softening strategies, such as double-sided reflections or turning sustain talk into change talk, are used less consistently.
