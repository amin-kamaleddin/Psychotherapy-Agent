# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client's concerns. Acceptance could be deepened with more individualized affirmation rather than generic phrases.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's ambivalence and parenting conflict. The counselor could attend more deeply to the emotional weight behind her fear of choosing wrong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the vaccine decision and moves from hesitancy to a practical plan. Direction could be tightened by reducing information-heavy sections and anchoring to one shared target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frames the work as shared and offers options instead of imposing a decision. The client's own priorities could guide the plan more consistently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and avoids pressure or directive advice. Autonomy support could be strengthened by explicitly affirming the client's ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions draw out concerns, values, and tentative willingness. The counselor often moves into information-giving before fully eliciting and deepening the client's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys a collaborative stance and invites the client's perspective throughout. More explicit framing of the client as the expert on her family's values would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked through questions about priorities and acceptable options. The counselor does not consistently amplify, return to, or strengthen this emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or harsh correction. The counselor could use more double-sided reflections to help the client hold both concerns and goals together.
