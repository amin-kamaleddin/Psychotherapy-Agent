# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoided shaming Adrian for methamphetamine use. However, acceptance was sometimes practical or administrative rather than consistently warm and individually affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was adequate for summarizing Adrian’s practical concerns and preferences. Empathy was often factual rather than emotional, with limited reflection of underlying shame, stigma, or fear of judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session had a clear practical focus on wound follow-up, privacy, and immediate safety. Direction was weaker on the target behavior of methamphetamine use, as much of the time was spent on logistics rather than a broader change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked collaboratively by seeking Adrian’s input and incorporating his preferences into the plan. Collaboration was mostly task-focused rather than deeply exploratory of values, ambivalence, or broader substance-use goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a strength, with repeated emphasis on choice, privacy, no pressure, and respect for Adrian’s decisions. Some more directive or administrative language slightly reduced the autonomy-affirming tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist began by asking open questions and eliciting what Adrian wanted to happen next. Evocation became less consistent after the early phase, shifting toward options, information, and scheduling details.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited Adrian to help shape the session. Partnership was strong but not fully client-led, as the psychiatrist set much of the agenda and presented expert-driven options early.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited, such as wanting the infection not to return and being more careful about where he gets substances. The psychiatrist did not consistently reinforce, expand, or deepen these statements into stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by avoiding argumentation, blame, or persuasion when Adrian minimized use or rejected a program. The score was not higher because there was limited strategic reframing to link his current statements with safer-use goals.
