# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor remained respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Kareem’s choice to continue smoking while managing the new policy. Acceptance was strong, though somewhat professional/formulaic and could have more explicitly affirmed Kareem’s ownership of the decision.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Kareem’s surface concerns about routine, hassle, and keeping family out of it. Empathy was more cognitive than emotionally nuanced, with limited reflection of underlying feelings such as irritation, autonomy, or relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking-related workplace behavior, particularly breaks, route planning, and keeping smoke away from the family. Direction was clear, but the target remained logistics of continuing to smoke rather than a shared change agenda around smoking reduction or cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor adjusted the conversation based on Kareem’s rejections and co-created a simple, practical plan. Collaboration was generally strong, but counselor-generated lists and options reduced the client’s role in developing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor consistently respected Kareem’s autonomy, avoided pressure, and supported his decisions about route planning and solo follow-up. Kareem’s own success criteria were honored, reinforcing ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few evocative questions were used, such as asking what matters most and what will tell him the route works. Evocation was limited overall, with insufficient exploration of Kareem’s values, ambivalence, or internal motivation for changing smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor behaved as a partner rather than an authority and invited Kareem to set priorities. Partnership was strong, but offering multiple structured options and brief advice-like lists made the interaction somewhat prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor made limited attempts to invite change-related thinking, but rarely elicited or amplified client language about quitting, cutting down, or protecting family. The session largely reinforced Kareem’s focus on keeping the habit and managing logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor avoided arguing with or reinforcing Kareem’s sustain talk, which prevented a push-pull dynamic. However, there was limited use of double-sided reflections, scaling, or curiosity questions to help Kareem explore ambivalence.
