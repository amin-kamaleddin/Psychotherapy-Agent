# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental, supportive language that reduces shame and frames the encounter as support rather than punishment. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with fewer explicit affirmations of the client's strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Isabelle's concerns accurately, especially her pressure to keep the session brief and her sense of intrusion. Empathy is solid but remains more task-focused than deeply exploratory of underlying fear, shame, or personal meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays anchored to alcohol risk, the screening result, and the need for a direct question. Direction is effective but drifts into scheduling and process management rather than consistently deepening focus on change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Isabelle to shape the conversation and incorporates her suggested question and wrap-up criteria. Collaboration is strong, though it is more procedural than substantive regarding her goals, values, or change plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes Isabelle's choice, avoids coercion, and respects her request for brevity. This is model autonomy support for a resistant, time-pressed client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and reflections to draw out Isabelle's preferences and comfort level. Evocation is present but mainly focused on process rather than eliciting internal motivation related to alcohol use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative, power-sharing stance and lets Isabelle influence how the conversation unfolds. Partnership is strong, though the counselor still sets the broader frame around screening, follow-up, and boundaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session focuses mostly on logistics, screening, and the direct question rather than the client's own reasons to change. There is little systematic elicitation or amplification of DARN-C-style change talk about reducing or monitoring alcohol use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to sustain talk without arguing or pressuring, and validates Isabelle's desire for choice. However, sustain talk is mostly accommodated rather than reframed or connected to change-related goals.
