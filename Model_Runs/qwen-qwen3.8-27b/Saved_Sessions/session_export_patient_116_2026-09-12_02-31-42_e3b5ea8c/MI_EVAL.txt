# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and affirms Samir’s choices. It reaches a 5 only if warmth and validation are more individualized rather than somewhat formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are strong reflections and interpretations of Samir’s meaning, including identity and autonomy concerns. Some responses remain surface-level and do not fully explore deeper emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on medication adherence and related decisions. Focus is diluted by repeated practical options, making it feel more like problem-solving than motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and invites choice, but the interaction remains fairly counselor-driven. The session would benefit from more co-constructed agenda-setting and less menu-style option offering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through explicit respect for choice and the inclusion of “do nothing” as an option. The repeated introduction of structured practical options subtly steers the session toward action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some useful open questions to elicit motivation and meaning. Evocation is inconsistent because the conversation repeatedly shifts into practical suggestions before fully developing Samir’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms Samir as the decision-maker and accepts his final choice. The session is less fully client-driven when the clinician introduces multiple specific options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate evocative questions, but Samir’s emerging change talk is not deeply amplified or reinforced. More use of importance, confidence, and value-based follow-up would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively normalizes and responds to sustain talk without argument or confrontation. More strategic double-sided reflections or tension-focused follow-up could further soften sustain talk and move toward change.
