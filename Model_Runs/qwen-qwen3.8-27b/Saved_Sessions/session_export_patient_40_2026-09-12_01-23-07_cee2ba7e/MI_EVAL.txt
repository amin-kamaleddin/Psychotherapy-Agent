# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, nonjudgment, and respect for the client’s experience. Client fears, pride, and uncertainty are met without labeling or blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently reflects the client’s ambivalence and mixed motives accurately. Empathy is sometimes cognitive and moves quickly into problem-solving, missing deeper themes of shame and fear of being alone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol/pill use, relapse prevention, and urge management. It moves efficiently from ambivalence to a concrete, behavior-specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan with the client, building on client-generated cues and supports. The client contributes key elements such as the reminder phrase, 10-minute plan, and sister text.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers choices and accepts the client’s minimal plan without pressure. The client is treated as the decision-maker in selecting small, realistic experiments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits client-owned steps, values, and support resources. However, there is heavier reliance on externally supplied scripts than deeper open-ended evocation of the client’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as an expert on his own life and accepts a simpler plan over a more complex one. The balance is not fully client-centered because several clinical structures and scripts are introduced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client produces meaningful change talk and the psychiatrist reinforces strengths and realistic planning. More explicit elicitation and amplification of desire, ability, need, and confidence language would strengthen this.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s doubts without challenging or amplifying them and redirects toward practical experiments. More consistent use of double-sided reflections would more explicitly pivot sustain talk toward change.
