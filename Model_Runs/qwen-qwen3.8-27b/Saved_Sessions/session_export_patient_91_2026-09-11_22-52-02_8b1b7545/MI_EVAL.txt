# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, with several affirming and normalizing statements. Acceptance is somewhat surface-level and can feel more clinical or minimizes the emotional weight before moving quickly into advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate perspective-taking, especially around blood sugar monitoring and difficulty with reminders. Empathy is limited by brief, generic responses and missed opportunities to reflect Marcus’s frustration, self-doubt, and ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior of blood sugar monitoring and moves toward a concrete plan with follow-up. The focus is task-oriented rather than fully MI-driven, and could better link the behavior to Marcus’s values and personal reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and shared decision-making, as Marcus chooses details like the cue, script, alarm wording, and follow-up timing. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently acts as the expert providing ready-made plans rather than co-creating them with Marcus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, avoids overt pressure, and supports Marcus’s ownership in some ways, such as allowing him to define a nonpunitive consequence. Autonomy support is inconsistent because the psychiatrist is often directive and prescriptive rather than repeatedly reinforcing Marcus’s own choices and voice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist’s approach is largely advice-based, providing many strategies, steps, and solutions rather than eliciting Marcus’s own motivations and confidence. There are limited evocative questions, and multiple opportunities are missed to draw out Marcus’s values, self-efficacy, and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial partnership by asking for input and offering choices, and Marcus influences some parts of the plan. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist often adopts an expert-driven posture and gives multi-step instructions before fully exploring Marcus’s perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus spontaneously produces important change talk, but the psychiatrist only weakly acknowledges it instead of reflecting, amplifying, or deepening it. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to ask why those changes matter or how Marcus sees himself as capable of follow-through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Marcus and gently softens some resistance by offering practical, low-pressure alternatives. The skill is limited because sustain talk and ambivalence are often worked around rather than strategically addressed with more advanced MI reflections.
