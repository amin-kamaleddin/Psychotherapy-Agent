# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-judgmental and normalizing throughout, with strong validations of the client's emotional states. Slightly professional in warmth rather than deeply individualized; lacks a moment of explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking for most of the session, accurately tracking the client's emotional arc. The final response ('I understand. Can you tell me more about that?') is a weak, generic reflection that misses the opportunity to deepen connection at a critical moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use with a logical arc from assessment to feedback to exploration of change. The session lacks a purposeful closing move that would establish a concrete between-session focus or forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative interaction where the client's responses substantially shape the session's direction and content. The psychiatrist contributes perspective but consistently returns to the client for interpretation and meaning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model autonomy support with explicit centering of the client's expertise and right to choose. No persuasion, advice-giving, or implied obligation at any point in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of evocative questions (five-or-ten-year framing, 'what made it easier to consider that?') that elicit the client's own change talk. The final missed opportunity to evoke deeper meaning from the client's ambivalence statement weakens the close.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is structured as a genuine dialogue with consistent framing of the work as collaborative. The psychiatrist positions himself as curious and open, never assuming authority over the client's experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questioning successfully elicits the client's first substantive change talk ('maybe four instead of six') and the psychiatrist explicitly names and amplifies this shift. The session ends without further developing the rich change talk and ambivalence in the client's final messages.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles minimization without arguing, redirecting to the client's own values rather than lecturing. Could have used more double-sided reflections to explicitly honor the sustain talk while keeping the door open for change.
