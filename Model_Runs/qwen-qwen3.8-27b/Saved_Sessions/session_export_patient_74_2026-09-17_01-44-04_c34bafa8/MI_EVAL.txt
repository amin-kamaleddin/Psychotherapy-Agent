# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Linda’s ambivalence and fear of judgment. It is not a 5 because the warmth is sometimes formulaic and the rapid succession of reflections/questions can feel slightly leading.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Linda’s ambivalence, the function of smoking, and the emotional meaning of protecting her children. Empathy is not a 5 because some reflections remain more cognitive than emotional and could more fully acknowledge her exhaustion, fear, and sense of being trapped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally maintains focus on smoking as a stress-management behavior in Linda’s daily life. It is not a 5 because the focus is not always explicitly collaborative and not every question is clearly tied to a specific change-related endpoint.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and builds on Linda’s ideas, but the interaction can feel counselor-led due to repeated therapist turns. More explicit co-construction, agenda-setting, and check-ins would strengthen partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Linda’s choice by normalizing her lack of readiness and avoiding directive or coercive language. The score is not 5 because the rapid sequence of questions can create subtle pressure and assumes some focus without explicit consent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Linda’s own perspectives and values, particularly around her children and patience. However, the evocation is uneven because many questions draw out sustain talk or ambivalence rather than consistently expanding change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist signals that Linda is the expert on her experience and follows some of her leads. Partnership is not stronger because the therapist often drives the conversation and there is limited explicit agenda-setting or power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda produces some values-based change talk about not wanting to model smoking and not yelling at her children. The psychiatrist does not consistently affirm, amplify, or deepen this change talk, and the session ends with sustain talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Linda and respects her statements that she is not ready to stop. However, some questions prolong focus on the benefits of smoking rather than using double-sided reflections or redirection toward discrepancy and change.
