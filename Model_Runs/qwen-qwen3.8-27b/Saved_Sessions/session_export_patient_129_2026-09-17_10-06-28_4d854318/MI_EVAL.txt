# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys nonjudgmental positive regard and validates the client’s perspective without blame or harsh correction. The stance remains warm and affirming even when the client minimizes the issue or externalizes responsibility.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects the client’s perspective, conflict, and desire to protect his identity. However, the reflections remain somewhat cognitive and could better attend to underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or anger.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and coherent focus on the presenting concerns, including the phone, peer pressure, grades, and conflict with his mother. Direction could be strengthened by more directly exploring the substance use itself, its risks, and the client’s values related to use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a collaborative interaction shaped by the client’s concerns and avoids lecturing or imposing solutions. The collaboration could be strengthened by more explicitly framing the session as a shared problem-solving process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support for client autonomy is evident through open-ended questions, avoidance of direct advice, and emphasis on the client’s choice. Some phrasing, such as “show your mom you’re taking this seriously,” could feel subtly directive or pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to identify goals and small steps but does not deeply explore values, confidence, or internal motivation. Evocation would benefit from more sustained exploration of what matters to the client and his reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, partner-like stance and respects the client as an expert in his own life. However, some questions subtly introduce the psychiatrist’s agenda and could better invite co-creation of the session focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand on it. Use of DARN-C change talk strategies is limited, reducing the depth of change-focused engagement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client but does not consistently redirect sustain talk toward change. Some reflections may inadvertently reinforce the client’s desire to keep things as they are rather than gently highlight values or goals for change.
