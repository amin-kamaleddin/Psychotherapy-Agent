# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s ambivalence without disapproval or confrontation. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly honoring the client’s inner conflict, values, and sense of self.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate, especially in capturing the tension between protecting grandparents and wanting to avoid extra burden. Empathy would improve by exploring deeper emotional or identity-based themes rather than remaining mostly cognitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session starts with a clear focus on the flu vaccine but becomes diluted by many alternatives and later confusing references to the COVID-19 vaccine. A stronger session would maintain a single agreed-upon target behavior and use summaries to keep the focus clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and options, but often shifts into advice-giving and practical planning. More collaboration would involve helping the client generate his own solutions and priorities before offering logistical support.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through permission-seeking, explicit statements that the decision belongs to the client, and respect for his preference for simplicity. Some language subtly nudges toward vaccination by framing it as easier or a “caring choice,” which slightly weakens neutrality.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does draw on the client’s value of protecting his grandparents and asks some open questions. Evocation is limited by frequent shifts to information provision and problem-solving rather than deeper exploration of the client’s own values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction shows some partnership, but it often becomes counselor-led problem-solving rather than a fully collaborative exploration. The client’s expertise in his own values and decision-making is not consistently foregrounded.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk about protecting his grandparents and reducing hassle, and the psychiatrist affirms some of it. The process would be stronger with more consistent MI strategies, such as scaling questions, DARN-CAT questions, and deeper amplification of the client’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and uses normalizing statements that reduce defensiveness. However, the softening is inconsistent, and more effective use of double-sided reflections would help resolve ambivalence without reinforcing the client’s reasons to stay the same.
