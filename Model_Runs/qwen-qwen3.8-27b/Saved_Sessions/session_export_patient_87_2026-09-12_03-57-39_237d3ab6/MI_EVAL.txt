# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful, nonjudgmental stance with supportive validations. The approach is somewhat formulaic and occasionally mildly evaluative, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture key feelings such as frustration, exhaustion, and being trapped by food cues. They remain surface-level and do not consistently deepen shame, guilt, or emotional complexity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of weight-related eating and nighttime grazing. Repetitive cycles through multiple options reduce momentum and make the direction feel procedural.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a generally collaborative tone with inclusive language and invitations for input. The counselor remains the primary source of ideas, limiting deeper shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects the client's final selection of tea only. Repeated suggestions of a concrete plan can feel prescriptive and undermine a sense of freedom and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful client-driven statements about goals, confidence, and willingness to try. Evocation is not sustained, with limited exploration of values, ability, and deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-based questions and invitations to choose demonstrate some partnership. The interaction becomes increasingly strategist-driven, shifting away from equal power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as a confidence rating of six and willingness to try tea. These openings are not amplified, reflected back, or connected to deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with the client's concerns but does not effectively soften or redirect strong sustain talk. Responses often add more options instead of using ambivalence-based or double-sided reflections.
