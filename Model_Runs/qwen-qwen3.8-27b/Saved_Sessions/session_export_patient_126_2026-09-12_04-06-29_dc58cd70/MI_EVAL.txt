# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids blaming the client. Warmth is somewhat clinical and does not consistently affirm the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate but mostly content-based rather than feeling-based. Deeper emotions such as guilt, fear, or fatherhood values are underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays mostly focused on the client's drinking and its possible impact on parenting. Focus is slightly diluted by multiple practical options and logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction feels collaborative on the surface, with the client contributing to the final plan. The clinician still drives much of the agenda through risk questions and structured options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking permission, emphasizing client control, and respecting the client's limits. Repeated suggestions for alarms, texts, and check-ins can feel mildly controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions about concerns, values, and preferences. Evocation is limited because the conversation quickly shifts to practical problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's input shapes the session, and the psychiatrist invites collaboration. However, the session remains largely led by the clinician's expert assessment and recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, such as stopping earlier and using the 'one more' rule. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk and often normalizes it. More MI-consistent responses, such as double-sided reflections, could better reframe denial and resistance.
