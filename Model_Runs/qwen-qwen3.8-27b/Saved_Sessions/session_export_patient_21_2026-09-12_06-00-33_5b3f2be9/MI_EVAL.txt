# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, with frequent support for Camila's effort and choices. Minor issue: some praise feels evaluative rather than fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects goals, tiredness, fear of being graded, and self-criticism. More emotionally nuanced, double-sided reflections of ambivalence would improve.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on realistic activity goals, timing, cues, and measurable signs. It remains clear and organized despite an initial cluster of questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed, with Camila selecting cues, backup options, follow-up timing, and support style. Slightly less perfect if more initial options were generated by the client first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently emphasizes choice, respects boundaries, and avoids pressure or directives. Minor evaluative praise does not undermine the strong autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits motivation, confidence, and client-generated ideas through open questions. Offering many suggestions before the client generates options reduces evocation depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician collaboratively invites input and lets Camila shape the plan and follow-up preferences. It would be stronger if the clinician waited longer for client-generated options before suggesting alternatives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully draws out desire, ability, reasons, and commitment language around activity. It would benefit from deeper amplification of the strongest change talk before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician responds effectively to tiredness, failure worry, and low confidence with gentle, workable alternatives. More double-sided reflections would better explore ambivalence.
