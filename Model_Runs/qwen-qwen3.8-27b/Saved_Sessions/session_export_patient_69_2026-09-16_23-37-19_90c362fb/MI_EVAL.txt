# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental and respectful stance while honoring the client's boundary for brevity. Validation is generally strong but can feel procedural and may subtly reinforce disengagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's experience of monotony and external pressure. The counselor misses deeper underlying feelings such as resentment, feeling controlled, or being treated as a problem.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear MI direction toward meaningful engagement and instead narrows to administrative compliance. There is no strategic roadmap or collaborative focus that moves the conversation toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative language is present, but the interaction does not consistently function between equal partners. The counselor does not build a collaborative plan or shared next step when the client pushes to end.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client's choice and avoids pressuring engagement. The approach becomes somewhat permissive by not actively supporting autonomy in the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions are used to elicit the client's own experience and concerns. Follow-up is limited, so the client's minimal change talk is not deepened or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges the client's expertise and asks what matters, showing some partnership. However, the counselor does not meaningfully co-create the agenda or maintain collaboration when the client wants to leave.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few attempts to draw out the client's own language, but these are not developed. Strong seeds for change, such as wanting respect and not being treated as broken, are not amplified or connected to motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue, but the session largely accommodates the client's desire to sign and leave. Sustain talk is not effectively softened or redirected toward a more engaged stance.
