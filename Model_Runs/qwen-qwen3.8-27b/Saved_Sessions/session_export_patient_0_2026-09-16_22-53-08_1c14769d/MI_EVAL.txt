# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong, nonjudgmental respect for the client’s readiness and autonomy, including explicitly honoring that he is not ready to quit. It is slightly less than exemplary because warmth and affirmations are somewhat procedural and could be more individualized when he discloses how the infection affected him.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate complex reflections of ambivalence, boredom, social context, and practical concerns. It could be stronger by exploring the deeper emotional weight of shame, fear, defensiveness, and peer pressure more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the client’s concern about infection risk and moves toward safer use and practical coping. The target behavior could be clearer by collaboratively agreeing on a specific goal such as reducing frequency, delaying use, or avoiding high-risk contexts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a client-led, collaborative stance and allows Adrian’s preferences to shape the conversation and plan. Some therapist-generated menus and suggestions still subtly direct the agenda, so co-creation could be more consistent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client’s freedom, responsibility, and choice, which fits well with his resistance to being told he is out of control. Autonomy support could be stronger by avoiding embedded frequency targets such as waiting a week unless the client fully generates and owns that standard.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some useful change talk around safer use, spacing, gaming, and early warning cues. Deeper evocation is needed around the client’s personal values, confidence, and internal reasons for staying healthy or avoiding infection.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a respectful partnership by positioning the client as the authority on his life and following his lead on realistic options. The stance could be more fully partnership-based by co-creating the agenda rather than introducing specific options or intervals first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps the client generate concrete strategies and some reasons for change, especially around avoiding infection and using gaming as an alternative. The change talk is not consistently expanded, consolidated, or linked to deeper motivation, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client and respects his preference not to stop completely. More skillful softening would include double-sided reflections and gentle pivots that explore the tension between social use and health risk, rather than staying within the sustain frame.
