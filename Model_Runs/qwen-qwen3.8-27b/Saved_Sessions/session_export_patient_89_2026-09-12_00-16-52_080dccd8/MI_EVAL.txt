# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, affirming, and nonjudgmental while normalizing Marcus’s self-consciousness and frustration. It falls short of a 5 because some warmth feels premature and the closing reassurance question is left unanswered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate simple reflections of Marcus’s exhaustion and fear of failure. Empathy remains limited because the counselor often moves quickly into problem-solving instead of exploring deeper feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a practical target behavior, moving from a broad weight concern to a manageable walking and dancing plan. Direction is strong but slightly diluted by multiple tasks, ratings, notes, and safety reminders.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus contributes meaningfully by choosing the time, location, name, song, and boundaries. Collaboration is partial because the clinician supplies much of the structure and remains the primary agenda-setter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through low-pressure language, optionality, and permission-based invitations. Autonomy support is not a 5 because the plan includes prescriptive details that could reduce Marcus’s sense of full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk by asking about doable options, priorities, and preferences. Evocation is limited because the counselor often supplies solutions rather than drawing out Marcus’s deeper values, motivations, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Marcus’s ideas to shape the plan. Partnership is uneven because the session remains more clinician-driven and lacks stronger explicit co-creation of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus produces some change talk and the psychiatrist affirms his choices effectively. Change talk is not fully deepened because the counselor rarely amplifies Marcus’s own broader reasons for change or explores confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully softens sustain talk by normalizing fear of failure and offering low-pressure fallbacks. It does not reach a 5 because the clinician does not consistently use double-sided reflections or pivot sustain talk into change talk.
