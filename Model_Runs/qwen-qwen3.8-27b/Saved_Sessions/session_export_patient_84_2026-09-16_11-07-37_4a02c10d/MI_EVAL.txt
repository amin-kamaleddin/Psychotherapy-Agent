# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, including statements such as “I’m not here to preach.” However, the warmth was somewhat formulaic and repetitive, with limited individualized affirmation of Malik’s strengths or worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured accurate surface-level content about Malik’s mother’s insistence, friendship, and annoyance. Deeper emotions such as humiliation, fear of being seen as a bad son, resentment toward monitoring, and frustration that his mother does not listen were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began appropriately with substance use and peers but quickly drifted toward managing the mother conflict. The target behavior—early cannabis use—was not consistently maintained as the primary focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invited input, avoided lecturing, and framed the session as collaborative. The main limitation was that he often generated options and therapist-led suggestions rather than consistently following Malik’s lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Malik’s choice and avoided direct pressure, such as “You choose what we focus on.” However, repeated offers of a quick idea despite Malik’s resistance may have felt subtly pressuring and did not fully separate the psychiatrist’s role from Malik’s own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions were used to invite Malik’s perspective, such as what matters most or what would feel like “letting you be.” The evocation remained superficial because the psychiatrist often moved quickly to offering options rather than deeply eliciting Malik’s values, motivations, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often conveyed a collaborative stance by inviting Malik to choose the agenda. However, repeated positioning of himself as the person offering a “one ultra-quick, no-homework idea” subtly shifted the session toward therapist-driven problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few attempts to elicit client-centered language, but Malik’s limited change talk was not consistently reflected, amplified, or developed. Tools such as DARN change talk, importance/confidence rulers, and future-oriented questions were not effectively used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with Malik’s minimization, but he also did not effectively soften or redirect it. There was limited use of double-sided reflection, discrepancy, or rolling with resistance, leaving sustain talk relatively dominant.
