# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while normalizing the client’s reluctance. The client is never shamed for the DUI, peer pressure, or alcohol use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture the client’s concerns, ambivalence, and practical preferences. However, the empathy remains mostly cognitive and does not deeply explore underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of avoiding a repeat DUI by protecting the license and job. The conversation progresses clearly from the presenting concern to a concrete, practical plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and invites the client to co-create the plan. The client’s ideas strongly shape the final strategy, including parking at Mike’s and keeping cab money available.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasizes choice, respects the client’s rejections of rules and gadgets, and avoids coercion. Support is slightly less than optimal because the psychiatrist still supplies many options and structures the session through suggestion menus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits motivation from within the client rather than imposing reasons to change. The client generates his own practical ideas and increases confidence from 6 to 7.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client clearly influences the session’s direction and the psychiatrist adapts when ideas are rejected. The partnership is strong but not fully client-led because the psychiatrist still provides structured options and confidence scaling.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently reinforces the client’s practical change language and commitment statements. However, the change talk remains somewhat narrow and clinician-structured rather than broadly exploring deeper values and motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s minimization and redirects to practical, client-acceptable options. The response could be stronger with more explicit double-sided reflections or rolling-with-resistance strategies.
