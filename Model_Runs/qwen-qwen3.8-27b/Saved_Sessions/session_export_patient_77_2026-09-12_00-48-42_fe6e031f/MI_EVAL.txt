# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveys respect and nonjudgment while normalizing the client's frustration. The warmth is sometimes functional and task-oriented rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers several accurate reflections but often moves quickly into psychoeducation or action planning. There are missed opportunities to sit with the client's shame, loneliness, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains generally focused on the weight plateau, self-judgment, and small next steps. The rapid introduction of many tasks makes the direction feel somewhat scattered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor adjusts the plan based on the client's concerns and preferences, such as dropping food logging and gum. The collaboration still feels more like choosing from the counselor's menu than fully co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently offers choices, reduces pressure, and emphasizes that slipping up is not failure. A stronger score would require more explicit emphasis on the client being fully in charge and the plan being entirely optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some client-generated content, such as choosing the hunger check and water step. However, much of the counseling content, tools, and interpretations are supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's input shapes the plan, but the counselor often assumes the expert or coach role. The counselor could more explicitly honor the client as the primary expert on her own body, eating, and life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates some change talk, such as willingness to track reps and do the hunger check. The counselor could better reflect, amplify, and deepen these statements to strengthen the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively softens guilt, all-or-nothing thinking, and self-punishment. The focus could be strengthened by more consistently returning to the client's values and reasons for change rather than task options.
