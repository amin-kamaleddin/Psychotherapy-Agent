# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming the client. Strong affirmations are present but somewhat professional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are supportive but often generic and miss the client’s ambivalence, fear, exhaustion, and social motives. The psychiatrist does not fully name the tension between honesty and fear of consequences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session narrows to grades and paperwork instead of keeping alcohol use and its risks central. Academic concerns are not consistently used to explore drinking-related goals, values, or safety.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is useful collaboration in personalizing the email, but the psychiatrist leads with expert-driven scripts and plans. The client does not substantially co-shape the alcohol-related focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers choices and respects preferences without pressure. The plan is still largely generated by the counselor, which limits stronger client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open-ended questions are used, but the work shifts quickly to advice and problem-solving. The client’s motivations for safer sleep, academics, and alcohol use are not systematically evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and involves the client in drafting the email, supporting some partnership. However, the approach becomes advisor-driven before fully exploring the client’s priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to amplify the client’s own reasons for change. Opportunities such as wanting better sleep or avoiding trouble are not developed into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustain talk about fitting in, fear of her mother, and avoiding extra work is not actively softened. The psychiatrist avoids arguing but does not consistently pivot these statements toward the client’s goals.
