# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance throughout. Some affirmations are mildly evaluative or label-like, and there is no explicit statement of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses strong reflective listening to capture Lars’s ambivalence, shame, and tension. A deeper, more sustained sit with his fear of reoffending would strengthen the response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on concrete reentry goals, structure, and reducing alcohol-related risk. A briefer explicit link back to staying out of trouble would make the direction clearer.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructs the plan using Lars’s language, ideas, and choices. Some rapid, suggestive questions make the alliance feel slightly clinician-guided rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids commands and highlights choice, supporting self-determination. More explicit autonomy language, such as “this is your decision,” would strengthen this area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits Lars’s own motivation, strengths, and planned steps. Evoking deeper values, confidence, and readiness more explicitly would enhance motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly asks for Lars’s perspective and follows his own ideas. Not explicitly naming Lars as the expert in his own change limits the score slightly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently evokes change talk and reinforces Lars’s successful language. A more systematic exploration of desire, ability, need, and values would be stronger.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and gently connected to known consequences. More consistent pivots after later expressions of worry would improve this criterion.
