# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses validating language without criticizing the client’s guardedness. Warmth is somewhat procedural and could include more explicit affirmation of the client’s effort and responsibility.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s practical concerns, priorities, and logistical reasoning. Empathy is strong cognitively but could more fully name the client’s underlying anxiety, discomfort, or fear of being scrutinized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on completing court-ordered community service hours before the end of the month. The psychiatrist effectively guides the conversation from vague concerns to a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats planning as a joint task and builds the plan using the client’s own ideas, preferences, and limits. The psychiatrist still controls the sequence fairly tightly and could invite more explicit client evaluation or restructuring of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives and repeatedly invites the client to make choices, framing preferences like the 15-minute limit as autonomous decisions. The client’s freedom and ownership could be stated more explicitly, especially given the court-mandated context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses many evocative questions that draw out the client’s own ideas, plan, and reasoning. The evocation is mainly practical and logistical rather than deeply motivational or values-based.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys collaboration by asking the client’s opinion, affirming the client’s reasoning, and avoiding direct instructions. The session would be stronger with more explicit co-creation of the agenda and emphasis on the client as the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers some statements of need, desire, and intent to finish the service, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen them. More evocative follow-up could better strengthen the client’s own motivation and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and redirects concerns toward practical next steps. Avoidant statements about getting quiet or giving half-answers could be softened more fully using double-sided reflections.
