# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Luis’s frustration with lectures and being overwhelmed. Some opening labeling and repeated plan summaries create mild risk of sounding corrective, but overall acceptance is strong.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Luis’s feelings, constraints, and ambivalence about work, failure, and medical management. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore deeper fear, burden, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on asthma management, a simple plan, inhaler use, and safety. There is little topic drift, and work stress or follow-up concerns are continually tied back to self-management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Luis to choose options and incorporates his preferences into the final plan. Collaboration is strong, though the therapist still supplies much of the medical structure and decision-making framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms choice, ownership, and the client’s right to decline follow-up or extra tasks. Autonomy support is strong, but the directive clinical plan and safety instructions still feel somewhat controlling for a resistant client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some evocative moves by asking what would make sense and what small change Luis might try. However, the session frequently shifts into advice-giving and planning before Luis’s own motivation, values, and confidence can develop further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Luis as the decision-maker and adjusts the plan based on his choices and constraints. Partnership is solid, but the therapist still creates much of the framework rather than fully co-creating direction from the client’s perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Luis offers practical change talk, such as stopping ignoring early tightness and trying a restroom/water break. The psychiatrist affirms some of this but does not consistently expand, amplify, or deepen the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Luis and respects his boundaries when he pushes back. However, sustain talk is often met with practical simplification rather than strategic MI responses that soften or redirect resistance toward change.
