# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client's frustration with his mother. Minor risk of mild evaluation when commenting on the use of "whatever" prevents a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor offers accurate complex reflections that capture the client's social use of cannabis, peer concerns, and irritation with his mother. Empathy stays somewhat surface-level by not fully exploring underlying fear of being seen as "lame" or the emotional cost of parental conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session starts with the target behavior but quickly shifts to managing the mother's concerns rather than exploring the client's ambivalence about cannabis use. The counselor does not use summaries or future-focused questions to refocus the session on change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor actively invites client input and co-creates a short boundary script with the client's preferences. Collaboration is limited because it focuses narrowly on choosing a phrase instead of exploring values, goals, or motivation related to the presenting concern.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor supports choice through options such as "you're the one to decide" and allows the client to select and modify the final script. The later directive coaching about tone and walking off reduces full support for the client's self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor makes initial evocation attempts with open questions and a 0–10 importance ruler. After the client minimizes the behavior, the counselor stops eliciting internal motivation, values, or discrepancy and moves into script-based problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor demonstrates some partnership by offering choices and inviting the client to shape the final response. However, the counselor remains more of a provider of strategies rather than a true partner exploring the client's own direction and expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few initial attempts to evoke change talk, such as asking what would need to happen for the client to consider cutting back. The counselor does not reflect, affirm, or expand on the client's emerging concerns about grades, sports, or family conflict.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Counselor reflects sustain talk early but then reinforces the status quo by helping the client manage his mother's reaction without making changes. The focus on scripts to shut down his mother validates minimizing the concern rather than developing discrepancy or ambivalence.
