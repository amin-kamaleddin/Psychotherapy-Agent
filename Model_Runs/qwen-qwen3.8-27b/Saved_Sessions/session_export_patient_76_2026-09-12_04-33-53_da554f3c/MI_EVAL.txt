# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance with phrases like “I’m glad you reached out” and “That makes sense.” The main limitation is that the acceptance sometimes feels procedural rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several accurate reflections of exhaustion, relief, and the burden of the experience. However, the empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame, loss of control, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays appropriately focused on safety and self-harm, with little topic drift. The main issue is repetitive cycling through safety checks and options without clear MI momentum or a summary-based transition.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration through offering choices and inviting input on roommates and coping steps. The psychiatrist remains the primary driver, though, and does not consistently treat the patient as an equal expert on her own experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly uses permission-seeking, choice language, and respectful pacing. Some safety and crisis language remains directive, which slightly reduces the sense of full client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited effort to evoke the patient’s own motivations for change. Several important change-related statements are missed, and the session relies heavily on closed, option-based questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers options, and respects the patient’s boundaries. The interaction is more guided by safety checks and structured menus than truly partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are few and weak attempts to notice, reflect, or expand the patient’s small change-related statements. The session shifts quickly into safety scripting rather than developing intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk, which is important. However, sustain talk is not effectively softened or reframed through double-sided reflections, values, or agency-focused redirection.
