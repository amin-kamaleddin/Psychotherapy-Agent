# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, normalizing, and nonjudgmental stance. Acceptance is slightly weakened by repeated action-focused prompts that can feel subtly pressuring.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist reflects practical barriers such as routine disruption, exhaustion, and competing demands. However, deeper emotions, ambivalence, and the meaning of the client's past exercise identity are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity. The goal is effectively narrowed to a small, concrete, and observable action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites the client's input and frames the plan as joint. The power balance remains uneven because the psychiatrist frequently provides structured options, cues, and behavioral scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through low-pressure language and multiple choices. It is undermined by repeated directive suggestions, specific timing, and step-by-step instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited and relies more on therapist-provided suggestions than on eliciting the client's own motivations. The client's values, confidence, and meaningful change-related statements are not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites choices and the client influences the final plan. However, the interaction remains more expert-led than fully collaborative due to frequent provision of plans and instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some small commitments are elicited, but the therapist does not effectively expand or amplify the client's own motivations. The client's past success with exercise is not used as a source of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but remains central, with limited double-sided reflection or strategic redirection. The conversation often stays focused on barriers rather than reframing them toward change.
