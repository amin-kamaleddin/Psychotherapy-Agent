# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids challenging the client’s minimization. Warmth is solid but somewhat formulaic and less deeply affirming of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses adequate reflective listening to capture practical concerns and ambivalence. Deeper emotions such as shame, fear of losing his license, social judgment, or loneliness are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior: avoiding another DUI. It moves clearly from identifying the risk to developing a concrete refusal and exit plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor involves the client in problem-solving and lets his preferences shape the final plan. The interaction is collaborative, though sometimes more guided and solution-focused than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor asks permission, invites choices, and respects the client’s decisions without pressure. Ownership could be strengthened with more explicit autonomy-supportive statements such as “you’re in charge of what works for you.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some client-generated ideas, such as the taxi plan and backup text. There is limited exploration of the client’s values, confidence, importance, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor takes a collaborative stance and invites the client’s input throughout. The interaction is strong, though occasionally counselor-led because of frequent lists of options and practical tips.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits practical change talk, including intention, ability, and confidence in the plan. Change talk is not consistently deepened through values, confidence, importance, or consequences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor normalizes the client’s concerns and avoids arguing with sustain talk. Use of more advanced techniques, such as double-sided reflections, would further strengthen ambivalence work.
