# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the client’s perspective. Acceptance is reduced by repeated safety framing and corrective statements that make the tone more risk-managed than consistently affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate reflective listening and understands the client’s ambivalence. However, empathy is surface-level and misses deeper themes of shame, fear of being labeled an addict, and social identity when sober.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to methamphetamine use and its consequences, but the target behavior is not consistently specified. Direction becomes diffuse as the conversation shifts toward practical money, eating, and crash management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and allows the client to choose, such as the protein bar, water, and cash-limit experiment. The collaboration is limited by the clinician’s continued emphasis on practical advice and safety guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently respects the client’s refusals and emphasizes the client’s freedom to decide. Autonomy support is somewhat weakened by repeated safety instructions and continued suggestions after the client expresses discomfort with structure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client concerns about money, work, and crashes, but often interrupts with advice and practical steps. Missed opportunities to deepen the client’s own reasons for change reduce the overall evocative quality.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows the client’s preferences to shape the plan. However, the clinician still occupies an expert-led role and does not consistently position the client as the primary authority on his own life and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that draw out some change talk about money, work, and reducing the cycle of borrowing. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, summarized, or strategically reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directly arguing with the client’s sustain talk and accepts statements like “I’m not an addict.” The handling remains basic, with few advanced reflections or strategic moves to help the client resolve ambivalence.
