# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance. It falls short of full marks because the warmth is somewhat formulaic and does not fully address the alcohol-risk referral.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate but remain surface-level. Emotional undertones and ambivalence are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session focuses on stress and studying rather than the alcohol-risk referral. The clinically relevant target behavior is essentially absent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor involves Sofia in planning details, which supports collaboration. However, the counselor drives much of the agenda and supplies many concrete strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia is repeatedly affirmed as the decision-maker and given low-pressure choices. It falls short of a 5 because the counselor still introduces many suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some open questions are used early, but the session quickly shifts into advice-giving. Sofia’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change are not consistently elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is collaborative in details, but not fully co-constructed. The counselor retains a strong problem-solving role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is minimal and not strategically amplified. Questions focus more on doability than on evoking Sofia’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not effectively softened or redirected. The counselor moves quickly into suggestions rather than using MI techniques to explore resistance.
