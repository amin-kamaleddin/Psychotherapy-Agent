# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and respectful, but warmth often feels generic and formulaic. The client's final vulnerable question about adequacy and strength is left unanswered, weakening unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture some ambivalence and the client's physical freeze response, but often remain surface-level. Deeper feelings of inadequacy, anger, and fear of being seen as helpless are not fully explored before advice is introduced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays mostly focused on reducing co-parenting tension and creating a calmer environment for the son. However, it drifts toward scripts and techniques rather than integrating the client's core concern about being adequate, present, and strong.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of co-construction when the psychiatrist incorporates the client's objections and preferences. Overall, though, the psychiatrist sets much of the agenda and supplies most of the content, making the partnership uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choice and avoids explicit pressure, but repeatedly provides specific scripts and behavioral plans framed as recommended options. Ownership, freedom to decline, and the client's capacity to define what is meaningful are not consistently affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative questions, but much of the session consists of the psychiatrist offering solutions rather than eliciting the client's own motivations. Missed opportunities include exploring what being present, being a team, or being strong means to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often adopts an expert-driven stance by supplying concrete routines, scripts, and techniques. The client appears to be selecting from pre-made options rather than being supported as the expert on her own son and family values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses several clear change statements, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or expands them into stronger change talk. Evocative questions are frequently followed by prescriptive suggestions, limiting development of the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly reinforce the status quo, but the session does not skillfully pivot sustain talk toward change. Concerns about control, inconsistency, and failure are acknowledged without stronger double-sided reflections, reframing, or linkage to deeper values.
