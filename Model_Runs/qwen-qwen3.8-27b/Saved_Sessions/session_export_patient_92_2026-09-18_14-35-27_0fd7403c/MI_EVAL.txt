# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental, warm, and respectful tone throughout, with no labeling or disapproval expressed. Slightly formulaic phrasing (repeated 'I want to honor/hold onto' constructions) and a missed opportunity to deepen emotional attunement to Marek's fear of being 'broken' prevent a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures Marek's emotional landscape, including the 'what the hell?' surprise and the duality of his ambivalence. Some reflections could be deeper, particularly around his fear of being 'broken' and the social identity threat of being 'that guy' different from his friends.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a logical progression from rapport-building through ambivalence exploration to a concrete action plan. The transition from exploring the 'nagging' feeling to the tracking plan happens somewhat quickly, with room for more consolidation of ambivalence before moving to the action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration rather than a clinician-led assessment, with Marek's ideas consistently shaping direction. The final plan is framed as a mutual agreement ('Is that a plan you'd be comfortable with?') rather than a directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Marek's autonomy with explicit validation of his right to maintain the status quo and no pressure, guilt, or obligation language. Textbook demonstration — the framing of tracking as 'looking' rather than 'changing' perfectly respects his agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent and effective use of open questions that elicit Marek's own motivations, with skillful amplification of his change talk. Could go deeper in exploring values, confidence/ability language, and alternative motivators beyond the 'nagging' feeling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marek as the expert on his own life, never prescribes solutions, and explicitly acknowledges his agency ('That's you doing some real thinking in there'). The session is genuinely co-constructed, with Marek's responses shaping the direction throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and reinforces change talk at multiple points, including the shift from 'what am I supposed to do' to 'that's not crazy' and the 'lighter' feeling. Could push further into eliciting desire and ability language with more sustained follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively handles sustain talk through validation, double-sided reflections, and gentle curiosity without any righting reflex or argumentation. A 5 would require more explicit strategic use of double-sided reflections in the face of the strongest sustain talk (e.g., 'if it's 12 beers, I'm not doing anything about it').
