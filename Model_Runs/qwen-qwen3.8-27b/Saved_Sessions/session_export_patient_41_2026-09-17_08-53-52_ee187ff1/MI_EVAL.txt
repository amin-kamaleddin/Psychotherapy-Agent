# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing of Elira’s struggles. It falls short of a 5 by occasionally remaining formulaic and not fully affirming her agency, values, or strengths during intense self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking regarding emotions, body sensations, and ambivalence. Some reflections are interpretive and could benefit from more frequent accuracy checks or deeper exploration of underlying shame and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on anxiety-driven avoidance and follow-through with school and extracurricular responsibilities. The direction would be stronger if it moved from conceptual discussion to a specific target behavior and collaboratively defined next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Builds on Elira’s own ideas and invites joint exploration rather than imposing solutions. Collaboration could deepen with more explicit co-construction, agenda negotiation, and checking whether reflections are useful.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids directive advice and respects Elira’s uncertainty by continuing to explore her perspective. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly reinforcing her freedom to choose and ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently uses open-ended, client-centered questions that draw out Elira’s own motivations, resources, and meanings. Evocation would improve by more deliberately developing change talk into stronger desire, confidence, values, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions Elira as the expert on her experience and invites her input at key moments. Partnership would be stronger with more explicit power-sharing, such as negotiating the agenda or checking the usefulness of reflections.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits meaningful but tentative change talk, such as the “ten minutes, just show up” idea and past brief showing up. These statements are not consistently amplified, reinforced, or connected to values, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing against sustain talk and gently explores Elira’s barriers. It could be more effective with more frequent double-sided reflections, reframing, and strategic pivots that open change-oriented alternatives.
