# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Elena's stress, protectiveness, and efforts while avoiding judgment or pressure. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and does not fully affirm her ambivalence or ongoing smoking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Elena's ambivalence, protective values, stress, and somatic cues. Empathy is strong but can become repetitive and occasionally shifts toward problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing after-work smoking while protecting her son and managing stress. Direction could be stronger by summarizing the target goal and consolidating options into a clearer next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the strongest area, as the psychiatrist follows Elena's leads and co-constructs options around her ideas. The interaction avoids lecturing or imposing a predetermined plan, making it feel genuinely collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives and respects Elena's resistance to quitting for good by exploring realistic options. Autonomy support would be stronger with explicit affirmation that the decision and process belong entirely to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Elena's own values and practical solutions without supplying reasons to quit. Evocation could be stronger by drawing out deeper change talk, confidence, importance, or commitment related to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites Elena's perspective and positions her as the expert on her own life. Partnership could be improved by more explicitly acknowledging her primary expertise and negotiating the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena generates several change-relevant ideas, including limiting cigarettes, taking a reset, walking, and texting her mom. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk, especially confidence, commitment, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, including the need for cigarettes and the realism of relapse. However, the psychiatrist could more actively pivot sustaining statements into language of capacity, values, or change.
