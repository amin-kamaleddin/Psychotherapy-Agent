### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warm, specific, and unconditional positive regard, with affirmations that feel earned rather than formulaic. When Laura expresses self-criticism or guilt, the psychiatrist validates the human experience without reinforcing shame or implying her behavior is wrong.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections pick up on subtle emotional nuances (surprise, guilt, suspicion, relief) and validate complex paradoxes Laura is holding. The reframe 'the real threat isn't not doing it, it's not registering that you did it' deepens self-understanding in a way Laura hadn't articulated herself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear strategic focus on the target behavior (post-work transition) while following Laura's lead, moving logically from values clarification to concrete action plan. Pacing is well-calibrated—patient enough for exploration but with gentle strategic questions that maintain forward momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A fully collaborative session where Laura's contributions drive every aspect of direction, with the psychiatrist providing only reflections and evocative questions. The final plan (ten-minute reset + journal entry) is entirely Laura's creation, co-constructed through the dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Laura's ownership by attributing solutions to her agency ('you came up with it yourself') and structuring every question to invite her own judgment. No directives, obligations, or 'you should' language appears; the overall message is that this is her life, her choices, her standards.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation emerges from Laura herself through open questions like 'What would enough look like for you?' and 'What does that change about how you'd approach those days?' The psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change or uses consequences-based approaches.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session consistently positions Laura as the expert on her own experience, with the psychiatrist never offering external frameworks, information, or advice. Language like 'you're naming the exact moment' and 'you've already figured out the shape of what you need' reinforces that wisdom resides within Laura.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes abundant change talk through open questions that invite future-oriented language and solution-focused thinking. The psychiatrist consistently reinforces and amplifies Laura's own change talk (e.g., 'catch the moment,' 'first five minutes to not be automatic') without imposing an agenda.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with double-sided reflections and validation rather than argument or dismissal, such as 'There's something really mature in holding both of those at once.' When Laura expresses skepticism about the simplicity of the solution, the psychiatrist validates the concern while naturally connecting to her history of complicated systems falling apart.
