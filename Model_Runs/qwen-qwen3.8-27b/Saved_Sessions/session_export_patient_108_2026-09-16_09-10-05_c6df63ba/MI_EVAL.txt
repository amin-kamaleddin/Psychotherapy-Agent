# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while affirming Oliver's ambivalence and courage. It falls just short of a 5 because the positive regard is somewhat procedural and does not always deeply normalize Oliver's moral distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and understands Oliver's competing values and emotional complexity. However, it does not always fully track the shame, fear, or bodily anxiety in the most nuanced moments.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on the target behavior of setting a boundary with a defined timeframe. The structure is purposeful and coherent without becoming rigid or forceful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a collaborative working relationship by inviting Oliver to co-create scripts, timing, and role-play intensity. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist remains the primary source of structured guidance and practical content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, avoids coercion, and supports Oliver's input on wording and role-play intensity. The score is limited by the relatively therapist-directed scripts and instructions that reduce Oliver's sense of full authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client language and agency but relies heavily on instructive content rather than deeper evocation. It lacks consistent use of importance, confidence, or values-based exploration to draw out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance and allows Oliver's preferences to meaningfully shape the session. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still functions as an expert coach for much of the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encourages some change talk by inviting reflection on outcomes, timeframe, and the experience of speaking the script. However, he does not consistently amplify, deepen, or expand Oliver's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Oliver's hesitations respectfully, without arguing or dismissing them, and redirects toward actionable boundary-setting. It is not a 5 because it does not consistently use more nuanced MI strategies to fully transform sustain talk into change talk.
