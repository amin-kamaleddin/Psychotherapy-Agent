# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating without minimizing the client's fears. The warmth is genuine but somewhat measured and professional rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond paraphrasing to capture the emotional and cognitive core of the client's experience with escalating depth. Each reflection accurately distinguishes nuanced fears (e.g., the task itself vs. not knowing what to do with the result).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from overwhelm to identifying a manageable first step. The session could benefit from a clearer closing summary and concrete collaboratively identified next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is structured as a genuine joint exploration where the client generates all content and the psychiatrist's role is to reflect, validate, and guide. Session direction is entirely shaped by the client's responses and priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never directs, prescribes, or pressures, consistently affirming the client's right to choose. The client's own plan and decisions are affirmed as his discoveries rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits the client's own motivations, insights, and plans rather than providing external reasons for change. Could be deeper in explicitly tapping into the client's values or long-term motivations for a more durable motivational foundation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on his own experience, asking for input at every juncture. The psychiatrist explicitly credits the client's contributions, reinforcing the collaborative, non-hierarchical dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively highlights and amplifies the client's change talk, helping him recognize his own shift from overwhelm to actionable steps. Could push further into eliciting confidence and self-efficacy language to strengthen the client's belief in his ability to follow through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles the client's mild sustain talk (fear of not sticking with it, worry about doing it wrong) by acknowledging without reinforcing, then gently redirecting. The final piece of sustain talk—hesitation about calling the doctor—appears under-addressed in the visible transcript.
