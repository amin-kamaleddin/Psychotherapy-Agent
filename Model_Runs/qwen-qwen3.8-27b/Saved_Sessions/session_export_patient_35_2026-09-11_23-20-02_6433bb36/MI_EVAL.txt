# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and validates the client’s intent to protect her child. However, occasional corrective tone and quick shift to “facts” limit the depth of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client’s ambivalence and the practical role smoking plays in stress management. Deeper emotions such as shame, guilt, fear, and feeling cornered are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays broadly focused on smoking, stress, and parenting but lacks a clear target behavior. Agenda-setting or consolidation of the client’s change goal is missing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to choose among options and her preferences shape the plan. However, the psychiatrist frequently supplies the content, making the interaction more therapist-led than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirms the client’s right to choose and avoids pressure to quit. The frequent offering of plans and tips can slightly reduce the client’s sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to elicit the client’s own values, importance, or reasons for change. The focus shifts quickly to coping strategies rather than exploring internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes meaningfully by selecting breathing, stepping outside, and a timer approach. Still, the psychiatrist often generates multiple options and solutions, keeping the dynamic somewhat expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change-related statements, but the psychiatrist does not build on them with follow-up questions or amplification. Change talk is present but not actively strengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and accepts the client’s need for smoking as coping. However, double-sided reflection and rolling with resistance are not used effectively to redirect ambivalence.
