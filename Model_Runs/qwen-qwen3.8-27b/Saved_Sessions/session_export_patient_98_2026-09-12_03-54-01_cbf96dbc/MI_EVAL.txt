

# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Nonjudgmental, accepting language
- [x] Avoids blame or criticism
- [ ] Explicit individualized affirmation of client’s worth, effort, or persistence

**Comments:** Demonstrates unconditional positive regard through language such as “listen without judgment,” “makes sense,” and “understandable.” Acceptance is practical and problem-focused, with limited deeper affirmation of the client’s effort or resilience.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Accurate reflection of client’s emotions
- [x] Validation of client’s frustration and concern
- [ ] Deeper exploration of underlying feelings such as grief, fear, or loss

**Comments:** Provides accurate reflections of discouragement, fatigue, and fear of being perceived as a bother. Empathy is generally validating but often shifts quickly into problem-solving, leaving deeper feelings less fully explored.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Clear focus on target behavior
- [x] Consistent connection to rehab concerns and barriers
- [ ] Broader exploration of client’s goals, values, and motivation

**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on rehab participation, back pain, and communication with the rehab team. Direction remains on target, though the session becomes somewhat narrowed to wording and logistics of a note or script.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Offers choices and adapts to client feedback
- [x] Co-creates a workable solution
- [ ] Fully equal collaboration with client generating language first

**Comments:** Collaborates by offering options and responding to the client’s preferences regarding tone, length, and authenticity. The client’s ideas shape the final note, but the counselor remains the primary source of the specific language.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Offers options rather than directives
- [x] Respects client’s preference for writing the note
- [ ] Stronger emphasis on client’s complete ownership of the message

**Comments:** Supports autonomy by asking what feels helpful, offering alternatives, and respecting the client’s choice to write the note. Repeated provision of scripts and phrasing may subtly lead the client toward a counselor-generated solution.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Asks about concerns and desired change
- [ ] Draws out client’s values, hopes, or reasons for change
- [ ] Consistently elicits internal motivation

**Comments:** Makes some effort to evoke the client’s concerns and what would need to change. However, the session shifts toward practical advice and script creation, with limited exploration of the client’s deeper motivations for engaging in physical activity.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Invites client input
- [x] Offers choices
- [ ] Client-led formulation of language and plan

**Comments:** Supports partnership by inviting input and offering options. However, the counselor frequently provides scripts and notes, which positions the counselor as the main source of language rather than fully eliciting the client’s own words.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Elicits some change talk
- [x] Notes client commitment toward a small action step
- [ ] Amplifies or expands change talk
- [ ] Uses DARN-C evocation consistently

**Comments:** Elicits some change talk, including the client’s willingness to write the note and “give it a try.” Change talk is present but not consistently reflected back, amplified, or expanded using DARN-C.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3

**Criteria Met:**
- [x] Avoids arguing with or dismissing sustain talk
- [x] Normalizes and reflects the client’s concerns
- [ ] Uses double-sided reflections or values-based pivots
- [ ] Skillfully redirects sustain talk toward change

**Comments:** Does not challenge sustain talk and normalizes the client’s fear of complaining or making a scene. The counselor reflects concerns accurately but does not use more advanced MI strategies to soften or redirect sustain talk.
