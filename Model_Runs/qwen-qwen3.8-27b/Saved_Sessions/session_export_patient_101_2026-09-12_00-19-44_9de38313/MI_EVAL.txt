# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect, nonjudgment, and support for the client's chosen goal of cutting back. Warmth is sometimes procedural, and the client's self-critical feelings are not deeply affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's stated concern about drinking and missing birth control. However, responses often remain at the content level and miss stronger emotional themes such as fear, shame, and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability. There is some drift into broader problem-solving, but the target behaviors remain central.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and adjusts the plan based on her feedback, resulting in a co-created plan. The initial therapist-generated list of options reduces the client's lead in setting the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client's choice to cut back, allows her to reject some suggestions, and ends with a client-selected plan. There are still several directive instructions that limit stronger autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some open questions and use of a confidence scale to evoke the client's perspective. The therapist often supplies solutions rather than deeply eliciting the client's own values, reasons, and abilities.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows her choices to shape the final plan. The therapist also takes an expert role early, which can weaken the collaborative, non-directive stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk by asking what feels doable and how confident the client is. The session moves quickly into practical planning before fully deepening or amplifying the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles client barriers without arguing and adjusts suggestions to maintain engagement. Use of more MI-specific strategies, such as double-sided reflections or value-based pivots, would make this stronger.
