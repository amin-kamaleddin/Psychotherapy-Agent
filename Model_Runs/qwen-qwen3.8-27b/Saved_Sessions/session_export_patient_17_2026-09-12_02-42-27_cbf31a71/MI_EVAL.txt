# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance, including normalizing shame and slips. It falls short of 5 because the warmth becomes somewhat procedural later and does not consistently deepen unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections show reasonable perspective-taking early, but empathy becomes less consistent when the session shifts into advice and planning. It often captures surface concerns without fully reflecting deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or feeling judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on smoking cessation, cravings, triggers, and practical next steps. The main limitation is that the focus becomes somewhat task-driven and advice-heavy at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client chooses some options, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies the main content, strategies, and detailed plans. This creates an uneven, clinician-driven balance rather than fully equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is given choices and is not directly pressured to quit, but repeated prescriptive steps and dosing guidance can shift ownership away from the client. Stronger support would elicit more client-generated ideas before introducing clinician-recommended steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions evoke meaningful motivation, especially around the client’s daughter and importance of change. Later, however, the session provides strategies and instructions rather than consistently eliciting the client’s own reasoning and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and explores preferences, but increasingly takes on an expert or coach role with detailed behavioral plans. This reduces the sense of true shared decision-making and client-led problem solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses meaningful change talk, particularly wanting to quit for her daughter and not be that person anymore. The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or expand this change talk before moving into advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Barriers are acknowledged without argument, including normalizing cravings and reframing slips as learning opportunities. However, some direct psychoeducation can sound like counterargument and would be stronger with more client-centered reflection.
