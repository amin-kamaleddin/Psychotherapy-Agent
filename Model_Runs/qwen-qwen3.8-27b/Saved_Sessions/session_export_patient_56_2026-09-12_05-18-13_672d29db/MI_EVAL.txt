# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance throughout. Slightly evaluative praise prevents a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures the client’s values, fatigue, and guilt. Deeper emotional exploration and less formulaic reflections would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the client’s goal of being present with family. Occasional branching into problem-solving slightly reduces tightness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influences the final plan, but the counselor is often the primary source of options. More client-generated ideas would improve collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports client choice and avoids prescribing. Frequent counselor-generated options still feel somewhat steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks open questions and elicits some client goals. Missed opportunities to deepen values, importance, and confidence limit evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s input shapes the session, but the counselor remains a central option generator. More explicit power-sharing would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses useful change talk around presence and manageable steps. The counselor often shifts to suggestions before fully deepening the client’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Resistance is met with validation and flexible, low-demand alternatives. Repeated focus on obstacles keeps some attention on potential failure.
