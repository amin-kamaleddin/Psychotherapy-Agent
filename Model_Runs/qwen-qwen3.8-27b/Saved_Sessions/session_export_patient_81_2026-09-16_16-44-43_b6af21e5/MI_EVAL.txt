# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and accepts the patient’s ambivalence and reluctance to be labeled. The warmth is supportive, but it does not reach fully explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient’s mixed feelings and practical concerns about rent, sleep, work, and belonging. Empathy remains somewhat pragmatic and does not fully explore deeper identity-based fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on concerns tied to methamphetamine use, such as money, sleep, and functioning. The main limitation is that reducing methamphetamine use is not consistently named as the direct behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and patient co-construct a practical plan without imposing solutions. Ideas such as a separate rent account, smaller pocket amount, buddy support, and check-in tone are tested and refined together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is consistently strong, with clear protection of the patient’s freedom to choose and avoid feeling lectured. The psychiatrist avoids directives and frames choices as the patient’s own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out the patient’s own thinking, preferences, and practical solutions. Evocation could be stronger by more deliberately eliciting deeper change talk, confidence, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the patient as the expert on his own life and invites his input throughout. The session is shaped by the patient’s priorities, demonstrating strong power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and highlights the patient’s desire to protect what matters. However, the therapist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or expand the change talk into stronger reasons, desire, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient’s sustain talk is acknowledged without judgment, and some balanced framing is used. The session often stays within the sustain-talk frame rather than strategically reconnecting it to values or reasons for change.
