### Acceptance

**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment about continued drinking or partial progress. Ambivalence and self-awareness are validated respectfully, supporting dignity and safety.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing concerns about control, dignity, and respectful partner cues. Deeper emotional reflection could be added, such as shame, fear of losing face, or relapse anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol-related risk and preventing aggressive escalation. The conversation moves logically toward practical, client-defined safety steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner, framing the session as teamwork and joint problem-solving. The plan emerges from the patient's own ideas rather than clinician direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Luka's autonomy and ownership are strongly supported, including room for continued drinking and choices that avoid feeling controlled. Language emphasizes self-direction, dignity, and return on his own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently draw out Luka's own values, such as control, safety, dignity, and partnership. The psychiatrist avoids imposing external reasons for change and allows the client to articulate goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** There is clear power-sharing, with the psychiatrist treating Luka as the expert on his own life and needs. The counselor invites input throughout and does not prescribe the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, including statements about a clear line, safety net, reset plan, and respectful cue. The client-generated language is built on consistently to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Continued drinking and partial change are accepted without confrontation or framing as failure. The response is supportive but could be more strategic with double-sided reflections or a gentler pivot to strengthen motivation for a firmer plan.
