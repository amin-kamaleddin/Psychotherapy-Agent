# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts Daniel’s preferences without pressure. Acceptance is strong but could be made more explicit through clearer affirmations of Daniel as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently captures Daniel’s ambivalence, values, and emotional meaning with accurate reflections. Perspective-taking is clear, nuanced, and central to the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing alcohol overuse in social settings. The work moves smoothly from values to a concrete, practical plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist builds the plan using Daniel’s own ideas and contributions. The interaction is collaborative, though it remains slightly counselor-led rather than fully co-owned.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly respects Daniel’s choice not to quit completely and supports self-guided planning. There is no pressure, coercion, or directive advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist skillfully draws out Daniel’s own reasons for change and confidence from his smoking cessation success. Client-generated change talk is central to the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel is treated as the expert on his own life, and his concerns drive the session. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicit co-creation and acknowledgment of his role in shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently invites Daniel to articulate his own reasons, plans, and commitments. Change talk increases clearly and becomes the basis for the final plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist responds to sustain talk without arguing or amplifying reasons to drink. The final concern about peer pressure is not fully addressed with a strategic reflection or pivot.
