# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and validates Elena’s effort even when she minimizes risk. It falls short of 5 because acceptance could more clearly separate her worth from her performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture her stress, exhaustion, and fear of failing her son, but some responses remain at the level of balancing rather than deeper shame or fear. Deeper exploration of underlying emotion would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to smoking, child health, and stress, but focus drifts across ears, allergies, sleep, and support. It would benefit from narrowing to one concrete target behavior and measurable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and builds on Elena’s suggestions, but consecutive questions can create subtle steering. More silence and client-led pacing would balance collaboration further.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids pressure, repeatedly affirms Elena’s expertise, and frames choices as low-pressure. It could be stronger by explicitly validating that choosing not to change is still acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and reflections evoke some motivations, but they do not consistently develop values, confidence, or deeper reasons for change. Stronger scaling and values-based questions would increase evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally invites Elena’s perspective and avoids authoritarian advice, but sometimes drives the agenda with successive questions. More explicit co-creation and following vague client leads would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers change ideas, and the psychiatrist responds supportively, but the responses do not consistently amplify or expand that change talk. More strategic reflection and expansion of ability and values would strengthen the criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but often affirms the status quo, which can reinforce the belief that Elena is already doing enough. More double-sided reflections and forward pivots are needed to soften sustain talk.
