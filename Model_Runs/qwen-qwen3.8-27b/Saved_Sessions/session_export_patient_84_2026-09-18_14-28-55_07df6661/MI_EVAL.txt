# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with strong examples of unconditional positive regard (e.g., 'You're not some kind of lost cause'). Acceptance is slightly performative in places, with some phrases feeling rehearsed rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking throughout, including attention to small moments of change talk. The main limitation is staying at the surface level rather than exploring deeper emotional undercurrents like embarrassment, peer tension, or the internal cost of the behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, strategic focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from rapport to functioning to values. The session could benefit from a more explicit collaborative summary and invitation for the client to identify his own next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative frame with inclusive language and real-time responsiveness to the client's lead. The psychiatrist retains slightly more control over the session's flow and could more explicitly invite co-created agenda-setting at key transition points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional and standout aspect of the session. The psychiatrist consistently affirms the client's right to choose, gives explicit outs, backs off when challenged, and never creates urgency or pressure — autonomy support is woven naturally throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently focuses on drawing out the client's own motivations using open questions, reflections, and curiosity rather than providing arguments for change. Could be more strategically targeted to elicit specific DARN (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need) change talk categories to build momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing with the client positioned as the expert on his own experience, and the psychiatrist concedes ground when pushed back on. A 5 would require more actively handing over control of the agenda (e.g., 'Where do you want to go with this?').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does elicit genuine change talk (tiredness, fogginess, desire to make the team) but does not consistently amplify or deepen it once it emerges. More targeted evocative questions and stronger reflective amplification of the client's own words would build greater momentum toward change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful rolling with resistance throughout — the psychiatrist acknowledges the client's perspective without arguing, moralizing, or reinforcing the sustain talk. Could use more explicit double-sided reflections to help the client resolve ambivalence (e.g., 'On one hand it's normal, and on the other it does seem to touch the things you care about').
