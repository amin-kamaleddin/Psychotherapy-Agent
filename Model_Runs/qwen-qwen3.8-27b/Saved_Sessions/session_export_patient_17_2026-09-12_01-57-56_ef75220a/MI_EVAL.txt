# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids shaming related to smoking or past attempts. However, acceptance remains somewhat procedural and does not fully validate Anisa's shame or emotional pain.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Anisa's goals, stress, and confidence level. The empathy is mainly cognitive and plan-focused, with limited emotional depth regarding shame, self-blame, and the need for the change to be about her as well.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on smoking cessation. It effectively narrows the goal to a specific, manageable target: keeping the car smoke-free for one week.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Anisa's input and co-constructs a plan based on her lived experience and practical constraints. Collaboration is strong but occasionally interrupted by therapist-generated information overload.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses consent-based language and invites Anisa to choose doable options, supporting her sense of control. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly emphasizing that the plan is fully hers to modify or discard.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers, open questions, and past-attempt questions effectively draw out Anisa's own motivation, reasons for change, and confidence. Evocation is frequent and effective but sometimes becomes less client-centered when the psychiatrist moves into advice or planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative stance and invites Anisa's input in shaping the plan. Partnership is strong, though the agenda and plan remain somewhat therapist-directed rather than fully negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces Anisa's change talk, increasing her confidence and commitment to a concrete first step. Change talk is used more to build the plan than to deeply explore values or identity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anisa's doubts and past failures are acknowledged without argument and redirected toward smaller, manageable actions. Sustain talk is not consistently reframed or resolved using MI-consistent techniques such as double-sided reflections.
