# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, validating Jordan's overwhelm and fear without minimizing or imposing expectations. Even when Jordan sets a boundary about not returning, the psychiatrist simply honors it without pushing back.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflects are specific and nuanced, tracking subtle shifts in Jordan's language and emotional state with high accuracy. Demonstrates sophisticated perspective-taking by treating Jordan's internal parts as real and present in the room.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a remarkably clear and consistent focus on moving Jordan from stuckness toward a concrete, actionable step with no topic drift. The session ends with a mutually agreed-upon next step (one listing), demonstrating excellent directional control.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the work as joint exploration ('let's treat this like an experiment'), and Jordan's ideas shape the session direction at every turn. The power dynamic is genuinely equal — Jordan sets the pace, depth, and boundary.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Jordan's ownership and choice, never using 'should' or 'need to' language. Explicitly removes pressure ('No pressure to like the answer') and accepts Jordan's terms when he sets a boundary.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Jordan rather than providing external reasons for change. The entire session is structured to draw out Jordan's own goals, motivations, and self-identified next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom about change reside within Jordan, using open questions to let him define his own goals rather than imposing them. The psychiatrist never takes the expert stance of directing or advising.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates a natural escalation of change talk and skillfully highlights Jordan's own progress (e.g., 'a few minutes ago you said it wasn't worth the energy, and now you're asking what the first step would be'). Could have more explicitly evoked confidence or self-efficacy language, such as asking Jordan to rate his confidence on a scale.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk without arguing or dismissing, then skillfully redirects by noticing what the client already did (e.g., 'you said one thing, not a whole program'). Handles the 'tired of starting from the bottom' comment with empathy and a gentle pivot rather than counter-argument.
