# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and honors Seamus’s “not a project” boundary without arguing or lecturing. Minor deduction because the early MI-focused framing may have subtly imposed an agenda before fully centering his immediate needs.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, need for control, and discomfort with being pushed. They could be less repetitive and more attuned to deeper emotions such as feeling “like trash” or frustration with prior advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician begins by linking smoking reduction to the dental visit, but later shifts fully to tooth care after Seamus’s boundary. A clearer, respectful guide would keep the smoking target in view while still honoring his autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally feels collaborative, with invitations for Seamus’s input and adaptation to his stated preferences. However, the clinician still holds a more expert dental-provider role, especially early in the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, consistently emphasizing choice, self-direction, and lack of pressure. The clinician avoids directive advice and repeatedly returns control to Seamus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician draws out Seamus’s own ideas, such as delaying a cigarette, skipping one after lunch, and the coffee alternative. Evocation remains somewhat narrow and repetitive, with limited deeper exploration of values, confidence, or future self-image.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses collaborative language and responds well when Seamus redirects to the tooth. However, earlier repeated MI questions make the session feel more clinician-led than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Seamus offers meaningful change talk, including the coffee idea and small-step options, but the clinician does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen it. Repeated similar questions begin to feel pressuring.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument, dismissal, or reinforcement, and is often reframed toward autonomy and control. It could be stronger with more double-sided reflections that connect resistance to Seamus’s values or health goals.
