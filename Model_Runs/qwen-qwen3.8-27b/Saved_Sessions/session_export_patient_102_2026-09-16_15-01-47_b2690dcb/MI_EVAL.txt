# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses supportive reflections that help the client feel heard. However, the acceptance is not yet fully warm or affirming of the client’s courage and emotional difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately tracks the client’s perspective, needs, and ambivalence. The empathy is mostly cognitive and behavioral, with less exploration of underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior and progressively narrows toward useful self-monitoring. The direction remains purposeful and change-relevant without moralizing or drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the conversation as a joint exploration and consistently follows the client’s own priorities and preferences. The client’s input substantially shapes the content and direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s right to decide, repeatedly framing choices and self-checks as the client’s own. Advice, pressure, or imposed solutions are avoided throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions to draw out the client’s own standards, warning signs, and potential self-monitoring cue. The evoked material is strong but could be further developed into stronger motivation or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life, values, and risk thresholds. Power is genuinely shared, and the session follows the client’s own criteria rather than the clinician’s agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits useful change talk, including early warning signs and a simple self-check. There is room to more actively reinforce, consolidate, and build the client’s confidence in making changes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument or judgment and redirecting toward self-evaluation. The main limitation is relying more on neutral reflection than strategic reframing to resolve ambivalence.
