# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and normalized Marcus’s irritation, but the tone became formulaic and less warm when the session shifted to advice-giving. Acceptance was present but not consistently unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected surface feelings and concerns adequately, but often relied on paraphrase rather than deeper reflection of ambivalence. Missed opportunities included Marcus’s frustration, embarrassment, and exhaustion with planning an escape.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on drinking at parties and peer pressure, with clear direction toward refusal skills. It became more procedural and less connected to Marcus’s deeper values, risks, or personal goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was collaboration in the role-play and some use of Marcus’s preferred low-drama approach, but the psychiatrist often took on an expert or coach role. Problem-solving was more guided by the counselor than co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and asked permission, which supported autonomy at a basic level. However, frequent advice and scripts weakened the emphasis on Marcus’s ownership, especially when he asked to end the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation was limited because the session moved quickly into giving strategies, scripts, and tips. The psychiatrist did not consistently draw out Marcus’s own motivations, concerns, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some partnership through invitations to choose a focus and the role-play sequence, but the interaction remained largely expert-driven. The psychiatrist often supplied options and explanations rather than sharing control of the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offered useful change talk, such as “I’ll pass for now” and “I just say no and walk away,” but the psychiatrist did not consistently affirm or expand it. The session relied too much on external scripts rather than strengthening Marcus’s own reasons and ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with or invalidating Marcus’s sustain talk and often acknowledged his concerns. However, responses frequently returned to additional tips or scripts rather than using reflections to redirect ambivalence toward change.
