# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, nonjudgment, and encouragement without challenging the client’s identity or ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but somewhat task-oriented and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately summarizes the client’s worries, goals, and practical steps. However, empathy remains mostly factual rather than emotionally deep, with missed opportunities to reflect shame, fear, pride, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of reducing recidivism and building a stable, law-abiding life. Practical details such as the depot call, probation check, and post-call debrief remain tied to the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the client’s preferences shaping the plan, script, and text message. The psychiatrist still supplies much of the concrete content and structure, so collaboration is strong but not fully client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supports choice, respects the client’s decisions, and reinforces ownership of the plan. Some suggestions are directive and coach-like, preventing a higher score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-generated change is evoked early, especially around stability, work, and rebuilding trust. However, the session shifts toward advice, scripting, and problem-solving rather than consistently exploring values, confidence, or internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on his own life, and his preferences materially shape the action plan. The psychiatrist still supplies substantial scripts and structure, so the partnership is strong but not fully client-led throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes practical change talk and affirms the client’s concrete plan. The evocation remains mostly behavioral rather than motivational, with less emphasis on importance, confidence, values, or self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and barriers are not reinforced; nervousness is normalized and redirected toward practical planning. The psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or explicitly pivot barrier-focused language toward change.
