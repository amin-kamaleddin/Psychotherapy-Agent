# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepts Eric’s goal of cutting back rather than quitting, including his slip talk. Praise and support are present but sometimes formulaic and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate summaries and reflective listening, but the empathy remains more factual and task-oriented than emotionally nuanced. Deeper cues about irritation with his wife, autonomy, pride, and health-related defensiveness are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking reduction and a small, realistic step, especially the truck cigarette. The direction is somewhat mechanical and repetitive, emphasizing practical planning more than motivational movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Eric contributes to the plan, and his preferences are incorporated into the final steps. However, the psychiatrist often leads with structured options and menus, so the collaboration is real but not fully client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, respects Eric’s refusal of a phone reminder, and avoids harsh pressure or moralizing. It would strengthen autonomy support to more explicitly emphasize Eric’s freedom to take time or choose not to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and requests for Eric’s preferences draw out some client-generated problem-solving and small steps. The evocation remains procedural and does not deeply explore Eric’s values, fears, identity, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone, especially when Eric selects the truck target, gum flavor, stretch, and backup plan. The psychiatrist still maintains a relatively expert-driven stance by offering many structured options rather than fully positioning Eric as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses appropriate eliciting questions and Eric offers some change talk about trying the truck change and reducing tension with his wife. The change talk is not consistently amplified, reflected, or developed with DARN-C strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area; the psychiatrist avoids arguing but mostly tolerates Eric’s sustain talk rather than strategically softening it. Statements like “I feel fine” and “my work keeps me strong” could have been explored with double-sided reflections or connected to future health and identity.
