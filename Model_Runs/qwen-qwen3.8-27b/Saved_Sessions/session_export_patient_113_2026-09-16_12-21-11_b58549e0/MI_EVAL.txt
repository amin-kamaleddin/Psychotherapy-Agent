# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or lecturing while acknowledging Rowan's juggling of work and health. It falls short of a 5 because the stance is somewhat task-oriented and a few reflections feel mildly inferential, such as the bag feeling safer than owning the problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately summarizes barriers, the chosen plan, and confidence level. However, empathy remains mostly cognitive and behavioral, with limited exploration of underlying emotions such as frustration, self-doubt, overwhelm, or defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused on the target behavior of charging and using the device. The counselor maintains clear momentum toward a specific, low-effort plan with defined boundaries and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed, with Rowan selecting the side pocket, timeframe, and follow-up preference. Collaboration is somewhat uneven because the psychiatrist frequently generates the menu of options before eliciting Rowan's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, respects Rowan's preferences, and avoids pressuring a larger commitment. Some language remains slightly directive, such as "we'll try that" and "good choice," which can imply the plan is already the counselor's recommendation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and question about raising confidence from 5 to 7 are useful evocative moves. However, the counselor often provides practical strategies instead of consistently eliciting Rowan's values, reasons for change, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rowan is involved in decisions about options, timeframe, and follow-up. The partnership is weakened by frequent expert-driven suggestions and tweaks that position the counselor more as problem-solver than equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, such as "I'll try" and "zip means check," and the counselor affirms these statements. The counselor does not consistently draw out Rowan's own desires, needs, or values related to using the device.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Rowan's resistance and acknowledges statements like "don't get your hopes up" and "I'm out" if problems continue. Softening is inconsistent because some sustain talk is accepted or redirected into problem-solving rather than explored with double-sided reflections.
