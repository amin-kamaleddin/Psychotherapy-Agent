# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally respects Laura’s choices, but repeatedly offers added options after she requests simplicity. This slightly undercuts full acceptance of her minimal approach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate summaries of Laura’s values and preferences, but reflections often remain surface-level. The counselor misses opportunities to explore doubt, self-judgment, and ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of creating a doable health-related step. It becomes overly logistical at times, reducing attention to deeper motivational work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura co-creates the reminder wording, showing real collaboration. However, the overall plan remains therapist-led, with the counselor introducing many options and structures.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices and accepts Laura’s decisions without pushback. The main limitation is continued suggestion-making after she asks to keep things simple.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial questions successfully draw out Laura’s values and a concrete idea. Later, evocation weakens as the conversation shifts to logistical details rather than deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Laura’s preferences and includes her input in planning. Partnership is uneven because the therapist maintains a strong agenda and introduces multiple plan components.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura expresses clear change talk early, such as wanting to feel steady and not just busy. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or reinforce this change talk after the initial planning stage.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Laura offers important ambivalent statements, but the psychiatrist often responds by adding more plan pieces rather than exploring the tension. Sustain talk is acknowledged only partially and left unresolved.
