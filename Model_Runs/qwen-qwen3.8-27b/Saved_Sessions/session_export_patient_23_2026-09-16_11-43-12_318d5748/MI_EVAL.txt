# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment, treating Claudia's ambivalence as reasonable rather than resistant. Warmth is somewhat formulaic with recurring phrases like 'clear, realistic plan' and 'solid, doable plan,' lacking deeper normalization of struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening capturing the ritual-versus-overdrinking tension and emotional undertones like guilt and stress. Empathy stays at practical understanding rather than deeper emotional amplification, notably not engaging with Claudia's lapse/shame disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on reducing evening wine consumption and preventing bottle-finish nights throughout the entire session. Moves coherently from ambivalence to a concrete Friday plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Builds directly on Claudia's responses and asks what she would add, showing a collaborative stance. Mild expert-driven flavor from leading with several concrete strategies (pre-pour, swap drink, walk/bath options) before Claudia had explored her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently frames work as Claudia's choice and supports a kind-response-to-lapse stance. Offering 'options people try' lists and the confidence-scale nudging could subtly steer toward therapist-generated solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses the confidence scale and plan-focused questions effectively, but lacks deeper evocation of Claudia's values, desires, and meaning around change. Much of the questioning is functional (which strategy, when, what cue) rather than drawing out authentic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Claudia to set the agenda and repeatedly asks which option feels most doable, letting her shape the plan. Could benefit from more permission-seeking language and additional space for unprompted client-generated ideas rather than supplying several options first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits useful change talk around confidence, concrete action, and coping phrases like 'good job, I stopped.' Evasion is more plan-focused than motivation-focused, not consistently deepening into desire/need/values or reflecting back amplified change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by acknowledging the wish to keep the ritual and redirecting toward a compatible reduction goal without arguing. Does not consistently use double-sided reflections or explicitly reframe stigma-laden statements like 'confessing a crime.'
