# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honored the client's limits. It fell short of a 5 because warmth was more reflective than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured the client's ambivalence, practicality, and need for ease. The empathy remained somewhat surface-level and did not fully explore deeper feelings such as shame or relational hurt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session had some coherence around one practical coping strategy, but it drifted away from the stated target of reducing drug use. There was little direct work on substance use, its consequences, or related discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited the client's input and co-constructed a plan using the client's own ideas. Collaboration was strong, though the psychiatrist's suggestions occasionally made the process feel slightly therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported choice, ownership, and the client's right to maintain his routines. There was no pressure, persuasion, or hidden agenda, which was excellent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evoked practical, client-generated solutions and some self-efficacy. Evocation was limited in depth, especially regarding intrinsic motivation, values, and the broader goal of reducing drug use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed respect for the client as the expert on his own life and invited meaningful input. It was not a 5 because the counselor still guided and structured several parts of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered some practical change talk about trying a pause and managing jaw tension. The change talk was not deeply developed in terms of importance, confidence, values, or future motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with the client's resistance and used some balanced reflections. However, he did not consistently help the client weigh sustain talk against change or gently pivot the conversation.
