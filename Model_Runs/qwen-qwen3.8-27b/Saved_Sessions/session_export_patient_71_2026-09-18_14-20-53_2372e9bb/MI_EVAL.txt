# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warm, nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations of Laura's honesty and self-awareness. Could reach a 5 with more explicit, individualized expressions of unconditional positive regard that stand apart from the reflective process.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture not just content but the emotional texture of Laura's experience, going one step beyond what she said without overreaching. Specific, timely, and accurate throughout (e.g., 'scoreboard,' 'smallness as a shield,' 'two versions of Laura').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical momentum from the general gap to the specific Tuesday yoga commitment, with each question building on the last. Could benefit from a slightly more explicit collaborative summary or sense of arrival at the end of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Insights are co-constructed — the 'scoreboard' metaphor and 'two versions of yourself' emerge from Laura, shaped by the psychiatrist's evocative questions. The session feels like two people thinking together rather than one guiding the other.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No coercion, advice-giving, or implicit pressure at any point. Laura retains full control over direction and decisions; the psychiatrist explores ambivalence without pushing toward resolution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation emerges from Laura herself — the psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change or offers information. Questions are precisely designed to draw out internal wisdom (e.g., 'what would that free up?' eliciting the scoreboard insight).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Laura is consistently positioned as the expert in her own life; every intervention is a question or reflection inviting her own insights. The 'scoreboard' insight and 'put your shoes on' language emerge entirely from her, not the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation producing numerous change talk statements, including a specific commitment (Tuesday yoga) and powerful self-generated language. Could be slightly more systematic in consolidating change talk or adding a confidence/commitment question near the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated without challenge or dismissal (e.g., 'the smallness is almost a shield'). Could occasionally use more explicitly double-sided reflections to honor both sides of ambivalence simultaneously.
