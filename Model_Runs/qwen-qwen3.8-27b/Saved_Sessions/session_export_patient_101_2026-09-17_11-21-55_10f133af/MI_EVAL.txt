# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, with warm, individualized validation tailored to each client statement. No labeling, judgment, or conditional approval detected at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening with emotional depth, precisely tracking shifts from fear to self-doubt to pragmatism. Captures nuanced internal experiences (e.g., 'should vs. could') that the client was struggling to articulate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on both target behaviors with a logical session arc from exploration to concrete planning. Minor gaps: the transition between alcohol and contraceptive topics was abrupt, and no collaborative summary or confirmed next steps closed the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative dialogue where the client's ideas consistently shape the direction and the final plan is entirely self-generated. The psychiatrist serves as a skilled facilitator, building on client contributions rather than steering toward a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's ownership and freedom to choose, never using 'should' or 'need to' language. All suggestions are framed as questions back to the client, and her self-generated plan is explicitly validated as her own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation—every piece of motivation originates from the client, drawn out through open-ended questions without a single externally supplied reason for change. The final insight ('could' vs. 'should') emerges entirely from the client with minimal scaffolding.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on her own life ('You know your own brain better than I do'), never prescribes solutions, and allows silence and struggle. The power dynamic is clearly egalitarian with the client driving content, pace, and conclusions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions consistently elicit rich change talk (ability, hope, reasons for change), which is amplified and deepened rather than moved past. The session closes with the client articulating a clear, self-generated plan with a support system.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well with two-sided reflections that validate without arguing, but the binary thinking ('drink with them or be boring') could have been explored more concretely for coexistence. The self-doubt ('I don't fully trust myself') was acknowledged but not deeply softened before moving to planning.
