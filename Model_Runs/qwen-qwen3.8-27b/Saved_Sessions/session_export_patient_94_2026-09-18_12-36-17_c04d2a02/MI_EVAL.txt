# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, even when the client was openly hostile. The psychiatrist never minimized concerns, used labeling language, or responded defensively to resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated highly accurate and sophisticated empathic understanding, capturing underlying emotional exhaustion and subtle shifts in position. Reflections consistently went beyond surface content to validate ambivalence without pathologizing it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear and logical focus on the target behavior throughout, progressing naturally from resistance to exploration. However, the session lacked a concrete collaborative closing that identified a specific next step or small commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong inclusive language and joint exploration throughout, with the client's contributions clearly shaping the session's direction. Could be elevated with more explicit co-creation language (e.g., 'How do you want to think about this?') rather than slightly leading reflections.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly reinforced the client's decision-making authority with multiple direct statements ('There's no agenda,' 'You're the expert,' 'You don't have to have an answer yet'). Never pressured, persuaded, or implied the client 'should' change her mind.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out the client's own motivations and insights rather than providing external reasons for change, leading her to her own realization about possibly 'only reading the scary parts.' Could have evoked more ability language and confidence-building to reach a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance and explicitly positioned the client as the authority ('You're the expert on what feels right in your body'). Could be deepened with more explicit power-sharing language about how to use the session time together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivated change talk through strategic evocation, leading the client from blanket rejection to acknowledging her own questions and uncertainties. Did not explicitly ask about confidence or readiness, which would have built toward commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk skillfully by validating the client's concerns without arguing, correcting, or engaging in the righting reflex. Could have more strategically used double-sided reflections to actively pivot sustain talk toward change.
