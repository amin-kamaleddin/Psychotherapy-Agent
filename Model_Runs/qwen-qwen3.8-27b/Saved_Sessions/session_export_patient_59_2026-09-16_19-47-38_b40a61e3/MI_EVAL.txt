# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard, validating Jamal's effort to attend despite ambivalence. It falls slightly short of a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat procedural and focused on compliance rather than deeply normalizing the emotional difficulty of being in juvenile justice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, capturing the practical logic of Jamal's coping statements. However, the reflections sometimes remain surface-level and do not consistently reach the deeper emotional layer beneath the behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear practical focus on staying out of trouble and managing the school week, but it drifts away from the specific target behavior of marijuana possession or use. A stronger session would more consistently link Jamal's school and peer behavior to the specific concern that brought him to MI.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and treats the interview as a joint process, responding to Jamal's contributions rather than imposing his own plan. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still controls the direction of the conversation and does not fully invite Jamal to set the agenda or define success.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Jamal's perception of choice and avoids directive or pressuring language, emphasizing his pace and choices. It could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Jamal's freedom to choose no change and separating his identity from the behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, nonleading questions to elicit ideas from Jamal, allowing him to generate his own coping strategies. However, the evocation remains practical and behavior-focused rather than motivational, and does not systematically elicit values, reasons for change, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative partnership stance and explicitly positions Jamal as an authority on his own life, inviting input through questions. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still sets the frame and agenda, and does not fully negotiate the broader focus of the session with Jamal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable efforts to evoke client-generated language by asking what would make school or avoiding trouble feel more under Jamal's control. However, the statements generated are mostly practical compliance or coping statements rather than clear change talk about marijuana use, possession, or values-based change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Jamal and does not reinforce his minimization or defensive statements, which helps reduce escalation. However, the softening is limited because he does not use strategic MI techniques such as double-sided reflections to hold both sustain talk and potential change talk in view.
