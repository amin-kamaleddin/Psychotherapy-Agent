# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, using affirmations like 'That makes sense' and 'That sounds fair.' Some affirmations were somewhat generic or tied to task completion rather than deeply individualized positive regard for Luka as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured Luka's values and practical difficulties accurately. The reflections tended toward cognitive/plan-focused rather than emotionally deep, with missed opportunities to reflect the emotional intensity behind statements about aggression and worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently focused on reducing alcohol use and preventing aggressive outbursts throughout the session. Minimal drift, with clear movement from values and past success to a concrete plan for Friday as a high-risk day.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and invited input; the client meaningfully contributed key elements of the plan such as the cold-air cue and Friday as risk period. Collaboration was partly limited by the psychiatrist's structured agenda and pre-supplied specific options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly asked about willingness, doability, and preference, accepting Luka's choice to use his own words rather than a stiff script. A few mildly directive moments, including suggesting texting Dave 'now on standby' and the specific leave-immediately safety instruction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative questioning elicited much of Luka's own content, including the cold-air cue, walking backup, and Friday risk plan. The psychiatrist occasionally supplied specific interventions (five breaths, a text script, a list of supports) before fully allowing the client to generate ideas independently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative stance with Luka's ideas clearly shaping the session direction, including his rejections and alternatives. The psychiatrist introduced several specific plans and scripts before fully exploring whether Luka would have generated them independently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited desire, ability, reasons, and need for change through scaling questions and concrete planning prompts. Did not consistently amplify or strategically reflect the client's change talk immediately after it occurred, which would have pushed the domain closer to 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and used normalizing statements like 'It's okay that breath counting feels impossible.' However, the psychiatrist often moved directly back into practical planning rather than using double-sided reflections or discrepancy-based questions to actively pivot ambivalence into change talk.
