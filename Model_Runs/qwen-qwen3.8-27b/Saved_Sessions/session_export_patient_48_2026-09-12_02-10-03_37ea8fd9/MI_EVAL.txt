# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental, normalizing stance throughout. Some statements were slightly corrective, limiting a perfect acceptance rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client's ambivalence and exhaustion. Deeper emotional themes such as shame, defeat, and identity concerns were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on increasing physical activity with strong strategic momentum. There was no topic drift, and the conversation moved effectively toward a concrete action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently framed the work as joint and incorporated client preferences into the plan. However, the therapist still led much of the structure through options and scaling prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasized choice, low pressure, and low-risk options. Some therapist-driven structure and specific plan wording reduced the degree of fully client-owned autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempted to elicit motivation through importance, confidence, and past-success questions. Evocation was limited because the client's own reasons and change talk were not consistently deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and allowed the client's choices to shape the plan. The agenda and structure remained partly therapist-led, preventing a perfect partnership rating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produced some change talk and confidence increased. The therapist did not consistently develop the client's own desire, ability, need, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responded to sustain talk without argument, normalizing the client's barriers. The sustain talk still remained prominent and was addressed more through reassurance than strategic MI techniques.
