# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard. Some language is slightly generic and does not fully individualize the client’s avoidance or health anxiety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses frequent and generally accurate reflections of fear, judgment, and protective concern for the client’s child. Deeper medical and body-related fears are not fully explored, and some reflections are slightly leading.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a small, manageable step toward medical follow-up. Momentum is slightly reduced by broader emotional discussion and unclear alternate response segments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses “we” language and co-creates practical supports with the client. However, the therapist still introduces much of the plan, so collaboration is strong but not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently preserves choice, ownership, and exit options. When the client rejects a check-in call, the therapist does not press and continues to support the client’s chosen plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist elicits the client’s own motivations and values rather than providing external reasons for change. The client’s final statements reflect internally generated motivation, such as “I’m not broken” and “I’ll try.”

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas substantially shape the plan, including the fridge note and the “for her” statement. The therapist still introduces key interventions, so the session is strong but not fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk, moving the client from uncertainty to concrete statements of desire, ability, and commitment. Value-based change talk, especially around “for her,” is strongly reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fear and failure-related sustain talk are generally acknowledged without argument and reframed. The session remains heavily focused on fear, and double-sided reflections are not used consistently after each sustain talk statement.
