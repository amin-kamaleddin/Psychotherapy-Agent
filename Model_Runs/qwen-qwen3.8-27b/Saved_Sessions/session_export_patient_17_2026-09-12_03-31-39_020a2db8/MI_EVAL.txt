# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Anisa’s ambivalence and past quit attempts. Acceptance could be stronger by adding deeper, individualized affirmations of her worth independent of the outcome.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflective listening to capture Anisa’s ambivalence, practical stress, and emotional tone. The main limitation is not fully following the emotional core of her shame and “fraud” feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and moves from ambivalence to a concrete behavior plan. It would benefit from a final client-centered summary and more polished if-then language that keeps ownership with Anisa.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well, and Anisa’s choices clearly shape the final plan. Collaboration could be stronger by first evoking Anisa’s own ideas before offering structured lists of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, avoids pressure, and frames the plan as Anisa’s to try. Autonomy support could be enhanced by more explicitly reinforcing her ownership during moments of doubt or self-sabotage worry.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and rulers help Anisa express motivation and confidence, but the therapist does not deeply elicit her values, identity, or self-efficacy. More follow-up questions are needed to expand her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and lets Anisa choose key parts of the plan. Partnership would improve by reducing therapist-generated options and more consistently inviting Anisa to generate her own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is present and gently affirmed, but it is not consistently deepened or amplified. The psychiatrist could ask more questions that expand Anisa’s reasons, hopes, and sense of capability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Anisa’s barriers and uses normalization to keep sustain talk from dominating. More double-sided reflections would help honor the meaning of sustain talk while gently opening space for change.
