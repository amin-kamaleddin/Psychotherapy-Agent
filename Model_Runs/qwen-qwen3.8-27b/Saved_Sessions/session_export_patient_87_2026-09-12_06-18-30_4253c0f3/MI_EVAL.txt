# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with frequent affirmations, though the acceptance sometimes feels formulaic. A few statements occur before the client confirms completion, which slightly weakens unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several content reflections that track the client's concerns and barriers. Deeper emotional nuances, such as dread, discouragement, or ambivalence, are not consistently reflected, and one reflection appears premature.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on a specific, actionable target behavior and ends with a clear follow-up plan. The main limitation is some repetitive scaling and planning language that slightly interrupts flow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and invites the client to choose among options, but the interaction remains fairly therapist-driven. The agenda is co-created in part, yet the psychiatrist often leads with suggestions and repeated prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear moments where the client's choice is honored, including deciding what would count as a win and how to structure the plan. However, repeated emphasis on reaching a higher confidence score can feel pressuring rather than fully autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's own ideas, coping strategies, and practical plan components. The main gap is a less consistent exploration of deeper personal values or motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client's insights and allows his contributions to shape the plan. Still, the interaction feels more expert-driven than fully shared, with the psychiatrist supplying many options and directing the problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits useful change talk and helps the client articulate concrete steps. The limitation is that this change talk is not consistently amplified or explored before moving on.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or blaming the client for obstacles like beer, the couch, and fatigue. However, the session remains heavily focused on barriers and uses limited double-sided reflections to actively soften sustain talk.
