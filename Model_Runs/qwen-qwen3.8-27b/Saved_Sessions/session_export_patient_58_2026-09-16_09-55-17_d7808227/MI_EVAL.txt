# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Jamal’s frustration with mornings and reminders. The stance is consistently supportive, though warmth remains somewhat procedural rather than fully affirming Jamal’s worth beyond the problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate reflective listening of the practical and relational content, including mornings feeling rushed and wanting his mom’s trust. However, reflections mostly stay at the content level and do not consistently access deeper feelings such as autonomy, disrespect, guilt, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session starts with a clear focus on school lateness and mornings, then narrows to a practical plan involving packing and a short message. However, the focus often shifts toward stopping nagging or obtaining his mom’s agreement rather than consistently linking actions back to getting to school on time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses Jamal’s preferred short text and hallway idea, showing a collaborative tone. Yet much of the problem-solving is driven by counselor-generated scripts, options, and contingencies, making the collaboration feel uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some autonomy-supportive language such as “you’re in charge” and permission-seeking phrases. However, repeated suggestions, scripts, and focus on external conditions—especially his mom’s agreement—undermine the message that the change is under Jamal’s control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers, scaling questions, and open prompts help Jamal generate practical ideas such as packing the night before and sending a short message. The evocation remains largely surface-level and does not consistently draw out intrinsic motivation, values, or identity-based reasons for being on time.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys some partnership by offering choices and adapting to Jamal’s input, including shortening the text. However, the counselor frequently drives the conversation with multiple questions and plans, which makes the interaction feel more directive than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through readiness and confidence rulers, and Jamal offers several change-oriented statements. However, the counselor does not consistently deepen or amplify change talk around Jamal’s own values, benefits, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk centered on stopping the nagging, proving compliance, and avoiding his mom’s checking is present throughout the session. The psychiatrist reflects some of this language but does not actively redirect it toward autonomy, trust, or self-respect, leaving the plan externally focused.
