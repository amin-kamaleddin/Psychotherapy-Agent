# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoided shaming or confrontation. Warmth became somewhat procedural during harm-reduction logistics, with less emphasis on deeper positive regard for Adrian as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured surface content but often remained factual rather than feeling-based. Deeper emotions such as fear, defensiveness, shame, and frustration about being managed were not consistently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few initial attempts to open change, but after Adrian rejected broader change, the psychiatrist shifted mainly into harm-reduction education. Change talk was not consistently elicited, reflected, or amplified.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session began with collaboration and adapted to Adrian's preferences for privacy and autonomy. However, it became more expert-driven, and the psychiatrist did not fully reset into client-led problem-solving after Adrian said he did not want to be managed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and respect for Adrian's choices supported autonomy throughout much of the session. Repeated instructions, links, and suggestions after he said he could find the information himself may have subtly undermined his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation was limited because most of the session was informational rather than exploratory. The psychiatrist missed opportunities to draw out Adrian's own motivations, values, confidence, and reasons for safer use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The early session showed a collaborative stance by asking for permission and inviting Adrian to set priorities. As the session progressed, the psychiatrist increasingly took an advisory role, reducing the equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made a few initial attempts to explore change, but largely accepted Adrian's refusal to stop and moved into practical harm-reduction information. There were few DARN/CRAFT-style questions to elicit or strengthen his own reasons for safer use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with or invalidating Adrian's sustain talk. However, the session often reinforced the status quo by framing the goal as continuing use with lower risk, rather than exploring the costs of continuing without safer habits.
