# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows warm, nonjudgmental regard by validating the client’s ambivalence and fears. There is no shaming or moralizing about smoking or social belonging.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s conflict between health and connection, as well as her fear of seeming odd. The empathy could be stronger if it explored deeper emotional meanings such as rejection, identity, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on reducing smoking in social situations, especially after work with friends. It moves efficiently from ambivalence to a concrete, behavior-specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates the plan with the client by incorporating the client’s preferred low-key strategies. The interaction feels like a collaborative, client-owned plan rather than therapist-directed instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers choices and asks for the client’s preference rather than giving directives. Language is respectful and noncoercive, leaving decisions with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits client-led motivation, confidence, and a concrete plan. However, some responses rely on providing options or scripts rather than asking deeper questions about personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and treats the client as the decision-maker. The partnership is somewhat weakened when the therapist frequently supplies options and scripts, making the session feel more expert-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits practical change talk, such as using gum, chewing before sitting, and keeping coffee in hand. It would be stronger if it more fully explored the personal values and meanings behind change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk by acknowledging the client’s fear of judgment without arguing or minimizing it. It would be enhanced with more double-sided reflections that fully honor both connection and health.
