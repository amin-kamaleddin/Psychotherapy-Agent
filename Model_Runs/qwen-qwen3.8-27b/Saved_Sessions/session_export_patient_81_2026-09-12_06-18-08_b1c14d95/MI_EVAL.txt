# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, often validating Lukas's experience and effort. The acceptance is strong but slightly less deep because some affirmations feel mildly evaluative and the session moves quickly from validation into planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate, complex reflective listening and captures key tensions around identity, belonging, and stress. However, the empathy does not always go deep enough to fully explore the emotional pain or shame underneath the sustain talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is well focused and moves toward concrete, realistic steps with a clear follow-up plan. The main weakness is that the focus shifts heavily toward financial behavior without consistently linking it back to the core substance-use target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor acts as an equal partner and co-constructs the plan with Lukas. The final plan reflects the client's lived experience, preferences, and limits rather than an imposed treatment agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Lukas's sense of choice, ownership, and ambivalence. The plan is clearly client-driven, with the counselor offering options rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended and choice-based questions that elicit some client-driven language and useful self-statements. However, the evocation does not consistently draw out deeper values, identity, or intrinsic motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client clearly directs the session, choosing priorities, goals, plan details, and follow-up preferences. The psychiatrist follows this direction effectively while keeping the conversation connected to the broader concern.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several meaningful change statements are evoked, including small steps, self-compassion, and confidence-building language. The psychiatrist does not, however, consistently and deeply cultivate change talk around values, future goals, or personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through acknowledgment, normalization, and offering manageable alternatives instead of challenging the client. The psychiatrist does not always consistently transform sustain talk into deeper change language, especially around belonging and identity.
