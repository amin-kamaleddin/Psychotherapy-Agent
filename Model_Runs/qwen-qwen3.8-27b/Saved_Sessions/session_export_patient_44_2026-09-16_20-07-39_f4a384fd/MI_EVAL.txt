# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental regard throughout, especially around gym comparisons, dietary slips, and self-doubt. The client is validated without criticism or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are strong and often capture Erik's ambivalence, but some reframing moves ahead of his fully expressed feelings. More space for raw frustration or fear would strengthen perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on exercise consistency, gym discouragement, and snacking patterns while narrowing broad goals into specific behaviors. The conversation moves clearly from motivation to concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor builds on Erik's ideas rather than prescribing them, though the structured questioning can feel slightly guided. More client-led momentum before affirming or reframing would make collaboration more seamless.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported through questions that return decision-making to Erik and avoid directive advice. The client repeatedly frames actions as choices rather than obligations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully elicits Erik's own values, intentions, and reasons for change instead of supplying them. Erik generates clear change talk about back pain, sleep, attention, and control.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Erik is treated as the expert on his own life, with the counselor facilitating rather than directing. The session is driven by Erik's ideas and problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently evokes and amplifies change talk through affirming, open-ended questions. Erik's language becomes increasingly focused on control, small wins, and choosing rather than habit.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged, normalized, and not argued against, but the session spends meaningful time in the problem side of ambivalence. More decisive pivots from fear and self-doubt to change would strengthen this area.
