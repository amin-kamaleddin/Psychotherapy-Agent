# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a nonjudgmental, respectful, and affirming stance, normalizing the client’s reactions and avoiding shame. Acceptance is strong, though it remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence, surprise, and need to balance decompression with concern about the alcohol result. Empathy is clear and accurate, but it could more deeply explore underlying emotions, identity, and the protective role of the ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains appropriate focus on alcohol use, sleep, recovery, and running performance, and moves toward a concrete low-pressure experiment. Direction is clear but would benefit from stronger consolidation of the target and a clearer closing summary or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven stance and builds on the client’s ideas about sleep, running feel, and tracking concerns. Collaboration is strong, though the counselor still introduces some conceptual frames rather than fully co-creating every part of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly emphasizes the client’s choice, ownership, and right to decide whether the experiment is meaningful. Autonomy support is strong, with only mild directive moments when suggesting a cut-back or specific plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out the client’s own ideas about a small experiment, evidence, and what to notice. Evocation is practical but does not consistently bring out deeper values, goals, self-image, or stronger change motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently invites the client to define what matters, what is useful, and what feels manageable, reinforcing shared decision-making. Partnership is strong, though some agenda-setting and framing remain counselor-guided.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some change talk by inviting the client to define a small experiment and evidence that would matter. Change talk remains tentative and practical because it is not consistently amplified, summarized, or connected to deeper values and goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s reasons for maintaining the status quo. However, sustain talk is not strongly redirected, and the session does not consistently help resolve ambivalence toward change.
