# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated consistent nonjudgmental respect and affirmed Marcus's perspective. Acceptance was slightly task-centered and could have been stronger with more person-level validation beyond his diabetes performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided accurate, emotionally attuned reflections of Marcus's ambivalence and fear of failure. The counselor clearly identified and tracked the underlying emotional meaning behind his concerns about monitoring.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a specific, behaviorally concrete goal of morning blood sugar monitoring. Questions progressively narrowed toward a manageable plan involving timing, target, cue, and sticky note.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used shared language and treated Marcus as a co-author of his own rule. The final plan was built collaboratively from Marcus's own ideas about after-breakfast testing, under-150 target, coffee cue, and checkmark note.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor largely avoided prescribing a fixed rule and invited Marcus to define what would feel fair and workable. A slightly stronger score would come from more explicitly reinforcing his freedom to modify, delay, or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked Marcus's own reasoning, target choice, and coping rule rather than providing advice. The main limitation was not continuing to evoke deeper confidence after Marcus asked whether the under-150 target was a good place to start.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Marcus as the expert on his own life, limits, and daily capacity. The client's choices clearly shaped the session and the resulting self-management plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited strong ability and commitment language from Marcus about trying once a day and making one check feel possible. The score is lowered slightly because the change talk could have been amplified more directly to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled Marcus's fear of failing and his sustain talk without arguing or minimizing. The counselor reframed the concern empathically and helped convert it into a self-protective rule that reduced emotional charge.
