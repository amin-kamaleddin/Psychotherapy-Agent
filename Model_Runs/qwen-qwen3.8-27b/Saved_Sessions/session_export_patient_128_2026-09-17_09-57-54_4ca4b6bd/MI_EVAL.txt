# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s motives without shame or blame. However, the warmth remains somewhat clinical, with less explicit individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures the tension between connection and safety. A few responses are relatively generic and miss opportunities to deepen underlying feelings such as shame or FOMO.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on drinking in social situations and progressively narrows toward concrete planning. The target behavior is not always explicitly named or consolidated, which limits it from reaching a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats the session as a joint exploration and builds on the client’s own ideas, such as setting limits and using a text check-in. The counselor could show more equal partnership by explicitly negotiating which strategy to focus on.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports client autonomy by inviting Tyler’s own perspective and avoiding pressure or controlling language. Decision-making is clearly kept with the client, including the need for a check-in that does not feel like being managed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly evokes motivation and solutions from the client through open, client-centered questions. Tyler generates most of the change talk and problem-solving ideas, such as caring about his degree and planning a limit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor clearly positions Tyler as the expert on his own social world and invites his input on practical planning. The session could improve by more explicitly co-creating the agenda and confirming the client’s equal authority over next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively creates opportunities for change talk and reinforces some of the client’s values and intentions. The client’s change talk could be amplified, summarized, or consolidated more strategically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through validation rather than argument, especially around wanting to have fun and not feeling boring. The counselor could more consistently use strategic redirection to link social values with safer alternatives.
