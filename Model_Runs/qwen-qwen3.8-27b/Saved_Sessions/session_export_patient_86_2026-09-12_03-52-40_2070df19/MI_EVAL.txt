# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids arguing with the patient. However, the stance remains fairly procedural and does not fully convey deeper acceptance or unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects some of the patient’s stated preferences, such as wanting simplicity and no lectures. The reflections stay surface-level and do not fully address the underlying fatigue, guardedness, or cynicism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session shifts quickly from smoking cessation to practical dental hygiene and does not maintain focus on the original target behavior. There is little effort to connect the dental choices back to smoking or broader health goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and allows the patient to make low-effort decisions, which supports collaboration. However, the advisory list of tips gives the session a mildly expert-led tone rather than a fully co-constructed exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by avoiding pressure, respecting the patient’s boundaries, and leaving choices open. This is a clear strength, especially given the patient’s resistance to lectures and persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly uses closed or choice-based questions and provides practical advice rather than drawing out the patient’s own motivations. There is limited use of questions to elicit values, importance, or confidence related to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates basic partnership by offering options and letting the patient guide the conversation. The collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist still leads with practical recommendations before fully exploring the patient’s perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does little to amplify or deepen the patient’s own change-related statements. The session misses opportunities to explore meaning, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not confront or argue with the patient’s reluctance, which helps reduce resistance. However, the responses mostly bypass sustain talk rather than using double-sided reflections to validate reluctance while keeping change possible.
