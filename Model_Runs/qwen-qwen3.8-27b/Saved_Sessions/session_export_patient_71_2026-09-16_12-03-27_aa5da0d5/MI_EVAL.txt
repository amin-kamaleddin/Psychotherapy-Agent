# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and support without labeling ambivalence as failure. Acceptance could be slightly warmer and more explicitly affirming when Laura expresses self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening and accurately captures Laura’s experience and need for low pressure. Reflections could go deeper into the emotional content behind worry, guilt, or fear of self-failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session narrows effectively from broad values to a specific, doable behavior. Some time spent on sticky-note wording and check-in logistics could have been tied more explicitly back to the behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is highly collaborative, with the plan co-created from Laura’s preferences, phrases, and lived constraints. The psychiatrist builds on the client’s contributions rather than imposing a clinician-generated plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent and consistent, with repeated invitations for Laura to choose the behavior, cue, wording, start day, and reporting preference. The psychiatrist honors boundaries and avoids pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some values and preferences, but the session is more plan-completion oriented than deeply evocative of intrinsic motivation. More eliciting questions would draw out and expand Laura’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly treats Laura as the expert on her own life, values, and constraints. Laura’s choices substantially shape the session, with no imposition of expert authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura expresses some values and change language, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this change talk. More follow-up questions could strengthen her own reasons and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully normalizes misses, reduces pressure, and reframes inaction as data rather than failure. This effectively prevents argument and maintains motivation.
