# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with several affirmations, including validation of honesty and progress. However, the warmth can feel formulaic, and more individualized positive regard for Timur as a person would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of frustration and unfairness, but empathy is inconsistent and often shifts into advice. Deeper reflection of shame, identity conflict, relationship pressure, and the meaning of the porch would improve this criterion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally remains connected to reducing recidivism and staying drug-free, but it often drifts into practical advice and repeated safety disclaimers. A clearer MI focus on ambivalence, values, and one collaboratively chosen next step would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and eventually follows Timur’s porch preference, showing some collaboration. However, the counselor frequently acts as the expert problem-solver rather than a true collaborative partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are helpful autonomy-supportive phrases such as “if you want” and “check in only if you want.” The score is limited by repeated directive suggestions and tasks that create pressure, especially when Timur explicitly pushes back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has limited evocation of Timur’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change. The counselor could have explored what the porch means, what “the man she fell in love with” means, and how his strength in sticking to decisions could support change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership by asking what Timur wants to work on and accepting his preference for the porch. Nonetheless, the counselor drives much of the agenda with therapist-generated plans, CVs, scripts, and coping tasks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms progress but does not consistently elicit or amplify Timur’s own desire, need, ability, or commitment to change. Statements such as “I’m the type who sticks to what he decides” and “I’ll try to make it twenty-three” could have been reflected and explored more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and offers low-pressure or non-decision options. However, the session could more consistently use double-sided reflections and rolling with resistance, and some task suggestions risk increasing Timur’s sense of pressure.
