# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and respects Marcus’s stated choices, but the acceptance can feel mechanical. Repeated safety reminders after the client says he is leaving may weaken unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at a surface level but remain shallow and repetitive. Deeper themes such as autonomy, peer acceptance, and frustration with being treated as helpless are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use at parties, but it becomes less strategically effective after the client wants to end. The repeated safety reminders make the interaction feel more like a lecture than guided MI.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some early collaboration through offering choices and inviting input. However, the psychiatrist shifts into a more directive informational mode and does not fully co-create the plan with Marcus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice early by offering options and respecting the client’s decision to end. Repeated unsolicited safety advice after the client’s frustration undermines autonomy and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session includes basic opening questions but lacks deeper evocative work. There is little effort to elicit values, confidence, future-self concerns, or personal reasons for safer behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks for input and respects the client’s choice to leave, showing some partnership. However, the interaction becomes more clinician-led rather than genuinely collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effective cultivation of change talk. Useful client statements about safety and self-management are not reflected, affirmed, or expanded into motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not directly argue with or reinforce sustain talk, but the response remains superficial. The session shifts back to repeated safety reminders instead of strategically rolling with resistance.
