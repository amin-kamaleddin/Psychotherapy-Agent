# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoids judgment and offers validating, nonjudgmental responses throughout the session. Acceptance occasionally feels formulaic with repeated phrases like "sounds frustrating" and "I hear you."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A strong empathic reflection captures the overload/control theme, but empathy is inconsistent as the counselor quickly shifts to advice and options. Key emotional material around fear and ambivalence is underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, sustained focus on the target behavior with a concrete one-minute action plan. Direction is effective but more practical and plan-oriented than MI-motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some inclusive language and patient input shaped the final plan, but the counselor consistently supplies the main options, safety instructions, and step-by-step plan. Rowan largely responds from a position of refusal or minimal agreement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are repeatedly offered and the patient's dislike of being told what to do is validated. Minor directive language like "Good choice" and "Quick plan" slightly undermines the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area, with the counselor quickly shifting to options, practical solutions, and safety information. The patient's own motivations, values, and inner reasons for change are rarely drawn out or explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership-consistent behaviors are present with choice-offering and "I won't push you beyond this," but the interaction remains largely counselor-led. The counselor repeatedly generates options and the concrete plan rather than inviting Rowan to lead more explicitly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rowan offers some change talk but receives only brief affirmations with no active eliciting or expansion. There is minimal DARC-style questioning to help Rowan generate and elaborate his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue with or moralize over the sustain talk and reflects some emotional content. However, the conversation remains anchored in reasons not to charge without using double-sided reflections to redirect toward change.
