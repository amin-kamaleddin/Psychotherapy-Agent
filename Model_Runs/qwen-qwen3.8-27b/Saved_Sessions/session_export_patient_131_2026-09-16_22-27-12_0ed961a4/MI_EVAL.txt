# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect, nonjudgment, and dignity. The client's identity, independence, and role as a provider are honored without criticism or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers accurate, complex reflections that capture the client's ambivalence and practical concerns. Empathy could be strengthened by reflecting deeper emotional experiences such as shame, fear, grief, or the weight of repeated relapse.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use, improving safety, supporting the Stopping Violence program, and reconnecting with the children. There is little drift away from the client's stated goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the client actively co-constructing the plan rather than receiving a clinician-imposed one. The psychiatrist treats the process as joint exploration and problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's sense of choice, ownership, and self-directed decision-making. The language emphasizes internal control and personal responsibility rather than external pressure or compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively draws out the client's own values, reasons for change, and protective resources. Evocation could be further strengthened by adding scaling questions, deeper future-imagining, or explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert on his own life and builds on the client's contributions throughout the session. The interaction demonstrates genuine power sharing and avoids an expert-driven posture.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes and amplifies the client's own change talk, especially around the children, safety, and staying steady. The skill could be further strengthened by more explicitly evoking confidence, commitment, and future-oriented change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician handles sustain talk respectfully by acknowledging the client's desire for independence without arguing or reinforcing resistance. The approach could be even more skillful with more explicit double-sided reflection and gentle pivot language.
