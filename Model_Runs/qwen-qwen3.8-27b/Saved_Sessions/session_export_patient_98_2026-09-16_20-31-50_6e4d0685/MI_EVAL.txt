# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, patience, and nonjudgmental regard without pressure. There are no labels, arguments, or coercive undertones.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures the client's tension, ambivalence, and need for safety. It falls short of a 5 because deeper emotional fatigue or resignation is not explicitly reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on increasing physical activity within safe limits. Each question productively narrows the focus toward a specific, achievable action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and builds directly on the client's ideas. The plan is co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, emphasizing the client's freedom, pace, and private choice. There is no pressure to push through pain or meet external expectations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client's own practical plan and small steps. Evocation could be stronger by drawing out deeper values, confidence, or importance language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert on his own body, limits, and preferences. The psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven stance and supports client-led decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk related to agency and a small concrete plan. It remains more procedural than motivational because deeper commitment, values, or confidence language is not explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without arguing or reinforcing reasons to stay stuck. Some sustain talk is acknowledged indirectly but not fully transformed into autonomy, coping, or self-compassion.
