# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed respect, warmth, and unconditional positive regard without judgment. Affirmations and normalizing statements supported the patient even when she remained ambivalent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate and attuned to the patient's stress, protective motives, and need for a pause. Empathy could be deeper, especially when the patient expressed self-criticism about the cigarette still being harmful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on reducing secondhand smoke exposure and building a concrete step. The clinician guided the conversation toward a specific behavioral experiment without drift or forcing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with the patient's ideas shaping the plan. The driveway rule and drive-home target emerged from the patient's own life context and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly emphasized choice, permission, and the patient's control over the plan. Some language was slightly directive, such as imperative phrasing and repeated suggestions, which slightly limited autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited the patient's own motives, barriers, and preferred small step. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, and internal reasons for change would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used collaborative language and followed the patient's lead in developing the plan. Introducing multiple specific options could be framed more explicitly as patient-driven choices to fully maximize partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encouraged the patient to generate and articulate a realistic change plan, reinforcing the driveway rule and smoke-out-of-the-house commitment. Some change talk could have been amplified more by exploring the patient's values and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician handled sustain talk without arguing, minimizing, or confronting the patient's concerns. The final self-critical belief that the small step was 'still cigarettes' could have been reflected more precisely to reduce ambivalence.
