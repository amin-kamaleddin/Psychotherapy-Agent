# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids escalating Marcus's defensiveness. The acceptance is strong but can be more individually attuned to Marcus's vulnerability and identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate, helpful reflections of Marcus's perspective, including fitting in and fear of being caught. The empathy could deepen into more affectively nuanced reflections of peer identity, authority anxiety, and autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to alcohol use and safety, but the focus narrows too much toward logistics. A stronger strategic MI direction would connect safety choices to broader motivation, values, and risk reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and builds on Marcus's choices. A stronger collaboration score would involve more explicit co-creation of the plan rather than providing a checklist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Marcus's choice, avoids coercion, and respects his refusal of follow-up. This is a major strength that helps preserve the therapeutic alliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some effective attempts to draw out Marcus's perspective, especially early in the session. The evocation becomes less consistent once the conversation shifts to practical advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance and affirms that Marcus is the decision-maker. The partnership could be stronger by reducing expert-led advice and increasing shared problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus offers some change-relevant or harm-reduction language, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it. The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit or expand Marcus's reasons, confidence, values, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Marcus's sustain talk. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflection to hold ambivalence and pivot toward change or harm reduction.
