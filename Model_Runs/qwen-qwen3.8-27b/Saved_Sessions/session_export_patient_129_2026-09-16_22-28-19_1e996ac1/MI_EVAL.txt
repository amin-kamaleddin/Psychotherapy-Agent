# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance without blame or moralizing. Acceptance is consistent but somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate and complex reflections that capture ambivalence and burden. Empathy could go deeper into emotional meaning and the cost of the conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps reasonable focus on reducing home tension and improving communication with the mother. The target is not fully chosen by Tyler and is not consistently linked to substance use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses joint language and builds on Tyler's statements to create a collaborative process. Collaboration remains therapist-led and does not fully share agenda ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently affirms Tyler's right to make choices and avoids coercive language. Autonomy support is strong but remains narrow, focusing mainly on communication rather than broader choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses open questions to draw out Tyler's ideas and perspective. Evocation remains surface-level and does not fully explore values, risks, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a collaborative stance and invites Tyler's input. The partnership is not fully realized because the therapist still sets much of the agenda and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk about reducing conflict and being home more. Change talk is not consistently amplified, deepened, or connected to substance use or personal values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and generally rolls with resistance rather than confronting Tyler. Sustain talk about substance use and peers is not consistently redirected toward change.
