# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing past quit attempts and concerns. However, warmth was sometimes procedural and the final fear of being the "odd one out" was not explicitly addressed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Linda’s ambivalence, burden, and social concerns. Deeper emotional meanings, especially around identity and fear of rejection, were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and the high-risk social context with friends. The progression from general worries to ambivalence, triggers, and a specific plan was logical and well-structured.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-created through Linda’s choices among offered options, showing collaborative input. However, the psychiatrist often generated the main strategies first, making the collaboration somewhat asymmetrical.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and choice-based language supported Linda’s sense of control and avoided pressure. The amount of clinician-generated scripts and specific plan steps may have limited fully client-led autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several open questions and rulers elicited Linda’s motivations, barriers, and a small initial plan. Later, the approach became more solution-focused and didactic rather than consistently evoking internal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative agenda-setting and invited Linda’s preferences throughout the session. However, the session was more clinician-structured than fully driven by Linda’s own expertise and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited through questions about desired changes and use of importance/confidence rulers. These moments were not consistently amplified, deepened, or strategically reflected to strengthen self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing or confrontation and was gently redirected toward possible changes. Some responses were more neutral than skillfully turning sustain talk back toward autonomy or goals.
