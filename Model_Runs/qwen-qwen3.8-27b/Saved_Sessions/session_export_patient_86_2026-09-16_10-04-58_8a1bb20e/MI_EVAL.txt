# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Marco’s experience without pressure or labels. Some affirmations feel tied to task completion rather than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Marco’s ambivalence, need for privacy, and resistance to being lectured. The empathy remains somewhat content-focused and does not fully explore the deeper emotions beneath his ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the morning coffee-and-cigarette routine rather than broad smoking cessation. Introducing several small behaviors slightly dilutes the central focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and allows Marco to reject options, which supports a collaborative tone. However, most plan details and suggestions are clinician-generated rather than client-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is frequently supported through phrases such as “if you want,” “your call,” and respect for privacy. Some prescriptive behavioral steps still feel directive before Marco’s own plan is fully elicited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence question and initial ambivalence questions help evoke some client motivation. The clinician frequently supplies options and instructions, which limits deeper discovery of Marco’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco is able to influence the plan by choosing the water glass and keeping the experiment private. The psychiatrist remains the primary source of ideas, which weakens true collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco offers some change talk, including choosing a small step and giving a confidence score of six. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or develop this change talk using a fuller DARN-C approach.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Marco’s reluctance and uses “not a battle,” “private,” and “no promise” framing effectively. More double-sided reflections could further soften sustain talk while moving toward change.
