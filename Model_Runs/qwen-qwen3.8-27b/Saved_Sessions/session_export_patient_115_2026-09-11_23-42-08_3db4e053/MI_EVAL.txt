# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using validating phrases, but the warmth is often formulaic and more problem-solving than deeply affirming. This supports a moderate score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses are limited, with only a brief reflection of frustration and little exploration of the patient's ambivalence, fatigue, or practical burden. Most of the session is medical and directive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management, nighttime symptoms, inhaler use, smoking, alcohol, and dust exposure. It is clear and relevant, though more biomedical than MI-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through offering options and helping create a doctor note, but the psychiatrist dominates the medical content and plan. The patient influences some topics, but the session remains largely counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is moderately supported by presenting the nicotine patch and breathing exercise as optional, but the counselor frequently gives directive recommendations and does not consistently emphasize the patient's ownership. This is respectful but not strongly autonomy-affirming.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is a major weakness, with only one initial open question before shifting into advice and psychoeducation. The psychiatrist does not systematically draw out the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial partnership by asking for focus and offering choices, but frequently assumes an expert role and drives the medical plan. The power balance is uneven, with the client contributing preferences but not substantially guiding the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are weak attempts to encourage change talk, and the patient offers some change-oriented statements, but they are not skillfully developed. The counselor quickly moves into medical recommendations instead of amplifying the patient's reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not confront the patient's sustain talk and offers harm-reduction options, but also normalizes cutting down and does not consistently pivot toward change. Sustain talk is softened to some degree, but not fully redirected.
