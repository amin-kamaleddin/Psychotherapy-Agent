# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with frequent normalizing statements. Acceptance is strong but not maximal because some language is task-focused or mildly normative.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture key themes of ambivalence, belonging, and fear of standing out. Empathy is reduced by repeated mischaracterizations, such as confusing school/team context with work, and by missing some deeper social-emotional nuances.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use in team-related social situations and moves toward a concrete boundary step. Focus occasionally broadens into general problem-solving, but it does not significantly drift off target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist invites client input and uses the client's preferences to shape the role-play and next step. Collaboration is strong but not perfect because the therapist still frequently supplies options and scripted responses.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist consistently supports client choice and avoids pressure or commands. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client's freedom to decide and by reducing reliance on therapist-provided scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions help the client articulate motivation and confidence. Evocation is limited by the therapist's early shift toward practical script-building and limited exploration of deeper values or identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes meaningfully, especially in choosing the target step and preferred wording. Partnership is somewhat limited by the therapist's tendency to supply practical options rather than fully positioning the client as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist evokes some client change talk and affirms the client's boundary language. Cultivation is not consistently strong because several useful phrases are therapist-generated rather than fully evoked from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client's sustain talk and redirects toward small steps. Softening is not optimal because the therapist sometimes invites the status quo and relies more on neutral acknowledgment than on double-sided reflections.
