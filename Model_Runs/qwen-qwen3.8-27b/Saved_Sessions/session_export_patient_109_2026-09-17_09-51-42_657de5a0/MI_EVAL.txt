# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout the session. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicit unconditional positive regard and normalization of the patient’s hesitation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Empathic listening is strong, with accurate reflections of conflict, values, and self-criticism. Some reflections are interpretive and could explore deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or self-beliefs more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of returning to the gym. Direction is strategically progressive, moving from ambivalence to a small, concrete, personally meaningful action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively by building on the patient’s own ideas, values, and proposed plan. The intervention is shaped as a joint exploration rather than a one-way delivery of advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the patient’s sense of choice and avoids pressure, persuasion, or guilt. Autonomy could be further strengthened by adding explicit statements that the patient is not required to decide or act immediately.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes internal motivation by exploring past success, personal values, and the meaning of taking a small step. Evocation could be maximized by using more scaling, confidence, and commitment questions with fewer counselor-generated suggestions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is strongly collaborative, with the patient’s contributions shaping the plan and direction. Partnership could be improved by using more open-ended, client-led exploration and fewer counselor-introduced hypotheticals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reflects and amplifies the patient’s change talk, including desire, need, ability, and taking steps. Change talk could be further developed by more explicitly eliciting confidence, commitment, and capability language in the patient’s own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without argument, using validation, reframing, and double-sided reflections. The psychiatrist could further neutralize sustain talk by more consistently separating the person from the problem and exploring the source of the self-critical “lazy” voice.
