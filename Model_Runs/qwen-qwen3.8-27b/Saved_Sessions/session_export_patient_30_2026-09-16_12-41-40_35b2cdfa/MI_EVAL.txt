# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates David’s priorities around work, family, and muscle strength. Some language is formulaic, and “You sounded frozen” could feel slightly pathologizing if not further normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects David’s concerns about weakness, medication, and being present for family, but often stays at a surface level. There is limited exploration of the deeper emotional meaning of his ambivalence, such as loss of control or tension between trusting his doctor and protecting independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on cholesterol, medication decisions, and related lifestyle changes, moving toward concrete actions like walking, eating changes, and lab follow-up. A minor weakness is adding extra planning layers after David had already chosen a simple, low-key approach.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and helps co-construct a plan based on David’s constraints and preferences. The collaboration is slightly uneven because the psychiatrist frequently offers specific tools, scripts, and structures before David asks for them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice by asking permission, offering options, and respecting David’s decisions to avoid medication, tracking, and scripted responses. Autonomy support could be stronger by offering less structure unless David requests it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks several open-ended questions and helps David articulate some doable steps. However, evocation is limited because the session often moves quickly into clinical options and practical suggestions rather than deeply drawing out David’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats David as the expert on his own life, asks for input, and lets his choices shape the session. The main limitation is that the psychiatrist still introduces several clinical structures and suggestions that can subtly position them as the problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as cutting back on heavier foods, walking, and saying “I’m not saying no forever.” There is not enough amplification, summarization, or strategic deepening of David’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or dismiss David’s concerns about medication and side effects. However, repeated focus on medication options and risk information keeps the conversation on the concern rather than using double-sided reflections to widen the frame toward David’s values.
