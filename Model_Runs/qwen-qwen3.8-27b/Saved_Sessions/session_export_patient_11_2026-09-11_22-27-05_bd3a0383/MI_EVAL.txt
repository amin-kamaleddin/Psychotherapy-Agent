# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, validation, and nonjudgment throughout the session. The tone is supportive, though it can feel somewhat procedural and formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds reasonably to Amira's concerns, but reflections often remain surface-level. The responses quickly shift to practical options without exploring the emotional meaning of her anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong, coherent focus on a specific movement cue and follow-through. The session stays tightly linked to the target behavior without meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira's preferences are genuinely incorporated into the plan, especially regarding cues, timing, and follow-up. The collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still supplies many ideas and structures in a somewhat prescriptive way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, permission, and noncoercion. Setbacks are treated as neutral information rather than rule violations, which strongly supports Amira's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits task-level preferences more than deeper motivations, values, or confidence. There is limited use of evocative questions or reflections to help Amira articulate reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids being fully authoritarian, but the stance remains fairly expert-driven. There are repeated lists of options, which reduces the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks practical selection questions but does not consistently draw out deeper client-initiated change talk. Amira offers some change-oriented language, but the psychiatrist does not amplify or expand it effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles ambivalence and fear of failure effectively by reframing misses as information and reducing judgment. The softening is clear and MI-consistent, though it remains relatively brief.
