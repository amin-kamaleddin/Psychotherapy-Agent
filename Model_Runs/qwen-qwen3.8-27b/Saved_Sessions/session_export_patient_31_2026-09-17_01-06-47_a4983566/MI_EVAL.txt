# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and accepts David's ambivalence without judgment or correction. Validation is specific to his concerns, such as wanting relief without an all-or-nothing commitment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately tracks David's practical perspective and need for autonomy. However, it stays largely concrete and behavioral, with less attention to deeper emotions such as fear of failure, shame, or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing daytime smoking-related irritability and work stress. It progressively narrows toward a concrete, testable plan involving the patch and end-of-shift self-check.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses shared problem-solving language and invites David to define the experiment rather than prescribing a plan. David's criteria directly shape the proposed trial, including duration, use, and success markers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David's freedom to choose, modify, or stop the patch experiment is repeatedly emphasized. The psychiatrist avoids adding monitoring, pressure, or a quit-or-fail framework.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively draw out David's own criteria for success and internal standards for feeling more in control. However, evocation remains primarily practical and behavioral, with limited exploration of deeper values, confidence, or emotional drivers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions David as the expert on his own life, work, and habits. The session is guided by his stated limits and preferences rather than an imposed cessation plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk by asking David to define improvement, notice functional signals, and imagine a better end-of-shift experience. The approach could be deepened by further exploring confidence, values, and personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced, and the psychiatrist accepts David's boundary of not quitting. However, some statements could have been reframed more strategically to honor his reasons for smoking while also highlighting his desire for better stress management.
