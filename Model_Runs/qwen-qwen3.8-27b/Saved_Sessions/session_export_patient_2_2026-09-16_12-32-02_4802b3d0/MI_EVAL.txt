# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates Aisha’s competing responsibilities and preference for a small change. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicit affirmation of Aisha’s values and self-worth independent of performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and tracks Aisha’s practical constraints, fatigue, and fear of added “homework.” A stronger 5 would more fully explore and reflect underlying emotions such as guilt, self-doubt, or pressure as a parent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on reducing evening smoking and moves steadily toward a concrete, realistic plan for tonight. There is no drift into unrelated topics, and the pacing remains goal-directed while respecting Aisha’s pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is strongly co-constructed, with Aisha choosing the target level, tracking method, coping response, and follow-up approach. The psychiatrist adapts quickly and summarizes the plan in a way that reflects Aisha’s priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission before suggesting options, normalizes missing goals, and avoids shaming or pressure. Autonomy support could be made more explicit by more directly emphasizing that Aisha can modify or stop the plan at any time.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Aisha’s preferences and willingness through open and preference-based questions. Evocation is limited because the session is more plan-focused than exploring Aisha’s internal motivations, values, confidence, or readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally positions Aisha as the expert and invites her to choose among options. Partnership could be strengthened by giving Aisha more explicit lead time in defining what a good next step or success would look like before options are offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports some change talk with affirmations and helps Aisha articulate a manageable plan. More deliberate evocation of importance, desire, ability, and commitment language would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk such as not being ready to quit or wanting to avoid another expectation is handled respectfully and used to build a low-pressure plan. Greater use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots would make this even more skillful.
