# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with supportive language and no blaming. However, the acceptance feels somewhat procedural and is diluted by the repeated provision of options and scripts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate but mostly stays at the content level of the patient's concerns. Deeper emotional themes, such as identity, fear of being seen as dramatic, and the weight of the doctor's warning, are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation and moves toward a concrete, small step. The main limitation is that the direction is more behavioral and problem-solving than MI-style.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration, as the psychiatrist asks for input and incorporates the patient's preferred language. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, making the partnership uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses optional language and avoids overt pressure, but the repeated generation of options and scripts can feel subtly prescriptive. The patient's ownership is not explicitly and consistently reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with questions mostly focused on practical choices and triggers rather than the patient's own motivations, values, or confidence. Key evocative techniques, such as importance rulers or exploring benefits, are not used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses the patient's own words, showing some partnership. However, the interaction frequently becomes clinician-driven, with long lists of options and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some attempts to move the patient toward action, and the patient offers some change talk. However, the psychiatrist does not skillfully expand this change talk through questions about importance, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce the patient's reasons against change, but also does not effectively soften them. The response largely stays within the problem domain by offering management strategies rather than exploring ambivalence or values.
