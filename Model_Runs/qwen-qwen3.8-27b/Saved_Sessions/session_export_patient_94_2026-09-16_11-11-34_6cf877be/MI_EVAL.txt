# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Maria's preferences. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and repetitive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician captures surface concerns about busyness and side effects. Reflections remain factual and do not consistently explore the underlying fear or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and inhaler use. Momentum is weak because the conversation repeatedly returns to the same summary and safety plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts the plan to Maria's rescue-only preference. The session is still more counselor-directed due to repeated summaries and offers to draft a one-line message.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms Maria's right to choose and avoids pressuring her to start a daily medication. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by repeated offers for a clinician-written line.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks one initial open question but largely fails to draw out Maria's values, fears, and motivations. It misses key opportunities to expand Maria's own reasons for better asthma management.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses collaborative language and allows Maria to influence the final plan. The repeated plan and repeated one-line offer make the session less mutually co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only weak attempts to reflect or amplify Maria's statements about monitoring and concern for breathing. Change talk remains mostly client-generated rather than skillfully evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against Maria's resistance and accepts her concerns. The clinician does not use enough double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to examine the meaning or consequences of sustain talk.
