# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Therapist uses respectful, non-judgmental language and avoids reducing Kieran to a compliance problem. Acceptance could be stronger by explicitly validating his frustration, effort, and autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture frustration and the desire to “check the boxes.” They remain somewhat surface-level and miss deeper emotions such as loss of control, resentment, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and narrows toward a concrete, manageable plan. Direction becomes repetitive around logistics without advancing to a broader motivational goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist works from Kieran’s stated rules and keeps the interaction mostly joint rather than instructive. Full collaboration would require more explicit co-creation of the next step and less steering after disengagement cues.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist supports autonomy by inviting Kieran to identify what would feel easier and by avoiding directive pressure. Repeating questions after “I don’t have anything else to say” slightly undermines Kieran’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions draw out Kieran’s preferences for a quicker, simpler process. Evocation remains practical and does not consistently elicit deeper change talk related to values, confidence, or future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses a collaborative stance and mirrors Kieran’s language, supporting shared understanding. Partnership is limited by continued questioning after Kieran signals he does not want to continue.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist elicits some change talk, such as wanting a shorter process and the “fill it out, hand it in, go” structure. These statements are not consistently amplified or connected to Kieran’s own reasons, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without judgment or argumentation. The therapist could more actively reframe resistance as understandable while opening space for change, rather than staying stuck in practical details.
