# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Rosa's ambivalence without framing her as a failure. Affirmations were respectful and identity-sensitive, with only minor generality in a few statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both sides of Rosa's ambivalence and the emotional meaning behind her worries. Depth was slightly limited when reflections stayed summarizing or moved quickly toward problem-solving instead of fully exploring shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking cessation and narrowed into a concrete, client-relevant target behavior around the break-room moment. Direction would be stronger with a clearer shared summary of the specific goal and less time on secondary details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely collaborative, with Rosa's ideas shaping the plan and the psychiatrist building on her contributions. Some suggestions and script options were offered before fully waiting for Rosa to generate more options on her own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized Rosa's right to choose, avoided pressure to quit immediately, and framed all suggestions as optional. Rosa was clearly positioned as the decision-maker and expert on her own life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out Rosa's own ideas, practical strategies, and criteria for success. Evocation could be stronger by more actively expanding deeper change talk around health motivation, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treated Rosa as the expert on her body, work, and social world. Rosa's suggestions substantially shaped the plan rather than the counselor imposing a predetermined approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and affirmations helped Rosa generate practical change talk and behavioral options. The work would be stronger with more deliberate expansion across DARN-C, especially desire, ability, need, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled without argument, minimization, or direct persuasion, and concerns were validated in a respectful way. It would be more effective if sustain talk were more consistently reframed through values, hope, or confidence questions.
