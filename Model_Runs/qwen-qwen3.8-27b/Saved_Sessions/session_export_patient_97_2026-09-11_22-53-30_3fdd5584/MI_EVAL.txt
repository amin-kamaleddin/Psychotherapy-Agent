# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, but the tone is practical and somewhat administrative rather than consistently warm. Acceptance is present, but it does not consistently express deeper affirmation or unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some reflective listening, especially around tiredness and uncertainty. Empathy remains shallow because it does not consistently explore ambivalence, fear, exhaustion, or the emotional meaning behind the patient's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a concrete target behavior, avoiding calls to the patient's old crew this week. Direction is strong and clinically useful, though the ending becomes repetitive and does not consistently connect the behavior to longer-term motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, accepts adjustments, and includes the patient in selecting reminder language. Collaboration is limited because the clinician frequently drives the session with structured options, a preformed goal, and suggested scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options, accepting modifications, and stating that slips are not failures. Autonomy support is strong, though the clinician remains somewhat prescriptive in structuring the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to draw out the patient's own voice by asking him to choose a focus and reminder. Evocation is present but superficial, with little deeper exploration of values, confidence, or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates through choices and incorporates some patient input. Partnership is partial because the session remains largely clinician-led rather than fully shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only brief, weak change talk statements from the patient, such as saying he will try. The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit or amplify the patient's reasons, confidence, importance, or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and responds with a low-judgment, practical tone. Softening is limited because the psychiatrist does not use more strategic MI moves, such as double-sided reflections or reconnecting sustain talk to the patient's goals.
