# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming Daniel for drinking or labeling him an alcoholic. The stance is warm but sometimes procedural, with early planning and risk focus slightly reducing unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Daniel’s practical concerns, surprise, and low-effort preferences with adequate content-level understanding. However, it misses deeper emotional or identity tensions, such as ambivalence about beer, training, self-image, or discomfort with being flagged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and its possible links to sleep, recovery, and marathon training. It is somewhat solution-oriented because new plan elements are introduced repeatedly rather than fully following Daniel’s evolving motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language, preferences, and Daniel’s cues to shape the final plan. Collaboration is uneven because early psychoeducation and structured clinician options lead more than Daniel’s own values and problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices, avoids coercion, and adapts when Daniel rejects alarms, apps, and formal tracking. The main limit is offering several specific behavioral recommendations early, which can feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions and elicits a low-effort plan, but also provides much advice and psychoeducation before drawing out Daniel’s own values, discrepancies, and confidence. More client-generated motivation is needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes Daniel’s suggestions and modifies the plan, showing some partnership. However, the expert-driven early stance and menu of options reduce the sense that Daniel is the primary expert on his own habits and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel contributes useful change talk about changing timing, checking ABV, and trying a brief test. The psychiatrist affirms this but does not consistently deepen or amplify it with evocative questions about importance, values, confidence, or discrepancy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and uses neutral responses when Daniel defends beer or doubts the connection. More advanced MI moves, such as double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance, would better soften sustain talk and connect routine to Daniel’s health goals.
