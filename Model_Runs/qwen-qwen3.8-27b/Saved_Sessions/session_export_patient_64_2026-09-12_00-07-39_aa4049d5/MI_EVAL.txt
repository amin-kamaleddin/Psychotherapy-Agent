# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Jordan’s needle anxiety without shaming. However, the acceptance is somewhat generic and the early shift to practical options can feel more clinical than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Jordan’s conflict about health, grandparents, and needles. Empathy is limited by not fully exploring the emotional weight of guilt, shame, or fear of being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the flu vaccine decision and related barriers. The conversation remains somewhat circular, lacking a clear movement from ambivalence toward a stronger, collaborative change focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s input and responds to his concerns about time, needles, and family. Collaboration is uneven because the therapist often generates the options, scripts, and action steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Jordan’s right to decide and avoids direct pressure or coercion. Autonomy support could be stronger by allowing more space for Jordan to generate his own reasons and choices rather than offering specific recommendations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions about what Jordan wants to change and which option feels doable. Evocation is limited because much of the session is informational and the client’s intrinsic motivations are not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial collaboration by asking for input and adapting to Jordan’s concerns. The partnership is weakened by frequent introductions of plans and options before Jordan has fully explored his own perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan produces some change talk related to protecting his grandparents and getting the shot if it is quick. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on these change statements to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about being healthy and not needing the flu shot is prominent throughout the session. The psychiatrist sometimes validates or reinforces this status-quo language instead of redirecting it toward protective or relational reasons for change.
