# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, even when the client expresses strong fear, biomedical certainty, and distrust of Dr. Brown. There is no invalidation, blame, or labeling, and the counselor accepts both the client's fear and the need for scans without moralizing or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the client's multiple stressors and ambivalence. However, the empathy remains somewhat content-focused and moves quickly to problem-solving rather than fully exploring the emotional weight of isolation, unemployment, chronic pain, and anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on a specific target behavior: obtaining a GI specialist referral/scan and preparing a way to request it from Dr. Brown. Each exchange is anchored to this goal, keeping the work clinically relevant and moving toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner throughout, inviting the client's perspective and allowing her ideas to actively shape the direction of the session. The interaction feels collaborative rather than prescriptive, with the client selecting the referral and helping formulate the key message for Dr. Brown.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's autonomy and freedom of choice, offering options rather than directives and explicitly stating that support will be provided without pressure. The counselor also checks that suggested language feels true to the client, demonstrating strong respect for her authority over her own experience.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits the client's own motivations, fears, and solutions, enabling her to generate the plan for a written request and key message. The evocation is somewhat narrow and occasionally relies on a ready-made formulation rather than fully allowing the client to elaborate and own the language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly conveys that expertise about the client's life and goals resides with her, repeatedly inviting her perspective and treating her as the authority on her own experience. The session is a collaborative process in which the client substantially shapes the plan and key messages.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes and affirms change language, especially around action, determination, and self-efficacy, as the client articulates a clear plan. The change talk is somewhat narrow, focusing mainly on obtaining a referral/scan, with less systematic exploration of importance, ability, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with or challenging the client's sustain talk, but does not consistently soften or redirect it. The session remains heavily oriented toward biomedical certainty and the need for scans, with few double-sided reflections to hold both sides of the client's ambivalence.
