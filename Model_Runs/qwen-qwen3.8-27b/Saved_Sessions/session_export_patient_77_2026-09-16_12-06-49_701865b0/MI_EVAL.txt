# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, affirming stance toward Liyana’s weight, efforts, and emotions. Validation was offered without correction or criticism throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected frustration, loneliness, exhaustion, and self-critical thinking with good perspective-taking. However, the reflections could have explored the emotional meaning of her body-image distress and feelings of being unseen more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear target behavior: reducing post-workout self-comparison and using a low-pressure self-compassion note. The conversation moved efficiently from concern to goal selection, implementation details, confidence, obstacles, and a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructed the plan with Liyana rather than imposing a solution. Her suggestions directly shaped the final intervention, including muting the phone, using a short factual prompt, leaving the phone in her bag, and using a one-tap note.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported autonomy by offering choices, emphasizing that forgetting is acceptable, and positioning Liyana as the decision-maker. Language such as asking which option feels most doable reinforced her freedom and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked Liyana’s own motivations, preferences, and realistic next steps, and helped her generate her own client-led phrases. Evocation could be strengthened by using more open-ended, values-based questions about what staying kind to herself would mean or how she wants to feel after workouts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly treated Liyana as the expert on her own life, body, and preferences. Rather than prescribing a plan, they repeatedly asked what she had tried, what felt doable, and which option sounded best, allowing her contributions to drive the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited change talk by inviting Liyana to identify goals, preferences, confidence, and next steps. It could be strengthened by more explicitly amplifying change talk and exploring deeper DARN-CAT themes such as the importance, need, or future benefits of the change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk skillfully by avoiding argument, persuasion, or invalidation, and by normalizing concerns about feeling stuck or fake. More consistent strategic redirection from sustain talk toward autonomy, values, or change talk would make this criterion stronger.
