# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental, respectful language and honors the client’s harm-reduction goals without shaming. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural rather than deeply affirming the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence and practical concerns around safety, housing, and legal fear. Empathy is mostly cognitive and practical, with limited exploration of deeper emotions such as shame or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on safer heroin use and overdose prevention. Each step moves toward concrete risk-reduction actions such as naloxone, testing, clean supplies, and 911 planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options and co-creating a script and checklist with the client. However, some counselor-provided materials remain slightly expert-directed rather than fully client-built.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with frequent invitations for choice and no pressure to quit. The client’s decisions about buprenorphine, disclosure, and next steps are consistently respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions such as the readiness ruler and “What matters most to you right now?” Evocation is limited because the session quickly moves into information, scripts, and logistics rather than deeper client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shapes the agenda around the client’s priorities and asks which options feel most useful. Partnership is strong but not fully explicit, as the counselor still leads much of the conversation and provides structured scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and affirms concrete change talk related to safer use, housemate preparation, and naloxone access. Change talk remains largely practical and logistical rather than deepening values, confidence, or ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging the client’s resistance to quitting and supports the client’s non-abstinence stance. Softening is not at a 5 because it does not use more advanced skills such as double-sided reflections or strategic reframing of ambivalence.
