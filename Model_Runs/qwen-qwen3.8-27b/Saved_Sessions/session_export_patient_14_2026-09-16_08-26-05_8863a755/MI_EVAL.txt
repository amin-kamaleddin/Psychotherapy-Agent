# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepts the client’s time constraints without moralizing. Acceptance is consistent but somewhat formulaic and does not fully acknowledge the client’s underlying frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client’s practical constraints well, such as limited time and preference for simplicity. However, empathy stays mostly factual and does not deeply capture the emotional frustration or defensiveness behind the client’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on blood pressure and a feasible short-term behavior change. Direction is clear, though the later repetition of the plan adds little strategic movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options and adapting the plan to the client’s preferences. Collaboration is practical but still somewhat clinician-led in generating the meal plan and shopping list.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s autonomy by respecting his choices and boundary against unsolicited follow-up. The client is consistently positioned as being in charge of his plan and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some effective evocative questions to help the client identify a specific change. However, there is limited exploration of the client’s deeper motivation, values, or confidence in making the change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shares decision-making by asking the client to choose options and preferences. Partnership is limited because the psychiatrist frequently takes on the role of practical problem-solver rather than fully positioning the client as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps the client identify and affirm a concrete, doable goal. Change talk is present but not consistently deepened, amplified, or linked to the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles the client’s sustain talk well by accepting barriers and using them to simplify the plan rather than arguing. Ambivalence is not explicitly resolved, but the response avoids reinforcement of resistance.
