# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Seamus's preference to focus on dental care. Acceptance was strong but somewhat procedural, with limited deeper affirmation of frustration or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician used accurate content reflections and demonstrated adequate understanding of Seamus's practical concerns. However, empathy remained mostly surface-level and did not consistently capture underlying frustration, irritation, or desire for control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial focus was on smoking cessation, but the session quickly shifted to dental planning and stayed there. The clinician did not strategically link dental concerns to smoking or maintain the MI target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited agenda-setting and offered choices, supporting some collaboration early on. However, much of the session became clinician-led dental information rather than co-constructed planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician supported choice by offering options, respecting Seamus's decision, and avoiding pressure. Autonomy support was strong for dental logistics but less explicitly tied to smoking-related decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was one early evocative question about quitting and past attempts to stop. After Seamus redirected to dental care, the clinician did not continue eliciting smoking-related motivations, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally adopted a collaborative stance and allowed Seamus's preferences to shape the session. Partnership weakened when the dental plan and aftercare instructions were delivered more one-way.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician made one initial attempt to evoke change talk, but did not follow up after Seamus resisted. There was little development or amplification of Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, or Commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledged Seamus's sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it. However, the responses were mostly accepting rather than using MI-consistent reflections or redirection to soften resistance.
