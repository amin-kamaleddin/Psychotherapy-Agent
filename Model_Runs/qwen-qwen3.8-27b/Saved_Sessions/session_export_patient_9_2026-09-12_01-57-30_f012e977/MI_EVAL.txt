# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout, which helps preserve a safe therapeutic climate. However, occasional prescriptive language subtly narrows the depth of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Amina’s core ambivalence and emotional concerns with clear perspective-taking. The rating is limited because reflections are often quickly followed by problem-solving, reducing deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and relevant focus on Amina’s stated concern about her son’s safety, stability, and routine. The discussion is effectively narrowed from broad co-parenting conflict to a specific, manageable bedtime plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is evidence of collaboration as the psychiatrist adjusts language, co-creates a list, and invites Amina to choose among options. However, the therapist frequently supplies the content of the plan, creating an uneven power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice and respects Amina’s preferences, supporting her sense of ownership. Autonomy support is weakened by frequent directive advice and prescriptive action steps that can reduce client-led decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses helpful evocative questions and a confidence ruler to elicit Amina’s values and motivations. The session often shifts into advice-giving and scripts before fully developing Amina’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates some partnership by inviting Amina’s input and allowing her preferences to shape the session. The partnership is uneven because the counselor often takes an expert, agenda-driven role by providing scripts and structured plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes several effective moves to elicit change talk, such as exploring values, past confidence, and small manageable steps. The counselor does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen Amina’s own change language before moving into problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Amina’s desire for peace without arguing or dismissing her concerns. The softening is limited by a lack of consistent double-sided reflections, balancing, or reframing that would more fully address her fears while guiding change.
