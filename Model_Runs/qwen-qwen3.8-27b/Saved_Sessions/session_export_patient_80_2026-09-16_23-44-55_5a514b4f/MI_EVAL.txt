# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the client's effort, agency, and concerns. Acceptance is strong, though some affirmations are slightly therapist-centered and could more explicitly normalize ambivalence and stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's ambivalence around drinking, autonomy, and fear of being managed. Empathy could be deeper by more directly attending to underlying shame, anxiety, and loss-of-control feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and anger management, with a logical progression toward a concrete safety plan. Direction could be strengthened by more consistently linking plan details back to the target behavior and summarizing the agreed focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively to co-create practical steps based on the client's own ideas. Collaboration could be more consistently client-generated by inviting the client to create, compare, and choose among options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's choice, ownership, and self-direction, especially around the client-initiated signal and avoiding monitoring. This is one of the strongest areas of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses many open-ended questions to elicit the client's motivations, resources, and plan details. Evocation could be stronger by reducing leading questions and allowing more client-generated formulation before offering a frame.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance and treats the client as the expert in his own life. Partnership could be more consistently non-directive by reducing subtle framing or steering of the client's thinking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and supports strong change talk related to desire, ability, reasons, and commitment. The session could more explicitly amplify this change talk through scaling, summarizing, or deeper commitment exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging the appeal of drinking without arguing or reinforcing it. Softening could be more consistent by further exploring discrepancy between the client's values, safety goals, and continued risk.
