# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and showed positive regard, though some affirmations felt formulaic. There was limited explicit normalization of the body-shame content, which kept the score from being a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist recognized the client's practical burden and tiredness, but often stayed more cognitive and planning-focused. Deeper reflection of body shame, disappointment, and self-criticism would have strengthened empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior and moved efficiently from intention to a concrete plan. Interventions remained relevant, strategic, and action-oriented without wandering off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-created and the tone was collaborative rather than authoritarian. The psychiatrist sometimes supplied many options and scripts, which made the collaboration less fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respected Aisha's choices and avoided pushing her beyond her willingness. Some repeated scaling and provided options introduced a mild directive expectation to increase confidence or add supports.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful planning details and some client-generated ideas. Evocation remained somewhat superficial because it focused more on action steps than drawing out deeper motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Aisha as the expert by asking her to choose meaningful, realistic, and kind options. The interaction was collaborative, though the psychiatrist still structured the plan by supplying specific menus of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicited meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or build on it with reflections and evocative questions. There were missed opportunities to deepen Aisha's own statements of desire, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with or reinforce Aisha's ambivalence and used some helpful reframing. The handling of sustain talk was acceptable but not consistently skillful, with limited double-sided reflections or exploration of mixed feelings.
