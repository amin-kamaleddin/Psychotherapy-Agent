# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used nonjudgmental, validating language that reduced defensiveness. It was strong, though the warmth mainly tied to goals rather than explicitly affirming the client’s worth despite risky behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections captured ambivalence well. Empathy could deepen further by exploring shame, fear, embarrassment, and uncertainty more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing or pausing use to allow the nose to heal. It could be stronger by more consistently connecting the immediate goal to broader safety and long-term health risks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-created using Adrian’s own ideas, such as checking his nose, writing it down, and finding distractions. The clinician still structured the conversation more than fully collaborative pacing and agenda control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice and avoided direct advice or abstinence pressure. Some questions assumed a plan was already chosen, which could be made more autonomy-neutral.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some practical change ideas, but often moved quickly into problem-solving. Deeper evocative questions about values, confidence, and self-efficacy would strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Adrian’s input and let his ideas shape the next steps. Partnership was good, though the session remained largely clinician-led rather than fully negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged around harm reduction and a short break. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify desire, need, ability, and confidence in change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and accepted sustain talk appropriately. However, normalizing weekend use may have unintentionally kept sustain talk central instead of softening it.
