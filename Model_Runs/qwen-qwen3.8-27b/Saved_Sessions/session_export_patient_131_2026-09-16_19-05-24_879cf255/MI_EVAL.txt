# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the client’s strengths. It is not a 5 because acceptance could be more explicit as unconditional, especially during moments of shame or self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s central conflict between safety, dignity, independence, and fatherhood. A repeated question after the client has already answered reduces empathy and misses some opportunities to deepen shame and mana.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and building a stable, accountable support plan. It would be stronger with explicit connection to the Stopping Violence program and broader accountability.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works as a collaborator, allowing the client’s ideas to shape the plan. The repeated question and occasional therapist pacing slightly weaken the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the strongest criterion, with consistent emphasis on the client’s right to choose and no pressure or moralizing. The counselor repeatedly reinforces that the plan must remain the client’s own decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor draws motivation from the client by asking him to identify a steadier future, a doable call, and self-monitoring. It could be stronger by more fully amplifying change talk, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites the client’s perspective throughout. It would benefit from explicitly acknowledging the client as the expert on his own life, values, and mana.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes desire, need, ability, and commitment language through open-ended, client-centered questions. It could be stronger by more systematically labeling, amplifying, and deepening the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully, with the counselor reflecting concerns about freedom and manhood without arguing. It could be stronger by more strategically transforming sustain talk into change talk through double-sided reflections or values-based reframing.
