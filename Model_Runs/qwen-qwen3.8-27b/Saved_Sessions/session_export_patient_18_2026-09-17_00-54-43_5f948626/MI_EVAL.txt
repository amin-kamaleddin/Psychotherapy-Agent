# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth, respect, and nonjudgment. The client was validated for being referred and for her choices without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected the client's awkwardness, suspicion, and need for privacy. Deeper exploration of underlying emotions, such as embarrassment or fear of being misunderstood, could have strengthened empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session mostly remained focused on the alcohol screening and its connection to school, mood, and sleep. However, it sometimes drifted into broader wellness planning without consistently linking back to the target behavior of alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked as a true partner and invited the client to shape the conversation and follow-up plan. The final plan was co-created and incorporated the client's exact language and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported the client's autonomy by emphasizing choice, privacy, and control over the process. The client's boundary that "fine" means no follow-up questions was clearly respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used open-ended questions and reflections to draw out the client's own priorities and concerns. Deeper evocation of values, future goals, or confidence in maintaining healthy habits could have strengthened the client's internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently operated as a partner rather than an authority figure. The client was treated as the expert on her own experience and was invited to define the session's direction and logistics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reinforced the client's change-oriented language around protecting sleep, focus, mood, and GPA. However, the cultivation of change talk could have been more explicit and focused on alcohol-specific motivation rather than broader functioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully avoided amplifying or challenging the client's mild sustain talk and reluctance. A stronger response could have more explicitly softened ambivalence about the counseling process itself and gently turned it toward the client's own agenda.
