# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful and nonjudgmental, using supportive statements such as respecting Margaret’s independence. Acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and does not fully validate her ambivalence, pride, or frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some good reflections of Margaret’s ambivalence, especially around the pub, independence, and falls. Empathy is adequate but inconsistent, with missed opportunities to track irritation and defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and preventing falls through practical steps such as a soft drink first on Thursday. Direction is clear, though the plan and safety advice become repetitive near the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret is invited to choose a doable step, and her idea becomes the central plan. Collaboration is present but uneven because the clinician frequently provides options, safety tips, and summaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Margaret is consistently given choices and is allowed to set her own plan, including declining a formal check-in. Autonomy support is strong, though repeated offers of follow-up and safety advice feel somewhat guiding.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks useful questions about confidence and what would make a step more manageable. Evocation is present but limited, as the clinician moves quickly to advice before fully eliciting Margaret’s own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites Margaret’s input and collaborates on a concrete plan. Partnership is present but not consistently strong, as the repeated structure and safety guidance can feel more expert-led than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret offers some change talk, including willingness to try a soft drink first on Thursday. The clinician supports this, but does not consistently deepen or expand her own reasons, importance, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with or dismissing Margaret’s sustain talk, such as not wanting to make a big deal of one drink. However, it is not consistently softened through reflective or double-sided responses.
