# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone, validating Aizada's frustration and fear. However, acceptance is often generic and quickly shifts into problem-solving and medical directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is surface-level acknowledgment of the client's fear and frustration, but limited reflection of the deeper emotional experience behind her symptoms. The response remains mostly factual and practical rather than truly perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Aizada's gastrointestinal symptoms and the practical goal of obtaining testing. However, direction is narrowly medical and procedural rather than MI-focused on ambivalence, values, or desired change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and helps construct a message, showing some collaboration. Nonetheless, the counselor largely supplies the content and structure, making the interaction more advisory than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects some boundaries, such as skipping breathing exercises. At the same time, frequent directives and task prescriptions can feel controlling rather than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with little systematic effort to draw out Aizada's own values, motivations, or reasons for change. Most of the session involves advice, scripts, and concrete steps rather than eliciting internal change arguments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, indicating partial partnership. However, the role is often that of a medical expert supplying exact scripts, tests, and next steps rather than a true collaborative partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some action-oriented statements, but they are largely prompted by external scripts and directives. The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit, amplify, or reflect the client's intrinsic change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly aligns with the client's insistence on tests and rejection of emotional framing, reinforcing her rigidity. There is little use of double-sided reflection or gentle redirection to soften sustain talk.
