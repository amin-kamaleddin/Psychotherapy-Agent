# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard, warmth, and non-judgment throughout. No moralizing or pressure to change beyond the patient's own stated concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate reflections of ambivalence, routine, and social context. Some missed opportunities to deepen emotional exploration, especially around the fall, son's concern, and next-day haze.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from goals to baseline, limits, a small change, implementation plan, and success indicators. The focus remained tightly on alcohol use without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as genuine shared exploration, with the patient's ideas actively shaping the plan. Language and problem-solving consistently reflect a partnership between equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's choice and ownership are consistently honored, including her boundaries around Thursday/Friday and the size of change. The psychiatrist facilitates decisions rather than prescribing them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within the patient through skillful questions and reflections. The patient generates the plan, goals, and change talk rather than receiving external persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the patient as the expert on her own life and choices. Power-sharing is explicit, and the patient leads the direction and content of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-crafted open questions effectively elicit meaningful change talk and practical ideas. The psychiatrist could more actively amplify change talk and explicitly explore confidence or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged, validated, and woven alongside change talk without argument or minimization. The psychiatrist skillfully honors the patient's valued routine while supporting a small, self-defined change.
