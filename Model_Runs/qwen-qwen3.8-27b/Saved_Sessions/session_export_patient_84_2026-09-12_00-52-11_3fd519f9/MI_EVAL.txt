# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, avoiding shame or argument. The stance is somewhat procedural and repetitive rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist accurately reflects surface-level feelings of annoyance and pressure. However, there is limited exploration of the deeper peer, identity, and ambivalence-related meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session quickly narrows from cannabis use to a practical script for managing conflict with his mother. The target behavior of concern receives little sustained MI-directed attention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is an initial collaborative appearance through offered options, but the interaction becomes therapist-driven with prescribed scripts and instructions. The client is not meaningfully positioned as an equal partner in problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects some client modifications, supporting autonomy at times. However, repeated directives and scripted language reduce the overall sense of client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the therapist mainly provides solutions rather than drawing out the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. There is little strategic questioning around what change might mean to Malik.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist begins with a choice-based approach but quickly shifts to expert-driven instruction. The client contributes minimally and is not fully positioned as the expert in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only limited attempts to elicit change talk, mainly through initial options. The therapist does not effectively develop the client's own desire, need, ability, or reasons to change cannabis use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The therapist reinforces the client's minimizing and status-quo language by incorporating it into the plan. There is no effective double-sided reflection, discrepancy work, or strategic softening of sustain talk.
