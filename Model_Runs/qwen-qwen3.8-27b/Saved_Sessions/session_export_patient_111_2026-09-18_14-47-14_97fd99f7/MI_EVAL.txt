# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations and a genuine apology when over-interpreting. However, the warmth remains somewhat formulaic without deeper individualized emotional engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with accurate reflections of the cigarette's functional role and emotional significance. Reflections occasionally remain at the cognitive level rather than fully accessing the underlying emotional weight, such as the fear of loss behind 'the only part that's actually mine.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a general through-line from resistance to understanding function to health concerns, but the middle section lacks consistent momentum and the ending feels open-ended. A more strategic consolidation or collaborative next step would have strengthened the session's trajectory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative tone with mutual inquiry language and active adaptation when Richard signals resistance. However, the psychiatrist still does much of the framing and direction-setting, which, while collaborative in tone, keeps Richard in a slightly more passive role than a true equal co-author.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout, consistently affirming Richard's right to choose, never pressuring, and explicitly framing exploration as optional rather than directive. Phrases like 'this space is yours' and 'you don't have to decide anything today' are skillfully deployed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws out Richard's own reasons and meanings without providing external reasons for change, successfully evoking his own distinction between cigarette and quiet time. However, the evocation could be deeper in following through on evoked material, particularly the health concerns and what the breathing might mean to him personally.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance maintained with frequent invitations for input and appropriate deferral to Richard's self-knowledge, including a clear apology and course correction. Could be stronger in explicit power-sharing, as the psychiatrist still leads much of the framing rather than truly co-creating the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited around health concerns and the separation of cigarette from quiet time, but these moments are not consistently amplified or deepened. The strongest change talk (breathing, tests) is acknowledged but not further explored or followed up on.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is mostly neutrally reflected without reinforcement or argument, which is appropriate. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections to gently expand Richard's perspective or strategically pivot toward change.
