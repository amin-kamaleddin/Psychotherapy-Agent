# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, normalizing freezing, shame, and avoidance. Affirmations such as “honest and brave” support unconditional positive regard without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are supportive and accurately capture the client’s frustration and anxiety. However, the psychiatrist sometimes moves too quickly to planning before fully exploring the emotional meaning of exposure, vulnerability, and fear of judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior: initiating a concrete first step with the laptop/assignment. New concerns are tied back to the immediate plan with minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client meaningfully contributes choices, such as the bag-by-the-door plan, flexible timing, and the self-statement “I just opened it.” However, the psychiatrist generates many options and structures much of the plan, limiting maximal client-led co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites choice, respects the client’s discomfort with rigidity, and avoids coercion. Stopping or avoiding is framed as information rather than failure, reinforcing client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits client-generated language, including “I just opened it” and “I’ll try tonight.” There is less evocation of deeper intrinsic motivation, values, self-efficacy, and what would increase confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The stance is collaborative, with repeated invitations for the client to choose among options. The volume of therapist-generated options slightly reduces the sense that the client fully holds the agenda and expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session produces concrete change talk such as “I can do one minute” and “I’ll try tonight.” It remains more action-focused than motivation-focused, with fewer moments of exploring desire, reasons, need, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk about quitting, faking, doom-scrolling, and failure is consistently softened without argument or moralizing. The psychiatrist reframes these thoughts as useful information and gently redirects them toward small, doable alternatives.
