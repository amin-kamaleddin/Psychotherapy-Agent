# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgment, validation, and respect are evident, including acceptance of Jordan's frustration and hopelessness. Some affirmations feel generic, and repeated safety reminders make parts of the session more procedural than emotionally unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures fatigue, frustration, distrust, and ambivalence. It does not fully explore the deeper meaning behind core statements such as "I can’t get a break" or "nothing changes."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a concrete, manageable resume task tied to Jordan’s stated stress. The focus occasionally becomes logistical, and repeated safety reminders slightly fragment the motivational flow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, asks permission, and allows Jordan to shape the plan. The collaboration is somewhat guided because the psychiatrist still drives much of the structure and introduces specific content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on choice, respect for declining follow-up, and absence of coercion are major strengths. Offering multiple optional suggestions and directly providing resume bullets may slightly reduce Jordan's active ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions help Jordan identify a small step and express willingness to try. The session shifts too quickly into directives and practical content, leaving deeper evocation of values, confidence, and personal reasons incomplete.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance is clear through permission-seeking, offering choices, and allowing Jordan to decide the plan. It is not fully optimal because the psychiatrist still acts as a resource-expert by supplying specific task details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan offers some desire and commitment language, and the psychiatrist supports it with affirmations. Development using the DARN-C framework is limited, with less amplification of reasons, abilities, needs, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well without arguing, minimizing, or contradicting Jordan. The most final sustain talk, such as "If I don’t, I don’t," is accepted but not fully worked through with a gentle pivot.
