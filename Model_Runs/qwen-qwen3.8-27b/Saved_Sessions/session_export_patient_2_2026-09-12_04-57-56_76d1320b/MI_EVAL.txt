# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect, nonjudgment, and support, especially by honoring the patient's boundary around alcohol. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicitly validating the patient's identity as a mother and the emotional weight of her situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate but remain more summary-like than emotionally nuanced. Deeper themes such as exhaustion, maternal responsibility, and fear of being labeled were not fully explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on smoking reduction and avoids drifting into alcohol despite the patient's defensive remarks. Direction could be improved by making the closing less repetitive and using it to consolidate motivation more effectively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the patient's preferences, boundaries, and ideas shaping the session and the final plan. The psychiatrist avoids an authoritative stance and co-creates a practical, patient-owned approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through permission-seeking language, respect for the patient's choices, and non-coercive framing of the plan. The psychiatrist effectively supports reduction rather than insisting on abstinence and reinforces self-directed learning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong evocative questions, including the confidence scale and coping prompts, to draw out the patient's own reasons and strategies. Evocation could be deepened by reducing reassuring/instructive language and exploring meaning more thoroughly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the patient as the expert on her own life and allows her priorities to guide the session. The patient's choices clearly determine the focus, plan, and follow-up structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits concrete change talk around smoking reduction, confidence, and practical steps. Change talk could be further amplified by exploring the deeper meaning of the patient's maternal values and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging or reinforcing sustain talk, particularly around alcohol, which prevents defensiveness. More active reframing or double-sided reflections could better acknowledge sustaining reasons while gently shifting toward change.
