# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, affirming, and nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations throughout; minor deduction because acceptance is somewhat tied to the client's progress rather than deeply unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A few accurate surface-level reflections ('the tray feels like pressure,' 'that surprise factor is real'), but missed opportunities to explore the client's feelings of social discomfort and underlying values, keeping empathy at feeling/meaning level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol at gatherings with steady pacing and a concrete actionable plan; slightly one-directional with prescriptive steering rather than collaborative MI exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client chooses among options and contributes to the plan, but the psychiatrist is the primary generator of ideas, scripts, and strategies, limiting true co-creation between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client freedom to choose is regularly affirmed and no pressure is applied; the volume of unsolicited strategies (five bullet points at a time) slightly dilutes the client as source of his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely uses open-ended questions to elicit the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; questions are mostly closed/multiple-choice and the session relies heavily on external information rather than drawing out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative questions signal collaboration, but the power dynamic remains uneven with the psychiatrist dominating content and structure while the client mainly selects from provided options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client spontaneously offers several change-talk statements but they are rarely amplified, reflected back, or deepened; no targeted questions to elicit more change talk or connect to values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk present in the session, and the psychiatrist neither reinforces it nor actively engages with the ambivalence; lacks double-sided reflections to honor both sides of the client's tension.
