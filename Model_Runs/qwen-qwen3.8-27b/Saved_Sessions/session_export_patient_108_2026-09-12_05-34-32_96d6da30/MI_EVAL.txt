# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and normalization of Oliver’s feelings. The approach is supportive but sometimes procedural and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is accurate reflective listening and good capture of Oliver’s ambivalence. The empathy could go deeper into the emotional weight of guilt, fear of seeming cruel, and self-critical identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target behavior of assertively communicating a move-out boundary. The conversation moves efficiently from ambivalence to a concrete, time-bound action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well on practical details and adjusts language to Oliver’s preferences. The collaboration is somewhat uneven because the psychiatrist still generates much of the script, timeline, and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver is given choices about tone, date, and timing, which supports autonomy. However, directive prompts such as “send now” and “paste, send” create urgency and can reduce the sense that Oliver is fully in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions about readiness and preferred timeline. More evocative questions and reflections of Oliver’s own motivation and confidence are needed to reduce reliance on supplied solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver’s input is invited and incorporated, especially around wording and timing. The style remains fairly expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying much of the language, structure, and follow-through plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some useful change talk and confidence from Oliver. The psychiatrist could do more to reflect, amplify, and build on this change talk before moving into problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument or dismissal, and concerns are reframed as firm and kind. More double-sided reflections or evocative pivots would help connect Oliver’s guilt and ambivalence to change more effectively.
