# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and normalizes the client’s concerns without judgment. The warmth remains somewhat task-focused rather than deeply affirming of the client’s overall worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, practical concerns, and the feeling of being burdened. They would be stronger with more focus on deeper emotions such as stress, fear, or social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused and progressively narrows the target from quitting generally to handling the 2 PM break. A brief summary or collaborative goal statement would strengthen momentum and closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the plan from the client’s ideas, including the car, watch, and “time” cue. It is not fully collaborative because the psychiatrist still guides the sequence without inviting the client to steer the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives and emphasizes the client’s role in choosing realistic options. It would be stronger with more explicit reminders that the client controls the decision and plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks the client to generate his own solutions rather than providing them. The evocation would improve by drawing out deeper motivation, values, or confidence related to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s perspective and follows his ideas, creating a collaborative feel. It falls short of full power-sharing by not explicitly naming the client as the expert or letting him choose priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-oriented planning, such as targeting the 2 PM break and using a hard-stop cue. However, the session stays mostly solution-focused and does not consistently draw out desire, need, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns about stress, peer cues, and complexity are acknowledged without argument or reinforcement. Some sustain talk remains unresolved rather than being pivoted into stronger self-endorsement of change.
