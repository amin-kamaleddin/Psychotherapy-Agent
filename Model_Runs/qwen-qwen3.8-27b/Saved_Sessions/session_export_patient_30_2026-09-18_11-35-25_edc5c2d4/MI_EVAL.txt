# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with phrases like 'that's not weakness, that's honesty' and never disapproves of David's hesitation. Could be slightly more explicitly affirming of David as a whole person beyond his cholesterol concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional empathic accuracy with complex reflections that capture the emotional core beneath surface complaints, such as identifying the existential dimension of David's fear of uncertainty. Consistently high-level empathic work that goes deeper than what David explicitly states.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on medication adherence with a logical progression from exploring concerns to developing a concrete plan. The middle portion on 'what the numbers mean' could have been more efficiently guided toward the decision point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framing the conversation as a joint exploration, building on David's language and metaphors like 'slow rust' and 'driver's seat.' Could be stronger by more explicitly inviting David to shape the agenda and sharing more of the psychiatrist's own perspective as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support at the highest level — never pressures David to decide, never imposes a timeline, and consistently frames all options as David's choice. Particularly strong in validating 'maybe I just keep doing what I'm doing' as an exercise of autonomy rather than trying to talk David out of it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within David rather than providing external arguments, using questions like 'what would make this decision feel more right for you?' Could be slightly more targeted in the middle by asking David to rate confidence or explore what would increase it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with language like 'I'm curious' and explicitly defers to David's expertise about his own body. Could be stronger by more explicitly co-creating the session direction and sharing more of the psychiatrist's own perspective as an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through questions that draw out David's own motivations, and affirms it with statements like 'You're not closed to the medication, and I can tell that.' Could more explicitly amplify the strongest change talk moments (e.g., the yard work comment, the decision to call his doctor) to further strengthen David's sense of agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well with double-sided reflections and positive reframing, such as 'That's not a rejection of the medication. That's you protecting your right to decide on your own terms.' Could more actively pivot away from the side-effect focus when it became circular and use more strategic reflection to help David see tensions in his own statements.
