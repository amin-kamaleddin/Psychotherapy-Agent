# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, with clear acceptance of Linda's experience. It falls short of a 5 because some questions subtly frame smoking as the problem to be removed, slightly reducing full acceptance as she is in the moment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses strong reflective listening and understands the meaning and function of the cigarette for Linda. The main limitation is that some reflections move quickly toward change-focused interpretations rather than fully exploring her emotional state.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking, its function as a pause, and its implications for her children. It is less than a 5 because the direction is narrow and repeatedly framed around cigarette-free alternatives without a clearer collaboratively defined next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as a partner, invites Linda's perspective, and avoids an expert or didactic stance. The collaboration is not a 5 because the agenda is still led by a change-focused frame rather than being fully co-constructed with Linda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids pressure, commands, and direct advice, while supporting Linda's ownership of her own choices. It is not a 5 because several questions assume that the goal is to separate the pause from smoking, subtly limiting fully open autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable attempts to elicit Linda's own values, goals, and ideas about what a pause might need. The evocation is not consistently deep or strategic, as the session repeatedly returns to hypothetical cigarette-free scenarios rather than expanding Linda's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance and allows Linda's ideas to shape the conversation. It is not a 5 because the clinician still carries a clear change agenda and does not fully share agenda control with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda does generate some change talk around her values, role modeling, and wanting to keep the pause without losing herself. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or strategically build on this change talk, and some questions presuppose a cigarette-free outcome.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and respects Linda's reasons for continuing to smoke rather than challenging them directly. However, the session does not consistently soften or pivot sustain talk, often keeping the cigarette's function central rather than redirecting toward discrepancy, values, or change.
