# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, nonjudgmental, and consistently normalizes struggle rather than disapproving. However, much of the positive regard is tied to the client's plan-making and performance, rather than being fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's feelings, ambivalence, and self-awareness. The limitation is that it does not fully deepen the emotional core of body-image pain, self-criticism, or repeated disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays mostly focused on one concrete target behavior and steadily narrows it into an actionable plan. The early opening and initial multi-behavior menu briefly dilute the single-target focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the client's ideas visibly shaping the plan. It falls slightly short of 5 because the therapist occasionally supplies structure, options, or frameworks rather than staying fully emergent with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist frequently supports choice, ownership, and the client's right to reject tracking or external accountability. Some well-intentioned suggestions for structure and interventions slightly reduce fully client-led autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively elicits the client's own ideas, resources, confidence, and plan details. However, it does not deeply evoke the client's personal reasons, values, or the meaning of change beyond the practical level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Power is meaningfully shared, and the client chooses the target, timing, cue, label, and approach. The therapist occasionally inserts options or structure from the expert side, which briefly tilts the interaction away from full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist reliably evokes and reinforces change and commitment language, including confidence ratings and concrete plan steps. It does not fully develop deeper change talk around importance, meaning, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing, normalizes caution, and redirects sustain-related concerns in a validating way. It does not fully use more advanced softening skills with deeper themes like past failed diets or fear of burnout.
