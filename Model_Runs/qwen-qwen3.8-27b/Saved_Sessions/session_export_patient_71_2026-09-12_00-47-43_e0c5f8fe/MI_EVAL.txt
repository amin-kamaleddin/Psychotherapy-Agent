# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout, normalizing the client's concerns. However, the language is sometimes formulaic and could more fully convey individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some reflective listening and validation of the client's practical concerns. However, empathy remains more plan-oriented and does not fully reflect deeper emotions such as self-criticism, shame, or relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on developing a concrete, achievable grounding practice. The main limitation is that direction stays more task-focused and does not consistently connect the behavior to the client's underlying values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts well to client input and allows the client's preferences to shape the plan. At times, however, the psychiatrist functions more like an advisor by proposing structured options before fully inviting client-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client's boundaries and supports choices such as reducing frequency, removing logging, and avoiding scripted language. Some early suggestions, however, remain somewhat prescriptive rather than fully client-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Much of the questioning is practical and planning-oriented rather than evocative. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out the client's internal motivations, values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and accepts the client's revisions, supporting a degree of partnership. However, the session often begins with expert-led suggestions and lacks stronger language about shared power and client authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses some change talk, including wanting to feel grounded and making a small plan. The psychiatrist creates space for this but does not consistently amplify or deepen the change-related language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled in a nonarguing, validating way and often reframed as useful information. The main area for growth is using more active ambivalence-resolution strategies, especially with the client's final concerns about self-criticism.
