# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with no labeling, disapproval, or minimization. The psychiatrist treats Nathaniel as a whole person navigating a complex experience rather than framing coffee use as a problem to fix.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy that goes beyond surface-level reflection to identify deeper meaning (ownership, identity, a space that's his). Reflections consistently capture emotional nuance and the person behind the words, such as 'the number is just a fact, not a verdict.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression from establishing the current experience through exploring ambivalence to a self-defined plan. Minor deduction for lacking strategic consolidation—the session ends without clearly summarizing or confirming the emerging next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-person exploration where the client's ideas (counting, step tracking, defining 'not being blindsided') directly shape the session's direction and outcomes. Inclusive language and shared-agenda framing are evident from the opening question onward.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and consistently affirms the client's right to self-direction, decision-making, and ambivalence without pressure. Phrases like 'you get to decide what to do with it, if anything' and 'you're allowed to hold both of those things at once' reinforce freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation, plans, and definitions emerge from Nathaniel himself; the psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change or imposes a framework. Questions like 'what do you make of that?' and 'what feels right to you?' elicit the client's own language and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as the expert on his own experience and invites self-interpretation rather than offering the psychiatrist's. The session direction is substantially shaped by Nathaniel's own ideas and language, with no hint of the therapist knowing what's best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Progressively and naturally evokes change talk—from 'my hands shake if I skip a cup' to 'I just want the option to exist'—entirely through the client's own words. Non-forceful questions create space for autonomous motivation without ever telling Nathaniel what to want.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully validates sustain talk first ('that's not nothing,' 'I can hear how much that means') before using it as a springboard for change. Reframes the 'unfair trade' to show that change doesn't require loss, and explicitly distinguishes 'it's not stop doing coffee, it's just actually knowing.'
