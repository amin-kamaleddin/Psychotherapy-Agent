# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, including acknowledgment of Marco’s sustain talk and past effort. Acceptance could be deeper and less formulaic by normalizing mixed feelings and affirming Marco beyond progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful reflections of frustration and practical concerns, but empathy often stays at the behavioral/plan level rather than exploring underlying emotions, values, or ambivalence. Stronger empathic responses could capture the conflict between enjoying juice and wanting to protect health progress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing fruit juice as the target behavior. Each exchange moves purposefully toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting input, using Marco’s ideas, and offering role-play. Collaboration could be stronger by sharing more power, slowing the pace, and letting Marco generate more solutions before the psychiatrist introduces options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco’s preferences are respected, and he is asked what feels workable, but the psychiatrist frequently offers practical options and scripts that can subtly steer choices. More explicit ownership language would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes confidence, commitment, and problem-solving, but often provides ideas rather than drawing out deeper internal motivation. More evocation of values, meaning, and self-belief would support lasting change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marco’s input and allows his ideas to shape the plan. The approach remains somewhat agenda-driven and solution-focused, which can reduce the sense that Marco is the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is effectively evoked through concrete action-oriented questions about doability, next steps, and confidence. Deeper change talk related to values, identity, and intrinsic motivation is less consistently developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about enjoying juice is acknowledged and redirected toward manageable action rather than challenged. More nuanced softening, such as double-sided reflections, could better honor the ambivalence while opening change.
