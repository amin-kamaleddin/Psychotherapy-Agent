# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently expressed warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, normalizing setbacks and Laura’s hesitation. Acceptance was strong but sometimes embedded in a prescriptive, problem-solving tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured Laura’s values, worries, and ambivalence accurately but remained largely content-level. Deeper emotional meaning, such as exhaustion, fear of failure, and perfectionism, was underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on a specific evening wind-down behavior, especially a short breathing practice. The focus was practical but sometimes overly technical and not consistently linked back to broader values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician adjusted the plan based on Laura’s input and made several elements optional. However, most strategies and troubleshooting ideas were clinician-generated rather than co-created with Laura.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice and avoided coercion by offering options and accepting Laura’s refusal of tracking or affirming phrases. Some directive language still framed the plan as a recommended next step rather than purely Laura’s own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relied heavily on advice, psychoeducation, and structured problem-solving rather than drawing out Laura’s internal motivation. There were few open-ended questions exploring deeper values, meaning, or confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited Laura’s preferences and adjusted the plan in response to her concerns. Still, the agenda and content remained largely clinician-driven rather than genuinely shared.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura offered meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist sometimes invited commitment and ability language. The clinician did not systematically deepen or expand Laura’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responded to sustain talk gently, reducing shame and pressure around missing a night or tracking. Some earlier tracking and monitoring language risked reinforcing a performance-oriented frame.
