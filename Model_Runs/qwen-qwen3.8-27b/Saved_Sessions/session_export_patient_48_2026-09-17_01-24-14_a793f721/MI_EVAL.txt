# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental tone while normalizing Farid’s fatigue and late-night phone use. It falls short of a 5 because some statements feel slightly evaluative or interpretive rather than fully affirming unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong reflective listening, especially in capturing Farid’s conflict between needing late-night quiet and knowing it harms his sleep. The score is reduced because he sometimes shifts too quickly from emotional content, such as fear of failure, into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a coherent focus on fatigue, sleep, and late-night phone use as relevant barriers to activity. However, it does not consistently connect that barrier work back to the original target behavior of increasing physical activity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats the session as a joint exploration and follows Farid’s lead in identifying key concerns. Some moments become more expert-driven when he proposes specific strategies or categories rather than continuing pure co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and avoids coercion, allowing Farid to contribute ideas about his own evenings and habits. It is limited because he does not explicitly affirm Farid’s freedom to choose or ownership over what he is willing to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tries to elicit Farid’s own ideas instead of giving reasons to change, but the questions often move toward practical problem-solving. There is limited deep exploration of Farid’s values or internal motivation behind wanting to move more.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Farid as the expert on his own life and invites his input in shaping the conversation. The partnership is not fully power-shared because he occasionally steers toward specific interventions or suggests what Farid needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some efforts to draw out change-oriented ideas, such as asking what Farid would want to explore and how he might protect his energy. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify Farid’s statements about wanting to move more or use stronger change-talk strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Farid’s sustain talk and offers useful normalizations about the feed and fatigue. Still, several questions continue to focus on barriers, and he does not consistently use double-sided reflections or other techniques to reduce the dominance of sustain talk.
