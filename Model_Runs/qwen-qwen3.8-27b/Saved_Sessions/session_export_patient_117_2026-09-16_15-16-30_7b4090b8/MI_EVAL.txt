# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and continues reflecting Samuel's concerns even when he expresses doubt. Acceptance is solid but somewhat procedural, with less explicit normalization or affirmation at moments of self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong double-sided reflections accurately track Samuel's ambivalence, gains, and worries. The empathy could be deeper by more directly reflecting the emotional meaning behind statements such as feeling graded or worried about lack of self-control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing smoking, especially in high-risk work and lunch contexts. The conversation moves clearly from journal review to identifying cues, selecting alternatives, defining success, and building a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as an equal partner and builds the plan around Samuel's own ideas, such as the gum cue, simple tally, and backup plan. Samuel's contributions substantially guide the discussion, reflecting strong joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by using open questions and avoiding direct advice, commands, or pressure. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit affirmation that the decision remains entirely Samuel's, including how to interpret success or setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Samuel's own ideas and practical motivation for change. Evocation would be stronger by exploring deeper personal values, meaning, or confidence related to managing work stress and smoking reduction.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Samuel to contribute his own expertise and uses his suggestions to shape the session. Partnership is clear but mostly implicit, with less explicit acknowledgment of Samuel's expertise or shared control over agenda and pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions successfully evoke multiple change-talk statements, including goals for reducing cigarettes, using gum as a cue, and defining proof of progress. The psychiatrist could further amplify and deepen change talk more consistently as it emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to Samuel's doubts without challenging, arguing, or dismissing them. Sustain talk is handled appropriately, but responses could be more strategic by using double-sided reflections that acknowledge both the function of smoking and the desire to change.
