# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone throughout. Acceptance is somewhat task-focused and would be stronger with deeper, individualized validation of Erik's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Erik's functional self-view, resentment at being labeled sick, and desire to avoid medication. Empathy is good but often cognitive rather than deeply exploring underlying fear, family concerns, or identity threat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on diet, alcohol reduction, and diabetes-related goals. Direction becomes slightly checklist-like when multiple additional medical items are introduced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Erik's input and helps build a practical plan around his preferences. Collaboration is strong but not fully equal because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Erik's choice-making and avoids overt pressure. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by repeated therapist-generated plans and doctor-centered framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik verbalizes motivation and confidence, but the psychiatrist often supplies reasons, scripts, and medical rationale first. Evocation would be stronger with deeper exploration of Erik's own values and personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik's choices shape the session, but the psychiatrist frequently takes an expert role by adding labs, scripts, and checklists. More explicit expert-reversal and pauses after Erik's suggestions are needed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and affirms concrete change talk, including a snack swap, limiting alcohol, and a six-week trial. Cultivation would be stronger with more systematic elicitation of change-related values and reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Erik's skepticism and redirects resistance toward a self-directed trial. It would be stronger if he more consistently used double-sided reflections to pivot sustain talk into change talk.
