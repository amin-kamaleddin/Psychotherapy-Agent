# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgmental regard, and low-pressure support throughout. Acceptance could be slightly warmer by explicitly affirming the client’s honesty or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses reflective listening effectively and accurately tracks the client’s ambivalence about drinking. A perfect score would require deeper exploration of the emotional meaning behind the drinking and related concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use, reducing or modifying drinking, and next steps. Direction could be stronger by consolidating a single target behavior and using more strategic summaries.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the client’s preferences shaping the plan and the experiment negotiated rather than imposed. The process becomes slightly expert-led when the psychiatrist introduces multiple clinician-generated options and clinical concepts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports autonomy by offering choices, avoiding pressure, and framing the next step as optional. Stronger reinforcement of client ownership, especially around fear of “messing up,” would improve this criterion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to elicit the client’s motivations by asking about importance, confidence, and what drinking does. More follow-up questions could better evoke the client’s own reasons, desires, and curiosity for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, partnership stance by asking permission and inviting the client’s input. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly positioning the client as the expert and co-constructing options from the client’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session obtains a small commitment, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify the client’s own change language. More evocative questions could better develop desire, ability, reasons, and need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges resistance without arguing or reinforcing sustain talk. However, the session does not consistently pivot those concerns toward values, goals, or possible change.
