# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepts the client's preference for minimal disruption. Warmth is present but somewhat procedural, with few affirmations of the client's strengths or difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's practical preferences, such as keeping the check quick and contained. However, the response misses deeper emotional themes like control, normalcy, and burden.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on elevated blood pressure and builds a clear self-monitoring plan with little drift. It is problem-solving oriented rather than fully motivationally directed, and does not explore possible alcohol links.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a plan using the client's suggestions, such as checking after coffee and using a reminder. However, the counselor remains the primary agenda driver and the client stays fairly passive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids commands and repeatedly invites the client to define what would be workable and least disruptive. It could more explicitly return ownership when the client says, 'You're the one who should say.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical preferences and a small step toward action. It does not deeply draw out the client's internal motivation, values, confidence, or what normal numbers mean.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses partner-like language and incorporates the client's ideas into the plan. Still, the counselor leads heavily through structured questions and could better return decision-making to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some change-oriented language, such as wanting normal numbers and a manageable plan. It does not consistently elicit or amplify the client's reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client's resistance and reframes the issue as a small, contained step. It could use more active MI skills, such as double-sided reflections, to gently move the conversation toward change.
