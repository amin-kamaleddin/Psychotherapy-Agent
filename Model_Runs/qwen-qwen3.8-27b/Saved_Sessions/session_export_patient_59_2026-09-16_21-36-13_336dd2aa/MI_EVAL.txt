# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance while tolerating the client's resistance and minimization. Warmth was strong but somewhat task-focused, limiting explicit affirmation of the client's broader worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client's irritation, desire for fairness, and practical concerns. Some emotional themes, such as shame or fear of being labeled a repeat offender, could have been explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moved clearly from broad concerns to a practical compliance plan. However, focus remained more on logistics, probation, and family dynamics than on the specific marijuana-related behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was strongly co-constructed, with the client choosing key details such as curfew, phone placement, and communication rules. The clinician effectively worked with the client as a partner rather than imposing a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized the client's choice, control, and ownership of the plan. There was no pressure or coercive language, and the plan was framed as limited, fair, and client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client was prompted to contribute practical solutions, but deeper internal motivation, values, and readiness were not consistently drawn out. More evocative questions about importance, confidence, or future goals would have strengthened this dimension.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positioned the client as the primary source of his own plan and preferences. The partnership was strong but remained largely implicit rather than explicitly stated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged, especially around keeping the record clean and proving change, but it was not consistently reinforced or amplified. More strategic reflections or questions could have strengthened internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Resistance was handled well by rolling with it rather than arguing or confronting. The minimization of the marijuana behavior was acknowledged, but it was not fully transformed into discrepancy or stronger change talk.
