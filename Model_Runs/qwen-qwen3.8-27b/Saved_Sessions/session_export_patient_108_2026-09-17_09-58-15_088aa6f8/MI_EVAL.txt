# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental regard throughout the session. Ambivalence and self-critical labels are received without challenge and reframed in terms of care and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and captures both content and emotional meaning, including ambivalence, fear, and the meaning of the client's metaphors. A few interpretations are slightly ahead of the client but remain grounded and client-centered.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on setting a boundary and asking the flatmate to move out, moving from ambivalence toward more concrete action-related questions. It could be stronger by ending with a more explicitly negotiated action plan, such as a specific date or rehearsed statement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds effectively on the client's language and metaphors, creating a collaborative working stance. Some therapist-generated suggestions and sequencing limit the degree of full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and self-direction by inviting the client to identify what feels doable and true. Occasional therapist-led suggestions reduce the extent to which the client is positioned as the sole author of the next step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client's own motivations and generates meaningful change talk, including increased confidence and a sense of lightness. More systematic eliciting of reasons, ability, need, and confidence would make this area stronger.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as an expert and invites his perspective, with the client's language substantially guiding the session. More explicit power sharing, such as agenda-setting or offering choices before suggesting ideas, would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client produces clear change talk, and the psychiatrist creates space for it through open questions and reflections. The session would benefit from more consistent and targeted MI techniques to deepen desire, ability, reasons, need, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by normalizing concerns and reframing them without arguing or minimizing. Some reflections of sustain talk could be followed more consistently by active change-direction or double-sided reflections to move the conversation forward.
