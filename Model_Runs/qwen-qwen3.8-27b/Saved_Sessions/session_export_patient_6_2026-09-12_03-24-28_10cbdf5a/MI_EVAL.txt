# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout. Validation was clear, though a few reflections were more evaluative than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated strong reflective listening and attunement to the client’s emotions. Responses were generally accurate and validating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stayed focused on the client’s goals and the problem behavior. Direction was clear, though it occasionally moved quickly toward solutions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician worked with the client as a partner rather than as an authority. Some suggestions were practical, but the exchange remained collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offered choices and invited the client to take ownership of next steps. Support for autonomy was strong, though advice was provided fairly directly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician drew out some client motivation and personal reasons for change. More evocative questions would have helped the client generate stronger change talk independently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction reflected a cooperative, respectful working relationship. The clinician generally let the client lead while offering guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was present and reinforced at times. The clinician could have further amplified and reflected the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged, but at times the clinician addressed it too directly. A more indirect reflection would better reduce defensiveness.
