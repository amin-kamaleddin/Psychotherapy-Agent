# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Rosa’s ambivalence and concerns. The warmth is supportive but remains somewhat procedural and task-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and helpful, capturing key worries about weight, cost, cravings, and social reactions. It could go deeper into identity-related feelings such as not wanting to seem “weird.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on smoking cessation and moves toward a concrete 3-day patch plan. Direction is clear, though it could allow more client-led exploration before problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist seeks permission, offers options, and co-constructs a practical plan with Rosa. Collaboration is evident, but most solutions and content are still supplied by the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rosa is invited to choose and her decisions, such as the 3-day trial and keeping coffee steady, are respected. Frequent prescriptive advice and structured plans somewhat reduce autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions are used effectively to explore importance and confidence. However, the session shifts quickly into advice-giving rather than deeply evoking Rosa’s own values, motivations, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa’s choices influence the plan, and the counselor asks which options feel doable or safest. The psychiatrist still positions themselves as the main source of solutions, limiting full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerges, including Rosa agreeing to a 3-day patch trial and hoping it works. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or consolidate this commitment by exploring confidence, past successes, or personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully rather than dismissed. MI strategies to soften barriers, such as double-sided reflections or elicit-change responses, are used inconsistently, with frequent shifts to practical advice.
