# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and avoids judgment, but repeated risk-focused questioning and advice can feel administrative rather than warm. Acceptance is present but inconsistent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Ryan’s mixed emotions, validation, and internal experience. Empathy is strong, though sometimes interrupted by advice-giving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on the drinking incident and safety, but drifts into rehearsing phrases without consistently linking back to Ryan’s goals. Direction would benefit from stronger connection to values and consequences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and co-creates practical steps with Ryan. Frequent structured advice slightly reduces the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ryan is consistently invited to choose his own pace, plan, and language. Repeated safety questions and lists can feel directive, but autonomy is well supported overall.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions but often supplies explanations and harm-reduction options before deeply drawing out Ryan’s own reasons for change. A stronger focus on Ryan’s academic values would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Ryan’s lead and avoids taking over the conversation. However, frequent advice-giving makes the role feel more instructor-like than fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ryan’s own change-oriented statements, such as pacing and not taking shots, are affirmed and built upon. The psychiatrist could elicit deeper values-based change talk to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without confrontation or argument, and Ryan’s concerns are validated. The main area for growth is more consistently linking sustain talk to discrepancy.
