# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoided blame despite the client’s resistance. The main weakness was repeatedly not fully honoring her request for silence, which made the acceptance feel somewhat intrusive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made consistent reflective efforts to understand the client’s guardedness and annoyance. However, empathy was weakened by over-interpreting minimal responses and repeatedly filling the silence rather than tolerating it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remained focused on engagement and rapport-building without drifting into unrelated topics. Its limitation was a somewhat circular quality, repeatedly asking what the client wanted rather than making a clear, low-pressure proposal and following through.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and offered choices, showing an intent to share control. Collaboration became less effective when the counselor continued to prompt after the client clearly selected quiet.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Many autonomy-supportive statements were used, such as “no pressure” and “your choice.” However, the counselor’s continued verbal presence after the client asked for quiet undercut the expressed support for autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was one initial evocative question, but evocation remained superficial after that. The psychiatrist mostly offered external options rather than drawing out the client’s own reasons, values, or subtle signals of willingness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempted a partnership stance by following the client’s lead and offering options. In practice, the repeated prompts and continued speaking made the session feel less balanced and more counselor-steered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was very limited effort to build change talk, especially around the client’s need for control, boundaries, or reasons for tolerating the session. The counselor mostly responded directly to resistance rather than using reflections to create a path toward engagement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with or reinforcing the client’s sustain talk, using reflection and apology instead. The limitation was a mostly neutral stance without more strategic moves to bridge resistance toward willingness or engagement.
