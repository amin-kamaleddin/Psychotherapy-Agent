# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, validating the client's burden without criticizing her drinking. The acceptance could be more individualized and emotionally grounded.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate empathic understanding, particularly early in the session, but empathy is inconsistent in depth. Important emotional statements about feeling like a 'ghost' next to her son could have been reflected more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use while addressing related stress and sleep. There is little topic drift, and safety concerns are appropriately addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts the plan based on the client's feedback, which supports collaboration. However, the interaction remains largely clinician-led rather than fully mutually co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering options, simplifying the plan, and respecting the client's stated limits. It could more explicitly emphasize that the client remains in charge of what she wants to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions and preferences, but does not deeply explore the client's own motivations, values, or confidence in change. More open-ended exploration of her reasons would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is real collaboration, but the psychiatrist frequently leads with concrete plans and repeated reminders. The session would benefit from more client-led direction and explicit recognition of her expertise in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client's willingness and small steps, but does not consistently expand or amplify her change talk. More questions about her values, confidence, and vision for change would strengthen this skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without argument or invalidation, gently acknowledging the client's need for something to unwind. The skill could be deeper by more consistently pivoting sustain talk toward the client's own values and goals.
