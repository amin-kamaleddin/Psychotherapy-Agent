# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout, consistently affirming Tariq’s frustration and concerns. The repeated use of the phrase “I hear you” becomes somewhat formulaic, limiting the depth of individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are strong reflective statements that accurately capture Tariq’s fear, urgency, and need for dignity, including the reflection that he wants to “feel like a normal kid.” However, the reflections are repetitive and do not consistently explore the deeper emotional pain beneath his anger.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear broad framing that connects the immediate issue to compliance, stress management, and release planning. However, the psychiatrist quickly narrows the focus to the immediate task of securing a phone call, losing strategic momentum toward larger therapeutic goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and seeks Tariq’s input on specific decisions, including wording and whether to involve the caseworker. However, the collaboration remains somewhat one-directional, with the psychiatrist generating options and acting as the primary problem-solver rather than an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Tariq’s autonomy by asking for permission, offering choices, and avoiding coercive or pressuring language. The statement “I can’t promise the outcome” is a strong example of honest, non-coercive communication that respects Tariq’s right to make his own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is only one significant evocative question early in the session, after which the psychiatrist shifts into problem-solving and advocacy mode. The psychiatrist does not deeply explore Tariq’s values, hopes, strengths, or reasons for wanting to manage frustration and prepare for release.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Tariq’s input and offers multiple choices, demonstrating a collaborative stance. However, the psychiatrist drives the agenda by structuring options and making decisions, which positions them more as a facilitator than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes minimal efforts to evoke change talk, relying primarily on a single initial evocative question. The session quickly shifts to problem-solving and option selection rather than eliciting Tariq’s own desires, abilities, reasons, needs, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately validates Tariq’s frustration with Officer Miller without reinforcing the blame or becoming confrontational. However, the psychiatrist does not actively use MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or gentle reframing to soften the sustain talk or explore ambivalence.
