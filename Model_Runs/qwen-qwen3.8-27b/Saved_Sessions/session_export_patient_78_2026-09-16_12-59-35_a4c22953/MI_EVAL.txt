# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoids criticizing the client’s resistance. Acceptance is slightly limited by scripted warmth and less deep emotional engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects core themes such as frustration, low confidence, and concern for her daughter. However, reflections remain surface-level and repetitive rather than deeply exploring emotional discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of managing smoking urges at work. The focus is generally clear, though repeated plan-checking slightly dilutes momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and follows the client’s preferences for gum and Tuesday. However, the session remains somewhat counselor-led by repeatedly proposing a specific plan and checking whether she will try it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s boundaries by accepting no check-ins and emphasizing no pressure. Autonomy support is limited by repeated task framing and confidence ratings that can feel evaluative.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that elicit some change talk, especially around her daughter and using gum as a smaller step. However, concrete suggestions and planning reduce the evocative focus on the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, and the client’s choices shape parts of the plan. The partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently leads the structure and asks repeated questions about trying a plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out small pieces of change talk, such as using gum and wanting to avoid worrying her daughter. These moments are not consistently amplified, as the session often shifts to planning before the client’s change talk can expand.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing, such as feeling lectured or fed up with extra steps. However, repeated plans and questions keep the focus on difficulty rather than clearly redirecting toward change talk.
