# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently expresses respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance while normalizing Nathaniel's ambivalence and continued coffee use. Acceptance is strong but remains somewhat procedural and less deeply warm or explicitly individualized, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, reflective listening to capture Nathaniel's ambivalence, desire for low-effort clarity, and importance of his morning routine. The empathy is practical and cognitive but does not fully explore the deeper emotional weight of possible health damage or identity loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on coffee consumption and quickly narrows to a specific, bounded five-day plan. Some procedural detail and the unanswered final question about iced coffee make the closure less clean, but overall direction is clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan with Nathaniel by inviting input on the easiest change, duration, check-in time, and tracking method. Collaboration is strong, but the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure and clinical framework, so it is not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by asking for Nathaniel's preferences and avoiding pressure, guilt, or ultimatums. Autonomy support is consistent, but the specific plan, template, and safety instructions remain counselor-driven rather than fully non-directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Nathaniel's own language, such as wanting clarity without guilt and being open to a fair test. However, the session relies more on psychoeducation and behavioral problem-solving than on deeper MI evocation of internal motivation or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as a collaborative partner by inviting Nathaniel to set the focus, choose a small change, and decide on check-in logistics. The session is partnership-based, but the psychiatrist still introduces much of the structure, keeping it from being fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nathaniel offers several change-relevant statements about feeling wired, possible blood pressure concerns, and willingness to review data. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify this change talk or elicit deeper desire, ability, need, or plan language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to sustain talk without argument and often reframes continued coffee use as useful information rather than failure. Softening is present but not consistently strategic, and some psychoeducation may inadvertently validate the status quo.
