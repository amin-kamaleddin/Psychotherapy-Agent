# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard without lecturing or moralizing. Acceptance is strong but not yet exemplary because it is somewhat professional and reflective rather than deeply emotionally validating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of Kevin’s ambivalence and the social conflict between fitting in and protecting his body. Empathy is good but remains more cognitive than emotionally deep, especially around embarrassment, shame, and fear of being seen as the odd one out.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on drinking in social settings and protecting Kevin’s body after his injury. The work progresses coherently from ambivalence to identifying a safer approach and developing a concrete, realistic plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels collaborative, with Kevin’s ideas directly shaping the two-drink limit, swap night, dinner first, short line, and exit plan. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist occasionally introduces solution options that slightly steer the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports self-direction by emphasizing that Kevin is the expert and encouraging his own choices. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reminders that the plan is optional and entirely Kevin’s decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Kevin’s own reasons for change, especially the powerful “weird guy with the water glass” versus “broken guy with the cast” comparison. It is not a 5 because the therapist could deepen evocation by exploring values, importance, confidence, and discrepancy more explicitly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys a partnership stance and lets Kevin substantially guide the session. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist sometimes introduces specific options before Kevin has fully generated them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits meaningful change talk and builds on Kevin’s own statements about not wanting to keep hurting himself. It is strong but not exemplary because it does not consistently deepen change talk through explicit scaling, readiness, capability, or discrepancy strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and uses double-sided language to reduce the all-or-nothing feel of quitting. It is not a 5 because the therapist occasionally moves into problem-solving before fully softening or resolving the social tension.
