# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates basic respect and nonjudgment, validating Mai's frustration and hesitation. However, acceptance remains fairly intellectual and neutral, with limited deeper affirmation of her autonomy or experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are mostly content-focused and do not fully track Mai's emotions, values, or ambivalence. The counselor misses deeper meaning-level content, such as concern about invisible disease, lifelong medication, and not wanting to feel controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior, moving clearly from confusion to clarification and then to a concrete plan. The direction is somewhat psychoeducational rather than MI-oriented, with less emphasis on progressively clarifying motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaboration in that Mai contributes important details, preferences, and goals, and role-play is offered. However, the counselor's expert-driven delivery and prewritten scripts limit true co-authorship of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, shared decision-making, and client voice by asking what Mai wants and offering scripts. Some prescriptive language and highly detailed scripts still subtly reduce the sense that Mai is generating her own decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few evocative questions are asked, but the counselor mostly supplies medical facts, reasons, and language for Mai to use. Mai's own motivations and reasons for change are not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction has collaborative elements, such as asking what Mai wants to focus on and inviting her to choose a measurable goal. Still, the counselor often functions as the expert, providing scripts and medical content rather than building primarily from Mai's perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai produces useful change talk, including commitments to walking, dietary change, and an LDL goal. The counselor elicits some action language but does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing but does not effectively soften Mai's sustain talk or reconnect it to her values and autonomy. Some risk-focused statements may increase the sense of external pressure rather than supporting her own decision.
