# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and normalizing, but often moves quickly from acceptance to advice, labeling, or corrective statements. This reduces the warmth and unconditional positive regard needed for fully MI-consistent acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflects some ambivalence and fear accurately, but often shifts into interpretation or CBT-style explanations. The deeper pain, shame, isolation, and identity meaning behind the client's words are not consistently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the exam, study avoidance, and concrete next steps, which provides good momentum. However, the direction can feel mechanical and repetitive, with some drift into broader recommendations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently positions themselves as the expert providing structured techniques and menus of choices. The client's experience does not meaningfully shape the direction or agenda of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some supportive choices and nonjudgmental language, but much of the language remains directive and prescriptive. The client's freedom and ownership of the next step are not explicitly and consistently reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers more strategies and techniques than they elicit from the client. Powerful change-related statements about identity, motivation, and the meaning of the exam are not sufficiently explored or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely expert-driven and psychoeducational rather than collaborative. The client's perspective does not substantially reshape the conversation, despite a few invitations to choose among clinician-defined options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only rare and weak efforts to evoke or amplify the client's own reasons, desire, ability, or confidence for change. The counselor more often moves to behavioral experiments, scripts, and practical steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not reinforce sustain talk, but also does not consistently soften or redirect it with MI-consistent reflections. Some responses are corrective or problem-solving, which can increase resistance rather than explore the client's ambivalence.
