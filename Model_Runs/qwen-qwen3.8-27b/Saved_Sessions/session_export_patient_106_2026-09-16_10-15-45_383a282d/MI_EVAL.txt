# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental, with clear normalization of coping and affirmation of effort. It could be warmer and more explicitly unconditional if the client failed, hesitated, or abandoned the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and responsive to the client's ambivalence, stress, and preference for blunt communication. It does not fully explore deeper emotional themes such as exhaustion, fear of failing her son, or guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on a specific smoking-reduction goal and progresses logically to a concrete, actionable plan. There is little to no topic drift, making this the strongest area.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed around the client's chosen rule, coping strategy, reminder time, and wording. However, the psychiatrist remains the main architect of options rather than a fully equal partner throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with frequent choice language and no pressure to quit completely. It could be stronger by reducing specific directives and more clearly emphasizing the client's full ownership to modify or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated content, such as the indoor rule, gum strategy, and reminder wording. Evocation is uneven because the psychiatrist often supplies ideas before deeply exploring the client's own values, hopes, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client selects key elements of the plan, but the psychiatrist takes a fairly expert-driven role by repeatedly supplying concrete strategies and structures. Stronger partnership would more consistently position the client as the expert on what will work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces a practical plan and some change-oriented language, which is affirmed by the psychiatrist. Change talk is not consistently amplified through questions about values, benefits, or what the chosen step means to her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without argument, and likely lapses are normalized rather than framed as failure. More advanced MI moves, such as double-sided reflections or value-linked reframes, would make the response more strategic.
