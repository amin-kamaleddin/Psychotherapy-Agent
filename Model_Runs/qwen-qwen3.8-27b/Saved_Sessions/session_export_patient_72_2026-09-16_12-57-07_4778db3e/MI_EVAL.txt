# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone and frequently affirms Leila’s efforts. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming her worth and emotional experience beyond adherence to the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and helpful, especially around wanting low pressure, fresh air, and feeling stronger or less tired. Deeper emotional or identity-related reflections could further strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on returning to regular physical activity through small, doable steps. There is clear guidance from values to concrete planning and implementation without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Leila’s choices meaningfully shape the final plan, including timing, backup options, reminder text, and no tracking. Collaboration could be stronger if the psychiatrist invited her to generate the initial options earlier in the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers choices and respects Leila’s preference to avoid extra tracking. Some language remains prescriptive, so autonomy support would improve with more emphasis on her right to modify or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful initial questions draw out Leila’s values and confidence. Evocation is limited because the session quickly shifts to providing strategies, scripts, and implementation details instead of continuing to elicit her own reasons and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Leila’s experience and asks for input, but the interaction is largely counselor-led. More consistent invitation for Leila to generate options, prioritize steps, and lead the planning would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Leila expresses clear change talk about wanting to feel stronger, less tired, and more upright. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk before moving into logistics and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging Leila’s concerns and helps make barriers such as sales pressure, rain, and fatigue more manageable through practical alternatives. More MI-specific softening, such as double-sided reflection, would strengthen this area.
