# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance, normalizing Camila's nerves and framing a possible beer as information rather than failure. Affirmations were frequent but sometimes tied to compliance and could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate at a surface level, capturing the shift in drinking and its practical consequences. However, the emotional underpinnings—shame, social anxiety, fear of appearing weird—were not fully explored or reflected with depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained clearly focused on reducing alcohol use and on concrete targets such as skipping Tuesday's drink, capping intake, and preparing for Friday. The direction was strong but somewhat narrow, spending limited time on broader patterns or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-created the plan, including the chosen script, 8:30 check-in, and nonjudgmental debrief. However, the amount of structured advice and scripts made the interaction more coach-like than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported choice by inviting Camila to select the goal, script, reminder timing, and debrief format. Repeatedly supplying specific scripts and tactics subtly reduced opportunities for Camila to generate her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited some initial change talk, such as wanting to feel less on autopilot and to sleep better. The psychiatrist often moved into advice or logistics before deeper motivation was explored and expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was generally collaborative, with repeated invitations for Camila's input on goals, strategies, and support. The heavy use of scripts, tactics, and step-by-step instructions created a somewhat more guided dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helped Camila articulate practical goals and some commitment, such as trying the 'pacing myself' line and a check-in. Change talk was not consistently deepened, amplified, or linked to stronger personal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively softened sustain talk by normalizing social pressure, supporting a flexible plan, and reducing the fear of lapse. Use of double-sided reflections and strategic redirection of ambivalence was less consistent.
