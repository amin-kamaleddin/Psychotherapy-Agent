# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly removing judgment from the outset. Maintains a warm, patient, non-judgmental tone even when the client is at his most dismissive, with zero judgmental language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and emotionally precise, including a particularly sophisticated double reflection that names the emotional subtext beneath the client's defensiveness. Tracks identity-level concerns (autonomy vs. pleasing) with remarkable nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, purposeful arc from safety-establishment through trust dynamics toward behavior-specific evocation. The middle section on trust and mom's reactions could have been more efficiently bridged toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with the psychiatrist positioned as curious and appreciative rather than managing or directing. The client's contributions substantially shape the session's direction at multiple turning points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is woven naturally throughout without feeling formulaic, explicitly framing any change as self-directed ('on your own terms, for your own reasons'). Avoids the common error of using autonomy language as covert persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within the client rather than providing external reasons for change. The session successfully draws out genuine change talk including a powerful identity-based statement about wanting to be 'the kid who gets it.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the expert on his own experience, with reflections framed as hypotheses inviting correction. Never offers expert opinions about cannabis use or what the client 'should' do.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk, particularly around the foggy feeling and grades, leading to a strong identity-based statement. The score is not a 5 because the final, powerful change talk statement could have been more explicitly amplified and reinforced before the session ended.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively uses double-sided reflections and rolling with resistance to validate sustain talk while gently opening doors. The peer-based sustain talk ('my friends don't think it's a problem') could have been further softened through more targeted discrepancy work.
