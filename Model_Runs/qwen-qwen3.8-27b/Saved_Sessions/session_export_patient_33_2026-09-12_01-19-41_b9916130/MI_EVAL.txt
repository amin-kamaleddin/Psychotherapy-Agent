# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and nonjudgment without shaming Diego for his use. The warmth is somewhat procedural and could include more consistent emotional validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist gives brief validation but misses opportunities to accurately reflect Diego's fear, shame, and emotional experience. Responses remain largely factual and logistical rather than deeply reflective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on heroin-related harm reduction. It effectively guides Diego toward concrete, behaviorally specific safety steps without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well on the housemate communication and resource planning. However, the session is at times expert-driven, with the clinician supplying complete checklists and scripts rather than co-creating all elements.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego's choices are respected, including his focus on safety and decision not to share his ZIP code. Some prescriptive instructions and agenda-setting limit fully autonomy-supportive practice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers an initial open question but then shifts into advice and information delivery. There are limited efforts to elicit Diego's own motivations, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego actively shapes the session by choosing the focus, message style, and next steps. The partnership is somewhat weakened by the psychiatrist's expert-driven delivery of information and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego expresses clear motivation for harm reduction, and the psychiatrist reinforces it with affirmations. The evocation is moderate, with few questions exploring the importance, confidence, or meaning behind his change-oriented steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Diego's ambivalence without arguing or moralizing. The response could be stronger by using balanced reflections to actively work with his reasons for maintaining the status quo.
