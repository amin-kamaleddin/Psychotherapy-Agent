# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly normalizes Farid's fatigue and self-doubt. The score is limited because some validation feels slightly corrective rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Farid's ambivalence, exhaustion, and fear of failure. The score is limited because the reflections remain somewhat repetitive and do not consistently deepen into underlying values or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete, manageable target behavior: increasing physical activity through a brief walk. The score is limited because attention shifts toward implementation details without consistently linking the behavior to Farid's broader motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting Farid's input and adjusting the plan based on his feedback. The score is limited because the counselor frequently supplies many options, making the process feel more guided than mutually generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasizes choice, low pressure, and a no-guilt experimental approach. The score is limited because repeated invitations to commit can create subtle pressure, slightly reducing space for Farid's autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts to elicit Farid's motivations, confidence, and preferences. The score is limited because the counselor often provides concrete solutions instead of drawing out Farid's own ideas and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally invites Farid's input and respects his preferences. The score is limited because the counselor often assumes an expert-driven problem-solving role by generating multiple concrete options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and scaling to invite change talk. The score is limited because Farid's change-related statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or strategically developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Farid's resistance and uses normalizing language. The score is limited because the counselor does not consistently redirect sustain talk toward the change side using double-sided reflections or evocative questions.
