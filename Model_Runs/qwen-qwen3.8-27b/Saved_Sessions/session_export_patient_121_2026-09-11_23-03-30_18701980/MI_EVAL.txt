# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and validates the client’s feelings in several moments. However, prescriptive scripts and implicit expectations to perform coping behaviors weaken unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflects unfairness, isolation, and exhaustion, but often stays at a surface level. Deeper ambivalence, fear, shame, and the pain of being blamed are not consistently captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally remains focused on peer conflict, school distress, and attendance coping. Direction is weakened by repeated narrowing into specific scripts and logistics rather than a clear change-focused arc.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor offers options and asks for preferences, but often responds with new scripts and structured plans. The client’s ideas do not consistently shape the session as an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are occasional invitations to choose, such as asking which option feels doable. Frequent directive scripts and step-by-step plans reduce the client’s sense of control and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial open-ended question is appropriate, but the counselor mostly provides reasons and suggested behaviors. There is limited eliciting of the client’s own values, strengths, and internal motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely expert-driven and problem-solving rather than collaborative. The client is not consistently positioned as the primary source of wisdom about her own life and change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor makes only limited efforts to evoke or amplify the client’s own change talk. There are few open-ended questions or reflections that deepen the client’s stated goals and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids direct confrontation but does not effectively soften or redirect the client’s sustain talk. Ambivalence is tolerated rather than explored with skillful double-sided reflections.
