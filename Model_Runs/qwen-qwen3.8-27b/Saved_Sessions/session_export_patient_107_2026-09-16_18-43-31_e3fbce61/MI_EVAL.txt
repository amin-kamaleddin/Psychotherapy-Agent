# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and accepting stance, repeatedly validating Nathaniel’s perspective without disapproval. However, the warmth can feel somewhat formulaic, and the opening slightly overstates the client’s level of distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Nathaniel’s ambivalence, practical concerns, and internal tension. The main limitation is that empathy remains more cognitive than deeply emotional, with less exploration of the meaning or fear behind losing coffee’s familiar support.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on coffee consumption and moves logically from ambivalence to a small experiment and safety plan. It could be stronger if the focus were more consistently tied to Nathaniel’s deeper goals, values, or motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by building on Nathaniel’s own ideas, such as a time limit, backup beverage, and one-time blood pressure snapshot. The process is effective but still somewhat structured by the clinician rather than fully equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by avoiding pressure, advice, and urgency, and by framing change as optional and client-directed. Autonomy could be reinforced further by explicitly affirming that Nathaniel is in charge of the decision either way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Nathaniel’s own ideas, plans, and criteria for trying a small change. Evocation could be deeper by eliciting stronger values, fears, hopes, or more meaningful change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites Nathaniel’s input rather than assuming the expert role. Partnership could improve by more explicitly recognizing Nathaniel’s authority over his own agenda and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session does evoke useful change talk, such as skipping the second cup, using a backup plan, and possibly cutting back one cup. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen Nathaniel’s own change language or connect it to deeper values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with Nathaniel’s sustain talk, but several responses validate the status quo without gently pivoting toward change. Stronger use of double-sided reflections could balance caution with concern about jitters, blood pressure, or long-term health.
