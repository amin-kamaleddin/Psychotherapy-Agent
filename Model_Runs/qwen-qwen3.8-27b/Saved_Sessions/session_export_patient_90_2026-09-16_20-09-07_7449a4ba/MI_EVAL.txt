# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, even when Marcus expresses frustration or fear of burdening others. Statements validate Marcus’s goals and life context without disapproval, blame, or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Marcus’s ambivalence, values, and relational concerns, especially the tension between health responsibility and protecting his routine. Reflections go beyond paraphrase and capture underlying self-respect and care for his wife and dog.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on a concrete, clinically relevant asthma-management behavior. The conversation moves purposefully from broad manageability to a specific, actionable plan involving the remote-card reminder.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and joint problem-solving, integrating Marcus’s wife, dog, finances, and daily routine into the plan. The interaction feels collaborative rather than prescriptive, with the counselor acting as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supports Marcus’s sense of choice by avoiding pressure, coercion, or implied obligation. The emerging plan is presented as something Marcus can adapt, try, or decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from Marcus’s own values rather than imposed through advice or risk-focused lecturing. Questions elicit his reasons for change, such as staying out of the ER and keeping his dog and routine intact.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treats Marcus as the expert on his own life and repeatedly asks for his judgment. Marcus’s ideas substantially shape the direction of the session, from the reminder idea to how to ask his wife for help.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and amplifies Marcus’s own change talk, including his goals, success criteria, and commitment language. Affirming reflections reinforce the client’s emerging motivation without imposing reasons to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor handles Marcus’s concerns about cost, appointments, and “another thing” without arguing or minimizing them. Sustain talk is acknowledged and gently redirected toward a manageable, low-burden action.
