# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, warm, and individualized acceptance throughout, including a masterful moment of respecting Elias's boundary ('Fair. No poetry'). No judgment, labeling, or conditional regard is present anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Displays exceptional empathic accuracy with complex, emotionally attuned reflections that go well beyond paraphrasing. Particularly strong in capturing the grief of losing an identity built around struggle and in recalibrating when the client pushes back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on recovery and identity in sobriety with a logical progression, though occasionally drifts into abstract territory. A tighter anchor to specific behavioral goals or relapse-prevention strategies throughout would strengthen the direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplifies high-level collaboration through genuine co-construction of meaning, with the 'Tuesday' thread developed through mutual dialogue. The psychiatrist builds on Elias's own metaphors rather than imposing external frameworks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports autonomy by presenting options, asking for Elias's own perspective, and never giving advice or directives. Autonomy support is woven naturally into every question and reflection rather than being performative.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation from within Elias rather than imposing it, with all reasons for change emerging from the client himself. Could be stronger by more systematically targeting all four DRAV categories, particularly ability and confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates from a genuinely equal power dynamic, explicitly sharing vulnerability ('So here's where I'm stuck with you') and consistently returning the question to Elias. The psychiatrist never positions themselves as having the answer.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits significant change talk ('Practice at being someone who stays') through well-designed questions and effective amplification. Could be further developed by reflecting back change talk to deepen it or exploring confidence in maintaining it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles ambivalence about motivation and the pull of the old identity with remarkable skill, never arguing or dismissing. The reframing 'You're not flattening. You're just not screaming anymore' is a brilliant softening move that acknowledges the pull without framing it as a warning sign.
