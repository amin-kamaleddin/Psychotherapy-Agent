# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout; the therapist never judges, labels, or expresses disapproval, meeting resistance with consistent warmth and validation. Language is consistently affirming of Seamus as a person rather than a problem to be solved.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture the emotional and identity-level core of Seamus's statements, such as naming the weight of being seen as 'defined by thirty-odd years of smoking' by someone close. The therapist accurately distinguishes somatic fear responses from pathology without overstepping.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Coherent, purposeful, and client-paced structure that moves naturally from rapport through dental concerns to a concrete actionable step. Could have more explicitly tied the dental work back to the smoking-cessation goal within an MI frame, though the therapist's restraint is arguably appropriate given the client's resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully egalitarian power dynamic with genuine co-construction of understanding; the therapist explicitly cedes expertise where they don't have it and follows Seamus's stated priorities without resistance. The client's contributions substantively shape the session's direction and content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional and consistent emphasis on Seamus's right to choose, with no use of 'should,' 'need to,' or 'you have to' language. Every statement about choice is genuinely open with no strings attached, and boundaries are immediately honored without passive resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Seamus's own motivations, decisions, and problem-solving without providing external reasons for change. Somewhat limited in explicitly targeting smoking-specific change talk, though the therapist's restraint is appropriate for the client's precontemplation stage.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary positioning of Seamus as the expert on his own needs, with questions consistently framed to invite his own judgment rather than direct him. The therapist cedes agenda control when Seamus redirects to his teeth, doing so without passive-aggressive resistance or guilt.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively identifies and amplifies change talk embedded in Seamus's own statements (e.g., 'that "for now" tells me you're keeping the option alive'). Score is limited by the session's brevity and the client's stage, though the therapist's restraint in not forcing more smoking-related change talk is appropriate MI strategy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully validates sustain talk without arguing, contradicting, or minimizing, and avoids the common trap of countering with 'but it could work differently this time.' Could be stronger with more explicit double-sided reflections that hold both sustain and change talk simultaneously.
