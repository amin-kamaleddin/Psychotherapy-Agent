# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, affirming Isabelle's request that the session not feel punitive. Acceptance is solid but somewhat procedural rather than deeply affirming of her strengths or identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and summaries, but they remain mainly factual. There are missed opportunities to reflect underlying feelings, such as discomfort with being flagged or pressure from school and band.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is organized and responsive, but it shifts from the alcohol-risk referral to IB workload and planning. Direction is productive, though not consistently tied to the primary MI target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a practical study plan and invites Isabelle's preferences throughout. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still retains much of the structure and agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks permission, respects boundaries, and supports Isabelle's choice around follow-up. This is one of the strongest MI behaviors in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses helpful questions that elicit Isabelle's goals and a small success measure for academic planning. However, evocation of her motivations, ambivalence, or values regarding alcohol risk is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Isabelle's input and allows her preferences to shape the session. Partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist still directs some of the planning process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is limited, especially regarding the target alcohol-use risk. The session elicits planning language for academics but does not effectively elicit or amplify client language favoring change in alcohol risk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing or challenging Isabelle's minimization of alcohol use, which keeps the session nonadversarial. However, the sustain talk is not actively reframed or softened to open space for change.
