# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects David's ambivalence and practical limits without judgment or pressure. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with less deep unconditional regard for David as a person beyond the change process.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of David's stress, need for breaks, and fear of failure. Empathy is strong but stays mostly at the content level and does not fully explore deeper meaning or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking and a practical next step with NRT. The target behavior is slightly blurred between harm reduction and full cessation, but the direction is still clear and workable.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is shaped by David's preferences, such as cinnamon gum, first break tomorrow, and pocket storage. Collaboration is good, though the psychiatrist remains the main source of NRT method and instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms David's choice and avoids forcing a quit date or appointment. Autonomy support is strong, though some directive NRT instructions make the interaction more advisory than fully self-directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions about value, worry, and what would help tomorrow. Evocation is present but practical and shallow, with limited exploration of confidence, values, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, collaborative stance and respects David's decisions. Partnership is good, but the psychiatrist still functions as an information provider rather than fully centering David as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as trying gum on the first break and wanting a less annoying option. The main weakness is insufficient amplification, reflection, or summarizing of David's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without arguing, lecturing, or reinforcing resistance. The psychiatrist does not fully use double-sided reflections to turn ambivalence into a stronger exploration of change.
