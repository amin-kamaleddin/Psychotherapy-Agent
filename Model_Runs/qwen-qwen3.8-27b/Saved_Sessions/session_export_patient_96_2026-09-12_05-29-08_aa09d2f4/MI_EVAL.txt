# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive. However, the warmth is somewhat formulaic and at times over-aligns with the client rather than individualizing validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate for surface frustration and unfairness. It does not consistently reach deeper themes of autonomy, identity, competence, or feeling controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear, mutually agreed focus on the referral target. It drifts toward frustration with the dentist, boundary-setting, and scripts rather than oral health risk or decision-making.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through offers of choice and invitations for input. The interaction is uneven because the psychiatrist repeatedly supplies direct medical answers and proposed options after the client declines them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports the client's autonomy and avoids forcing removal. Repeated suggestions for evidence, second opinions, boundaries, or scripts can subtly steer the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open questions evoke frustration rather than internal motivation for change. There is little exploration of values, self-efficacy, long-term oral health, or possible reasons to reconsider.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some power-sharing by asking what would help and offering choices. However, the partnership is inconsistent and often interrupted by expert-led advice and factual information.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk are weak and rare. The psychiatrist does not use scaling, decisional balance, or open questions to draw out reasons for reducing risk or reconsidering.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly reinforces the client's decision to keep the piercing and monitor. There is no use of double-sided reflections or gentle exploration of ambivalence, which weakens the MI process.
