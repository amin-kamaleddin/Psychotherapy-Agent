# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the patient's ambivalence. However, the warmth becomes somewhat formulaic as the session shifts quickly into advice-giving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is basic reflective listening and acknowledgment of practical concerns. Empathy remains mostly surface-level, with limited exploration of underlying feelings such as frustration, fear, or vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of preventing anger escalation and reducing risky alcohol use. The focus is clear, though a stronger MI emphasis would more consistently link the behavior to values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites patient input and builds the plan around stated preferences. However, the interaction remains expert-driven, with many tools and scripts supplied by the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient is asked to choose doable steps, which supports some autonomy. Repeated directive safety statements reduce the emphasis on self-direction and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are brief open prompts, but the psychiatrist mostly provides tools and instructions rather than drawing out motivation. The patient's reasons for change are not consistently explored or strengthened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's preferences influence the plan, and the psychiatrist asks which options feel doable. The interaction, however, remains more counselor-led than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is acknowledged and reinforced. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to deepen it by exploring values, confidence, or the meaning of change to the patient.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not argued with and is acknowledged calmly. The session does not effectively reframe or resolve ambivalence, often returning to managing drinking rather than shifting toward change.
