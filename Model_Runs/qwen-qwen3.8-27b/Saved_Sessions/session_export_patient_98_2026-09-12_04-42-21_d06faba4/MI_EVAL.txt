# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, validating Mateo’s frustration, pain, and effort. This is not a 5 because the acceptance could more explicitly name and affirm the emotional weight of his final disclosure about feeling fragile and exhausted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflections that capture ambivalence, fatigue, pain, and fear of being dismissed. This is not a 5 because deeper emotional content is sometimes moved past too quickly and not fully explored, especially the meaning of standing up without feeling like he will “fall apart.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays connected to the target behavior of rehab-related movement and pain barriers. However, it drifts into messaging and communication logistics, which reduces focus on actual physical activity change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively on drafting a short message and adapts well to Mateo’s preferences. This is not a 5 because the collaboration is still driven by psychiatrist-generated scripts rather than primarily by Mateo’s own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Mateo’s control over pace, choices, and boundaries, and does not pressure him to send, rehearse, or escalate. This is not a 5 because the psychiatrist could more explicitly reinforce Mateo’s ownership of the overall decision and his body’s limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evoking questions that invite Mateo to identify what matters and what would help. However, the psychiatrist often supplies external solutions, scripts, and options instead of continuing to draw out Mateo’s internal motivation, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and incorporates Mateo’s preferences, such as keeping the message short and avoiding a pain number. However, the session is still led by the psychiatrist’s expert problem-solving rather than by Mateo’s own agenda and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo expresses some change-relevant language, but the psychiatrist does not consistently expand, reflect, or amplify it. The conversation quickly shifts to external problem-solving instead of developing Mateo’s own reasons, confidence, or commitment to move.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Mateo’s discouragement, fear of dismissal, and worry about making a scene without arguing. However, the response does not consistently use advanced MI strategies to balance sustain talk with change talk or reduce the salience of the “what if they dismiss me” concern.
