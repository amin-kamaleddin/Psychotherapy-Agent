# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates nonjudgmental acceptance and validates Nabil's anger, frustration, and apology. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper by more individualized affirmation of his specific struggle, values, and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking regarding Nabil's bind between respect, work demands, and anger. It falls short of a 5 because some reflections are repetitive/general and miss opportunities to probe shame, apology patterns, or fear of the boss's reaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to the presenting concern of anger related to waiting and feeling disrespected. However, it lacks a clearly defined target behavior or concrete, actionable plan for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting Nabil's input and building on his ideas, such as the clinic text and being told the truth. The collaboration is strong but not fully equal because the therapist still controls the agenda and question sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives and pressure, supporting Nabil's freedom to choose his own responses. A 5 would require more explicit reinforcement that any decision—coping, communicating, or not acting—is his to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some of Nabil's values, sensations, and potential solutions by asking for his perspective. It does not consistently elicit or strengthen strong DARN/CRAF change talk such as desire, confidence, reasons, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Nabil as the expert on his own experience and repeatedly asks for his perspective rather than giving expert advice. The partnership is strong but could be enhanced by more explicit co-created agenda and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-relevant statements, such as not wanting to blow up and wanting to be treated like a person. These statements are not consistently reinforced, amplified, or developed into a coherent internal motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and uses a useful double-sided reflection. It does not consistently soften sustain talk by redirecting toward Nabil's agency, change options, or protection of his goals.
