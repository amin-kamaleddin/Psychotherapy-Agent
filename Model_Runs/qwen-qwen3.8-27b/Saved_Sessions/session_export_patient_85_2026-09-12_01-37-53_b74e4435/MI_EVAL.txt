# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and uses supportive language, but acceptance is not consistently expressed as unconditional positive regard. Praise is often tied to plan compliance rather than affirming the client's effort and honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures some surface frustration but misses deeper emotional conflict. The psychiatrist does not fully reflect the client's pride, disappointment, ambivalence about loving the juice, and feeling of trying hard yet failing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing orange juice intake and stays on target with substitutions, tracking, and the Friday check-in. Direction is somewhat rigid and checklist-like, limiting a more fluid, client-led exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes meaningfully to the plan, but the psychiatrist frequently leads with expert-driven suggestions and practical steps. Collaboration would be stronger if the plan were more clearly co-constructed from the client's perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes some autonomy-supportive questions and avoids forcing commitments. However, many uninvited practical suggestions and tracking requirements reduce the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some practical change-related statements, but the approach is often replaced by direct advice and psychoeducation. Key opportunities to draw out the client's values, confidence, and motivations were not fully used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for client input, but the interaction often feels guided by the clinician's practical agenda. Partnership would be stronger if the client were more consistently positioned as the expert on his own habits and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk, including confidence and control over what he puts in his body. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or strategically develop this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's sustain talk about loving the juice and its place in his day is acknowledged only minimally. There is little active softening, double-sided reflection, or redirection toward the client's goals.
