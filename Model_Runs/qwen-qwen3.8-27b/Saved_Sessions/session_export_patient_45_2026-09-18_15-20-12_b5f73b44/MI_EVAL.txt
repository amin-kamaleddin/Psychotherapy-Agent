# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm throughout, with multiple affirmations and normalization of Erik's experience. Occasionally reads as somewhat formulaic (e.g., repeated use of 'I hear you'), preventing it from reaching the depth of individualized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong empathic understanding with sophisticated complex reflections that capture the emotional meaning behind Erik's resistance. Occasionally reflects the intellectual/cognitive content rather than exploring the deeper emotional layers (fear of identity loss, grief of change) beneath the surface.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression from acknowledging the diagnosis through exploring ambivalence to a concrete first step, with good momentum toward change. The food/diet thread is introduced but somewhat dropped after the beer focus is established, and a more explicit acknowledgment of this choice would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing with language that invites co-creation and builds on Erik's ideas rather than imposing the psychiatrist's own. Could be elevated by more explicit invitations for Erik to shape the session direction (e.g., 'Where would you like to go with this?').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support; the psychiatrist consistently reinforces Erik's ownership of change and never prescribes, controls, or argues. Directly results in Erik internalizing motivation ('I'm doing this for me. Not because some doc told me to').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently skilled evocation of Erik's own motivations, reasons for change, and implementation plans through well-timed questions. The final internalized motivation statement is a clear example of successfully evoked intrinsic change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance positioning Erik as the expert in his own life, with clear language that expertise resides with the client. Could be stronger with more explicit power-sharing language and equal co-creation moments that would warrant a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** One of the strongest aspects of the session, with skillful highlighting and amplification of Erik's own agency, motivation, and self-generated plans. The psychiatrist consistently reflects Erik's own words back to reinforce his sense of ownership and capability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by holding multiple perspectives without arguing or contradicting, and explores underlying needs rather than pushing back. Could be more strategically active in acknowledging difficulty before pivoting in certain moments.
