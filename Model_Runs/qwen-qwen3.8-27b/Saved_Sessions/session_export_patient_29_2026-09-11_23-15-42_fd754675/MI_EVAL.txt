# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates the client’s concerns about stress, medication, and social pressure. Warmth is present but does not always become deeply individualized or emotionally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows understanding of the client’s perspective with statements like “You’re right to worry” and “Totally fair.” However, empathy is often factual or problem-solving rather than using complex reflections of the client’s emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on smoking cessation, including cravings, triggers, medication options, and role-play. There is no meaningful drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The role-play shows collaboration because the client helps choose the line and directs the difficulty. Earlier in the session, however, the psychiatrist delivers much of the information in a one-directional, expert-led way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and acknowledges the client’s preference for a lower-risk option. At times, the directive scripts and instructions reduce the sense that the client is the author of his own change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are few open questions exploring the client’s values, importance, confidence, or personal meaning of quitting. The psychiatrist often provides reasons and solutions instead of eliciting the client’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of shared decision-making, especially when selecting and practicing a refusal line. Overall, the session is more expert-driven and instructional than fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses clear desire to quit, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, ask about, or amplify that change talk. Stronger MI responses would have explored the client’s reasons, confidence, and personal goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client’s barriers and validates some concerns with “Totally fair.” However, sustain talk is not consistently redirected using double-sided reflections or deeper motivation exploration.
