# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, validation, and unconditional positive regard throughout. The psychiatrist normalizes Mateo's position, never judges past behavior, and communicates respect without being formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures emotional weight and validates the burden of shame around fatherhood. Reflections occasionally stay at the cognitive level rather than fully capturing the deeper grief of feeling like a 'lie' in the family room.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from abstract to concrete. Each question builds on the previous answer, consistently tying back to the family goal with clear momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language and builds on Mateo's own words to frame subsequent questions. However, the structure remains primarily psychiatrist-led; true co-construction would involve more explicit invitations for Mateo to steer the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellently removes pressure, respects pace, and consistently places authority with Mateo. Never prescribes or says 'you should'; all questions are open and place solution-finding squarely in Mateo's hands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest domain—consistently elicits motivation from within Mateo using open, curiosity-driven questions. Every insight in the session comes from Mateo himself, with the psychiatrist's role purely to create conditions for that insight to emerge.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with language that conveys Mateo holds the expertise and frames suggestions as exploratory. Could be stronger by more explicitly inviting Mateo to steer, as the session structure is primarily the psychiatrist's.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk at multiple points, eliciting Mateo's own values, strategies, and concrete plans. Could be more deliberate in explicitly naming and reinforcing change talk statements to build stronger self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating concerns without dismissing or arguing, and reframes the 'I should be further along' narrative as an external comparison. Consistently avoids reinforcing the patient's limiting beliefs while gently opening space for alternative perspectives.
