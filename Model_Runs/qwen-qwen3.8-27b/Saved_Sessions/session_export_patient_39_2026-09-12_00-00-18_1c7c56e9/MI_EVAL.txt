# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, repeatedly reducing shame around limited mobility. Warmth was somewhat procedural and blended with medical/safety instruction, so it did not consistently reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected some core experiences accurately, such as the mixed feelings about the class and the sense that caution made sense. However, the empathy often stayed factual and safety-oriented rather than deeply exploring embarrassment, fear, discouragement, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and effectively narrowed the discussion to a specific, manageable action: ankle circles at home after dinner. Direction was sometimes maintained through repeated instruction and safety planning rather than MI-guided clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was meaningful collaboration because Elias’s preferences shaped the plan, including ankle circles, a vibration alarm, doing it after dinner, and texting his wife. The interaction was not fully between equals because the psychiatrist frequently provided structure, safety rules, and recommended actions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Elias’s right to choose and affirmed his independence in several moments. However, many directive statements and detailed instructions reduced the sense of client ownership over the plan and stop criteria.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked some useful questions about barriers and doability, but did not consistently draw out Elias’s own reasons, values, goals, or confidence for change. Change talk was acknowledged but not deeply elicited or expanded throughout the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and used Elias’s preferences in shaping the plan. However, the frequent expert stance, detailed instructions, and safety rules created a more clinical/coaching dynamic than a true MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elias offered some change talk, and the psychiatrist responded supportively to parts of it. The psychiatrist did not systematically evoke, reflect, or expand change talk or explore values such as independence, health, or preserving mobility.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided arguing with or dismissing Elias’s concerns and validated caution and small steps. However, the conversation repeatedly returned to limitations and safety precautions rather than using double-sided reflections or pivots to resolve ambivalence.
