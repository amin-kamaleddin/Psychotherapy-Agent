# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations embedded in reflections. Some empathic statements read as slightly formulaic rather than deeply individualized, and no explicit unconditional positive regard statements appear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate, complex, and emotionally attuned, capturing both cognitive and affective dimensions of Amina's experience. Particularly strong is the reflection 'your love is the problem,' which names something Amina implied but did not explicitly state.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a consistent, logical focus on the co-parenting conflict without drifting into unrelated topics, progressing from identifying the urgent issue through pattern exploration to existing strategies. The session ends somewhat open-endedly without a clear collaborative plan for concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the conversation as a joint exploration using language like 'I'm curious' and validates Amina's existing strategies rather than introducing advice. Information flow is primarily one-directional (ask-answer-reflect), with limited evidence of genuine mutual influence or Amina's ideas visibly reshaping the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports autonomy through open questions and explicit statements like 'this space is entirely yours,' never telling Amina what she should do. Autonomy support is implicit and consistent but could be more explicit in moments where Amina expresses self-doubt, such as 'You know your son best.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws out Amina's own motivations, values, and existing competence through well-timed questions rather than introducing external rationales for change. The evocation could go further by developing change talk into commitment language or explicitly exploring Amina's confidence and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong collaborative stance, following Amina's lead and positioning her as the expert on her own experience. The session is somewhat therapist-paced, and more explicit power-sharing language or evidence of Amina's ideas substantially shaping direction in unexpected ways would elevate this to a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively cultivates change talk through strategic questions that elicit Amina's goals, vision, and existing competence, then amplifies these with statements like 'You've already found a version that works.' The session could push further into commitment language, such as confidence ratings or a concrete plan for the coming week.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully by reframing helplessness ('that's not a contradiction, that's just the reality of being a human in a messy situation') and avoiding direct contradiction. One reflection of pain (feeling undermined) could have been paired more explicitly with a pivot toward agency rather than left as a pure reflection of struggle.
