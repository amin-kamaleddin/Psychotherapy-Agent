# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental throughout, validating the client's effort, honesty, and constraints. Never labels or judges; reframes self-critical statements in a compassionate light (e.g., 'life choice under real constraints' vs. 'character choice').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate experiential reflections (e.g., 'something in between wanting to go and actually walking through the door'). Could be elevated by going deeper into the emotional weight of shame and self-judgment before offering cognitive reframes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical arc from barrier exploration to action planning, with each question building on the last toward the target behavior. A brief collaborative summary or explicit bridging statement at transitions would strengthen the directional flow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the work as joint exploration using curiosity-based language ('I'm curious,' 'I wonder') and credits the client's own insights. Could be elevated by more explicitly inviting the client to co-create direction at transition points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary non-directive stance — never prescribes, never suggests specifics, and the final action plan emerged entirely from the client's own voice. The psychiatrist consistently positions Rafael as the expert on his own experience and agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every intervention is designed to draw motivation and solutions from within the client rather than providing information or reasons for change. The client's final self-generated plan ('twice a week, twenty minutes') is textbook evocation at the highest level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear partnership stance with client-directed language and no prescriptive advice. Could be elevated by more explicit agenda negotiation and acknowledging the psychiatrist's role as a guest in the client's life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively amplifies ability talk and elicits desire/ability through targeted questions, culminating in the client articulating a concrete doable plan. Could be stronger by more systematically eliciting all four types of change talk (desire, ability, need, commitment) before the session ends.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles self-blame and the 'lazy' narrative skillfully through compassionate reframing and exploration rather than argument or dismissal. Could be elevated with more double-sided reflections to explicitly hold ambivalence (e.g., 'It makes sense you want to go, and it also makes sense the day is full').
