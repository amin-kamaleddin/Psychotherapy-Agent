# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a generally nonjudgmental, respectful tone and avoided shaming or lecturing. Acceptance was somewhat generic and occasionally shifted into practical advice in a mildly paternalistic way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reflected Dylan’s frustration and practical concerns accurately, but often stayed at a surface level. Deeper emotional themes such as shame, fear, identity, or being treated as an adult were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted toward procedural problem-solving, housing logistics, scripts, and safety advice rather than staying focused on the target behavior. There was not enough sustained direction toward exploring Dylan’s drinking, responsibility, or discrepancy between values and actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited Dylan’s input and offered choices, which supported collaboration. However, the clinician repeatedly provided structured advice and alternatives even after Dylan declined, making the interaction feel more directive than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Dylan’s choices were largely honored, and the clinician avoided arguing, pressuring, or forcing compliance. Minor limitations included some advisory language that carried a subtle directive tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician used open-ended questions, but many were task-focused rather than motivational. Dylan’s own values and goals were not consistently used to evoke deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician showed some partnership by asking for preferences and offering options. At times, the expert/problem-solver role became dominant, especially when providing scripts and procedural guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan offered several change-related ideas, such as wanting to keep housing, focus on classes, and act normal. The clinician did not sufficiently reflect, expand, or amplify these statements into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan repeatedly minimized the incident and externalized blame, and this sustain talk was not effectively softened. The clinician often reflected frustration but did not consistently use double-sided reflections or discrepancy to open space for change.
