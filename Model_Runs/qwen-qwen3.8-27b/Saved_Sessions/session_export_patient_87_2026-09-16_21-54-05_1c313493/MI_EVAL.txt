# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent respect, normalizes difficulty, and counters self-criticism without judgment. The main gap is that the warmth remains somewhat structured and does not fully affirm the client’s worth independent of progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The reflections are accurate and capture both content and ambivalence well. Deeper emotional processing, especially around shame and identity, could be more explicit.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior and home-based consistency goal. There is no topic drift, and each question moves the discussion toward a workable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas clearly shape the plan, and the psychiatrist avoids prescribing. Explicit co-creation of the agenda or session focus could be stronger.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the work as optional and self-directed. The client is repeatedly supported in choosing, pausing, or stopping without that being treated as failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does a strong job eliciting client-generated strategies and self-standards. More explicit evocation of values, confidence, and commitment could strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on his own life and is repeatedly invited to define what is workable. The interaction is still somewhat therapist-guided in sequencing and agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and affirms client change talk about smaller, doable steps. It would be stronger with more deliberate development of desire, confidence, reasons, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument, denial, or moralizing. More consistent use of double-sided reflections could better balance doubt with the change side.
