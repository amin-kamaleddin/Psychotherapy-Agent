# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the difficulty of quitting in social contexts. However, some responses are formulaic or subtly corrective, limiting deeper positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s fear of awkwardness and the social function of smoking. Reflections remain mostly surface-level and do not fully capture the emotional core of her ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation in social settings. The limitation is that the direction becomes heavily tactical rather than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices but frequently shifts into advisor-mode by introducing scripts and plans. The client’s concerns do not consistently shape the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission is requested and some options are offered, which supports autonomy. However, the repeated specificity of advice and scripts makes the interaction more directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist misses several opportunities to draw out the client’s own values, meanings, and confidence. Instead, the session relies heavily on suggested behaviors and practical plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few collaborative moments, such as asking what feels doable. Overall, the session becomes expert-driven, with the psychiatrist leading toward concrete coping plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers multiple instances of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently notice, reflect, or amplify them. The conversation shifts quickly to practical strategies instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and offers some helpful reframes. However, responses often move into problem-solving rather than rolling with resistance in an MI-consistent way.
