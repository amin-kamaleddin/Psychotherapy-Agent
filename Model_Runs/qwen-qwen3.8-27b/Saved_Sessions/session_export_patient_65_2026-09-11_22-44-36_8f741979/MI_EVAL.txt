# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone but comes across as more procedural than warmly relational. More explicit affirmation of the client's effort and honesty would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges deadline stress and practical concerns, but reflections remain somewhat surface-level. Deeper tracking of ambivalence, frustration, or fear of judgment would improve empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well organized and consistently focused on completing the court-mandated service hours. The focus is strong but more task-oriented than motivational interviewing direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and offers practical options, but often shifts into providing scripts and solutions. More co-construction from the client's perspective would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers some choices and avoids overt pressure, but the session still carries a directive, problem-solving flavor. More explicit support for the client's ownership and freedom to decide would be needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to draw out the client's own values, motivations, or reasons for completing the service hours. More evocative questions would be needed to help the client articulate personal change reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks for input at times, but the interaction often becomes clinician-led rather than genuinely collaborative. Treating the client more consistently as an equal partner would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician focuses mainly on logistics and next steps rather than the client's desire, ability, or reasons for change. The client generates little change talk, and evocative change questions are minimal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids direct argument but tends to move into problem-solving rather than reflecting and reframing ambivalence. Double-sided reflections would better soften the client's reluctance.
