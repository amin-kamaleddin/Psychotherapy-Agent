# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and accepts the patient’s concerns without frustration. It stops short of a 5 because the warmth is general and occasionally assumes stress despite the patient’s denial.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient’s preference for a low-burden, manageable approach. It remains mostly content-level rather than exploring deeper emotions such as worry, annoyance, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session focuses mainly on making blood pressure monitoring convenient, leaving alcohol use and its possible role unexplored. It lacks clear MI direction toward the central target of linking alcohol to hypertension.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds a practical routine around the patient’s own ideas, supporting a sense of teamwork. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still leads the structure and offers options rather than fully joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent autonomy support is shown through repeated invitations for the patient to choose what is manageable and important. There is no evidence of pressure, persuasion, or controlling advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to elicit preferences, but the evocation is mostly logistical rather than exploring values, confidence, or internal motivations. It does not deeply evoke reasons for change or reducing alcohol.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as the expert on his daily life and invites him to define what would work. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still structures the agenda and offers suggestions without fully prioritizing patient-led topics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that elicit mild change talk about keeping blood pressure in check and avoiding medications. It fails to amplify, deepen, or strategically reflect that change talk using scaling, values, or stronger evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the patient’s minimization of alcohol and stress at face value, sometimes embedding beer into the routine. It does not use double-sided reflections or gentle exploration to soften sustain talk.
