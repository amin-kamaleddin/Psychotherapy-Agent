# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, immediately normalizing the client's discomfort and consistently rejecting self-judgment without being condescending. There is no labeling, no disapproval, and no moment where resistance is met with judgment; the warmth feels genuine and calibrated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shows highly accurate reflective listening with nuanced emotional attunement, capturing emotional subtext and underlying meaning beyond simple parroting. Key reflections such as 'annoying, and underneath that, scared' demonstrate sophisticated complex reflections that help the client name what they were struggling to articulate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target behavior with a logical arc from resistance through understanding to a small commitment to continue. However, the session lacks explicit collaborative agenda-setting at the start and a clear closing summary that names accomplishments and next steps, preventing a perfect score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction feels like two people talking rather than a clinician extracting information, with genuine 'I' statements and language of mutual presence. The session is co-created, with the client's responses directly shaping where the conversation goes, and no moment where a clinical agenda overrides the collaborative flow.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout feature of this session, with consistent explicit permission-giving ('you don't have to figure this out tonight,' 'you don't have to go there') and a complete absence of directives. Every intervention is framed as an invitation or reflection, never a directive, and the psychiatrist never once uses 'you should' or 'I recommend.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the client's own words and solutions without providing external reasons for change, using questions like 'what would need to be true for it to just fit?' to invite self-generated insight. The evocation is subtle, patient, and developmentally appropriate for a precontemplation client, resulting in genuine client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life and the primary agent of any change, explicitly rejecting the authority position. The power dynamic is genuinely balanced, with the psychiatrist's role framed as companion rather than guide or authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates conditions where change talk emerges naturally rather than forcing it, using open questions and naming the client's own shifts to help him see his agency. The client generates genuine change talk ('I'll try,' 'I want to actually do the thing') as a result of the psychiatrist's evocative strategy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never argues with or dismisses sustain talk, consistently using it as a springboard rather than an obstacle through curiosity and double-sided reflections. Every instance is met with understanding and gently redirected, with the psychiatrist acknowledging the protective function of resistance without endorsing the minimization.
