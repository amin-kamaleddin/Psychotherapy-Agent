# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, with validating language and no blame. Acceptance is slightly limited by affirming the client’s effort and plan rather than his worth independent of follow-through.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures frustration, fear of failure, and ambivalence. Emotional depth could be stronger when moving quickly from charged statements toward implementation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on increasing physical activity and moves purposefully from barriers to a concrete, low-pressure plan. The focus is clear without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and co-creates cues, reminders, and self-credit options. Collaboration is somewhat guided because the clinician supplies many specific options rather than fully client-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice, pace, and permission to stop are repeatedly emphasized, supporting the client’s self-direction. Autonomy support could be strengthened with more explicit noncontrolling language and longer pauses before suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions draw out the client’s own reasons, past successes, and readiness through small steps and confidence scaling. More evocative questions about values, identity, or meaning could broaden motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s agenda, preferences, and reactions, maintaining a collaborative stance. Power-sharing could improve by allowing the client to generate options before clinician suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client produces useful change talk about walking, showing up, and reducing pressure. Change talk could be broadened to include why exercise matters to mood, health, or sense of self.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed with lower-stakes options, permission to stop, and self-compassion planning. Some ambivalence about failure and hidden obligations is accepted but not fully explored.
