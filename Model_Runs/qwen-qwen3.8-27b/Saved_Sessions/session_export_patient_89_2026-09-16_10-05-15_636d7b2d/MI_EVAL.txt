# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoided blaming Marcus for past dieting cycles. Warmth was present but occasionally sounded efficient and formulaic rather than fully emotionally validating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflected Marcus's ambivalence, practical constraints, and preference for low-stakes options. Empathy was mainly cognitive and practical, with limited deeper exploration of his emotional fatigue and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on increasing physical activity in a realistic way. Each exchange moved purposefully toward a concrete, actionable plan without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options, incorporated Marcus's ideas, and treated the plan as a joint problem-solving process. The session remained strong but was still noticeably structured and clinician-driven in its sequencing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasized Marcus's choice and avoided coercive or pressuring language. Some task-driven prompts, such as repeated confidence scaling and “now” commitments, created mild directive momentum.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician drew out Marcus's own ideas, resources, and practical reasons for change rather than giving advice or lectures. Evocation was strong behaviorally but did not deeply explore personal values, identity, or intrinsic meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus was positioned as the expert on his own schedule, constraints, and feasibility, and his input substantially shaped the plan. The partnership was limited slightly by the structured flow and lack of more explicit “you are the expert” language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited and reinforced Marcus's readiness, confidence, and commitment statements. The focus was more on practical behavioral planning than deeper change talk tied to values or long-term meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoided arguing with Marcus's doubts and reframed them toward smaller, safer experiments. The softening was skillful but somewhat implicit, with less explicit use of double-sided reflections to address ambivalence.
