# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, consistently affirming Marco's openness and never using judgmental or labeling language. Even when Marco is resistant or dismissive, responses remain warm, validating, and individualized (e.g., 'I don't think stubborn does you justice,' 'I respect that you did').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects not just content but underlying meaning and emotion with precision, accurately capturing the weight of failed attempts, the protective function of avoidance, and Marco's emerging ambivalence. Reflections are non-intrusive and consistently attuned, such as noticing 'that little I think at the end tells me even you're not completely sure of that.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear and consistent focus on smoking and its physical effects, building a progressive arc from current role to awareness of deterioration. The session could be slightly stronger with a more explicit closing summary that ties Marco's shift to a concrete behavioral goal, though the open ending is appropriate for precontemplation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is framed as a collaborative exploration from the start ('a conversation, not a lecture'), with genuine curiosity driving questions and Marco's contributions shaping the direction. The psychiatrist never dominates or lectures, consistently using Marco's own language and metaphors to build the conversation forward.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly affirms Marco's right to choose at multiple points ('You don't owe anyone a different answer,' 'I'm not going to push,' 'Either way, it's yours'). No specific actions or treatments are ever imposed, and even evocation is framed as curiosity about Marco's own perspective rather than persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses questions and reflections to draw out Marco's own motivations and insights rather than providing information or reasons for change. The client does nearly all of the motivational work, guided by strategically timed questions that elicit his own recognition of deterioration and emerging ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Marco as the expert on his own life, using questions like 'What do you make of where things are?' and 'What do you think would need to be different?' The language is inclusive and non-hierarchical, with explicit statements reinforcing the collaborative stance ('I'm not here to add another one').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes change talk through questions that draw out Marco's own observations about worsening symptoms and the limits of his current strategy. The main area for growth is more explicitly reinforcing Marco's statements of desire or ability when they emerge, such as the implicit admission of readiness within 'I'm not ready.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Marco's pervasive sustain talk with exceptional skill, using normalization, reframing as understandable self-protection, and gentle redirection without ever arguing or confronting. Even when Marco deflects or minimizes, the psychiatrist rolls with resistance masterfully, never getting drawn into debate.
