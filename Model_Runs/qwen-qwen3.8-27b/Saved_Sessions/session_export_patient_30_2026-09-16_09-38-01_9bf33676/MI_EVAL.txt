# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor remains respectful and nonjudgmental, validating David’s side-effect concerns without pressuring him. Acceptance is strong but occasionally sounds mildly approving rather than fully affirming his right to remain uncertain.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures David’s fear of weakness, need for practical change, and desire for a straight answer. It remains somewhat surface-level, missing deeper emotions such as feeling like a guinea pig or being burdensome.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on medication adherence and moves from ambivalence to concrete steps, including parking, a phone alarm, and a doctor sentence. It would benefit from a closing summary linking the small step to broader cholesterol goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works jointly with David, offering options and adapting the plan to his preferences. Collaboration is effective but somewhat counselor-structured, with less fully client-generated exploration before suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David’s choices are consistently honored, and the counselor avoids coercion while helping him select practical steps. Support is strong, though the prescriptive medical options slightly reduce client-generated autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks open questions that draw out David’s preferences, practical plans, and some self-efficacy. Evocation is limited by frequent advice-giving and insufficient exploration of deeper values or internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats David as the expert on his life, frequently asking his preferences and letting his choices shape the plan. Partnership is strong but not fully client-led because the counselor supplies much of the structure and medical content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling evoked useful change statements, such as trying farther parking, using an alarm, and discussing muscle weakness with the doctor. The counseling could deepen change talk by amplifying David’s own values and benefits more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges David’s side-effect concerns without arguing, but does not consistently soften or reframe his lingering ambivalence. More double-sided reflections or pivots toward change would improve this criterion.
