# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids lecturing about weight or past attempts. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly acknowledging the patient’s shame and frustration about repeated weight-loss cycles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several reflections capture the patient’s ambivalence and practical concerns, showing effort to understand his perspective. However, the reflections remain mostly surface-level and miss deeper emotional cues such as feeling annoyed, tired, or relieved about not already failing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on increasing physical activity, specifically a 10-minute walk. The conversation moves efficiently from identifying the problem to selecting a concrete, actionable plan with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively seeks the patient’s input on timing, supports, problem-solving, and check-in format, and the final plan reflects his preferences. Collaboration is strong but not fully equal because the clinician still introduces the main behavioral suggestion and much of the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking questions and offering options rather than instructing. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly emphasizing the patient’s full ownership and by making the follow-up feel more like optional support than monitoring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative techniques such as open questions and a confidence ruler. Motivation is not consistently drawn from within, as the clinician provides rationale and quickly moves into advice and planning rather than exploring the patient’s values and deeper reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient’s contributions and uses them to shape the plan. Partnership is strong but not fully client-led because the clinician still proposes the core solution and many specific options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk and confidence through a confidence ruler and questions about raising confidence. The change talk remains mostly logistical and does not develop into deeper reasons, values, meaning, or self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles barriers and past failures without arguing or disapproving, reframing them as fit issues and pivoting to smaller steps. It is not a 5 because there are missed opportunities for deeper double-sided reflections or stronger reframing of the self-defeating narrative.
