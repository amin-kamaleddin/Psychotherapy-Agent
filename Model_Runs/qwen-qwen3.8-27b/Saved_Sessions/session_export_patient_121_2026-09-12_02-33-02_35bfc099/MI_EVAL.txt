# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, nonjudgmental tone and validates Sofia's frustration without blame or argument. Acceptance is strong but occasionally becomes procedural when quickly moving into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Sofia's frustration, torn feelings, and fear of looking weak. They could be deeper in reflecting underlying loneliness, shame, and cumulative exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to peer conflict and response strategy, but becomes micro-focused on exact text wording. It lacks a clear strategic link to broader goals and a clear collaborative endpoint.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Sofia's lead, adapting to her preferences for short, direct language and inviting input on wording. It falls short because the provider still generates many specific scripts rather than letting Sofia generate her own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia is repeatedly given choice, permission not to act, and noncontrolling invitations to try low-risk steps. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming her full authority and reducing subtle pressure to choose a step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some open questions and readiness scaling, but often supplies solutions, scripts, and options instead of drawing out Sofia's values and reasons for change. More evocation of her internal motivations is needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks Sofia to choose among options, showing some collaboration, but frequently acts as a solution provider. It would be stronger if Sofia's expertise and ideas more clearly shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia offers useful change talk, such as a short line and readiness to say it if confronted, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or expand it. More DARN-CAT-style evocation and amplification of her own reasons are needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia's reluctance, waiting, and fear of looking weak are often validated in ways that maintain the status quo. The psychiatrist rarely uses double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to soften sustain talk and open a doorway to change.
