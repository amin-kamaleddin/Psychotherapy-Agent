# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Jamal’s frustration and chosen plan. It does not reach a 5 because the acceptance is somewhat procedural and does not fully affirm his ambivalence as understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects surface frustration and external pressure, but misses deeper feelings such as shame, powerlessness, and the tension between autonomy and compliance. More complex reflections of Jamal’s internal experience would be needed for a higher score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of improving morning routine and school punctuality. It is not a 5 because the focus is more behavioral-management than motivational, guiding toward a concrete plan rather than self-generated motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts when Jamal rejects tracking, scripts, or rewards, but still remains the primary generator of options. The session feels more like selecting from the counselor’s menu than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by making tracking, scripts, and conversations optional. However, several directive suggestions reduce the emphasis on Jamal’s self-direction and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some initial change talk but then shifts into advice and problem-solving rather than expanding Jamal’s own reasons, values, and confidence. Missed opportunities include not exploring what a calm morning or “keeping my sanity” means to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some power-sharing through questions and adaptation to Jamal’s preferences. The stance remains more expert-driven because the psychiatrist frequently provides the solutions, scripts, and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal offers change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke, amplify, or build on it. The session moves too quickly into practical suggestions instead of strengthening intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or shaming Jamal when he rejects scripts, tracking, or formal conversations. It does not consistently turn his sustain talk into change talk or help resolve his ambivalence about doing it for himself.
