# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, normalization, and nonjudgment throughout the session. Warmer, deeper affirmation of the client's guilt and exhaustion would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally supportive but remain surface-level. The psychiatrist misses the central push-pull between needing a break and guilt about being present with her son.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays mostly focused on reducing alcohol use and developing practical coping strategies. Some time is spent on administrative formatting rather than deeper exploration of ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to choose plan elements, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the content. A more collaborative process would draw out the client's own ideas and priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and honors the client's flexibility, but the session becomes quite prescriptive. More open-ended questions would strengthen the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation is elicited, but the psychiatrist does not deeply explore the client's values or reasons for change. The session moves quickly into advice and planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial partnership through shared decisions about the plan. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary author of the intervention and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and reinforces some change talk but does not consistently deepen it. More use of DARN exploration would strengthen the client's motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and accepts the client's need for a break. However, sustain talk is not strategically softened or explored through double-sided reflections.
