# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with clear validation and respect for Linda's non-quit position. However, acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and could be deeper by reflecting more individualized warmth, ambivalence, and regard for Linda as a person rather than just her behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are several strong reflections that capture Linda's ambivalence and help her feel understood. The empathy is limited by frequent surface-level reflections that miss deeper emotional themes such as shame, guilt, identity, and fear of being judged by her daughter.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on smoking and child safety without drifting to unrelated topics. However, the conversation becomes overly logistical, narrowing to small practical details instead of maintaining a broader MI focus on motivation, readiness, and discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and allows Linda to choose among options, which supports some partnership. However, the interaction is partly advisory because the therapist supplies most of the content and structure rather than fully co-creating a client-led plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda's autonomy is strongly supported through noncoercive language, pacing, and respect for her stated limits. This is slightly undermined when many suggested options are still framed by the clinician rather than elicited from Linda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist uses some open questions and a confidence scale that elicit useful client speech. Overall, evocation is weak because the session quickly shifts to advice and practical options instead of drawing out Linda's own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of collaboration and invitations for Linda's input, creating a partially partnering stance. The psychiatrist often functions as an expert problem-solver by offering detailed practical suggestions rather than treating Linda as the expert on her home and routine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda expresses some change-compatible language around protecting her children and not wanting them to pick up bad habits. These statements are not consistently developed or amplified through follow-up questions, so change talk remains underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is prominent and is not met with argumentation, which is appropriate. However, the therapist largely accommodates the status quo and does not strategically soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections or discrepancy exploration.
