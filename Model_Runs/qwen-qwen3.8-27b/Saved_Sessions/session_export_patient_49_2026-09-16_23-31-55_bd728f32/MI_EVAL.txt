# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistently nonjudgmental, warm, and respectful stance toward Hana. Ambivalence was normalized without pressure, shaming, or correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate, consistent, and captured both content and emotional meaning. The clinician clearly understood the role of the cigarette as a low-pressure reset ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking-related behavior and a concrete, testable step. It could have more explicitly connected the exercise to broader cessation or health values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed in real time with Hana leading key decisions. The psychiatrist built on Hana's ideas rather than imposing a predetermined intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language consistently emphasized choice, low pressure, and personal ownership. The clinician respected Hana's boundary that she was not trying to quit today.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helped Hana articulate her own reasons, goals, and possible steps. Some interpretations were slightly leading, and evocation could be deepened with scaling or confidence work.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session reflected a strong partnership stance, with Hana's ideas shaping the direction of the conversation. The clinician behaved as an equal partner rather than an authority figure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively evoked desire, need, and ability statements. The clinician could have more explicitly amplified change talk, such as confidence, importance, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled without argument or a righting reflex. Double-sided reflections or more explicit pivots could have further softened ambivalence and tilted it toward change.
