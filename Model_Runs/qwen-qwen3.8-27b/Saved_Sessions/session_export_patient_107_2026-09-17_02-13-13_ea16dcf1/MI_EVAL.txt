# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicated nonjudgmental respect and acceptance, using language that reduced shame. Some statements were slightly interpretive or labeling rather than purely reflective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed good reflective listening, especially around the client’s ambivalence and core dilemma. Empathy could have been deeper and more emotionally specific in places.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of reducing later coffee consumption. Some time shifted toward abstract definitions, but the focus remained strong overall.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative, with the client’s ideas shaping the plan. The psychiatrist still led the structure and prompts, so it was not fully balanced co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported the client’s control, pace, and choice. Repeated planning language around testing and next steps slightly reduced the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions evoked the client’s own ideas and preferences. The psychiatrist also provided suggestions and examples, which limited deeper evocation of values, discrepancy, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the client as the expert on his own experience and allowed client-generated goals to guide the session. The clinician still organized much of the agenda and problem-solving structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produced useful change talk and the psychiatrist affirmed it. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, expand, or consolidate the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and used softer framing. More consistent use of double-sided reflections or discrepancy would have helped shift toward change.
