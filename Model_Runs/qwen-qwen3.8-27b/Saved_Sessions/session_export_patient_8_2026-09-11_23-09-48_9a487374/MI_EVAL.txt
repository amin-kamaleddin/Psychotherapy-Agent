# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Amina’s phone use without shaming. However, the acceptance feels somewhat scripted and often shifts quickly into concrete advice rather than staying with Amina’s experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is reasonable perspective-taking around Amina’s anxiety and the phone’s protective role. The empathy is interrupted by frequent problem-solving and does not fully explore key emotional content or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting issue of excessive phone use and its impact on sleep, studying, and mood. A clear one-week behavioral target is established, though the direction is more clinician-led than MI-guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina’s input is acknowledged and the plan is adjusted in response to her preferences. Overall, the psychiatrist remains the primary architect of the intervention, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices and avoids forcing a rigid commitment. Autonomy is frequently undermined by the large number of clinician-generated recommendations and structured planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to elicit Amina’s own motivations, values, and solutions. The session relies more on advice and structured strategies than on drawing out her internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does invite some client input, but the session is largely driven by the psychiatrist’s suggestions and structured options. There is limited explicit positioning of Amina as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina offers several change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen them. There is little use of MI-consistent questions to evoke desire, ability, reasons, or need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Amina’s reasons for maintaining current phone use without arguing or dismissing them. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections or active work to pivot sustain talk toward values and goals.
