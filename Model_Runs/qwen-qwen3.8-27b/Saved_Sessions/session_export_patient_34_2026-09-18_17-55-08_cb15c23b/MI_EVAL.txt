# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful with genuine warmth, but one moment subtly implies the client is being less than honest, preventing a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with complex reflections capturing internal conflict, though reflections sometimes remain at the cognitive level rather than fully accessing the affective layer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear progressive focus from initial framing through to the internal conflict, with each question building logically on the last and strong overall momentum toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently invites the client's input and builds on their own words, but collaboration is primarily question-response rather than fully co-constructed, with the client not steering the conversation's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy with open questions and explicit avoidance of authority, but could be strengthened with more explicit affirmations of the client's right to decide and avoiding subtle pressure from repeatedly returning to the same theme.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocation in early and middle portions, but becomes less effective later with a potential error in one response and lacks strategic variety such as scaling, future-imagining, or values-based techniques.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the conversation as collaborative with open-ended questions and cedes authority to the client, but the clinician retains primary direction-setting rather than the client co-creating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked but not consistently amplified or deepened when it appears; no scaling questions, confidence statements, or strategic DARN-CAT framework use is present.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some double-sided reflections appropriately, but softening is largely reactive rather than proactive, and the session doesn't effectively help the client reconcile sustain talk with emerging change talk.
