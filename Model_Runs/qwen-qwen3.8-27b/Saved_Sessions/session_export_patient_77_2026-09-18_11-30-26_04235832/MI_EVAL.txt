# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout. Opening statement immediately establishes unconditional positive regard, reinforced with affirming language like 'you're not a burden' and 'that's not something you'd do if you didn't know you were worth being heard.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that move beyond surface content to capture underlying emotional meaning, such as connecting the weight issue to a deeper need for autonomy/control. Client consistently affirms the reflections, confirming their accuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear trajectory from initial frustration through emotional exploration to a concrete forward-looking goal. Could have more explicitly connected the final self-compassion insight back to the original weight loss behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook collaborative practice with the client's contributions substantially shaping session direction. Power dynamic is genuinely equal; the psychiatrist functions as a facilitator rather than a director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes client's ownership with language like 'what do you think?' and frames all suggestions as options to evaluate rather than directives. Zero pressure, coercion, or implicit expectation throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight—control, self-compassion, what support should look like—comes from the client herself, elicited through strategic questions. The psychiatrist never provides reasons for change or gives advice, making this exemplary evocation practice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on her own experience, asking her sense of what's going on and what things would look like to her. The session is genuinely shaped by her contributions, with the psychiatrist never assuming the expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces multiple instances of change talk including desire, ability, and plan, strategically amplifying statements like 'a plan that actually makes sense.' Could have more explicitly explored the client's confidence or ability to follow through with a scaling question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively acknowledges sustain talk without amplifying it, reflecting the pain beneath and pivoting toward change with strategic questions. Could have used a double-sided reflection to more explicitly hold both the want to change and the discouragement simultaneously.
