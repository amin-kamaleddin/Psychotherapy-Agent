# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard without labeling or moralizing. Acceptance could be deeper by more explicitly normalizing the social pressures and ambivalence driving Ethan's behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections that captured Ethan's ambivalence, competing priorities, and resistance. Empathy could be strengthened by exploring the emotional and social meaning beneath his statements, such as fear of exclusion or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained focused on alcohol use, consequences, and developing a practical plan without drifting off-topic. Some redundancy in summarizing the plan and repeating options made the pacing less efficient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructed a plan with Ethan and invited his input throughout the session. The interaction could feel more fully client-led by offering fewer therapist-generated options and more space for Ethan's own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by asking which options felt doable and avoiding directives. Autonomy support could be more explicit in affirming Ethan's freedom to modify, delay, or abandon the plan without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan offered some change talk about avoiding vomiting, trouble, and looking weird, but the psychiatrist did not consistently draw out deeper motivation. More evocative questions about values, priorities, and personal reasons for change would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and allowed Ethan's preferences to shape the session and final plan. Partnership could be stronger by more clearly recognizing Ethan's expertise in his own social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited several change statements but often moved quickly into problem-solving and options. Change talk could be amplified more by reflecting, elaborating on, and staying with Ethan's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively avoided arguing with sustain talk and used validation and double-sided reflections to reduce defensiveness. Some responses could have explored the meaning of sustain talk more fully before shifting into strategies.
