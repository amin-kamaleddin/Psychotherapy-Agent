# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, nonjudgment, and validation without criticizing the client’s hesitancy. Acceptance could be stronger by explicitly normalizing her conflicted feelings independent of her being “grounded.”

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and helpful, capturing ambivalence, protectiveness, and need for trust. Deeper emotional meaning around fear, moral testing, and exhaustion could be explored more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on moving toward a concrete vaccination-related conversation with the doctor and partner. Direction would improve with clearer agenda-setting and an explicitly confirmed action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s perspective and builds on her ideas, including partner involvement and trusted information sources. Collaboration could be more co-directed by explicitly inviting the client to choose where to focus next.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently honors the client’s self-determination, pace, and right to decide without pressure. The client is able to express reluctance without being corrected or pushed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own reasons, standards, and imagined future through evocative questions. Evocation would deepen by more directly exploring confidence, values, and past success in managing worry.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on her family, values, and decision-making process. Medical authority is not used to persuade, and the client’s contributions substantially shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit meaningful change talk about feeling safer, trusting the doctor, stopping late-night Googling, and planning next steps. The counselor could more consistently amplify and deepen this change talk with scaling or values-based elicitation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument and acknowledged through double-sided language. The counselor could more actively reframe the client’s need for certainty to reduce its dominance while reconnecting to her goals.
