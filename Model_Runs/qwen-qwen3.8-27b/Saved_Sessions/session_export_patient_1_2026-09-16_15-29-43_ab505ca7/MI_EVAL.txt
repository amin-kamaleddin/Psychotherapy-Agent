# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used nonjudgmental, validating language and avoided correction or moralizing when the patient minimized mini-cigar use. Warmth was supportive but somewhat clinical, so it fell just short of the highest level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the patient’s ambivalence, need for control, and conditional willingness to change. Some reflections stayed at the surface and did not fully explore deeper emotions such as fear, embarrassment, or personal meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on mini-cigar use, throat/gum irritation, and practical next steps. Direction could have been stronger by more clearly connecting the monitoring plan to a specific tobacco-reduction or change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a collaborative, dialogue-based style and co-constructed a plan using the patient’s own ideas. The agenda and overall flow remained largely therapist-led rather than fully patient-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported choice and avoided controlling language, repeatedly emphasizing the patient’s own judgment, control, and self-defined next steps. This was one of the strongest aspects of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicited the patient’s own reasons, preferences, and possible steps rather than providing advice. Evocation could have been deeper by exploring values, future concerns, confidence, or stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the patient as the expert on his own life and invited his input in shaping the plan. Some statements were subtly leading, and the psychiatrist still set much of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicited small, conditional change statements and reflected them back to strengthen the patient’s own language. Change talk could have been deepened by exploring desire, need, confidence, or stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with or reinforcing the patient’s minimization and used gentle reframing to reduce defensiveness. However, the conversation was not consistently redirected toward discrepancy, values, or small steps away from sustain talk.
