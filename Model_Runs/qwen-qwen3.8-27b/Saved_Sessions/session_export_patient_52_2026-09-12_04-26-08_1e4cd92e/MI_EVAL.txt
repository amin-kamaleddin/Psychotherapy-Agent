# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids moralizing about drinking. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with limited individualized affirmation of the client's broader stress and caregiving burden.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of ambivalence, stress, and practical concerns. Empathy could be deeper by attending more fully to loneliness, exhaustion, guilt, and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on drinking, sleep, work functioning, and a concrete plan. Direction is clear, purposeful, and responsive to the client's practical style.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and the client actively shapes the plan. Collaboration is high but not perfect because several structured options are offered before fully eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with clear choices, nonpressuring language, and normalization of slips. The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits client-generated solutions, preferences, and confidence. Evocation could be deeper by drawing out the client's values, self-efficacy, and personal reasons for change more consistently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shares decision-making and respects the client's expertise and preferences. Partnership is strong but partly clinician-guided, with more predefined options than fully co-led planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits several change statements and affirms the client's commitment. Change talk could be strengthened by more deliberate amplification and reflection of its personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without confrontation and normalizes the client's ambivalence. More advanced double-sided reflections would be needed to consistently open curiosity about the costs of maintaining the current pattern.
