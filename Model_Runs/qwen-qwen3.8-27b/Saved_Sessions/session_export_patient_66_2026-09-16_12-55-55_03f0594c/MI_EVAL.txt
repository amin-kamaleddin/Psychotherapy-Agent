# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoids judgment and respects the client's stated limits and resistance. The warmth is respectful but somewhat formulaic, and the opening introduces a mild assumption about the client's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening and accurately captures the client's practical concerns and ambivalence. However, reflections remain mostly content-level and do not fully address the client's emotional or identity-level concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays organized and goal-directed while adapting to the client's resistance by focusing on managing smoking at work. The broader change focus becomes diluted as the discussion shifts toward practical logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well and co-creates a practical plan with the client's input. However, the psychiatrist still provides much of the structure and options, making the process less fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's choice, avoids pressure, and treats the client as the decision-maker. This is one of the strongest areas of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evoking questions, but they are mostly logistical rather than deeply motivational. The client's change talk is present but not consistently drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and allows the client to shape key decisions in the plan. The psychiatrist still leads much of the agenda and structure, making the interaction somewhat consultant-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to evoke the client's reasons and plans for change, but the work is limited in depth. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce the client's sustain talk, which is a strength. However, the sustain talk is not consistently redirected toward change or discrepancy.
