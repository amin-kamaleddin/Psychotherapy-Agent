# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally nonjudgmental tone and offers affirmations, but frequently shifts into problem-solving and safety directives. This reduces the sense of unconditional acceptance after the client expresses reluctance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledge frustration and the difficulty of not choosing to attend, but remain mostly surface-level. The counselor misses deeper emotions such as defensiveness, shame, or the conflict between autonomy and concern for harm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear MI-guided focus and drifts from substance use and peer influence into conflict management, safety planning, and scripts. The stated target concerns are not consistently explored or prioritized.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks for input and acknowledges the client’s plan to go to his room and text Jay. However, collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist continues introducing multiple alternatives and scripts after the client declines them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses consent-seeking language and respects refusals in the moment, which supports choice. Nevertheless, repeated suggestions after refusal make the interaction feel more directive than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few initial open questions, but much of the session relies on advice, scripts, and safety strategies. The client’s own values, reasons for change, and motivation are not consistently drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts collaboration by offering choices and asking what the client wants from the session. The stance often becomes expert-driven when teaching scripts, exit plans, and backup contacts rather than remaining an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited early, such as wanting less fighting and more space, but it is not developed, reflected, or amplified. The session shifts to safety planning instead of strengthening the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly argue with the client’s sustain talk, which avoids confrontation. However, the counselor does not skillfully redirect or soften statements like “I’m not doing anything wrong” or “I don’t need to negotiate.”
