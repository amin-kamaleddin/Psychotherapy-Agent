# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warm, specific, nonjudgmental acceptance throughout, with personalized affirmations that validate Marco's struggles without minimizing them. Notable for distinguishing his real wins from the juice issue and validating his embarrassment without dismissal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond paraphrasing into genuine emotional and cognitive nuance, capturing the identity threat, symbolic meaning, and autopilot mechanism Marco struggled to articulate. Marco repeatedly affirms the accuracy of these reflections, confirming their precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, purposeful progression from identifying the hidden obstacle through understanding its significance to developing a concrete, specific plan. There is no topic drift, and every question serves the forward momentum toward operationalizing change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a model of equal partnership where all substantive content—the plan, the scripts, the risk identification, the solutions—comes entirely from Marco. The psychiatrist's role is process-focused (reflection, clarification, invitation) while Marco's expertise in his own life is treated as essential and sufficient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Marco's ownership by offering choices rather than prescriptions and validating his decisions to drop less realistic options. Notable for affirming that dropping the dog walk was a strength, not a failure, and framing the entire plan as Marco's own engineering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every insight in the session—the identity function, the ritual distinction, the tripwire metaphor, the final plan—is Marco's own, elicited through targeted reflective questions and summaries. The psychiatrist never provides reasons for change or offers their own solutions, allowing Marco to arrive at a confident, self-generated plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that wisdom about change resides within Marco, using phrases like 'You know your own routine best' and 'You've already got the script in your head.' The psychiatrist never assumes the expert role; all significant insights are Marco's, with the psychiatrist serving as a reflective collaborator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marco's change talk is abundant and specific, elicited through targeted questions and amplified through reflections that reinforce his ownership. The session naturally progresses from ambivalence to a concrete, detailed plan that Marco articulates with clear confidence and specificity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I still love that juice') is acknowledged and validated rather than redirected or minimized, then naturally integrated into the change plan. The psychiatrist works with the attachment rather than against it, helping Marco arrive at 'one glass and actually taste it' as his own resolution.
