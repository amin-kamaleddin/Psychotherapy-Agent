# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard through warm, nonjudgmental language such as "Your hesitancy isn't a flaw" and "You're not overthinking."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening by accurately capturing the client's ambivalence and protective motivation. However, reflections are sometimes repetitive and do not fully deepen into underlying emotions such as guilt, fear, or anxiety about irreversible decisions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on the vaccination decision and the client's safety concerns. Direction could be strengthened with a more concise collaborative summary and clearer focus on the specific decision or next behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fosters collaboration by inviting the client to choose how to proceed and using the client's past decision-making experience. The partnership could be stronger by more explicitly co-creating a plan and allowing the client to lead more of the process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by respecting the client's need for time, avoiding pressure, and emphasizing her right to make a decision she can live with. The client's request for clarity is honored without directive persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses effective evocative questions that draw out the client's values, self-efficacy, and tentative willingness, such as "What would it take to make this next step feel right for you and him?" The session could improve by further developing the client's emerging readiness language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites the client to shape the conversation through questions like "How would you like to tackle this?" The partnership could be stronger by more explicitly positioning the client as the expert on her child and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some change-oriented language, especially near the end of the session. The psychiatrist could more effectively amplify, consolidate, and expand this change talk using importance/confidence scaling, values exploration, or concrete next-step language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client's sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, the session remains heavily focused on the client's reasons against change without strategically redirecting toward values, benefits, or a clearer decisional pivot.
