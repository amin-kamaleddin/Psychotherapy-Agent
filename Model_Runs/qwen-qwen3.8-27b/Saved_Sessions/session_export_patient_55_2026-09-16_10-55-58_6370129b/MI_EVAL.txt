# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client’s honesty and effort. Acceptance is sometimes procedural and could be more emotionally affirming of the client as a whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate summaries and reflections that capture ambivalence, stress, and self-management. Misses opportunities to reflect deeper emotional concerns, such as identity, control, or fear of judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and consistent focus on alcohol-related risk and safety. The conversation stays practically relevant with little irrelevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites the client’s input and co-creates a practical plan around her preferred strategy. The overall agenda and structure remain somewhat clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses permission-seeking, options, and boundary-respecting language to support client choice. The session remains more advice-giving than fully client-directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes some evocative questions about worries, barriers, and confidence, but the exploration is not deep. There is limited drawing out of values, motivations, or stronger reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance and builds on the client’s own ideas, especially the safety text plan. The clinician still sets much of the agenda and provides the main safety framework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk around ability and planning, with brief affirmations. The session does not systematically develop desire, reasons, need, or commitment for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids direct confrontation and acknowledges the client’s self-management perspective. More consistent use of double-sided reflections would better soften sustain talk and explore ambivalence.
