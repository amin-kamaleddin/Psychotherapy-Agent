# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates frustration, though they could more explicitly affirm strengths and positive behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture frustration and anger adequately, but remain somewhat surface-level and miss deeper shame, embarrassment, and social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has some direction, but it mainly follows the parental conflict rather than consistently linking it to substance-use ambivalence and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally invites choice and respects refusal of advice, though repeated offers of practical plans can feel somewhat therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is clearly supported through acceptance of the client's preferences and lack of pressure, though ownership could be stated more broadly around life decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions are present, but they focus mostly on the mother-son conflict rather than eliciting the client's values, risks, confidence, or future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves more like a partner than an authority figure, but the session still shows a noticeable intervention agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Small statements of responsibility or intention are not consistently recognized or amplified, so intrinsic motivation remains underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing, but largely follows the client's minimization and resentment without using double-sided reflections to expand perspective.
