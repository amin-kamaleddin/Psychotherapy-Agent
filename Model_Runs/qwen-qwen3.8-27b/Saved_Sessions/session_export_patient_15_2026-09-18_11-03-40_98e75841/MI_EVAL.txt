# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations and a self-correction that shows humility. Solid but somewhat standard; lacks the deep, individualized unconditional positive regard that would reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful reflective listening, particularly capturing the patient's identity concerns. One notable interpretive overreach (the 'getting older' reflection) is self-corrected, but the psychiatrist occasionally leads slightly ahead of the patient's expressed feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression and a skillful pivot from medication to the broader concept of 'taking care of it.' A notable gap is that the session focuses entirely on blood pressure without addressing alcohol use, which is the primary clinical target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and seeks the patient's input before interpreting. In practice, the power balance is unequal—the psychiatrist does most of the talking while the patient remains passive, and this is not actively addressed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently removes pressure and gives the patient full ownership of his answers. Could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the patient's right to choose not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The pivot question 'what would taking care of it look like to you?' is a masterful evocation that leads directly to the patient generating his own change plan. The evocation could be deeper with further exploration of the running motivation or use of scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with language like 'work through this together' and demonstrates partnership through self-correction. The partnership is somewhat one-directional, as the psychiatrist does most of the talking and the patient's passiveness is not actively addressed to create more equal participation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk, leading the patient to generate his own change idea (picking up running again). The session is too brief to demonstrate sustained cultivation across multiple dimensions of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without arguing or countering, using reflections that acknowledge while gently redirecting. The sustain talk around medication dominates a significant portion of the session before the pivot, and a more strategic earlier redirect would have been ideal.
