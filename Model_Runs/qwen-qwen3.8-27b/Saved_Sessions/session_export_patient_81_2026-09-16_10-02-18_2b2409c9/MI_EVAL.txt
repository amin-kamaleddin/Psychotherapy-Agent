# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, avoiding moralizing or pressure to quit completely. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and tied to practical compliance, with limited deeper emotional validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflections of the client’s perspective and practical concerns. Empathy remains mostly cognitive rather than deeply exploring emotional conflict, identity, belonging, and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on methamphetamine use, rent, sleep, work, and auditions. The clinician consistently returns to concrete, relevant change goals without significant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaboratively builds a concrete plan using the client’s own ideas, such as the rent envelope, late shift, and script page. The clinician still supplies much of the structure, so the partnership is strong but not fully balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices, respects the client’s desire to keep things low-pressure and private, and avoids directive pushback. Autonomy support is limited slightly by the number of specific plans and recommendations introduced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits readiness, confidence, and practical motivations for change. Evocation is limited by the quick move to problem-solving before fully exploring deeper values, ambivalence, and intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids an authoritarian stance. The session remains quite therapist-driven, with the clinician supplying most of the agenda, structure, and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk through concrete actions, confidence ratings, and coping plans. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk with values-based or commitment-focused questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s reasons for using and uses some normalizing reframes. There is limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to fully process ambivalence and soften sustain talk.
