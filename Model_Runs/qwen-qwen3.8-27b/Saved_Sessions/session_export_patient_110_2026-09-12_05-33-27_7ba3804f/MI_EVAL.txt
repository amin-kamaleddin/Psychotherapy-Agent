# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated respect, normalized the client’s hesitations, and avoided judgment or pressure. The acceptance was somewhat task-oriented and could be warmer and more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client’s ambivalence, preferences, and practical goals. The empathy was more cognitive than emotional, with less exploration of deeper feelings and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on cholesterol management, medication, lifestyle change, and follow-up. Some time was spent on medical logistics, which slightly reduced MI momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and the client meaningfully helped shape a simple plan with his wife. The psychiatrist still supplied much of the content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used open questions, asked permission, and supported the client’s choice to try lifestyle changes first. Repeated “want me to draft/turn this into...” statements subtly positioned the psychiatrist as the planner.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions drew out importance, confidence, and concerns. Much of the session shifted into medical information, missing opportunities to evoke deeper personal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influenced the plan and was not overridden, but the psychiatrist remained the main information provider and plan designer. Less explicit recognition of the client as the expert on his own life and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered meaningful change talk, and some questions evoked motivation. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, deepen, or summarize the client’s change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with concerns about medication and tracking, often normalizing them. However, the sustain talk was not consistently redirected toward values or change, and some language reinforced the avoid-medication framing.
