

# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing. Some acceptance is mixed with corrective or advisory language, which limits the depth of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates an adequate effort to understand the client’s perspective and recognizes the central conflict between family happiness and self-care. Reflections often remain surface-level, and deeper ambivalence is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target concern: aligning actions with family values while protecting the client’s needs. The session stays focused, practical, and progresses toward a concrete boundary-setting experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 3
**Comments:** Shows partial collaboration by accepting and refining the client’s proposal for shared quiet time. However, much of the content is therapist-generated, resulting in uneven power sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 3
**Comments:** Supports autonomy through choices and choice-based language. This is mixed with prescriptive guidance and structured instructions, so client ownership and pace of change are not consistently emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 2
**Comments:** Makes limited evocative attempts, but frequently supplies reasons for change rather than drawing out the client’s own values, fears, strengths, and motivations. More open-ended questions would be needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 3
**Comments:** Invites client input and accepts some of the client’s ideas, but often assumes the expert role through structured steps, scripts, and behavioral experiments. The client’s expertise is not consistently foregrounded.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 2
**Comments:** Some attempts to elicit change talk are present, and the client expresses willingness to try a small step. However, the therapist mostly provides solutions and scripts, with little amplification, scaling, or follow-up on intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4
**Comments:** Effectively addresses worries about selfishness, distance, and “checking out.” Boundaries are reframed as preserving love, presence, and family connection. The use of shared quiet time honors the client’s values while supporting change.
