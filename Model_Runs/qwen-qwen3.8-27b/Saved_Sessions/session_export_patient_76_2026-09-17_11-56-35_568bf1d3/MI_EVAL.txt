# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance maintained throughout a highly sensitive session on self-harm, with no disapproval or moral judgment. Falls short of a 5 because acceptance is somewhat focused on the content of Liora's statements rather than deeply person-centered unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections that accurately capture the somatic and functional dimensions of Liora's experience (e.g., pain as a location for unnamed pressure). Occasional formulaic structure and missed opportunities to probe deeper emotional underbelly such as shame, isolation, or grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on self-harm as a coping strategy but lacks clear progression or momentum toward a specific change goal. The session feels circular, with Liora repeatedly arriving at 'I don't know' without strategic narrowing or a concrete collaborative focus for future work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed as a joint exploration with genuine building on Liora's contributions (e.g., validating her 'location' insight). Slightly one-directional in practice, as the psychiatrist tends to name and synthesize insights on Liora's behalf rather than fully co-constructing understanding with her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Liora's pace and right to choose, never pressures or implies she 'should' stop. Occasionally questions subtly imply a direction toward finding an alternative, which slightly undermines the autonomy-neutral stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks good evocative questions drawn from Liora's own experience, but responds passively when she says 'I don't know' rather than re-approaching from a different angle. The session ends without a final evocative move to draw out any sense of possibility or build momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Establishes a clear collaborative stance early, positioning Liora as the expert and using inclusive, non-prescriptive language. Slightly undermined by the psychiatrist's tendency to interpret and synthesize on her behalf, which can shift the dynamic toward the clinician as the one who 'gets it.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited ('I'm tired of doing it,' 'I don't even enjoy it anymore') but underdeveloped and not consistently amplified or built upon. The session ends without a clear evocation of confidence or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or dismissing, with some gentle reframing attempts. However, there is no consistent pattern of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to hold ambivalence more productively toward change.
