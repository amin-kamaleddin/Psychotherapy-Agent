# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and generally avoids shaming. Acceptance is strong but not perfect because advice-giving and endorsing a "white lie" can shift the role toward expert guidance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate but relatively surface-level reflections of Ethan's ambivalence. There are missed opportunities to deeply explore the emotional complexity of social pressure, fear of judgment, and the need to belong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use, immediate risks, and a concrete plan for the next weekend. The focus is strong but somewhat narrow, with less consistent connection to broader values, autonomy, or long-term identity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements, such as inviting choices and practicing scripts, but much of the session is driven by the psychiatrist's lists and suggestions. Ethan contributes, yet he does not consistently generate or drive the session content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly affirms Ethan's choice and asks permission before offering information. However, frequent advice, scripts, and the endorsement of a lie reduce the emphasis on Ethan's own ownership and values-based decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions, rulers, and role-play elicit some client-generated motivation and change-related language. The evocation is not consistently deepened, as the psychiatrist often shifts quickly into practical advice and therapist-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes collaborative gestures and invites Ethan's input, but the overall stance remains somewhat expert-driven. The session would feel more genuinely partnered if the psychiatrist elicited Ethan's own ideas first and built the plan more from Ethan's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some change talk, including concern about getting sick, upsetting his mom, and willingness to try. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen this change talk by exploring desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment in a more structured MI way.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Ethan's minimizing language and social reasons for drinking without arguing. However, sustain talk is often bypassed with practical advice rather than softened through double-sided reflections, reframing, or values-based exploration.
