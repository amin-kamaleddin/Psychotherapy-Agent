# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, non-judgmental, and respectful throughout, with strong self-correction when challenged. However, occasional over-interpretation of Amina's minimal responses slightly undermines pure acceptance by imposing meaning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine effort to track Amina's experience, but calibration is inconsistent—frequently over-interpreting one-word responses with multi-clause reflections that mismatch her minimal register. Client explicitly corrected the psychiatrist twice for reading too much into her words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriate focus on rapport-building given the clinical context, but the session lacks a clear directional arc and ends without forward momentum or a gentle next-step signal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is largely one-directional; the psychiatrist drives the interaction through reflections and interpretations rather than achieving genuine co-construction. The isolated self-correction ('I'll keep it more... normal') is a strength but not sustained.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong throughout, with explicit language removing power dynamics ('You don't owe me a conversation') and consistent validation of Amina's right to choose. Slightly undermined by interpretive statements that subtly position the psychiatrist as having insight Amina hasn't articulated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several appropriate MI-consistent open questions were used, but evocation lacks strategic depth and does not produce substantive client-generated change talk. The 'maybe' was acknowledged but not skillfully explored further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally non-authoritative stance maintained, but frequent interpretive assumptions ('I think what you're really saying is...') position the psychiatrist as the meaning-maker rather than inviting Amina to construct her own understanding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Appropriate MI-consistent questions were asked, and Amina's 'maybe' was affirmed, but it was not skillfully built upon. No DARN change talk was elicited and the session remained at the rapport level without moving toward any specific change goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was neither reinforced nor actively softened; the psychiatrist mostly avoided it rather than using MI-consistent techniques like double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to redirect it toward change.
