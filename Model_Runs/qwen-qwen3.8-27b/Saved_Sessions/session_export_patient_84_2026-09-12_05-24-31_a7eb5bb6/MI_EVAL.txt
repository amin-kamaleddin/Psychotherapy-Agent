# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, validating stance and respected Malik's autonomy, though the warmth was somewhat formulaic. More individualized, emotionally attuned positive regard would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected Malik's stated concerns accurately but remained largely surface-level. Deeper emotions, such as irritation at being controlled and wanting to be seen as capable, were not consistently addressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stayed focused on cannabis use, peer context, and conflict with his mother. However, it increasingly shifted toward conflict-management scripts and safety planning rather than deepening ambivalence about cannabis.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was a collaborative surface, as Malik's preference to reduce yelling shaped the session. However, frequent pre-made options, scripts, and plans made the interaction feel more directive than truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported autonomy by affirming Malik's choices and avoiding pressure to quit. Continued offering scripts and safety plans occasionally undercut his sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made several attempts to evoke Malik's own motivations through open-ended questions. The evocation was limited by the repeated use of external scripts and multiple-choice options, which kept his motivation largely external.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and framed choices as Malik's, supporting some partnership. However, the repeated role of expert problem-solver, with structured plans and scripts, reduced the equal-partner stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked open-ended MI-style questions and elicited some change talk around school, team, and responsibility. This change talk was not consistently amplified, refined, or connected to deeper personal values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Malik's sustain talk and accepted his framing without confrontation. There was limited use of double-sided reflections or gentle redirection toward discrepancy and change.
