# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client’s safety concerns without arguing. The stance was strong but somewhat formulaic, limiting it to a 4.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client’s ambivalence, urgency, distrust, and fear of being dismissed. Empathy was consistent but could have explored the deeper emotional meaning behind her concerns more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma management, inhaler safety, and practical barriers. The conversation moved clearly toward a concrete, client-owned next step: a lung function test.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked collaboratively and let the client’s concerns shape the session, especially around trust and proof. It was not a 5 because the psychiatrist still led the structure and did not explicitly invite the client to co-direct the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly reinforced the client’s right to make her own decision and avoided pressure. The final plan was framed as the client’s own choice, which is excellent autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was largely evoked from the client rather than imposed, especially when she identified the lung function test herself. It could have been stronger with deeper exploration of values or confidence/commitment questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a non-expert stance and allowed the client’s priorities to guide the session. It fell short of a 5 because power sharing was not made more explicit through agenda negotiation or invitations to co-lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions helped the client articulate reasons for a practical next step. Change talk emerged well but was not fully expanded with scaling, importance/ability questions, or stronger commitment building.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled the client’s distrust and sustain talk without debate, using double-sided reflections and redirection. It would have been stronger if her safety concerns had been more explicitly linked to her values and long-term goals.
