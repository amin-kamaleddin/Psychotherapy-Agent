# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and low-pressure throughout the session. The main limitation was that warmth remained practical and task-focused rather than deeply affirming the client's ambivalence or personal worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered several accurate reflections of the client's torn position about coffee, health concerns, and jitters. However, the empathy remained mostly cognitive and did not deeply explore the emotional or identity-based meaning of his morning routine.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on coffee/caffeine use and moved efficiently toward a concrete one-week tracking experiment. There was no meaningful drift, and the intervention remained highly relevant to the client's presenting concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by inviting the client's preferences and co-constructing a practical plan around his own ideas. It fell short of a 5 because much of the structure and several intervention options were still therapist-initiated rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by asking what mattered, what felt doable, and by respecting the client's firm wish to protect his morning coffee. It was not a 5 because the therapist still drove much of the structure through suggested plans and options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used scaling questions and client-generated options to invite some change talk, particularly around confidence and small workable steps. However, the session more often supported compliance with a monitoring plan than deeper elicitation of personal values, desired outcomes, and intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist took a genuinely collaborative stance by asking for the client's input and building the plan around his suggestions. A mild expert-driven quality remained because the psychiatrist still set the agenda and proposed specific early interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful client-generated change ideas such as tracking, a 2 PM cutoff, a ≤6 goal, tea, and a simple alarm. However, the change talk was not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened in a sustained DARN-C way.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided confrontation and acknowledged the value of the client's morning routine and focus. However, sustain talk was more accommodated than fully softened, with change-focused options introduced before deeper exploration of the benefits of keeping the current routine.
