# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and generally nonjudgmental toward Viktor's guardedness and denial. Warmth is solid but sometimes procedural and could be more individualized or normalizing of the strain of being pushed into care.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Viktor's perspective, including his ambivalence about keeping his wind-down while recognizing it creates friction. Empathy remains mostly content-level and does not fully explore the emotional undercurrent such as irritation, defensiveness, or fear of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session starts with a clear focus on substance use and its effects, but then shifts toward a practical texting plan rather than maintaining momentum on the target behavior. A stronger direction would have included a summary and a client-led refocus on what Viktor might want to do differently about his use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Viktor to choose among options and adapts well to his preferences, such as accepting the preset text and rejecting a more formal plan. Collaboration is good but not fully client-led because the psychiatrist generates and steers toward specific behavioral options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a clear strength: the psychiatrist repeatedly asks permission, offers choices, respects Viktor's boundaries, and avoids pressure or persuasion. Viktor's decisions about the plan are consistently honored without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions about what Viktor wants to keep, change, and what might get in the way. However, evocation remains surface-level and does not deeply access Viktor's intrinsic motivation, values, health concerns, or confidence about changing substance use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that Viktor is the expert on his own life and lets his preferences shape the session. Partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist still drives the conversation somewhat by introducing concrete options and strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, especially around small communication changes such as texting when running late. The change talk is narrow and focused more on reducing his wife's worry than on reducing or reevaluating substance use itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce Viktor's sustain talk and responds in a neutral, accepting way. However, the session often settles into accepting the status quo rather than using double-sided reflections or discrepancy more effectively to pivot toward change.
