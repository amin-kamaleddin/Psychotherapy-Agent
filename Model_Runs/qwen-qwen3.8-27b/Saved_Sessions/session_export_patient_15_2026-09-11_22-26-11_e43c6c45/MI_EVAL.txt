# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful but mostly prescriptive. There is limited affirmation of the client's struggle, values, or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Validation is brief and task-focused. The client's ambivalence, minimization, and underlying concerns are not accurately reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on blood pressure and practical lifestyle steps. However, the direction is more directive than client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some client input is accepted, but the psychiatrist remains largely in charge of the agenda and plan. The interaction resembles advice-giving more than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Choices are occasionally offered, but client ownership and freedom to decide are not consistently reinforced. The client's right to make the final decision is not strongly supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies more on giving reasons for change than on eliciting the client's own motivations. There is limited exploration of personal values, goals, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely expert-driven, with the client responding passively. The psychiatrist does not fully co-create the plan with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, but it is not reinforced or expanded. The session does not systematically strengthen the client's own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not effectively softened or redirected. The psychiatrist's reassertion of advice may leave defensiveness unresolved.
