# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes slips without blame. The warmth is sometimes formulaic, which limits a higher rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Linda's ambivalence about being a role model and needing smoking to decompress. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and repetitive rather than deeply attuned to underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing Linda's smoking around her children and identifying small, doable steps. The focus becomes somewhat narrow on logistics rather than broader motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a collaborative stance by adapting the plan to Linda's feedback and simplifying steps that felt like too much. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist still generates much of the content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda's choice is frequently supported through permissive questions and confidence scaling. The repeated provision of specific options subtly steers the client toward clinician-preferred actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to elicit Linda's ideas about balance, doable steps, and slip responses. Evocation is limited by frequent introduction of suggestions rather than waiting for Linda to generate her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and adjusts the plan, showing some partnership. However, the psychiatrist frequently leads with specific ideas, reducing Linda's role as the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some appropriate attempts to draw out change talk through questions and confidence scaling. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify Linda's existing motivation, especially when she mentions not wanting her children to see her smoke.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and accepts Linda's fatigue, doubt, and reluctance to quit. Sustain talk is acknowledged but not effectively softened using deeper MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or discrepancy development.
