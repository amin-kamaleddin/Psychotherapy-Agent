# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses validating, nonjudgmental language and avoids labeling the client as avoidant or weak. It stops short of a 5 because the warmth could more explicitly affirm her worth beyond performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence and inner conflict, especially around wanting to follow through and fear of being “fake.” Empathy could deepen by slowing down and naming the emotional pain behind the self-criticism rather than staying cognitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear behavioral focus on stepping in and staying for five minutes despite panic. It could be stronger by periodically grounding abstract distinctions and moving more explicitly toward a concrete, doable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Elira’s own ideas, such as observing, five minutes, breathing, and noticing her feet, using her language throughout. Collaboration could improve by simplifying layered questions and giving her more explicit control over direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks what would work for Elira and avoids pressuring or imposing techniques. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit affirmation that any choice, including not attending, is hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are good evocative questions, but the psychiatrist also introduces several interpretations and possible interventions before fully exploring Elira’s own values and confidence. More direct evocation of change talk, confidence, and reasons for change would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elira’s experience and ideas clearly shape the session, and the psychiatrist avoids a purely expert-driven stance. Some questions are complex and multi-directional, which can feel slightly guided rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elira produces some change talk, especially around staying with discomfort and not letting fear drive her out. The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or deepen this language, nor systematically evoke confidence, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes some avoidance and protective motives but frequently returns to language around bailing, freezing, and doing it wrong. A stronger score would require more consistent strategic redirection using double-sided reflections that connect protection to her valued goals.
