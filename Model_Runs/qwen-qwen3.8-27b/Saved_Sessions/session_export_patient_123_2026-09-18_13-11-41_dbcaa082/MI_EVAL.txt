# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook unconditional positive regard throughout — no moment of judgment, labeling, or disapproval. Explicitly communicates safety and non-coercion ("I'm not here to get you in trouble or tell you what to do") and normalizes behavior without minimizing concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nuanced, layered reflections that honor both minimization and underlying value (e.g., catching both 'not really anything special' and 'nice to just be around other people'). Slightly limited by one interpretive question near the close that edges toward challenge rather than pure reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good rapport-building before introducing the target behavior, but the session ends abruptly without a summary or forward-looking close. A brief thematic recap or invitation to identify something to carry forward would have strengthened the sense of purpose.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session feels like a genuine conversation between equals, not a clinical interview. Language is consistently collaborative ("I'm wondering," "What's your sense") and the psychiatrist follows Sofia's lead without imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question is framed as an invitation, not an expectation; no directive language or pressure to change, reflect more deeply, or commit to anything. The strongest demonstration is accepting Sofia's pushback at the close without further intervention — the right to resist without penalty.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws out Sofia's own thinking and values without lecturing or providing external reasons for change. Could benefit from more strategic moves like scaling questions to deepen self-generated insights, and the final evocation question is slightly risky in tone.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Sofia as the expert on her own life ("Not what you think I want to hear — what do YOU actually think?") and repeatedly disclaims the expert role. The session is structured as a conversation between equals with the psychiatrist adjusting pace based on Sofia's engagement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evokes some spontaneous change talk (Sofia distinguishes between 'a couple drinks' and being 'into it'), but the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or strategically build on these moments. Change-oriented statements are passed over rather than expanded into deeper self-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing or arguing against Sofia's minimization, and uses strong softening statements like "I'm not here to manufacture a problem that isn't there." Could be strengthened with a double-sided reflection that more actively pivots sustain talk toward change without invalidating it.
