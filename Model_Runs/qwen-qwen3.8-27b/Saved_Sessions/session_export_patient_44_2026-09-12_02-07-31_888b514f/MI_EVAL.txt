# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental, normalizing the client's struggles without shaming him. Positive regard was strong but somewhat focused on progress and practicality rather than deeper, unconditional validation of Erik as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the client's progress, ambivalence, and some emotional impact of comparisons and guilt. However, the counselor often moved quickly into problem-solving before fully exploring the deeper shame, discouragement, or self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on improving gym consistency and related dietary choices. It effectively narrowed the conversation into specific, actionable targets and maintained momentum toward concrete change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and client jointly built a plan, with Erik contributing key ideas such as using nuts, placing them by his keys, and adding a warm-up. Collaboration was strong, but the counselor still provided structured option lists and drove the session with rapid, structured questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported client autonomy by inviting Erik to define his goals, choose options, and commit to small steps. The stance was supportive but occasionally felt slightly directive in its repeated emphasis on selecting and committing to specific actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked client-generated motivation through scaling questions and follow-ups that raised confidence and produced practical change statements. The evocation was strong behaviorally but could have explored values, identity, and deeper personal meaning more fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fostered partnership by allowing Erik's choices to shape the plan and building on his ideas rather than imposing a preset solution. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly positioning Erik as the expert and inviting his own solutions before offering options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicited change talk through confidence scaling and reinforced client-generated steps such as packing nuts and using a warm-up. The change talk was effective but primarily confidence- and behavior-based rather than value- or identity-based.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk without arguing or dismissing the client's concerns. However, the counselor did not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic redirection to transform sustain talk into deeper change talk.
