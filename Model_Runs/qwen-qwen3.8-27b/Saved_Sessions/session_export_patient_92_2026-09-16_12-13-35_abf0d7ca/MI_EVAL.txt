# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes ambivalence without disapproving of the client’s drinking. However, the stance is somewhat clinical and procedural, with fewer deep affirmations of the client’s strengths, values, or self.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate reflective listening about the client’s functional use of alcohol and his desire for concrete data. Deeper empathy around identity, fear of being labeled, and self-image is missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use, screening results, risks, and possible next steps. The main limitation is that it becomes information-heavy at times, which can dilute a more focused strategic direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences, respects the client’s request for the exact score, and offers choices. However, much of the structure and content remains expert-led rather than fully co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s autonomy is consistently supported through statements that he is in charge of next steps and that he is not required to change. Repeatedly offering experiments and risk information may create a mild sense of pressure despite the respectful delivery.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses appropriate evocative questions about importance, risks, and future outcomes. However, the session relies too heavily on external risk information rather than deeply eliciting the client’s own values, hopes, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partnership by asking for preferences and offering choices. The session still feels more psychiatrist-directed, with multiple options and clinical definitions shaping the conversation rather than fully following the client’s agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some attempts to elicit change-related thinking, but the client mainly offers sustain talk. The psychiatrist does not sufficiently amplify, affirm, or build on the limited change-oriented statements that do appear.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing or arguing against sustain talk, using neutral and accepting responses. More strategic MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections and deeper rolling with resistance, are limited.
