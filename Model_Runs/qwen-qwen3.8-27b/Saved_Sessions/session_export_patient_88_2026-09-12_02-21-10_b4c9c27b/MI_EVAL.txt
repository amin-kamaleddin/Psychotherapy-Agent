# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with statements like “that makes sense.” However, acceptance remains somewhat generic and repetitive rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Marcus’s perspective and several key concerns. The main limitation is that empathy stays mostly at the content level and does not consistently explore deeper feelings such as frustration, embarrassment, or fear of losing independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session moves toward a practical and manageable goal of sending a brief text to reduce family conflict. However, the focus drifts from the original concern of alcohol use and does not clearly maintain a specific drinking-related target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative tone and invites Marcus to choose among options. Collaboration is not fully equal, though, because the therapist generates much of the structure and many of the possible solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a strong area, with consistent respect for Marcus’s choice and minimal pressure. The counselor reinforces ownership by allowing Marcus to select the message, limit when he uses it, and decide not to answer calls.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-generated solutions are evoked, such as saving the text in notes and using it only on high-risk nights. The counselor does not consistently draw out Marcus’s deeper values, ambivalence about drinking, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative, with repeated invitations for Marcus to choose what feels doable. Partnership is limited by the fact that the psychiatrist still structures much of the agenda and moves the conversation toward specific interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus offers some change-related statements, and the counselor asks for a confidence rating. There are missed opportunities to more fully amplify his reasons, values, concerns, and confidence in making change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Marcus’s resistance. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened through double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of ambivalence.
