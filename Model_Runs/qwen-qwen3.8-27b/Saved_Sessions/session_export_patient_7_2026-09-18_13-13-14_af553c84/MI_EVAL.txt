# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates nonjudgmental, accepting language and normalizes the client's resistance. One moment of over-interpretation on 'It's fine' slightly undermines acceptance by attributing emotional complexity the client did not express.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking on feelings of coercion and autonomy struggles. However, empathy is sometimes directed at therapist-generated interpretations rather than the client's actual stated experience, limiting precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a general focus on the client's experience and referral context but lacks a clear strategic direction or arc. The session wanders through a series of reflections without building toward a specific outcome or identifying a focus for future work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language but the interaction is not genuinely collaborative; the psychiatrist does the vast majority of the talking. More silence, more questions, and fewer unsolicited interpretations would better honor the collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms the client's right to choose and explicitly validates her sense of being coerced without pressing further. Could go further in affirming the client's capacity for self-direction and naming her strengths in managing her own boundaries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only one evocative attempt is made and it is shut down without follow-up. No scaling questions, exploration of values, or varied evocative strategies are attempted despite the client's extreme resistance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Non-authoritarian stance with some collaborative language, but the psychiatrist responds to every minimal response with interpretations, driving the interaction. True partnership with a resistant client would require more tolerance of silence and letting the client set the pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal attempts to evoke change talk; no development of desire, ability, reasons, or need talk. The one evocative question is answered with 'I had to' and is not revisited or reframed through alternative approaches.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is absorbed without arguing or reinforcing it, and the response to 'It's not your business' is well-handled. However, there is no active redirection, double-sided reflection, or creative reframing to transform the sustain talk toward change.
