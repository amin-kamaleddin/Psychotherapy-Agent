# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client’s concerns. Could show deeper individualized warmth around his stress, vulnerability, and past failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and perspective-taking regarding his frustration, work caution, and preference for simplicity. Reflections remain mainly cognitive and could explore emotional undertones and ambivalence more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps the session tightly focused on smoking cessation, triggers, NRT planning, Friday start, and follow-up. Strong, consistent focus with little irrelevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Builds a practical plan around David’s stated preferences and constraints. Interaction is partly expert-driven because the clinician selects the specific regimen and dose rather than co-constructing it more explicitly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and respects his refusal of role-play and limited spousal involvement. Does not consistently reinforce that David owns the decision and change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some opening MI-consistent questions and rulers to draw out motivation. The session shifts too quickly into advice, missing opportunities to evoke confidence, values, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and adapts to preferences, showing collaboration. The clinician still occupies an expert decision-maker role, especially in recommending combination NRT.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk with initial questions and rulers but does not consistently develop or amplify it. More targeted follow-up questions could strengthen reasons, values, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and acknowledges concerns without judgment. Does not consistently use strategic or double-sided reflections to reframe doubts, past failure, or social discomfort as ambivalence.
