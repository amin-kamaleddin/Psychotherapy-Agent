# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout the conversation. Acceptance could be stronger by explicitly normalizing Mai’s frustration about receiving a prescription without explanation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening reflection accurately captures Mai’s confusion and practical concern. Empathic engagement fades as the session shifts into an informational role, with less attention to her uncertainty, worry, and relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on understanding and safely starting atorvastatin, including dosing, timing, interactions, and warning signs. Direction could be improved by more consistently linking the plan to Mai’s personal goals or reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses Mai’s routines and preferences, such as taking medications at night and using a phone reminder, to build the plan. However, the clinician remains the primary source of medical information and direction, limiting full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician supports Mai’s sense of choice by offering options, accepting her decision to proceed, and avoiding pressure. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing that she is in charge of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician provides the medication rationale directly rather than eliciting Mai’s own values, concerns, or confidence. There is little effort to draw out personal motivations or amplify her reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician incorporates Mai’s input and preferences into the plan, showing some partnership. However, the session is heavily expert-driven in the middle, and the collaboration does not consistently feel fully mutual.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai offers practical change talk and the clinician affirms it. The clinician does not actively deepen the change talk by exploring importance, confidence, or long-term meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai’s mild sustain talk is validated without argument, and the clinician avoids reinforcing doubt. The response is more educational than MI-skillful, lacking double-sided reflections or strategic redirection toward her own reasons.
