# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows unconditional positive regard, normalizes ambivalence, and separates identity from behavior without judgment or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially in double-sided reflections of the client's conflict. It would be stronger with more explicit validation of the emotional weight behind the client's thoughts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session focuses well on shoplifting and moves toward a concrete in-the-moment plan. Earlier direction could be clearer by explicitly connecting the plan to the referral and reducing future legal consequences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner, co-constructing the plan through reflection and questions. The client's ideas directly shape the session rather than being met with expert-driven solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with no directive or pressuring language. The psychiatrist invites choice and helps the client own the plan as his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended, client-centered questions effectively draw out the client's own plan. Evocation would be stronger if it more explicitly accessed values, confidence, importance of change, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert on his own life, triggers, and friends. The session avoids lecturing and builds on client-generated ideas rather than imposing a predetermined intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes and reinforces meaningful change talk, including the client's self-generated coping plan. It would be stronger with more explicit deepening through DARN-C language, confidence rulers, or commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing or moralizing and is paired with change talk. It would be stronger if more explicitly reframed as a value conflict that can coexist with change.
