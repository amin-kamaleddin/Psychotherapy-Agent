# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful, normalizing, and nonjudgmental tone. However, repeated introductions of practical steps despite the client's explicit reluctance make the acceptance feel conditional on behavioral change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's surface-level ambivalence about smoking and parenting. The reflections remain mostly cognitive and miss deeper emotional and identity-based concerns, such as her need to be seen as a good person.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking and its impact on the client's children. The direction is process-inappropriate for a pre-contemplative client because it repeatedly steers toward concrete behavioral steps rather than motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely advisor-led, with the psychiatrist supplying lists, scripts, and structured options. The client's own ideas are accepted but then expanded by the counselor rather than treated as sufficient and explored from the client's perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and acknowledges the client's right to decline. Nevertheless, continued suggestions undermine the client's stated autonomy and her clear message that she is not quitting or ready for major life changes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with a strong open question, but evocation largely stops after the initial exchange. The psychiatrist rarely explores values, meanings, readiness, or the client's internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative language is used, but the dominant pattern is the psychiatrist offering pre-designed options and scripts. This positions the psychiatrist as the expert with the solution rather than a partner following the client's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some spontaneous change talk, especially around being a role model for her son. The psychiatrist does not actively amplify, deepen, or build on this language to create momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's sustain talk is clear and recurring, but the psychiatrist mostly acknowledges it and then pivots to advice. There is little use of double-sided reflections or exploration of what the sustain talk protects or means.
