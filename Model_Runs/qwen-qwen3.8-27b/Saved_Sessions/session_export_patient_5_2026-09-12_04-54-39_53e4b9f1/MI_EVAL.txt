# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating Alessia’s concerns. The validation is effective but sometimes brief and somewhat formulaic, limiting deeper individualized rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective statements accurately track Alessia’s worry, ambivalence, and need for structure. The empathy is accurate but surface-level in places, missing opportunities to explore deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused and logically moves from education to a concrete medication-transition plan. Momentum is maintained without drift, ending with clear, actionable deliverables.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and responds to Alessia’s preferences when building the plan. Collaboration is strong in form, though the psychiatrist still generates most of the substantive content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers choices, avoids directive persuasion, and respects Alessia’s hesitation. Autonomy is respected overall, though the structured plan subtly positions the psychiatrist as the main decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions elicit Alessia’s plans, supports, and readiness, but much of the session relies on information-giving rather than drawing out her own thinking. The psychiatrist could better evoke her own reasoning, values, and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone with frequent input-seeking and option offers. However, the psychiatrist’s strong expert role creates an uneven dynamic that limits genuine co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges naturally as Alessia’s confidence increases. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or strategically reinforce this language to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Alessia’s resistance without judgment and addresses practical concerns. However, MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or values-based reframing are not consistently used to work with ambivalence.
