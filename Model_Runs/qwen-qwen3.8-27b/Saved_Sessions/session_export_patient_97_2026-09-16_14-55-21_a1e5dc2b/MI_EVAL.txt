# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental regard while affirming Mateo’s strengths and efforts. Mateo is treated as a person with ambivalence, not reduced to his offense.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Mateo’s ambivalence, frustration, and fear of relapse. The counseling would benefit from deeper affective reflections exploring shame, fear of failure, or hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing recidivism and building a stable family future. The conversation moves logically from motivation to concrete action, such as the work license and proof of address.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Mateo as an equal partner and adapts to his preferred pace. Choices are made together, such as starting with one form and asking one question at a time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mateo’s choice and ownership are consistently emphasized without pressure or persuasion. The therapist offers options while reinforcing that Mateo is the expert on his own process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Mateo’s goals, concerns, and practical ideas, but some questions are multiple-choice or mildly leading. More open evocative questions could deepen Mateo’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly conveys that expertise about Mateo’s life and change resides with Mateo. Session direction is shaped by Mateo’s preferences and contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently draws out practical reasons for change and reinforces Mateo’s efforts. Greater emphasis on evoking confidence, commitment, and ability would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, minimizing, or moralizing. Stronger double-sided reflections could better balance sustain talk and change talk.
