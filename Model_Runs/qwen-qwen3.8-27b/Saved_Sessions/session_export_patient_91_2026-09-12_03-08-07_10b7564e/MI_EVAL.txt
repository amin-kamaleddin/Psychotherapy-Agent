# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, often affirming Marcus's caution and effort. Acceptance was slightly limited by a formulaic, mildly directive tone and less individualized recognition of his fear and role transition.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected surface concerns about confusion, overwhelm, and wanting to avoid mistakes. Empathy remained largely cognitive and did not fully explore Marcus's underlying fear of failure, shame about wasting resources, or identity-level challenge.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on diabetes self-management and successfully narrowed the work to a concrete first step: turning the meter on. Direction was somewhat overly procedural at times, with less connection to Marcus's broader values or reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus's preferences shaped some decisions, and the psychiatrist offered choices and supported his selections. Collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist remained the main source of instructions, checklists, and plan organization.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized Marcus's choice, pace, and control over whether and how to try the step. Autonomy support was slightly weakened by the amount of scripting, instruction, and checklist-building.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions elicited Marcus's own intentions, preferences, and reasons for trying. Evocation was limited by frequent shifts into external instruction and limited follow-up on his confidence and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered choices, showing genuine partnership in moments. However, the expert-led procedural role reduced the sense that Marcus's own expertise and perspective were central.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions such as choosing a first step and rating confidence elicited some change talk and willingness to try. Change talk was not consistently deepened, affirmed, or amplified, especially after his 4/10 confidence statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalized concerns and reframed barriers into small, manageable actions without arguing or reinforcing them. Some sustain talk was addressed with practical problem-solving rather than more advanced MI strategies like rolling with resistance.
