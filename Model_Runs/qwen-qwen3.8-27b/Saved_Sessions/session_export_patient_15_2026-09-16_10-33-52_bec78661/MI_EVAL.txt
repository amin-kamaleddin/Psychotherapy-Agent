# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance toward Andrei’s practical and low-effort preferences. Acceptance is solid but could be deeper by more explicitly affirming his strengths, values, or the difficulty of changing long-standing habits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Andrei’s perspective, especially his desire for control and low-effort solutions. However, the reflections remain relatively surface-level and do not fully explore deeper meanings or underlying ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on blood pressure management and progresses toward a concrete action plan. However, it is limited by the failure to explore alcohol use despite it being part of the stated clinical focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a strong collaborative stance by inviting preferences and allowing Andrei’s choices to shape the final plan. It falls short of full collaboration because the clinician still generates many of the specific solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Andrei’s sense of choice and avoids pressuring him to make larger changes. It could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing that the patient owns the decision-making process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses choice-based questions and confidence scaling to elicit intention and confidence. However, evocation is limited because much of the motivational content is supplied by the psychiatrist rather than drawn out from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative posture by inviting Andrei’s preferences and offering choices. However, the plan feels partly expert-driven because the psychiatrist generates many options before fully eliciting the patient’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk, particularly intention and planning language, which helps move the patient toward commitment. However, the evocation is not consistently deep or skillful, and change talk remains limited to brief intention statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and redirects it toward simpler, doable options. However, the softening is not consistently skillful, and alcohol-related sustain talk was effectively avoided rather than skillfully addressed.
