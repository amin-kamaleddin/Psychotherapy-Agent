# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally uses respectful, supportive language that conveys acceptance. However, warmth is sometimes generic and does not fully validate the client's urgent medication-safety concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy improves when safety is addressed, but earlier responses miss the client's central need for a direct answer. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect the urgency and specific meaning of the client's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist eventually maintains clear direction by directly addressing the dangerous medication combination. Early in the session, however, the conversation drifts into broader questions before answering the client's urgent question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative questions and teamwork-oriented language, but collaboration is uneven. The client's stated priority for a direct safety answer is not fully incorporated early in the interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the client's choice to leave and frames her safety decision as her own. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing her ongoing freedom to decide after consulting her prescriber.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help the client articulate a safety-focused reason for action, such as not getting poisoned. The psychiatrist does not fully expand the client's change talk, values, confidence, or planned next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and avoids an authoritarian stance. Partnership is weakened by continuing broader questions after the client repeatedly requests a direct medication-safety answer.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some meaningful change talk and reflects the client's decision to avoid the medication. Evocation remains limited because there is little deeper exploration of desire, need, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles the client's time constraints and desire to leave without arguing or pressuring her. Softening is strong but mostly neutral acknowledgment rather than more skillful reframing of the barrier.
