# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or arguing with the patient. Acceptance is clear but somewhat formulaic, without deeper affirmation of the patient as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient's practical stance and skepticism, but misses opportunities to reflect deeper emotions and underlying concerns. Empathy is adequate but remains surface-level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on mini-cigar use, oral health concerns, practical steps, and warning signs. There is some repetition near the end, but the focus remains clear and relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on a practical plan using the patient's own ideas, such as the truck, dash, and rinsing. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary organizer and information provider rather than fully sharing control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist honors the patient's choices, accepts his limits, and avoids pushing rejected options like alarms or check-ins. Autonomy is well supported, though the psychiatrist still offers a fairly detailed and structured plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions about what would need to change and which steps feel doable. However, the session becomes more educational and prescriptive, with limited follow-up on the patient's own values, concerns, or motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient's input and choices, showing some partnership. After the opening, the interaction becomes more counselor-directed, with the psychiatrist driving much of the structure and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that encourage the patient to consider reduction and identifies a small concern about loose teeth as a reason to try. The patient's change talk remains limited and is not consistently expanded or strengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or reinforcing the patient's minimization of the problem. However, the strongest sustain talk is not consistently redirected using more nuanced reflections or pivot statements.
