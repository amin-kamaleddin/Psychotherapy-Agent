# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance. It falls slightly short because the positive regard is more practical and some reflections are mildly leading.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures the patient's ambivalence and emotional experience. It lacks a perfect score due to some slightly repetitive reflections and a missed opportunity to explore deeper emotional fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a clear and tight focus on the target behavior without any topic drift. The focus is progressively and skillfully narrowed to a concrete, manageable checking routine.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is highly collaborative, treating the patient as an equal partner whose constraints directly determine the final plan. The psychiatrist avoids imposing a standard protocol and instead invites joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is highly consistent, explicitly emphasizing the patient's freedom to choose without any directive or controlling language. The clinician clearly respects the patient's boundaries regarding how much information and action the routine demands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of open-ended questions effectively draws out practical feasibility and concrete action ideas from the patient. It lacks a perfect score because it does not fully elicit deeper personal values or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent demonstration of partnership by consistently placing authority and expertise with the patient. The session is entirely shaped around Jonathan's stated constraints and needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently and effectively uses questions to evoke the patient's own change language regarding a manageable routine. It lacks a 5 because it doesn't systematically deepen the change talk or powerfully summarize and amplify the patient's statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully handles the patient's resistance by normalizing his concerns without arguing or pressuring. It falls short because the underlying worry about what to do if the number is high remains unresolved.
