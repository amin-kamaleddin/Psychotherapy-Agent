# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and uses respectful, approving language. Acceptance remains more procedural than warmly individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor offers brief acknowledgments but limited accurate perspective-taking. There is little deeper exploration of the patient’s feelings, ambivalence, or personal meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on BP monitoring, logging, and safety. However, direction remains task-completion oriented rather than motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses collaborative language and offers joint review of the log. The session remains largely clinician-led with repeated practical instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient is given some choices about pace and start time. The repeated prescriptive plan limits stronger autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks an open opening question but then moves quickly into closed, practical guidance. The patient’s own values and motivations are not deeply evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes partnership cues such as “set a small goal together.” The overall stance remains more expert-driven than genuinely collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some commitment to start the log and breathing practice. Deeper change talk about health values, ability, or reasons for change is not developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue with the patient’s downplaying of alcohol. Sustain talk is accepted but not strategically redirected toward change.
