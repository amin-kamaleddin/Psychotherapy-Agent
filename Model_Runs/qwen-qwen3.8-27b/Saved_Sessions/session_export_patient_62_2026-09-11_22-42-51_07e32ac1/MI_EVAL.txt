# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental. Acceptance is steady but somewhat generic, without consistently expressing deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, capturing exhaustion, frustration, and repeated disappointment. Some deeper emotions such as shame, loneliness, and feeling trapped are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear focus on the electric bill and related stress. Direction is weakened by repeatedly introducing multiple options, making the conversation feel scattered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, creating a collaborative surface. The session remains counselor-led, with the client's preference to be heard not fully shaping the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are repeated offers of choice and respect for the client's decision. However, continued suggestions after the client declines can feel like gentle pressure rather than full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked early, especially around not feeling stuck. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or expand Jordan's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone is collaborative, with questions and options offered rather than a single plan imposed. The psychiatrist often shifts into problem-solving, limiting the client's role in guiding the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges when Jordan says he wants to not be stuck. These statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or built on throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or reinforcing hopelessness directly. Sustain talk remains central because softer MI strategies, such as reframing or cost-benefit questions, are not consistently used.
