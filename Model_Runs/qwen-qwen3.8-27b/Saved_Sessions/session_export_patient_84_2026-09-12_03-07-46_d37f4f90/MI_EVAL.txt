# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming Malik. However, some validating statements risk reinforcing minimization rather than fully accepting the underlying ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections track Malik's stated perspective and frustration well. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore deeper adolescent concerns about autonomy, peer belonging, or being labeled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to cannabis use, risk, and the mother-child conflict. Direction toward a specific collaborative change target is inconsistent, with more emphasis on harm-reduction advice and ending the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites Malik to select or modify options, supporting collaboration. Some key content remains clinician-generated, which can create a subtle expert-driven dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes Malik's right to choose and avoids coercion. Autonomy support could be stronger by affirming Malik's ownership of change, not only his freedom to refuse or leave.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate open-ended attempts to elicit Malik's perspective and concerns. Evocation is limited by the use of clinician-generated advice and scripts instead of consistently drawing out and amplifying Malik's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting input, offering options, and asking permission before sharing ideas. The structured list of suggestions and scripts reduces the sense of Malik's own direction shaping the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions but does not consistently notice, reflect, or amplify small pieces of change talk. The session shifts toward advice and script writing rather than developing Malik's own reasons or commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation with Malik's sustain talk, which is appropriate. There is limited strategic softening, and some statements inadvertently reinforce the status quo rather than exploring ambivalence.
