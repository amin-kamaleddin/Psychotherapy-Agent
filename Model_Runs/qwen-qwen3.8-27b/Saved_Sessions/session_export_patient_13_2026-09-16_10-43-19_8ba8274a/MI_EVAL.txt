# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Anara’s fear and fatigue. Acceptance is warm but somewhat formulaic, with less individualized affirmation of her strengths, values, and capacity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture key concerns accurately but remain relatively surface-level and repetitive. There are missed opportunities to reflect deeper ambivalence, shifting emotions, and values such as protecting herself and her baby.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of obtaining medical evaluation while reducing fear and overwhelm. It is somewhat logistics-focused rather than consistently tied to Anara’s internal motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaboration in shaping the plan and co-creating a short note, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies most of the content. The interaction feels more counselor-led than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and respects Anara’s limits, such as allowing her to sit with the decision for a day. However, repeated provision of ready-made scripts may reduce her ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited after the initial open-ended questions, as the session quickly shifts to scripts and practical options. There is little exploration of Anara’s values, confidence, reasons for change, or internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anara’s preferences shape the plan, showing some partnership. However, the psychiatrist often assumes an expert or scribe role rather than consistently inviting Anara to lead with her own knowledge and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Anara produces some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke, reinforce, or expand it. There is limited use of scaling, values-based questions, or amplification of her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, blaming, or dismissing the client. The psychiatrist does not use more strategic MI moves such as double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or reframing to help resolve ambivalence.
