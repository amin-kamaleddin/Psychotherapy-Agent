# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, affirming language and validates Jonathan’s frustration without judgment. The approach is supportive but somewhat formulaic, lacking deeper affirmation of identity and chronic illness loss.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and consistent, but they remain surface-level and miss the deeper grief around aging, loss of autonomy, and identity. This limits the depth expected for higher empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays topically focused but moves in a somewhat circular pattern of validation, offering techniques, and preference checks. It lacks strategic momentum, summarization, or a collaborative focus on diabetes self-management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and offers choices, but the structure relies on presenting a menu of techniques rather than following Jonathan’s lead. This makes collaboration feel somewhat superficial.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms Jonathan’s freedom, avoids pressure, and explicitly permits refusal. However, the repeated offering of alternatives after refusal subtly reintroduces the pressure to choose something.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly closed or referential and do not deeply explore Jonathan’s values, motivations, or reasons for change. Jonathan’s brief change talk is acknowledged but not amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, which supports a collaborative stance, but frequently fills pauses with techniques and positions the clinician as the solution provider. This unevenness weakens true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is minimal; the important chip-on-shelf behavior is acknowledged but not explored as a foundation for further motivation. The session misses opportunities to expand DARN-CAT language or evoke reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated without argument or dismissal, which is appropriate. However, the psychiatrist does not use deeper softening strategies, such as double-sided reflections or exploring the cost of the status quo.
