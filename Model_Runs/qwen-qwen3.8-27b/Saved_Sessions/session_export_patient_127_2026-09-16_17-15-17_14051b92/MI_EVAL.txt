# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained respect, patience, and nonjudgment while validating Timur’s ambivalence and struggles. Acceptance was strong but somewhat procedural, with room for warmer, more individualized affirmation of his worth independent of performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used effective reflective listening to capture Timur’s mixed feelings and ambivalence. Empathy was solid but did not consistently reach deeper emotional layers such as shame, fear, or identity struggle.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on reducing the risk of returning to old, crime-related patterns and building a prosocial routine. The focus became increasingly concrete, moving from broad goals to specific, testable behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist followed Timur’s ideas and built on his suggestions rather than controlling the session. Collaboration was strong in practice but could be strengthened with more explicit joint language such as “let’s” or “we.”

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Timur’s choice and avoided directives, ultimatums, or pressure. Open questions and language of ownership kept decision-making firmly with Timur.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out Timur’s own motivations and strategies rather than offering external advice. Evocation was good but sometimes relied on counselor-framed concepts that subtly steered Timur’s thinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treated Timur as the expert on his own life and experience. Timur’s ideas substantially shaped the direction, content, and final behavioral plan of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used evocative questions and reflective reinforcement to elicit Timur’s reasons for change. Change talk was effectively supported but not consistently amplified or deepened into stronger identity- and values-based confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk without argument or resistance while pairing it with change-oriented meaning. The work could be stronger by more actively minimizing sustain talk and pivoting Timur further toward values, safety, and identity.
