# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, never expressing disapproval of Erik's food choices or inconsistent gym attendance. Language is respectful throughout, with no labeling or shaming present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are nuanced and capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions, such as the complex reflection of the sundae-donut pattern and the attunement to Erik's vulnerability in asking for permission. These are not generic but demonstrate deep, individualized understanding of Erik's specific experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from progress update through barrier exploration to a concrete coping strategy without drifting off topic. The closing could benefit from a more explicit collaborative summary tying the insight back to the specific behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine collaboration where Erik's contributions substantially shape the direction, including key insights like 'no neutral option' and 'there you are.' The psychiatrist builds on Erik's language without overriding it, maintaining a flat power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Erik's ownership of the change process, explicitly rejecting a top-down authority dynamic with 'You don't need permission.' There is zero evidence of pressuring or directive language; the final coping strategy is entirely Erik-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every major insight in the session is Erik-generated, with the psychiatrist's role limited to creating conditions for emergence through strategic questions and reflections. The psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change; all motivation is drawn from Erik's own values and self-knowledge.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Erik, using open-ended questions and reflections that invite confirmation rather than imposing interpretation. When Erik shares the insight about his dad, the psychiatrist allows Erik to make his own connections rather than offering a psychodynamic reading.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes substantial change talk through well-timed strategic questions that elicit Erik's own values and intentions. Could have more explicitly affirmed and amplified Erik's change talk before moving forward to their own interpretation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Erik's barriers without reinforcing them or arguing against them, gently redirecting toward his own resources. Could have more actively pivoted sustain talk into change talk in a few moments, such as when Erik said 'maybe this isn't for me.'
