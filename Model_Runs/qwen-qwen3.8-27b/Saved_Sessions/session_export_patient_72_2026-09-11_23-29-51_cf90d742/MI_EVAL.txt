# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental throughout. However, acceptance is somewhat task-oriented and could more deeply affirm the client’s emotional experience, courage, and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some effort to understand the client’s perspective, but reflective listening is limited. The counselor often moves quickly into problem-solving rather than accurately reflecting the client’s feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a very clear focus on the target behavior of re-establishing a flexible exercise routine. It consistently moves toward concrete, relevant action and implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and accepts several client preferences, but much of the plan, schedule, and advice is still counselor-generated. This leaves the power balance uneven and limits fully shared ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, uses flexible aim-for language, and respects the client’s dislikes of contracts and rigidity. Autonomy support could be stronger by reducing directive advising and more explicitly reinforcing client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s focus and preferences, but evocation is not consistently developed. There are missed opportunities to elicit values, confidence, motivations, and commitment before shifting into planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influences parts of the plan, but the session remains fairly counselor-led with quick structured recommendations. A stronger partnership stance would invite the client to lead more of the agenda and co-create the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses clear change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or expands those reasons for change. Evocation is present but underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with the client’s barriers and redirects them into practical alternatives. However, the use of double-sided reflections or gentle reframing is limited, making the softening skill only moderate.
