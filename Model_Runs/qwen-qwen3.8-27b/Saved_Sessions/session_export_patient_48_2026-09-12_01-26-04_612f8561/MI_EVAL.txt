# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing self-doubt, fatigue, and missed first attempts. Acceptance is supportive but somewhat procedural, often moving quickly into planning rather than fully validating emotional concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are adequate for practical worries but often brief and content-focused. Deeper feelings such as fear of self-criticism and ambivalence about follow-through could have been explored more skillfully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a concrete, realistic physical-activity step, from choosing a walk to reducing friction and planning a backup. The target behavior remains central throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and incorporates Farid’s preferred options, such as a lunch walk, 30-second reset, and mental picture. Collaboration is strong but sometimes tempered by substantial therapist-generated advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Farid is offered choices, low-pressure options, and permission to leave backup plans open. Autonomy support is generally good but occasionally undercut by early directive advice and specific recommendations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session asks some logistical questions but does not consistently evoke intrinsic motivation, values, or reasons for change. It moves quickly from ambivalence into advice and behavioral planning, leaving internal reasons underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership through invitations, shared language, and use of Farid’s input. The stance is less fully developed because the psychiatrist still leads with advice rather than consistently treating Farid as the expert on his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws some tentative change talk through small commitments and affirms those moments. However, it does not consistently amplify intrinsic reasons, confidence, values, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes and reframes shame without arguing, such as saying tiredness is not laziness and missing a first time is useful information. It does not consistently use strategic reflections or redirections to shift barrier-focused sustain talk toward change.
