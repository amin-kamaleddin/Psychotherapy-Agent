# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard. Judgment or disapproval was absent even when the client resisted change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client’s ambivalence, stress, and boundary needs. The repeated use of “I hear you” made the empathy feel somewhat formulaic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clearly related target: reducing marital conflict through a specific 20-minute boundary. The pacing moved logically from broad exploration to a concrete weekly plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invited the client’s input and co-constructed the intervention. The final plan was shaped by the client’s preferences rather than imposed by the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor reinforced the client’s freedom to choose and owned his values. Directive pressure was avoided, especially when the client resisted additional steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and reflective prompts helped the client identify his own motivation and practical plan. The psychiatrist successfully drew out the client’s ideas rather than giving advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative, non-expert stance throughout the session. The client was positioned as the primary guide for the direction of the work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited actionable change talk from a resistant client. The client moved from denial to committing to a specific, small behavioral plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reflected without argument or confrontation. The psychiatrist gently redirected resistant statements toward smaller, compatible changes.
