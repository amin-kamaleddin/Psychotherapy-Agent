# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, conveying care without blaming the client. However, the warmth sometimes feels procedural because the conversation quickly shifts into advice, reassurance, and safety scripting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor reflects some of the client's exhaustion and difficulty, but the reflections are often brief or generic. Deeper emotional themes such as fear of failing the baby, guilt, isolation, and frustration are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly focused on postpartum exhaustion, sleep, and securing support. However, the counselor introduces many practical topics at once, which makes the focus feel diffuse rather than narrowed to one target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moves, such as drafting a text message and asking about who could help. Overall, though, the counselor frequently leads with their own list of options and recommendations rather than treating the client as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor respects some choices, including offering a text draft, a shorter version, and no-pressure language. At the same time, the frequent advice-giving and directive tone can weaken the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor evokes very little of the client's internal motivation, spending much of the session giving information and practical suggestions. There is limited effort to draw out the client's own reasons for change, values, or confidence in her ability to get help.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some open or clarifying questions and collaborates on the text message. Nevertheless, the session is often driven by the counselor's agenda and expertise, creating an uneven problem-solver dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses change talk, but the counselor does not consistently elicit or deepen it. Affirmations are present but limited, and most counselor turns remain solution-focused rather than evocative.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally does not argue with or dismiss the client's sustain talk, and offers some normalizing statements. However, MI-style softening strategies such as double-sided reflections or value-based reframing are not consistently used.
