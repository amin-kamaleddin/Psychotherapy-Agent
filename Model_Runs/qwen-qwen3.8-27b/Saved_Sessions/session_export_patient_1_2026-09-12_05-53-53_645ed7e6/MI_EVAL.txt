# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful with unconditional positive regard, but feels formulaic and procedural rather than deeply warm. Few explicit affirmations of Ahmed's strengths or willingness to engage despite limited belief in the smoking explanation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures Ahmed's perspective and skepticism toward the cigars. Largely cognitive without exploring underlying emotional undercurrents like fear of being lectured or loss of a valued stress-relief ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session quickly drifts from the mini-cigar target to brushing technique, flossing, and general oral hygiene. Little strategic momentum toward clarifying readiness or a specific goal related to cigar use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices, seeks Ahmed's preferences, and adapts the plan based on his responses (floss picks, electric timer). However, options are largely clinician-generated rather than drawn from Ahmed's own problem-solving ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong respect for autonomy with options offered, "only if you're open" language, and questions supporting Ahmed's sense of choice. Some advice remains quite prescriptive (specific angles, timing, frequency), though softened by alternatives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some open-ended questions, but the conversation becomes a practical problem-solving session about brushing and flossing. No evocative questions targeting Ahmed's values, health concerns, or internal reasons for considering cigar reduction.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative stance with invitations for participation and some plan adaptation to Ahmed's input. Still frames the agenda around dental hygiene and a two-week monitoring plan rather than tobacco-related change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some weak attempts to evoke change language, but not consistently directed at reducing mini-cigar use. Ahmed's limited change talk (e.g., "maybe I'll talk" if symptoms persist) is not amplified, explored, or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoids direct confrontation and judgment (a strength), but does not effectively redirect Ahmed's strong sustain talk about cigars being "tiny" and "just a quick unwind." Minimal use of double-sided reflections, gentle reframing, or questions to help him notice the cigars' function.
