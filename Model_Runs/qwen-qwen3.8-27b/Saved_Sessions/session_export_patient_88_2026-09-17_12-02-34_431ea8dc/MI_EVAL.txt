**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout. Opening with "That takes guts, honestly" and "I respect that" validates Marcus without conditions. ### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Frames the entire session as a collaborative exploration. Language like "help me understand" and "I'm curious" positions the psychiatrist as a fellow explorer rather than a clinician conducting an assessment. Actively incorporates Marcus's contributions into the direction of the session—building on exactly the criteria Marcus names rather than introducing external ones. Session feels like a genuine conversation, not a clinical interview.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** The hallmark of this session. Never pressures, persuades, or implies Marcus "should" change. Establishes freedom from external coercion ("I'm not here to get you in trouble"). Explicitly affirms ownership ("it's your call"). Removes deadlines or pressure ("tonight, next week, never"). Credits Marcus for his own shift rather than taking credit. Offers a complete exit when resistance emerges. Textbook autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws out Marcus's own motivations and values rather than imposing external reasons for change. The strategic question "What would have to be true for you to go, 'okay, that's actually worth a closer look'?" effectively elicits Marcus's own threshold criteria. Allows Marcus to articulate his own ambivalence rather than labeling it for him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that Marcus is the expert on his own life. Phrases like "help me understand," "I'm curious," and "what would it look like" position the psychiatrist as a curious learner rather than an authority figure. Explicitly acknowledges Marcus's autonomy in decisions. Never assumes expertise about Marcus's peer group, experiences, or values. When Marcus resists, doesn't assert authority—offers an exit, reinforcing Marcus is in charge.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits change talk through strategic open questions rather than directives. The question about what would have to be true for the situation to become a problem effectively draws out Marcus's own values and concerns. However, Marcus's brief acknowledgments (e.g., "a little, not a lot") could have been explored with slightly more strategic follow-up questions to build momentum toward confidence and ability statements.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Perhaps the most impressive aspect of the session. Never argues with Marcus's sustain talk ("everyone does it," "it's not a big deal"). Uses double-sided reflections and gentle reframing. When Marcus says "it's not my whole identity," the psychiatrist reflects this back as evidence that things DO matter to him, without invalidating his point. When Marcus asks "what's the point of worrying," the psychiatrist doesn't counter-argue but gently explores whether it's a belief or a gut feeling. Exceptional handling.
