# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and avoids moralizing or blaming Jason. The tone is supportive but somewhat procedural, and it does not fully affirm Jason’s broader worth beyond his frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Jason’s frustration, sense of unfairness, and conflicted feelings about appealing. However, empathy stays mostly at the administrative level and misses deeper fears of being labeled or of losing his sense of responsibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the write-up and possible administrative next steps, avoiding drift into unrelated topics. However, the direction is largely procedural and does not consistently guide Jason toward examining his alcohol use or aligning his behavior with his goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses collaborative language, offers options, and adapts to Jason’s preference for speaking over writing. Still, the clinician supplies much of the content, so Jason is not fully positioned as the primary source of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jason’s choice is frequently respected, including his discomfort with writing and his preference for a spoken script. The provision of fairly specific wording slightly weakens autonomy support by implying a preferred response.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician begins with useful evocative questions and readiness rulers, but then shifts toward advising, scripting, and problem-solving. This limits the development of Jason’s own values, motivations, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and avoids an authoritarian stance, which supports a collaborative tone. However, the clinician often becomes the problem-solver and script provider rather than drawing out Jason’s expertise about his own decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jason offers some change-related statements, such as wanting to focus on school and getting home safely, but the clinician does not consistently amplify these as values-based reasons for change. The session moves into practical scripting before building on Jason’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jason expresses considerable minimization and sustain talk, and the clinician does not directly argue, but often normalizes the minimization. The scripts and responses keep the conversation centered on defending the behavior rather than exploring discrepancy, responsibility, or longer-term consequences.
