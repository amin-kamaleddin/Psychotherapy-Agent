# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of the client’s effort, which helps reduce shame. Some affirmations are plan-focused rather than fully unconditional toward the client’s ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a useful reflection of drinking as an automatic way to quiet the mind, but the session quickly shifts to practical problem-solving. Deeper feelings, doubts, and ambivalence are underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on reducing automatic after-work drinking and moves clearly from concern to a small experiment. It becomes behavioral a little quickly before fully exploring motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is strongly co-created through the client’s choices about the walk, music, alarm, and backup. The psychiatrist still supplies much of the structure and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses invitational language, offers options, and explicitly supports the client’s right to choose. Repeated commitment prompts could feel subtly pressuring given the client’s ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some of the client’s own views and practical change talk are drawn out effectively. There is limited exploration of values, identity, or deeper intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on his routine and choices, and the client substantially shapes the plan. The session would be more partner-like if the client were invited to set the agenda earlier.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, the confidence ruler, and focus on a small personal benefit effectively elicit change talk. The change talk remains relatively practical rather than values-based.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client’s doubts and offers practical downscaling. There is less use of reflective or double-sided responses to transform sustain talk into change talk.
