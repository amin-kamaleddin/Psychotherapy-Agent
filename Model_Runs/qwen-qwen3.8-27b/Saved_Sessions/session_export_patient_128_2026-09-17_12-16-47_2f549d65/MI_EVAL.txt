# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone, validating Tyler's complexity and self-awareness without any labeling or disapproval. Could be elevated to a 5 with slightly more explicit statements of unconditional positive regard or direct normalization of the struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and nuanced reflective listening throughout, capturing ambivalence, the 'blurry line' experience, and the 'room' concept with deep experiential precision. These are specific, emotionally attuned reflections rather than generic summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a logical progression from exploring ambivalence to identifying the escalation mechanism to supporting an autonomous reframe, with no drift. Slightly more explicit connection back to the specific incident (e.g., police interaction) as a motivational anchor would push this to a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative dynamic where Tyler's own language and ideas ('middle ground,' 'the room,' 'the data') drive the session forward. Could be elevated with slightly more explicit co-construction moments, such as inviting Tyler to help shape direction mid-session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout — the psychiatrist never prescribes, never says 'you should,' and explicitly returns decision-making to Tyler. The closing statement ('That clarity is going to be way more useful than any rule or number, because it's yours') is a masterful autonomy-supportive affirmation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently excellent evocation where every motivational shift comes from Tyler's own mouth rather than external advice or reasons for change. The final reframe ('stop feeling like a choice between party and monastery') is a direct product of the psychiatrist's evocative stance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the session as collaborative, using invitations rather than directives and returning ownership to Tyler. Could be slightly stronger with more explicit acknowledgment of Tyler as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions effectively elicit change talk and Tyler's own data, with strong amplification of his insights. Could have pushed slightly further into eliciting explicit commitment language or confidence statements to close a small gap.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by acknowledging it within a broader frame rather than arguing or dismissing it. One moment could have used a double-sided reflection to hold both benefit and cost more explicitly, which might have accelerated the softening process.
