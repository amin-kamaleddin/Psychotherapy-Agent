# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and affirmation of David's perspective and efforts. Warmth was clear but somewhat structured, limiting fully exceptional individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured David's ambivalence, practical concerns, and need to stay in control. Empathy was strong but focused more on cognitive and practical worries than deeper emotional themes such as fear or medical distrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on cholesterol management and the medication decision. Each turn moved purposefully from concern to a concrete food trial, follow-through supports, and planning the doctor conversation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist framed the interaction as shared problem-solving rather than a directive conversation. David's ideas strongly shaped the plan, and the clinician built on them without imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David was consistently positioned as the expert in his own routines and choices. The psychiatrist avoided pressure, persuasion, or minimizing his hesitation, reinforcing ownership and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked David's own ideas and language, leading him to generate a practical plan and self-generated statements for the doctor. The evocation was less motivational, with fewer probes into deeper values, confidence, or hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respected David's expertise and avoided assuming authority over his values or preferred plan. The session was strongly shaped by David's contributions, reflecting clear power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change-related language, especially around practical first steps and the doctor conversation. Broader change talk about desire, ability, need, or benefit for cholesterol management was not strongly developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully reflected David's concerns without challenging or reinforcing them as reasons to avoid care. Sustain talk remained central, though it could have been more actively balanced with stronger change-related reflections.
