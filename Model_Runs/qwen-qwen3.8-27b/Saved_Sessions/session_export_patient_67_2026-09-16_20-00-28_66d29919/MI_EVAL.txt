# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes discomfort around discussing drinking, while maintaining a somewhat procedural task focus. This prevents a top score due to less deep, individualized validation of Kenji's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Kenji's mixed feelings about wanting to see his drinking while fearing judgment. It could go deeper into underlying shame, self-judgment, and emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use through self-monitoring and practical tracking steps. It could be stronger by linking tracking more consistently to broader change goals, values, and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan with Kenji, using inclusive language and incorporating his practical preferences. The interaction reflects genuine teamwork rather than clinician-led direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kenji's right to choose is consistently emphasized, with open-ended questions and no pressure or controlling advice. The plan remains his to shape, modify, or abandon.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions are useful for evoking practical preferences but focus mainly on tracking logistics. More targeted questions about values, confidence, and reasons for change would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Kenji's input and lets his preferences shape the plan, showing a strong partnership stance. However, the psychiatrist still leads the agenda and focus on tracking, limiting full client-driven collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked through interest in seeing the facts and a low-pressure plan, but the session does not consistently deepen importance, confidence, or commitment to change. More targeted amplification and questions would improve this domain.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk such as homework, report cards, and fear of failure is normalized and reframed effectively to reduce pressure. It could use more advanced double-sided reflections to better redirect ambivalence toward change.
