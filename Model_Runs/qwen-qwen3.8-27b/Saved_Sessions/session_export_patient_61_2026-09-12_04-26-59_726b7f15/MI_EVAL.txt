# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental tone throughout. The warmth is supportive, but it becomes somewhat formulaic due to repeated phrases such as 'totally understandable.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening by accurately capturing the client's feeling of being overwhelmed and guarded. However, the reflections remain somewhat surface-level and do not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a reasonable focus on a specific, manageable behavior: a short rest during the workday. However, the connection between this small step and the client's broader diabetes management goals is not made explicit enough.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options and simplifying the plan based on the client's pushback. However, the counselor still drives much of the conversation by proposing multiple experiments and alternatives, resulting in a partially client-led partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported by emphasizing the client's freedom to choose, stop at any time, and avoid extra tasks. It could be further strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client's authority over his own health decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open-ended questions and elicits some of the client's goals and concerns. However, much of the session shifts toward problem-solving and suggestion-generating rather than deeply evoking the client's personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and allows the client's preferences to influence the final plan. However, the balance of power remains somewhat uneven because the counselor is the one generating most of the intervention ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes some change talk, such as the client's willingness to try a five-minute rest. However, the counselor does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk to further strengthen the client's motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by normalizing the client's concerns and reducing the burden of the task. However, the response lacks advanced MI moves, such as double-sided reflections, to more effectively turn resistance into a discussion of autonomy.
