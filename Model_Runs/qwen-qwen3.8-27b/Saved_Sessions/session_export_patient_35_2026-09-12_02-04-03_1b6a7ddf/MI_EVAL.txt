# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Elena’s caregiving stress without blaming her for smoking. Acceptance was slightly weakened by frequent problem-solving offers that shifted the interaction toward advising.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections that captured the functional meaning of smoking, especially as a private break from constant demands. Empathy could have gone deeper into guilt, fear, exhaustion, and self-doubt before moving to suggestions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed connected to stress and smoking but became broad, drifting into general coping and logistical issues. A clearer strategic thread linking stress, values, and the smoking target would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was meaningful collaboration through permission-seeking, open questions, and willingness to follow Elena’s lead. However, the psychiatrist frequently generated lists of options, making the session more counselor-driven than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena was repeatedly given permission, choice, and reassurance that she would not be pushed. The repeated option lists slightly limited autonomy support by leading toward the psychiatrist’s suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used useful open questions that helped Elena identify smoking as a way to step away and be off duty. Evocation was limited because the discussion moved quickly into alternatives rather than drawing out change motivation, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Elena as the expert on her life and respected her decision to focus on stress rather than forcing a quitting agenda. Partnership was slightly reduced by frequent structured option lists that positioned the psychiatrist as the main source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made only one initial invitation to explore quitting and did not develop Elena’s brief change talk, “I’ve thought about quitting.” More targeted DARN-CAT questions and follow-up would be needed to build internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with Elena, but the session often stayed within her sustain talk about needing the smoke. Double-sided reflections and pivot questions would help loosen the rigidity of her reasons for continuing smoking.
