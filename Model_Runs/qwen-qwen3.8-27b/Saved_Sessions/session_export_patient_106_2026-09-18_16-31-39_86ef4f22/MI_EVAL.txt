# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with frequent affirmations and normalization of the client's experience. Warmth occasionally reads as slightly formulaic, and could be elevated by affirming her more as a person rather than primarily as someone managing a problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate and frequent reflections that capture the essence of her experience (e.g., the function of the cigarette, the weight of being the sole caregiver). Reflections occasionally focus more on cognitive content than on deeper emotional experiences such as grief, exhaustion, or isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior and its specific context, with logical progression from broad ambivalence to the 5:30 kitchen moment. The session ends somewhat abruptly without a clear closing summary or collaboratively identified next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the conversation as a joint exploration with open questions and invitations for input. Some key reframings (e.g., the 5:30 kitchen moment) are delivered by the psychiatrist rather than co-constructed with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support woven into nearly every response—never pressures, never gives direct advice, and consistently frames all exploration as optional and self-directed. Phrases like 'you don't owe me optimism' and 'it's on your own terms' feel genuine and responsive to her specific needs.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses effective evocative questions that draw out the client's own motivations and insights rather than providing reasons for change. Could be strengthened by more explicitly evoking her confidence or ability to manage the 5:30 moment differently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership stance with language that conveys expertise about her life resides within her. Occasionally positions slightly above the client when delivering reframings rather than co-constructing them, which would elevate this to a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some effective moves toward evoking change talk with good evocative questions, and the client does generate meaningful shift in perspective. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or build on the change talk that emerges, and the session ends before the most significant change talk can be developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk by exploring function rather than arguing, validating past attempts, and avoiding debate about whether she 'needs' to quit. Could be further strengthened with more explicit double-sided reflections that hold both sides of ambivalence simultaneously.
