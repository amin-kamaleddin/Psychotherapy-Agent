# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental while normalizing the client’s concerns. Some affirmations, however, subtly tie approval to moving toward vaccination, which limits unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, fear of harm, and the need for trustworthy information. Deeper emotional themes, such as the client’s shift in self-perception and self-doubt, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on the vaccination decision throughout. The conversation progresses logically from ambivalence to specific worries, risk information, and a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses plain language, offers choices, and co-constructs practical materials with the client. Collaboration is strong, though the practitioner remains the primary information source and agenda-setter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is supported through options, non-demanding language, and flexibility in planning. The final plan and script are somewhat prescriptive, and some affirmations subtly endorse the recommended path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies heavily on information delivery and practical guidance rather than evoking the client’s own motivations. There are few opportunities to draw out values, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences meaningfully shape the session, including focus areas and format. However, the psychiatrist retains a strong expert role in content, structure, and planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some change talk and appears more willing to proceed by the end of the session. The counselor does not systematically elicit or amplify this change talk through targeted MI questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced or argued with, and concerns are redirected toward practical information. Responses are more factual than MI-based, with limited double-sided reflection or rolling with resistance.
