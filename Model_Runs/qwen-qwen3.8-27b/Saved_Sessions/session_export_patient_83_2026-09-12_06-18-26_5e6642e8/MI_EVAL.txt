# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains warmth, respect, and validation throughout, even when Mai expresses skepticism. There is no judgment or pressure, supporting a strong therapeutic alliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening and validates Mai’s uncertainty and concerns. Deeper emotional exploration of her fear and identity-level concerns was less developed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on understanding and adhering to the new statin prescription. It could have ended with a clearer summary and concrete adherence plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Mai’s input and incorporates her concerns into next steps. However, the psychiatrist still leads much of the agenda and clinical content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects Mai’s preferences rather than imposing decisions. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit emphasis on Mai’s ownership of the final decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open-ended questions help Mai express concerns and preferences. The session becomes more informational, with less consistent elicitation of Mai’s deeper motivations and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and asks for Mai’s preferences. It remains somewhat expert-led and could more explicitly position Mai as the expert on her own routine and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai expresses meaningful willingness and planning, but the psychiatrist often acknowledges it rather than amplifying it as motivation for change. More deliberate reflection of change talk would strengthen evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes Mai’s concerns and avoids arguing against her doubts. Responses could be further enhanced with more explicit double-sided reflections or ambivalence-focused statements.
