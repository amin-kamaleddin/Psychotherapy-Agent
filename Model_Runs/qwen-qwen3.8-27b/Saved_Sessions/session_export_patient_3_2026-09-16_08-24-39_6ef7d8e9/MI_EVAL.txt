# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, especially when Aisha expressed fear about failure and body image. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming Aisha’s worth independent of whether the plan succeeds.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflective listening and captured Aisha’s conflict between wanting change and fearing that change would not be enough. Deeper reflection of shame, self-judgment, and identity-related pain would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of starting movement while managing body-image distress. The psychiatrist consistently guided the conversation toward a specific, actionable plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated effectively by building the plan around Aisha’s preferences, such as walking, a playlist, Wednesday, and a note-based check-in. Collaboration could improve by inviting Aisha to generate more options before offering specific suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy through choices, preference-based questions, and low-pressure language. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicitly stating that Aisha can modify, pause, or stop the plan at any time.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited Aisha’s own ideas through scaling, confidence questions, and preference-based planning. Evocation could go deeper by drawing out Aisha’s values, reasons for change, and sense of identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated partnership by following Aisha’s agenda, respecting her routines, and asking which options felt doable. A slightly stronger partnership stance would give Aisha more lead time before offering implementation options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked confidence and commitment, moving Aisha from a 5 to a 7 and helping her articulate a concrete plan. Change talk cultivation was limited by a focus on planning rather than deeper values, identity, and future-self motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk skillfully by normalizing fears about body image, failure, and a missed day without arguing. Softening sustain talk could be stronger with more double-sided reflections and active pivots toward values and self-compassion.
