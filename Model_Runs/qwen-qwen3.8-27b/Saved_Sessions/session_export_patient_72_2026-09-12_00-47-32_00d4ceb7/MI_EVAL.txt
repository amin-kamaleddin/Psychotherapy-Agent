# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental while affirming Leila’s choices and concerns. Acceptance is somewhat practical and could more explicitly normalize her ambivalence and self-critical feelings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate normalizations and acknowledgments, but empathy often remains surface-level rather than reflecting deeper emotions. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to accurately reflect fears of all-or-nothing collapse, frustration, or self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on establishing a sustainable, flexible exercise routine. The psychiatrist effectively narrows the goal into a concrete, actionable one-week plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Leila contributes preferences and helps shape the final plan, but the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure and content. The interaction is collaborative at a surface level rather than deeply co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, flexibility, and non-perfection while avoiding pressure or coercive language. Autonomy support is weakened by the amount of directive advice and structured options offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most of the motivational content comes from the psychiatrist’s suggestions rather than Leila’s own reasons for change. There are missed opportunities to evoke her values, confidence, past successes, and expected benefits.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and respects Leila’s preferences, but remains the primary provider of the plan. More power sharing and client-led planning would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Leila offers several spontaneous change statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or develop them. Questions are mostly logistical rather than motivational.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers and sustain talk are acknowledged without arguing, dismissing, or reinforcing them. However, frequent problem-solving keeps attention on obstacles rather than pivoting toward Leila’s own reasons for change.
