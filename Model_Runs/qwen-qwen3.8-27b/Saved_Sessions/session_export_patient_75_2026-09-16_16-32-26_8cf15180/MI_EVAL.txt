# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout. Language conveyed respect, validation, and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both practical and emotional concerns. The therapist demonstrated strong perspective-taking on the client's fear of dependence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma management and inhaler use. The conversation progressed logically from adherence concerns to a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist worked in a non-directive, joint-exploration manner. The final plan was co-constructed from the client's own statements.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly respected the client's autonomy and noncommitment. Next steps were framed as optional choices rather than demands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively elicited client-generated motivation and ideas. Some offered options slightly reduced the purely evocative quality of the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's perspective shaped the session, and input was actively invited. However, the therapist still guided the conceptual frame toward testing and tapering.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions helped the client articulate reasons for change and possible plans. The occasional suggestion of specific options slightly limited client-generated language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fears about side effects, dependence, and long-term use were acknowledged without challenge. These concerns were gently reframed into collaborative problem-solving.
