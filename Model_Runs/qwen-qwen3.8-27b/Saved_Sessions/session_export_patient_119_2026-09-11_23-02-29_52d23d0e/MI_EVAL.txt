# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing missed doses and framing small wins positively. Warmth is present but somewhat formulaic and task-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is accurate recognition of the client’s exhaustion and limited mental bandwidth. Empathy is mostly cognitive and does not deeply reflect shame, fear, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of diabetes medication adherence. There is no significant drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences shape parts of the plan, such as the pill case, coin jar, and Monday start. However, the psychiatrist supplies most of the structure, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects some client decisions. Language remains fairly prescriptive and does not strongly emphasize freedom, ownership, or self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical barriers and preferences but does not consistently draw out deeper motivations, values, or confidence for change. Client change talk is often left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely expert problem-solving rather than equal partnership. The client’s expertise about her own schedule, barriers, and preferences is not fully positioned as central.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses clear health goals and a desire to stop second-guessing, but these are not evoked or amplified. The psychiatrist focuses more on practical options than reinforcing change reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client frequently minimizes missed doses and says she feels fine, but these statements are not consistently reflected or reframed. Sustain talk is often accepted rather than gently linked to her long-term health goals.
