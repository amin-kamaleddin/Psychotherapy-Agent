# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows nonjudgmental respect and reassurance. However, some validation is repetitive and could be more individualized to Maria’s specific fears and burdens.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects distress and tracks key safety information well. Deeper, more precise reflections of guilt, shame, loneliness, and her reasons for staying are needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on immediate suicide risk and safety. The focus could be stronger if it more smoothly moved from validation toward change-related exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative questions inviting Maria’s input. The psychiatrist remains the primary driver, which limits genuine co-creation of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes some autonomy-supportive language and asks for Maria’s choice. The repeated safety directives reduce the sense that Maria is leading her own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most questions are logistical and safety-focused rather than exploring Maria’s values, hopes, or motivations. Her meaningful change talk, especially concern for her friend, is not sufficiently evoked or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids a purely lecture-based approach. The interaction remains expert-led, especially around risk assessment and safety planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria offers several important change statements, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects or amplifies them. Her values and relationship motivations are not developed into stronger reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Maria’s sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, it is not effectively softened through MI techniques, so reasons against change remain dominant.
