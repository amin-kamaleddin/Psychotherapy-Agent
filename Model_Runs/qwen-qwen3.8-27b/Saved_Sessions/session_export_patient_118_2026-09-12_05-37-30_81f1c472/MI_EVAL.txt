# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and nonjudgmental stance with several validating statements. However, the acceptance feels procedural and managerial rather than deeply attuned to the patient's frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some accurate reflections of the patient's frustration with smoking lectures and dental delays. These reflections remain surface-level and task-focused rather than fully exploring the emotional meaning of his pain and being stuck.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session quickly shifts away from the MI target of smoking-related change and focuses instead on practical dental-care navigation. The psychiatrist does not use the patient's dental concern as a bridge to explore ambivalence, values, or change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are collaborative surface features, such as asking which option the patient prefers. However, the psychiatrist largely directs the session through lists, scripts, and resources rather than co-constructing the conversation with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and avoids direct commands, but repeatedly offers structured tasks after the patient has declined them. This can feel pressuring rather than autonomy-supportive, especially without affirming the patient's right to focus only on the exam.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with a reasonable open question and asks some practical questions about what the patient wants. However, the session becomes didactic and information-heavy rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts to sound collaborative by asking which option the patient prefers. The interaction remains more problem-solver or care-navigator than true partnership, because the patient's clear desire to simply be seen is not fully integrated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is almost no effort to cultivate smoking-related change talk or explore the patient's own motivations around smoking and dental health. The session remains focused on dental logistics rather than readiness or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly argue against the patient's resistance, which is appropriate. However, the psychiatrist does not actively soften sustain talk and keeps offering similar structured tasks that the patient repeatedly rejects.
