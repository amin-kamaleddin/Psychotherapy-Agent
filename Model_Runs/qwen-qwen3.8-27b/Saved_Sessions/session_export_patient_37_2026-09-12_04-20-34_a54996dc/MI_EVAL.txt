# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client’s ambivalence. Warmth is sometimes procedural rather than deeply individualized, with limited explicit acknowledgment of the client’s parental stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, helpful reflections of the client’s ambivalence and concerns. Empathy is solid but does not consistently explore the deeper emotional meaning, such as fear, guilt, or the weight of parental responsibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior, moving logically from vaccine worry to a concrete next step. The psychiatrist effectively narrows vague uncertainty into actionable planning without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is strongly shaped by the client’s preferences, and the psychiatrist adapts the plan as she requests risk comparison, a script, shorter phrasing, role-play, and a checklist. The counselor treats the client as an active partner rather than a passive recipient of advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and emphasizes that the decision belongs to the client. Autonomy could be further strengthened by more explicit affirmation of the client’s ownership, capacity, and authority to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions, but the client’s shift is driven more by direct education than by eliciting her own reasons for change. The session would benefit from stronger use of questions that draw out the client’s values, beliefs, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input, offers choices, and adapts to her preferences in a collaborative way. The counselor still occupies a central informational role, and could more explicitly position the client as the expert on her family and decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client does express some change talk, but it is largely prompted by clinician-provided risk information. The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit, amplify, or deepen the client’s own values, motivations, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without judgment or argument, including concerns about side effects and natural immunity. The session does not consistently use MI-specific strategies to turn sustain talk into change talk, such as double-sided or balanced reflections.
