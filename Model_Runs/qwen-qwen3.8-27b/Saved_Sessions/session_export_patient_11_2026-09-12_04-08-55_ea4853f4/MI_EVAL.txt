# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, supportive tone and affirms the patient's cautious goals without judgment. Praise is slightly evaluative and task-oriented, so acceptance is strong but not fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is one strong early reflection of the patient's perspective, but later responses remain more summary- and action-oriented than emotionally exploratory. Key concerns about perfectionism, losing control, and spiraling are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior—increasing physical activity—and moves logically toward a concrete, realistic plan. There is little topic drift, and the structure is clear and well organized.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's ideas meaningfully shape the plan, including reminder wording, shoe placement, water bottle preparation, and backup steps. Collaboration is strong, but the psychiatrist still drives much of the sequence and offers many suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options, asking preferences, and allowing flexibility in timing, location, and backup plans. Autonomy is further limited by frequent steering toward a specific implementation plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of the patient's own reasons, confidence, and self-generated strategies. However, there are missed opportunities to deepen personal motivation, values, and intrinsic meaning before moving into problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributes practical ideas and preferences, and the psychiatrist builds on several of them. The session feels more therapist-guided than co-led because of structured menus, multi-part questions, and frequent steering.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient produces useful change talk, such as valuing energy, showing up, and keeping the habit alive. The psychiatrist mostly summarizes or affirms these statements rather than reflecting, amplifying, or deepening them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles barriers well, especially around all-or-nothing thinking, pressure, fatigue, and fear of slipping. Responses such as the no-penalty rule, 5-minute backup, and neutral reminder reduce the risk of reinforcing resistance.
