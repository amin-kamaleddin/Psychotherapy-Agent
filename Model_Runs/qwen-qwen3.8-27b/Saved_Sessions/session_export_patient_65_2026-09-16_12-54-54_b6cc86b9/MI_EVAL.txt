# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized the client's concerns without pressure. However, the language was somewhat repetitive and formulaic, limiting it from reaching a higher level of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used accurate reflective listening to capture practical concerns and underlying uncertainty. The main limitation was that reflections remained mostly cognitive and task-focused rather than deeper emotional or ambivalence-based.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior of completing court-ordered community service on time. The counselor moved logically through barriers, planning, and concrete next steps with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely collaborative, with the counselor adapting scripts, schedules, and message tone based on client input. However, the counselor still led much of the problem-solving, so the collaboration was not fully co-created by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered options, respected boundaries, and invited client input throughout the session. At times, the counseling became more directive in logistics, which slightly reduced emphasis on the client's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited the client to identify needs and preferences, but much of the session became counselor-led problem-solving. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, and personal motivation was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally acted as a partner by inviting choices and adapting to the client's input. The limitation was that the counselor still took a strong problem-solving role rather than consistently positioning the client as the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflected and built on some statements focused on getting the task done, but change talk remained superficial. It was not consistently expanded through importance, values, confidence, or personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged the client's concerns without arguing or moralizing, then redirected them into practical steps. The main limitation was that ambivalence was not always fully transformed using more skillful double-sided reflection.
