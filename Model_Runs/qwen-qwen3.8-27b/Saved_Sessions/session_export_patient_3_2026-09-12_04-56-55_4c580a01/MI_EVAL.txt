# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently demonstrated warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming regard without shaming or moralizing. A minor limitation was that the acceptance sometimes felt structured as the session moved into planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor made strong, accurate reflections of the client's perspective and internal conflict. However, some reflections stayed more content-focused and did not fully explore the deeper shame, fear of failure, and self-criticism underneath her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, steady focus on the target behavior and guided the session logically toward a concrete plan. The conversation stayed anchored on a realistic first step without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used collaborative language and invited the client to choose among options, making her input central to the plan. The interaction remained somewhat clinician-led because the counselor provided much of the structure and content for the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supported autonomy by avoiding pressure, offering options, and repeatedly affirming the client's right to choose. The plan included a clear stop rule that further supported her freedom to pause or stop if needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew out the client's own motivations, preferences, and steps rather than giving external reasons for change. The score was limited by the amount of clinician-generated structure, strategy, and planning details introduced during the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a collaborative stance by asking for the client's preferences and incorporating her choices into the plan. The partnership was strong but not fully equal because the counselor supplied much of the plan structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited clear change talk, including desire, ability, and commitment, and helped consolidate it into a coherent action plan. A deeper exploration of values, meaning, and confidence would have further strengthened motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with or dismissing the client's sustain talk and often softened it through validation and reframing. A more strategic shift from barriers to possibilities or deeper double-sided reflections could have further reduced resistance.
