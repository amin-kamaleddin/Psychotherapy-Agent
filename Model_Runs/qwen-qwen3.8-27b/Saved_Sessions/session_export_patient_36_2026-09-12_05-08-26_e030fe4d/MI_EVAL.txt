# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used warm, nonjudgmental affirmations and avoided blame or pressure. Acceptance was strong but somewhat formulaic and could more explicitly validate the client as a person beyond the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured key content such as balance, guilt, and a past success. They remained surface-level and did not fully attend to the underlying fear of seeming selfish, like a bad colleague, or overapologizing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on reducing overcommitting and practicing saying no. The direction was useful but could better balance practical planning with evoking values, self-respect, and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor co-created a practical if-then plan using the client’s ideas. Collaboration was strong, though much of the content was counselor-generated rather than fully client-elicited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supported choice by asking which scripts felt natural and how much rehearsal the client wanted. Some plan-promoting language and early provision of multiple strategies slightly limited autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor drew out desire, ability, and commitment through questions about success, confidence, and wording. However, frequent advice and scripts reduced opportunities for the client to generate deeper motivation from values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and let the client shape the pause, script, and rehearsal preference. However, offering multiple scripts and techniques made parts of the session more expert-directed than client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered useful change talk about a small win, willingness to pause, and confidence. The counselor did not consistently deepen or amplify that change talk before moving to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Guilt and related sustain talk were acknowledged without reinforcement or argument. Responses were effective but could use more nuanced MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to hold ambivalence more fully.
