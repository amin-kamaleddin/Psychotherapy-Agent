# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a generally nonjudgmental, validating tone and avoids shaming Rosa for ambivalence or fear of relapse. However, occasional advisory praise and quick moves to planning prevent full unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some solid reflections of fear, identity, and loss, but the psychiatrist often shifts quickly into advice before deeply exploring Rosa’s perspective. Missed opportunities include her embarrassment, job worries, social burden, and ambivalence about tolerating discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and related dependence, cravings, and relapse concerns. However, the direction is more psychoeducational and plan-driven than MI-consistent, with less progressive elicitation of Rosa’s own motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa’s preference for a minimal experiment shapes the plan, showing partial collaboration. The clinician still frequently provides structured options and problem-solving advice, so the process is cooperative but not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, supports a low-stakes experiment, and respects Rosa’s decision to avoid a formal schedule. Repeated suggestions for plans, tools, and check-ins, however, weaken consistent emphasis on Rosa as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies heavily on providing reasons, tools, and plans rather than asking questions to draw out Rosa’s intrinsic reasons, values, and confidence. The doctor’s warning is not explored deeply enough to evoke stronger internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some partnership questions about focus, doable options, and check-in timing, but the psychiatrist often assumes an expert role. More explicit statements that Rosa is the expert in her own life would strengthen the stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa generates a concrete commitment to try the car rule, and the psychiatrist reinforces it as doable. Still, there are few questions designed to develop her desire, ability, need, or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates concerns such as weight gain, cost, irritability, and relapse fear without arguing. However, sustain talk is often met with practical solutions rather than double-sided reflections that redirect toward change.
