# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently counters Lars's self-critical language with specific, warm validation (e.g., 'That's not an idiot move, Lars'). Zero judgment or condescension throughout; every reflection affirms Lars's worth and intelligence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently go beneath surface behavior to identify underlying emotional meaning, such as recognizing shame and past failure beneath avoidance. Demonstrates genuine perspective-taking rather than parroting, accurately capturing the pull between competing worlds.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, coherent thread connecting boredom to recidivism risk, with each step building organically on the last. Could benefit from slightly more intentional momentum toward a concrete, achievable next step before the session ends.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine dialogue shaped entirely by Lars's lead, with every topic emerging naturally from his own words. The psychiatrist operates as a fellow explorer rather than a guide with a map, using inclusive language like 'I'm curious about…' and 'What do you think?'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never prescribes, recommends, or fills gaps for Lars; instead, he creates space for Lars to discover his own answers. Language consistently positions Lars as the decision-maker, with questions framed as invitations rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Lars articulates all his own reasons for change—protecting Donna, avoiding prison, being a different dad—while the psychiatrist merely creates the conditions for that motivation to surface. This is gold-standard evocation where the client does all the motivational work.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise and wisdom are consistently located within Lars ('you already know what puts you at risk, which is more than a lot of people starting out can say'). The psychiatrist follows Lars's lead throughout, never imposing an agenda or offering unsolicited advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits substantial DARN-C statements through strategic, evocative questions that deepen rather than direct. Could push further on confidence/ability language (e.g., scaling questions) to systematically develop all change talk dimensions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively handles sustain talk without arguing or dismissing, using double-sided reflections that honor the resistance while opening a door toward change (e.g., 'Does using Donna's brain mean you're not handling it, or handling it in a way that gets you there?'). Could employ even more strategic double-sided reflections that explicitly hold both sides in tension.
