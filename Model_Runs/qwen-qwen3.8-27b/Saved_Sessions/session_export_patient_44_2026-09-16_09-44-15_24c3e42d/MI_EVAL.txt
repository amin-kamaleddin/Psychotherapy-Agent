# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone while validating Erik’s struggles. Acceptance is slightly weakened by repeated evaluative approval, such as calling choices “wise” or “smart,” which feels less like unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate understanding by acknowledging Erik’s comparisons, back pain, and practical concerns. However, empathy remains surface-level and misses deeper reflections on Erik’s shame, discouragement, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session has a clear, consistent focus on Erik’s target behaviors: gym consistency, comparisons, and the evening candy bar. The conversation stays well-directed and moves steadily toward concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting Erik’s input and building the plan around his choices. Collaboration is strong but is somewhat reduced when the psychiatrist provides specific scripts and exact wording rather than eliciting more of Erik’s own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik is regularly given choices about focus, gym days, food swaps, and communication, and is not directly commanded. Autonomy support is slightly limited by the psychiatrist’s prescriptive suggestions and labels that certain choices are “wise” or “smart.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Erik’s own motivations, strategies, and confidence through open questions, scaling, and planning prompts. Evocation is strong but is sometimes reduced when the psychiatrist shifts into a more directive, script-providing mode.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves collaboratively and lets Erik shape the session focus and plan. The partnership is occasionally undermined by directive language and expert-approved phrasing, which limits full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently encourages Erik’s own change-oriented language and successfully elicits concrete plans and confidence. The evocation could be stronger through more complex reflections that amplify and deepen Erik’s emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes barriers and redirects sustain talk toward manageable action without reinforcing reasons to maintain the status quo. Handling is effective but somewhat practical and brief, with limited use of double-sided reflections or deeper ambivalence exploration.
