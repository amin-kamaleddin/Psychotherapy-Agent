# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and explicitly supports client autonomy. The warmth is consistent, though sometimes remains more task-oriented than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately summarize ambivalence and track confidence changes. They could go deeper in exploring underlying emotions such as fear of standing out.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing smoking, especially Friday with her social group. Multiple questions at once and repeated return to the backup text slightly diluted focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is largely built collaboratively using the client's own language and preferences. Some counselor-led suggestions make the collaboration feel less fully client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, avoids coercion, and accepts the client's refusal of a text or alarm. Occasionally, specific suggestions make the interaction feel slightly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes the client's own motivations, strengths, and confidence through questions and scaling. This leads to meaningful change talk and increased confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client preference and allows her ideas to shape the plan. There are occasional moments where the counselor takes a more directive, recommendation-based role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits change talk through strengths, scaling, and small-step questions. It could be strengthened by more actively amplifying the client's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or escalation, and the client's low-key approach is respected. The counselor could better integrate competing ambivalence with double-sided reflections.
