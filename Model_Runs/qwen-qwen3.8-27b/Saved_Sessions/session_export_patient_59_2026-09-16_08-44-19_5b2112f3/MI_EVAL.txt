# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but the repeated task-focused prompting can feel pressing. This may partially undermine the client’s sense of being fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is present at a surface level, with summaries of the client’s situation and goals. However, it remains shallow and repetitive, missing deeper reflections of underlying feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor keeps focus on reducing marijuana-related risk and connecting it to probation, school, and work goals. However, the session becomes repetitive and circular, with less clear movement from ambivalence toward personal planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moves are made, such as offering choices and asking which option feels doable. Yet the counselor frequently provides lists and concrete details, making the process more directive than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the counselor uses respectful language like “no pressure” and “I won’t push,” the repeated requests for timing, reminders, scripts, and details undermine autonomy. The client’s irritation suggests the interview process feels more controlling than freedom-supporting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some motivation related to probation, school, work, and independence. However, the evocation is limited in depth, with little exploration of personal values, future self, or meaningful discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor attempts partnership by asking permission and offering options. Still, the repeated planning prompts and agenda-driving make the collaboration feel partial rather than truly equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some relevant change talk is elicited around staying out of trouble, finishing school, and getting a license. However, the counselor does not consistently develop or amplify this change talk using deeper MI techniques.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids direct arguing and occasionally redirects toward goals. However, the client’s minimization and confidence in being careful are not consistently softened through nuanced reflection or discrepancy work.
