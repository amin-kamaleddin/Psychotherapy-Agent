# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout Richard’s ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural and could be more individualized and affirming of Richard’s specific effort and honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and track Richard’s torn feelings, values, and practical worries. Depth is limited because reflections are brief and often followed quickly by problem-solving rather than sustained emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of starting a cholesterol medication trial. Discussion of retirement and family is consistently connected to the behavioral goal and a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language, invites choices, and adapts the plan based on Richard’s preferences. Collaboration is occasionally reduced by clinician-supplied scripts, clinical information, and a structured checklist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking Richard to identify doable steps, preferred wording, and timing. Autonomy support could be stronger by reducing directive scripts and more explicitly affirming Richard’s ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some motivation by exploring retirement values, protected activities, and readiness to make a call. Evocation is limited because much of the session is driven by clinician-provided information, scripts, and planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a partner by inviting input, following Richard’s lead, and using collaborative language. Some segments feel expert-driven when the psychiatrist introduces the treatment plan, scripts, and clinical details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change talk by asking about values, goals, and commitment, and reflects Richard’s willingness to try. It could be stronger by amplifying change talk, exploring ability, and using summaries to deepen confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and normalizes concerns such as added hassle, fatigue, and fear of chicken-ing out. Softening is often superficial because the response quickly shifts to practical problem-solving rather than exploring ambivalence.
