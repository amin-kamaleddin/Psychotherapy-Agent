# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent, genuine unconditional positive regard throughout, with no trace of judgment or disapproval. Key examples include normalizing Mateo's self-criticism ('I don't think you're stupid') and repeatedly validating small contributions without inflating them ('That's not nothing').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently goes beyond surface content to capture emotional and relational meaning, using vivid metaphors like 'standing at the edge of something but the ground just fades out.' Reflections match Mateo's level of engagement rather than pushing for more elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains excellent focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from acknowledging the mandated context to a concrete, client-generated commitment (going to church on Sunday). The final outcome is a specific, achievable behavioral target that directly relates to reducing recidivism.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The power dynamic is genuinely balanced throughout, with the psychiatrist using co-construction language ('What if we just started with...') and adapting immediately when Mateo pushes back. The church conversation is entirely co-constructed—Mateo identifies the resource and generates the specific plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges the lack of choice in the mandated context and consistently removes pressure ('You don't owe yourself a perfect plan,' 'No label required'). When Mateo resists being called as 'deciding' something, the psychiatrist immediately drops the language rather than defending it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivational language in the session is Mateo's own; the psychiatrist never states a reason for change or provides information about benefits. The progressive build from 'I'll be here' to 'I'll go Sunday' is entirely Mateo-driven, with the psychiatrist creating space and amplifying what is already there.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that expertise resides within Mateo, using questions like 'What's pulling you forward?' and explicitly returning authority ('You made it yourself'). The willingness to modify language based on Mateo's response demonstrates genuine, not performative, power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk using techniques like 'I want you to really hear yourself say that' and 'What do you think it would look like if you made going there a regular thing?' The shift from 'a place to be' to 'something I look forward to' was directly elicited by the psychiatrist's questioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged and gently reframed without being reinforced or argued against, using double-sided reflections and normalization. The only area for improvement is that slightly more explicit double-sided reflections could have been used in a few moments, and Mateo's minimizing pattern could have been explored slightly more deeply as a potential barrier.
