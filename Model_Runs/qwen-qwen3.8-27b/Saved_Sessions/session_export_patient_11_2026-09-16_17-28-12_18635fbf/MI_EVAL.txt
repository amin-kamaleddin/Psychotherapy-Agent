# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental regard while validating Amira’s choices without pressure. The final reflection about leaving the shoes inside as a choice, not a failure, is especially accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are frequent, accurate, and nuanced, capturing Amira’s ambivalence, need for safety, and fear that one missed day could become a spiral. The psychiatrist moves beyond paraphrasing into underlying feelings and meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of a small, sustainable walking routine. The conversation moves clearly from intention to concrete implementation details without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira’s ideas shape the plan, and the psychiatrist builds on her contributions rather than imposing an external plan. However, occasional counselor-offered options and concepts slightly reduce the equal, client-led collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Amira’s ownership, choice, and flexibility without directive or pressuring language. Statements such as “You don’t have to have the answer for it yet” strongly reinforce autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Amira’s own reasons, plan, and coping ideas through open questions. Evocation is limited by the lack of deeper importance, confidence, values, or identity-level questions and no scaling or evocative summary.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Amira as the expert and allows her to shape the plan. The partnership could be strengthened by inviting more client-generated options before offering structured suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira generates change talk about feeling stronger, more confident, and less wiped out, and the psychiatrist supports it. Cultivation is not fully maximized because there are no explicit importance, confidence, ability, or evocative summary questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles barriers without arguing and uses gentle reframes such as “paused, not reset” and “starting again tomorrow.” Some turns remain centered on doubt, low energy, and missed days before pivoting more fully toward change-compatible language.
