# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with validation and normalization. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly affirming Maya’s worth and accurately acknowledging the teacher-referred context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and captures ambivalence, fatigue, pressure to perform, and relief. Empathy could be deeper by exploring core emotions, shame, failure fear, and the role of alcohol in coping.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session remains focused on general stress, social performance, and rest rather than the alcohol-related referral target. This is a major weakness because the specific clinical concern is never connected to the discussion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Maya’s preferences and builds on her statements rather than imposing advice. Collaboration is limited by the counselor’s introduction of structured options and a specific behavioral experiment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with repeated emphasis on choice, pace, and non-coercive language. It is not a 5 because the counselor still steers toward trying a low-pressure experiment and possibly reporting back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses questions to invite Maya to identify what helps, what feels like herself, and what mattered most. Evocation is limited because it does not consistently connect to the alcohol-related target or deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, leaves the agenda open, and lets Maya shape much of the session. Partnership could be stronger by more explicitly co-creating the next step from Maya’s own ideas rather than offering a menu of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk around noticing what helped and taking small steps. This is limited because the change talk is not consistently amplified and remains mostly about stress relief rather than reducing alcohol risk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Maya’s resistance to pressure and reframes next steps as optional and low-pressure. Softening is good but could be stronger by more explicitly linking her concerns to change-oriented values.
