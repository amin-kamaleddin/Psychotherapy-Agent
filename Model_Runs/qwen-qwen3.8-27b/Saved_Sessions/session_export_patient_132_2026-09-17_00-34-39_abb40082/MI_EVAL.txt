# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, validation, and nonjudgment while avoiding blame or moralizing about dieting and weight. Acceptance is strong but not perfect because warmth is sometimes framed around responsibility and strength rather than explicitly communicating worthiness regardless of outcomes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and captures Yasmin’s ambivalence, internal rules, and fear of losing control. Some responses remain surface-level and could more deeply reflect the shame, self-punishment, and emotional cost of perfectionism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on moving from rigid dieting and scale-based control toward a more sustainable relationship with food, exercise, and body. There is some drift into broader self-criticism and the session ends without a clear collaborative summary linking the safety plan to a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a collaborative stance, frequently asks for Yasmin’s perspective, and invites her to generate her own ideas. However, some therapist-led interpretive formulations limit the session from being fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with repeated emphasis on Yasmin’s choice, uncertainty, and self-generated goals. The psychiatrist could more explicitly reinforce Yasmin’s freedom to choose whether and how to use any of the ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses effective open and evocative questions to draw out Yasmin’s motivations, values, and possible strategies. Evocation is not a 5 because the therapist does not consistently deepen or reflect the client’s change talk once it appears.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Yasmin as the expert on her own life and invites her to define what would feel different. The collaboration could be stronger by more explicitly negotiating the agenda and allowing Yasmin’s priorities to visibly shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through open questions about a different week, safer signals, and a tiny safety plan. Change talk is not consistently amplified, affirmed, or expanded into confidence, ability, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce Yasmin’s sustain talk and uses some normalizing language. However, the session remains heavily focused on fear and reasons against change, without consistently pivoting toward values, exceptions, competence, or change.
