# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout most of the session. The main limitation is repeating the same question after Amina says “Stop,” which briefly prioritizes the therapist’s process over the client’s boundary.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Amina’s stress, need for respect, and self-protective coping. Empathy is strong but somewhat surface-level, with less exploration of deeper emotions such as hurt, loneliness, anger, or humiliation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on home stress, family conflict, and Amina’s need for steadiness. However, repeating the question after Amina says “Stop” disrupts focus, and the overall target remains broad rather than clearly negotiated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language, offers options, and respects Amina’s choice to end the session. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist continues to drive much of the early session with questions and proposed topics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Amina’s autonomy by following her lead and respecting her request to end. This is undermined when the same question is repeated after Amina explicitly says “Stop.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to draw out Amina’s own ideas about quiet and safety. Evocation remains superficial because the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen Amina’s values, confidence, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and acknowledges Amina as the expert on her own experience. Partnership becomes clearer only after Amina resists, with earlier parts being more therapist-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina offers several change-related ideas, such as wanting quiet, closing her door, using headphones, and sleeping. The psychiatrist reflects these ideas but does not consistently amplify or develop them into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and at times reframes Amina’s resistance as self-protection. However, the discussion remains centered on her resistant position for several turns, including after she says she does not want the caseworker involved or does not want to make a plan.
