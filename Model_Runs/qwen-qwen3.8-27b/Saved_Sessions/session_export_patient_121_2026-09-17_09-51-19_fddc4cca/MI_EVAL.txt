# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard while avoiding blame and normalizing Sofia’s stress. This is strong, but moments of polished/generic warmth and slight minimization of the problem keep it just below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening by accurately reflecting the feeling of unsafety, the role of avoidance, and the meaning of physical cues. It does not always go deep enough into underlying emotions such as humiliation, fear of being labeled, or isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior of staying in class longer rather than leaving immediately, and it moves logically from broader concerns to concrete strategies. It falls short of a 5 because the emerging plan is not explicitly consolidated into a mutually agreed goal with a clear next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance by building directly on Sofia’s ideas and inviting joint problem-solving with her friend. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still drives the agenda and introduces several options rather than engaging in more explicit shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Sofia’s sense of choice by avoiding pressure, respecting her boundaries, and validating her need for a fair, low-production response. It would be stronger with more explicit statements affirming that the decision and plan are hers to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses open questions to elicit Sofia’s own values, motivations, and solutions rather than giving directives. It could be stronger by more deeply exploring confidence, meaning, and commitment to the emerging plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Sofia as the expert on her own experience and invites her to co-construct ideas. It remains just below a 5 because the structure is still counselor-led and could include more explicit negotiation of priorities and the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively invites and reinforces Sofia’s own change talk, such as wanting fair treatment and using a private plan with a friend. It would be stronger with more sustained development through confidence scaling, commitment language, and deeper exploration of values and ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the relief that avoidance provides without challenging or arguing against it, then gently redirects toward safety and fairness. It could be stronger by more actively reducing the power of sustain talk using double-sided reflections.
