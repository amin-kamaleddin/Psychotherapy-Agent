# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while honoring Elena’s request for no pressure. It is not a 5 because some language is formulaic and occasionally minimizes the emotional intensity of her fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Elena’s mixed relief, fear, and concern about control. It is not a 5 because reflections remain somewhat surface-level and miss deeper emotions and full double-sided ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to contraception and the emotional aftermath of the scare, but it lacks a clear MI direction toward a client-defined next step. The conversation can feel circular as the psychiatrist repeatedly offers smaller options after Elena’s resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaboration, and Elena’s choices influence the session, but the interaction is still largely shaped by the psychiatrist’s offered menu of options. It would be stronger if Elena’s own language more fully guided the exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with repeated affirmation of Elena’s right to choose and avoid coercion. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still offers multiple suggestions after repeated reluctance, which may feel like gentle pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently evoke Elena’s internal reasons, values, hopes, or confidence for change. Questions focus more on preferred supports than on eliciting change talk or deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally collaborative stance and invites Elena’s preferences. However, the session is not fully client-led, as the psychiatrist frequently supplies a menu of clinician-generated options rather than following Elena’s lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena offers some change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or uses them to build momentum. Questions are mostly service-oriented rather than actively eliciting reasons or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Elena’s sustain talk and respects her avoidance. However, at times the response normalizes the status quo rather than softening sustain talk into healthier ambivalence.
