# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Isabella's fear and ambivalence. Some evaluative praise and rapid planning make acceptance feel slightly conditional rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture both the benefits and costs of avoidance. At times, the reflections remain content-focused rather than fully exploring deeper emotions such as shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session keeps a clear focus on a manageable target behavior and related safety supports. It becomes procedural at times, with multiple options shifting attention toward logistics rather than exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Isabella's choices, including the mailbox step, timing, and escape plan. However, the interviewer often drives the pace and presents multiple options, limiting fully client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, no obligation, and an escape plan, supporting Isabella's sense of control. Some therapist-generated micro-plans and approving language can still subtly guide toward preferred options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and a confidence ruler to draw out internal motivation and previous successes. The session frequently shifts toward planning, leaving deeper values and meaning less explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites permission, preferences, and input, allowing Isabella to shape the action step. Still, the interviewer supplies substantial structure and strategies, so the session is not fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk related to loneliness, connection, and the mailbox step. It does not consistently expand, deepen, or amplify Isabella's own change-related statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and uses balanced reflections to honor safety while noting loneliness. It could more consistently pivot sustain talk toward values or change.
