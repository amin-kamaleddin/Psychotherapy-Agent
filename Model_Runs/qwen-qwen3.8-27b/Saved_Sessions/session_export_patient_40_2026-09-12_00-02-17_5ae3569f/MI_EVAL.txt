# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and support while reducing shame around asking for help. It is not a 5 because the warmth is sometimes procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Elias’s pain, ambivalence, and fear of being seen as weak, but they remain relatively simple and repetitive. More nuanced reflections linking feelings to values and self-concept would be needed for a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol, pill use, and immediate safety, but it becomes repetitive in crisis planning. It lacks a concise summary that would consolidate progress, values, and next steps toward a broader recovery goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias makes choices, such as moving the bottle and drafting the text, and the psychiatrist adapts to those choices. The collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, such as scripts and safety steps, rather than building the plan from Elias’s own resources.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, avoids forcing a crisis-line call, and accepts that drafting the text is enough for the moment. However, frequent directive safety instructions can reduce the client’s sense that he is leading the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are good early evocative questions, such as asking what helped during the three months. After that, the psychiatrist shifts largely into advice, scripts, and safety directives rather than continuing to elicit Elias’s own motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers choices, and respects Elias’s pace, which supports partnership. Still, the interaction remains substantially expert-driven, with many specific strategies and safety directives limiting collaborative exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms progress, the importance of his children, and protective behavior, which helps elicit some change-related language. However, he does not consistently build on Elias’s change talk to deepen importance, desire, ability, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reframes sustain talk in a supportive way, such as normalizing crisis-line contact and describing means removal as protection rather than weakness. However, the softening often relies on direct reframing and safety instruction rather than MI techniques like double-sided reflection.
