# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client's personal choice. Some warmth feels slightly idealized, which may overstate the client's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections generally track the client's meaning and acknowledge her preference for autonomy. A few reflections add interpretive value frameworks the client did not explicitly express.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays mostly focused on the target concern of oral health and the lip piercing. A more explicit shared agenda linking values to a concrete target behavior could strengthen focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's answers shape the conversation, and the psychiatrist invites input on what feels normal. At times, the psychiatrist frames meaning in ways that feel slightly leading rather than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong and consistent throughout the session. The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms the client's right to make her own choices without pressuring change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client's own criteria, routines, and standards rather than giving reasons for change. Deeper exploration of values, importance, confidence, or future self could make evocation stronger.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally positions the client as the expert in her own experience. Some interpretive framing makes the stance feel slightly leading rather than fully partner-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several questions prompt change-related statements, but the change talk remains relatively shallow. It is not consistently amplified, summarized, or developed into stronger DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the client's sustain talk without arguing and gently reframes it as leaving room to notice meaningful changes. Some responses still reinforce the status quo rather than consistently pivoting toward change.
