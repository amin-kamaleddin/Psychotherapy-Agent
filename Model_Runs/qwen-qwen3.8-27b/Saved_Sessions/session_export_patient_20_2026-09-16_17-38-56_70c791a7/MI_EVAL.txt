# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honors Brandon’s boundaries. The warmth is somewhat professional and includes an unearned assumption that may feel intrusive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some strong reflections of Brandon’s needs, but the empathy is inconsistent and misses deeper emotional layers. The opening framing also sounds scripted and repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays within the alcohol-use domain but becomes narrow and circular around a low-pressure check-in. The psychiatrist misses the opportunity to follow up on a concrete target such as limiting drinks on weekends.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed with Brandon rather than imposed, reflecting his preferences for privacy and low effort. The counselor still channels the session toward a specific monitoring strategy and uses repeated framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a strong area, with clear support for Brandon’s choice, ownership, and right to stop if it feels like pressure. The psychiatrist avoids persuasion and honors his no-reporting boundary.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits several client-generated action ideas, but the evocation remains mostly procedural. There are missed opportunities to elicit deeper reasons, values, confidence, or motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a fairly strong partnership stance and allows Brandon’s ideas to shape the conversation. However, repetitive and prescriptive framing prevents the session from reaching the highest level of power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brandon produces useful change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or consolidate it. A major missed opportunity occurs when he does not follow up on the two-drinks-on-weekends idea.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Brandon’s sustain talk, but does not actively soften it. The response mostly accommodates resistance rather than using double-sided reflections to work with ambivalence.
