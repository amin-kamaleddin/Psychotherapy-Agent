# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates warm, nonjudgmental acceptance throughout, respecting Aisha's boundary around alcohol and reframing her self-protection positively. No pressure or labeling is evident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures Aisha's practical burden and emotional need for a break. However, empathy could be deeper, especially around her fear, self-doubt, and panic near the end.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation remains focused on the target behavior: reducing evening smoking. The psychiatrist moves steadily from identifying the problem to a concrete, small plan without drifting to alcohol.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaboratively builds a plan using Aisha's own ideas, such as tea, the couch, and a face-down phone. The interaction is strongly collaborative, though the psychiatrist still introduces some specific options and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha's autonomy is regularly supported through questions about her instincts, choices, and what feels doable. The psychiatrist could strengthen autonomy support by more explicitly reinforcing her full freedom to adjust or abandon any part of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Aisha's own ideas and solutions through open, evocative questions. Evocation is practical and strong, but could also draw out deeper motivation, values, ability, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and allows Aisha to set the agenda. Partnership is strong, but could be enhanced by letting Aisha generate more options before the psychiatrist introduces ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha generates meaningful change talk, such as limiting smoking to three, making tea, and creating quiet time. The psychiatrist effectively supports this, though could more consistently amplify change talk into broader motivation and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled appropriately without arguing or reinforcing avoidance, particularly around alcohol and self-doubt. The psychiatrist could more explicitly use double-sided reflections to further soften resistance.
