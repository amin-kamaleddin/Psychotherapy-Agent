# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, warm, and genuine unconditional positive regard throughout. Never labels, judges, or minimizes the client's beliefs, even when she expresses anger toward her husband.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond paraphrasing to capture the emotional and existential meaning behind the client's words, such as the fear that relaxation equals vulnerability. Expert-level accuracy in tracking the client's oscillation between biomedical certainty and emerging insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and coherent trajectory from rapport-building through exploring what 'okay' feels like and identifying the wedding as a contrast experience. However, the target behavior is not explicitly named or collaboratively defined, making the direction somewhat implicit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functions as a genuine dialogue where the client's spontaneous contributions shape the conversation's direction. A subtle therapeutic agenda toward the stress-symptom connection is present but appropriate for MI.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's right to choose without pressure, suggestion of specific interventions, or implied obligations. Could be strengthened by more explicit affirmations of the client's ownership and agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoking questions are well-timed and successfully draw out the wedding memory and the client's own analysis. However, the most powerful change talk moment is not deeply explored, and opportunities for scaling or confidence-building are missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert-driven stance with open, exploratory questions and no imposed agenda. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicit acknowledgment of the client's expertise in her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evokes meaningful change talk including the powerful 'almost anything' statement. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect back, amplify, or explore confidence around this change talk rather than immediately moving to barrier questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never argued against or dismissed, with good use of the 'two truths at once' framing. However, the biomedical fear remains dominant throughout, lacking double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to actively soften it.
