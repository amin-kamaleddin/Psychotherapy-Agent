# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Jordan’s perspective without pushing the vaccine. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and could more deeply attend to his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses basic reflective statements about awkwardness and worry about grandparents. Empathy remains surface-level and misses the emotional weight of Jordan’s fear of harming vulnerable family members.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to the vaccine decision and protecting his grandparents, but often expands into practical infection-control details. It lacks a clear summary or progressive movement toward a clarified target behavior and next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, such as asking preferences and incorporating Jordan’s idea to test before visiting. The session is uneven because much of the plan is delivered as expert information and recommended steps rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Jordan’s right to choose and avoids pressure or coercion. Support is strong, though the amount of advice and recommendations may position the counselor as the primary guide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, as the counselor quickly moves to information, pros and cons, scripts, and precautions. It misses key opportunities to elicit Jordan’s own values, motivations, and confidence around protecting his family.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, allowing some patient preference to guide the session. The interaction remains partly counselor-led through structured information, scripts, and expert framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan offers change talk about protecting his grandparents, but the counselor does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen it. The session provides plans and scripts rather than evoking Jordan’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not aggressively reinforce resistance, but accepts the decision not to vaccinate and builds a precaution plan around it. This misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or redirecting sustain talk toward Jordan’s values.
