# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with less explicit affirmation of strengths or emerging insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and meaningfully capture the tension between relief and morning fogginess. The main limitation is that empathy is somewhat repetitive and does not fully explore deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays tied to alcohol use and its consequences, but the target behavior is not consistently specified. Direction would be stronger with a clearer alcohol-related goal or concrete decision-making step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows the client’s topics and builds on his own ideas rather than imposing a plan. More explicit shared problem-solving language would make the collaboration more evident.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and repeatedly invites the client’s own impressions, which supports autonomy. The limitation is less explicit naming of the client’s freedom to choose and capacity to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions consistently draw out the client’s own views, needs, and possible solutions. Evocation is solid but remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully deepen values or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys collaboration by deferring to the client’s expertise and allowing his ideas to shape the session. The partnership would be more explicit with direct language about working together as equals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk around clarity, sharpness, and feeling more like himself. There are missed opportunities to amplify, deepen, or explicitly reflect back the client’s change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or dismissing it. The response could be stronger by using double-sided reflections or balancing the familiar route against the client’s desire for clear mornings.
