# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist never labels, judges, or expresses disappointment. Affirmations like 'You're not reckless. You're intentional about what matters' and 'I respect that' are consistently warm and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections that capture ambivalence (e.g., 'a part of you is genuinely open... and another part says but I've got a handle on this') and subtle linguistic cues like 'betting.' However, some reflections slightly overstate Thomas's emotional depth given his flat, rational presentation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moves logically from establishing the frame through exploring drinking patterns to presenting results, maintaining clear focus on the target behaviors. The ending lacks a concrete collaborative resolution or next step, leaving the conversation somewhat open.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong co-construction of meaning with inclusive language ('walking through them together,' 'does that match what you're hearing?') and permission-seeking before sharing results. The dynamic temporarily shifts to expert-patient when presenting lab results, which is handled well but not fully resolved.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout feature; the psychiatrist consistently and explicitly affirms Thomas's right to choose ('I'm not going to push you toward a goal that isn't yours,' 'That's your call, and it's a clear one'). The liver enzyme finding is framed as a 'signal' rather than a 'warning,' respecting Thomas's desire for reassurance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Thomas's own motivations and self-awareness through open questions, eliciting genuine client-generated insight ('the weekend thing got a little looser'). The evocation could be deeper in exploring underlying values (e.g., what 'presence' means to Thomas) and the discrepancy between his stated values and behaviors.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership stance with language positioning Thomas as the expert on his own life and agenda co-construction. The inherent power asymmetry created by the psychiatrist holding lab results is handled well but not fully resolved through more genuine peer-level sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes effective attempts to elicit change talk, particularly the 'betting vs. knowing' exploration and the question about timing that prompted Thomas to self-identify looser weekend drinking. However, the overall frequency and depth is moderate; the psychiatrist does not consistently build on or amplify the change talk that does emerge, and there is no systematic use of DARN-CAT questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk remarkably well by validating rather than arguing (e.g., affirming 'That's your call, and it's a clear one' and acknowledging the logic of the variance explanation). The sustain talk about 'I don't think I need to be better than I am' could have been explored more deeply rather than primarily validated and moved past.
