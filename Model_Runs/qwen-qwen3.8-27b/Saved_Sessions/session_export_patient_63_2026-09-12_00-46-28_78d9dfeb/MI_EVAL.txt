# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client's distress. However, the warmth feels somewhat clinical and the session quickly shifts into advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers accurate but surface-level reflections and captures the client's central ambivalence. Deeper emotional meanings, such as fear of abandonment, shame, and identity loss, are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on shoplifting, urges, peer triggers, and coping strategies. The direction is practical and behavioral, though it could better emphasize motivation and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and the client is offered choices in building a plan. The interaction remains uneven, as the psychiatrist often supplies ready-made options rather than fully co-constructing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor provides choices and asks which steps feel doable. However, much of the session is directive, with limited emphasis on the client's right to choose and ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides more advice and scripts than evocative exploration. There are few efforts to draw out the client's personal values, hopes, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor involves the client in some choices and asks for preferences. The overall interaction remains more clinician-driven than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently elicit, amplify, or reinforce it. There is limited use of MI-specific questions to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fear of losing friends and feeling isolated is acknowledged but not consistently softened. The response often shifts to problem-solving rather than exploring both sides of the ambivalence.
