# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive of Derrick’s effort and prior motivation. Acceptance was somewhat task-focused and could be strengthened by explicitly normalizing his ambivalence and peer-pressure fears.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist captured key concerns, such as protecting his license, job, and peer respect. Empathy remained largely surface-level and did not deeply explore emotions such as shame, loneliness, or fear of being seen as a “buzzkill.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing risky drinking and driving while protecting his license, job, and peer relationships. Some broader topics, such as counseling, medications, and interlock devices, slightly diluted the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Derrick contributed to the plan, including the Uber, two-drink limit, exit time, and refusal language. However, the psychiatrist often led with structured options and step-by-step plans, making the collaboration less balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked permission, offered choices, and respected Derrick’s decision to write his own text. Autonomy support was somewhat reduced by frequent structured guidance and tactical lists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and prompts helped Derrick articulate his own reasons for change and plan. Evocation was more practical than motivational, with less exploration of values, meaning, or emotional consequences.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and allowed Derrick to choose parts of the plan. The interaction sometimes felt more like expert coaching than a mutually guided exploration of his motivations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Derrick expressed clear change talk about keeping his job, driving, and controlling the evening. The psychiatrist could have more consistently amplified and deepened this change talk rather than moving quickly into additional tactics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided directly challenging Derrick’s minimization, which is preferable to arguing. However, the session used few MI strategies to soften sustain talk, such as double-sided reflections or exploring the tension behind his statements.
