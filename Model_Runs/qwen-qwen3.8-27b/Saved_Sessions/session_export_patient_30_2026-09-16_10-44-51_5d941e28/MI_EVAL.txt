# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized David’s concerns. Acceptance was solid but somewhat procedural, with limited deeper affirmation of his strengths and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several reflections captured David’s practical concerns and ambivalence. However, the responses stayed mostly at the content level and did not fully explore his underlying fear and emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stayed focused on the medication and 10-minute walk. The direction became scattered by repeatedly adding supports, which diluted momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David was invited to choose between options and his preferences were incorporated. The psychiatrist remained the primary agenda-setter, so collaboration was present but uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided pressure and respected David’s decisions. Repeated safety instructions and additional offers reduced the sense of David owning the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions were mostly logistical rather than evocative. There was limited effort to draw out David’s own motivations, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and allowed David to select practical details. However, the role remained expert-led, with less emphasis on David as the expert on his own readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David’s small commitment was acknowledged, but not amplified or expanded. The session did not actively develop his reasons, ability, need, or desire for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with David’s concerns. However, sustain talk was not effectively softened, and repeated safety warnings kept focus on risk rather than change.
