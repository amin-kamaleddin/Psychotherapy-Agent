# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental. However, parts of the session feel procedural and are not always deeply individualized to the client’s fear, self-worth, and emotional meaning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are helpful but often stay at the level of symptoms, exhaustion, and logistics. The client’s deeper themes of fear of being broken, self-criticism, and feeling like a failure are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on accessing medical care and related barriers such as fear, cost, and portal access. The trajectory is coherent, though repeated logistical scripting makes it feel somewhat procedural.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and adapts to the client’s preferences, which supports some collaboration. However, much of the work is done for the client through drafted messages, scripts, and troubleshooting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports choice, respect for the client’s decision to wait, and lack of pressure. Still, frequent offers of scripts, reminders, and next steps can subtly steer the client toward action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open-ended questions help draw out fear and goals, but evocation is often interrupted by reassurance and problem-solving. The client’s own internal motivation, values, and emerging agency are not consistently developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client preferences are incorporated, and the psychiatrist asks which option the client wants. Nevertheless, the interaction often feels expert-guided rather than a genuine partnership centered on the client’s own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change-related language, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or develop it. The session focuses more on fear and logistics than on the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally acknowledges the client’s fears without reinforcing avoidance. However, stronger double-sided reflections and strategic pivots are needed to help resolve ambivalence.
