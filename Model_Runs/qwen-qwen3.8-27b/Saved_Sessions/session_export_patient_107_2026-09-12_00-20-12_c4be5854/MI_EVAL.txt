# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, with statements that reduce defensiveness and pressure. The warmth is strong but somewhat procedural and repetitive, which prevents a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist captures the client’s ambivalence early but often relies on summary-like, advice-focused statements. There are missed opportunities to reflect the deeper emotional tension between health concerns and dependence on routine.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on coffee use and blood pressure, with little drift into unrelated topics. It is directionally solid but more logistics-heavy than strategic in helping the client clarify readiness or personal goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and adapts the plan to the client’s preferences. However, frequent unsolicited lists and medical guidance make the interaction more clinician-led than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with explicit low-pressure language and respect for the client’s choices. The large number of specific recommendations creates an implicit agenda that limits fully client-directed ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few useful open questions but quickly shifts to practical advice and monitoring instructions. There is limited exploration of the client’s internal values, motivations, benefits, or confidence in making changes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences clearly influence the plan, such as the decaf swap and GP follow-up. However, the frequent provision of checklists and recommendations makes the psychiatrist appear more like an advisor than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk appears, and the client makes small commitments, but the psychiatrist rarely elicits or amplifies reasons, importance, benefits, or confidence for change. The session is dominated by practical suggestions rather than motivational exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and accepts the client’s reasons for continuing coffee use. However, the clinician often validates the status quo rather than using double-sided reflections to gently pivot sustain talk toward change.
