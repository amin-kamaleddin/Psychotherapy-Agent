# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s discomfort. However, repeated formulaic affirmations and some endorsement of minimization limit deeper individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s perspective and concerns. The empathy remains mostly cognitive and does not fully explore underlying emotions such as shame, fear, or loss of autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on drinking and related risks without drifting off topic. The direction remains more risk-checking and problem-solving than guiding toward deeper change motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative surface structure, and the client influences the session’s direction. The interaction remains partly clinician-led because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content and planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently emphasizes the client’s choice and avoids pressure. Concrete suggestions and scripted responses are somewhat directive, preventing a top score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some attempts are made to elicit the client’s own views and worries about drinking. The evocation is limited and does not consistently draw out deeper values, confidence, or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and acknowledges the client’s existing strategies. The partnership is limited by the clinician’s control over risk categories, plans, and follow-up suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some early attempts to evoke change language. Genuine change talk is rarely captured, amplified, or used to build motivation, and the session shifts toward advice-giving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but does not strategically soften the client’s strong sustain talk. Responses such as agreeing that the client’s approach is responsible may inadvertently reinforce minimization.
