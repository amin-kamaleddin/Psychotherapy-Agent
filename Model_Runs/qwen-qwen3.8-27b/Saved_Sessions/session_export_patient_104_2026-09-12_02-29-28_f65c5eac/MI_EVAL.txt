# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, repeatedly normalizing Nadine’s exhaustion, worry, and fear of failing as a parent. Acceptance is strong but sometimes formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Nadine’s fatigue, guilt, and uncertainty early in the session, but they become repetitive and do not consistently deepen her emotional experience. Some powerful underlying feelings, such as helplessness or fear of harming her baby, are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains generally focused on the main concerns of infant feeding, sleep, and exhaustion, but it often broadens with many options, checklists, and instructions. This keeps the topic relevant but reduces the sense of a tightly guided, collaborative focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some shared decision-making, with the psychiatrist asking which options feel doable and Nadine selecting a cue to try. However, the interaction often becomes more teacher-to-student than equal partners co-constructing a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by asking permission and offering choices, but the repeated advice and directives can feel more controlling than truly empowering. Safety guidance is appropriate but could be framed in a more autonomy-supportive way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with most questions focused on logistics or practical choices rather than drawing out Nadine’s values, hopes, self-efficacy, or intrinsic reasons for change. There is little use of scaling questions or eliciting self-generated motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the psychiatrist asks permission and invites preferences, the session is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist positioned as the primary source of information and guidance. The core MI idea that Nadine is the expert on her own baby, experience, and priorities is not strongly fostered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes only limited effort to elicit or strengthen Nadine’s own language in favor of change. Small expressions of willingness are present but are not affirmed or developed into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or amplifying Nadine’s doubts and instead responds with reassurance and normalization. However, the responses do not consistently use double-sided reflections or help turn ambivalence into curiosity about values and change.
