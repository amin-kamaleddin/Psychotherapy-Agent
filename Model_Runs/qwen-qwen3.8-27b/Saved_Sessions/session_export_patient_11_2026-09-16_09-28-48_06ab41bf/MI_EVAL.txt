# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client’s tiredness and difficulty with structure. Acceptance is strong, though affirmations are sometimes tied to plan completion rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several accurate and normalizing reflections, especially early in the session. Empathic depth becomes less consistent as the conversation moves into planning, with fewer reflections of the client’s underlying emotions or tensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has clear focus and momentum toward a concrete small step. However, it shifts from the broader physical activity goal to a narrow morning breathing ritual without explicitly reconnecting it to movement or the original target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan is co-created and shaped by the client’s preferences. However, the psychiatrist introduces most of the specific options and structure, making the interaction less fully collaborative and somewhat expert-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly invited to choose the most doable option and is allowed to decline additional supports. Autonomy is well supported, though the clinician still offers many specific suggestions and briefly frames expectations in a mildly directive way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some useful questions that draw out preferences and small wins. Deeper evocation is limited, with less exploration of the client’s values, confidence, and broader meaning behind the change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session feels collaborative on the surface, and the client helps shape the plan. However, the frequent introduction of specific options and structures makes the interaction more directive than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses clear change talk about wanting to build momentum and keep the habit going. The psychiatrist elicits some of this but does not consistently amplify the client’s reasons, confidence, or deeper motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is relatively limited, and concerns such as tiredness and fear of failure are acknowledged without judgment. The psychiatrist does not consistently use strategic reflections to turn these barriers into change talk.
