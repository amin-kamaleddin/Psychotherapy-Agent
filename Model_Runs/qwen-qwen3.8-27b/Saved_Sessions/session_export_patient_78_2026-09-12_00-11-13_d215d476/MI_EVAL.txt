# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, but the acceptance is somewhat procedural. Emotional experiences and ambivalence are not consistently acknowledged with deeper empathy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate surface-level understanding of the patient's perspective and practical constraints. Deeper emotional themes such as frustration, self-criticism, and fear of failure are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking-related stress and practical coping strategies. The discussion stays connected to a specific, doable target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative in that the psychiatrist invites choices and adapts to patient preferences. The session remains somewhat counselor-led, with structured options provided rather than fully co-constructed plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's autonomy is supported through choices and respect for rejections. However, the psychiatrist still presents a fairly prescriptive plan, which can subtly shift the dynamic toward counselor direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used around preferences and feasibility. The psychiatrist does not deeply elicit the patient's intrinsic motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, but also takes a fairly expert role by presenting structured interventions. The patient's expertise is used but not fully centered in the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some change-oriented language and agreement to try a small plan. Deeper exploration of desire, ability, need, and values related to quitting smoking is limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing sustain talk or arguing with resistance. However, opportunities to reflect ambivalence or pivot sustain talk toward change are not actively used.
