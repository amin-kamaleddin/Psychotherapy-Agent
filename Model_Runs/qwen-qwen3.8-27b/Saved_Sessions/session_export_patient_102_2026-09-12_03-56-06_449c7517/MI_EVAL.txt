# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a calm, nonjudgmental tone and uses supportive statements. However, acceptance becomes over-accommodating of the client’s minimization and lacks deeper MI-congruent regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate surface-level reflections of the client’s feelings and concerns. However, the responses do not fully explore the underlying ambivalence, shame, or emotional stakes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts toward how the client should explain the event rather than staying focused on alcohol use. There is limited sustained direction toward the target behavior and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative language is used, but the interaction remains largely counselor-led problem solving. The client’s stated preferences are repeatedly overridden by the counselor’s agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids overt pressure. However, repeatedly suggesting scripted responses after client refusal subtly undermines autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some client language about the event and worries. However, there is minimal effort to evoke the client’s own reasons, values, or motivation to change drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few partnership-like choices are offered, but the session becomes counselor-driven and scripted. The client’s expertise and preferences are not meaningfully used to guide the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is essentially no effective effort to evoke change talk related to alcohol use. The counselor does not explore reasons, desire, ability, or need to reduce drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids direct confrontation but reinforces the client’s minimizing statements. There are no skilled double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to address sustain talk.
