# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or moralizing. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with less deep acknowledgment of the emotional weight and identity meaning of smoking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Marco's practicality and need for control, but the empathy stays mostly at the content level. Some reflections are formulaic and do not fully explore the vulnerability underneath his statement that cigarettes are his.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is organized and client-centered, but it drifts from smoking cessation to dental procedure logistics. The intended MI target of smoking is not consistently maintained as the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-creates a practical plan and invites Marco's input throughout. Collaboration is strong, though it remains somewhat clinician-framed within the dental task rather than fully mutual problem-solving about a larger change goal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with clear affirmation of choice, freedom from pressure, and respect for client-set limits. The clinician avoids imposing a quit goal and supports Marco's right to steer or stop the conversation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician elicits values and priorities relevant to the dental visit, but not intrinsic motivation for smoking change. There is limited exploration of importance, confidence, or small steps related to reducing or stopping smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently positions Marco as the expert on his own life and choices. Marco's contributions substantially shape the session, including the focus, limits, and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only weak, occasional attempts to invite Marco to articulate his own reasons for change. The clinician does not use DARN/CRAF-style questions or build on emerging openness to develop smoking-related change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without arguing, confronting, or escalating resistance. However, the clinician does not use more active MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections or reframing, to pivot sustain talk toward change.
