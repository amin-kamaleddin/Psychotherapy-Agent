# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Kareem's right to like smoking and his emotional experience. The clinician never labels negatively, normalizes social needs, and gently pushes back on self-deprecation without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go well beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture the emotional and relational core of Kareem's experience. The distinction between 'the banter itself' and 'the feeling of being part of that group' demonstrates nuanced, accurate understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent logical progression from identifying the problem, to exploring existing strategies, to identifying harder moments, with clear momentum at each turn. The session never drifts and each question builds purposefully on the previous response.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session feels like a genuine collaboration between equal partners, with the clinician consistently building on Kareem's ideas rather than imposing their own. The tone communicates mutual respect without any lecturing or expert-driven positioning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support woven into every interaction, including 'You like smoking, and that's not going to change' and 'on your own terms.' The clinician never pressures, never implies Kareem should quit, and frames all options as his choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Kareem's own strategies (ten-minute pause, cutting down) and invites him to generate solutions. Could be deeper by more explicitly drawing out what his existing progress tells him about his own ability and motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Kareem as the expert on his own life with questions like 'What do you think would actually work in your day?' The partnership is present but somewhat implicit rather than explicitly fostered through shared goal-setting or power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively highlights Kareem's existing change behaviors and frames them positively ('That's not nothing'). Misses opportunities to more explicitly elicit deeper change talk, such as asking what cutting from 7 to 4 tells him about what he's capable of.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without arguing or reinforcing it, fully accepting 'I like smoking' and validating that patches feel like 'admitting defeat.' Could use more double-sided reflections to help Kareem see that both his love of smoking and his social needs can be honored simultaneously.
