# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment, often reframing checking the phone as information rather than failure. Affirmations are generally strong but become somewhat formulaic and do not fully normalize the client's ambivalence in a more individualized way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses specific reflective listening to capture the client's ambivalence, boredom, fear of missing out, and fatigue. However, the empathy remains largely pragmatic and does not fully explore the deeper emotional meaning behind the client's phone use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing nighttime phone use and its effects on sleep and coursework. The final plan is concrete, behavior-specific, and shows little aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by adapting the plan to the client's preferences and using the client's own language. It falls short of a 5 because the clinician still drives much of the agenda and generates most of the solution options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice, offers low-effort options, and avoids overt pressure. However, the session is quite prescriptive, with the clinician designing much of the experiment and reducing the client's opportunity to fully own the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the weakest component, as early open questions are not followed up to draw out the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. The session quickly shifts to advice-giving and behavior planning instead of eliciting intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership through collaborative language and by inviting the client's input. Nevertheless, the stance remains fairly expert-driven, with the clinician serving as the primary generator of ideas and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only rare and weak attempts to cultivate change talk, and the session moves quickly into behavior experiments without expanding the client's own reasons for change. The clinician does not effectively elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist softens sustain talk by validating the client's barriers without judgment. However, the session gives substantial attention to reasons for maintaining the status quo and uses limited double-sided reflection or redirection toward change talk.
