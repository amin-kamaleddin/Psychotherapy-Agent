# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, affirming, and nonjudgmental, consistently validating the patient’s goals and effort. Warmth is strong, though affirmations are often tied more to task completion than to the patient’s personal struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the patient’s ambivalence usefully but often moves quickly into problem-solving. Deeper attention to feelings such as fear, exhaustion, or embarrassment would strengthen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on reducing alcohol use and consistently returns to concrete, relevant goals and implementation steps. It is task-oriented, occasionally advancing planning faster than the motivational process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient’s preferences clearly shape the final plan, showing real collaboration. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure and options, making the process more guided than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear autonomy-supportive statements and the patient is invited to choose doable options. The rapid pace, multiple questions, and scripted suggestions can limit the sense of full self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits reasons for change, importance/confidence ratings, and a workable plan from the patient. More time could be spent drawing out the patient’s own values, hopes, and self-efficacy before moving into advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the patient as in charge and repeatedly asks for preferences. The role can shift quickly into an expert problem-solver, reducing the sense of true equality in the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient generates useful change talk and confidence increases throughout. Change talk is not consistently amplified or deepened through elaborative, MI-consistent follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is accepted without arguing or reinforcing the status quo. The psychiatrist could more consistently use advanced softening strategies such as double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance.
