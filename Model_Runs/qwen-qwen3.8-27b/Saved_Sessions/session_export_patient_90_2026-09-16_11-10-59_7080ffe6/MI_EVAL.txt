# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect, nonjudgment, and support while honoring the client's boundary around his wife and medications. The warmth is sometimes procedural rather than deeply individualized, and a few affirmations are evaluative.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects frustration, burden, cost, and concerns about being bossed around. The empathy is often summary-like and misses deeper emotions such as overwhelm, fear of failure, and the personal meaning of the dog.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior of daily controller inhaler use. The psychiatrist consistently guides the conversation from readiness and barriers toward a concrete, achievable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and adjusts the plan based on his preferences and constraints. At times, the psychiatrist supplies practical options rather than fully waiting for the client to generate them, which makes the interaction feel more guided than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly affirms the client's choice and avoids pressuring him about follow-up or medication management. Occasional offering of multiple options and subtle references to the physician's focus slightly reduce the sense of full autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling and open questions to help the client articulate goals, barriers, and confidence. The evocation is not consistently deep, and practical problem-solving sometimes begins before the client's own motivation and values are fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that the client's input matters and allows his choices to shape the plan. The partnership is not fully developed because the psychiatrist occasionally shifts into an advisory or problem-solving role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes useful change talk by asking about goals, readiness, small steps, and confidence. There are missed opportunities to deepen and amplify the client's values and importance statements before moving into practical planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging concerns about cost, appointments, and pressure without arguing or confronting them. More strategic MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, could further help the client hold and turn ambivalence into change talk.
