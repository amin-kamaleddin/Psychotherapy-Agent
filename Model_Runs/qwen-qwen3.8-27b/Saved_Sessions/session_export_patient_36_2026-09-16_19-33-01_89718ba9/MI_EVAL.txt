# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, unconditional regard, and nonjudgmental validation. Even when the client is self-critical, the response reframes the experience without labeling or dismissing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Perspective-taking and reflective listening are strong, often capturing both sides of the client's ambivalence. It falls short of a 5 because some underlying frustration, fatigue, and self-criticism are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of pausing before automatically saying yes. Each question and reflection follows a coherent, client-led sequence toward a workable boundary practice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates the plan rather than leading with expert advice. The client generates the examples, cues, and practice ideas, and the psychiatrist builds on them using the client's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice, ownership, and the client's right to decide what feels manageable. The language and questions avoid pressure, directives, or turning the practice into an obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out the client's own values, motivations, and change ideas rather than supplying external reasons. It could be stronger by evoking confidence, identity, and commitment more explicitly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions expertise and wisdom within the client, inviting the client to shape the agenda and solution. There is no premature advice or expert-driven stance, making the interaction genuinely equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicits and amplifies the client's language in favor of change, especially around self-respect, control, and planning. It is not a 5 because the change talk is not systematically expanded across broader motivational domains.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by normalizing concerns and redirecting them toward workable change. The psychiatrist prevents objections from dominating the session while keeping the practice light and sustainable.
