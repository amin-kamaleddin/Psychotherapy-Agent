# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirms the client’s progress without arguing or correcting. Acceptance could be strengthened by more warmly normalizing setbacks and validating the client’s self-critical distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses accurate reflections of the client’s ambivalence, progress, and barriers. The main limitation is that the responses are more cognitive than emotional and do not fully reflect shame or all-or-nothing distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains strongly focused on the target behavior of sustainable gym consistency. The therapist effectively narrows a broad concern into a concrete, actionable plan with minimal topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist treats the client as a partner and co-constructs a plan that reflects the client’s preferences and constraints. The therapist still provides a fairly large menu of options, which keeps the interaction somewhat facilitated rather than fully co-designed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist consistently supports choice by inviting the client to lead, offering options, and respecting the client’s rejections. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming that the decision remains entirely the client’s, including the freedom not to follow through.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist elicits the client’s own motivations, reminders, and confidence increases rather than providing reasons for change. The evocation is somewhat plan-focused and could be deeper in exploring the client’s underlying values and self-image.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist maintains a collaborative stance and lets the client’s choices shape the plan. However, the frequent use of structured options and suggestions makes the interaction less fully power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively uses scaling and planning questions to generate concrete change talk and increase confidence. The skill is present and useful, though it would benefit from more values-based and identity-based amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging barriers without arguing and by creating flexible alternatives. Some expressions of sustain talk, such as “one bad day and I’m back to square one,” are not fully reflected or softened.
