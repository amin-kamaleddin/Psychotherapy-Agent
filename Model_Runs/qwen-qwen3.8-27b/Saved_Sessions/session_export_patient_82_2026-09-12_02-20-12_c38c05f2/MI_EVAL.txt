# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a respectful, nonjudgmental, validating tone without shame or arguing. Some warmth felt formulaic, and continued pushing for more planning slightly undercut unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured ambivalence and anxiety but remained somewhat generic. Deeper issues such as authenticity, belonging, and identity conflict were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the presenting concern and related alcohol-use issues. Direction was clear, though it sometimes shifted toward problem-solving rather than deeper MI exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited choices and valued the client’s plan, supporting some collaboration. However, frequent advice-giving made the interaction more counselor-driven than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial autonomy support was strong, with clear statements that the client was in charge. Later suggestions for scripts and role-play created pressure despite the client saying he was ready to stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers drew out some change talk and client motivations. The psychiatrist did not consistently deepen or sustain the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session began with a partnership stance and offered choices. It became uneven when the psychiatrist moved into expert advice and repeated planning suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helped evoke meaningful change talk related to team, grades, and the plan. Change language was not consistently amplified or built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without defensiveness. It was not strategically softened through balanced reflections or value-based weighing of consequences.
