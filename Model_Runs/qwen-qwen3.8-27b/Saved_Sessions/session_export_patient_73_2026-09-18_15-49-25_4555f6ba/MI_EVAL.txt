# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful throughout, with zero labeling or minimization of the client's frustration. Immediately acknowledges and corrects when a reframe doesn't land, validating the client's perspective without pushing back.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate and deep perspective-taking, capturing both content and emotional undercurrents with remarkable precision. Reflections like 'like the failure is almost rehearsed' and 'you're not even in the driver's seat anymore' show genuine experiential understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, purposeful progression from opening through exploring past attempts, social meaning, fear of the first break, and recognition of shift. No topic drift or loss of momentum; each question builds on the previous response while remaining responsive to the client's pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as a joint exploration, inviting the client's input at every decision point and letting her words shape the conversation. Even gentle pushback is framed collaboratively rather than as a correction, maintaining a genuinely equal power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never gives advice, imposes a timeline, or says 'you should/need to'; consistently returns decision-making power to the client. Respects the client's self-set six-month timeline and affirms her agency in choosing to engage.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates from the principle that the answer is inside the client, using open questions and strategic silence to draw out her own motivations rather than providing external reasons for change. Questions like 'What do you think would need to be true for quitting to feel possible?' are textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong collaborative stance with phrases like 'I want to push back gently' and 'I'm curious,' positioning the client as the authority on her experience. Could be elevated by more explicitly acknowledging the client's expertise in her own life or co-creating a concrete next step together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategically positions questions that invite the client to generate her own change language and consistently catches and amplifies change talk with reflections. The six-month prompt and the question about what would need to be true for quitting to feel possible are particularly masterful evocations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating social attachment and gently pushing back on self-deprecating language without being adversarial. A missed opportunity at the point of maximum ambivalence (fear of the first break) where a double-sided reflection could have helped the client see the tension between her desire to quit and the rehearsed failure narrative.
