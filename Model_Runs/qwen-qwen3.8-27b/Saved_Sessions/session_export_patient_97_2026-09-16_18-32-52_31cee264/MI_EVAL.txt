# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and explicitly works to reduce pressure. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and does not fully normalize re-entry, past behavior, or fear of relapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses good reflective listening and accurately captures the client’s ambivalence, pressure, and concern about his daughter. Empathy remains partly cognitive and does not fully deepen into the client’s shame, fear of failing his daughter, or identity-level meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on re-entry, avoiding old habits, and supporting the client’s relationship with his daughter. However, the target behavior is broad, and direction would benefit from a clearer, behavior-specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input and co-creates ideas around daughter-focused, low-pressure check-ins. Collaboration is strong, but the counselor still leads through repeated question prompts that can make the exchange feel somewhat scripted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports the client’s choice, ownership, and freedom, while reassuring him that his requests are not asking for too much. Some agenda-setting and problem-solving questions feel mildly directive, though they remain largely autonomy-respecting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful client preferences and small practical steps, such as keeping the phone charged. Evocation remains somewhat superficial and does not fully draw out deeper motivation, discrepancy, or reasons the client might be able to succeed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys a collaborative, partner stance and invites the client to shape the process and check-in style. The partnership is strong, but repeated similar questions can limit the sense that the client is truly co-leading the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk around his daughter and small steps, such as keeping the phone charged. The counselor does not consistently strengthen, amplify, or develop this change talk into a stronger plan or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and acknowledges the client’s sustain talk about mandatory sessions, self-doubt, and fear of relapse. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened through deeper MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or discrepancy work.
