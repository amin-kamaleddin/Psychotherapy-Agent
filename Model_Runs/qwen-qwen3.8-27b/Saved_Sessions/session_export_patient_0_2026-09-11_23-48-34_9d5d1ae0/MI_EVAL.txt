# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing with the client’s minimization. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic at times, with moments of evaluative or corrective language that limit warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of the client’s affect and concerns, such as not wanting to be judged or feeling annoyed. However, the empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore shame, fear, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting issue of methamphetamine use and the nasal infection. It lacks a clear MI-driven change goal and becomes repetitive in practical medical advice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some input and appears willing to co-create a simple plan. The interaction remains largely one-way, with the clinician supplying most of the content rather than fully partnering with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with permission-seeking, open invitations, and explicit respect for the client’s decision not to quit. The stance is partly weakened by frequent directive medical advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions to elicit the client’s preferred action. The session shifts quickly into advice-giving instead of drawing out deeper values, concerns, or confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Partnership is limited because the interaction is strongly expert-driven and advice-heavy. The client is not meaningfully positioned as the expert on his own priorities, values, or change process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are small client statements of willingness, such as trying rinses or seeking care if symptoms worsen, but little deliberate effort to elicit or expand change talk. The session focuses more on practical steps than on developing motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging the client’s sustain talk and redirects toward lower-stakes actions. The sustain talk is not deeply explored or strategically softened using MI techniques.
