# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates a respectful, nonjudgmental, and time-sensitive stance while honoring Maria’s urgency and autonomy. However, occasional agenda-focused framing, such as “inhaler and asthma routine,” slightly reduces the depth of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong perspective-taking by reflecting Maria’s time pressure, ambivalence, distrust, and concern about side effects. Some reflections remain formulaic or interpretive, limiting deeper exploration of her frustration and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session largely stays focused on asthma management and adapts to Maria’s rescue-only question. Direction is strong, though occasional language subtly steers toward routine or planning beyond the client’s stated immediate concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and allows Maria to shape the final note and plan. Collaboration is effective, though the clinician still maintains a medical-management frame for much of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly affirms Maria’s choice, avoids pressure, and does not impose a medical recommendation. Autonomy support is strong, but some questions presume movement toward considering or confirming the inhaler.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to elicit Maria’s own perspective and evokes some relevant client language. However, evocation remains shallow because the conversation quickly shifts to practical next steps rather than deepening motivation, values, or consequences.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and invites Maria’s input on the note and plan. Partnership is solid, though some questions subtly guide toward inhaler consideration or prescriber confirmation rather than fully following the client’s lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk around lung protection and willingness to use the inhaler for acute symptoms. The evocation is not consistently developed, as the session moves quickly into practical steps rather than expanding Maria’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Maria’s sustain talk and acknowledges her concerns without confrontation. However, sustain talk remains dominant and is not consistently addressed with more effective MI strategies such as double-sided reflections or exploring consequences.
