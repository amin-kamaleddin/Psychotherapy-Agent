# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and reduces shame around Anya’s gambling, debt, and ambivalence. Acceptance is strong, though it remains somewhat practical and does not consistently affirm her worth beyond progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some accurate reflections, especially of frustration, fear, and ambivalence. Empathy is uneven because several emotional cues are missed and responses often shift into practical planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing gambling-related financial risk and follows through on concrete steps such as card removal, avoiding pubs, and texting her sister. There is little drift, and the conversation steadily moves toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates plans and uses Anya’s preferences, such as the short text, sister support, and chosen timeframe. Collaboration is strong, though frequent structured checklists make the interaction feel somewhat counselor-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice and avoids direct pressure, allowing Anya to select the plan, timing, and wording. Autonomy support would be stronger with less reliance on directive checklists and more explicit reinforcement of her freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Anya’s own motivations and commitments, especially around small steps and confidence. However, the work remains action-focused and does not deeply explore values, hope, or identity-level reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and affirms Anya’s judgment, but the interaction can become expert-driven when full plans, scripts, and checklists are supplied. Power sharing is good but uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes and reinforces Anya’s change-oriented language and uses confidence questions to support commitment. The limitation is that the change talk is not consistently deepened through values, hope, or identity-level benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge or amplify sustain talk and accepts Anya’s ambivalence without judgment. Active softening is limited because there is little use of double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of the function of gambling.
