# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, non-judgmental stance throughout, reframing Seamus's resistance as self-protection rather than defiance. The warmth is consistent but somewhat measured and professional; a stronger explicit normalization of ambivalence or statement of unconditional positive regard would push this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several strong complex reflections capture Seamus's emotional meaning (e.g., 'You're not saying I want to quit smoking, you're saying I want my teeth to stop getting worse'). However, two consecutive generic 'I understand. Can you tell me more about that?' responses represent a notable lapse in reflective depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the smoking-dental connection with a logical arc from resistance to exploration to affirmation. The two consecutive passive 'I understand' prompts show a moment of losing strategic direction, and a mid-session summary would have consolidated progress more effectively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is genuinely co-constructed, with Seamus's ideas about his goals, boundaries, and definition of 'useful' shaping the session direction. The power dynamic is genuinely balanced, with the psychiatrist explicitly acknowledging Seamus as the expert on his own life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — the psychiatrist never pressures, implies obligation, or frames inaction as failure. Key moves include explicitly rejecting a controlling stance ('we're not going to be handing you anything') and affirming that timing is entirely Seamus's.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws motivation from within Seamus (his teeth, his autonomy) rather than providing reasons for change. However, evocation could have gone deeper — when Seamus said 'I'll think about it,' the psychiatrist could have explored what that thinking might look like.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Seamus as the authority on his own experience, inviting him to set the frame and define what 'sorted' means. The session direction is substantially shaped by Seamus's contributions, with the psychiatrist following his lead while maintaining MI direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questioning and affirmative reflections effectively draw out change talk (e.g., 'You just said you've thought about it — and that's not nothing'). The psychiatrist could have more aggressively amplified and expanded Seamus's own motivational statements, particularly at the session's close.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through validation rather than argument, and the pills experience is skillfully used as a pivot to open a door. The psychiatrist does not employ more advanced techniques like double-sided reflections, which would further soften resistance.
