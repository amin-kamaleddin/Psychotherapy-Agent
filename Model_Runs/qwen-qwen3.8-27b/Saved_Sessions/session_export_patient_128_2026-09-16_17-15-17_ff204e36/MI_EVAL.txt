# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains nonjudgmental, respectful, and validating while acknowledging Tyler's ambivalence and risk concerns. There is room to add warmer, more explicit affirmations of his strengths, honesty, and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Tyler's core conflict between staying socially connected and protecting his record. The reflections are clear but could go deeper into underlying emotions such as shame, embarrassment, or need for belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a very clear and logical focus on reducing the risk of another alcohol-related incident while preserving social connection. Each step advances smoothly from ambivalence to a concrete, client-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the exit plan using Tyler's own ideas, such as leaving with a friend and using a subtle cue. The interaction could become more fully collaborative by using fewer menu-style questions and more open-ended agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct advice and respects Tyler's boundary that he is not ready to quit, steering toward risk reduction. It could be stronger by more explicitly affirming Tyler's ownership of the plan and his right to decide in the moment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits client-generated solutions and motivation for change, including the subtle friend cue plan. The session could strengthen evocation by drawing out more discrepancy, confidence, and values-based change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses client-centered questions and allows Tyler's ideas to shape the direction of the session. Partnership could be strengthened by reducing menu-style prompts and inviting more explicit, open-ended choice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk and concrete intentions from Tyler, such as leaving early and reducing escalation. The session could further develop change talk across importance, need, ability, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by not arguing with Tyler's statement that he is not ready to quit and by redirecting to risk reduction. This could be strengthened with more double-sided reflections that normalize both sides of the conflict.
