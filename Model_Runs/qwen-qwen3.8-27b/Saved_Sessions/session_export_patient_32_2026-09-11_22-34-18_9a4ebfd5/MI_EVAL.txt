# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Derrick’s honesty and plan without moralizing about the DUI. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural and task-focused rather than deeply emotionally present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of Derrick’s ambivalence and stress, but the counselor often moves quickly into practical problem-solving. Key emotional themes such as fear of being mocked, loss of control, and relational pain are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior, consequences, and a concrete weekend plan to reduce DUI risk. It is well-directed but remains more behavioral and procedural than fully MI-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Derrick is invited to choose among options and contributes to the plan, which supports engagement. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the content and structure, making the collaboration only partially balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor respects Derrick’s choices and does not push unwanted suggestions, such as a written reminder or refusal script. Still, the directive plan and structured recommendations can feel counselor-led rather than fully autonomy-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some useful MI questions elicit Derrick’s choices and confidence rating, and he offers practical ideas. The counselor does not consistently draw out Derrick’s deeper motivations, values, or meaning behind his change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Derrick’s preferences and allows his choices to shape the plan. The partnership is uneven because the counselor frequently acts as the expert providing the menu of options and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Derrick naturally produces meaningful change talk, including clear limits such as not driving if drinking and keeping beer but not driving. The counselor affirms these statements but does not consistently reflect, elaborate, or expand them into stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with Derrick’s minimization and validates his desire to avoid giving up his whole life. The session does not actively use more advanced MI techniques to reframe ambivalence or soften sustain talk around peer pressure and risk.
