# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Jordan’s ambivalence. The warmth is generally supportive, though occasionally slightly generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Jordan’s perspective accurately and validates his concerns about hassle and uncertainty. The reflections could have gone deeper into the emotional meaning of protecting his grandparents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the flu vaccine decision and related ambivalence. Direction could be stronger by consistently linking practical steps back to Jordan’s values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative stance and invites Jordan’s input on how to proceed. There is occasional counselor-driven structuring through multiple offered options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Jordan’s right to choose and avoids pressuring him toward vaccination. Autonomy support could be further strengthened by explicitly affirming his permission to choose not to vaccinate.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses readiness questions and explores what would make vaccination more acceptable. There is less depth in drawing out Jordan’s internal values, motivations, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan is treated as the expert on his own decision, and his preferences are generally honored. The interaction can feel slightly structured by counselor-generated options at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, especially around protecting his grandparents. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk with more evocative follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met without argument or judgment, which reduces defensiveness. The psychiatrist could use more double-sided reflections to gently tilt toward change.
