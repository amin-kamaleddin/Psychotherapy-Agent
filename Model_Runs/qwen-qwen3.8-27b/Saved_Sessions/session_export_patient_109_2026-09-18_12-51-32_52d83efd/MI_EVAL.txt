# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout the session. Could be slightly deeper in moments where Rafael expresses self-deprecation, offering more direct affirmation of his worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and layered reflective listening that tracks Rafael's emotional shifts and reflects underlying meaning rather than just content. Reflections advance understanding and distinguish between nuanced feelings (e.g., disliking the activity vs. showing up depleted).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior with clear strategic momentum, progressing logically from ambivalence to a specific, actionable goal. Pacing respects the client's brief responses without losing forward movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership where the session direction is substantially shaped by Rafael's own contributions. The morning walk goal, transition insight, and time-of-day specification all emerged from his own words rather than a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is woven into every interaction rather than being a stated principle. The psychiatrist never imposes, prescribes, or frames change as obligation, consistently inviting Rafael's own standards and choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Rafael without providing external reasons or arguments for change. Rafael arrives at his own goal (morning walks) through his own words, which is the hallmark of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never assumes the role of expert or prescribes solutions, consistently positioning Rafael as the one who holds the answers. The session is co-constructed, with reflections helping Rafael see his own thinking more clearly rather than redirecting toward a predetermined goal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces genuine change talk from Rafael, strategically creating space for it to emerge. Could be elevated by more explicitly amplifying the strongest change talk moments or exploring confidence with a scaling question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating barriers without arguing, then gently reframing (e.g., noting all barriers occur after stepping outside). Could benefit from more strategic use of double-sided reflections to resolve specific sustain talk statements.
