# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance but moves quickly into advice and behavioral planning. This can make acceptance feel procedural rather than deeply supportive of Jordan’s ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses brief reflective statements that show some understanding of stress, pressure, and peer influence. However, the empathy remains surface-level and often misses deeper emotions such as shame, fear of being mocked, and belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of shoplifting and related high-risk moments. The main limitation is that the direction becomes overly focused on behavioral tactics rather than motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan contributes some ideas, but the psychiatrist frequently generates most options, scripts, and plans. The interaction is therefore not fully collaborative in an MI sense.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive statements, but the psychiatrist also gives many detailed behavioral instructions and rules. This reduces Jordan’s sense of ownership over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few questions but does not strongly evoke Jordan’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change. The session shifts too quickly into advice and problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s input and incorporates some of his choices. However, the psychiatrist often takes the lead as the expert problem-solver, making the partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some planning-related input but does not effectively evoke or expand Jordan’s own change talk. There is limited exploration of why stopping matters or how it connects to Jordan’s values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Jordan’s sustain talk about friends, belonging, and pressure, but often responds with practical countermeasures. There is little MI-consistent softening, such as double-sided reflections or exploration of long-term costs.
