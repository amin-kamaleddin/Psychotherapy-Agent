# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone while the client minimizes responsibility and rejects counseling. Acceptance is slightly weakened by reflections that begin steering toward safer alcohol use or responsibility.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s ambivalence, frustration, and concern about being treated as the “main guy.” Empathy is somewhat surface-level and occasionally reframes the client more positively than stated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays anchored to the conduct incident and the next step, but becomes circular around what is “fair,” “safe,” or “proportionate.” Momentum is limited because the conversation does not consistently move toward a concrete change-focused goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting the client’s perspective and co-constructing the next step rather than imposing a solution. Collaboration remains narrow, mostly focused on procedural consequences rather than broader values or identity.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s agency is consistently affirmed, with no pressure to accept counseling, admit fault, or change behavior. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by repeated framing of the fine or signature as “responsible” or a “good choice.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite the client’s perspective, but evocation remains superficial and procedural. The psychiatrist does not deeply explore values, fears, future consequences, or the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert and asks for the client’s preferred approach to the situation. Partnership is slightly limited by agenda-driven language around safety, health, responsibility, and protecting the future.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area, with only limited and superficial attempts to elicit change talk. The client’s brief procedural commitments are acknowledged, but deeper motivations, abilities, or commitments for safer alcohol use are not developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or agreeing that the consequences are absurd, and uses balancing language to acknowledge the client’s concerns. The session still stays too focused on minimizing the consequence without consistently pivoting toward discrepancy or broader motivation.
