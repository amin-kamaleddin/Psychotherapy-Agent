# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates Margaret's choices and independence. The warmth is somewhat procedural, and repeated safety/administrative content gives parts of the session a case-management tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and plan-focused, showing understanding of Margaret's perspective. They do not consistently access deeper feelings such as fear of being controlled, concern about being labeled, or the emotional impact of the fall.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol-related safety and a concrete small-change plan. Some repetition of safety steps makes the session feel less efficient and less forward-moving than an optimal MI session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Margaret's input and builds the plan around her stated preferences. The clinician still supplies many suggestions, so the collaboration is strong but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through explicit statements of choice and respect for Margaret's refusal of routine follow-up. Repeated safety reminders and contact documentation subtly imply some degree of clinical oversight.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful evocative questions about confidence, barriers, and what would signal success. The process elicits action steps more than deeper intrinsic motivation, values, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a nonexpert stance and frequently invites Margaret's choices and input. The agenda remains shaped more by the clinician's risk-management concerns than by a fully co-created direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret generates useful change talk and a specific plan, such as ordering water first and having one glass. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen this change talk by exploring underlying reasons and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Margaret's sustain talk and continues collaborating. He does not consistently use double-sided reflections to validate her perspective while gently redirecting toward change.
