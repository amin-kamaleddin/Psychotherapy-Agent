# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, explicitly avoiding labels such as "alcoholic." Some warmth is somewhat formulaic, and repeated safety warnings make acceptance feel procedural rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several empathic statements, but reflections often stay at the surface and repeat. Deeper ambivalence, social identity, and fears about change are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, risk, and a clear practical plan. However, the emphasis on safety information and plan details sometimes takes priority over exploring the client's motivations and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited collaborative elements, such as asking permission and offering options, but the psychiatrist largely leads the problem definition, feedback, and plan. The client's own ideas do not substantially shape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client's readiness and offers optional next steps, supporting some autonomy. However, repeated structured plans and firm safety guidance reduce the sense that the client is fully in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is used only superficially, mainly at the start and in a few information-gathering questions. The psychiatrist mostly provides reasons for change rather than eliciting and expanding the client's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for permission and invites input, showing some partnership. Still, the role remains expert-driven, with the psychiatrist primarily offering criteria, risks, and a structured plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms it, but there is limited strategic development of those statements. The session does not consistently use evocative questions or summaries to strengthen the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not mock or directly confront the client's sustain talk and acknowledges the value of social drinking. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened with double-sided reflections or gentle pivots, and the response often shifts back to information and planning.
