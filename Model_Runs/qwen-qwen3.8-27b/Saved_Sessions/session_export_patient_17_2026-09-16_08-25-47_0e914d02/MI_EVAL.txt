# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses warm, nonjudgmental affirmations and normalizes difficulty. However, some statements feel mildly evaluative or quickly shift into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture practical stress and ambivalence. They remain mostly content-based and do not consistently explore deeper feelings such as shame, fear, or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation. The counselor moves effectively to a concrete smoke-free goal, backup plan, and follow-up time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client to choose options and adapts the plan to her input. It is collaborative, but the counselor still supplies much of the content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice, respects the client's preference for quitting clean, and frames slips without judgment. It is somewhat directive in generating specific next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions evoke importance and confidence, but the counselor quickly shifts into advice-giving. The session does not consistently draw out the client's deeper motivations or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor checks what feels doable and uses client choices in the plan. However, the interaction remains expert-driven and does not fully position the client as the primary problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful change talk about protecting her daughter and improving health. She does not fully develop it with scaling follow-ups, values, or broader meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled nonconfrontationally and the counselor avoids arguing with the client's doubts. However, the counselor often moves to options before fully processing the fear underneath the ambivalence.
