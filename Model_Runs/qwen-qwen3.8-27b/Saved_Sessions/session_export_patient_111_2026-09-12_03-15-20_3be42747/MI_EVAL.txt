# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of Richard’s resistance without moralizing. Acceptance is strong but sometimes more process-focused than deeply affirming of Richard’s personal values and relationships.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and summaries that validate Richard’s perspective, including his need for autonomy and concern about his wife. The empathy could be deeper by exploring the emotional meaning behind his fear of losing his marriage.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target issue of smoking and its impact on his wife. The psychiatrist skillfully guides the conversation toward practical, relevant options while respecting the client’s limits.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Richard’s input and follows his lead, particularly when choosing the patio change and focusing on communication. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, especially in the final script portion, which slightly reduces co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Richard’s autonomy by repeatedly emphasizing that the decision is his and offering options as optional. The response to his resistance and warnings against a program is especially effective.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions that help Richard identify some preferences and values, such as keeping his break and not wanting to worry his wife. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently draw out stronger change talk related to desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and asks for Richard’s preferences throughout the session. The guiding role becomes stronger when presenting specific options and scripts, which slightly limits the fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated ideas, such as the patio change and concern about his wife. The counselor could more consistently amplify and expand Richard’s own change-relevant statements to build internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Richard’s sustain talk and accepts his reluctance to quit. More strategic MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or reframing concerns toward his values, could better soften resistance.
