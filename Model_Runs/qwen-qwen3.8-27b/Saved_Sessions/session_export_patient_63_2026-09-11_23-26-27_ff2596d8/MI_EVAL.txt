# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers brief affirmations such as “You’re not alone.” However, acceptance remains somewhat formulaic and shifts quickly into practical problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate simple reflections of Jordan’s conflict, panic, and social pressure. Deeper feelings such as shame, fear of isolation, guilt, and the unreality of consequences are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on shoplifting and clear high-risk situations, including specific people, places, and moments. The focus remains more on immediate behavior management than linking the behavior to broader values and long-term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan contributes useful input, such as choosing “I’m out,” using his keys as an exit cue, and naming his sister as a contact. The psychiatrist still frequently drives the plan with structured lists and recommended steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some choices, such as selecting a refusal line and exit cue, but many directive scripts and fixed steps reduce autonomy. Jordan’s statement that he does not feel he has a choice is not deeply explored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly supplies strategies and reassurance rather than drawing out Jordan’s own reasons, values, or past successes. There are few evocative questions that help Jordan articulate his own motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan’s ideas are used in several parts of the session, such as the refusal line and exit cue. The partnership remains uneven because the psychiatrist remains the primary source of plans, scripts, and steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan offers clear change talk, including wanting to stop and not returning to court, and the psychiatrist occasionally affirms it. The change talk is not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened to strengthen motivation and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is prominent around peer pressure, fear of being seen as “lame,” and the feeling of being part of the group. The psychiatrist often adds coping steps rather than helping Jordan examine the ambivalence or separate from the behavior.
