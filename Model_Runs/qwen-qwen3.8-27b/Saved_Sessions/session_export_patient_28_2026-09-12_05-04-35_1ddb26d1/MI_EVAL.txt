# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing of Dante’s hesitation and fatigue. Some praise-like language is mildly evaluative, but acceptance is generally strong.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and helpful, especially in summarizing Dante’s ambivalence and barriers. Reflections could be deeper emotionally, particularly around identity loss, frustration, and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on increasing physical activity and moves toward a concrete, manageable step. Pacing is solid, though exploration could be slightly longer before planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist checks wording, offers choices, and builds the plan with Dante’s input. It would be stronger if Dante generated more of the options first rather than selecting from the psychiatrist’s menu.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Dante’s choices are respected, including rejecting the counting strategy and not promising success. The frequent proposals still subtly pressure Dante to choose from the psychiatrist’s agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some useful motivation through scaling, prior routines, and confidence. More evocation would require less advice-giving and more client-generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids imposing the plan, but often drives problem-solving. More client-led exploration would make the partnership feel less directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Dante offers meaningful change talk, such as choosing laying out clothes and accepting a 60-second step. The psychiatrist often moves to planning too quickly instead of amplifying Dante’s own language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without confrontation using normalization and low-pressure language. More double-sided reflections would better soften barriers such as fatigue and avoidance.
