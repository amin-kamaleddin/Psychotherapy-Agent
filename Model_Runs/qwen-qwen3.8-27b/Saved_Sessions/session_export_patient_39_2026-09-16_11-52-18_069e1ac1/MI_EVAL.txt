# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental language and affirms Elias’s honesty and small wins. The tone is respectful, though it feels somewhat task-centered rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects key ambivalence about wanting to move while being limited by pain and mornings. Many responses summarize plan details rather than fully exploring the emotional meaning behind Elias’s fears and self-judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior of increasing daily activity despite mobility limitations. It moves smoothly from ambivalence to confidence scaling, barrier identification, and a concrete small-step plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is built collaboratively, with Elias choosing late-morning activity, heat, spouse involvement, a paper reminder, and a two-minute fallback. It is not fully client-driven because the counselor frequently supplies multiple options and problem-solving prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses choice-based language and respects Elias’s limits, supporting his sense of ownership. Some repeated options and commitments make the interaction somewhat more structured than fully autonomy-maximizing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and choice questions elicit useful statements about confidence and willingness to try the next day. The evocation is somewhat superficial, with limited exploration of Elias’s values, independence, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Elias as the expert on his body and routine by inviting him to choose what feels doable. The interaction remains slightly clinician-led because of frequent structured suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence scaling, choice questions, and reinforcement of small wins help Elias express intent and value a two-minute fallback as a success. The change talk focuses mainly on behavior steps rather than broader quality-of-life reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers such as pain, dozing, weather, and fear of failure are acknowledged and reframed into manageable supports rather than being argued with. More evocative double-sided reflections would soften sustain talk even further.
