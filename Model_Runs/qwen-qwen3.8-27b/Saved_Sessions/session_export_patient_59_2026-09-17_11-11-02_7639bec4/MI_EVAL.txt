# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm throughout, with explicit statements like 'I'm not in the got you business' and 'I'm not surprised or judgmental.' The psychiatrist validates Jamal's perspective without pushing, reinforcing that staying out of trouble is meaningful and that he doesn't owe anyone a five-year plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with nuanced picks-up such as 'there's something in pretty much it that sounds like it's not really filling you up.' Some reflections are slightly interpretive (e.g., 'sounds like peace'), which risks mild projection, but overall demonstrates genuine effort to understand Jamal's inner experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear thread connecting Jamal's stated priority of 'staying out of trouble' to the target behavior of marijuana use. The opening is somewhat broad, but the psychiatrist effectively narrows focus onto the specific discrepancy between his values and his smoking behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the conversation as a joint exploration, inviting Jamal to shape the discussion ('Can you help me understand,' 'I want to hear how you actually think about that line'). The psychiatrist drives the direction appropriately given the resistance but maintains a genuine collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly reinforces client ownership throughout, never pressuring Jamal to quit or change. Statements like 'You don't owe anyone a five-year plan' and the use of the hypothetical 'friend' question as a reflection opportunity rather than a directive exemplify excellent autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The 'friend' question at the end is a well-deployed evocation technique for developing discrepancy, and the psychiatrist effectively reflects back Jamal's own language to help him examine his thinking. However, evocation is somewhat concentrated in the final exchanges rather than distributed throughout the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions himself as curious rather than expert, using language like 'I'm genuinely curious' and 'I'm not gonna manufacture one.' Strong collaborative stance, though could be slightly more explicit about power-sharing in a few moments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of indirect techniques including the discrepancy question and reflecting back Jamal's own decision language ('That's not just a shrug — that's a decision'). The actual change talk elicited is limited due to Jamal's resistance, but the final exchange does produce a meaningful shift where Jamal himself suggests his friend should 'just stop for a bit.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Jamal's minimization and dismissals without arguing or pushing back, using excellent rolling-with-resistance language ('Fair enough — if you're not seeing a gap, I'm not gonna manufacture one'). The 'friend' question at the end provides a gentle, non-confrontational way to challenge sustain talk.
