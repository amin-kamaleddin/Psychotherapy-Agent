# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, using frequent affirmations and normalizing statements to support Samuel’s efforts. Acceptance is somewhat generic and task-focused, with limited deeper acknowledgment of his ambivalence and emotional discomfort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable cognitive reflections connecting smoking to work stress, social cues, and deadlines. However, there is less emotional attunement to feelings such as exclusion, anger, awkwardness, and fear of looking silly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on smoking reduction, work triggers, and a concrete next step. Direction is somewhat tactical and checklist-like, with less strategic movement toward deeper motivation or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Samuel’s suggestions, especially the walk-first strategy and gum backup, and adapts to his preference to avoid scripts. Collaboration is partial because the psychiatrist often supplies structured advice rather than fully co-creating solutions as equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges choice and accepts Samuel’s preference for a simple, low-script plan. However, many prescriptive recommendations reduce the sense that Samuel is the sole author of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses questions to elicit triggers, confidence, and doability, but the approach remains more instructional than evocative. There is limited effort to draw out Samuel’s own motivations, benefits, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses Samuel’s contributions to shape the plan, such as choosing the walk and gum as a backup. However, the interaction is uneven due to frequent expert-driven, plan-delivering language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through questions about triggers, confidence, and doable options. The psychiatrist misses key opportunities to deepen Samuel’s own motivation, values, and confidence in his ability to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and normalizes doubts about NRT and breathing techniques. Softening is more practical redirection than nuanced MI reflection, with limited use of double-sided reflections.
