# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and autonomy-supportive statements that reduce shame. The warmth remains somewhat clinical, with fewer moments of individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Farid's ambivalence. Some deeper emotional content, such as fear of failure and feeling on a clock, is not fully explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains clear focus on increasing physical activity and narrows it to a manageable behavior. The conversation sometimes repeats similar questions without clearly consolidating progress toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative, with the counselor following Farid's ideas and inviting his perspective throughout. There is limited true co-construction of a shared plan, and the counselor still leads through similar questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, as the psychiatrist avoids pressure and repeatedly emphasizes Farid's choice. The language could be less formulaic and more explicitly affirm any decision, including not changing right now.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes meaningful insight about permission and rest, leading to important change language. Much of the evocation remains focused on reducing burden rather than exploring values, importance, confidence, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly frames Farid as the expert on his own life and avoids an advice-giving stance. The partnership feels somewhat procedural, with less explicit invitation for Farid to steer the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and uses one especially effective question about permission and rest. The counselor does not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen Farid's own reasons, benefits, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk reasonably by acknowledging both sides rather than arguing. The session remains anchored in reasons for not changing, and there is less active use of double-sided reflections or pivots toward change.
