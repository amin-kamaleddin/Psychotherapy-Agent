# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, often normalizing David’s concerns. However, the tone is more corrective and solution-focused than deeply affirming, especially when quickly shifting to medical information.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects David’s concern about weakness and not rushing, but the reflections remain surface-level. There is limited deeper exploration of the personal meaning, fear, values, or ambivalence behind David’s resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cholesterol, statin concerns, side-effect monitoring, and next steps with the prescribing doctor. The main limitation is that the direction is more medically problem-solving than motivationally evocative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers to help with scripts and questions, but the interaction remains largely clinician-directed. David’s preferences for simplicity, not rehearsing, and lifestyle-focused plans are not fully integrated into the session’s direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive phrases, but the psychiatrist repeatedly provides detailed medical plans, scripts, and specific next steps. This can subtly pressure David toward a medication-focused path rather than emphasizing his full ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with an open question but then mostly provides information, reassurance, and specific scripts. There is limited use of core evocation techniques to draw out David’s own reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and offers choices, but the overall stance remains expert-driven. David’s lead and preferences do not substantially shape the direction of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offers several change statements, such as trying a low dose, cutting back on bacon, and walking more. The psychiatrist acknowledges these but does not actively expand or evoke David’s own deeper motivations for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with David’s sustain talk, but it is not effectively softened or redirected. The response continues to emphasize a medication-focused plan, which may inadvertently reinforce the idea that taking a statin is the expected next step.
