# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, with frequent affirmations of the client’s plan. However, acceptance is somewhat plan-focused and does not fully validate the emotional weight behind the client’s concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathy is limited because responses mostly address factual or behavioral content rather than underlying feelings. There are few emotional reflections or complex reflections of the client’s frustration, embarrassment, or pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Direction is strong, with consistent focus on concrete next steps such as visiting the YMCA, calling, using an exit line, and taking a walk. The session remains anchored to action and does not wander.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some input and accepts the client’s preferences, but most of the plan, scripts, and checklists are supplied by the psychiatrist. Collaboration is present but uneven, with the client not fully co-creating the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is moderate autonomy support through questions about feasibility and respect for the client’s boundaries. However, the amount of directive instruction and lack of a strong statement that the client is fully in charge limits the score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the psychiatrist quickly shifts into advice-giving rather than drawing out the client’s motivations, values, or confidence. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk and self-motivation are frequent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely expert-led, with the psychiatrist providing plans, scripts, and specific steps. Although the client influences some decisions, the psychiatrist does not consistently honor her as the primary expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client generates change talk independently, but the psychiatrist does not systematically elicit, reflect, or amplify it. The focus on logistics and advice-giving reduces opportunities to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with the client’s concerns and redirects barriers into practical alternatives. However, the responses are more problem-solving-oriented than MI-specific softening of ambivalence.
