# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, normalizes awkwardness, and avoids blaming the client. However, some directive language and task-focused warmth limit deeper acceptance of the client's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately summarizes content and barriers related to the plan. Reflections remain more event- and plan-focused than emotionally exploratory.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is strongly focused on reducing reoffending risk, with clear attention to boredom, Friday nights, and the car job. The target behavior and concrete next step are consistently maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates some client preferences, such as using Donna and a short text. However, the psychiatrist often supplies wording, timing, and coping steps, making the collaboration feel uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and checks whether the plan works for the client. Direct instructions and expectations, such as “Go ask Donna tonight,” reduce the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some client-generated motivation, confidence, and a concrete intention to act. This is partially undermined by frequent advice-giving and practical problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and allows the client to influence some decisions. The session often shifts into an expert-driven coaching role, limiting the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk about the car job and asking for help. The psychiatrist affirms this but does not consistently deepen the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges boredom, Friday night pulls, and discomfort with asking for help without arguing. However, sustain talk is often met with counter-plans rather than deeper reflection or redirection.
