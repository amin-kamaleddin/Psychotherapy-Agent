# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, accepting stance throughout, avoiding labels and moralizing. The response to the client’s challenge effectively counters shame and pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate complex reflections that track the client’s ambivalence and practical concerns. Empathy could be strengthened by reflecting more of the underlying emotions beneath the client’s minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the relevant behavior and legal consequences without drifting into unrelated topics. The direction remains somewhat narrow because it stays tied primarily to avoiding legal trouble rather than broader behavior-change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves the client in shaping the plan and uses the client’s own language. Collaboration could be more explicit with additional “we” statements or joint agenda-setting language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through statements that emphasize client choice and the absence of pressure. The therapist avoids advice, demands, or a punitive frame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, non-persuasive questions to invite the client to generate his own plan and concerns. Evocation remains somewhat surface-level because it does not use scaling or deeper values exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and signals that the client is the expert on his own life. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicit invitations to co-create the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, but most of it stays tied to avoiding legal consequences. The client’s change-relevant statements are not consistently amplified or connected to values, confidence, or intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s minimization and uses reflection and gentle discrepancy questions. The session remains anchored in legal-avoidance sustain talk more than would be ideal.
