# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and uses respectful, supportive language, but the stance often becomes task-focused. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming Aisha’s worth and courage more explicitly before moving into planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of distress, but they often remain surface-level and quickly shift into problem-solving. The therapist could explore deeper concerns such as body-image shame, fear of failure, and the “heavy feeling in her chest.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target behavior: starting to walk. The main limitation is that direction is sometimes maintained through repeated suggestions of plans and routines, which can make the therapist feel like they are driving the agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to Aisha’s pushback by removing strength training, tracking, and written schedules, and invites her to choose some plan details. However, the session begins with a fairly prescriptive plan and continues with several therapist-generated suggestions, so collaboration feels uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through flexibility, low demand, and acceptance of Aisha’s preference for a simple approach. It is weakened by repeated directive language and specific behavioral recommendations that may reduce Aisha’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few practical questions but does not consistently draw out Aisha’s internal motivation, values, or reasons for change. More evocation would focus on her own goals, confidence, and what would make walking meaningful to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Aisha’s input and adjusts the plan based on her feedback, which supports partnership. However, the therapist often takes an expert role by proposing specific actions, timing, and routines rather than letting Aisha lead the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha expresses clear change talk, and the psychiatrist occasionally acknowledges her progress and ability. The therapist could better deepen this change talk by asking questions that expand her reasons, values, confidence, and desired outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk reasonably by normalizing Aisha’s doubts and lowering demands rather than arguing. It could be stronger by using more double-sided reflections to connect her concerns with her desire to feel less stuck.
