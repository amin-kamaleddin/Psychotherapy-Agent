# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, with clear affirmations of the client's experience. Acceptance is slightly limited by occasional framing of the client's worth or approval around responsibility and behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates strong reflective listening, accurately capturing the client's double bind and sense of limbo. Empathy could be deeper by more fully exploring underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or powerlessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on the target concern of appointment delays and their impact on the client's day and emotional state. Direction could be stronger by collaboratively clarifying a specific focus before moving into practical options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses a collaborative stance, offers choices, and invites the client's perspective. Collaboration is somewhat limited by therapist-led problem-solving that occasionally outpaces the client's stated needs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites the client to define what would work and avoids commands or persuasion. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by language that can feel like narrowing choices or shifting the burden onto the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses evocative questions to draw out some client ideas and change talk. However, the counselor often moves to problem-solving before fully developing or reinforcing the client's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client to define fairness, workability, and possible next steps, showing a collaborative partnership. The partnership could be more optimal by negotiating the agenda and keeping more expertise with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, including statements about planning, leaving, and managing the situation. The counselor does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or deepen this change talk with follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids arguing with the client's sense of being stuck. Softening is limited by occasional pressure toward practical solutions that may increase resistance rather than gently turning sustain talk toward change.
