# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, validating Linda’s ambivalence and past quit attempts. Acceptance is occasionally generic or reassurance-focused rather than deeply individualized when Linda expresses fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, especially around health, cost, and social comfort. Empathy is somewhat task-oriented later, with missed opportunities to fully reflect fear of failure and skepticism about coping options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays organized and focused on tobacco cessation, health risks, cost, social situations, and next steps. It becomes somewhat over-planned, with a strong emphasis on practical logistics rather than MI-specific motivation and ambivalence resolution.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Linda’s input and allows her choices to shape the session, such as the checklist, showing it to her doctor, and preferring a call. Collaboration is strong procedurally, though much of the content is therapist-generated rather than co-explored from Linda’s own values and ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Linda’s pacing and avoids direct pressure, affirming that she does not need to commit immediately. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated focus on “this week,” scripts, and forward-moving planning that can subtly encourage action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions elicit useful client language about importance, confidence, fear, past attempts, and desire for a plan. Evocation becomes superficial as the session shifts to options, scripts, and practical planning without continuing to draw out Linda’s own motivations and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and invites Linda to choose among options, which supports partnership. However, the frequent offering of clinician-generated menus, scripts, and tactics shifts the interaction toward advice and planning rather than treating Linda as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk through early questions about success, importance, confidence, and realistic first steps. There are few responses that deepen, amplify, or reflect back Linda’s motivation, so her own change language does not develop strongly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Linda’s reasons for continuing smoking, such as social belonging, routine, cost, and fear of awkwardness. Strategic MI responses, such as double-sided reflections that connect those reasons to her health and self-respect goals, are used only limitedly.
