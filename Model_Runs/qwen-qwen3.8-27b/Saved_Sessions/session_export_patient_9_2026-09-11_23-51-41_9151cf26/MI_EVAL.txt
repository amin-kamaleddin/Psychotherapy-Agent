# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and offers brief affirmations. However, the acceptance feels shallow because it is often quickly followed by directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of stress and worry, but deeper emotional pain and ambivalence are under-explored. The response often pivots to practical content rather than continuing empathic reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on co-parenting communication and protecting the son. However, the direction is more problem-solving and agenda-driven than client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely counselor-driven, with the psychiatrist offering structured options and scripts. There is limited evidence of true co-creation or equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices and acknowledges Amina's role in selecting an approach. Yet the prescriptive advice and scripts undercut autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly practical and informational rather than designed to elicit Amina's own motivations, values, or change talk. There is little active effort to draw out her reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions themselves as the expert providing solutions, rather than a partner in the process. This reduces the sense of mutual exploration and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina naturally expresses strong values-based change talk, but the psychiatrist does not systematically reflect, amplify, or build on it. The focus remains on providing content rather than evoking intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina's ambivalence and sustain talk are acknowledged but not effectively addressed using MI-consistent techniques. The psychiatrist often moves directly to solutions without exploring the underlying conflict.
