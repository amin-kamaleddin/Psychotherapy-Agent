# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance and avoids blaming or minimizing Maria's pain. Acceptance is solid but can feel procedural, and repeated safety checks create a monitoring impression.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of Maria's intense pain and need for presence. Reflections are sometimes repetitive and miss key themes such as guilt, exhaustion, fear of being seen as broken, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on immediate suicidal risk and safety. However, the direction is narrow and repetitive, with limited movement toward broader safety or change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language, asks for preferences, and negotiates check-ins. The broader agenda remains structured around the psychiatrist's safety plan rather than being fully co-created with Maria.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently asks permission and offers choices, supporting Maria's sense of control. Frequent safety questions and reassessments can feel intrusive and partially undermine her autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some early evocation of Maria's preferences and protective factors. The session relies heavily on safety checks and therapist-provided hope rather than eliciting Maria's own reasons for safety.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to Maria's requests for silence, no counting, and no discussion of her brother. The crisis protocol still makes the interaction feel somewhat clinically directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some change-relevant content but does not consistently reflect, amplify, or develop Maria's own reasons for living. Therapist-provided hope statements are less effective than evoking Maria's own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Maria's sustain talk and validates her distress. Fewer MI-specific strategies, such as double-sided reflections or gentle reframes, are used to help her hold pain and the part of her still here.
