# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and accepts the refusal to discuss the house. However, acceptance is inconsistent because the psychiatrist keeps talking and offering options after the client asks for quiet.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some effort to acknowledge resignation, but reflections remain surface-level. The psychiatrist misses deeper irritation, boundary-setting, and frustration at being managed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on immediate safety and avoids the house topic. Direction is limited because the psychiatrist repeatedly returns to the same menus without collaborative narrowing or a small co-created plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and offers choices. Collaboration is uneven because the counselor keeps directing the next step rather than letting the client's quiet fully shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy-supportive language is used, such as no pressure and being available. Support is undermined when the psychiatrist continues to talk and offer options after the client explicitly requests quiet.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening question is the main evocative moment. After that, the psychiatrist relies on closed choice menus rather than eliciting the client's own concerns, values, or motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids overt lecturing. Partnership is superficial because the client's repeated request for quiet does not actually determine how the session proceeds.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal change-focused language, including a request to focus on one small thing. This is not developed, reflected, or strategically used to evoke stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client's reluctance, but does not actively soften sustain talk. There is little use of double-sided reflections or gentle exploration of ambivalence.
