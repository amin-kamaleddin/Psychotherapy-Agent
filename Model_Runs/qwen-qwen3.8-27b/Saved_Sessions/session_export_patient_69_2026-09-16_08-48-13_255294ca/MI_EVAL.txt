# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful and validating, but the acceptance feels more transactional than deeply affirming. It does not consistently convey the warmth needed for a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The reflections are accurate but remain largely behavioral and logistical. The psychiatrist does not meaningfully attend to the emotional depth of the client’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session briefly connects to the presenting concern but quickly becomes administrative. There is no sustained momentum toward the target behavior or a clear MI-focused direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative in structure, with the client’s preferences accepted and incorporated. The collaboration remains narrow because it focuses on task completion rather than therapeutic exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers clear choices, avoids pressure, and respects the client’s request for silence. However, autonomy support is applied mainly to session logistics rather than the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few open questions are asked at the start, but they are not followed through. The client’s motivations, values, and possible exceptions are left largely unevoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids an overly expert-driven stance. However, the partnership stays mostly logistical and does not fully position the client as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are initial MI-style questions, but they are dropped once the session shifts to paperwork. There is little effort to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges sustain-talk statements but does not actively reframe or soften them. The status-quo agenda is accommodated rather than redirected toward possibility.
