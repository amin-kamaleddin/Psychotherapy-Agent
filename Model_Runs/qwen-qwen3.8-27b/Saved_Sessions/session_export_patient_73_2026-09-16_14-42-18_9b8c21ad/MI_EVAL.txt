# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes Linda’s ambivalence about health and social belonging. Acceptance is occasionally generic or slightly interpretive, keeping it from a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the social meaning of smoking and Linda’s core ambivalence. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level, missing deeper feelings like shame, fear, and identity threat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation but drifts into repetitive logistical problem-solving. It does not consistently advance the MI process through ambivalence, motivation, readiness, or confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint language and builds on Linda’s own ideas rather than imposing a standard plan. Repetition of similar question structures makes the interaction feel somewhat therapist-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda is offered choices and invited to generate her own solutions without pressure to quit. Minor therapist suggestions, such as food, parking, or helping, slightly reduce the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions invite Linda to identify her own priorities and acceptable social strategies. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist does not consistently draw out values, confidence, or stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance by inviting Linda to set the agenda and emphasizing her expertise about her group and routine. The repeated focus on low-key behavioral scripts makes the direction feel somewhat therapist-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session rarely evokes, amplifies, or reinforces Linda’s own reasons for change. Forward-looking questions mostly lead to practical planning rather than strengthening change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Linda’s reasons to maintain smoking. However, the session does not consistently use double-sided reflections or pivots to reduce the weight of sustain talk.
