# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, using affirmations and normalizing statements. The warmth is somewhat procedural, and the final disclosure about feeling behind and failing is not explicitly accepted or affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers nuanced reflections of Rafael’s ambivalence, fatigue, and fear, validating both the desire to exercise and the difficulty of doing so. However, the final statement about his wife and feeling behind is not reflected, missing a chance to deepen understanding of shame and self-worth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on returning to exercise and progressively narrows the goal into realistic steps. It does not end with a consolidated summary, agreed next step, or clear commitment, leaving the direction incomplete.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the session around Rafael’s own ideas, such as shorter sessions, the parking lot, and a five-minute timer. The collaboration is strong but remains implicit, with limited explicit agenda-setting, power-sharing, or co-created summary.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Suggestions are framed as exploratory and choice-based, reducing pressure and supporting Rafael’s freedom to choose. The psychiatrist could more explicitly reinforce Rafael’s ownership and self-determination, especially during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses evocative questions to help Rafael generate his own solutions and examine his inner barriers. The evocation is strong but does not always deepen into values, identity, or future self after key statements like fear of being out of shape.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rafael’s perspective and avoids a didactic or prescriptive approach, treating him as the source of solutions. The partnership is clear but somewhat implicit, without explicit statements that Rafael is the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and affirms concrete change talk, including a 30-minute goal, walking plus weights, the parking lot, and a five-minute timer. It does not consistently amplify the strongest change talk or explicitly elicit confidence, readiness, or values-based reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately acknowledges fatigue, avoidance, fear, and the “next week” pattern without arguing or dismissing them. However, the session gives considerable space to sustain talk without consistently redirecting Rafael’s language toward change, agency, or values.
