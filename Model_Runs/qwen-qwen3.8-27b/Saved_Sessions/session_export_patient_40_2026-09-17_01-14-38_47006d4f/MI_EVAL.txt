# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming without blame or moralizing. Some slightly leading language about commitment and choice prevents a perfect 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially in holding ambivalence around fear and the desire to be present. Deeper exploration of shame, relational fear, and pain would strengthen the empathy further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on recovery from alcohol and pill use while staying connected to the client’s family values. The conversation moves toward concrete behaviors, though some later discussion becomes more relational and abstract.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses the client’s own language and concerns to guide the session, showing a strong collaborative stance. More co-created next steps and less therapist structuring would make the collaboration feel more equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives and pressure, inviting the client to consider his own choices and coping steps. Explicitly reinforcing the client’s right to choose whether or not to change would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out internal motivation related to fatherhood, trust, and avoiding the “ruined” version of himself. More explicit evocation of confidence, ability, and commitment would deepen this strength.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative stance and allows the client’s ideas to shape the session. Some interpretive framing makes the interaction slightly therapist-led rather than fully co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk around presence, quiet, and being there for his children. More targeted follow-ups could strengthen DARN-C language such as confidence, ability, and reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized without challenge, especially around fear of relapse and perfectionism. More advanced double-sided reflections would further soften sustain talk while maintaining MI spirit.
