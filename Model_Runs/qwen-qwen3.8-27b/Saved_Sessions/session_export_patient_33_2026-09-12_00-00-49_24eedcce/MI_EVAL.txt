# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s honesty and harm-reduction goals. The warmth is somewhat procedural and occasionally shifts into an instructional mode, which limits emotional attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate perspective-taking around stress, escape, and awkwardness, but several deeper emotional concerns are missed. Empathy is competent but not consistently emotionally precise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on overdose safety, naloxone access, test strips, sterile supplies, and communicating with the housemate. Some educational and logistical drift occurs, but the focus remains relevant to harm reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes to practical decisions, such as choosing a casual message and requesting specific resources. However, the counselor remains the primary driver of the agenda and information.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s preference against full rehab and avoids pressure to quit. Frequent directive safety instructions reduce the sense that the client is fully in charge of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial permission-based questions elicit some client concerns and safety priorities. More client-centered questions would better draw out values, confidence, and self-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to choose scripts, resources, and next steps, but the counselor often assumes the expert role. The interaction is collaborative in parts but remains largely counselor-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses meaningful change talk about staying alive and stopping dirty gear. The counselor affirms this but does not consistently evoke, amplify, or elaborate on the client’s motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pushing abstinence or full treatment and keeps the focus on realistic harm reduction. More active softening of ambivalence would strengthen this area.
