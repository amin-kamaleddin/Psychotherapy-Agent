# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with strong acceptance statements. Some opening and affirming comments feel slightly abstract or agenda-focused before fully inhabiting the acute distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening, especially later in the session, capturing emotional complexity. Early questioning feels solution-oriented before fully validating the intensity of her suffering.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays within the crisis but drifts between broader relief themes and concrete safety actions. A clearer, collaboratively selected focus on a specific safety goal would strengthen momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Maria’s perspective and positions herself as being with her. At times, counselor-led options and sequential questions reduce the sense of fully client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice and avoids overt pressure, respecting Maria’s pacing. Could more explicitly reinforce that she has full freedom to accept or reject any suggested step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses open questions to draw out Maria’s perspective. Evocation remains more action-focused than motivation-focused, with less emphasis on values, hope, and reasons to stay safe.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a collaborative stance and acknowledges Maria’s expertise. Repeated questioning and early introduction of multiple options can feel interview-like and slightly directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Notices and affirms Maria’s caring and reasons to stay connected. Misses opportunities to deepen and expand these statements into stronger safety motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoids arguing but rarely softens absolute negative statements with double-sided reflections or pivot questions. Sustain talk remains largely undiminished and not consistently turned toward change.
