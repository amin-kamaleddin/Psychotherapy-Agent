# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong nonjudgmental warmth and respect are evident throughout. Some moments shift quickly toward reassurance or hope rather than fully sitting in the depth of Liora’s despair.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers solid reflections of ambivalence, weariness, and relational boundaries. At key moments, responses remain minimal and miss deeper feelings of futility, shame, and exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing self-harm and developing immediate coping options. Direction could be sharper by consolidating a more concrete, behavior-specific safety and risk plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds collaboratively from Liora’s own ideas, such as ice, privacy, and low-pressure check-ins. The interaction remains somewhat therapist-led, with less explicit co-creation of the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly honors Liora’s autonomy, privacy, and need not to be monitored or instructed. Some safety-focused questions subtly position the clinician as a necessary source of safety.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate evocative questions, but they mainly elicit coping strategies and logistics. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Liora’s own values, desires, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently treats Liora as the expert and builds on her contributions. However, the session remains somewhat controlled through consecutive questions and a structured safety agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-relevant language appears, such as wanting to be “less wrecked” and trying ice. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this with stronger DARN/CRAFR-based evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument, moralizing, and false optimism. The strongest final sustain talk, “I still need the knife,” is not fully addressed with a more skilled double-sided reflection or pivot.
