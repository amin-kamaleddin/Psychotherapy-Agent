# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, accepting David's goals and boundaries. However, repeated clinical reminders create a practical, informational tone rather than deeply warm affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately recognizes David's practical preferences and concerns, but reflections are mostly factual. He misses opportunities to explore the emotional meaning of smoking as stress relief, identity, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on smoking reduction and daytime nicotine gum use. The main weakness is that the focus becomes procedural, with more emphasis on instructions than motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts to David's preferences, such as gum instead of patches and a small pack. However, frequent lists, recommendations, and reminders keep the interaction more counselor-led than mutually co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects David's decision not to quit, accepts continued nighttime smoking, and avoids pressure. Repeated safety reminders are appropriate but slightly reduce the sense that David is the primary decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tends to provide options, advice, and practical details rather than drawing out David's own motivations. David offers useful material about stress and daytime cravings, but it is not consistently reflected or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaborative language and some use of David's input, but the psychiatrist still controls much of the agenda. The session feels uneven, with David mostly reacting or giving brief agreements.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offers meaningful change-oriented statements, but the psychiatrist mainly labels them as practical or smart and shifts back to advice. There is little reflective amplification or exploration of David's own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with David's statements about liking smoking and keeps a nonconfrontational stance. He does not, however, use double-sided reflections to honor the appeal of smoking while opening space for change.
