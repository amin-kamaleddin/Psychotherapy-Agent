# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honors the client’s low-pressure framing. However, the warmth is sometimes task-focused and misses opportunities to explicitly affirm ambivalence or fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate at a surface level, especially regarding frustration and wanting less next-day misery. Deeper empathy is limited, with less exploration of shame, identity, values, or double-sided ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on alcohol use and related consequences. The direction is clear and behaviorally specific, though it can feel more prescriptive than client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates client-generated ideas such as the limit of six, Saturday choice, phrase, and sticky note. However, the clinician remains the primary source of suggestions and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through noncommittal language and acceptance of the client’s preferences. The repeated detailed plans and scripts can still make the interaction feel directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open-ended questions elicit meaningful client decisions and motivation. Evocation is limited by a quick shift into advice and insufficient exploration of values, confidence, or deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and acknowledges client expertise in several places. Nonetheless, the session is often clinician-driven, which reduces the sense of true co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces useful change talk, including a limit, practical coping ideas, and a self-reminder phrase. Change talk is not strongly amplified or consistently developed into deeper motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and offers low-barrier alternatives that redirect toward small change. Sustain talk is often bypassed rather than used therapeutically to explore ambivalence.
