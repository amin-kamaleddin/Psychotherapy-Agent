# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Ryan’s conflicting goals without shaming him for the incident. Acceptance is strong but somewhat structured, with less explicit normalization of college social pressure or deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of Ryan’s concerns about identity, social belonging, and balancing safety with enjoyment. Empathy is solid but occasionally repetitive and does not fully explore deeper feelings such as shame, fear, or a sense of being punished.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on social drinking, safety, the conduct incident, and practical pacing strategies. Direction is generally coherent, though it becomes narrow around specific cues and lacks clearer consolidation into a defined target behavior or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Ryan’s ideas rather than imposing solutions, creating a collaborative problem-solving tone. Collaboration is strong but could be deepened by more explicitly inviting Ryan to set the agenda and emphasize shared equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure, moralizing, and rigid rules, while inviting Ryan to choose what feels realistic. Autonomy support is solid but could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Ryan’s right to decide and own his choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to elicit practical ideas and avoids direct advice. However, evocation remains more behavior-planning focused and does not deeply explore Ryan’s values, confidence, readiness, or intrinsic motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and repeatedly invites Ryan’s input, allowing his ideas to shape the plan. Partnership is clear but would be stronger with more explicit emphasis on Ryan as the expert on his own life, social world, and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk around pacing, limits, and practical strategies. However, the session moves too quickly into behavioral problem-solving and does not consistently amplify Ryan’s values, confidence, desire, or readiness to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Ryan’s reasons for drinking and acknowledges the social benefits of alcohol. Nevertheless, some prompts remain centered on maintaining the party experience rather than gently strengthening discrepancy with Ryan’s educational and safety values.
