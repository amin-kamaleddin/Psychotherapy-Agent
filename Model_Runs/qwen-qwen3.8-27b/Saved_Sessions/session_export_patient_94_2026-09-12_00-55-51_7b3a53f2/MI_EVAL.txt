# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers several affirmations. Acceptance is weakened by continued scripts and safety reminders after the client clearly declines them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflections, especially later, but empathy is inconsistent and delayed. Early in the session, the psychiatrist shifts to psychoeducation before fully reflecting the client’s fear and frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on inhaler use and asthma safety. Direction is weakened by repeated offers of scripts, safety plans, and warning signs without a clear client-owned next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and offers choices, but quickly becomes the main source of information. The interaction feels more expert-driven than a mutual, client-centered collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s decision and allows the session to end when the client asks to leave. Support for autonomy is inconsistent because the counselor repeatedly offers rejected scripts and safety reminders.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with open questions but quickly shifts to providing medical information and scripts. There is limited exploration of the client’s deeper values, fears, or motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are brief moments of respect for the client’s expertise, but the overall interaction is more directive than collaborative. The client’s preferences do not substantially reshape the session or problem-solving process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes very little effort to elicit desire, ability, reasons, need, or confidence for change. The session mainly relies on information and may inadvertently reinforce the client’s sustain talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and validates the client’s concerns, but does not effectively soften sustain talk or explore the costs of maintaining the status quo. Responses rely more on medical content and warnings than on double-sided reflection.
