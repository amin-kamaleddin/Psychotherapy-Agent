# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a nonjudgmental, supportive stance and affirms the patient without labeling him. It is strong, though somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or explicitly affirming his identity and struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the patient’s mixed feelings and practical concerns well, including ambivalence about staying clean and pressure from relationships. It does not fully capture deeper emotions such as shame, stigma, and identity conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target behavior, moving from ambivalence to concrete steps for relapse prevention and daily safety. Each turn is clinically relevant and efficiently guided toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by helping organize the patient’s own ideas into a workable plan. It falls slightly short of full equality because the therapist often leads with structured suggestions and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering options and allowing the patient to make decisions about timing, wording, and supports. Some suggestions are mildly steering, so the patient’s ultimate decision-making could be reinforced more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical intentions and confidence statements but does not consistently draw out deeper personal values, identity, or meaning behind change. This makes the evocation useful but not fully deep at an identity level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys that the patient is the expert on his own life and invites collaboration in planning. It remains somewhat therapist-led in offering specific times, drafts, and prompts, which reduces the fully co-constructed feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully creates opportunities for the patient to express intention, ability, and planning, leading to increased confidence and concrete actions. It is not a 5 because it focuses more on practical steps than deeper values, identity, or future-self change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and redirects it toward manageable steps. It is not a 5 because attention to risks and triggers can sustain vulnerability, and more active reframing or double-sided reflections are limited.
