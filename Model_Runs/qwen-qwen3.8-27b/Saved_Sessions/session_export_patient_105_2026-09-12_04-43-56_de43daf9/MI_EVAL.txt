# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment while normalizing Naomi’s anxiety and doubts. It is not a 5 because the tone occasionally becomes more task-oriented and advisory, with fewer moments affirming Naomi’s inherent worth beyond her effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Naomi’s emotional and physiological experience, including fear, shame, and ambivalence. However, the empathy does not consistently deepen into the broader meaning of her anxiety, such as her identity, shame, or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on social anxiety and its impact on freelance photography, moving toward a concrete, actionable plan. The direction could be stronger by linking the small step more explicitly to Naomi’s own personal goals and values before offering options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively, with Naomi choosing the grounding step, car setting, phrase, phone reminder, and backup cue. Full collaboration is limited by the early introduction of specific interventions before exploring her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, normalizing low confidence, and avoiding pressure to feel more confident. However, the frequent supply of specific techniques, scripts, and experiments can subtly steer the client rather than fully evoke her own sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that draw out some change talk and motivation, but also provides a fair amount of advice and rationale. Evocation would be stronger with more focus on eliciting Naomi’s own values, past strengths, and reasons for change before offering solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fosters partnership by inviting input and allowing Naomi to shape the plan through her choices. It is not a 5 because the initial approach is somewhat directive and expert-led before fully exploring her preferred solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is meaningful evocation of change talk, including confidence ratings and statements about wanting to handle shoots and conversations more effectively. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify the change talk with additional DARN-CAT or value-based follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively acknowledges and softens Naomi’s doubts by normalizing uncertainty and reframing success as trying rather than symptom elimination. A 5 would require more advanced double-sided reflections to strategically transform sustain talk into stronger change talk.
