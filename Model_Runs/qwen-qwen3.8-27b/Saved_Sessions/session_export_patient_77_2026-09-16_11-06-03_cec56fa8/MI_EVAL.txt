# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental and validating language, but repeated expressions of medical worry can feel parental or alarmist and limit unconditional acceptance. There is also limited explicit positive regard for Liyana’s worth outside of weight, effort, or performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and captures key emotions such as frustration, fear, loneliness, and embarrassment. However, reflections often shift quickly into questions or advice, which limits deeper exploration of shame and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the presenting problem of weight loss, exercise, eating, and related health concerns. The medicalized shift is clinically reasonable but narrows the MI target and can make the conversation feel more like risk management than motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers structured choices and honors Liyana’s preferences, including privacy and not involving her parents. However, the counselor still supplies much of the content, so the interaction is collaborative but not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, permission, and privacy throughout the session. Directive safety language is appropriate but slightly reduces the sense that Liyana is fully in charge, even of safety-related decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful open questions begin to draw out motivation, but the counselor supplies many external reasons for change based on medical risks. Liyana’s internal values and self-defined reasons for change are not consistently evoked or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking, choices, and privacy support partnership, but the session becomes expert-driven with medical explanations and specific recommendations. More co-creation of meaning and next steps would strengthen the collaborative relationship.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions invite change talk, especially around wanting to know whether she is in danger or capable of fixing things. The counselor does not consistently reflect, amplify, or develop DARN-C language, and some questions focus more on accepting medical advice than evoking internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not consistently softened or redirected toward change. More double-sided reflections and gentle pivots are needed to reduce resistance and address reasons against change.
