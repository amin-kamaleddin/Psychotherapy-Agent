# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, without moralizing or labeling. Positive regard was professional but could become more explicit and warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate, especially around ambivalence, control, and the difficulty of change. It stayed mostly at the behavioral level and could explore deeper emotions such as shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained clearly focused on the target behavior of getting to school on time. The discussion stayed organized and moved logically from goals to a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked as an equal partner and co-created the plan with Jamal. The client’s ideas shaped the intervention, including the alarm, late pass, and self-talk.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jamal was consistently supported as the owner of his choices and pace. There was no pressure, advice-giving, or attempt to force a stronger commitment than he wanted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited motivation from Jamal rather than supplying reasons for change. It was practical but could have evoked deeper intrinsic motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Jamal’s input and followed his concerns and plan. The stance was collaborative, though the partnership was not made fully explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited desire, ability, and commitment, with some strong affirmations. It could have amplified change talk more strategically and explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by reframing avoidance of conflict as dignity, fairness, and self-management. It could have redirected more deliberately toward Jamal’s own values and school-related reasons.
