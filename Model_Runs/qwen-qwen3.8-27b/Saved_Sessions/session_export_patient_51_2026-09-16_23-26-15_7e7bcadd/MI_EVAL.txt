# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, affirming the patient for showing up and honoring her limits. Acceptance is slightly reduced because the language is somewhat formulaic and repetitive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is adequate but inconsistent, with early questions missing the patient’s immediate urgency and need for a direct medication answer. Later reflections become more accurate and attuned to her time constraint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist refocuses effectively on the citalopram/phenelzine interaction and provides a clear, safety-relevant next step. Direction is weakened by the opening drift toward general stress, work, and home before the urgent medication concern is addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction becomes collaborative after the patient asks for a brief answer, with shared control over format and content. Early therapist-directed questions limit the collaborative feel from the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, as the psychiatrist respects the patient’s time, asks how she wants information shared, and accepts her limited commitment without pressure. It could be strengthened by more explicitly naming her right to decide and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions are used, and the psychiatrist elicits a concrete next step by asking the fastest way for the patient to check with her doctor or pharmacist. Evocation remains limited because it is mostly action-focused rather than deeply exploring the patient’s own motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the patient as the expert in her situation and invites her input on how to share information. Partnership is not fully explicit throughout the session, especially in the early more therapist-directed exchanges.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the patient’s plan to call her doctor and frames it as a clear next step. Change talk is not consistently deepened, amplified, or expanded into stronger motivation, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by normalizing the patient’s time pressure, limited capacity, and practical choices without arguing or reinforcing the barriers. The response is not a 5 because it does not strongly pivot the sustain talk into additional change motivation.
