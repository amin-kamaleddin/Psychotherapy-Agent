# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates the client’s concerns and choices. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client’s effort, personhood, or difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate reflections of the client’s concerns, preferences, and obstacles. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not deeply explore the emotional meaning of statements such as not wanting to feel like a patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains related to cholesterol management but drifts into snacks, brushing teeth, tea, and TV habits. A clearer guiding focus on the client’s ambivalence about cholesterol medication would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions, offers options, and co-constructs a concrete plan with the client. Collaboration is limited by repeated introduction of medical and tracking options that the client has declined.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options, avoiding pressure, and respecting the client’s refusals. However, repeated clinical advice and structured medical suggestions can subtly push the client toward a framework he is resisting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and implementation questions elicit a practical plan and some client-generated problem-solving. Evocation does not fully draw out deeper values, fears, or personal reasons for change, especially around being healthy rather than a patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally collaborative stance and allows the client’s choices to shape the plan. The session remains somewhat clinician-driven because the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure and repeatedly introduces medical ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some practical change talk and affirms the client’s plan for snacks and brushing. The work is behavior-specific and does not consistently deepen desire, need, ability, or reason talk related to cholesterol management or medication adherence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s resistance to medication, tracking, and testing. However, the sustain talk is not actively softened through double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of the client’s ambivalence.
