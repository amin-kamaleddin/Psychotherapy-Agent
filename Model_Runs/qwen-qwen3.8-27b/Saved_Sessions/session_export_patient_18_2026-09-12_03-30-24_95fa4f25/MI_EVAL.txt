# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates basic respect and nonjudgment, but early pressure to change and continued plan suggestions can feel subtly pushing. Acceptance is present but not consistently warm or unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor makes several accurate reflections of the client’s uncertainty, desire to belong, and fear of being judged. Some reflections are generic, and deeper concerns about social rejection are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target: navigating alcohol-related social pressure and making a small next step. Focus is occasionally diluted by scripts, broad planning, and rapid question sequences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor collaborates by following the client’s café idea and Sarah plan. However, the counselor frequently supplies solutions, making the interaction less fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and supports the client’s preference to use her own words. Repeated readiness/confidence checks and continued suggestions after the client declines rehearsing can reduce autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits the client’s own motivation, café idea, choice of Sarah, and Friday plan. Evocation is less consistent when the counselor provides scripts and structured steps before the client’s own reasons are fully developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats the client as the expert on her social world, and her ideas clearly shape the session. Partnership is limited by the counselor’s introduction of his own strategies and less explicit emphasis on the client’s authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client to generate change talk, resulting in concrete ideas such as the café, texting one friend, and rising confidence. Change talk could be strengthened by amplifying the client’s values and readiness before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client repeatedly expresses fears of being judged, boring, or losing friends, but these are mainly acknowledged rather than skillfully softened. There is limited use of double-sided reflection or reframing to link sustain talk to values and change.
