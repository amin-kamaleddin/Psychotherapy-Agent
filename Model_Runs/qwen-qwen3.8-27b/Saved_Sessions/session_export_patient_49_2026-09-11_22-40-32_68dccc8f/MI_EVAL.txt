# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and normalizing tone throughout. However, affirmations remain somewhat generic and do not deeply individualize Hana’s strengths or story.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Hana’s perspective mainly at a cognitive level. Deeper emotional reflections about her social fear, ambivalence, and prior relapses are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation, especially the social/drinking context. The progression from triggers to a low-effort experiment is logical and purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but the psychiatrist remains the primary problem-solver and knowledge holder. Hana’s ideas do not substantially shape the direction or content of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames options as choices and respects Hana’s uncertainty about quitting. However, some highly specific behavioral instructions feel slightly directive despite the invitational framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions successfully elicit behavioral patterns and situational triggers. They do not consistently evoke deeper personal values, motivations, or readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana is invited to choose from options, which supports some partnership. The session, however, remains largely clinician-driven, with long structured suggestions limiting true co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to elicit or amplify change talk. Hana’s brief change-oriented statements are acknowledged but not strategically reflected back or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is accepted without judgment or pressure. The psychiatrist does not use MI-consistent strategies such as double-sided reflections or reframing to explore or soften ambivalence.
