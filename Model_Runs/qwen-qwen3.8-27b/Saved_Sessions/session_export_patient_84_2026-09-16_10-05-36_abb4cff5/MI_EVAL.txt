# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental with respectful, normalizing language and no labeling of cannabis use. Somewhat procedural and evaluative of plans rather than deeply affirming of the client as a person amid the family conflict.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective tracking of Malik's perspective on peer smoking, social belonging, and conflict with his mother. Remains largely cognitive without probing deeper emotional undertones such as resentment toward maternal control or fear of social rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on cannabis use, peer context, and family conflict with good momentum. Moved relatively quickly from a readiness rating of 2 to concrete action scripts and exit strategies before motivation was more fully developed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interactive and incorporated Malik's preferences (texting mom, low-drama plan) into the emerging approach. Frequently leading with structured option lists and practical tips made the interaction feel therapist-guided rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked Malik's preference among choices and affirmed he need not decide today, supporting ownership of the plan. Rapid succession of specific scripts and tips subtly shifted the direction toward therapist-recommended solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used readiness/confidence scaling and open questions about likes/dislikes and what would need to change. Frequently provided options and practical tips before allowing Malik's own internal motivations and discrepancies to fully surface.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided authoritarian stance and invited input on preferred lines and strategies, and Malik's choices shaped the plan. Frequent introduction of structured options and rehearsal scripts made the interaction more therapist-directed than truly client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some small change statements (skip on school nights, texting mom, trying one night) but they were mainly logistical, avoiding tiredness or maternal yelling. Therapist offered options and scripts rather than evoking Malik's own values, needs, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and accepted sustain talk with 'that makes sense' and 'I hear you,' keeping the dialogue open. Reflecting sustain content (friends do it, mom is dramatic, not his problem) without a double-sided pivot left the status-quo focus unrebalanced.
