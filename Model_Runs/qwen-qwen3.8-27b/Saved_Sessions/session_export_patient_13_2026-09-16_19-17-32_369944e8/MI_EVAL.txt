# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys a nonjudgmental, supportive stance toward Anara's fear and ambivalence. Some language feels slightly formulaic, and the emotional pain beneath her avoidance could be accepted more specifically.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Anara's ambivalence, fear, and subjective experience, including her metaphors and worry about voicemail. Some reflections are lightly interpretive or framed in the counselor's own language rather than fully tracking her felt meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on obtaining a health check-up and progresses from fear to barriers to a concrete, change-relevant next step. The overall trajectory is clear and well paced for a client in contemplation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Anara's own ideas, such as using her mom and the parking-lot step. However, frequent multiple-choice questions can make the conversation feel partly therapist-led rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids direct advice or pressure and repeatedly affirms Anara's knowledge and choice. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming her right to proceed slowly, stop, or choose inaction without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections invite Anara to identify her own barriers and possible next steps. The evocation is more practical than values-based, with less emphasis on eliciting deeper intrinsic motivation or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Anara as the expert on her experience and follows her client-generated ideas. Offering repeated option menus can subtly shift solution generation toward the counselor rather than the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are effective attempts to support small client-identified steps, but the counselor does not consistently elicit or amplify deeper change talk such as reasons, values, or confidence. More strategic questions and reflections could strengthen Anara's own motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Anara's reasons for avoiding the appointment without arguing or dismissing them. More strategic pivots from sustain talk toward change-relevant meanings would further resolve ambivalence.
