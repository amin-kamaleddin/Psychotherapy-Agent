# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental stance while normalizing Natalia’s difficulty. Acceptance is not higher because the response sometimes shifts to practical problem-solving before fully validating the emotional weight of her ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of Natalia’s conflict between needing a break and protecting her son. Empathy remains limited because the deeper emotions behind her stress, guilt, and protective-mother role are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing smoke exposure for her son and identifying a small, workable step. The direction is clear but more task-oriented than strategically linked to strengthening motivation and discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a realistic plan around the balcony, jacket, ashtray, and keys while adapting to Natalia’s limits. Collaboration is strong, though the counselor sometimes leads with prescriptive options before fully inviting Natalia’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Natalia’s choices and refusals are respected, and permission is asked before sharing options. Autonomy support is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still introduces several suggestions after Natalia sets a narrow limit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and small-change questions evoke some change-related language from Natalia. Evocation is moderate because the psychiatrist often supplies coping strategies rather than drawing out deeper values, confidence, or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Natalia’s input shapes the final plan, showing a collaborative stance. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently introduces lists of options before fully exploring Natalia’s own priorities and obstacles.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Natalia expresses some willingness to try small changes and to protect her son from smoke. The psychiatrist rarely amplifies or strategically reflects this change talk to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and accepts Natalia’s continued smoking without confrontation. However, she mostly moves into practical planning rather than using double-sided reflections to gently resolve the tension.
