# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses nonjudgmental, respectful language and normalizes ambivalence, reducing shame around smoking. It falls short of a 5 because broader personal worth and burden (single mother, household demands, worry about her son) are not fully affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture her ambivalence between relief and concern about harm to her son. It is less strong on the deeper emotional content, such as fear of relapse, exhaustion, and the emotional toll of repeated attempts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking, moving coherently from triggers to a small harm-reduction experiment and follow-up. It could be stronger by periodically linking the micro-experiment back to the broader goal of reducing smoking around her son.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses inclusive language and invites Natalia to shape the plan, which is genuinely collaborative. However, the frequent offering of structured options makes the session feel somewhat clinician-guided rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a major strength: the psychiatrist consistently reinforces choice, avoids control, and explicitly normalizes the option of smoking if the plan does not feel helpful. The client’s ownership is preserved throughout, including when she declines to promise anything.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks evocative questions that help generate a concrete plan and some client-generated language. However, the evocation is more behavioral than motivational and does not consistently draw out deeper values, internal reasons, or change talk related to her son.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and avoids an authoritarian tone, allowing Natalia’s ideas to shape the plan. The session is not fully client-led because the clinician frequently introduces structured options and drives the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are effective MI moves that elicit some change talk, including confidence scaling and reframing the pause as “see if the air clears the head.” The cultivation is uneven and could be deeper by reflecting, amplifying, and exploring her own reasons for change more consistently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is a significant weakness: while the psychiatrist avoids arguing, some reflections reinforce sustain talk by validating outdoor smoking or the perceived effectiveness of the cigarette. There is limited use of double-sided reflection, discrepancy development, or gently redirecting sustain talk back to her concern for her son.
