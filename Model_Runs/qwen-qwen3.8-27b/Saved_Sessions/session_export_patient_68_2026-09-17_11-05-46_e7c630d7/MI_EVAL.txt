# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, never judging Kevin's drinking or amplifying his shame. Responses like 'That's not a small thing to say' and 'That's a quiet, heavy thing to carry' show deep, non-conditional acceptance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate, specific, and nuanced — capturing the social identity threat ('you're weighing up your place in the group') and the weight of setting boundaries solo in real time. Kevin repeatedly validates these reflections, confirming they land.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange stays connected to the target behavior (drinking on Friday nights) with no drift or tangents. The session progresses logically from establishing the incident through ambivalence, social stakes, the binary challenge, and the underlying support gap.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functions as a genuine dialogue between equals, with Kevin's ideas ('vehicle not destination,' 'default,' 'signpost') consistently picked up and built upon. The psychiatrist explicitly seeks Kevin's validation and adjusts based on his responses, with no sense of a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Kevin's ownership: 'on your terms, not mine,' 'You clearly already know your limits,' and 'You don't have to solve every Friday at once.' No unsolicited strategies are offered, and all potential change is framed as Kevin's own decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin generates most key insights himself ('vehicle not destination,' 'I do know where the line is'), drawing on his intrinsic values rather than external reasons. Slight opportunity to go deeper: the psychiatrist occasionally offers small interpretive moves (e.g., 'a default is something you can actually change') that Kevin could have generated on his own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The collaborative stance is maintained throughout with frequent invitations for Kevin's input and no assumed expertise over him. Minor growth area: introducing reframes like 'a default is something you can actually change' slightly shifts toward the psychiatrist leading rather than fully waiting for Kevin to generate the insight.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin produces recognition, ability, and desire statements, and the psychiatrist skillfully reflects and amplifies them ('That's not nothing — that's you already testing a third option'). The session could have gone deeper in eliciting confidence or commitment language, particularly around Kevin's ability statements and the 'signpost' metaphor.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without reinforcement — both sides are validated ('Those aren't contradictions at all') and the all-or-nothing binary is reframed ('Is that really the full menu?'). Could have more explicitly explored whether the 'boring one' perception is truly how friends would react, rather than only reflecting the discomfort.
