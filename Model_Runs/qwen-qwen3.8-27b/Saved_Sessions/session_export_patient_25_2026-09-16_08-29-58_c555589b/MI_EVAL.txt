# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and nonjudgmental stance, affirms strengths, and normalizes possible slips without shaming. The tone is supportive but somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s goals, concerns, and confidence level. Empathy is primarily content-focused and could deepen by exploring underlying emotions, meanings, or values more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation remains tightly focused on limiting alcohol at social gatherings, moving from concerns to importance, confidence, strategies, and follow-up. The pacing is purposeful and guides the client toward a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely collaborative, with the client helping shape the plan and follow-up approach. The collaboration is slightly limited by the psychiatrist introducing a fairly structured menu of strategies before fully co-generating ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently invited to make choices about limits, strategies, follow-up, and contact preferences. The psychiatrist respects the client’s rejections without pressuring, keeping the plan client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling and open-ended questions effectively elicit the client’s own motivations, barriers, and confidence. The clinician occasionally offers strategies and language that, while useful, slightly reduce the degree of client-generated evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client clearly shapes the session by choosing strategies, limits, refusal language, and follow-up. Partnership is slightly less strong because the psychiatrist presents a detailed list of options before fully inviting the client to generate his own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes change and confidence talk, and the client’s confidence rises as he articulates goals and plans. The intervention could be stronger by more powerfully reflecting the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles mild sustain talk well by accepting the client’s perspective without arguing or exaggerating the problem. He reframes the client’s position into low-effort, nonjudgmental options that support change.
