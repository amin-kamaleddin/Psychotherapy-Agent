# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Therapist consistently validates mixed feelings, avoids judgment or shaming, and maintains warmth even when the client emphasizes that drinking is fun.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures the client's ambivalence, but could deepen emotional meaning such as embarrassment, fear of exclusion, and the importance of belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on weekend drinking and its consequences, steadily guiding toward a concrete behavior-specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan emerges from client-generated ideas with the therapist helping organize them, reflecting a clear equal-partner stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client choice is respected and pressure is avoided, but ownership of the decision and next step could be reinforced more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist effectively draws out the client's own goals and solutions, but could use scaling, values, or confidence questions to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The collaborative stance is clear and client-led, though explicit framing of the client as the expert could make the partnership more transparent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client generates useful alternatives that are affirmed and extended, but change talk could be further amplified and deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and redirected toward healthier alternatives, though double-sided reflections could more consistently pivot toward change.
