# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment while validating the client’s exhaustion, fear, and wariness. Acceptance is strong, though at times slightly formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist names several emotions and reflects ambivalence around restriction, rest, and scale-related fear. Empathy is adequate but often remains surface-level before shifting to advice or problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting concerns of body dissatisfaction, restriction/crash cycles, scale distress, and safe movement. Direction is clear and purposeful, though somewhat treatment-plan-driven rather than deeply MI-guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language, checks willingness, and adjusts the plan when the client resists complexity or wants to keep the scale. Collaboration is solid, though the counselor still leads much of the session with structured advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering experiments, checking consent, and respecting the client’s preference to keep the scale. Autonomy support is strong, though still somewhat prescriptive in its safety and coping guidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Much of the session relies on psychoeducation, advice, and concrete coping plans rather than drawing out the client’s own values, hopes, and reasons for change. The client’s change talk is limited and not deeply developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers choices, and adapts to the client’s request for simplicity. However, partnership remains uneven because the counselor often takes on an expert, plan-providing role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some willingness and commitment through open invitations and small low-risk experiments. Change talk is present but not consistently deepened or amplified through values-based questions and reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about weight loss, scale use, and compensatory exercise remains central to the session. The psychiatrist validates these concerns but does not consistently redirect them toward values, self-trust, or long-term well-being.
