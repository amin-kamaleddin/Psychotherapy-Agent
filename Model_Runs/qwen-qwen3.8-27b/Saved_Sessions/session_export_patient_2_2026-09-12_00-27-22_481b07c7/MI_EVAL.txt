# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, normalizes stress, and avoids judgment about alcohol. It falls short of 5 because warmth is often procedural and the repeated focus on alcohol can subtly signal it remains important.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflections and understands Aisha's stress and motivation. It is not a 5 because responses often remain cognitive/task-focused rather than fully exploring underlying feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on blood pressure, smoking reduction, and practical preparation. It is not a 5 because much of the time is administrative and the repeated return to alcohol slightly blurs the change-focused direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and incorporates some of Aisha's ideas, such as tracking and a reduction plan. However, most of the structure and advice is clinician-supplied rather than co-created with Aisha.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses optional language, respects Aisha's stated focus, and avoids direct pressure. It is not a 5 because the detailed plan and repeated alcohol information can subtly shift the interaction toward the clinician's agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with Aisha's priorities and acknowledges her motivation to protect her health for her son. However, evocation is limited by frequent advice-giving and a lack of deeper questions about values, confidence, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and includes some patient input, but the interaction is often expert-led. A stronger partnership stance would involve more co-construction and explicit recognition of Aisha as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms some change talk, such as reducing evening cigarettes and staying healthy for her son. However, the responses are mostly brief affirmations rather than skillful evocation or deepening of Aisha's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation and uses some softening language around alcohol. However, sustain talk is not consistently redirected with MI-consistent techniques, and repeated alcohol information can unintentionally reinforce its clinical importance.
