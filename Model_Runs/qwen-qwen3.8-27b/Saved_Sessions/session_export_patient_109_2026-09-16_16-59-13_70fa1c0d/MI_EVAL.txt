# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Rafael's realistic plan. The warmth is somewhat restrained and could more explicitly affirm his effort or worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate double-sided reflections to capture Rafael's conflict between needing a manageable plan and fearing it is too small. The response could go deeper into the shame, self-criticism, or emotional meaning beneath statements like "I hate admitting I did the bare minimum."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of returning to the gym/exercise consistently. The counselor narrows the conversation progressively toward a concrete, workable morning routine without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively rather than as an authority, repeatedly inviting Rafael to co-construct the plan. Rafael's ideas, concerns, and preferences shape the next steps in a genuinely shared problem-solving process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports Rafael's sense of choice and ownership, asking what he is willing to try rather than prescribing actions. There is no pressure to commit, no unsolicited advice, and clear respect for his ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Rafael's own values and criteria for success, such as wanting "I went" to feel honest and not skipping. The evocation is skillful but somewhat task-focused and could draw out a broader motivational story, past success, or deeper confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Rafael as the expert on his own life and behavior, explicitly inviting his knowledge about workdays, logistics, and mood. The session is client-directed rather than expert-driven, with Rafael's responses substantially shaping the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes change talk through open, client-centered questions and reflects Rafael's emerging commitments. The response could more explicitly amplify hope, momentum, or confidence in Rafael's ability to start.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument or dismissal and reframing concerns in a way that preserves autonomy. The final "bare minimum" statement could be more fully connected to values, self-respect, integrity, or progress.
