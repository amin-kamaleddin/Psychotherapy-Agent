# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoided moralizing Kevin's injury or drinking. Acceptance was strong but somewhat formulaic and could have included warmer, more individualized affirmation of his struggle and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made several accurate reflections, especially about ambivalence around relaxing, injury, and embarrassment. Empathy remained somewhat brief and focused more on behavior than on the emotional meaning of Kevin's embarrassment, fear of losing control, and concern about making a scene.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on reducing alcohol-related injury risk during social drinking and ended with a clear target behavior and specific timing. This was one of the strongest parts of the interaction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan reflected Kevin's input, but much of the session was shaped by the psychiatrist offering multiple options. Collaboration would be stronger if Kevin's own ideas and social-context expertise were elicited more before suggestions were offered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized Kevin's choice, ownership, and right to reject strategies without pressure. This area was a clear strength, especially in honoring his boundaries around reminders, scripts, and social announcements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used open questions and confidence ratings to draw out Kevin's plan and some motivation. However, the session relied heavily on offering options, which limited deeper exploration of his values, self-efficacy, and internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin's preferences clearly shaped the plan, and the psychiatrist avoided imposing rejected strategies. Partnership was uneven because the psychiatrist frequently led with long lists of options rather than positioning Kevin as the expert on his own social life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful change talk around intention, confidence, and wanting to avoid injury. The change talk was present but not consistently deepened, reflected, or amplified before moving into behavioral planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally acknowledged Kevin's reasons for maintaining drinking without judgment or argument. Softening was more passive than active, with limited use of double-sided reflections, mild discrepancy, or strategic redirection when Kevin expressed ambivalence about slipping.
