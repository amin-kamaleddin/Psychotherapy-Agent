# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepts the patient's time limits and practical stance. Acceptance is solid but feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or emotionally normalizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient's frustration, time pressure, and preference for practical, data-driven options. However, the reflection remains mostly cognitive and logistical, with limited exploration of underlying worry, resentment, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session starts with the patient's broader concerns but quickly narrows to blood pressure monitoring logistics, device selection, and app details. This drifts away from sustained focus on weight, diet, and lifestyle change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by framing choices around the patient's need for speed, practicality, and data. However, the psychiatrist still does much of the planning, providing the main options, lists, and protocol.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through repeated choices, respect for the patient's limits, and reinforcement of the patient's selected plan. It could be strengthened by more explicit emphasis on the patient's capacity to decide and by fewer additional optional tasks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks which option feels doable and which tool the patient prefers, drawing out some client-driven decisions. However, there is limited evocation of deeper internal motivation, values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids a lecturing stance and invites the patient's input into decisions about the plan and tools. Partnership is strong but not fully realized because the psychiatrist remains the primary organizer of the practical content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some commitment, such as choosing nuts, a 7-day log, and the Omron Evolv. However, the session does not systematically explore the patient's reasons, needs, or confidence for broader weight and lifestyle change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the patient's sustain talk and works within the patient's practical frame. However, sustain talk is mostly accommodated rather than actively reframed toward deeper health motivation.
