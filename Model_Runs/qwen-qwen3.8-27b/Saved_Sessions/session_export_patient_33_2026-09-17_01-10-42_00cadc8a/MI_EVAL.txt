# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates Diego without moralizing or shaming. Acceptance is warm and professional, though less explicit in unconditional positive regard or normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses solid reflective listening, especially in capturing Diego’s mixed feelings and safety needs. Empathy is somewhat surface-level in parts, with limited exploration of deeper shame, loneliness, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on harm reduction, safety, and concrete next steps. A clearer closing summary tying the plan back to the target behavior would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the work as collaborative, inviting Diego to shape the plan and script. The final safety plan is clearly co-created rather than clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Diego’s autonomy through open questions and by positioning him as the decision-maker. Autonomy support is strong but could be made more explicit by directly affirming his freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Diego’s own reasons, plans, and confidence for harm reduction. Evocation is somewhat action-focused and could be deepened by exploring values, meaning, or broader motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Diego as the expert on his own life and follows his lead. Diego’s ideas directly shape the session, demonstrating a strong partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Diego’s desire, ability, and readiness, and reinforces his emerging change talk. Some opportunities were missed to amplify confidence, values, or stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing for abstinence and accepts Diego’s harm-reduction stance without judgment. Sustain talk is handled well, though it could be more strategically pivoted into broader change talk.
