# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is largely nonjudgmental and respectful, avoiding argument with the client's resistance. However, the warmth can feel formulaic, and the abrupt crisis language at the end slightly reduces attuned positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture surface frustration and pressure accurately but are repetitive and shallow. Deeper feelings, ambivalence, and identity tied to smoking are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains relevant to smoking and marital strain but lacks a clear strategic focus. It shifts among options, scripts, and ending without a summary or targeted change direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and offers topic choices, but frequently moves into directing by supplying specific scripts and options. The dynamic remains more expert-driven than a genuine collaborative exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms the client's choice, freedom, and right to refuse. The support is strong, though providing scripts and the somewhat mismatched crisis statement slightly undermines autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some open invitations and preference questions, but little consistent effort to draw out the client's values or motivation. The psychiatrist often supplies content rather than evoking the client's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaborative choices are offered, but the session becomes directive through expert-led scripts and repeated options. The client's preference not to be pushed is respected, yet the continued offering of materials limits true power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers openings for change talk, but the psychiatrist does not actively evoke or amplify them. The session focuses more on providing options and scripts than on developing the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally met without argument, and the client's boundaries are acknowledged. However, the psychiatrist does not skillfully redirect statements of not being ready into ambivalence or possible change.
