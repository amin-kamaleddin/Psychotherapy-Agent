# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the client's difficulty comparing himself to younger men. There is no judgment, blaming, or minimization of the client's struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, especially around the client's mixed experience of progress and discouragement. However, the emotional weight of feeling "dumb," behind, or self-critical could have been reflected more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on managing comparison at the gym and creating a specific first-step plan. However, the plan is not consistently connected to the client's broader weight loss, lifestyle, and health goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites the client's input and treats him as an equal partner in problem-solving. The plan is co-constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated emphasis on choice, optional experimentation, and removal of pressure. The psychiatrist avoids directive or coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client's own ideas, motivations, and action plan through open-ended questions. However, the evocation is more action-focused and could more explicitly draw out confidence, past mastery, and deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert on his body, goals, and gym context. The client's contributions substantially shape the direction and content of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits meaningful change talk, including specific behavioral cues and a realistic first-step plan. However, the session does not explicitly elicit confidence, past success, or deeper values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges the client's concerns and reasons for not changing without arguing or dismissing them. Sustain talk is redirected in a change-supportive way that makes the next step feel safer and more doable.
