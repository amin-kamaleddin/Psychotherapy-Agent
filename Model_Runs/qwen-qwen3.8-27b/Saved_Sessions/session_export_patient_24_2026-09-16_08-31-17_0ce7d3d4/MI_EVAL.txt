# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Daniel without arguing. The warmth is professional rather than deeply individualized, and mild evaluative language slightly limits full acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of Daniel’s surprise, defensiveness, and use of alcohol as an unwind ritual. Empathy is strong but does not always fully explore the deeper emotional meaning behind his resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, marathon training, and practical next steps. The approach becomes somewhat task-focused at times, which slightly reduces the MI-driven emphasis on ambivalence and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Daniel’s input and adapts the plan to his preferences. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still generates much of the structure and solution options rather than fully client-led exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, permission-seeking language and respects Daniel’s flexibility and boundaries. Autonomy support is strong, but structured expectations such as tracking and a set check-in can feel mildly controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions about what Daniel wants to keep or change and what worries him. Evocation is limited because the session quickly shifts to counselor-generated options and practical planning rather than fully drawing out Daniel’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and gives Daniel meaningful input in shaping the plan. Partnership is strong, though the session is more counselor-guided than fully client-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel offers some change-relevant statements, and the psychiatrist asks a few evocative questions. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand on change talk, reducing the depth of MI evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Daniel’s minimization and maintains low-pressure language. More advanced MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections or highlighting discrepancy, are not used consistently.
