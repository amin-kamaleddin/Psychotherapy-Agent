# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with strong affirmations and validation of Samir's experience. The warmth occasionally feels slightly performative or scripted, and no explicit statement of unconditional acceptance is made.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate complex and dual reflections that track Samir's emotional tone well. Reflections become somewhat formulaic by mid-session, and deeper emotional attunement (e.g., the 'sister's look' comment) is missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on medication adherence but is diffuse across multiple subtopics without clear progressive momentum. It feels somewhat circular, returning to 'managing' and 'being told what to do' without narrowing toward a concrete therapeutic target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the interaction as collaborative from the opening and avoids lecturing or imposing frameworks. However, the collaboration is somewhat one-directional, with the psychiatrist doing most of the reflecting and guiding while Samir contributes minimally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook demonstration woven throughout nearly every response; the psychiatrist never pressures, uses controlling language, or minimizes Samir's right to refuse. Explicitly affirms his autonomy with phrases like 'on your own terms' and 'it's not my call to make.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes consistent evocative attempts with appropriate open-ended questions, but the evocation is superficial in impact and does not progressively deepen. Rich material (e.g., the sister's look, 'I'll figure it out' pride) is not leveraged for deeper exploration of values or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely positions Samir as the expert on his own experience and avoids an expert-driven or hierarchical stance. Could be deepened by more explicit power-sharing and client-led agenda setting (e.g., 'Where do you want us to go from here?').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some efforts to evoke change talk, but Samir produces very little and the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify the small positive elements that emerge. No explicit desire, need, or commitment language is elicited from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing against Samir's sustain talk and does not reinforce it, but does not actively redirect or use double-sided reflections to gently open the change side. Responses are mostly affirmations of the current state rather than subtle pivots.
