# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating with clear unconditional positive regard. However, the warmth borders on sycophancy, affirming nearly everything the client says, which risks undermining the therapeutic frame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures the client's emotional experience and perspective, particularly around autonomy and resistance to external judgment. Reflections are accurate but don't always probe deeper into underlying feelings or add new insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Lacks clear strategic progression toward any specific target behavior or exploration goal. The session follows the client's dismissiveness without building momentum, ending with full validation of the status quo and no concrete direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Non-directive and inclusive in tone, but the collaboration is more accommodation than true partnership. The psychiatrist contributes almost nothing of their own perspective beyond mirroring and affirming what the client already believes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports the client's autonomy with clear messages that the choice is hers, without pressure or advice. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming her capacity to make informed decisions and framing future engagement as truly optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very limited effort to elicit the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for potential change. Missed key opportunities such as exploring what 'change' she was seeking or using scaling questions to assess confidence in her current approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a non-authoritarian stance but is more one-directional than truly collaborative. The psychiatrist functions as a warm listener who agrees rather than an equal partner who brings their own perspective to joint exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only minimal and weak attempts to evoke change talk, with no use of importance rulers, confidence rulers, or future-focused questions. Given the client's strong sustain talk, the session should have made more active efforts to elicit even a sliver of change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk, but also does not actively soften or redirect it using double-sided reflections or strategic summaries. The client's strong sustain talk goes essentially unchallenged and unredirected throughout the session.
