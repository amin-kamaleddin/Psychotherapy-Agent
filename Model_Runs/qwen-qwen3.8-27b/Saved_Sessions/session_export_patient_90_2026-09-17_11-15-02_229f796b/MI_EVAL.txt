# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and non-judgmental throughout, with affirmations like 'Those aren't just obligations, they're who you are.' Slightly functional in tone rather than deeply relational — lacks explicit statements of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with creative, non-repetitive reflections (e.g., 'a problem that keeps changing shape,' 'those aren't moral failings, they're just what life looks like when you're stretched thin'). Consistently tracks both cognitive and emotional dimensions, adding meaning rather than merely paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful arc from broad ambivalence to a specific, actionable step (inhaler with morning coffee). Each question builds logically on the previous response, narrowing focus without forcing, while remaining patient-paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely egalitarian power dynamic with inclusive, joint-exploration language ('If we zoomed way in…', 'If we went with 'smaller'…'). Marcus's own contributions drive the session's direction; the psychiatrist serves as a skilled guide rather than an advisor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Marcus's ownership and choice, never prescribes or uses directive language. Actively frames change as something Marcus designs ('what would fit into how things already are') and explicitly places authority in the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically draws out Marcus's own solutions rather than providing external reasons for change. Marcus arrives at the inhaler-with-coffee plan himself; the psychiatrist creates conditions for intrinsic motivation to surface without ever stating 'you need to…'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently placed within the client ('You've got more of a life than I do,' 'That's you knowing your own life better than any handout could'). The psychiatrist never assumes they know what will work and cedes interpretive authority to Marcus throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk emerges organically and is amplified at key moments ('That's not stupid. That's you knowing your own life…'). Could be stronger in systematically building momentum — e.g., not exploring confidence level when Marcus says 'I don't know if that's enough.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without arguing or dismissing (e.g., the dog boundary is validated as non-negotiable). The final sustain talk ('what if it's not enough?') is left somewhat unaddressed; a stronger response would gently explore the doubt while still honoring it.
