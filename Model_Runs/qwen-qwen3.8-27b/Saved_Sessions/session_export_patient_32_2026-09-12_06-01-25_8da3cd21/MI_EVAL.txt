# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard while accepting Derrick’s reluctance and practical reasons for attending. Warmth remains somewhat formulaic and does not fully normalize Derrick’s ambivalence, shame, fear, or isolation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Derrick’s practical concerns and cognitive position, but the empathy remains more factual than emotionally attuned. Deeper feelings such as isolation, shame, fear, and ambivalence about friends or independence are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing drinking-related risk, avoiding another DUI, and protecting Derrick’s license and job. The direction becomes somewhat logistics-heavy and repetitive, with less strategic movement from ambivalence to values to commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Derrick to shape the plan in multiple ways. Collaboration is weakened at times because the psychiatrist offers scripts, options, and check-ins rather than consistently eliciting Derrick’s own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Derrick’s autonomy by avoiding pressure, respecting his refusals, and framing the plan as Derrick’s own choice. Repeated offers to draft texts and check in slightly undermine Derrick’s stated boundary and preference to handle it alone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Derrick’s own motivations, priorities, and increasing confidence rather than relying on lectures. Evocation is less consistent because the psychiatrist frequently provides therapist-generated options, texts, and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance by asking permission and inviting Derrick to choose among options. Partnership falls short of fully client-led when the psychiatrist provides specific scripts and management strategies rather than eliciting Derrick’s own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits and reinforces change talk around protecting the license, using Marcus, avoiding the bar, and making a concrete plan. Change talk could be more consistently amplified using Derrick’s own language to deepen his motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids confronting sustain talk and often redirects it toward practical goals. However, sustain talk around labels, friends, tracking, and monitoring is not consistently or skillfully softened with reflective or double-sided responses.
