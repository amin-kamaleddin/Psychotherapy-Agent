# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Matteo's preference for low-key change rather than abstinence. Warmth was mostly task-focused, with occasional phrases that emphasized plan quality more than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered adequate early reflections of Matteo's social drinking and ambivalence, but later shifted to summarizing, affirming, and planning. Emotional material such as self-criticism, awkwardness, and fear of missing out was not consistently explored with depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use, the ankle injury, and concrete harm-reduction goals. Direction was strong but sometimes became repetitive in planning or advice-giving rather than progressively clarifying Matteo's own change agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Matteo's input and built a plan around his preferences, such as water on Wednesdays, avoiding shots, and arranging a ride. Collaboration was limited by the amount of clinician-generated options and specific suggestions offered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by asking permission, respecting Matteo's boundary against quitting, and offering options. Autonomy support was mixed with directive suggestions, including specific numeric limits that could feel prescriptive after Matteo chose his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and future-focused prompts helped evoke Matteo's own reasons for change, especially protecting his ankle and returning to PT. Evocation was present but not consistently dominant, as the psychiatrist often moved into practical advice rather than amplifying Matteo's change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed partnership by asking which options felt doable and allowing Matteo's choices to shape the plan. However, the session was somewhat clinician-driven, with the psychiatrist presenting a menu of practical ideas rather than fully co-exploring Matteo's own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked useful change talk around ankle recovery, PT, using water, avoiding shots, and increasing confidence. This change talk was often met with praise and practical advice rather than deeper reflection or strategic amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with Matteo's sustain talk about social drinking, avoiding quit, and discomfort with refusal. Softening was mostly passive, with limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to help Matteo resolve ambivalence.
