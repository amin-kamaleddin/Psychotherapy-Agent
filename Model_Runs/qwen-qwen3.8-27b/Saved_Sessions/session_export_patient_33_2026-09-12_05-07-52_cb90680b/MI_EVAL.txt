# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Diego’s harm-reduction goals. Acceptance was somewhat formulaic and task-focused rather than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate but mostly factual and plan-oriented rather than emotionally attuned. The psychiatrist missed opportunities to validate the emotional weight of ambivalence, disclosure, and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on heroin harm reduction, overdose safety, and concrete safety planning. Each part of the conversation contributed directly to the target goals with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego participated by choosing scenarios and refining scripts, but much of the content was clinician-supplied. The collaboration was practical but not fully equal or co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices, avoided coercion, and respected Diego’s stated limits. Frequent prescriptive scripts somewhat reduced explicit client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some open questions, but the session mostly shifted into directive planning rather than deeply evoking Diego’s motivation. Strong statements about safety and readiness were not expanded into values, confidence, or meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego had some input and influence, but the psychiatrist frequently supplied the plan, scripts, and structure. The interaction was partly collaborative but unevenly expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego produced meaningful harm-reduction change talk, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify it with questions about importance, confidence, or values. Emerging motivation was supported but not deeply expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted statements about not quitting or avoiding medication and redirected toward safety without pressure. Softening was effective but more avoidance and redirection than skilled double-sided reflection.
