# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, normalizing, and nonjudgmental, helping the client feel heard and safe. However, acceptance often feels procedural and shifts quickly into problem-solving, limiting deeper relational warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, surface-level reflections of exhaustion, fear, and perfectionism. Empathy remains limited because reflections are brief and often move quickly into advice, reframes, or techniques.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on test anxiety, exam preparation, and a concrete practice task. The main weakness is that multiple techniques sometimes make the focus feel fragmented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client participates in choosing exercises and tasks, but the interaction is largely driven by clinician-prepared options. True MI collaboration would involve more co-creation from the client's own concerns and language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and avoids pressure, respecting the client's control over the session. Autonomy support is reduced somewhat by the heavy use of prescriptive scripts, breathing patterns, and structured tasks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions and prompts the client to identify successes and next steps. Evocation is limited because the session often shifts to supplying techniques rather than exploring the client's values, confidence, or deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and lets her choose among options, showing some collaboration. Still, the psychiatrist remains the main agenda-setter and intervention provider, so partnership is not fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are a few attempts to draw out ability and confidence, such as recalling successes and noting one thing done well. These moments are not consistently followed up to expand or deepen the client's own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce the client's fears and uses supportive validation. However, sustain talk remains prominent because the session frequently addresses doubts directly rather than using MI-specific softening or redirection.
