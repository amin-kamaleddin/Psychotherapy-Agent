# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently respects Ahmed’s perspective, avoids judgment, and accepts his minimization and autonomy. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with occasional shifts toward corrective, problem-focused language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and help normalize Ahmed’s ambivalence and practical concerns. Empathy remains mostly content-level and could deepen into underlying feelings such as embarrassment, fear of control, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on Ahmed’s mini-cigar use and its connection to throat/gum irritation. The conversation is purposefully guided from concern exploration to values, ambivalence, a small step, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Ahmed’s input and builds the plan around his chosen options, such as mint gum and no alarms or check-ins. Collaboration is strong, but the clinician still maintains a fairly structured agenda and steers the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently emphasizes Ahmed’s right to choose and accepts his limits, such as a short reduction rather than full abstinence. Pressure, coercion, and controlling language are avoided throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, scaling, and menu questions help Ahmed articulate some change-related motives, especially around work and family. Evocation is appropriate but could more deeply explore the meaning of those values and strengthen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ahmed is treated as the decision-maker, and his preferences meaningfully shape the plan. Partnership is generally strong, but the clinician still drives the structure and agenda at key moments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some change talk around practical steps, protecting work, and family. Change talk is not consistently developed, amplified, or strategically reinforced, so it remains shallow and easily offset by sustain talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids argumentation and responds respectfully to Ahmed’s minimizing statements. However, double-sided reflections, discrepancy, or strategic reframing are not used consistently enough to reduce the strength of the sustain talk.
