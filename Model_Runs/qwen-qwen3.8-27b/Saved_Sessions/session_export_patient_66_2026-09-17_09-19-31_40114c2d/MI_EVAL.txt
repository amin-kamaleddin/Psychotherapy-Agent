# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance, normalizing Kareem’s ambivalence and protecting his sense of control. The tone was supportive but not fully warm or explicitly affirming, which kept this from reaching the highest level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Perspective-taking was strong, with reflections that accurately captured Kareem’s central conflict between health and preserving his routine. However, the empathy remained more cognitive and behavioral than deeply emotional, especially regarding his fear of losing himself or being controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking reduction or cessation in a way that felt acceptable to Kareem, with questions consistently tied to the target behavior. A 5 was withheld because the broader shared target was not explicitly framed early or consolidated at the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaved as an equal partner, inviting Kareem to co-create the plan through open questions and joint problem-solving. Kareem’s ideas clearly shaped the session, such as the mental check-in, 10 a.m. alarm, and keeping the pack in his pocket.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently supported by emphasizing Kareem’s freedom to choose, avoiding pressure, and respecting his boundary against formal quit plans. The proposed experiment was framed as optional, self-monitored, and reversible.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited Kareem’s own motivation, values, and self-management plan rather than offering external reasons for change. Questions helped Kareem articulate his goals around calm, comfort, social ease, and self-control.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Kareem as the expert on his own experience and choices, repeatedly reinforcing that the plan should remain his own. This reflected strong power sharing and respect for Kareem’s authority in the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked meaningful change talk by inviting Kareem to imagine change in his own terms and generate a self-directed experiment. The main gap was a lack of consistent amplification, summarization, or deeper strengthening of the change talk that emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled Kareem’s sustain talk skillfully by integrating his reasons for continuing smoking into a collaborative, autonomy-consistent process rather than arguing against them. His concerns were used as material for a gradual, self-directed experiment.
