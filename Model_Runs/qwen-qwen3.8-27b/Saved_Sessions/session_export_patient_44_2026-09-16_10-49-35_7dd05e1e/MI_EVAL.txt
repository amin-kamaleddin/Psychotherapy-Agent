# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with statements that communicate positive regard. However, acceptance is often tied to progress and problem-solving, and the final disclosure of guilt is not fully addressed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is present but often cognitive and practical rather than emotionally precise. The counselor misses key emotional undercurrents, such as fear of damaging a relationship and the final guilt spiral.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a strong, clear focus on the target behaviors of exercise and diet. Each discussion stays connected to the broader goal of improving physical health and consistency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor builds on Erik’s ideas and allows him to select plans such as the 15-minute slot and apple strategy. However, the counselor still supplies many options, so idea generation is collaborative but not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites choice and uses a generally permissive tone, supporting Erik’s ability to modify suggestions. Some mildly prescriptive language and many clinician-generated solutions slightly reduce emphasis on client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited through confidence rulers and doability questions, but the counselor rarely explores deeper values or personal meaning. The session relies more on practical behavior-change options than on drawing out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Erik’s input and asks which options feel doable, supporting a collaborative stance. However, the repeated generation of many plans and scripts gives the session a more expert-driven, problem-solving feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, especially confidence statements, through questions and rulers. These statements are often redirected into practical options rather than amplified, reflected, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally normalized and not reinforced, with some reframing of missed days as information. The counselor could more consistently use MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to pivot barriers into change talk.
