# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, using validating language such as “that makes sense” and “that sounds brave.” The acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and procedural, especially with repeated safety checks, so it does not fully convey deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflections on Liora’s fatigue, urges, and practical difficulty, but often remains at the surface level. Deeper emotional meanings such as shame, fear of burdening others, isolation, and helplessness are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on safety, self-harm reduction, and a small first step. However, the direction becomes somewhat repetitive and remains more task-focused than motivational or values-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and asks for Liora’s preferences, but the interaction remains mostly clinician-led. Liora’s ideas do not visibly reshape the plan or session direction in a meaningful way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through options, privacy respect, and a no-explanation message, but repeated safety prompts and offers to help send or adjust the message can feel mildly pressuring. The psychiatrist could better honor Liora’s limits by asking what she is willing to explore rather than repeatedly offering new strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with useful evocative questions, but quickly shifts into advice, options, and practical problem-solving. There is limited exploration of Liora’s values, fears, past survival, or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses partnership language such as “we can work together, at your pace,” but remains the main agenda-setter and task-driver. The collaboration is present but somewhat superficial rather than genuinely co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some change talk, including Liora’s tiredness and the concrete step of sending the knife-locking message. However, there is little systematic use of DARN/CRAVET questions to expand desire, ability, reasons, need, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges why cutting may feel necessary. However, there is limited active softening through double-sided reflections, reframing, or exploration of exceptions to statements like “nothing else works.”
