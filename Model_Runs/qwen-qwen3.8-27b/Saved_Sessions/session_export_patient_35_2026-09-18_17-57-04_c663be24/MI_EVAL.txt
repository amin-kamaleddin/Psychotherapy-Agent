# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard without judgment or lecturing, even when the client expresses defensiveness. Validates the client's behavior as understandable given her circumstances throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate reflections that capture felt meaning beyond surface content (e.g., 'those two minutes are the only part of the day that's just yours'). Reflections progressively deepen and the client consistently accepts and builds upon them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation with a logical progression from ambivalence to a concrete strategy. However, the session ends abruptly at the 3 AM sustain talk without resolving that obstacle or collaborating on a follow-up step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative with the client's ideas genuinely shaping the session direction and becoming the driving force of the conversation. The tone is that of two people working together rather than a professional advising a patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's ownership of the change process, never prescribes or imposes a plan, and attributes insights to the client rather than the counselor's guidance. Suggestions are always framed as the client's own discoveries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never provides reasons for change, health information, or lectures; all questions are calibrated to draw out the client's own motivations. Motivation and solution-finding (stress insight, stepping away realization, leaving the pack in the car) emerge entirely from within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fosters power sharing by explicitly placing expertise in the client and inviting her to shape the process itself. The session is genuinely client-led in its direction, with the client's contributions determining where the conversation goes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk through well-designed open questions and affirming the client's own insights. Could more systematically develop all four DARN categories, particularly ability talk and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without reinforcing it, using double-sided language and validation to gently pivot (e.g., 'both of that is true, and that's okay'). The 3 AM sustain talk at the session's end is acknowledged but not actively redirected or reframed toward change.
