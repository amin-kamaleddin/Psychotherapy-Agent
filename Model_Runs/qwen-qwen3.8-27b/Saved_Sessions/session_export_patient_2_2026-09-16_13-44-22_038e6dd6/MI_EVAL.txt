# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of Aisha’s current readiness without pressure or shaming. However, affirmations sometimes remain procedural rather than deeply explicit in unconditional regard for her as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Aisha’s ambivalence and practical experience, such as needing a break while noticing smoking is not ideal. Deeper empathy would involve exploring underlying feelings like exhaustion, guilt, or fear of losing control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing evening smoking and developing a concrete, achievable step. There is little drift, and each question ties back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed by summarizing Aisha’s ideas and inviting her to choose among options such as tea, an alarm, or a sister check-in. A 5 would require more explicit sharing of agenda-setting and plan-structure authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through statements that Aisha is not ready to quit, is still allowed to smoke, and can choose the most doable step. The plan is framed as low-pressure and client-selected rather than directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist primarily uses questions to elicit Aisha’s own solutions, motivations, and ideas for making the first pause easier. Greater evocation would more consistently draw out deeper values, self-efficacy, and internal motivation beyond next-step planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels collaborative through repeated invitations for Aisha’s opinion and use of her ideas. However, the psychiatrist still largely controls the sequence of questions and structure of the microplan rather than fully handing agenda-setting to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit many change-related ideas, such as reducing from six to three, staying inside, trying tea, and moving the pack. More systematic amplification of DARN-C change talk, values, confidence, or identity-based change language would strengthen this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, and the psychiatrist pivots toward smaller, low-pressure steps. A 5 would require more explicit softening of the strongest sustain talk, such as the fallback “if it’s too much, I’ll just have one,” using double-sided reflections that validate resistance while reorienting toward change.
