# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is generally respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental while accepting the client’s concerns without arguing. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and lacks deeper affirmation of courage, worth, or alcohol-related shame/risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor shows adequate reflective listening and content-level understanding of school, sleep, and connection. Reflections are more cognitive and task-focused than emotionally nuanced, with limited exploration of fear of exclusion or being monitored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear focus on sleep and phone use once the client raises those topics. However, the session drifts from the core referral concern about alcohol risk and lacks strategic focus on the relevant target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats the conversation as joint problem-solving and adapts the plan to the client’s stated boundaries. The counselor still drives much of the agenda with structured options, so collaboration is not fully equal-partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice through permission, options, a reversible one-night trial, and respect for a short check-in. Some repeated structuring and follow-up questions may subtly increase pressure when the client fears being watched.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some evocative questions and elicits modest client-driven language and a confidence rating. The session moves quickly into implementation without deeply exploring the client’s underlying values, goals, or ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicates a collaborative, non-expert stance and lets client preferences shape the final plan. The counselor still provides a fairly structured set of options and drives the process, limiting fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some change talk around sleep and phone use, including a one-night trial and confidence rating. There is limited effort to amplify, summarize, or deepen the client’s change language and broader motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges the client’s reasons for staying connected and avoids direct argument. MI-specific techniques, such as double-sided reflections to work through ambivalence, are used less consistently.
