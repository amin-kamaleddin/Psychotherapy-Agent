# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician is respectful, normalizes awkwardness, and offers supportive praise, but acceptance is task-focused and does not explicitly validate ambivalence, vulnerability, or the referral concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflecting shows basic understanding of workload and stress, but empathy remains surface-level and does not explore deeper meaning of perfectionism, image concerns, or ambivalence about asking for help.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Session drifts to study and time management without connecting to the stated alcohol-risk referral, so the core clinical target is not addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client contributes tasks, times, and preferences, but the clinician supplies the structure and even fills in the plan, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent options, preferences, and respect for the client's boundary about accountability messages show strong autonomy support, though limited to task planning rather than the referral issue.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client-generated details are used for task planning, but the clinician does not elicit the client's own values, concerns, or reasons for change related to alcohol risk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative questions are present and some client choices shape the plan, but the clinician often leads with a structured template rather than treating the client as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is limited to making and sticking to a study plan; the clinician does not evoke motivation, benefits, or identity-related reasons for addressing the target alcohol concern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little explicit resistance is addressed, and potential avoidance is met with task completion rather than double-sided reflections or MI-consistent softening.
