# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance even when Luis is defensive or sarcastic. The stance is strong, though it could include warmer, more person-centered affirmation rather than only practical, task-focused acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Luis’s concerns and normalizes practical barriers such as forgetting the inhaler. However, the empathy often stays at the level of logistics and stated concerns rather than fully exploring underlying frustration, fear of being controlled, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on the target behavior: asthma management and a small step to improve inhaler use. The clinician maintains clear momentum toward a concrete, actionable plan without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Luis’s input and adapts the plan based on his preferences, resulting in a co-constructed final arrangement. The collaboration could be stronger by giving Luis more opportunity to generate options and define the one-pager in his own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Luis’s autonomy by repeatedly emphasizing choice, avoiding pressure, and respecting his refusals. Language such as “no promises required,” “totally your call,” and “no follow-ups unless you want them” clearly conveys that decision-making authority belongs to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client-driven motivation by asking about success, tolerable options, and doable steps. However, the evocation is mainly practical and does not deeply access Luis’s values, internal motivation, or confidence in his ability to manage asthma.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a collaborative stance and treats Luis as an important contributor to the plan. The partnership could be strengthened by further acknowledging Luis as the expert on his own life and giving him more space to generate options rather than selecting from clinician-provided ones.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some willingness to try the key-clip idea and settles on a small behavioral step. However, the session misses opportunities to amplify, reflect, and deepen Luis’s change talk more consistently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument, moralizing, or direct challenge, while normalizing Luis’s concerns. The response could be stronger by more consistently reframing sustain talk into change-oriented language that preserves independence while supporting inhaler use.
