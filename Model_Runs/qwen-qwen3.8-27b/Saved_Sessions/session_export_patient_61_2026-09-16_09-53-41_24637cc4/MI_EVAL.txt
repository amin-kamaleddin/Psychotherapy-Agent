# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language, validating Jonathan’s burden, skepticism, and resistance. Minor normative phrasing (“it’s great you’re taking steps”) kept this below exemplary.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflected Jonathan’s practical constraints and his view that the plan felt like extra work or rules. Reflections were often cognitive rather than deeply emotional, especially around frustration and fear of being monitored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on diabetes self-management and narrowed to one concrete, manageable action: morning glucose checks while brushing teeth. The direction was somewhat clinician-led because options were offered before fully clarifying Jonathan’s own priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Jonathan’s input and adapted the final plan around his preferences for low effort, privacy, and minimal follow-up. Collaboration was strong, though much of the agenda was still structured by clinician-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized choice, flexibility, and no reminders or pressure, supporting Jonathan’s self-direction. Some phrases subtly implied positive expectations, making the autonomy support less consistently pure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked some evocative questions, such as what matters most and a confidence rating, but did not deeply follow up on Jonathan’s reasons, values, or concerns. The session relied more on offering options than eliciting Jonathan’s own motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintained a collaborative, non-expert stance and allowed Jonathan’s preferences to shape the final plan. The interaction was partnership-oriented, but it remained within a clinician-driven menu of suggested options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jonathan offered meaningful change talk, including that checking while brushing teeth felt doable and that he would try it for a week. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on this change talk to strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled Jonathan’s sustain talk respectfully without arguing or becoming defensive. Responses often acknowledged his concerns but did not consistently reframe or turn them into motivational momentum.
