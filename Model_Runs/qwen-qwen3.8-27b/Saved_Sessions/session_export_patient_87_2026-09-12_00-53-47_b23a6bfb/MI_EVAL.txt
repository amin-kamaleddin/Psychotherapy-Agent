# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and did not shame the client for his eating struggles or concerns. Acceptance was consistent but somewhat procedural, with fewer moments of explicit emotional validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist understood and normalized the practical issues well, using several accurate reflections. However, empathy often stayed at a content level and missed deeper emotional themes such as shame, self-criticism, and family sensitivity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and moved clearly from problem identification to a concrete weekly plan. Direction was strong but more logistical than MI-oriented, with less emphasis on values, readiness, and personal motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and incorporated the client's ideas, such as moving snacks and using a notes app. Nonetheless, most of the substantive plan was clinician-generated, limiting true co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and avoided direct pressure, supporting autonomy at a basic level. The session was still highly prescriptive, which reduced the emphasis on the client's ownership and freedom to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some client input, but mostly through logistical questions rather than deeper exploration of motivation. Many solutions were offered before the client's own reasons, values, or confidence were drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was cooperative, but the psychiatrist often acted as the expert solution provider. The client's role was not consistently framed as the primary source of expertise about his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client produced some change talk, but the psychiatrist did not actively amplify, deepen, or build on it. There were few questions designed to elicit stronger personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with or reinforce the client's sustain talk and responded supportively. However, ambivalence was often bypassed with practical advice rather than strategically reflected or resolved.
