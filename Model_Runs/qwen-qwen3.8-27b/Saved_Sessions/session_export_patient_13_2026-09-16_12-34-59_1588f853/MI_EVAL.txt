# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental acceptance. Responses effectively normalize fear, reduce shame, and affirm the client without blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures the client’s ambivalence and logistical concerns. It could be strengthened by exploring deeper emotional themes such as shame, self-doubt, and protective guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of arranging a low-barrier medical check-in. It would benefit from more consistent narrowing toward one collaboratively chosen next step and a clearer closing summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting choices and following the client’s lead. It could be improved by co-creating the message and plan with the client rather than supplying multiple prewritten options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated affirmations that the client can choose whether, when, and how to send the message. The psychiatrist avoids pressure and honors the client’s decision to hold off.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful evocative questions but does not consistently draw out internal motivation for change. More attention to values, confidence, and client-generated reasons for action would strengthen this skill.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively and frequently invites the client’s participation in decisions. The expert-driven provision of scripts, options, and safety language slightly limits the client’s role as the primary decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate attempts to encourage small, change-oriented steps, but the session does not consistently deepen or expand the client’s own reasons for change. More reflective amplification of change talk is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally acknowledges sustaining concerns without argument or dismissal. It would be more effective to reduce the emotional grip of fear-based sustain talk through ambivalence-aware reflections rather than expanding threat-focused safety details.
