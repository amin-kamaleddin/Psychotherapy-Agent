# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong respect, validation, and nonjudgmental stance reduce defensiveness. Acceptance is occasionally weakened by quick shifts to problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist recognizes fatigue and frustration, but reflections often remain surface-level. Deeper feelings of burden, loss of autonomy, and identity pain were not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began with a reasonable diabetes focus but quickly scattered across many strategies. It lacked a clear, mutually agreed target behavior and consistent strategic direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration through offering options and asking for preferences. However, the therapist supplied most ideas and did not fully follow the patient’s expressed desire to stop and be heard.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and reduces pressure, but repeatedly introduces structured solutions. This can feel subtly agenda-driven given the patient’s resistance to more tasks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocative questions are limited, and the psychiatrist often shifts into advice-giving and practical suggestions. The patient’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change are not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone is collaborative, with repeated invitations for the patient to choose among options. In practice, the psychiatrist remains the primary problem-solver and drives much of the session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some initial attempts to elicit motivation are present, but important change-related statements are not built upon. The focus often returns to practical suggestions rather than deepening the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with resistance and uses validating language. However, sustain talk is not consistently redirected toward values or change, and some options reinforce the status quo.
