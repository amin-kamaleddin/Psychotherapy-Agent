# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with frequent affirmations of Lukas’s effort. Acceptance is strong but occasionally tied to task performance, such as praise for having a plan, which keeps it from feeling fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Lukas’s surprise, conflict, and the tension between belonging and staying on track. Later, the response shifts more toward strategies than deeper reflection of his fear of freezing and social embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the peer pressure/alcohol situation and a concrete goal for responding differently next time. Related topics such as grades, team identity, and friendships are consistently connected back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well, building the plan from Lukas’s own ideas such as the exit line, text alarm, coat cue, and pizza reward. It becomes less balanced when the clinician adds multiple therapist-generated strategies and cues.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lukas is given real choice in the agenda, plan, and check-in timing, and the plan feels client-owned. Some repeated prompts to raise confidence and added techniques after he wants to keep it simple create mild pressure to do more.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Lukas’s own values, readiness, and confidence, supporting a rise from about 4–5 to 6–6.5. Evocation could go deeper by drawing out his broader reasons for change and identity as a student-athlete.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear partnership stance, with Lukas choosing the focus, plan options, and preferred level of rehearsal. At times the psychiatrist becomes more instructive or coach-like when offering lines, breathing, and cue suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is well-evoked through values questions, future-focused prompts, confidence rulers, and client-generated plans. It is strong but somewhat scaffolded and centered on a specific exit plan rather than broader commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the fear of looking weird without arguing, but does not fully explore or validate the relational concern. The sustain talk is often redirected toward coping strategies, leaving the emotional core of his social anxiety partially unaddressed.
