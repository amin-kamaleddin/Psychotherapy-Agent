# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental and validates Liora’s distress without shaming or moralizing. Acceptance remains somewhat procedural and crisis-focused, with limited deepening of her confusion, exhaustion, and helplessness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of pain but often stays at the surface level of distress. Important emotional content, such as exhaustion, fear of failure, and ambivalence, is not sufficiently reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on self-harm, urge intensity, and immediate safety, but the direction becomes repetitive crisis containment. It does not consistently guide Liora toward a specific MI-oriented change goal or collaboratively identified next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some shared decision-making, as Liora selects from options and her choices are incorporated. However, the therapist supplies most of the safety plan structure, coping menu, and check-ins, limiting full equal-partner collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers options and asks permission, supporting choice more than a purely directive approach. Some prescriptive language and repeated safety prompts reduce Liora’s sense of ownership, although this is clinically understandable in a high-risk context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the therapist provides many options and procedures instead of eliciting Liora’s own motivations for safer coping. There is limited use of scaling, values-based, or future-oriented questions to draw out change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Liora to choose among options, but the session remains largely therapist-driven. Liora is not consistently positioned as the expert on her own experience or invited to shape the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist praises small steps and asks about delaying or securing blades, but does not systematically elicit Liora’s reasons, desires, needs, or confidence for change. Change-relevant statements from Liora are not reflected, amplified, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers one softening reflection but does not skillfully address Liora’s repeated belief that cutting is the only thing that works. Rather than rolling with resistance or using double-sided reflections, the therapist keeps offering additional alternatives.
