# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used respectful, validating language and avoided pressure or judgment. Warmth was present but somewhat task-focused, with less explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and captured Mateo’s ambivalence, frustration, and fear well. Empathy could deepen further by exploring the emotional meaning behind his concerns about usefulness, dependence, and independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on increasing physical activity and improving rehab progress. The conversation moved clearly from general discouragement to a concrete, manageable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used reflective listening and open questions to build shared understanding with Mateo. Collaboration was strong, though the clinician still provided some direction that limited fully mutual co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo was repeatedly invited to define safe pacing and choices based on his own body. Adding more explicit language affirming his freedom to choose would strengthen this criterion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions helped Mateo generate his own ideas about pacing, warm-up, and safety. Evocation would be stronger with more deliberate focus on deeper values, confidence, and motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Mateo as the expert on his body and experience, inviting him to define safety and pacing. A stronger partnership would more explicitly emphasize shared decision-making and Mateo’s control over the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo expressed some change-oriented language and generated small action ideas. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or reinforce this change talk to build motivation and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo’s concerns about pain, stiffness, and risk were reflected respectfully without argument or dismissal. Using more double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to change would strengthen this area.
