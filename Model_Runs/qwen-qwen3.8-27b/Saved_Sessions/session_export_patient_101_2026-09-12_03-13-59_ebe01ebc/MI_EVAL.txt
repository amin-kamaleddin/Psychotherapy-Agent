# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, validating, and nonjudging, affirming concerns without moralizing. Acceptance is somewhat professional and task-focused rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and validate fear of loss of control, but they are often surface-level and quickly followed by suggestions. Deeper exploration of overwhelm, low energy, and financial anxiety is needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session focuses practically on alcohol and contraception risk reduction. However, after the IUD emerges, the earlier alcohol target is not fully integrated into the final plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes actively and choices drive next steps, but the clinician often presents expert problem-solving menus. More co-construction and client-led decision-making would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers choices, respects the client’s wording, and avoids pressuring a specific method. Repeated scripts and prep suggestions slightly shift toward gentle direction rather than pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks about importance, confidence, and doable steps, eliciting meaningful change talk. However, solutions are offered too early, limiting deeper exploration of values, self-efficacy, and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites the client to choose focus and options, and the client’s contributions shape the plan. The partnership is uneven because frequent expert advising makes the interaction more counselor-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates strong change talk about importance, family history, and taking pregnancy risk off their own responsibility. The clinician does not consistently amplify or expand this client-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges resistance to plans without arguing and redirects to smaller options. More double-sided reflections would better resolve ambivalence about safety versus overplanning.
