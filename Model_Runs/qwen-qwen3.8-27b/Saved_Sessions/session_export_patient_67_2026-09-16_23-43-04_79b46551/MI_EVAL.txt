# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, nonjudgment, and validation of Kenji's experience and ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper emotionally, especially around fear, shame, and feeling monitored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture Kenji's meaning, values, and hesitations well. Empathy would improve by exploring the deeper emotional and bodily experience behind the 'noise' and need for relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on reducing evening alcohol use and follows a clear MI-consistent path. It could be tightened with an MI summary and more explicit anchoring to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Kenji's input and uses his own language to shape the conversation. Collaboration could become more fully client-generated by letting Kenji name the next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honors Kenji's choice, avoids pressure, and supports his right to decide in his own way. Autonomy support is a clear strength throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open-ended, evocative questions rather than giving advice, helping Kenji notice the function of drinking. Evocation would be stronger if the client's emerging change talk were amplified more actively.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Kenji as the expert and avoids an expert-driven stance. Partnership could improve by ceding more of the agenda-setting and next-step selection to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some useful change talk, such as trying one night or looking for another way to get quiet. This area needs more strategic reflection, amplification, and use of importance or readiness questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without arguing, labeling, or moralizing, and uses balanced reflections effectively. It would be stronger if sustain talk were more consistently redirected into change talk.
