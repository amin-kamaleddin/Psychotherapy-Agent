# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout, never dismissing the patient's limitations or ambivalence. However, some affirmations (e.g., 'That's you, choosing') feel slightly premature before the patient has fully committed, which marginally undercuts the unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong empathic reflections accurately capture the emotional core of the patient's experience (e.g., the sting of being a spectator, the fear of the same chair cutting deeper). Some reflections are slightly leading or interpretive rather than purely exploratory, which prevents a perfect score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on physical activity and the community walking program with no topic drift. Pacing is deliberate and purposeful, progressing from exploration through ambivalence to a concrete, behavior-specific goal (calling the community center while coffee is still hot).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine collaboration with inclusive language and co-construction (e.g., the joint 'finding out, not forcing' reframing and the co-created 'coffee, phone' plan). Both parties actively contribute to the solution, and the balance of power remains equal throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces the patient's ownership, explicitly validates his right to decide, and honors his hedging ('not a promise, but it's not nothing'). The session ends with the patient self-selecting a concrete action in his own words and on his own timeline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within the patient rather than imposed externally, with his own language reflected back in ways that help him hear it more clearly. The progressive deepening—from 'when's this going to stop?' to a concrete action step—is entirely driven by the patient's internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the patient as the expert on his own experience and never takes over or imposes an agenda. When the patient resists, the psychiatrist follows rather than pushes, and the patient's contributions substantially shape the session's direction and outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits multiple instances of desire, ability, and commitment language by reflecting and amplifying the patient's own words. The score is held at 4 because the psychiatrist could push further into confidence-building (e.g., asking the patient to rate his confidence on a 0–10 scale).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without dismissing or arguing—e.g., exploring 'I'm not a person who forces myself' rather than challenging it, and validating fear rather than minimizing it. A 5 would require more explicit double-sided reflections or more strategic reframing of the sustain talk itself.
