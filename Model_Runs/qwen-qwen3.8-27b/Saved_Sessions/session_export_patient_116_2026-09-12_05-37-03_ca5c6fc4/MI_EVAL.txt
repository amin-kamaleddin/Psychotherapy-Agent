# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s right to choose. However, the acceptance is sometimes formulaic and occasionally reframes ambivalence in a way that feels slightly premature.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are reasonable efforts to reflect the client’s perspective, such as feeling pushed or like a 'guinea pig.' However, empathy remains largely surface-level and misses deeper emotions like distrust, frustration, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins on the relevant issue of medication resistance, but the direction quickly narrows toward refusal scripts and logistics. There is limited guidance toward exploring ambivalence or building motivation for the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and offers choices, but the process remains uneven and largely counselor-driven. Samir’s preferences are acknowledged, yet the agenda is mostly shaped by the interviewer’s problem-solving tools.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently affirms the client’s autonomy by inviting choice and avoiding direct pressure to take medication. However, the use of specific scripts can feel somewhat prescriptive despite being offered as options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and a confidence scale are used, showing some effort to elicit the client’s own perspective. However, evocation is superficial and does not effectively draw out deeper motivation, values, or ambivalence about medication adherence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and offers options, supporting some level of partnership. However, the interaction often becomes advisor-like, especially when repeatedly introducing scripts, role-plays, and brief notes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some initial attempts to explore the client’s perspective, such as asking what he would like to change. However, the session does not meaningfully evoke change talk in favor of medication adherence or treatment engagement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** A major weakness is that the client’s sustain talk is repeatedly validated and reinforced through refusal scripts and praise for saying no. There is minimal use of double-sided reflections, reframing, or gentle redirection toward possible reasons for change.
