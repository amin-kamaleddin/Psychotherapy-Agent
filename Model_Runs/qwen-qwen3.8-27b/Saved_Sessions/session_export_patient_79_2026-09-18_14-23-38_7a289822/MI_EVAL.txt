# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard with zero judgment or condescension throughout. Key examples include reframing inconsistency as 'a solvable problem, not a character flaw' and honoring the patient's defensive humor without invalidating it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is remarkably accurate, capturing both cognitive and emotional layers of the patient's experience. Reflections consistently add depth rather than merely paraphrasing, such as identifying the underlying fear of being re-controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains crystal-clear focus on a single concrete target behavior (keeping the rescue inhaler accessible at 2 AM) with zero topic drift. The masterful narrowing question ('what would that even look like to you?') skillfully funneled a broad complaint into one manageable action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine partnership between equals, with the patient setting boundaries that the psychiatrist respects. The solution (nightstand) is entirely the patient's own — the psychiatrist never suggested, hinted at, or steered toward it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is woven into nearly every interaction, from 'I'm not here to lecture you' to 'I'll honor that boundary. That's a deal.' The follow-up is framed as an open invitation ('how are the nights?') rather than a requirement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws motivation from within the patient using classic evocative questions like 'What would the first night look like?' and 'What would that feel like?' The patient's own values (rest, quiet, autonomy) are elicited and connected to the behavior without any external pressure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the patient as the expert on his own life, using open questions like 'What's your take?' and 'I'm curious what you actually think, not what you think I want to hear.' The closing line 'You didn't need me to figure that out' explicitly affirms the balanced power dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified throughout — when the patient says 'that'd be... Progress?' the psychiatrist immediately locks onto it, and when the nightstand solution emerges it is reinforced as the patient's own insight. The closing summary reinforces the patient's change talk as his own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well but not perfectly — the psychiatrist acknowledges self-doubt without fighting, such as 'You're not wrong about yourself.' However, some moments of sustain talk are absorbed rather than actively redirected with double-sided reflections that could have further tipped the balance toward change.
