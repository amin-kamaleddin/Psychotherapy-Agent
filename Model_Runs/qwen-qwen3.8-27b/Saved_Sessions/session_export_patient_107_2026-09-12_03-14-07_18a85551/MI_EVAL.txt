# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental but often procedural. Deeper affirmations of ambivalence, values, and struggle are needed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture the surface ambivalence about coffee and health concerns. More complex reflections linking work, routine, and underlying fear would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on coffee, blood pressure, sleep, and work-related impact. It becomes too focused on monitoring logistics rather than motivation and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nathaniel is often invited to choose next steps and contribute to the plan. More co-creation of medical information and interpretations would make it more balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently honors Nathaniel’s choice not to change and avoids direct persuasion. Early suggestions about cutting back or switching decaf slightly weaken full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions elicit concerns, but the session relies heavily on the psychiatrist supplying facts and plans. More questions about values, meaning, and personal reasons are needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative and invites Nathaniel to lead. The balance shifts toward an expert role when medical details and monitoring instructions are delivered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk appears but is not consistently developed or amplified. The counselor should explore benefits, values, and readiness in Nathaniel’s own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, but the psychiatrist rarely reframes it to preserve autonomy while opening space for change. More double-sided reflection is needed.
