# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates Kareem’s choice not to quit. A 5 would require more individualized positive regard and deeper normalization of his experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Kareem’s practical concerns about the pause, weather, and social ritual. Deeper emotional meaning—stress, identity, autonomy—was not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is clearly organized around managing smoking breaks under the new policy. However, the MI direction remains focused on maintaining smoking rather than exploring cessation or reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-created with client input on location, coworker, timing, and backup strategies. The clinician still leads with a structured menu of options, limiting fully client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through permission-seeking, non-coercive language, and respect for Kareem’s right to continue smoking. No pressure is applied to change his decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses useful evocative questions about preferences, barriers, and confidence. These remain practical rather than deeply motivational, with limited exploration of intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician conveys partnership through collaborative language and shared planning. A more equal stance would have allowed Kareem to set the session goal first and generate more solutions independently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is present around adapting to the workplace policy and managing breaks. It is not systematically developed around Desire, Ability, Reasons, or Need to change smoking itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, but it is not effectively softened or redirected. The conversation largely supports continuing smoking rather than exploring ambivalence or change.
