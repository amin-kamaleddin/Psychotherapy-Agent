# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent respect, patience, and nonjudgment, especially about smoking and parenting. Acceptance is sometimes generic and does not fully validate the client’s exhaustion, ambivalence, and broader parenting pressures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflective listening about ambivalence and coping, helping the session move forward. Empathy is limited because deeper emotions such as shame, fear of weakness, and feeling like a bad mother are under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing smoking, particularly linking change to the client’s concern for her son. The focus becomes narrowly procedural, bypassing broader motivational work on values and discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works with the client and integrates her preferences, such as dishes, a private break, and music, into the plan. Collaboration is somewhat less equal because the clinician still supplies menus and drives much of the structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respects choice by inviting input, allowing flexibility, and reducing all-or-nothing pressure. It could be stronger by more consistently affirming the client’s ownership of the decision and reducing clinician-generated prescriptions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses scaling and open questions to elicit some client-generated ideas and confidence. Evocation is limited because the clinician quickly shifts to suggestions rather than developing deeper values, hope, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites client input and allows her contributions to shape the session, which supports collaboration. The interaction can feel guided by clinician-offered options rather than fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical change talk through scaling and doability questions, and connects some statements to caring for her son. Development of value-driven change talk is limited due to early movement into clinician-generated plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or dismissing it, including its role in coping and the son’s current health. It does not strategically soften central sustain talk with double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy development.
