# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or lecturing. Stance is warm but somewhat professional/procedural rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and often captures Marisol’s core tension well. Empathy is mainly cognitive and occasionally repetitive rather than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the lip piercing and relevant health/appearance concerns. The target goal could have been clarified more explicitly early on.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marisol to define useful information and co-constructs a simple self-monitoring approach. The clinician still structures many choices and the question sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Marisol’s right to choose, avoids pressure, and reinforces that the decision remains hers. Language is especially careful not to impose a plan or timeline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Marisol’s own criteria for monitoring and comfort with reversibility. Evocation is mostly practical rather than directed toward deeper intrinsic motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative and allows Marisol’s preferences to shape the conversation. It would be stronger if the psychiatrist more explicitly affirmed her as the expert on her own body.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only occasional, weak attempts to build on Marisol’s change-related statements. The session focuses more on practical monitoring than on expanding desire, ability, reasons, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce Marisol’s sustain talk and redirects toward practical options. More active MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy, could have been used more often.
