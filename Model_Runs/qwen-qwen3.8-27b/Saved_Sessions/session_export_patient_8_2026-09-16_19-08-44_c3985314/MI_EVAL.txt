# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment, labeling, or shame around the client’s phone use and self-criticism. This creates a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures ambivalence, loneliness, fear of missing out, and boredom. Some reflections feel somewhat formulaic, and the deeper self-critical or shame-like dimension is not explored as fully as it could be.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on nighttime phone use and practical next steps related to the referral concern. However, the direction becomes somewhat narrow and problem-solving-centered rather than linking the plan back to broader goals, values, or wellbeing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner by inviting the client to evaluate options and framing the session as joint exploration. The client’s ideas substantially shape the direction of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s sense of choice by using permission-giving language and avoiding pressure to follow a specific plan. When the client expresses that turning the phone off feels too risky, the psychiatrist respects that concern and lowers the demand.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes client-generated ideas and preferences, but the questions are mostly logistical rather than motivational. There is limited exploration of the client’s own values, future goals, confidence, or discrepancy between current phone use and desired outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance and invites the client’s input, allowing the client’s ideas to shape the session. However, the psychiatrist still guides the direction and offers suggestions rather than explicitly positioning the client as the expert in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally evokes and reflects change talk, but does not consistently deepen or amplify the client’s own reasons for change. The conversation tends to move into practical problem-solving before fully developing the client’s motivation, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respectfully acknowledges the client’s reasons for maintaining the status quo, such as fear of missing out and boredom. The sustain talk is not reinforced or argued against, but it is not consistently redirected toward change-compatible alternatives.
