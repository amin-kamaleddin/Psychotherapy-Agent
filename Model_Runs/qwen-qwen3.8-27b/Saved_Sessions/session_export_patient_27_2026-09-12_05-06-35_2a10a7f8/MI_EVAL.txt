# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, especially when Daniela rejected practical aids. Warmth was solid but somewhat procedural, with limited explicit validation of her fear of stigma and emotional complexity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate and useful early reflections captured Daniela’s core ambivalence, such as wanting closeness without becoming “the sick one.” Empathy became repetitive and surface-level, missing deeper emotions like fear of burden, shame, or stigma.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on diabetes, peers, and disclosure, with practical emergency planning that was relevant to safety. It became too narrow and did not strategically revisit the central ambivalence around disclosure and peer perception.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative in practical planning, with Daniela choosing the pouch, location, phone method, and contact person. However, the broader therapeutic direction remained clinician-driven rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support for Daniela’s freedom to choose, including accepting rejections without pressure and emphasizing control. It fell short of a 5 because the clinician still supplied many options and practical solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician asked a few initial open questions but mostly offered external options and practical solutions rather than eliciting Daniela’s values, motivations, and confidence. The final plan was client-driven but not deeply connected to her own internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Daniela’s preferences and respected her decisions, which showed some partnership. However, the interaction was fairly expert-driven, with repeated menus of options that limited her expertise in shaping the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions invited some change language, but the session shifted quickly into problem-solving. Daniela’s emerging confidence and self-efficacy were not reflected, amplified, or developed further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing with Daniela’s reasons for staying private, which was a strength. However, the sustain talk largely set the agenda, with little use of double-sided reflections or exploration of costs, benefits, or ambivalence around disclosure.
