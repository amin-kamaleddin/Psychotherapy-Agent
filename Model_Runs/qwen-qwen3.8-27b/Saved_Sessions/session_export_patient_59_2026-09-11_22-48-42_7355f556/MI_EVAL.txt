# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers several supportive statements without criticizing Jamal. However, acceptance remains somewhat surface-level and focused on the task of finishing the session rather than the client's broader experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Jamal's frustration and fatigue, but the reflections are mostly factual. There are missed opportunities to reflect deeper feelings, ambivalence, or the meaning he places on the incident.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is clearly directed toward practical court preparation, which may be helpful but is not aligned with the MI target of exploring motivation around marijuana use or risk reduction. The psychiatrist does not steer the conversation toward values, discrepancy, or behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some collaborative interaction, such as offering choices and asking whether to rehearse. However, the psychiatrist frequently delivers structured advice, scripts, and checklists, making the collaboration feel limited and expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some autonomy-supportive language and defers legal decisions to the client's lawyer. Autonomy support is weakened by frequent prescriptive instructions, suggested scripts, and specific behavioral directions for court.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few open questions and invites the client to share what would be helpful. These openings are not followed through, and the client's own change-relevant language is not explored or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist sometimes asks for the client's preference, but mostly positions themselves as the expert providing solutions and court scripts. There is little evidence of treating Jamal as the expert on his own values, life, and decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not effectively evoke reasons for change related to marijuana use or safer choices. The session focuses on compliance, apology, and court logistics rather than client-driven motivation for behavior change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Jamal's minimizing statements, which is appropriate. However, there is no strategic softening, reflection, or reframing to use the sustain talk as an entry point for change-oriented exploration.
