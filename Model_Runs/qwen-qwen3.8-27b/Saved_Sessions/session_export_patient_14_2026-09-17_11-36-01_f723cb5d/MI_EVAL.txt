# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming the client's self-awareness and normalizing his constraints. Never lectures, moralizes, or expresses disappointment, even when the client voices self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses highly accurate complex reflections that capture both the emotional and practical meaning behind the client's statements. Exceptionally precise in reflecting back the client's own insights, such as 'You can handle a plan. You can't handle the in-between.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with good session momentum from initial engagement to a concrete, actionable plan. Could more explicitly connect the weight change to the client's stated health concerns (blood pressure, breathlessness) to strengthen the throughline.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative throughout, with the final plan co-created and the client directing what happens next. Builds on the client's own ideas rather than introducing external solutions, and adapts to the client's time constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never prescribes or directs; all actions are framed as the client's own decisions and the client's right to choose is consistently reinforced. Even normalizes the possibility of not following through without judgment ('If it doesn't, we don't fix it, we just adjust').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits substantial change talk and client-owned solutions at the practical level. Could deepen exploration of values and longer-term motivations (e.g., what health means to him, what he wants for his future) to push this to a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life, explicitly stating 'I want to make this feel like your conversation.' The psychiatrist never assumes the role of dietitian or health expert, instead asking for the client's analysis throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategically uses questions and reflections to elicit the client's own ideas, and effectively amplifies the change talk that emerges. Could push deeper into the client's values and underlying motivations for change to reach a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully validates the client's concerns while reframing them positively without arguing, minimizing, or being dismissive. Could use more explicit double-sided reflections to directly work with the ambivalence present in the session.
