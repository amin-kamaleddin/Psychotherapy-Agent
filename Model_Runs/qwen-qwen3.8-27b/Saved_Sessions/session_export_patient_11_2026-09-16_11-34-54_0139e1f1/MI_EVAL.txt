# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and validating stance with clear positive regard. Some affirmations focus on the client’s planning progress rather than unconditional self-worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflections of Amira’s context, ambivalence, and concerns about overcommitting. Reflections are helpful but remain more cognitive and plan-focused than deeply affective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on increasing physical activity and building a specific, behavior-based plan. Each step moves consistently toward a concrete and achievable action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is jointly constructed, with Amira choosing cues, days, reminders, and a motivating phrase. The clinician still supplies many structured options, which slightly limits full client-generated collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices, reduces pressure, and frames the plan as Amira’s own. Language such as “One set counts” and optional follow-up support reinforce ownership and flexibility.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some motivation through open questions and a confidence scaling question. Deeper exploration of values, meaning, or readiness is often shifted into logistics before it develops.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira is repeatedly invited to make choices that shape the final plan. The interaction can feel somewhat plan-driven because the psychiatrist introduces many specific options before asking for the client’s ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some change talk around strength, confidence, and commitment. There are missed opportunities to expand the client’s own desire, ability, need, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns about overcommitting, missing days, and quitting are met without argument or dismissal. The responses are helpful but more problem-solving than fully MI-sophisticated ambivalence work.
