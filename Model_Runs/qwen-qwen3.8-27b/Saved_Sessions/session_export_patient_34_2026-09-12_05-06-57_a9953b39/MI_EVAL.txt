# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Dylan’s feelings without pressuring him. Acceptance could be stronger by adding deeper individualized positive regard for Dylan’s strengths and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses consistent reflective listening and accurately tracks Dylan’s feelings of unfairness and defensiveness. Empathy would be stronger if it explored deeper underlying emotions such as shame, fear of labeling, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to the conduct issue, but the focus shifts toward practical responses such as drafting a statement rather than a clear MI agenda. The direction feels somewhat administrative and lacks sustained momentum toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, showing partial collaboration. However, much of the session is clinician-led, with the psychiatrist proposing interventions rather than co-constructing the agenda with Dylan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects Dylan’s choices and avoids coercion, which is a clear strength. Autonomy support could be stronger by linking Dylan’s decisions to his own values and capacity for self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to draw out Dylan’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change. The psychiatrist shifts into problem-solving instead of evoking internal discrepancy or deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial partnership by offering choices and respecting Dylan’s decision to end the session. The interaction remains more counselor-led than truly collaborative, as Dylan’s agenda does not substantially guide the work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions but does not effectively elicit or strengthen motivation for change. The session remains dominated by Dylan’s sustain talk, with little amplification of values or future-oriented change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Dylan’s minimizing statements, but does not effectively soften or redirect sustain talk. Responses such as focusing on minimizing consequences may unintentionally reinforce the idea that the main goal is to reduce blame.
