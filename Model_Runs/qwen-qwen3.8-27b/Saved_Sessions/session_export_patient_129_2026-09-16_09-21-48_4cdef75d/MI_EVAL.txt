# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveyed respect and nonjudgmental acceptance through affirming, calm statements. Acceptance was solid but sometimes generic or mildly evaluative, limiting deeper warmth and individualization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Tyler’s stated concerns about fitting in and conflict with his mother. Empathy remained adequate but did not deeply explore stronger emotions such as resentment, embarrassment, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed on topic and produced a concrete plan, but it frequently drifted toward managing conflict and concealment. Direction would have been stronger with more consistent focus on change in substance use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated with Tyler’s stated goal and helped shape a workable plan. Collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist often supplied options and safety rules rather than consistently eliciting Tyler’s own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offered choices, respected Tyler’s boundaries, and avoided direct persuasion. Autonomy support was strong, though repeated safety advice slightly reduced the sense of full self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used appropriate evocative questions, but the elicited motivation remained largely external. Deeper personal values, goals, and internal reasons for change were not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler was invited to set priorities and choose options, showing some shared decision-making. The interaction remained somewhat advisor-driven because the psychiatrist frequently supplied structure, options, and safety instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made several attempts to elicit change talk and acknowledged Tyler’s agreement to keep substances out of the house. Change talk was limited and not consistently built into stronger internal motivation for reducing use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was accepted without argument, which avoided confrontation. However, the psychiatrist did not consistently use double-sided reflections or help resolve ambivalence, and the plan may normalize concealment.
