# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Maria’s suffering without scolding or labeling. The main limitation is that the warmth remains somewhat procedural and risk-management-centered rather than fully affirming Maria’s worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses several accurate reflections, but they often remain at the surface level of exhaustion and noise. A key missed opportunity occurred when Maria described wanting “absence,” which was not reflected with deeper emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on acute suicidal ideation, emotional overwhelm, and immediate safety. The main weakness is that the focus is somewhat narrow and repetitive, with less momentum toward values, reasons for living, or concrete change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maria’s preferences shape some details, especially at the end, such as the 25-minute check-in and staying quietly. However, the overall session remains clinician-led around risk assessment, means restriction, and emergency planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly asks permission and offers choices, which supports autonomy in a formal sense. Repeated safety directives, however, can feel controlling and do not consistently emphasize that Maria is ultimately in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions, such as what quiet would feel like and what has helped before. These opportunities are not consistently followed with deeper exploration of Maria’s values, motivations, or capacity for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-giving and choice-offering language support a collaborative stance, but the session is heavily driven by the psychiatrist’s safety checklist. The psychiatrist does not explicitly affirm Maria as the expert on her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked and reinforced, including moving the pills, the dog as an anchor, and willingness to call for help. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or systematically develop this change talk through elaboration questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing and reflects Maria’s sustain talk rather than pushing back. However, repeated focus on risk, means, and emergency services can keep the conversation centered on danger, and more MI-consistent strategies such as double-sided reflections are not used.
