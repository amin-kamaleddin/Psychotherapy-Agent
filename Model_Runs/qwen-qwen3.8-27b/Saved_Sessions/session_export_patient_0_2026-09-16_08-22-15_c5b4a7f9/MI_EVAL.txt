# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing or pushing for immediate abstinence. The warmth remains somewhat procedural and does not fully normalize Adrian’s shame or fear of judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially in capturing ambivalence about weekends, safety, and not wanting a lecture. The reflections remain mostly practical and miss deeper emotional undercurrents such as shame, fear, or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on practical safety concerns, including infection, nose irritation, and safer use. However, the direction becomes repetitive without clearly consolidating information toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and lets Adrian’s concerns shape the focus of the session. The interaction remains largely question-and-answer rather than developing into a fully co-created safety plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adrian’s sense of choice is consistently respected, and the psychiatrist avoids pressure or direct advice. The clinician could more explicitly reinforce Adrian’s ownership of whatever decision he makes next.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client change talk is evoked around safer use, testing, and avoiding a risky source. The evocation remains superficial and does not fully explore importance, confidence, or underlying values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites Adrian’s input rather than acting as an authority. The agenda remains somewhat counselor-directed, and Adrian is not fully framed as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges around safer use, testing, and avoiding the ER, and the psychiatrist supports it by framing these as safety goals. These statements are not consistently amplified, summarized, or deepened in a way that strengthens client motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by reflecting Adrian’s resistance without arguing, blaming, or escalating. The clinician could more actively balance the repeated focus on keeping weekends with values-based reasons for safer behavior.
