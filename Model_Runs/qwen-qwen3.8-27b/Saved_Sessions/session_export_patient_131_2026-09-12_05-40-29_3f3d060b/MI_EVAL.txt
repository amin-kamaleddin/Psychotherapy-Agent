# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and clearly acknowledged the client’s progress and perspective. However, the warmth remained somewhat procedural and did not fully normalize the deeper shame or fear around parenting and recovery.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were clear, accurate, and showed good perspective-taking regarding the client’s ambivalence and values. The main limitation was that some reflections did not fully capture the emotional weight of his statements about his children, past mistakes, and whānau.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing alcohol-related risk, increasing accountability, and preparing for seeing his children. It could have more explicitly linked the final plan back to broader alcohol reduction and violence-prevention goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well in building a concrete plan and invited the client’s preferences throughout. The interaction remained somewhat therapist-led because the psychiatrist supplied many specific options and structured steps early.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported the client’s autonomy by allowing him to choose a low-pressure plan and reject options he was not ready for. The score was limited because the psychiatrist still provided a fairly high volume of concrete suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicitated some client-driven motivation and action, but the evocation remained relatively superficial. More attention to the client’s values, confidence, past successes, and reasons for change would have strengthened the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a collaborative stance and treated the client as a decision-maker in choosing his plan. It could have better emphasized that the client is the expert on his own life and what will work for his family.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered meaningful change-oriented statements, but the psychiatrist did not consistently deepen or expand them. There was a missed opportunity to explore his confidence, what “steady” means to him, and his reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by not arguing and reframing independence as a protective factor. The session could have used more double-sided reflections to bridge the tension between wanting his own space and showing accountability.
