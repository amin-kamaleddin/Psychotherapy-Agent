# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts the client’s boundaries without pressing. The stance is strong, though it could more explicitly validate the client’s emotional experience of being monitored or misunderstood.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflective listening of the client’s surface-level concerns. Deeper empathy is limited by not fully addressing feelings of being judged, misunderstood, or in need of autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target behavior and moves logically toward a concrete, manageable plan. The main limitation is repeated emphasis on external pressure, which can pull attention away from the client’s own values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting the client to choose and co-create a low-effort plan. The counselor still drives much of the problem-solving, which limits fully client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and respects the client’s limits when rejecting additional suggestions. It would be stronger if there were more explicit affirmation of the client’s ownership and right to decide the pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-driven ideas and confidence but also offers many therapist-generated options. Important moments for exploring intrinsic motivation and deeper meaning are not consistently followed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance and allows the client’s preferences to shape the plan. The session remains somewhat counselor-led in offering and organizing options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses questions that help the client express change language. However, change talk is not consistently reflected, expanded, or deepened when it appears.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client but does not consistently soften sustain talk by reflecting ambivalence and turning it toward autonomous motivation. The focus sometimes remains on avoiding external reminders rather than the client’s own reasons for change.
