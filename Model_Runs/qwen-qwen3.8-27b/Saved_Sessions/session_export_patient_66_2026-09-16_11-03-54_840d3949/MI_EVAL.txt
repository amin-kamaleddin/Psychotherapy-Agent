# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and normalizes Kareem’s ambivalence without arguing or shaming. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with limited explicit affirmation of strengths or deeper validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers accurate and some complex reflections that capture Kareem’s torn feelings and the core conflict around smoking. Empathy is solid but misses several emotional nuances and occasionally becomes repetitive or summarizing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, behaviorally relevant focus on the smoking hassle and moves logically toward a concrete plan. Direction is coherent, though it narrows toward avoiding the cold rather than the broader cut-down or quitting goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kareem helps shape the plan by choosing the gum, dose, timing, and check-in. Collaboration is strong but not fully equal because the clinician still provides much of the structure, options, and detailed instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Kareem’s freedom to choose, avoids controlling language, and presents the gum trial as an optional, low-effort experiment. This is one of the strongest aspects of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and questions about doable options elicit some change talk and commitment to try the gum. However, evocation is mostly surface-level and not sustained beyond the initial exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks permission and preferences, supporting a collaborative stance. The interaction remains partly expert-driven because Kareem mostly selects among clinician-provided options rather than leading the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are effective early attempts to evoke change talk, especially around trying a small experiment. The change talk remains limited in depth and centers mostly on trying gum rather than broader reasons for cutting down or quitting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Kareem’s sustain talk and respects his statement that he is not quitting. Sustain talk is not consistently softened because the clinician repeatedly anchors to the non-quitter identity without stronger reframing or ambivalence resolution.
