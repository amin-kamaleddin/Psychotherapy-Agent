# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s freezing and ambivalence. This could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client’s courage and values, not just the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The reflections accurately track the client’s sense of obligation, uncertainty, and fear of things falling apart. More sustained emotional exploration of these feelings, before moving into problem-solving, would deepen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target behavior: avoiding the bar on 4th and getting through Friday night. The work progresses clearly from general concern to a concrete, behaviorally specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts the plan to the client’s preferences, such as texting, going to the cousin’s, and using cold water. However, the session still feels somewhat guided, with the counselor supplying much of the structure and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses autonomy-respecting language and offers choices, but the session becomes quite directive with many suggested strategies. The client’s discomfort with feeling trapped is not directly reflected or reoriented around his right to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some intention and confidence but relies heavily on generating options rather than drawing out the client’s deeper motivations. More exploration of values, identity, and the meaning of being present for his child would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and the client’s choices clearly shape the plan, such as avoiding the bar, texting, and using the diner as a backup. The interaction is collaborative, but still counselor-guided because of the structured menu of interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is helped to identify concrete change goals, such as avoiding the bar and choosing a Friday-night alternative. Change talk remains mostly behavioral rather than connected to personal values, identity, or intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s sustain talk and responds neutrally to his reluctance. More active use of double-sided reflections would better pivot obligation and doubt toward personal reasons for change.
