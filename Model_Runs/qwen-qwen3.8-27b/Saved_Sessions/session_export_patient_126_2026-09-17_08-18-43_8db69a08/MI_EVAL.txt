# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids moralizing around alcohol. Acceptance is generally strong but could more explicitly affirm Thomas as a person, parent, and partner independent of his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Thomas’s values around being a dad, normalcy, and trust in his wife. However, the clinician does not fully explore deeper feelings underneath his minimization, such as fear, labeling concerns, or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays relatively focused on alcohol use and possible next steps. The main limitation is a repetitive, narrow return to check-ins or signals even after Thomas declines that focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Thomas’s input and frames the conversation as exploratory. Collaboration is limited by the clinician’s tendency to propose solutions or agendas before fully incorporating Thomas’s stated preference.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Language consistently emphasizes optional, low-pressure next steps and respects Thomas’s final decision. Autonomy is slightly undermined by repeated invitations to consider a check-in after Thomas says he does not want one.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses value-based questions and references Thomas’s existing safeguard with his wife. Evocation remains limited because it stays tied to a specific monitoring idea rather than broadly exploring his values, ambivalence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Thomas as an expert on his own life and avoids an authoritarian tone. Partnership is uneven because the conversation remains therapist-led toward a check-in despite Thomas’s repeated reluctance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal change talk, and the clinician does not effectively build on Thomas’s statement that he would return if something changed. More skilled evocation of desire, need, or commitment language is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Thomas’s minimization but often accepts it at face value. Softening sustain talk would benefit from double-sided reflections, externalization, or gentle reframing that honors both his view and lingering concern.
