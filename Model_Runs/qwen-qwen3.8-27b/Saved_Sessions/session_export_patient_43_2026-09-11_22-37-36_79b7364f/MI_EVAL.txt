# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect and nonjudgment through affirming statements. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic and procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate reflective listening but does not consistently capture the deeper emotional meaning behind the patient's statements. Key concerns such as defensiveness, identity, fear of weakness, and ambivalence are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking reduction and moves toward a concrete behavior such as delaying the first cigarette. The main limitation is that the direction becomes somewhat repetitive and advice-centered rather than strategically advancing the MI process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration at the beginning, especially when the patient chooses a target behavior. Collaboration becomes inconsistent later as the psychiatrist shifts into providing lists of tips and instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently communicates respect for the patient's choice and avoids direct pressure. Autonomy support is undercut by repeated prescriptive advice and specific instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some initial questions but mainly gathers factual or behavioral information rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations. There are missed opportunities to explore personal reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a reasonably collaborative stance, but the power balance shifts toward the clinician providing a plan. The patient's guardedness and objections are not fully integrated into a mutually negotiated process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are brief openings for change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently expand or deepen the patient's stated intention. After the patient mentions skipping the first cigarette, the response shifts to practical tips rather than evoking further change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk but does not effectively soften or reframe it. Responses remain neutral and do not help the patient hold both sides of his ambivalence.
