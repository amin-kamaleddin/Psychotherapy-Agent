# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed respect, nonjudgment, and affirmation without blaming Marco for past weight regain or his love of orange juice. The response could be strengthened by more explicit validation of the emotional impact of repeated weight cycling and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated accurate understanding of Marco’s goals, plan, and social context, including Karen’s role and weekend concerns. Empathy was mainly cognitive and behavioral, with less use of complex reflections for underlying feelings or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior of reducing juice intake and supporting a sustainable weight-management plan. Each step moved the work forward from identifying barriers to planning, tracking, scripts, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist worked collaboratively with Marco, incorporating his ideas about Karen, meal timing, tracking, and weekend coping into the plan. However, the therapist also generated much of the content, so deeper co-creation would further strengthen the criterion.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was supported through permission-seeking, offering choices, and encouraging Marco to try the conversation with Karen naturally. The therapist could more consistently emphasize Marco’s ownership, control, and right to modify or reject any part of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial scaling and questions evoked strong change talk, but the session shifted quickly into practical options and planning. More sustained evocation of Marco’s values, past successes, and future self-image would strengthen the score.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco’s preferences clearly shaped the plan, and the therapist invited input on wording, options, and priorities. The partnership was not fully equal because the therapist introduced many practical options and scripts relatively early.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited desire, importance, and confidence through scaling and open questions, and Marco provided strong change talk. Cultivation could be improved by more frequently expanding Marco’s own reasons, abilities, and commitment before moving into structured planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist did not argue with Marco’s sustain talk about loving orange juice and instead reframed the goal as making juice the exception rather than elimination. The score could improve with more explicit double-sided reflections that acknowledge the value of OJ while guiding toward change.
