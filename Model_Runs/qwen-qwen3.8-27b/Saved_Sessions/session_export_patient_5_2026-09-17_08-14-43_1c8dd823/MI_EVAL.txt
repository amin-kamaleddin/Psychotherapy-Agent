# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support. The tone remains warm and respectful throughout, affirming the client's values without correction or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures the client's content and emotional experience. The score is not a 5 because the clinician occasionally moves toward practical problem-solving before fully processing the client's emotional reaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication safety and the next step of communicating with the prescriber. Each intervention purposefully moves toward a concrete goal without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist genuinely invites the client to shape the conversation and co-constructs the plan. The client's ideas directly guide the session's direction and outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician respects the client's decision-making authority and avoids pressure. To reach a 5, the psychiatrist could more explicitly affirm the client's right to decide whether and when to start the medication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions are used to elicit the client's needs and preferences. However, evocation is limited in depth, as the clinician provides direct medical information before fully drawing out the client's own reasoning and intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction conveys that the client is an expert on her own life and treatment preferences. The score is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still maintains a relatively structured agenda and could more openly negotiate it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session produces some client change talk and planning language. The clinician could improve by more consistently reflecting, amplifying, or deepening the client's motivation and values-based reasons for proceeding carefully.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully reframes the client's concerns and barriers without reinforcing resistance. The score is not a 5 because the clinician occasionally provides direct medical information before fully helping the client work through the emotional impact.
