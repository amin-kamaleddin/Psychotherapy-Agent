# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect but repeatedly uses the same scripted response and subtly pathologizes normal teen behavior (e.g., 'a pattern you've gotten pretty good at'). Isabelle's clear statement that she is fine and not in crisis is never truly validated, instead being gently but persistently pushed past.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are often interpretive rather than accurate, imposing a narrative on Isabelle rather than reflecting what she actually said. The psychiatrist misses the clear boundary Isabelle is setting and the repeated identical question reveals a lack of genuine attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has poor direction—the alcohol risk that prompted the session is never mentioned once, and the conversation wanders without clear purpose or momentum. The psychiatrist appears to be fishing for something rather than working toward a specific goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal genuine collaboration is present; the psychiatrist does not incorporate Isabelle's responses to shape the conversation's direction. When Isabelle offers to talk about band season, the response is a generic 'Can you tell me more about that?' rather than engaging with the specific content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does acknowledge Isabelle's autonomy in some moments ('I'll take your word that you're fine,' 'the door's open'). However, this is undermined by the persistent questioning after Isabelle has clearly stated she has nothing to add, creating subtle pressure that contradicts the autonomy-supportive statements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is essentially no evocation in this session. Questions are primarily closed or factual in nature rather than open-ended exploratory questions that would draw out Isabelle's own reasons, values, or motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts to position Isabelle as an expert on her own life but this partnership is quickly eroded by persistent probing and the shift to extracting specific behavioral details. The interaction feels more like an interview or assessment than a partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is virtually no change talk in this session. The psychiatrist makes no effort to evoke Isabelle's own reasons for change, her values, or her goals, and never connects her stated interests (music, band, solo) to any broader motivational framework.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist inadvertently reinforces Isabelle's disengagement by repeatedly asking for more after she has clearly stated she has nothing to add. There is no skillful handling of her 'not in crisis' stance—instead it is gently but persistently pushed past in a counterproductive way.
