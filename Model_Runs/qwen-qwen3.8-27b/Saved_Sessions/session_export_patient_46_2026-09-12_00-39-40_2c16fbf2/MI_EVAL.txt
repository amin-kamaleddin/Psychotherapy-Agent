# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is nonjudgmental and normalizes concern, but warmth is limited by repeated alarming risk language. Ethan is respected more than consistently affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections capture some surface feelings such as being odd one out, but they are brief and often followed by advice. Deeper emotions like embarrassment and fear are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, which is a strength, but shifts unevenly among risk assessment, psychoeducation, and tactics. The motivational flow is interrupted by repeated safety content.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is not consistently a partnership between equals, as the psychiatrist provides structure and ready-made options. Co-creation of Ethan’s own plan is limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few autonomy-supportive questions, but frequent directive suggestions and safety instructions outweigh them. Ethan’s ownership of limits and strategies is not consistently emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial evocative questions are present, but most of the session is driven by counselor-provided risks and scripts. Ethan’s own values and motivations for change are not deeply elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying most content and strategies. Ethan’s role is mostly reactive rather than leading the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ethan offers change talk, but the psychiatrist does not systematically reflect, amplify, or ask evocative questions about it. Change talk remains mostly client-driven.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fun and fitting in is acknowledged but not softened with double-sided reflections or reframing. Risk-focused counters may inadvertently reinforce drinking as a problem.
