# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates respect, warmth, and non-judgment throughout the session, validating the client's experience without labeling or criticizing. The warmth occasionally feels slightly formulaic and could be more individually attuned to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections that effectively capture the emotional core of the client's statements (e.g., 'the one little thing that's mine'). However, some reflections stay at the paraphrase level rather than reaching full emotional depth, particularly around the client's isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear logical progression from ambivalence to alternatives to somatic awareness while staying relevant to the target behavior. The session ends somewhat open-ended without a clear consolidation point or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language, adapts to client's redirects, and acknowledges limitations, reinforcing a collaborative dynamic. However, agenda-setting remains somewhat one-directional with missed opportunities for more explicit co-creation of direction and pace.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary and consistent autonomy support woven into every interaction — never pressures, persuades, or implies quitting is the expected outcome. Immediately validates when the client pushes back, affirming her right to decide without obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks good MI questions but doesn't consistently build on or amplify the change talk that emerges. Sometimes offers interpretations and reframes that do the thinking for the client rather than letting her discover her own insights; lacks systematic use of DARN-CAT or scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with language like 'this space is yours' and follows the client's lead when she redirects. However, the partnership is somewhat clinician-driven in structure and the psychiatrist could more explicitly share power by negotiating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some attempts to evoke change talk through open questions, but the cultivation is inconsistent and not strategically deployed. Change talk that emerges is mostly spontaneous rather than amplified; the psychiatrist misses windows to build on the client's own 'if I did stop' language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating the client's perspective without reinforcing it, never arguing or creating a power struggle. Could more actively help the client see the discrepancy between her values and behavior in a self-generated way.
