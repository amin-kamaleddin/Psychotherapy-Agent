# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalization, and nonjudgment. Laura’s ambivalence, lapses, guilt, and self-critical beliefs are met with warmth and validation rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and captures both practical and emotional ambivalence. The main limitation is that the counselor sometimes reframes experience positively before fully exploring deeper feelings such as guilt, shame, burnout, and self-worth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and focused direction toward aligning daily actions with health and self-care values. The conversation moves smoothly from values to a concrete, manageable target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and patient co-construct a practical plan based on Laura’s own ideas and preferences. Collaboration is strong, though the counselor still leads the conversational structure and uses fewer explicit equal-partner phrases.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura’s choice and ownership are regularly supported through open-ended questions and client-generated planning. Some questions still subtly direct the change agenda and do not fully emphasize complete optionality.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits motivation and solutions from Laura rather than supplying them. Laura’s own values, reasons for change, and action ideas emerge clearly through open questions and attributed authorship.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura is positioned as the expert on her own life through questions that invite her ideas and solutions. Partnership is strong, though it would be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language or a negotiated agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session effectively elicits and amplifies Laura’s desire, need, reason, and intention to change. It would be stronger if it included more explicit confidence or commitment language, such as readiness or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is softened through reframing and nonjudgmental reflections rather than direct argument. The counselor could more consistently use double-sided reflections that explicitly balance Laura’s all-or-nothing beliefs with her values and prior successes.
