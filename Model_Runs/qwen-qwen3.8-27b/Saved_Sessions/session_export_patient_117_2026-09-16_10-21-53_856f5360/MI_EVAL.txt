# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, including appreciation for the journal and normalization of slips. Warmth was effective but somewhat task-focused, with fewer deeper person-centered affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections and summaries of Samuel’s patterns, preferences, and worries. Reflections were often brief and moved quickly into problem-solving, missing some deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing smoking, especially in the lunch/work context, with a logical progression from triggers to plan to check-in. The later focus on logistics and exact text wording slightly reduced the motivational core.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by using Samuel’s journal findings, cues, preferences, and chosen follow-up format. The interaction was interactive, though still partly clinician-led through multiple options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Samuel was regularly supported to choose, with permission-seeking, no-judgment language, and respect for his preferences. Some prescriptive strategies and scripts slightly reduced his self-generated ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used strong evocative questions about triggers, importance, confidence, and a doable step. The session then shifted toward advice and scripts without continuing to draw out Samuel’s own values, hopes, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Samuel’s input and allowed his preferences to shape the plan. However, the expert-driven use of multiple strategies, scripts, and specific wording made the session less mutually constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited change talk through the ruler questions and Samuel’s specific lunch plan, with some affirmation. Change talk was not consistently amplified or deepened, and the later yes/no check-in risked a compliance framing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately validated Samuel’s concerns and offered low-drama alternatives without arguing or minimizing. Responses were more problem-solving than strategic softening, with limited double-sided reflection of ambivalence.
