# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing the client's concerns. However, the tone remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm, with limited explicit affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic empathy and adjusts to the client's preferences. However, reflections often stay at a surface level and do not fully capture the client's underlying ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the referral concern and related practical steps. The main limitation is that the direction becomes more task-driven than MI-guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on scripts and client preferences. However, the clinician frequently supplies most of the content rather than drawing out the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and does not force the client to act immediately. Autonomy support is mixed, though, because much of the plan is directive rather than client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some initial questions, but the session quickly shifts to advice, scripts, and planning. The clinician makes limited effort to elicit the client's own motivations, values, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's input shapes the wording and approach, showing some collaborative responsiveness. On balance, the interaction remains more expert-driven than truly partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk appears mainly from the client rather than being skillfully evoked. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or build on the client's reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client and responds to ambivalence by adjusting language. However, MI techniques such as double-sided reflections are used infrequently, and some sustain talk is reinforced through expanded contingencies.
