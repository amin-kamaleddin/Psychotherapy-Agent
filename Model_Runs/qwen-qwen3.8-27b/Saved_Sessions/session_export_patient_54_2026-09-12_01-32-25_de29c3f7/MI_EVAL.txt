# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Isabella’s autonomy, reducing defensiveness. However, some affirmations feel formulaic or task-oriented rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of Isabella’s ambivalence, but the psychiatrist often misses deeper emotions about routine, identity, stress, and partner disappointment. Advising and problem-solving frequently interrupt emotional responsiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and ends with a concrete, client-owned first step. Some rapid topic shifts and practical advice slightly dilute the focused MI momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and collaborates on a practical final step, but often leads with expert-driven information and options. More open questions for Isabella to generate her own ideas would strengthen true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with repeated respect for Isabella’s choice and avoidance of coercive language. The large number of suggested options and scripts can still feel advice-oriented, especially after she says she is not ready for a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions that elicit Isabella’s perspective and values. However, the session often shifts into information-giving before fully exploring her intrinsic motivation, confidence, and readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership, as Isabella chooses the focus and final step. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist frequently takes on an expert-advisor role, making the interaction feel more clinician-led than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabella offers meaningful change talk about protecting the baby and making a first step. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this language with evocative, values-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk. More consistent use of double-sided reflections or value-based redirection would better soften sustain talk and pivot toward change.
