# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful and nonjudgmental tone while validating Jason’s frustration. However, acceptance is somewhat generic and occasionally normalizes the behavior rather than holding both the person and the conduct concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is basic acknowledgment of Jason’s frustration, embarrassment, and desire for a direct answer. The psychiatrist does not sufficiently explore the deeper meaning behind his defensiveness, minimization, shame, or concern about academic and residential consequences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the party incident, conduct referral, and immediate question of whether Jason can leave. However, the focus is mostly procedural and legal rather than being steered toward the target behavioral issue of alcohol-related conduct and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and invites input, showing some responsiveness to Jason’s needs. The interaction is nonetheless uneven and advisor-driven, with repeated scripts and guidance even after Jason states he can handle things himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jason’s choices are repeatedly acknowledged, especially regarding immediate decisions and whether to accept help. Autonomy support is weakened by frequent directive instructions and scripted language, which can feel controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with appropriate open-ended invitations to explore Jason’s experience and desired change. The session quickly shifts to advice and procedural guidance without eliciting or building on Jason’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jason is invited to choose topics and options, showing some awareness of partnership. The psychiatrist nonetheless assumes an expert/legal-advisor role and does not consistently co-construct next steps with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few early opportunities to invite change talk, but they are not followed up with deeper MI-consistent questions or reflections. Jason’s statements about not being a repeat offender, having classes, and wanting to go home are not used to build a case for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Jason’s minimizations and does not dwell on his sustain talk. The response is not fully MI-consistent, as the counselor mostly redirects to practical steps rather than using reflections or rolling with resistance.
