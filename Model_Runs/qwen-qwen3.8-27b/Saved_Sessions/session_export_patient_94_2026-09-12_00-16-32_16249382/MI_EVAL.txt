# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, patience, and nonjudgment, framing the issue as a medical problem rather than personal failure. The tone occasionally shifts toward corrective guidance due to repeated medical instructions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician makes brief reflections about the client’s time pressure and resistance to medication. However, deeper concerns about failing family and feeling pushed by the system are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on asthma management, inhaler use, and safety thresholds. The focus is sometimes narrow, remaining more on medical safety than on values or change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration through optional next steps and respect for the client’s refusals. The psychiatrist remains the primary source of information and recommendations, limiting true shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician respects the client’s choices and avoids overt coercion. Repeated offers to text, track, or plan can still feel pressuring despite the polite tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies heavily on providing information and safety instructions rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations. Important values such as work, family, and not failing are not systematically explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with collaborative invitations and allows the client to decline suggestions. The overall stance quickly becomes expert-driven, with the client’s concerns shaping the direction only minimally.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician rarely picks up the client’s openings for change, such as wanting to protect work or family. Instead of evoking the client’s own reasons for change, the response offers external scripts and directives.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s resistance with brief reflections such as “I hear you.” However, sustain talk is mostly met with repeated medical facts rather than effective reframing or exploration.
