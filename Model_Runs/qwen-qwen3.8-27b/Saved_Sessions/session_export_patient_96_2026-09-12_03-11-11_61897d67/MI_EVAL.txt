# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepted Marisol’s perspective without moralizing. Warmth was somewhat procedural, and repeated risk reminders subtly implied concern after she declined more information.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening tracked Marisol’s stated position accurately at a content level. Empathy remained more factual than emotionally nuanced, with limited exploration of her values, independence, and discomfort with being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the lip piercing, oral health concerns, and a practical follow-up plan. Direction became mildly repetitive in the middle, with similar tips repeated after Marisol indicated she did not want more reminders.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was a collaborative structure, with options offered and Marisol’s informal plan incorporated. Collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist frequently introduced specific advice and risk-management content before fully eliciting Marisol’s own priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used choice-based language and affirmed Marisol’s right to keep the piercing. Autonomy support was slightly weakened by repeated offers after Marisol had declined them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some confidence and a practical step using scaling questions. Evocation was limited by a strong focus on risk detection and practical compliance rather than Marisol’s own values, meaning, or deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and allowed Marisol’s responses to shape the final plan. The power sharing remained uneven because the psychiatrist supplied much of the agenda and specific health information.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marisol offered some change-related statements, such as asking the piercer about flat-back titanium and defining when she would call the dentist. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, expand, or build on her own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted Marisol’s sustain talk without arguing or rejecting it. Advanced MI techniques to reframe or soften sustain talk were limited, and repeated risk reminders kept the focus on warning signs rather than her autonomy.
