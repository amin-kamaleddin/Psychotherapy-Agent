### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Tyler’s ambivalence without blaming him. Some language, however, remains problem-focused around safety, control, and future consequences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses complex reflections to accurately capture Tyler’s conflict between social belonging and autonomy. Deeper emotional themes such as shame, embarrassment, or fear related to the incident are underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays clearly focused on alcohol use in social situations and progresses logically through possible action steps. The focus becomes somewhat narrow on logistics rather than consistently connecting the plan to deeper goals and discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the session around Tyler’s ideas rather than prescribing a plan or lecturing. The clinician still structures the agenda and steers toward a specific friend cue/cutoff approach, limiting fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tyler’s choice is strongly emphasized, and the psychiatrist avoids commanding or imposing a strict plan. Some leading questions and “safety plan” language still subtly push toward a specific solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts to elicit motivation from Tyler through open and hypothetical questions. Evocation remains somewhat superficial and implementation-focused, not consistently drawing out deeper values, self-efficacy, or broader change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Tyler to generate his own plan and affirms ownership of his ideas. The session still follows a fairly clinician-guided sequence, so collaboration is strong but not fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked around avoiding police involvement and protecting his GPA. The focus remains more on practical implementation than on developing intrinsic motivation, confidence, or values-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or moralizing and acknowledges Tyler’s reasons for continuing to drink socially. Much of the session, however, remains centered on barriers to asking for support rather than using advanced softening strategies to reduce sustain talk.
