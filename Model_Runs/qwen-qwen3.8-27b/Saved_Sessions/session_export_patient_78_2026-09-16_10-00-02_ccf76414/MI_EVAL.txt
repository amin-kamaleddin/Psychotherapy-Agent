# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment, pressure, or moralizing. Lucía's resistance and choices are met with respect and validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Lucía's perspective on stress, control, and failure. Emotional nuance could be deeper, especially around her fear of being judged or in trouble.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on smoking reduction, with a clear end goal of a low-effort gum plan. Focus occasionally drifts into logistics, reducing momentum toward deeper change motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and adapts to Lucía's preferences rather than imposing a plan. Collaboration could be stronger by using more open questions before providing structured choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated invitations, acceptance of refusal, and noncoercive language. Lucía's sense of control and choice is consistently preserved.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks some evocative questions about smoking and change. However, motivation is not deeply explored, and the session moves too quickly into practical problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative, client-led stance is evident, with Lucía's preferences shaping the plan. The partnership could be strengthened by more explicit affirmation of her expertise in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerges, but it is not consistently amplified, reflected, or consolidated. More strategic follow-up questions would help strengthen Lucía's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Lucía's sustain talk and maintains a nonconfrontational stance. Use of double-sided reflections and more active redirection toward change would improve this criterion.
