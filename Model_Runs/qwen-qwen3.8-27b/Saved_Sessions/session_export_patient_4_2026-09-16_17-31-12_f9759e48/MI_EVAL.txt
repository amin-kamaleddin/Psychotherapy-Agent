# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance. This affirms Aizada's experience and protects her dignity throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows accurate and consistent perspective-taking by reflecting Aizada's dilemma. The main limitation is that the empathy remains somewhat procedural and does not fully explore deeper emotional material.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear target and logical progression toward a concrete, client-owned next step. The limitation is that the focus stays narrow on obtaining the referral/scope rather than broader change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and co-constructs the next step with Aizada. The interaction is not expert-driven, though the psychiatrist occasionally leads by offering a polished sentence before fully exploring the client's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is very strong, with repeated emphasis on Aizada's choice and ownership of the process. The psychiatrist avoids coercive language and respects her right to frame the request as facts rather than feelings.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocation well by asking open questions that draw out Aizada's own concerns, priorities, and plan. The evocation is not perfect because it stays mostly at the level of practical problem-solving rather than deeply eliciting confidence, values, or broader change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that Aizada is the expert on her own experience and invites her input throughout. It is not a perfect 5 because the psychiatrist sometimes supplies a fairly complete drafted sentence before fully eliciting Aizada's own phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client-generated change language around taking concrete action and articulating a plan. However, the work is mainly logistical rather than motivational, with limited evocation of confidence, desire, values, or broader change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Aizada or dismiss her insistence on biomedical explanations, but the session remains heavily centered on obtaining tests. The sustain talk around fear, testing, and biomedical certainty is acknowledged but not effectively softened or expanded.
