# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, validating Mateo's exhaustion and fear of relapse without moralizing. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and symptom-focused rather than deeply person-centered, especially around re-entry and identity concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate early reflections, such as acknowledging how draining a “loud head” at night is, and some clarifying questions. Empathy remains surface-level and inconsistent, with limited reflection of deeper emotions like shame, fear, guilt, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on sleep after Mateo identifies it as the most urgent issue, which prevents wandering. However, it does not consistently connect sleep improvement to the broader goal of reducing recidivism or avoiding “messing up again.”

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo is asked for preferences, but the psychiatrist largely drives the content by introducing multiple strategies, a template, and repeated instructions. The interaction feels more clinician-led than genuinely collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo is repeatedly offered choices, allowed to modify the plan, and not pressured to commit to a larger behavior-change plan. Autonomy support is strong, though slightly undercut by the amount of clinician-directed advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo's motivation is present but not systematically elicited or expanded through values-based or confidence-building questions. The psychiatrist tends to supply practical solutions rather than draw out the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some partnership cues, such as asking what feels most urgent or doable, but the session quickly becomes expert-driven. The psychiatrist largely determines the intervention rather than sharing power with Mateo.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo offers meaningful change statements, such as wanting to “not mess up again” and being “tired of being tired,” but these are not actively reflected, amplified, or expanded. The psychiatrist uses only generic affirmations and does not consistently build on the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge Mateo's ambivalence and accepts his concerns about the plan feeling like homework. However, there is limited skilled use of double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of the values underneath the sustain talk.
