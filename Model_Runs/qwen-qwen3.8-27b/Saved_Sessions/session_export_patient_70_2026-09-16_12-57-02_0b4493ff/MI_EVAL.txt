# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with frequent affirmations of Lars’s progress and responsibility. Affirmations were supportive but somewhat formulaic and focused more on the plan than deeper individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflective listening and captured key concerns related to the Friday drinking plan. However, reflections stayed mostly cognitive and task-focused, with less exploration of underlying emotions such as fear, guilt, restlessness, or identity-related ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was highly focused and logically sequenced around a single target behavior: avoiding Friday drinking. Each question, scaling exercise, and planning step moved clearly toward a concrete, actionable plan without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist framed the work as joint and allowed Lars to generate most of the plan components, such as the car drive, DND strategy, saved text, and beer removal. At times the counselor offered specific options or examples, which slightly shifted the interaction toward guiding rather than fully co-creating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Lars’s choice and ownership through questions that invited his preferences and by respecting his desire to manage the plan without being policed. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing his right to change his mind and to learn from setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out Lars’s own motivations, ideas, and commitments using confidence scaling, open-ended questions, and planning prompts. Evocation was strong at the tactical level but could have been deeper in exploring values, identity, and future self.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited Lars’s input throughout, with Lars’s ideas strongly shaping the session. Some counselor-provided options and suggestions were helpful but slightly reduced the egalitarian balance in moments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evoked and built on Lars’s change talk, including desire, reasons, and need for change, while tracking confidence increases from 4 to 9. The evocation was skillful but could have more explicitly targeted additional change talk categories such as ability or hope.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk related to the “itch” to see the boys, boredom, and uncertainty about Friday. However, the response often moved quickly toward problem-solving rather than using double-sided reflections or reframing to fully reduce the pull of the old behavior.
