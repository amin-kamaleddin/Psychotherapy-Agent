# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming while honoring the client's stated preferences and boundaries. However, the warmth is somewhat formulaic and more task-based than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of the client's values and barriers, especially early in the session. However, empathy becomes increasingly task-focused and misses opportunities to explore underlying discomfort, self-doubt, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior and builds a concrete, goal-directed plan. The focus is specific, behavior-based, and consistently tied to the client's stated goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear collaborative quality because the client's choices shape the final plan. However, the psychiatrist often leads with structured options and ready-made scripts rather than first eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice through preference questions and respects the client's limits on reminders and visibility. However, the directive use of scripts and structured options reduces the impression that the plan emerges entirely from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes internal motivation by asking about values and readiness at the start. However, much of the later session involves selecting from clinician-generated options rather than eliciting deeper motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance and invites the client's input throughout. However, the session is still driven by the clinician's structured menu of options rather than fully equal co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes useful change talk early by exploring values and readiness. However, the session shifts quickly into practical planning and does not consistently amplify or deepen the client's change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's barriers and redirects them into practical solutions. However, the responses rely more on problem-solving than on MI-style reflections that soften or transform sustain talk.
