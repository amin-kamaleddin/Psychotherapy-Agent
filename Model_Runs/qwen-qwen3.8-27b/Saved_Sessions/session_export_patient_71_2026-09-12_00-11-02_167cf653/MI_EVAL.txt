# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance, but the posture quickly becomes more instructive and procedural. There is sufficient warmth to score this high, though not fully individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate but inconsistent empathy through reflections of frustration, skepticism, and normalizing all-or-nothing thinking. Deeper emotional and identity-level concerns are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a clear, behaviorally specific focus on movement consistency and a concrete two-minute desk stretch. Direction is strong but more implementation-focused than motivation-clarifying.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s ideas clearly shape the plan, but the psychiatrist often supplies most of the solution architecture. Collaboration is present but uneven and more counselor-led than equal-partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly asks for preference and permission, and supports the client’s choice to keep the plan simple and flexible. This is strong, but not consistently autonomy-centered because of the amount of directive advice offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area, as the psychiatrist often supplies rationales and explanations for change instead of eliciting the client’s own motivation. There are few evocative questions about values, desire, ability, or future self.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates the client’s contributions but often assumes an expert planner and problem-solving role. Power balance tilts toward the counselor’s knowledge rather than true power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some planning-related client input and elicits change language, but does not consistently deepen or amplify it. There is limited strategic follow-up to expand desire, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by normalizing resistance and reframing small actions as meaningful. There is some over-structure that could add pressure, but overall sustain talk is redirected effectively.
