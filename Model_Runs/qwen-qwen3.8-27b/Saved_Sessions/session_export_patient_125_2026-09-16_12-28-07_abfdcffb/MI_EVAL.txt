# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, using validating language to keep the conversation from becoming punitive. However, acceptance is limited by repeatedly tying Tariq’s worth or respect to being compliant and rule-abiding, which reduces unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Tariq’s anger, hurt, and frustration accurately enough to avoid dismissal. The main limitation is that empathy remains mostly at the surface level of the phone issue rather than exploring deeper emotions such as powerlessness, fear of being seen as a troublemaker, or concern about family connection before release.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the immediate issue of obtaining or arranging the supervised call without wandering into unrelated topics. Its limitation is that the direction becomes overly tactical, concentrating on exact phrases, denial responses, and escalation steps rather than connecting the behavior to Tariq’s broader goals, values, and release preparation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration because the psychiatrist and Tariq co-edit wording, and Tariq’s objections are heard and incorporated. However, the interaction remains uneven because the psychiatrist is still the main source of the plan and language, making the process feel more coach-led than genuinely equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by offering choices, respecting Tariq’s preferences, and not forcing him to accept specific scripts. The main limitation is that the support remains somewhat procedural because the counselor still supplies many structured lines and plans, which can reduce Tariq’s sense that he is the primary source of his own strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area of the session. The psychiatrist begins with an evocative question but quickly shifts into advice and script-giving, with little effort to elicit Tariq’s own values, reasons for change, confidence, or internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates some partnership by offering options, asking Tariq to choose, and adapting when he rejects certain phrases. However, the interaction remains partly expert-led because the counselor drives much of the agenda and supplies the specific scripts, plans, and fallback lines rather than fully helping Tariq identify his own goals and preferred direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tariq produces some meaningful change talk, such as following rules, wanting to understand the situation, and caring about his mom. The psychiatrist does not consistently cultivate or amplify this language, shifting instead into scripting and problem-solving rather than deepening Tariq’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk and occasionally redirects toward practical steps and fairness. However, the counselor still spends considerable time focusing on the officer, denial scenarios, and possible blame, without consistently using double-sided reflections to soften the conflict and invite change talk.
