# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout, with zero labeling or minimization. The psychiatrist gracefully handles corrections and explicitly protects the client from self-judgment (e.g., 'The pain is real, and I'm not asking you to take that away from yourself').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions, including nuanced positions like 'you're not saying the pain isn't real. You're saying maybe the attention on it gives it more power than it needs to have.' The psychiatrist genuinely sits with the client's metaphors and amplifies their meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session moves coherently from concerns through impact, values, a key insight, readiness, to a concrete self-generated action step. Each question builds logically toward the next, and the session ends with an ideal MI endpoint—a client-generated commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with joint agenda-setting and positioning the client as interpreter of her own experience. Could be elevated by more explicit co-construction language (e.g., 'Let's figure this out together') rather than a skilled guide/elicitor dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support—the walk idea emerges entirely from the client, and the psychiatrist consistently uses her criteria ('What would feel possible, not perfect?', 'What would tell you it was worth doing?'). Explicit attribution of ownership: 'you built that yourself, and it's on your own terms.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every insight in the session comes from the client—zero advice or external justification for change is offered. The psychiatrist's questions are skillfully sequenced to draw out what the client already knows, producing rich client-generated change talk throughout.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with open questions positioning the client as authority on her experience. Could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language (e.g., 'You're the expert on your body') to fully elevate the power dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rich and varied change talk is elicited—desire, need, ability, and commitment language all emerge naturally. The psychiatrist could more deliberately reinforce and amplify the strongest change talk moments (e.g., 'I can't keep sitting here waiting') as markers of readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without arguing or dismissing—fear of cancer is validated while not reinforcing the push for tests. Could be elevated by more explicit double-sided reflections that honor both sides simultaneously before gently redirecting.
