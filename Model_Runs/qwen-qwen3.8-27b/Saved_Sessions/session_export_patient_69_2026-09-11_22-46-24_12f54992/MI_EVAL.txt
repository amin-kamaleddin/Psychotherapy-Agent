# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Kieran’s frustration and sense of obligation. However, the acceptance remains transactional and does not fully acknowledge the deeper feelings of powerlessness or being trapped in a mandatory process.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some surface-level understanding but misses several emotionally meaningful themes, such as feeling disrespected, burdened, and resentful about systemic failures. Reflections are often generic and do not accurately capture Kieran’s underlying emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on the presenting issue and keeps the discussion tied to a concrete outcome. The direction is largely administrative and problem-solving oriented, but it effectively maintains momentum around the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the psychiatrist uses options and invitations, the process remains largely counselor-directed with the therapist controlling the format and supplying the plan. Kieran functions more as a respondent than an equal partner in shaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices and inviting Kieran to select options, which is helpful given his initial disengagement. However, autonomy support becomes inconsistent when the therapist shifts into more directive language and offers to “do the minimal work for you.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits a specific goal by asking what “done” would look like, which is a useful move. Beyond that, the session quickly shifts to providing solutions and instructions rather than exploring Kieran’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a minimal collaborative structure by offering choices, but the overall stance remains counselor-directed. Kieran’s contributions are limited to selecting predefined administrative options rather than shaping the session through his own values and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited change talk elicited, mainly when Kieran states a concrete goal for resolution. The psychiatrist does not build on this by evoking deeper motivation or reinforcing Kieran’s own desire for closure.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Kieran’s sustain talk but does not actively soften it or explore the ambivalence underneath. Instead, the therapist tends to accept the “that’s on them” framing and return to administrative problem-solving.
