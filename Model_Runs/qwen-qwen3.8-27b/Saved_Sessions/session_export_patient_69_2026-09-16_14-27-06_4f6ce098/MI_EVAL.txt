# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains warmth, normalization, and nonjudgment throughout. The score is limited by repeated use of “I hear you” after Kieran explicitly asked not to be met with that phrase.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture logistical frustration and surface tiredness but miss deeper emotions such as powerlessness, fear, and resentment. The repeated reliance on “I hear you” can feel mechanical rather than deeply attuned.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing friction and building toward a concrete, workable agreement. Pacing becomes slightly over-guided after Kieran establishes his limit for brevity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a clear plan, with Kieran defining the goal, format, limits, and signal. Strong, but not flawless because the counselor continues to manage pace and phrasing despite Kieran’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, respects the stop signal, and avoids pressure. Repeating “I hear you” and asking multiple questions after Kieran asks for brevity can subtly undermine his stated preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful open questions invite Kieran to define helpful next steps, but the evocation remains mostly procedural. It does not consistently elicit deeper values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership is shown by letting Kieran shape the session, limits, and communication rules. The score is held at 4 because the psychiatrist does not fully honor the requested brevity and phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked around engagement, presence, and reduced burden. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify the change talk into stronger reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and reframes resistance into clear boundaries and a workable structure. A 5 would require more active pivoting toward values-based reasons for engagement.
