# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and includes affirming statements such as "That makes sense" and "Good plan." However, warmth is somewhat task-focused, and repeated framing of the client as not seeing a problem can subtly imply denial.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers useful reflections of frustration, ambivalence, and practical concerns, especially around the license and social pressure. However, empathy often moves quickly into problem-solving and misses deeper emotional themes such as shame, fear, loss, or failed sobriety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of avoiding another DUI and protecting the client's license. The discussion moves logically from ambivalence to a concrete, practical plan, with only mild drift early in the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and incorporates some of the client's practical ideas into the final plan. However, collaboration is limited by the frequent use of pre-formed options, scripts, and structured steps that can feel more expert-led than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive phrases and confidence-building questions, and the client does make meaningful choices. However, the session is advice-heavy, with many suggestions supplied that may undermine the client's sense of being in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions about goals, barriers, confidence, and next steps, which elicit some client-driven planning. However, evocation is limited because the psychiatrist often supplies options and strategies rather than consistently drawing out the client's own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves the client in planning and follows some of the client's preferences, such as a low-key approach and a single sober night. However, the counselor-driven style emerges when multiple options and scripts are offered before fully inviting the client to lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk related to protecting the license, avoiding another DUI, and making a realistic plan. However, the client's own reasons, values, and confidence are not consistently amplified or deeply explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tolerates sustain talk and avoids arguing, using language such as "you don't see yourself as having a problem." However, there is limited active softening through double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of the ambivalence between social connection and legal risk.
