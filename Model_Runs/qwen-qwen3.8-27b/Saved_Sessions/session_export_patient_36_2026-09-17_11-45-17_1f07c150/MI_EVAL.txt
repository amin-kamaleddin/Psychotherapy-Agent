# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard throughout. Affirmations are specific and individualized (e.g., 'That's self-respect in action, not just on paper') rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections capture the emotional texture and meaning behind the client's words, not just content. The body-based question and progressive deepening of reflections demonstrate exceptional empathic accuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, coherent thread from values clarification to concrete behavioral plan. Could have been slightly sharper in the initial framing of the target behavior through a brief collaborative agenda-setting moment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth where the psychiatrist builds on the client's own language ('room,' 'pause,' 'old script') rather than introducing clinical frameworks. The power dynamic is genuinely egalitarian.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's ownership and agency (e.g., 'You did that yourself, out of your own values work'). The session ends with the client generating an entirely self-authored plan and anchor phrase.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All questions are designed to draw out the client's own thinking and motivation rather than introducing external reasons for change. The client generates all key insights, reframes, and the behavioral plan independently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert and agent of her own change. Questions are open-ended and directed at the client's experience, never asking for agreement or offering clinical interpretations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies the client's own change language with slight intensification (e.g., 'That's self-respect in action, not just on paper'). The session ends with the client articulating a concrete behavioral plan in her own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk given the client's low ambivalence, but when present, the psychiatrist gently explores its function rather than arguing against it. A small missed opportunity for a double-sided reflection when the client noted her desire to be a 'good person' wasn't going away.
