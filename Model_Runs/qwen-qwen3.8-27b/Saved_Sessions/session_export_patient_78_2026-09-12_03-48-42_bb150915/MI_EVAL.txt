# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard throughout much of the session. However, repeated movement into practical problem-solving occasionally undermines the consistency of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and emotionally attuned, especially in validating the private meaning of smoking. Deeper feelings of loneliness, exhaustion, and self-protective meaning are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to smoking, but the focus drifts toward managing social friction rather than ambivalence about cessation. The psychiatrist does not consistently return to the target behavior or guide the conversation toward values or health concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are attempts to involve the client through choice and preference-based questions. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist often provides the content of the options rather than building on the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s right to choose and avoids commanding or lecturing language. Some techniques, such as the ruler question and repeated suggestions, may still feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions that elicit meaningful self-reflection about the function of smoking. The evoked material is not consistently followed into deeper exploration of motivation, values, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks for preferences, demonstrating some collaborative stance. Partnership is weakened when the client repeatedly rejects the options and the psychiatrist continues providing scripts or actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses some change-relevant language, but the psychiatrist does not consistently elicit or amplify it. The session often shifts toward external suggestions rather than the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is present but not effectively redirected or balanced. Several suggestions risk reinforcing the client’s current smoking behavior by reducing social friction rather than exploring ambivalence about change.
