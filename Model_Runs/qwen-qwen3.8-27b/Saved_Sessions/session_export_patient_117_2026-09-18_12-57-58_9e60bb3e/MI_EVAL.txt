# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, from normalizing the discomfort of journaling to the masterful 'I'm not going to tell you it's small or not small.' No moment of judgment, labeling, or disapproval occurs in the entire session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Empathic reflections are remarkably accurate, specific, and emotionally resonant, consistently capturing complex layers of the client's experience (e.g., the 'pause missing its other half' reflection). The psychiatrist attends to the client's own surprise at his language and validates emotional weight without amplifying it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear strategic arc from journal review → trigger identification → alternative behavior → values connection → concrete action plan, all while allowing the client to move at his own pace. The session ends with a specific, client-generated action plan directly tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration between equals, with the psychiatrist consistently building on the client's contributions rather than redirecting. The final plan (walking in the grass strip) is entirely Samuel's idea, facilitated but not directed by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces autonomy through language like 'You don't have to. You're just... trying' and 'You don't owe anyone a twenty-year solution.' The client's closing statement is allowed to stand without added commentary, respecting his self-determined decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never provides a single external reason for change; every insight, motivation, and action step is elicited from within Samuel. Questions are designed to access his own values, identity, and internal landscape, resulting in all change talk being client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on his own experience, using language like 'What do you think?' as genuine invitations and explicitly deferring judgment to Samuel. The psychiatrist never provides information, suggests interventions, or positions themselves as having knowledge the client lacks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully notices and amplifies change talk, connecting surface-level plans to deeper values (e.g., linking the walk to the bench image of chasing his son). Successfully deepens change talk from behavioral change ('cutting down at work') to identity-level meaning ('a minute's all I've got').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with textbook double-sided reflections that honor both sides without arguing or minimizing (e.g., 'Both of those are true at once'). Never pushes back against sustain talk; the session ends with the client's own 'I'll try again tomorrow' left standing without concern or correction.
