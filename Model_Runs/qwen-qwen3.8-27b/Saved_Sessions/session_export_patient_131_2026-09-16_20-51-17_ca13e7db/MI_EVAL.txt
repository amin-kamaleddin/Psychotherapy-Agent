# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Wiremu’s difficulty balancing independence with safety. Not a 5 because acceptance is more tied to his efforts than explicit unconditional regard, especially around violence and family shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate, helpful reflections that track Wiremu’s perspective, independence concerns, and practical needs. Less a 5 because it does not deeply explore the emotional pain behind missing his children, past violence, or family shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, clinically purposeful focus on alcohol use, safe living, Stopping Violence, and reconnection with his children. Each turn moves toward a concrete, relevant next step without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner, co-constructing the boundary conversation and relapse plan with Wiremu. The session feels like shared problem-solving rather than clinician-led advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports Wiremu’s right to choose, emphasizing manageable options, independence, and ownership. Language avoids pressure, directives, or moralizing, reinforcing that the decision is his.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Wiremu by asking open questions about values, boundaries, and coping. Reflects and amplifies his own change-oriented language rather than supplying external reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions Wiremu as the expert on his own life, needs, and alcohol goals. The session is shaped by his contributions rather than the psychiatrist assuming the expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently evokes and builds on Wiremu’s values, commitments, and self-identified reasons for change. Less systematic in eliciting full change talk, including confidence and ability, and at times steers toward a specific plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles concerns about independence well by validating them and reframing the plan as a short, controllable trial. Not fully resolved, especially lacking explicit double-sided reflection between independence and safety for his children.
