# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent nonjudgmental warmth and validation of Dante’s ambivalence. Statements like “It counts” and “You made it as light as you could” preserve positive regard without imposing disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, nuanced reflections that capture both practical strain and deeper emotional concerns. Reflections about the “trash” feeling and fear of proof of failure show strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on increasing physical activity and building a sustainable routine. The psychiatrist strategically narrows the target from walking to shoes, five minutes, standing/stretching, and an end-of-day cue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely collaborative, with Dante’s ideas driving the progression. However, frequent questions and interpretive summaries create a slightly therapist-led rhythm.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Dante’s agency by emphasizing choice, small steps, and in-the-moment decisions. A few phrases such as “I think you already know that” are slightly leading and reduce full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Dante’s own motivation, values, and self-compassionate language. Questions about what would feel enough help Dante generate his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a collaborative stance and lets Dante’s contributions shape the session. Occasional therapist-supplied interpretations, however, slightly reduce the impression that Dante is fully authoring the meaning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and reinforces Dante’s own change talk, including walking, shoes, five minutes, standing, and self-affirmation. Affirmations such as “It counts” strengthen client-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids argument and reframes sustain talk without reinforcing resistance. The final worry about the “old voice” is not fully redirected into a concrete, change-oriented resolution.
