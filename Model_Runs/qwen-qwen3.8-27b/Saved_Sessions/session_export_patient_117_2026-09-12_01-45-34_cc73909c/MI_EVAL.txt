# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, with a supportive tone throughout. The warmth is somewhat formulaic and does not consistently show deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Responses are mostly advice, normalization, and task-focused planning rather than reflective listening. There are missed opportunities to accurately reflect Samuel’s frustration, self-doubt, ambivalence, and the emotional meaning of his concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on reducing smoking in specific high-risk work situations. Some repeated summaries and checklists slightly dilute the momentum but do not cause major drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samuel’s choices shape the final plan, which supports a collaborative stance. However, much of the session is counselor-driven, with many options and repeated checklists supplied by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses choice-oriented questions, respects Samuel’s simpler plan, and avoids pressure or coercion. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly emphasizing that Samuel has full control over what he tries and how.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative moves, such as asking which option feels doable and encouraging a small experiment. The session misses opportunities to elicit Samuel’s intrinsic motivations, values, confidence, and deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Samuel’s input and builds the plan around his preferences. The early expert/teaching role and large list of strategies make the power balance uneven at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces progress and invites small commitments, supporting some change talk. The change talk is not systematically developed through questions about reasons, values, ability, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Helpful reframes such as “slips are data” reduce the impact of failure-focused thinking. Some of Samuel’s stronger sustain talk about past failures and doubt is not fully reflected, reframed, or resolved.
