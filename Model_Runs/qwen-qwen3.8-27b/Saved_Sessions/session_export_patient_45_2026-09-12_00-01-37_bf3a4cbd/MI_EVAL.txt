# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and validating, though somewhat generic and quickly moves into advice. Warm and nonjudgmental but not deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections of frustration and confusion, but remains surface-level. Misses deeper reflections on fear of judgment, lifelong medication, and authority anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and task-focused on diabetes, drinking, diet, and medication decision. Drifts toward medical/logistical direction rather than staying motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and co-creates some plan elements, but the psychiatrist supplies much of the content. The interaction becomes more clinician-led than equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges choices and frames the trial permissively, but then gives specific instructions and a directive next step. This weakens Erik's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited evocative questioning and mostly advisory after the early part. Does not effectively elicit Erik's own values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for input and suggests working together, but assumes an expert-driven role with scripts and plans. The client becomes more of a receiver than equal contributor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Contains sparse affirmations and questions but does not amplify Erik's spontaneous change talk. Misses opportunities to deepen confidence or motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges ambivalence without judgment but does not skillfully examine downsides of sustaining. Advisory redirection may reinforce the status quo.
