# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Naomi's symptoms and effort. It is strong but occasionally procedural or moves quickly into planning before fully sitting with her vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is good reflective listening and accurate reflection of the physical and functional impact of anxiety. Empathy could deepen by more directly reflecting underlying emotions such as fear of judgment, embarrassment, or helplessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on anxiety during photography shoots and public settings. There is strong momentum from exploring the recent experience to identifying a target change and building an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is developed collaboratively through Naomi's choices, which reduces a prescriptive feel. However, the therapist still generates most of the options and techniques rather than fully co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses choice-based language, respects Naomi's pace, and supports small, manageable commitments. It would be stronger if the therapist more explicitly affirmed that Naomi is in charge and that choosing not to try is also valid.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurs through readiness, preference, and desired-outcome questions. The therapist frequently supplies techniques and rationales, limiting opportunities to draw out Naomi's values, confidence, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a collaborative stance by inviting Naomi's input and allowing her choices to shape the intervention. It remains partly therapist-guided because the counselor occupies an expert role in selecting techniques and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some change talk, such as wanting steadier hands and being able to see the shot. The therapist does not consistently amplify or follow up on this language to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through normalization and smaller, doable steps. The therapist could more consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to hold both sides of ambivalence.
