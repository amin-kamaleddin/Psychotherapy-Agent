# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms Amina’s right to control the conversation. However, continued questions and offers after Amina asks to be left alone partially undermine the acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable efforts to reflect Amina’s perspective, but the empathy remains mostly surface-level and repetitive. Deeper exploration of the meaning behind Amina’s defensiveness, such as privacy, control, or distrust, is limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on Amina’s home environment and caseworker involvement, but the direction is inconsistent and lacks a clear strategic next step. Movement between options, reassurance, and crisis information dilutes the focus on initial engagement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites input, but the session remains more therapist-managed than truly co-created. Amina’s minimal responses do not sufficiently shape the pace, content, or ending of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy-supportive language is frequent and appropriate, especially for a resistant adolescent. Yet behavior does not always match the words, as the therapist continues offers and asks a safety question after Amina requests space.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes an early evocative effort with open questions, but the session does not develop into consistent evocation of Amina’s values or reasons for change. Gentle, low-pressure techniques to draw out even minimal change talk are underused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys some partnership by affirming Amina’s control and offering choices, but the interaction remains uneven and therapist-directed. Amina’s minimal words do not fully shape the session’s pace or structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions are appropriate MI-style invitations, but the therapist does not effectively develop or build on any emerging change talk. The session relies more on therapist statements and repeated options than on evoking Amina’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with Amina’s resistance and apologizes when she feels pushed. However, the therapist mostly backs off rather than using more advanced techniques to reframe or create a pathway toward change.
