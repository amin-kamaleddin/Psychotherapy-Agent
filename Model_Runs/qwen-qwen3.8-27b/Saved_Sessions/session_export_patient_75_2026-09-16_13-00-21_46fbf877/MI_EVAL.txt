# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes concerns, but moves quickly from vulnerable emotional disclosures into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Captures ambivalence and practical concerns through strong reflections, though later fears of judgment and failure are not deeply reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on asthma management and inhaler adherence, but shifts into practical planning before fully exploring core ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites client input and adapts to preferences, but the counselor generates most of the options, scripts, and plan components.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses permission-seeking, choice, and experiment framing, but could more explicitly reinforce the client’s ultimate decision-making authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some open questions and confidence ratings, but does not consistently draw out the client’s values, self-efficacy, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and adapts to the client’s needs, but the client mostly selects among counselor-provided options rather than generating her own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and self-evaluation, but often moves to advice and practical planning before amplifying the client’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and uses a double-sided reflection, but often responds with direct information rather than helping resolve ambivalence.
