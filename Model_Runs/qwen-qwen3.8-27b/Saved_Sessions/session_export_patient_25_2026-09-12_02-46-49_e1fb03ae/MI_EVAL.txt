# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates strengths and the patient’s concern about not being pathologized. Acceptance is strong but sometimes formulaic and task-focused rather than deeply affirming the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture cognitive and practical concerns, but often stay at a factual level. Deeper emotional nuances such as fear, guilt, vulnerability, and social pressure are not consistently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of unplanned social drinking and its consequences. Focus is strong, though occasional rapid planning mode can feel slightly agenda-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient’s choices meaningfully shape the plan, and the counselor works with the patient’s preferred language. The main limitation is that the counselor still supplies many suggestions before fully eliciting patient-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor emphasizes choice, avoids coercion, and respects the patient’s boundaries about not wanting a big deal. Autonomy is well supported, though some suggestions are delivered in a somewhat directive manner.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions and the confidence scale help the patient express goals and commit to a plan. Later advice-heavy responses reduce the counselor’s ability to consistently draw out and expand the patient’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient participates in selecting strategies, but the counselor frequently offers multiple options before inviting additional patient-generated ideas. This makes the interaction somewhat counselor-led rather than fully partner-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits several useful change statements about being present for his daughter and avoiding negative consequences. The key limitation is a lack of consistent deepening or amplification of this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor handles sustain talk without judgment and acknowledges the social and relaxing appeal of drinking. However, the counselor does not consistently use double-sided reflections or help weigh costs and benefits toward change.
