# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, unconditional positive regard with specific, individualized affirmations grounded in what the client actually did rather than generic praise. No labeling, disapproval, or conditional regard at any point in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking that captures both content and emotional nuance, including the complex ambivalence and the session's emotional arc in a single evocative closing reflection. Reflections validate the client's own agency in generating insights rather than attributing them to the therapist.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and consistent focus on the target behavior with zero topic drift throughout. The session follows a logical progressive arc building momentum from contemplation to emerging readiness, with each turn building directly on the previous one.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** True collaboration where the client co-designs the intervention, sets her own terms, and generates all key insights while the therapist builds on her language and metaphors. Both parties contribute expertise: the therapist brings MI skill and structure, the client brings self-knowledge and lived experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy by removing pressure, inviting the client to set her own terms, and never imposing a timeline or evaluating which version is 'better.' The final insight is allowed to land as the client's own discovery rather than the therapist's teaching.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice is given and every key insight is generated by the client through strategically designed evocation questions. The session creates conditions for the client to discover her own readiness without the therapist ever telling her what to think or do.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance with language that communicates partnership, and the client's contributions clearly shape the session. However, pacing and sequencing remain therapist-led, and power-sharing is somewhat implicit rather than explicitly offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are strategically designed to elicit desire, ability, commitment, and recognition of self-efficacy, producing substantial change talk across multiple types. Change talk is skillfully amplified and reinforced without ever feeling pushed, moving the session from contemplation to genuine readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplifying, and the session consistently moves toward the change side with gentle pointing out of paradoxes. However, the specific fear of the friend telling others or changing her behavior could have been more actively softened with a double-sided reflection or reframe.
