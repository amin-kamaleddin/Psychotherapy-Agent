# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and respects Aisha's boundaries around alcohol and scope of care. However, positive regard remains somewhat logistical and does not deeply affirm Aisha's values, fears, or identity as a mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections about exhaustion and the need for evening decompression. Empathy is largely practical rather than emotionally penetrating, missing the deeper meaning behind Aisha's ambivalence and desire for quiet time.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing evening smoking from about six cigarettes to three, with a practical emphasis on the first five minutes after bedtime. The focus is somewhat checklist-like and plan-heavy rather than guiding Aisha toward deeper self-generated change readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to Aisha's preferences and incorporates her constraints into the final plan. Collaboration is limited by the fact that the psychiatrist still generates most options and drives the plan rather than allowing Aisha to lead exploration of her own goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by inviting preferences, respecting Aisha's declines, and honoring her chosen reminder phrase and coping steps. Autonomy support is reduced by the frequency of specific directives and prescriptive prompts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some open questions early, but the session quickly shifts to advising and supplying reasons for change. There is limited evocation of Aisha's own desire, ability, values, confidence, or commitment, especially around her need for a personal minute.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha's preferences influence the final plan, and the psychiatrist invites input throughout the session. The interaction remains more expert-driven than truly collaborative, as the psychiatrist supplies the menu of strategies and specific steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some questions that allow Aisha to voice intentions, but does not consistently strengthen, expand, or amplify Aisha's change talk. The psychiatrist often moves quickly into advising and supplies clinician-generated benefits rather than evoking Aisha's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with, shame, or reinforce Aisha's sustain talk and respects her boundary about alcohol. The response misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections, normalizing statements, or gentle reframes to soften Aisha's minimization of smoking.
