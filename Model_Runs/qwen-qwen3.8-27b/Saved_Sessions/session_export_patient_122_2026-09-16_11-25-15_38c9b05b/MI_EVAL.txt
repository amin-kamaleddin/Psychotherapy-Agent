# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, normalizes difficulty, and avoids blame or shaming. The main limitation is that warmth can feel procedural and task-focused rather than affirming Sofia's worth independent of her behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture exhaustion, guilt, fear of failure, and the need for a gentle plan. The score is limited because the therapist does not fully explore deeper emotions such as past failures, self-critical identity, or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, steady focus on a small sustainable activity change to address energy and stress-related eating. Even topics such as sleep, food, and medical testing remain tied to the target behavior and action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia contributes key plan details and the therapist adopts her language, showing strong co-creation. It is not full collaboration because the psychiatrist still drives much of the structure and offers options before the client generates them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, respects Sofia's boundary about not being graded, and frames the plan as an optional experiment. There is little pressure, persuasion, or controlling language throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions about what change matters, what feels doable, and how to track, but also supplies rationale and solutions that reduce the emphasis on eliciting client-generated motivation. More focus on values, hopes, past successes, and confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia's choices substantially shape the plan, and the therapist invites final adjustments, showing genuine collaboration. The main gap is occasional expert-driven presentation of detailed options and plan components before fully eliciting Sofia's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia offers useful change talk, including willingness to try for one week and commitment to placing shoes by the door. The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or amplify these statements before moving into plan details.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reframes doubts as experiments, data, and nonjudgmental blanks rather than arguing with them. It is not a 5 because some responses are more corrective or reassuring than deeply reflective and later options could shift attention toward deficits.
