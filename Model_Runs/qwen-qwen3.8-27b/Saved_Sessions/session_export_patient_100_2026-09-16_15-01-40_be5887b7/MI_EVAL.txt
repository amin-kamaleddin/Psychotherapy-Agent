# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician conveys respect and nonjudgment, normalizing the client's ambivalence without blame. However, validation is somewhat procedural and could include warmer, more explicit positive regard for the client's honesty and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures the client's ambivalence between belonging and self-protection. Empathy could be strengthened by attending more deeply to underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of creating an exit or cue for drinking situations. The direction could improve with periodic summaries and clearer links back to the client's values and a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites client input and builds on her ideas rather than taking an expert stance. Collaboration would be stronger with more explicit co-creation and shared agenda-setting rather than a mostly linear questioning sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, consistently positioning the client as the decision-maker without pressure or moralizing. The clinician accepts concerns about rules and helps the client retain ownership of her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently evokes the client's own ideas, culminating in a self-selected silent alarm cue. Evocation could deepen by exploring values, importance, confidence, and identity to strengthen underlying change motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert, and her contributions meaningfully shape the session. Partnership could be stronger with more explicit power-sharing, agenda negotiation, and client-directed next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions frequently lead the client to generate change talk and practical ideas. The clinician could improve by more explicitly amplifying, summarizing, and exploring importance and confidence in the emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and integrates the client's social concerns with future protection. Softening could be more strategic by explicitly balancing the weight of both sides and reducing the perceived burden of staying in the moment.
