# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance while normalizing the client’s ambivalence. The stance remains supportive without labeling, moralizing, or pressuring a full quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures the functional meaning of smoking during breaks, including the pause, noise, and ritual aspects. Empathy could reach a 5 by exploring more emotional depth, such as stress, exhaustion, or guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, concrete focus on reducing smoking during work shifts and building a healthier break routine. The conversation stays connected to specific work contexts such as the dock, breaks, noise, and time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates as an equal partner, actively building on the client’s practical ideas rather than prescribing solutions. The plan emerges from the client’s own suggestions, including gum, an unlit cigarette, earbuds, and checking the delivery app.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is strongly emphasized, with language that leaves room for the client to decide when and how much change feels possible. There is no pressure, warning-based persuasion, or implication that the client must comply.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own ideas and practical reasons for change. Evocation would be stronger if it more explicitly connected the plan to the client’s values, confidence, readiness, or long-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats the client as the expert on his own life, work environment, and coping preferences. Decision-making authority is clearly placed with the client throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes and affirms client-generated change talk, such as one smoke on shift, gum, and using earbuds. It could be strengthened by more systematically deepening change talk using DARN-C, confidence, or importance language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to sustain talk without arguing, correcting, or dismissing the client’s reasons for continuing to smoke. It could improve by using more double-sided reflections to help resolve ambivalence.
