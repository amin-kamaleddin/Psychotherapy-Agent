# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard without arguing with the client’s minimization. It falls short of 5 because the warmth feels somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s need to protect autonomy, identity, and work-based self-evaluation. Some underlying emotions such as resentment, pride, or fear of losing independence are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The discussion remains connected to smoking, health, and work functioning, but the focus becomes circular and vague. There is limited agenda-setting or clear narrowing toward a concrete change target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses partner-like language and invites the client to define terms in his own way. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist continues to lead with repeated questions rather than fully co-constructing the session agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is the strongest part of the session, with consistent emphasis on the client’s freedom to choose. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, persuasion, and directive advice while preserving the client’s control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions about what would be meaningful or meaningful in the client’s own terms. However, evocation remains superficial because values, identity, and the most evocative client statements are not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance by framing the interaction as partnership and emphasizing the client’s timeline and choices. It is not fully realized because the psychiatrist still steers with a repeated internal agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers some change-relevant statements, and the psychiatrist asks questions intended to evoke them. Change talk is not sufficiently amplified, expanded, or developed through scaling or strategic follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and uses some double-sided reflections. Sustain talk remains central because the psychiatrist does not consistently soften or redirect it toward a workable change focus.
