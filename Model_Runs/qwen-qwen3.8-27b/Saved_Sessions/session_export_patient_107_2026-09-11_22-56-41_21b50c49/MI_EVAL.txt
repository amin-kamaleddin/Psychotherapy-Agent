# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids pressure despite the client’s ambivalence. Acceptance is somewhat professional and transactional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic understanding is limited, with few reflections of the client’s feelings, ambivalence, or meaning of coffee. Responses are often practical and advisory rather than reflecting the client’s perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on coffee/caffeine use and related effects. The psychiatrist guides the conversation toward a clear, concrete behavior plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well in creating a low-burden plan that fits the client’s preferences. However, the early part of the session is more counselor-driven with information and suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices and respecting the client’s limited readiness to change. Autonomy language could be stronger, but there is no coercion or insistence on larger change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the psychiatrist provides many reasons for change and practical steps rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations. There are missed opportunities to deepen the client’s change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership, especially when the client helps choose the cutoff, tracking method, and prompt format. However, the psychiatrist often takes on an expert or educator role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates the client’s willingness to try a small change and frames it positively. Change talk is present but not actively expanded or explored in depth.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against sustain talk and preserves parts of the client’s routine. However, MI-style reflections are not used effectively to soften the client’s reasons for maintaining the behavior.
