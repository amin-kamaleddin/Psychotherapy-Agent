# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Rowan’s resistance without arguing or shaming. However, the acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with less explicit warmth toward Rowan as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides good reflective listening and captures Rowan’s ambivalence, need for autonomy, and fear of punishment. Some reflections remain more behavioral than deeply emotional, and the underlying dislike of the device is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and moves coherently from resistance to a concrete plan. Direction is slightly weakened by the confusing multi-response turn and lack of a brief collaborative consolidation of the first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with Rowan generating key elements of the plan and the psychiatrist inviting client-centered input. However, the psychiatrist still does much of the summarizing, labeling, and leading, which subtly positions them as evaluator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, consistently framing the plan as Rowan’s own choice and avoiding pressure, rules, or promises. The psychiatrist normalizes flexibility and directly addresses Rowan’s concern about being controlled.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Rowan’s own motivations and solutions, resulting in a client-generated plan. Evocation could be stronger by allowing Rowan more space to generate meaning, confidence, values, and commitment rather than supplying some interpretations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Rowan as the expert on his routine and allows him to shape the timing, method, and location. Partnership is strong but not perfect because the psychiatrist still leads with rapid questions and evaluative summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does a strong job eliciting and reinforcing Rowan’s change talk and helping him generate a detailed self-directed plan. It would be stronger with more systematic exploration of confidence, importance, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing or arguing with Rowan’s sustain talk and often redirects toward autonomy. However, important sustain statements are not consistently reflected or fully integrated using double-sided reflections.
