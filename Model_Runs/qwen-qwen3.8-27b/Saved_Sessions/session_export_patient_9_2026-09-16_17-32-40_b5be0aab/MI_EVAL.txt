# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and supportive validation without disapproving of Amina's ambivalence. Acceptance was solid but somewhat restrained, leaving room for more explicit appreciation of her effort and protective love.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Empathic listening was strong, with accurate complex reflections that captured Amina's ambivalence and subtle concerns. The style became somewhat formulaic and occasionally interpreted meaning without fully exploring deeper feelings underneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on keeping Amina's son out of the co-parenting conflict and developing practical, protective communication strategies. The focus became narrow on wording choices and could better connect micro-level decisions to Amina's broader parenting values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by building on Amina's ideas, such as texting, written logistics, and explaining the app to her son. The interaction remained somewhat structured by the psychiatrist's repeated question formats, limiting fully co-directed collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided directives, advice, and pressure, leaving decisions and interpretation with Amina. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement of her freedom to choose and ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions helped Amina generate her own ideas and use her own language to identify change-oriented solutions. Evocation was not consistently deepened into richer change talk about values, confidence, or the meaning of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Amina's knowledge as central and invited her perspective throughout the session. Partnership was effective but would benefit from more explicit power sharing, agenda negotiation, and recognition of Amina as the expert on her family.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change talk by asking Amina to generate practical ideas for protective communication. Development was limited because change talk was not consistently reflected, summarized, amplified, or connected to readiness, confidence, importance, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled respectfully using double-sided reflections and gentle redirection rather than argument or dismissal. The conversation remained overfocused on feared interpretations of Amina's behavior without sufficiently shifting toward change talk, strengths, or confidence.
