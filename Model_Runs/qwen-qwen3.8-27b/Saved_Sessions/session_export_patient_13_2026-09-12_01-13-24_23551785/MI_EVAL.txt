# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect, warmth, and validation throughout, normalizing the client's fear and avoidance. Acceptance is strong but occasionally feels procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture fear, shame, guilt, overwhelm, and helplessness. Empathy is interrupted at times by rapid movement toward action options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the client's physical changes and avoidance of medical evaluation. Some drift into sleep and grounding remains connected to the central concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and invites input, but much of the session is driven by counselor-generated menus. The client's preferences do not fully shape the direction of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice language is frequent and pressure is generally avoided. One mildly directive recommendation slightly weakens autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite the client to express fear and desire for change. The counselor does not consistently deepen these statements into stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invitational language and willingness to pause show some partnership. The counselor still generates most of the content and options rather than co-creating with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are few attempts to evoke the client's own reasons, desire, ability, or confidence for change. Key statements such as wanting to be normal again are not expanded or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's resistance but does not effectively soften or redirect it. Double-sided reflections are rare, so ambivalence remains unresolved.
