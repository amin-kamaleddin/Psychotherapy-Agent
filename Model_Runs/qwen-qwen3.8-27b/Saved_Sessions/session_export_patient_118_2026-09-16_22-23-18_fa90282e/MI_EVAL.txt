# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent unconditional positive regard by apologizing for any lecture-like tone and validating the client's preference for practical care. The tone remains warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive even when the client is dismissive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of the client's frustration and need for a practical, low-pressure approach. However, the reflections remain somewhat surface-level and do not deeply explore the underlying emotions behind the client's resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The initial framing connects to smoking and health concerns, but the session largely shifts to dental logistics. As a result, the MI direction toward the target behavior is inconsistent rather than sustained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is highly collaborative, with the psychiatrist inviting input, offering simple choices, and adjusting the plan based on the client's feedback. The client's preferences clearly shape the final approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the client's right to choose and follow his own lead. There is little directive pressure, and the client is positioned as the decision-maker throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with most questions focused on procedural preferences rather than the client's own motivations, values, or confidence for change. The psychiatrist does not systematically elicit the client's internal reasons related to smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and repeatedly checks in to ensure the plan fits the client's preferences. The partnership could be stronger by more explicitly positioning the client as the expert on his own values and readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is only minimal effort to cultivate change talk, with no systematic elicitation of desire, ability, reasons, importance, or need for change. The client's brief acknowledgment of health risks is not actively reflected or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and does not reinforce the client's sustain talk. However, the response is more of a withdrawal from the topic than active softening through double-sided reflection or gentle pivots.
