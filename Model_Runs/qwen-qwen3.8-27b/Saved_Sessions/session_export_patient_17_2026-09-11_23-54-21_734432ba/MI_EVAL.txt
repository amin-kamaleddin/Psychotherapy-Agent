# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client's shame and fear. Acceptance is strong, though it sometimes shifts quickly to practical advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of genuine empathy, but the counselor often remains surface-level or moves quickly into problem-solving. Deeper complex reflections of the client's shame and fear would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and practical application. It would be stronger if direction were more consistently connected to the client's own values and change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites participation through role-play and choice-making, creating some collaborative moments. However, the counselor frequently provides the main content, making the interaction more directive than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor supports autonomy by offering options and asking the client to choose language that feels real. Repeated prescriptive language such as "try this" reduces the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session moves quickly into advice and coping tools without deeply exploring the client's reasons, values, hopes, or confidence. The client offers strong change talk that is not consistently evoked or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative invitations, but the psychiatrist often takes the lead with lists, scripts, and instructions. More exploration of the client's existing ideas and priorities would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses important change-related statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand them. More targeted questions about reasons, importance, confidence, and values would help develop change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor normalizes slips and avoids reinforcing all-or-nothing thinking. However, sustain talk receives substantial space and is mainly addressed with practical steps rather than MI-style reflections.
