# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and validated the client’s frustration about the wait. Acceptance was strong but somewhat procedural and not fully emotionally individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client’s practical needs, time constraints, and plan details. Empathy remained mostly cognitive and logistical rather than exploring deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a concrete health behavior tied to blood pressure and weight within the client’s time limits. It ended with a clear, actionable plan and no drift into irrelevant topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client actively shaped the plan using their schedule, preferred tools, and acceptable level of friction. Collaboration was strong, though the psychiatrist still generated much of the main structure and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently framed the client as the decision-maker and accepted the client’s refusal of additional tracking or steps. Autonomy was well supported, with directives limited to appropriate safety guidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful client-generated strategies, especially around confidence and scheduling. However, deeper values, health hopes, or internal reasons for change were not consistently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was repeatedly invited to choose among options and substantially influenced the final plan. Partnership was strong, but the agenda and main options were still largely counselor-framed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered clear intentions and problem-solving statements, and the confidence question was useful. Change talk remained primarily behavioral rather than being deepened through values, benefits, or future-self language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with the client’s time constraints and offered low-friction alternatives. Sustain talk was accommodated practically but not actively transformed through ambivalence work or double-sided reflections.
