# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Margaret’s ambivalence about drinking. The tone supported open exploration without moralizing or direct challenge.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured her conflict between enjoying social drinking, her fall, and her son’s concern. They were solid but sometimes remained surface-level and could have explored the deeper emotional meaning more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on reducing after-dinner drinking at home and produced a specific, measurable harm-reduction goal. The target behavior was well aligned with the referral concern and safety focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-created using Margaret’s preferences, routines, and ideas, such as the sticky note and water strategy. The psychiatrist still guided the process heavily with specific suggestions, which slightly limited a fully client-led feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used choice-focused language and reinforced that the tally was information, not a pass/fail test. However, offering several specific tips slightly reduced the emphasis on Margaret generating the solutions entirely on her own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several questions encouraged Margaret to articulate her own goals, preferences, and small change ideas. The work would have been stronger with deeper evocation of her values, confidence, and personal meaning for staying safe.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Margaret was invited to choose options and was involved in developing the plan, showing respect for her expertise in her own life. The psychiatrist remained somewhat leading, which limited the sense of a fully equal, client-directed partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited willingness and action statements, such as trying a tally and skipping the after-dinner drink. Change talk was not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened before moving into practical planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was accepted without argument, which avoided increasing resistance. The psychiatrist had more opportunities to use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to gently explore the tension between keeping the pub routine and increasing safety.
