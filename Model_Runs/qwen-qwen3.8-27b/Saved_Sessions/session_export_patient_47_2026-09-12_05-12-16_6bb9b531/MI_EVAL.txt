# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Evelyn’s perspective and desire for facts without a lecture. Slightly task-oriented and less emotionally affirming overall.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflections of Evelyn’s view that her drinking feels social and that her son’s concern has created doubt. Could have reflected deeper emotional conflict, defensiveness, fear, and ambivalence more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on alcohol use and moves from concern toward a concrete, low-effort experiment. Could have more explicitly linked the plan to Evelyn’s personal values and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive, choice-based language and offers options that co-construct a simple plan with Evelyn. Somewhat counselor-led by the repeated factual and numerical framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent support for Evelyn’s pace, choices, and decision to avoid more extensive change. Does not pressure for abstinence and validates her autonomy when she resists stopping.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some client-generated thoughts and plans, especially around practical steps for monitoring and experimentation. Does not consistently evoke deeper values, meaning, or longer-term motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a generally collaborative stance and invites Evelyn to choose the pace and type of exploration. Positioned somewhat more as expert by early, repeated factual estimates and guideline framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites several client-generated, practical intentions such as checking the label and trying an alcohol-free Thursday. Does not consistently deepen or amplify these statements into stronger motivational change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with and gently accepts Evelyn’s desire not to stop drinking. Could have reframed or balanced her sustain talk more consistently with her emerging health concerns and ambivalence.
