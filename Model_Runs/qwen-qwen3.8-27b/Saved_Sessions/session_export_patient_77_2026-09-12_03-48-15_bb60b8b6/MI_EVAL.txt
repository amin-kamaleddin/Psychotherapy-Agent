# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Liyana’s effort, fear, and willingness to try. It falls just short of a 5 because the warmth sometimes feels structured rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately tracks ambivalence, frustration, exhaustion, and fear. Some emotionally significant material, such as shame, body distrust, and family-related isolation, is not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target concern and moves from vague distress to a concrete behavior-specific plan. It is not a 5 because the focus sometimes becomes overly centered on the scale and a counselor-designed experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Liyana to choose among options and rate confidence, supporting some collaborative interaction. The collaboration is uneven because much of the plan is counselor-generated rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through permission-seeking, pacing, and confidence scaling. However, the later prescriptive plan reduces Liyana’s ownership and generation of her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful client language about desire, willingness, and confidence. The approach is limited by frequent advice-giving and supplied explanations instead of consistently drawing out Liyana’s internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is an initial collaborative stance, but the psychiatrist increasingly positions themselves as the expert designing the plan. The client selects from options but does not substantially shape the overall direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including willingness to try, confidence increases, and desired outcomes. The work is limited by counselor-provided plans and missed opportunities to deepen change talk through values, reasons, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, and the psychiatrist redirects toward less urgent responses. However, the continued focus on the scale and limited use of advanced softening strategies prevent consistent skillful pivoting.
