# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Nadine's exhaustion and normalizes her need for sleep without judgment. They explicitly reassure her that she is not doing a bad job, maintaining a warm, accepting stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately capture the client's ambivalence, vulnerability, and burden of managing both her own exhaustion and her partner's feelings. Empathic depth is occasionally reduced by quick shifts to problem-solving or information-giving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on obtaining practical support and moves toward actionable goals. Direction is slightly weakened by a less explicit bridge from developmental concerns to partner communication and the absence of a final summary or explicit commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a short, usable message with the client and builds on the client's own suggestions. Provision of medical information and specific scripts introduces some expert direction, preventing fully balanced collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices rather than directives and respects the client's stated limits. The resulting plan remains the client's own, with no pressure or persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit the client's own motivations, language, and plan for asking her partner for support. Evocation could be strengthened by deeper exploration of underlying values, rest, and partnership.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the client as the expert on her family and invites her perspective throughout. However, expert information and specific suggestions subtly shift the dynamic away from fully balanced partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes desire and ability through open questions and reinforces the client's specific, doable ask. The session would benefit from more systematic exploration of change talk, including confidence, commitment, and taking steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument or dismissal, normalizing the need for help and acknowledging the client's concerns about miscommunication. More double-sided reflections could better hold both the desire for help and the fear of judgment.
