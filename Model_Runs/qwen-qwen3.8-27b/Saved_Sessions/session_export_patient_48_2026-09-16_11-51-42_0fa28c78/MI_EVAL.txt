# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s effort. Some reflections quickly move into reassurance or reframing, slightly bypassing the client’s self-critical perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects barriers and concerns, such as fatigue, time pressure, and fear of failure. The empathy could be deeper by exploring emotional undercurrents such as self-doubt and vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on increasing activity and small behavioral steps. It occasionally narrows to procedural details or secondary topics, which slightly reduces strategic focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses collaborative language and invites the client to select options. However, the clinician generates many options, making the collaboration somewhat clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports choice by framing the step as an experiment, not a commitment. Missing the step is normalized as information rather than failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative techniques are used, such as scaling questions. The session moves quickly into planning without fully drawing out the client’s own values, reasons, and benefits for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites the client’s perspective and choices, supporting partnership. However, the clinician remains agenda-setting by generating many options and structuring the next step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician affirms willingness and uses rulers to invite change-oriented thinking. Change talk is present but not consistently deepened, reflected, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or moralizing, and barriers are normalized and reframed. The clinician sometimes moves quickly into solutions before fully exploring the meaning of the resistance.
