# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizing uncertainty and reframing potential failures as positive indicators. Never labels Daniel's drinking as a problem or expresses disapproval; the tone is warm, genuine, and individualized throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate, nuanced reflective listening that tracks the client's emotional and cognitive process in real-time. Complex reflections capture dual ambivalence, identity shifts, and the mechanism of drift without over-interpreting or pathologizing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior while following a logical arc from awareness to understanding to planning to commitment. The session is client-led in content but clinician-guided in structure, culminating in a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats the interaction as occurring between two equal partners, ceding agenda control to the client and positioning Daniel as the expert on his own life. The tone is conversational and equal throughout, with each question shaped by Daniel's previous response rather than a pre-planned sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Daniel's ownership, choice, and self-determination, explicitly contrasting autonomy-supportive framing with controlling language. Never makes recommendations; all solutions and the final commitment emerge entirely from Daniel's own thinking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits Daniel's internal motivation through open questions and reflections that highlight his own values, without providing external reasons or statistics. The final commitment is entirely in Daniel's own language, demonstrating successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom about change reside within Daniel through repeated 'What do you think?' questions and explicit acknowledgment of his own insights. The psychiatrist serves as a facilitator rather than an expert, never taking over the thinking process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses well-timed evocative questions and affirming reflections that draw out Daniel's own motivations, reasons, and plans in a natural flow. Change talk emerges organically and culminates in Daniel stating his own commitment in his own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles the limited sustain talk appropriately through double-sided reflections and gentle reframing without arguing or minimizing. Scored 4 rather than 5 because the session contained relatively few challenging sustain talk moments to work with.
