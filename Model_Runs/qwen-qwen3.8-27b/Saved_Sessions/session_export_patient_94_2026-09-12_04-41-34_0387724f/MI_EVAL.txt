# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses validating language such as “I hear you” and “you’re in charge.” Acceptance is reduced by moments that feel mildly directive, such as unprompted medical details and a final checklist after the client wanted to leave.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist captures Maria’s skepticism, busyness, and desire for control with several useful reflections. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level, with less exploration of the emotional meaning behind her fears of dependence or loss of autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and inhaler use, but the direction toward change is uneven. Time spent on information, scripts, and logistics reduces strategic momentum, especially when a checklist is introduced near the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and ultimately follows Maria’s lead toward a one-month, stop-if-needed plan. Collaboration is limited by repeated expert-driven moves, including medical information, suggested doctor questions, a script, and a checklist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** This is a strength, with frequent language emphasizing choice and control and respect for Maria’s stated limits. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by unsolicited advice and a final checklist after she said she needed to leave.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some open questions that elicit useful client language, such as what would help Maria feel more comfortable deciding. Evocation is limited by reliance on information-giving rather than deeper questions about values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is real collaboration, as Maria’s one-month trial idea shapes the session’s outcome. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist repeatedly assumes an advisory role with detailed information, scripts, and checklist-style suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maria produces meaningful change talk, including willingness to try a one-month, stop-if-needed plan. The psychiatrist affirms these statements but does not consistently amplify or expand them with follow-up questions about benefits, confidence, or desired change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria’s sustain talk is prominent, with worries about side effects, dependence, and hassle. The psychiatrist reflects it but does not effectively soften or reframe it, instead continuing to emphasize risks and drawbacks.
