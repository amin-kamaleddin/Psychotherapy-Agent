### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and warm throughout, with multiple genuine affirmations of Erik's perspective and experience. However, affirmations follow predictable patterns ("That makes sense," "I appreciate how...") that prevent this from reaching excellence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures Erik's ambivalence and self-protective stance, with good perspective-taking on his night shift context. A significant flaw is the verbatim repetition of an earlier reflection after Erik had already moved the conversation forward, which broke the empathic flow.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session moves from broad ambivalence to concrete commitments (one beer, food exploration, three-month follow-up), showing good directional arc. However, the verbatim repetition of a reflection mid-session caused Erik to call out the error and ask "where are we?", disrupting the flow and indicating a loss of structural tracking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is clearly a joint exploration—Erik's ideas about his uncle, his shift work, and his preferred simplicity drive the session forward, and the final plan is co-constructed. The verbatim repetition of a reflection is a significant collaboration breakdown, showing the psychiatrist lost track of Erik's contributions in real time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of autonomy support. The psychiatrist never prescribes, never pressures beyond Erik's stated willingness, and consistently frames decisions as Erik's own; the final commitment is entirely in Erik's own language and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently uses evoking questions to draw out Erik's own motivations, and successfully elicits his key change talk ("the guy who noticed and dealt with it"). One notable lapse: the psychiatrist provides the "honest answer is yes" about whether changes will work, telling Erik rather than eliciting his own belief. Could also have evoked more of Erik's deeper values or long-term identity to strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance throughout, with language that positions Erik as the expert on his own life ("real expertise," "which one feels more within reach?"). Occasional moments of directive positioning (providing the "honest answer" and specific health information) slightly shift the dynamic away from equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces several instances of change talk, particularly Erik's self-identified motivation to be "the guy who noticed and dealt with it." The psychiatrist could have done more to deepen Erik's sense of self-efficacy (e.g., exploring what will help him maintain his commitment on a difficult night).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles Erik's minimization and doubt without arguing, lecturing, or reinforcing sustain talk; responses like "Looking for a middle ground that actually fits your life" validate while keeping the door open. One moment of providing the clinician's own reasoning ("The honest answer is yes...they do make a measurable difference") briefly shifts from evoking to advising.
