# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts Malik’s ambivalence without blame. However, some statements subtly steer toward a preselected “not using” stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections show good perspective-taking and use of Malik’s own language to capture conflict. Empathy could be deeper by exploring underlying fear, shame, or family tension.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on navigating cannabis use within peer settings. It would benefit from less repetition and stronger movement toward a concrete, client-owned next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Malik as the expert on his social life and builds on his language. Collaboration feels slightly guided when questions are stacked or framed around not using.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses choice-respecting, hypothetical language and avoids direct pressure. Some questions still embed a preferred “without using” outcome rather than fully opening the choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful attempts to reflect change-relevant language and imagine low-effort alternatives. Evocation remains limited because deeper values, self-image, and intrinsic motivation are not systematically explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys partnership by deferring to Malik’s expertise and asking for his perspective. The sense of co-created agenda is slightly reduced by leading or multiple sequential questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites some change-oriented thinking, such as fitting in without using. It lacks systematic elicitation of DARN-C language, importance or confidence statements, and deeper follow-up on change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and rolls with sustain talk by validating the social context. It could more consistently build discrepancy between belonging and longer-term goals or self-image.
