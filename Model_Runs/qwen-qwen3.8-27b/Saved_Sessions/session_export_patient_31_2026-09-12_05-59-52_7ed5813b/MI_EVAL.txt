# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, especially in accepting that David does not want to quit completely. However, acceptance is more procedural than warm, and some moments, such as concern about burdening his partner, were not deeply acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was adequate content-level understanding of David's stress, practical concerns, and preferences. However, the clinician mostly paraphrased behavior rather than reflecting deeper emotions such as fear of failure, resignation, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well focused on smoking reduction and effectively narrowed the goal to replacing the first after-work cigarette. The main limitation was that repeated instructional content made the session feel more educational than guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was a reasonable collaborative process, with David choosing the gum strength, timing, and reminder cue. The plan, however, remained more clinician-led than fully equal-partner because the psychiatrist repeatedly supplied detailed instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a relative strength, as the psychiatrist consistently respected David's right to keep smoking the rest of his cigarettes. It would have been stronger if the clinician more explicitly reinforced David's ownership and decision-making authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly asked practical questions about what to try and how to implement it. There were missed opportunities to elicit David's personal values, motivations, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and allowed David to shape parts of the plan. However, the clinician remained the primary expert and problem-solver rather than consistently positioning David as the expert on his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session produced some practical commitment language, but the psychiatrist did not actively cultivate the motivational content behind it. Change talk was not consistently amplified or used to strengthen David's reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted David's sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it, and reframed the goal in a manageable way. However, there was little active softening through double-sided reflections or value-based exploration of ambivalence.
