# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental reassurance but mixes it with corrective risk education. Acceptance feels clinical and formulaic rather than deeply emotionally resonant.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects some of Elena’s worry, guilt, and need for quick relief, but often moves quickly to strategies. Missed opportunities include deeper exploration of her shame, fear, and statement that she is not a monster.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to smoking, stress, and harm reduction, but is task-focused rather than strategically centered on Elena’s values and change direction. It offers many coping options without a clear MI-focused path.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some shared planning, such as choosing a trigger and co-creating a timer plan, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the content. The interaction is more menu-driven than a true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for choices and avoids overt pressure, but repeatedly offers specific directives like timers, swaps, and clothing changes. Autonomy is supported but not consistently foregrounded.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some practical questions but mostly provides information and suggestions rather than evoking Elena’s own motivations. Deeper exploration of values, strengths, and the meaning of quitting is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying research, strategies, and harm-reduction steps. Elena’s expertise in her own life and preferences does not substantially shape the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena offers some change talk, such as trying the timer, smoking outside, and keeping her son healthy, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect or amplify it. There are missed chances to build confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Elena’s perspective without harshly arguing, but returns repeatedly to risk information and harm reduction. A more MI approach would use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk and pivot toward change.
