# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, with some validating statements such as “That sounds really frustrating.” However, acceptance remains somewhat procedural and lacks deeper, individualized affirmation of Amina as a parent and worker.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic listening is present early, especially in naming frustration and discouragement. Later responses are more surface-level and miss stronger feelings of fear, shame, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target problem of securing the job despite childcare barriers. It provides clear practical direction, though it becomes broad and somewhat solution-saturated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration by inviting input and responding to Amina’s preferences. However, the psychiatrist supplies most options and scripts rather than helping Amina generate her own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks whether Amina wants help, which supports autonomy to a moderate degree. Yet frequent prescriptive language and directive recommendations weaken the sense of client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is a major area for improvement because the psychiatrist relies heavily on advice and scripts rather than eliciting Amina’s own motivations. There are few open-ended questions that help Amina articulate her values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites brainstorming and offers assistance, showing partial partnership. The interaction often shifts into expert-driven problem-solving, which reduces the collaborative power balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina offers several clear change statements, such as knowing she can handle the work and wanting to appear reliable. The psychiatrist does not consistently use these statements to evoke and expand her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Amina and responds to her concerns without judgment. However, sustain talk is often managed through advice-giving rather than MI-consistent ambivalence-resolution.
