# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed the client’s safety-focused choices. Warmth was somewhat task-focused and lacked explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, tracking the client’s information needs, concern, values, and emotional experience. The reframing of “I’m not ready” as a need for clear boundaries was especially strong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on citalopram, side effects, the phenelzine interaction, and a concrete safety plan. Questions moved the conversation logically toward a clear, behaviorally specific next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas about a checklist, call sequence, and involving their husband clearly shaped the session. Collaboration was somewhat reduced by early expert-driven medical information and safety directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respected the client’s hesitation and invited their input throughout. Direct urgent safety instructions were clinically appropriate but somewhat directive, which could reduce the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicited the client’s own motivation, plan, and preferred way of asking for information. The session began with direct psychoeducation and did not fully develop intrinsic motivation through scaling or deeper values-based evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client’s input and used their answers to guide the conversation. The early shift into expert role with medical explanations and directive safety advice limited the sense of equal power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicited and reinforced the client’s safety-focused change talk and concrete plan. Development of desire, ability, reasons, and need could have gone deeper beyond safety.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s hesitation was handled well by reframing it as a protective, values-consistent stance rather than resistance. The response could have used more advanced double-sided reflections to further resolve ambivalence.
