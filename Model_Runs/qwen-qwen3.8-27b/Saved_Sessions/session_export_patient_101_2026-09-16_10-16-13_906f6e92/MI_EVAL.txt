# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, normalizes concerns, and avoids blame. Some affirmations are behavior-focused, and acceptance could deepen further around ambivalence and fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures key concerns about drinking, reproductive health, and social pressure. Empathy is generally effective but often remains surface-level and is quickly followed by problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing risky alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability. Topic drift is minimal, and the conversation stays tied to actionable goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is invited to participate in role-play, tone selection, and strategy choices. However, the psychiatrist still generates much of the problem-solving framework and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice and avoids pressuring the client to commit. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing the client’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions and client-generated strategies, but the psychiatrist often moves quickly to external tools. Deeper exploration of the client’s values, motivations, and self-generated reasons for change would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows collaboration by inviting the client’s input, but often behaves as the active expert. A stronger partnership stance would involve more open exploration of the client’s own ideas before offering options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively invites goals and reinforces boundary-setting behavior. Change talk is present but not consistently developed, as advice-giving often interrupts deeper elaboration of the client’s own change reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without confrontation or defensiveness. The psychiatrist normalizes ambivalence but does not fully process conflicting reasons using more sophisticated MI techniques.
