# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming throughout, with strong examples like 'That's not being an asshole, that's a human response to feeling cornered.' However, some phrases feel slightly formulaic and the warmth occasionally lacks fully individualized depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking, including strong captures of Eric's feeling of being cornered and the distinction between nagging and silence. Could have explored deeper emotional vulnerability around fear of illness and the meaning behind his wife's silence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear arc from defensiveness through exploration to self-awareness, with all questions and reflections tied to the target behavior. Could have been slightly more focused toward identifying a small, concrete next step before closing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session framed as joint exploration with consistent invitations for Eric's input; follows his leads naturally (the pause, wife's silence). Language like 'from your perspective' and 'what would that look like to you?' reinforces genuine teamwork.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Throughout, autonomy is consistently and naturally supported—no pressure, no imposed timeline, explicit acknowledgment of Eric's right to choose. Phrases like 'I don't want to argue with what's working for you' and 'start wherever feels right for you today' are woven throughout without feeling performative.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong evocation through open questions that draw out Eric's own language and reasons; no external arguments or statistics introduced. Eric produces genuine desire, need, and acknowledgment language throughout, amplified by accurate reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys Eric as the expert on his own life with clear avoidance of authority ('from your perspective,' 'I don't want to get this wrong'). Session direction is clearly shaped by Eric's responses and he is invited to steer.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective strategic questions elicit and amplify change talk, including validating Eric's own admission and reframing his 'shift.' Could go deeper into exploring confidence, values-based ability language, or a concrete commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Eric's rationalizations ('it just works,' physical job) without argument or dismissal, reframing toward the underlying stress-relief need. Could be slightly more strategic in consistently redirecting from 'it works' toward costs or alternative explorations.
