# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Naomi's experience. Acceptance could deepen by more explicitly validating her broader self-doubt and fear, not only her anxiety symptoms.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening and accurately tracks Naomi's ambivalence, including her metaphors. A higher level would include more precise reflection of the emotional pain beneath her words, such as shame, frustration, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on managing social anxiety during photography shoots and moves logically toward a specific cue and behavior. Direction could be stronger by adding a brief summary that connects the small step to broader work and social functioning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by building on Naomi's ideas and language to co-construct a potential plan. More equal collaboration would involve Naomi more substantially shaping the agenda and direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by inviting Naomi's judgment and avoiding coercive language. It could be stronger with more explicit affirmations of her choice and ownership, such as making clear she decides whether and when to try the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions and client-derived metaphors to elicit coping ideas. Evocation is more behavioral and solution-focused than motivational, with less development of Naomi's values, confidence, reasons, or commitment to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Naomi as the expert on her own experience and follows her photography metaphors. More explicit power sharing would strengthen partnership by inviting her to set the focus and choose which piece to explore.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Naomi produces several change-related statements, but the psychiatrist often frames them as practical problem-solving steps. Stronger cultivation would involve more consistently eliciting her reasons, confidence, desire, or commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles doubt and sustain talk well by acknowledging both sides rather than arguing. The final sustain talk is left somewhat unresolved and could be pivoted more skillfully back into client-led change talk or confidence.
