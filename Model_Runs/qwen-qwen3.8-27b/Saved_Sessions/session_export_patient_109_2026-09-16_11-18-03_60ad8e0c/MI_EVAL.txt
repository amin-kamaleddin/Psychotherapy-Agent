# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent respect, patience, and validation without blame or pressure. The warmth is somewhat formulaic and does not fully affirm Rafael’s broader worth or resilience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Rafael’s practical barriers and ambivalence with strong reflective listening. However, the reflections remain mostly cognitive/behavioral and do not deeply explore emotions such as discouragement, fear of failure, or wife pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of returning to exercise and re-engaging with the gym. The counselor guides the conversation toward a concrete, actionable, and low-pressure plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rafael’s preferences and co-creates a plan based on his choices. The collaboration is not fully reciprocal because the counselor still proposes many specific options and structures the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Rafael’s sense of choice with non-coercive, optional language. The plan respects Rafael’s desire to avoid nagging and exam-like check-ins while encouraging self-ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks helpful questions about past routines, realistic options, and confidence. However, the session becomes solution-focused and does not deeply evoke Rafael’s intrinsic motivations or values connected to becoming more active.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rafael’s input and allows his ideas to shape the plan. Full partnership is limited because the counselor still drives the structure, agenda, and sequence of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rafael offers some change talk and the psychiatrist asks questions that draw out his own ideas. The session moves quickly into planning, missing opportunities to amplify, deepen, or reflect back the client’s change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce Rafael’s sustain talk and uses one helpful double-sided reflection. However, the counselor does not consistently soften or redirect concerns about failure, pressure, or sustainability toward change.
