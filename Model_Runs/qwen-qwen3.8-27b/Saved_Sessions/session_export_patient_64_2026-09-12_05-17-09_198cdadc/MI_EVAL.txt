# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, especially of the client’s concern for his grandparents. However, the initial therapy framing and limited validation of frustration prevent a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence, young/healthy identity, and protective concern for grandparents. The reflections remain largely surface-level and do not deeply explore underlying emotions or identity themes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor returns to the flu vaccine after an early drift into therapy and largely keeps the target behavior in view. Repeated logistical problem-solving and the initial mismatch reduce the focus and clarity of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites preferences, offers choices, and allows the client to contribute practical ideas. The interaction often feels expert-led because of frequent information-giving and suggestions, limiting genuine co-created collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates no pressure, invites choice, and supports the client’s ownership of the decision. The frequent practical suggestions and information-giving create mild pressure and prevent a perfect score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance and confidence scales help the client articulate motivation, particularly around protecting his grandparents. The counselor does not consistently evoke deeper values, self-efficacy, or future imagery, and relies too much on information-giving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor shows some partnership by asking preferences and avoiding an authoritarian stance. The early therapy drift and frequent expert-led information delivery reduce the sense of a balanced, client-led alliance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some change talk through scaling and evocative questions, especially around protecting grandparents. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, summarized, or deepened into stronger personal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally accepts sustain talk without arguing, using phrases like “that makes sense” and “I hear you.” However, the counselor does not consistently use double-sided reflections or client-generated counterarguments to soften resistance and resolve ambivalence.
