# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or lecturing. However, repeated instructional reminders occasionally reduce the warmth and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic reflections are limited and mostly factual rather than emotional. Important client feelings about stress, time pressure, ambivalence, and the effort required are not deeply acknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing smoking during the work shift. However, it often narrows into repeated dosing details and logistics instead of guiding toward a concise, client-led change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some collaboration at the start and accommodation of the client’s preference for gum. Overall, the plan remains largely expert-driven rather than co-created by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses autonomy-supportive language such as “no judgment,” “low-pressure,” and “smoke if you must.” This is partly undermined by repeated directive instructions and specific rules.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks an initial open question and acknowledges willingness to try the gum, but does not consistently elicit the client’s own reasons, values, or confidence for change. Important client motivations are left underexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with partnership language and invites the client’s input, but then quickly shifts to expert-led instruction. The client’s expertise about his work schedule and practical limits is not consistently honored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms it in a low-pressure way. However, the psychiatrist does not use evocative questions to deepen or expand the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with sustain talk and normalizes continuing to smoke. However, it is not skillfully reframed or redirected, and the response can reinforce smoking as the default option.
