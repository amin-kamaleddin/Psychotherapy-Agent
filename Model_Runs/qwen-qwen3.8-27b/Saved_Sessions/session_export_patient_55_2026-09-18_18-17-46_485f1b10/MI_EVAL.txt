# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful, with strong validation when Isabelle pushes back (e.g., 'You're not needy, and you don't owe me a performance of your private life'). The tone is genuine but somewhat measured and professional; an explicit statement of unconditional positive regard would push this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking with accurate, emotionally attuned reflections. However, some reflections lean toward summarizing content rather than fully exploring the deeper emotions beneath Isabelle's surface-level responses (e.g., the vulnerability of a 17-year-old in a session she didn't ask for).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a general focus on understanding the referral but lacks clear strategic direction toward the alcohol risk topic. The conversation drifts through several topics without converging, and the psychiatrist acknowledges losing the thread, risking an ending without concrete understanding or momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance treating Isabelle as an equal partner, reinforced by admissions of missteps and explicit acknowledgment of limitations. However, the collaboration is somewhat asymmetric in practice, as Isabelle largely responds to prompts and rarely steers the conversation herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy with strong statements that remove fear and pressure (e.g., 'I'm not here to tell you that your life is secretly in danger'). Could be slightly stronger by more explicitly affirming Isabelle's right to disagree with the screening or to decide for herself what to do with this information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to elicit Isabelle's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; questions are mostly exploratory or clarifying rather than evocative in the MI sense. The focus on understanding the screening and confusion misses opportunities to surface intrinsic motivation through questions about values, future goals, or self-image.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance from the opening with consistent language of shared exploration and positioning Isabelle as the expert on her own life. However, the dynamic remains counselor-led in practice; a 5 would involve more genuine co-creation, such as inviting Isabelle to help set the agenda or reflect on where the conversation is heading.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very limited attempts to evoke change talk, with no clear questions designed to elicit reasons for change, confidence in change, or desire for change. The session remains largely in the realm of clarifying the screening without strategically building momentum toward any behavior change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids reinforcing Isabelle's sustain talk statements, but responses are mostly neutral validations rather than active softening techniques. Misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections or create productive tension that might lead toward change exploration.
