# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Amira’s distress without blame or moralizing. The acceptance was consistent but somewhat formulaic and did not fully individualize her worth or resilience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections effectively captured Amira’s ambivalence, helplessness, regret, and fear of disappointment. Some reflections were repetitive and included a few inferences not directly confirmed by the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session had a coherent emotional theme but lacked clear direction toward the target behavior of reducing drug use. The conversation remained exploratory rather than strategically moving toward a concrete behavioral focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Amira’s input and adjusted to her stated needs, such as silence and not being fixed. The counselor still drove the session heavily with long, repeated prompts, limiting the client’s ability to lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly avoided pressure, advice-giving, and control, respecting Amira’s boundaries. The score was limited by continued questioning after the client asked for a pause and by somewhat repetitive autonomy-support phrasing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided giving reasons for change and used open questions, but these mainly elicited needs for safety and support rather than internal motivation for change. Amira’s change talk remained underdeveloped and was not systematically drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveyed respect for Amira’s self-knowledge and invited her preferences to guide the interaction. However, the psychiatrist still controlled much of the pace and direction through sequential prompts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amira offered limited change talk, and the counselor did not consistently elicit, expand, or reinforce her desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. The session focused more on support and loneliness than on developing motivational language for reducing drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with or dismiss Amira’s sustain talk, but did not effectively soften or redirect it toward change. Statements of helplessness and hopelessness were reflected repeatedly without sufficient pivoting toward agency or small steps.
