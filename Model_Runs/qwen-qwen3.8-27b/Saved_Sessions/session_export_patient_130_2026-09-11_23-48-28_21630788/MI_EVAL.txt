# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, avoiding shaming or blame. However, acceptance is somewhat generic and at times colludes with minimization of use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate but surface-level reflections of irritation, fatigue, and annoyance. Deeper emotions such as fear of being labeled an addict or marital hurt are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session starts with the target issue but quickly drifts into communication scripts and boundary-setting. It loses focus on drug use, ambivalence, and the core change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and incorporates the client’s edits, showing some collaboration. However, the psychiatrist frequently steers the session and provides structured advice rather than fully sharing power.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is given room to choose and is not forced into role-play, which supports autonomy. Yet directive instructions and coaching weaken the emphasis on the client’s freedom and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few initial evocative questions, but the session quickly shifts to external advice and scripts. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out the client’s own motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is a surface-level collaborative stance, but the psychiatrist quickly becomes a problem-solver. The client does not serve as the primary expert shaping the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes only weak, infrequent attempts to elicit reasons for change. Missed openings to explore desire, ability, need, or commitment limit the effectiveness of this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk such as “I’m functional” and “I don’t need to change how I relax” is often accepted or reinforced. The psychiatrist rarely uses double-sided reflections or helps examine the costs of maintaining the status quo.
