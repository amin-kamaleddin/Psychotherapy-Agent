# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard with warm, non-judgmental language throughout. Never labels, dismisses, or minimizes; validates the patient's experience while removing pressure (e.g., 'no judgment here,' 'you don't have to figure it out right now').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and progressively deeper reflections that track the emotional undercurrent (grief, identity loss, fear of dismissal) rather than staying on surface concerns. Key reflections like 'that's the part that really hurts' show deep attunement to meaning beneath the presenting complaint.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear trajectory from broad worry to a concrete, client-generated action step with good momentum. However, the session spends notable time in the grief/identity territory, slightly broadening focus beyond the specific target behavior of seeking medical care.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames the interaction as joint work ('if we were to look at this together') and actively follows the patient's lead in real-time. The session is shaped by her contributions rather than an imposed agenda; 'we' language is used naturally and consistently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the patient's ownership and right to choose, removing pressure to change, resolve ambivalence, or act on a timeline. The question offering 'equally stuck' as a valid option is a particularly strong autonomy-supportive move.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation emerges from the patient herself — the psychiatrist offers no external reasons for change or advice. Questions like 'is there one piece that feels slightly less heavy?' invite the patient to identify her own resources, which is textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the work as residing within the patient using ownership language ('that's yours,' 'you noticed that yourself') and exploratory framing ('I wonder…'). Never says 'you should' or 'you need to'; the role is clearly guide, not director.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully amplifies the patient's own change talk by reflecting it back with ownership language and highlighting her agency. The double-sided reflection on 'that's it?' honors both hope and doubt while subtly tipping toward the change side.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk appropriately by validating without reinforcing (e.g., 'I get why that makes you want to stay where you are'). However, the psychiatrist doesn't actively pivot or use double-sided reflections to more aggressively tip the balance toward change.
