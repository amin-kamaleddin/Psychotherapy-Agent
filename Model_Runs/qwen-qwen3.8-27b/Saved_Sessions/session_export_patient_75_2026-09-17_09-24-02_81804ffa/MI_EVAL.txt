# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, framing Linda as caring rather than nonadherent. Ambivalence is validated without labels or correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Linda’s ambivalence, fear, and emotional burden. A few emotionally important themes, such as fear of failure and ER terror, are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of managing asthma use while addressing anxiety. It progressively narrows from broad worry to a practical, patient-centered plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as an equal partner, building on Linda’s ideas rather than imposing a clinician-driven agenda. The plan is co-created from her suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda is consistently positioned as the decision-maker about what would work for her. There is no prescriptive pressure to use the inhaler or follow a fixed rule.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Linda’s own motivations, values, and possible solutions rather than providing reasons for change. Her goals around safety, family, and trusting her body are drawn out clearly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session reflects genuine power sharing by treating Linda as the expert on her own experience. The counselor avoids lecturing and follows her lead throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda generates strong change talk, including desire, need, reasons, and emerging ability talk. The psychiatrist effectively invites and reinforces her own motivations and plan ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by validation and reframing without argument. Some opportunities remain to deepen softening, especially for late-session fear of failure and being graded.
