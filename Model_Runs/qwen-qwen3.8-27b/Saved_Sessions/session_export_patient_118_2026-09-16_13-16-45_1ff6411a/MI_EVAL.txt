# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honors the client's boundary without arguing. The score is not a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat procedural and some smoking-related options continue to be offered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate but often surface-level reflections of the client's concerns about teeth, pills, and suffering. Deeper feelings such as embarrassment, being lectured, and frustration with past quit attempts are not consistently captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is practical and organized around the client's dental priorities. However, if smoking cessation is the MI target, the direction becomes inconsistent after shifting into dental logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is useful task-based collaboration, including checklist choices and dental priorities. The psychiatrist still controls much of the agenda by providing plans, explanations, and scripts, so the process is not fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently affirms the client's right to choose and avoids coercion. The score is not a 5 because some recommendations and scripts remain more directive than fully client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions about quitting importance, past attempts, and dental priorities, eliciting some client statements. The evocation is not sustained with confidence scaling, values exploration, or deeper follow-up on the client's own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, especially around dental care. The interaction remains partly expert-driven, particularly when providing a detailed plan, checklist, and script.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, such as acknowledging smoking is not good and saying he would quit if it did not ruin his day. The psychiatrist does not effectively amplify or develop this language, shifting instead to options and practical information.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client's sustain talk and avoids direct argument or confrontation. However, there is limited use of active MI strategies to soften resistance, resolve ambivalence, or gently connect the client's values to change.
