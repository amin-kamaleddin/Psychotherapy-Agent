# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and low-pressure stance with supportive statements such as “that’s totally okay” and “no pressure.” However, the acceptance is somewhat generic and could be warmer or more individually attuned to Elena’s fear and vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some useful reflective statements, especially around Elena’s mixed feelings and concern about forgetting. Empathy becomes less consistent as the session shifts into advice-giving and practical problem-solving, without deeper exploration of the emotional meaning of the pregnancy scare.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays related to the pregnancy scare but drifts toward coping strategies, reminder wording, and small behavioral cues rather than maintaining clear focus on contraception decision-making. There is limited movement toward clarifying readiness, barriers, or a client-led next step for the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some client input by offering options and allowing Elena to choose the “after sip” cue. However, collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist supplies most of the scripts, reminder ideas, and behavioral suggestions rather than fully co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with repeated emphasis on choice, low pressure, and permission not to make a decision now. It could be slightly stronger by more explicitly affirming Elena’s capacity and ownership of future decisions about her body and contraception.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist shifts quickly from an open question into coping strategies and reminder options. There is little use of open questions about Elena’s values, readiness, confidence, or personal reasons for change related to contraception.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative in form, with choices offered and Elena selecting a small step. However, the psychiatrist remains in an expert role by generating most of the options and scripts, rather than explicitly inviting Elena to lead with what matters most to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena offers a meaningful change statement, but the psychiatrist does not follow up with evocative questions that could expand her motivation, desire, or need for change. Change talk is minimally developed and quickly redirected into practical, low-effort coping plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Elena’s avoidance, using a gentle, non-confrontational stance. However, she does not use double-sided reflections or strategic softening to reconnect Elena’s ambivalence with her values and move gently toward change.
