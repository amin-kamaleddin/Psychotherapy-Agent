# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone while affirming Marco without blaming past failures. Some affirmations are slightly leading or overly positive, which could subtly pressure Marco to maintain confidence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects frustration, stuckness, and the desire for more energy with his children. Later reflections become more cognitive and planning-focused, missing opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as disappointment, fatigue, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains strongly focused on the target behavior, moving from ambivalence about weight management to a concrete plan for managing home food triggers. Even when stress or family dynamics arise, the psychiatrist ties them back to the specific behavior of craving chips and choosing almonds.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a non-didactic, collaborative style and builds the plan using Marco's own choices, constraints, and practical ideas. The structure and counselor-provided options sometimes make the interaction feel more guided than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supports autonomy by offering choices, asking preferences, and helping Marco take ownership of the plan. It would be stronger with more explicit language affirming that Marco is in charge and free to accept or decline any part of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and evocative prompts effectively draw out Marco's own confidence, values, and commitment language. The evocation could be deeper by exploring intrinsic motivation, identity, and long-term values before offering concrete options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marco's input throughout and follows his choices, such as selecting almonds, using a hallway closet, and setting a phone alarm. However, rapid-fire questions and structured options can reduce the sense of fully client-led collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits desire, need, ability, and commitment through questions about importance, confidence, and small next steps. It could be strengthened by amplifying and summarizing Marco's values-based change talk at key moments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Marco's sustain talk and redirects toward practical problem-solving. More sophisticated MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or pivots, would better soften the focus on reasons to maintain the status quo.
