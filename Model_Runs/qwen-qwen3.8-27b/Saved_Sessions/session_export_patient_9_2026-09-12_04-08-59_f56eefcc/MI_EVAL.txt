# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently warm, respectful, and affirming while avoiding blame or judgment. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicitly normalizing the emotional difficulty of the co-parenting conflict and affirming the client’s worth beyond her actions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong reflections of the client’s ambivalence, fear, and burden. Gains toward a 5 would come from adding more nuanced, emotion-focused reflections, especially around moments of grief or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays reasonably focused on the presenting concern and appropriately shifts to safety planning when concerning disclosures emerge. A stronger direction would involve narrowing the work to a single primary behavioral target for the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and invites input, but often supplies specific scripts and plan details rather than co-creating them with the client. More questions that draw out the client’s own ideas would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and avoids coercive language, supporting the client’s sense of ownership. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by the frequent introduction of clinician-generated scripts and planning steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes the client’s values and motivations at times but often moves quickly into advice and planning. More evocative follow-up questions would help build the client’s internal motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows the client to influence the direction of the session. The interaction, however, remains somewhat expert-driven when the psychiatrist provides structured scripts and plans before fully exploring the client’s own resources.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable efforts to elicit values, goals, and some change-oriented language from the client. The main limitation is not consistently amplifying or building on the client’s change talk with deeper reflective follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing the client’s sustain talk but does not frequently use MI-consistent strategies to soften it. There were missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections or gently reconnect concerns to the client’s core values.
