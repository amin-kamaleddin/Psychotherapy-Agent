# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Marek's social integration without shaming or arguing. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper by affirming the emotional and identity meaning of his weekend drinking, not just the practical plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Marek's perspective, ambivalence, and practical concerns about fitting in and not feeling like an odd one out. Empathy would be stronger with deeper reflections on underlying emotions, fear of embarrassment, and identity attached to bar nights.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of reducing weekend beer consumption while preserving his social experience. Direction is clear, coherent, and well balanced with the client's pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed from Marek's own ideas about eating, staying busy, and ordering early, which supports collaboration. Collaboration could be improved by giving the client more explicit control over the agenda and direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently frames change as Marek's own choice, using language such as “on your terms,” “choosing from the inside,” and “your own choice.” Autonomy is strongly supported without pressure, advice-giving, or external mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully elicits Marek's own ideas for change and builds a concrete, personally meaningful plan. Evocation could be broadened by exploring values, confidence, readiness, and deeper motivational statements beyond the specific behavior plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marek is treated as the expert on his weekend routines, and his ideas clearly shape the emerging plan. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly negotiating the agenda and sharing control over where the conversation goes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk around reducing beer intake and maintaining social involvement. Change talk could be expanded to include more explicit confidence, reasons, need, and commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is reflected rather than argued with, and the counselor gently redirects toward low-pressure conditions for trying a smaller change. The most central sustain talk—changing only after a serious health consequence—could be more fully explored with double-sided reflections.
