# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful positive regard throughout. Ambivalence and self-care are normalized and affirmed without criticism or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening effectively captures ambivalence, social concerns, and the meaning behind the client's goals. The main limitation is that some responses affirm and reframe rather than fully accessing the emotional weight of repeated failed attempts, frustration, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a coherent focus on tobacco use in a social context and moves effectively toward a small, concrete experiment. Direction could be strengthened by more explicitly anchoring the plan to the broader cessation goal and the client's core values about health, money, and self-efficacy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the conversation using the client's language, ideas, and social context. The client actively shapes the plan, and the psychiatrist does not behave as the sole problem-solver.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's choice, pace, and ownership are consistently respected. The small-step plan is framed as optional and self-directed, with no coercion, persuasion, or directive advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently elicit the client's own values, goals, concerns, and solutions. The psychiatrist avoids supplying external reasons for change and instead helps the client articulate her own motivation and ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly treats the client as the expert in her own life and social world. The client's contributions substantially shape the direction of the session and the development of the small-step experiment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Future-oriented questions effectively bring out concrete change talk related to breathing, money, self-monitoring, and small steps. Some opportunities are missed to deepen change talk across importance, need, ability, and commitment before moving into problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging social connection, fear of awkwardness, and readiness concerns without argument. A key growth area is more consistently redirecting these sustaining concerns back into the client's own reasons for change.
