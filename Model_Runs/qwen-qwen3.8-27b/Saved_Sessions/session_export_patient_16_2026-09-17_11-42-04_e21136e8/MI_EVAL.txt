# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, including validating Anika's defensive framing without judgment ('Fair. I'm not here to sort you into a box'). No labeling, disapproval, or minimizing language at any point in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking that captures nuances Anika couldn't articulate herself, such as naming the frustration as 'in a quiet way' and reflecting the involuntary nature of the referral. Every reflection builds precisely on what Anika actually said without misreading or over-interpreting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the target behavior with a logical arc from defensiveness to self-awareness, following Anika's leads (basketball, sleep, friendship). The session ends in exploration without a collaborative landing point or concrete next step, which prevents a full 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration rather than an interview, with the psychiatrist consistently using Anika's own language and metaphors back to her. Anika's ideas substantially shape where the conversation goes, reflecting true co-construction of meaning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Powerful and repeated autonomy statements ('You don't have to have it all figured out,' 'You don't have to fix anything today,' 'This is your space') that remove pressure and obligation. No directive language or implied correct answers at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is drawn entirely from Anika's own values and identity, with zero external persuasion or didactic content. Anika generates all change talk herself, with the psychiatrist serving purely as a skilled mirror and questioner.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Anika as the expert on her own experience ('You're the one who knows what it feels like,' 'I don't need to tell you this matters — you already do'). No advice-giving, no 'in my experience' or 'research shows,' and a clearly egalitarian power dynamic throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits multiple forms of change talk (desire, ability, need, commitment) through open questions that draw out Anika's own values and discrepancy. Could be strengthened by more explicit summary reflections that tie the accumulated change statements together.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is fully validated before any gentle pivot ('I believe all of that'), with no 'but,' no correction, and no making Anika feel wrong for her low-risk framing. The shift toward change is accomplished entirely through curiosity about her own values rather than counter-argument — textbook MI.
