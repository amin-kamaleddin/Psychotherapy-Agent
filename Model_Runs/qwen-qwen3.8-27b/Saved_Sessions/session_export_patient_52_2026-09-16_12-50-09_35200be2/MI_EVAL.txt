# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental regard. The clinician accepted the client’s realistic limits without moralizing or labeling her drinking as irresponsible.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and captured the client’s burden, exhaustion, and ambivalence. Some opportunities were missed to deepen the emotional meaning behind the behavior, such as guilt, loneliness, or the relief drinking provides.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained focused on alcohol use as a coping behavior and the client’s readiness for a small change. It moved clearly from concerns and ambivalence to a concrete, behavior-specific plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructed a practical plan with the client and used her preferences and routines as the basis for change. Collaboration was occasionally weakened by the psychiatrist’s tendency to supply options before fully exploring the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was allowed to define change in her own terms and maintain control over the pace and scope of the plan. Autonomy support was slightly reduced by repeated clinician-generated tactics and some imperative language in the recap.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited the client’s own motivations, concerns, and solutions through questions and scaling. Evocation could be strengthened by drawing out more of the client’s values and past resources before introducing concrete strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treated the client as the expert and invited her input on timing, strategies, and follow-up. The frequent introduction of option lists created a subtle expert-driven dynamic that could limit fully client-led problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked and reinforced the client’s motivations, especially around energy for her son, school, and having a plan that fits her life. The most powerful change talk could have been more consistently reflected, amplified, and expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician did not argue with the client’s minimization and respectfully acknowledged her ambivalence and preferences. The use of double-sided reflections or explicit validation of both sides of the ambivalence could have been stronger.
