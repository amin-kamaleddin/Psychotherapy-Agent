# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, validating stance and normalized the client’s childcare/work conflict without blame. Acceptance was strong but mostly situation-focused rather than explicitly affirming the client’s worth beyond her roles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured the practical and emotional weight of the client’s concerns, including ambivalence about asking her sister. Empathy could have gone deeper by exploring the fear, shame, or loss of control implied by feeling “stuck again.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stayed focused on the target problem and moved logically from childcare concerns to concrete options and contingency planning. Direction was strong but remained more logistical than motivational and did not fully consolidate into a clear client-owned action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked with the client to build a plan using the client’s own ideas and resources. Collaboration was evident but could have been made more explicit through shared “we” language or a co-constructed agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invited the client to choose and plan without pressure or coercion. Autonomy support was strong but occasionally weakened by clinician-generated suggestions before fully eliciting the client’s own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Most of the practical plan was evoked from the client, including her sister, community centers, backup options, and call preparation. Evocation was effective but focused more on next steps than on drawing out the client’s deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the client as the expert and allowed her ideas to shape the session. Partnership was strong but occasionally shifted when the psychiatrist offered specific suggestions before fully inviting the client’s own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expressed confidence and ability, and the psychiatrist reflected some of this back. However, change talk was not consistently deepened, amplified, or summarized in a way that strengthened the client’s motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged barriers without arguing, minimizing, or reinforcing a “can’t” narrative. Sustain talk was softened appropriately but not consistently transformed into evidence of capability or motivation.
