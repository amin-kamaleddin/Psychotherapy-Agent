# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental stance with multiple affirmations and respectful language throughout. Acceptance is functional and task-oriented rather than deeply emotionally resonant, with limited normalization of the client's broader experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking, including picking up meaning behind surface statements. Misses opportunities to explore the emotional layer beneath dismissive or brief responses from the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on the target behavior but the session has a circular quality with repeated similar questions. Lacks strategic momentum toward a specific objective or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative language and framing as a joint exploration, with invitations for client input. The psychiatrist still drives the conversation's structure and pace, keeping the client somewhat passive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support with explicit, repeated reinforcement of the client's right to make his own decisions. No pressure, coercion, or implied obligation to change; consistently frames choices as the client's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine attempts to draw out the client's own motivations through evocative questions. Evocation is superficial and backs off when the client gives brief answers, missing opportunities to find alternative angles.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear partnership stance positioning the client as the expert on his own life, with explicit deference to his knowledge. Could be stronger in explicitly acknowledging the client's expertise and allowing his contributions to more substantially shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Identifies and reflects change talk when it appears but does not consistently amplify or expand on it. Missed opportunities to turn brief statements into value or identity reflections that would deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles minimization and dismissiveness well without arguing, correcting, or reinforcing the sustain talk. Could have been slightly more strategic with double-sided reflections or gentle pivots in a couple of moments.
