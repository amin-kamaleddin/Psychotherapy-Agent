# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and normalizes the client's ambivalence without moralizing or pressure. Acceptance is consistently supportive, though it could include slightly more explicit affirmation of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening using the client's own metaphors shows accurate perspective-taking. It could go deeper on feelings of looking like a pain, sounding ridiculous, and loss of the break.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on managing the lost work break and progresses logically from identifying the hardest part to a concrete alternative. It would benefit from more consistent linkage to broader smoking reduction or cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently invites client input and builds on the client's ideas rather than directing the conversation. The client's contributions strongly shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, with repeated respect for the client's choice and explicit boundary against lectures. The counselor helps the client generate options without prescribing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, non-advisory questions elicit the client's own thinking about what the break meant and how a reset could work. Evocation could be strengthened by drawing out more cessation-specific reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor clearly positions himself as a partner rather than an expert, following the client's lead. The client's ideas drive the direction and content of the interview.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-relevant language is elicited about needing a break and trying a two-minute pause. However, there is relatively little explicit change talk about quitting, cutting back, or confidence in changing smoking behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, reassuring, or emphasizing health risks, and the conversation is redirected to the underlying need for a break. It could use more deliberate double-sided reflections to balance enjoyment of smoking with emerging alternatives.
