# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and generally avoids pressure while validating Eric’s practical concerns. Some responses slightly overstate emotional burden and lack fuller affirmation of Eric as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician shows strong reflective listening about Eric’s work, need for simplicity, and dislike of being tested. The empathy is mostly cognitive rather than deeply exploring underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with smoking but drifts into general coping, breaks, and symptom monitoring. It needs a clearer through line toward a specific smoking-related change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Eric’s lead and builds on his preferences for no counting, slower smoking, and simple checks. The clinician still controls the question sequence, limiting full co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes Eric’s choice and respects his preference for a low-control plan. It would be stronger by explicitly affirming his right to decide either way, including not changing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks Eric to generate practical steps, but the evocation is more procedural than motivational. It misses deeper exploration of values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Eric as an expert and accepts his practical preferences as central. It remains less fully partnered because the clinician still steers the agenda without more explicit direction invitations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Eric offers limited change talk, and the psychiatrist builds on it in a modest way. The session does not consistently ask about importance, desire, ability, or confidence to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Eric’s reasons to maintain smoking, but often lets sustain talk organize the conversation. It needs more skillful redirection toward change while preserving autonomy.
