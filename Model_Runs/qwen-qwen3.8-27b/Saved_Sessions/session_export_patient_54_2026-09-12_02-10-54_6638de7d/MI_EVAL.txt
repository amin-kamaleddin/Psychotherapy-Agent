# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of the patient’s concerns and boundaries. Acceptance is strong but slightly procedural rather than deeply personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient’s ambivalence and emotional shift toward readiness. Some deeper emotional and cultural tensions are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and moves logically toward a practical plan. The shift to planning could have allowed slightly more exploration of motivation first.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is largely shaped by the patient’s own ideas, especially involving her husband to hold the wine. Collaboration is slightly weakened when a more counselor-generated list of steps is introduced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces the patient’s right to choose, ownership, and control over the process. The patient clearly expresses that the decision is her own by the end.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions elicit practical plans, but there is limited evocation of deeper personal values and motivation. More questions about what motherhood, health, or success would mean to the patient could strengthen this.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, supporting a collaborative tone. The moment with the structured six-step list feels more expert-led before returning to the patient’s ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient generates useful change talk, but it is not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened. More strategic follow-up questions could help build stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk, which is appropriate, but does not actively soften or redirect it. More nuanced reflections around the family tradition and ambivalence could help reduce resistance.
