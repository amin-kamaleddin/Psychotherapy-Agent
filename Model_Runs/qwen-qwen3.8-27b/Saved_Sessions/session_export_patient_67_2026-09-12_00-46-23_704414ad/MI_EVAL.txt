# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment with affirmations and a compassionate closing statement. However, the warmth is somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper individualized validation of Kenji's fear, exhaustion, or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at the content level and help clarify Kenji's experiences. They remain mostly factual rather than emotional, with missed opportunities to explore the meaning behind statements about being scared, stuck, or minimizing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol reduction and related concerns without major drift. The early safety check and broad opening question are reasonable, though slightly expansive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and adapts to Kenji's choices, allowing his input to shape the plan. However, structured lists and a preformed script can make the power balance feel uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is supported through preference questions, options, and a non-punitive relapse response. The prescriptive script and preparation tip could feel directive, and stronger reinforcement that Kenji is ultimately in charge would improve this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness rulers and barrier questions help elicit useful client language about importance, confidence, and triggers. The counselor moves somewhat quickly toward counselor-generated ideas and a script before fully eliciting Kenji's own strategies, values, or strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is used and Kenji's contributions shape the session. Still, frequent therapist-generated options and a detailed script make the interaction feel more counselor-led than truly client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes change talk using readiness rulers and guided choices, leading Kenji to voice willingness to try a small step. However, the change talk is not consistently amplified, deepened, or explored in connection to values and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and normalizes ambivalence, which helps reduce shame. That said, key sustain talk is acknowledged only superficially and not actively reframed or softened with double-sided reflections.
