# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard, never expressing disapproval of smoking or moralizing about health risks. Actively defends the patient's self-worth and explicitly honors his boundary about not quitting, with warmth that is genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides highly accurate and complex reflections that capture both content and underlying emotion, progressively deepening from surface-level to identity-level meaning. Standout example: 'It's not the smoke that's changed, it's who's there with you' — a reframe the patient had not articulated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression from understanding the problem to identifying alternatives to a concrete first step, without drifting into tangential topics. Could benefit from one or two consolidation summaries to mark the shift from exploration to action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as joint exploration, incorporating the patient's own language and metaphors ('reset button') to shape the session in real time. The power dynamic is genuinely balanced — the patient sets boundaries and the therapist works within them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Perhaps the strongest aspect of the session; every suggestion is framed as the patient's choice and his 'out' is explicitly preserved. Never uses directive language; the patient's autonomy is the organizing principle of the entire session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is drawn from within the patient — his own values, language, and ideas ('reset button,' 'two minutes of body time') drive the session. The therapist never provides a single external reason for change, instead skillfully reflecting the patient's words back with depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within the patient, never assuming the role of expert. The therapist's self-correction ('I shouldn't have made it sound like a quick fix') levels the hierarchy and models a fellow-traveler stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully and consistently evokes change talk through well-timed open questions, culminating in a concrete, self-generated plan ('Tomorrow. Lunch, probably... I'd just step out, not light up'). The evocation never feels like interrogation and progressively builds toward action.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately validates the patient's sustain talk rather than arguing against it, and skillfully distinguishes between enjoying smoking and the actual concern (loss of social routine). Could be more strategically active with double-sided reflections or gentle pivots when the patient reasserts 'I'm not quitting.'
