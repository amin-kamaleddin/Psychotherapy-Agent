# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates respectful, warm, and supportive language with multiple affirmations and validation of Marco's feelings. The warmth occasionally feels slightly formulaic in structure (reflect + affirm + redirect), and a deeper emotional attunement to his vulnerability about being 47 would elevate this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate capture of emotional nuance throughout the session. Reflections occasionally lean toward interpretation and strategic direction rather than pure emotional attunement, which slightly limits the empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with logical flow from processing frustration to building commitment. Pacing occasionally moves slightly ahead of Marco's emotional processing, where an additional reflective pause could deepen the connection.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent collaboration throughout, with the psychiatrist working within Marco's frame using his own metaphors and treating him as an equal partner. The session is structured as a genuine joint exploration where Marco generates the majority of content and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports Marco's autonomy through open questions and reflecting back his own ideas rather than prescribing solutions. Could more explicitly affirm Marco's right to choose differently, as the strategic momentum occasionally overtakes explicit affirmation of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest area of the session, with the psychiatrist consistently eliciting motivation from within Marco rather than providing external reasons for change. Marco generates nearly all of the change talk himself, while the psychiatrist reflects, amplifies, and builds on it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook partnership with language consistently positioning Marco as the expert on his own life and the psychiatrist as a facilitator rather than a director. The psychiatrist never tells Marco what to do; instead, they reflect, affirm, and ask questions that let Marco generate his own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout area with skillful amplification of Marco's own words, highlighting his problem-solving, and reframing his plan as a strength. The psychiatrist consistently evokes and reinforces DARN-C change talk with excellent timing and specificity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating Marco's concerns while maintaining momentum, and honors the ritual aspect rather than arguing. Could be more explicit with double-sided reflections that acknowledge the pull of the old habit while gently tilting toward change.
