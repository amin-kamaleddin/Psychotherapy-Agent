# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, acceptance, and nonjudgment without moralizing about smoking. Warmth was strong but somewhat formulaic, with fewer explicit affirmations of Lucía’s strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Lucía’s frustration, fatigue, and need for simplicity. Some reflections remained surface-level and could have explored deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed generally focused on smoking-related stress, but direction became inconsistent after Lucía declined to make a plan. More connection between her values and a possible voluntary next step would have strengthened focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Lucía’s preferences and involved her in shaping the follow-up plan. The counselor still supplied structure and options, so the session was collaborative but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a major strength, with repeated affirmations that Lucía controls the process and no pressure to change. This reduced defensiveness and honored her choice to pause.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked an initial evocative question but did not follow up on Lucía’s values or change-relevant statements. Later questions were mainly logistical rather than eliciting her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session reflected a respectful partnership stance by inviting input and avoiding an expert-driven tone. It could have more explicitly affirmed Lucía as the expert on her own life and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Lucía offered some change-relevant material, but the psychiatrist did not consistently reflect or expand on it. There was limited use of values clarification, scaling, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by accepting Lucía’s need for a break without arguing or reinforcing resistance. Using more double-sided reflections could have better connected sustain talk to underlying values.
