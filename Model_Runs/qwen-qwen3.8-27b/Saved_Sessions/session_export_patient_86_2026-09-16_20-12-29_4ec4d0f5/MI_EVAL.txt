# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows unconditional positive regard with respectful, nonjudgmental language. Even when the client expresses resistance, there is no pushing, shaming, or corrective stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong reflective listening and accurately captures ambivalence and values. However, some reflections feel slightly inferential, and the emotional weight of the client’s long smoking history could have been explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and moves from general openness to a concrete, behavior-specific plan. The target behavior remains central throughout without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is mostly collaborative, with the client’s ideas shaping the plan and the psychiatrist using joint language such as “if we.” There are a few moments where counselor-initiated suggestions briefly reduce the egalitarian quality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated emphasis on the client’s freedom, choice, and right to stop if the process becomes aversive. The psychiatrist avoids coercion and keeps the plan client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits client-generated motivation, including personal warning signs and a simple self-check. The score is held at 4 because there are occasional counselor-led suggestions and less explicit elicitation of deeper values, confidence, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally positions the client as the expert and allows the client’s ideas to guide the session. A few counselor-generated suggestions, such as the one-line question or partner signal, briefly reduce the partnership quality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes meaningful change talk by inviting the client to generate his own conditions, signs, and self-observations. It is not a 5 because the full range of change talk, especially commitment and confidence language, is not explicitly developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and validates the client’s reasons for not changing rather than arguing with them. However, some sustain talk—especially around long-standing identity, past failures, and fear of another false start—could have been reflected more fully before redirecting.
