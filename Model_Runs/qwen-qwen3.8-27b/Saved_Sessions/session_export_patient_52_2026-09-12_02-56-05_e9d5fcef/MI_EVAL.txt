# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician showed basic respect with statements like 'You're juggling a lot,' but repeated advice and behavioral experiments weakened unconditional acceptance. The client's pushback on needing a science lesson suggests acceptance was present but not consistently warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflected exhaustion, guilt, fear of rumination in the quiet, and the store as a pinch point. Deeper empathy could explore the emotional meaning of alcohol as a coping tool and its connection to parenting and identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on nightly alcohol use and stress coping without drifting into unrelated topics. The main limitation was becoming narrow on specific behavioral steps rather than broader motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asked what would help and adapted to the client's preferred experiments, such as a podcast, dishes, stretching, and buying one beer. However, the clinician remained the primary driver of the agenda, so collaboration was uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used permissive language such as 'It's your choice' and respected the client's refusals of a timer and scheduled check-in. Repeated new suggestions and corrective information reduced the consistency of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and a confidence scale helped the client identify a practical plan. Motivation was not consistently drawn out because the clinician frequently supplied facts, options, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited input and permission, and the client influenced details like Thursday, a true-crime podcast, dishes, and buying one beer. Frequent directive suggestions created an uneven power dynamic rather than a fully collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicited some change talk around wanting to feel present, having more energy, skipping the second beer, and buying only one. The clinician did not consistently amplify or connect this talk to core values such as parenting, health, or autonomy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client repeatedly expressed sustain talk by saying two drinks are normal, that her family drinks similarly, and that experiments feel like chores. The clinician often responded with psychoeducation and more suggestions, which risked strengthening resistance rather than gently redirecting it.
