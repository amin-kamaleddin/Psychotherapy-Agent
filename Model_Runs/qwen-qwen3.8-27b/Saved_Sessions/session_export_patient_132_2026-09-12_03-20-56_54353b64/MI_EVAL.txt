# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout. However, some validation feels formulaic and does not fully acknowledge the emotional weight of the client's body-image struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's ambivalence and distress around the scale and motivation. Deeper exploration of underlying fear of failure could strengthen empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on specific target behaviors, including gym frequency, scale monitoring, and food-related guilt. The psychiatrist guides the conversation clearly from one barrier to the next.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input but generates most of the solution-focused content. More collaboration would involve drawing out the client's own ideas and past strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is asked to choose among options, which acknowledges agency. However, the choices are framed within psychiatrist-generated advice, limiting fully autonomous decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often provides reasons for change and specific strategies rather than eliciting the client's own motivations. This approach can reduce the client's opportunity to discover their own reasons and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently takes on an expert role by offering menus of options. This can make the session feel more directive than collaborative or equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through open questions. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or build on the client's expressed motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but often addressed by moving quickly into problem-solving or advice. More balanced reflections could help the client explore and resolve ambivalence.
