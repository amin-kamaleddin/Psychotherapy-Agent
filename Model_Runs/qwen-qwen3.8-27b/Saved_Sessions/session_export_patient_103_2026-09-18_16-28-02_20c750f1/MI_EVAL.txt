### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout, including when Nabil is confrontational. Validates his need for respect as legitimate and reflects his self-deprecation with specific, individualized warmth rather than generic reassurance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface content to capture emotional nuance, such as identifying the self-doubt in 'I don't know' and extending the phone battery metaphor to articulate the relational impact. Empathy consistently deepens rather than remaining at surface level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on Nabil's anger in medical settings with a logical progression from validation to exploration to commitment. Slightly less tightly coupled in the middle section where exploration of 'long ago' ventures into deeper relational territory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with consistent use of Nabil's own language and metaphors as the foundation for the work. Minor moments of clinician-led direction in selecting and framing material rather than inviting Nabil to choose the focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Nabil as the expert on his own life, using open questions and evocative language without any prescriptive or directive statements. Accepts his tentative 'that might... yeah' without filling silence or pressuring for a more concrete answer.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All insights and conclusions are Nabil-generated; the psychiatrist creates conditions for him to discover his own motivations without offering external reasons or techniques. The final spontaneous commitment ('telling her I'm at one percent') is entirely client-generated, hallmarking excellent evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance that treats Nabil's insights as central to the session's direction. Slight 'leading' quality in moments where the psychiatrist selects what to sit with rather than asking Nabil where he wants to go next.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk through strategic questions like 'What's the cost of that protection to you?' and well-calibrated closing questions. Could have more explicitly consolidated Nabil's final commitment language to fully amplify the shift.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles barriers like 'they're not gonna change' and 'nothing's different' by validating them while gently highlighting emerging change talk. Textbook avoidance of contradicting or debating while redirecting toward Nabil's own resources.
