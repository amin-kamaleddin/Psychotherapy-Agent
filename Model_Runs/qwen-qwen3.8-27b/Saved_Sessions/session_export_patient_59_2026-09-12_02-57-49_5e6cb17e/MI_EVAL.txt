# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, low-pressure stance and validates Jamal's concerns. However, acceptance feels administrative rather than deeply affirming of Jamal's intrinsic worth and struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Jamal's practical agenda and compliance focus. Empathy is limited by missed opportunities to explore underlying emotions such as fear, embarrassment, or family concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is well organized around the immediate administrative goal of completing court/probation requirements. Direction remains narrow and task-based rather than clearly focused on broader behavioral or motivational change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and follows Jamal's preference to read the paperwork and create a checklist. Collaboration is practical but uneven, with the psychiatrist serving as the main source of information and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses autonomy-supportive language early on and respects Jamal's preferences. Support is limited mostly to administrative decisions rather than reinforcing Jamal's ownership of choices about future risk, values, or behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with open questions but quickly shifts to information-giving and task completion. There is limited effort to draw out Jamal's own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist opens collaboratively and offers options, creating some initial partnership. The session then becomes more expert-driven, reducing the sense that Jamal is primarily responsible for direction and insight.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal offers several statements that could have been developed into change talk, especially around avoiding trouble and protecting family relationships. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect or expand these statements to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Jamal's minimization, which is appropriate. However, sustain talk is not actively redirected, reflected back in a balanced way, or transformed into motivation for change.
