# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, validates the client's willingness, and avoids judgment while she minimizes her drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses strong complex reflections and accurately captures ambivalence, values, and the coping role of alcohol. Deeper attention to fear, the fall, and family concern would strengthen emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and related concerns, such as falls, sleep, steadiness, and social worry. The conversation moves coherently from values to a specific target situation and then to concrete behavioral options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the clinician using shared language and inviting the client's input at each step. The client's ideas clearly shape the session rather than the clinician prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports the client's sense of choice and avoids pressure or coercion. Language affirming non-all-or-nothing goals reinforces ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively draws out the client's own motivations and solutions through open questions. Some questions are slightly leading or embed the desired answer, which limits the score.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician clearly positions the client as the expert on her own life and preferences. The plan is generated through questions and reflections rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently elicits change talk related to desire, ability, and specific plans. More strategic amplification of the strongest reasons for change would raise the score.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully handles sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing reasons to maintain drinking. Double-sided and validating language reduces defensiveness while keeping change possible.
