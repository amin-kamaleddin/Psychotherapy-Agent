# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, normalizing David's behavior and accepting his limitations without pressure. Could benefit from more explicit affirmations of David's worth or self-awareness as a person beyond his struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy throughout, capturing the emotional and experiential core of David's statements rather than just the content. Complex reflections such as identifying the control/identity theme and the final synthesis of the cigarette as tied to his identity story demonstrate deep tracking of his emotional shifts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and coherent therapeutic arc from surface presentation to deep identity-level insight, with each question building logically on the previous answer. The session ends at the insight level rather than gently steering toward a concrete, achievable next step or behavioral anchor.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a model of collaborative interaction with every intervention framed as a question or reflection inviting David's contribution. The psychiatrist consistently validates David's insights as genuinely useful to the process, maintaining an equal footing throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent and explicit support for David's autonomy, including the excellent use of qualifiers like 'even a little' and phrases like 'I don't think you need to solve it all tonight.' The psychiatrist never directs, prescribes, or pressures, respecting David's right to not know.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation and insight emerges organically from David rather than being provided externally, with the psychiatrist offering almost no information or reasons for change. The 'talking to a friend' question is a masterful evocation that bypasses self-criticism and accesses David's own compassionate, solution-oriented voice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David is consistently positioned as the expert on his own experience, with questions inviting his assessment rather than imposing the psychiatrist's. The session is clearly co-constructed, with each question emerging from David's previous answer and the 'talking to a friend' technique reinforcing his role as the one with the wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk emerges organically and powerfully in response to the psychiatrist's strategic questions, culminating in David's own self-compassion and statements like 'I'd like to be, though.' The psychiatrist skillfully amplifies David's insights at key moments, encouraging further self-exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively without reinforcement, and the response to 'I don't have a good answer' appropriately reduces pressure. However, when David expresses doubt about being ready, the psychiatrist could more explicitly affirm the validity of that ambivalence before pivoting to the next question.
