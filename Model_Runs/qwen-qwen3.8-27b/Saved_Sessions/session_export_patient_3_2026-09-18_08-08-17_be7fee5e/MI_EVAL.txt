# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent, warm, and genuine unconditional positive regard throughout, never judging Aisha's body or self-descriptions. Language is affirming and individualized, normalizing struggle as a universal human experience rather than personal failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface paraphrasing to capture identity-level loss and emotional mechanisms Aisha only implied, such as naming the fear of the failure cycle. Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistently attuned to the client's meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a clear logical arc from motivation through barriers to concrete planning, with each question building progressively. The final portion drifts slightly into broader self-judgment patterns beyond the specific exercise target, representing a minor departure from tight directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as genuine co-creation, with the psychiatrist contributing reflections and strategic questions while Aisha drives the content, solutions, and self-insights. The power dynamic is clearly equal, with the psychiatrist building on Aisha's exact language in real-time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Aisha's autonomy is the organizing principle of every question, from the opening 'no pressure' statement to explicitly validating that she defines what 'doing it' looks like. There is no directive language, advice-giving, or implied obligation anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never offers external reasons for change; every question is designed to draw out Aisha's own motivations, feelings, and plans. The evocation skillfully moves from global motivation to specific, felt-sense change talk, with Aisha generating her own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Aisha as the expert on her own life, using language like 'you're already showing real self-awareness' rather than prescriptive advice. Aisha's contributions drive the session forward, and the psychiatrist acts as a facilitator rather than a director.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are strategically designed to elicit and deepen Aisha's own language in favor of change, moving from vision to ability to planning to emotional relief. The progression successfully evokes increasingly specific and felt-sense change talk throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** When Aisha expresses doubt or self-limiting beliefs, the psychiatrist acknowledges them briefly without reinforcing or dwelling, then pivots to a change-oriented frame. The 'all or nothing' pattern is skillfully reframed, though a more explicit double-sided reflection technique would have strengthened this criterion further.
