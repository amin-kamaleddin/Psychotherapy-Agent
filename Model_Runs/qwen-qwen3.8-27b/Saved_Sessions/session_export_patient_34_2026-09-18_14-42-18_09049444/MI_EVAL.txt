# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm throughout, even when the client is dismissive. Slightly formulaic in repeated phrases like 'I hear you' without always adding a new layer of individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate reflections of the client's perspective, including the skilled double-sided reflection. Occasionally stays at the cognitive level rather than fully accessing the emotional texture beneath the words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from understanding the incident to exploring the tension between social life and alcohol. Could benefit from slightly more strategic summarization to maintain momentum toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treats the client as an equal partner, inviting joint exploration through open-ended questions. Language invites collaboration rather than directing, though the session remains somewhat counselor-led in terms of where the conversation goes next.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support throughout, consistently framing everything as the client's own choice and perspective. The final question about 'what would you even be figuring out?' is a masterful handling of autonomy in a resistant pre-contemplation client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently evokes the client's own thinking rather than persuading, using questions that draw out the client's internal reasoning. The distinction between 'don't know how' and 'don't want to' is a particularly strong evocation move.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong partnership stance with phrases like 'you know yourself better than I do' and positions self as a curious explorer. Could be elevated by more explicit power-sharing moments, such as asking the client what he'd like to focus on.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully identifies and explores change talk, such as picking up on the client's willingness to pace drinking. Could more explicitly reflect and amplify the change talk the client does offer to strengthen its impact.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating the client's position while gently opening doors (e.g., 'those two things don't actually have to be in conflict'). One moment where the response could have more explicitly reflected back the client's own words as a subtle seed of change talk.
