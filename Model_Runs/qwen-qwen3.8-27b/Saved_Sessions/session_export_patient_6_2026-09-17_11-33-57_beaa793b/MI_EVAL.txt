# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance with genuine affirmations and positive regard. However, the warmth occasionally feels slightly scripted or formulaic rather than spontaneously individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and consistent reflective listening that goes beyond surface content to capture emotional and meaning-level understanding. Reflections consistently mirror the client's ambivalence, identity concerns, and internal conflicts with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from understanding concern through ambivalence to practical strategy, with each question building purposefully on the previous. A brief collaborative summary or agenda-setting near the beginning could have strengthened the strategic architecture.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative from start to finish, with the clinician functioning as a guide who follows the client's lead and invites him to shape direction. The power dynamic is consistently balanced, with the client's contributions genuinely driving the session's content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports the client's autonomy, never pressuring or implying obligation, and frames all change as the client's own decision. The clinician explicitly reinforces ownership by positioning plans as the client's ideas, not external advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation—every significant piece of insight originates from the client, drawn out by well-timed open questions rather than clinician-provided reasons. The pivotal question about making the reason bigger successfully elicits the client's most powerful change statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life, inviting him to explain his own reasoning rather than validating it on his behalf. The clinician never assumes the expert role or provides unsolicited advice, maintaining an equal footing throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk with questions that prompt the client to generate his own compelling reasons and commit to practical steps. Could be strengthened by more systematic elicitation of confidence/ability talk, such as scaling questions or rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively handles sustain talk without reinforcing or arguing against it, holding both sides in reflections that keep the client's minimization neutral. More explicit use of double-sided reflections to honor sustain talk while pivoting could elevate the approach.
