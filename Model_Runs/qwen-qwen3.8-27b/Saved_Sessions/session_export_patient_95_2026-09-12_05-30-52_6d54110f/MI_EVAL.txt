# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently expresses respect, nonjudgment, and validation, even amid intense suicidal statements. However, repeated safety-focused questions sometimes make the acceptance feel less warm and uninterrupted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers good reflective listening and validates Maria’s feelings of invisibility, exhaustion, and pain. Some deeper emotional statements are not fully reflected, leaving the empathy strong but not consistently penetrating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on safety and emotional suffering, but it lacks a clear strategic direction toward a specific change goal or target behavior. The conversation often alternates between validation, safety questions, and small-step offers without coherent movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Maria’s input, asks permission, and offers choices, supporting a collaborative stance. Nevertheless, much of the session remains driven by the therapist’s safety assessment rather than fully co-created by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is frequently supported through explicit statements of choice, permission, and no-pressure language. Repeated safety and means-related requests, however, can feel directive and may undermine Maria’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some open-ended and evocative questions, but there are limited efforts to elicit Maria’s own reasons, values, or motivation for staying safe. Missed opportunities include not exploring statements such as “I’m still here, barely” as sources of change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session reflects a generally nonexpert, partner-like posture with strong language such as “You choose” and “You’re in charge.” The repeated safety agenda can still make the therapist appear more like an assessor than a true partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria offers several signs of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not actively reflect, amplify, or develop them. The session would benefit from more deliberate responses that highlight Maria’s own reasons for staying.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Maria and validates her suffering, but does not effectively soften her sustain talk. There is limited use of double-sided reflections, ambivalence-holding, or reframing statements such as “nothing works” and “I can’t take this anymore.”
