# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance that reduces shame. Acceptance could be strengthened with more individualized affirmations of Laura’s worth and effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture Laura’s frustration and ambivalence. Deeper exploration of her guilt, self-judgment, and vulnerability would increase empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on protecting a restorative yoga/break and moves logically from barrier to plan. Direction would be stronger if the concrete behavior were periodically linked back to Laura’s core values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura’s preferences visibly shape the boundary language and final routine. Collaboration could improve if she generated more alternatives rather than primarily selecting from clinician-provided options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura is repeatedly given choices and permission to adapt the plan. Autonomy could be further supported by more explicitly affirming her ownership and reducing prescriptive steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions invite Laura’s concerns, but the psychiatrist relies more on supplying solutions than eliciting her values, reasons, and confidence. More evocative questions are needed to draw out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session invites Laura’s input and she helps shape choices. Partnership is limited by frequent expert-led lists and problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Laura offers change talk, but the psychiatrist often shifts to problem-solving instead of reflecting or amplifying it. More evocative follow-ups would better build her motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met without argument and often normalized. Double-sided reflections or values-linked reframing would better soften resistance while preserving autonomy.
