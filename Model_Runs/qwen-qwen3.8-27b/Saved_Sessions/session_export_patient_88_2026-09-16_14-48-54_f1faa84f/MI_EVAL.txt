# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect and nonjudgment, especially while Marcus is defensive. Acceptance is strong but remains somewhat task-oriented rather than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Marcus’s practical concerns and ambivalence about leaving without appearing weak. More emotional mirroring of fear, embarrassment, or peer approval needs would make the empathy more nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the relevant issue and moves toward a concrete exit plan. It could be strengthened by connecting the plan more explicitly to broader values or goals around alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan with Marcus rather than taking an expert stance. Marcus’s ideas clearly shape the warning sign, exit strategy, and action steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes Marcus’s choice, awareness, and ability to manage the situation. There is no pressure to involve parents, pursue abstinence, or accept external control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out Marcus’s own strategies and self-awareness. Evocation is limited by staying more behavioral and situational than motivational about reducing alcohol use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Marcus as the expert on his own group, cues, and feasible options. The session feels collaborative and client-led rather than directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated capability and planning language, especially around leaving early. Deeper change talk about the importance, confidence, or values connected to reducing alcohol use is missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge Marcus’s sustain talk and redirects it toward practical options. The core idea that drinking is harmless or normal is avoided more than actively softened.
