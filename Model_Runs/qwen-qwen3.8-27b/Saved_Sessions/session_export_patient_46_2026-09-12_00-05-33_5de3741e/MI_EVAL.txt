# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Ethan’s annoyance and social context without shaming. Slightly reduced because occasional corrective interpretation and procedural warmth fall short of fully affirming values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor reflects emotions and social pressure but often moves quickly into questions or advice. Missed deeper reflections of ambivalence and belonging concerns keep empathy moderate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use, social pressure, and harm reduction. Direction is limited by moving too quickly to planning without guiding motivation and discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor invites choices and incorporates Ethan’s preferences into the plan, but supplies much of the content. The dynamic remains more expert-guided than jointly constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor explicitly offers choices, reduces pressure, and accepts rejections of tracking or buddy-checks. Advice-heavy recommendations slightly limit full self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor asks a few evocative questions but frequently provides explanations and solutions instead of eliciting Ethan’s values, identity, or reasons. Evocation is the weakest area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan helps choose some specifics, but the counselor often presents options and leads the session. The interaction is uneven, with less power sharing before recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor elicits several small action statements and asks evocative questions, but does not consistently deepen or reinforce Ethan’s own reasons for change. Change talk remains surface-level rather than strategically developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges social pressure, but rarely uses double-sided reflections or explores long-term costs. Sustain talk is managed more by acceptance than by strategic redirection.
