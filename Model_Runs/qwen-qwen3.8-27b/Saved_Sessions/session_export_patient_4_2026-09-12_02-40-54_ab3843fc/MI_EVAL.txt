# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance toward Aizada's fear and desire for testing. The warmth was somewhat formulaic and focused more on her medical-certainty demand than deeper individualized acceptance of her broader experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected surface-level emotions such as fear, frustration, and being dismissed. Deeper ambivalence, grief, exhaustion, fear of dying, and identity-level concerns were often missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on obtaining appropriate gastrointestinal evaluation and addressing practical barriers. However, the direction was mainly procedural and biomedical rather than strategically MI-oriented toward values, ambivalence, and personally owned motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited choices and adapted scripts to Aizada's concerns, showing partial collaboration. The interaction remained uneven because the psychiatrist supplied most of the content, tests, and scripts, making it more expert-assisted problem-solving than equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by offering options, respecting language preferences, and avoiding coercion. Some directive medical and behavioral guidance limited fully explicit affirmation that Aizada could modify, delay, or reject any part of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist began with an open question and asked about preferences, but much of the session was advice-giving and script-writing. There was limited drawing out of Aizada's internal motivations, values, ambivalence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Aizada's preferences and let her choices influence next steps. However, the psychiatrist often functioned as the expert providing ready-made solutions rather than consistently positioning Aizada as the expert on her own body and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aizada spontaneously offered several change-talk statements about needing care and not being able to keep living this way. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand these statements with evocative questions about values, confidence, or desired outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely validated Aizada's biomedical certainty and reinforced the push for more tests. There was little use of double-sided reflection or gentle reframing to help her hold both medical fear and possible stress-related contributions.
