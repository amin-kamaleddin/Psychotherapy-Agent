# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally respectful with supportive statements, but acceptance is sometimes evaluative rather than unconditional. A key miss occurs when the counselor repeats the checklist after the client explicitly rejects it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Makes brief empathic moves but misses the core emotional and identity-based concerns, such as fear of being seen as weird or losing peer belonging. Empathy remains surface-level and inconsistent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol use, next-day fogginess, school performance, and sports without wandering off topic. However, direction is repetitive and plan-focused rather than progressively building toward deeper consideration of change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Builds part of the plan around the client's tracking preference and asks which options feel doable, but still supplies most of the content. The session feels partially collaborative but more advisory than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Occasionally offers options and accepts the client's choice to focus on tracking, but repeatedly gives directives and reminders. Repeating the checklist after the client says he does not want supervision undermines his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Mostly instructive, offering strategies, scripts, and a tracking template rather than eliciting the client's own motivations. Strong evocable statements are not reflected back in a way that expands them or explores importance and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does not consistently treat the client as the primary expert on his own life and goals, often shifting into an advisory role. The repeated checklist near the end makes the interaction feel more managed than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client generates meaningful change talk about protecting his future, grades, and team spot, but the counselor does not consistently amplify or develop it. Change talk remains mostly client-generated rather than counselor-evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges fitting in and does not directly confront the client's status quo statements. However, more sophisticated MI moves are missed, such as double-sided reflections connecting sustain talk with the client's values.
