# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The provider was respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoided lecturing, especially after the client warned against a lung lecture. Acceptance was present but often functional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The provider accurately reflected the client’s practical concerns about stress, work, and wanting low-fuss relief. Empathy was less strong on deeper emotions such as frustration, embarrassment, fear, or feeling “like garbage.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking, daytime nicotine use, and the client’s ambivalence. It could have been stronger if the provider moved more intentionally toward clarifying values and a concrete client-centered focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some collaboration through asking preferences and reflecting the client’s perspective. However, the provider often dominated with product information, dosing, and medical cautions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The provider generally respected the client’s choices and avoided pressuring a quit plan. Autonomy support was slightly weakened by repeated prompts to try another option after clear resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The provider used some evocative questions, such as asking what would be different or how important change was. Evocation was inconsistent and often shifted too quickly into detailed product recommendations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The provider made some effort to involve the client in choosing an option and acknowledged the client’s expertise about his work. The interaction remained uneven because the provider frequently assumed an expert, product-advice role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few attempts to elicit change talk, but the provider did not consistently reflect, amplify, or develop the client’s own reasons for change. The session moved too quickly into product options rather than building the client’s motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The provider did not argue with the client, which is helpful, but did not effectively soften strong sustain talk or redirect it toward possible change. Repeated offers of nicotine replacement options did little to reduce the power of the client’s resistance.
