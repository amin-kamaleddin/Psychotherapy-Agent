# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental throughout, from the opening 'there's no pressure' to reframing Isabella's self-criticism ('That's not an excuse — that's a plan'). Never expresses disappointment or pressure, even when Isabella expresses doubt about following through.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly specific and emotionally precise, often capturing feelings Isabella had not fully articulated (e.g., 'if no one sees it, it didn't count'). Accurately identifies the internal critic and the double-sided nature of her ambivalence with remarkable nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a remarkably consistent strategic focus on the target behavior (community engagement) with no topic drift. The session follows a clear arc from abstract ambivalence to a concrete, self-generated plan (Tuesday, screenshot of the door).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is entirely driven by Isabella's ideas — the community center, the phone, the screenshot — with the psychiatrist never suggesting alternatives. Agenda is negotiated at the outset and every subsequent exploration follows the client's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly affirms Isabella's right to choose ('You don't have to decide tonight,' 'I'll think about it is a real, honest answer'). Never imposes a timeline, pressures for commitment, or implies obligation for inaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every piece of motivation emerges organically from Isabella's own mouth — the small step, the friend technique insight, the text message idea. The psychiatrist never provides external justification for change, instead creating conditions for Isabella to generate her own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Isabella as the expert on her own life and consistently returns focus to her experience and judgment. The closing powerfully attributes the work to Isabella: 'You built something today that you didn't think you'd get to.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk through the friend technique and progressive narrowing of the plan. Could be strengthened by more systematically eliciting confidence or readiness language (e.g., rulers) to consolidate the change talk generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well — validates it without arguing, normalizes uncertainty, and uses the friend technique to gently shift perspective. Could be more explicitly double-sided in moments (e.g., 'part of you wants to go, and part of you is worried — both can be true') before pivoting.
