# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client’s self-awareness and ambivalence. It fell short of a 5 mainly because the warmth and normalization could have been more explicit, especially around shame, avoidance, and the client’s worth independent of performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the client’s internal tension and exhaustion. A deeper exploration of the emotional meaning behind the fear, shame, and identity concerns would have moved this closer to a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior: studying for the exam while managing anxiety. The psychiatrist effectively guided the conversation from broad ambivalence to a concrete, workable five-minute study plan without losing the client’s pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative, with the client co-creating the five-minute timer plan and the psychiatrist following rather than imposing direction. It would be stronger if the psychiatrist more explicitly shared power or checked in on the collaborative process itself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by using open questions and framing the plan as the client’s own honest experiment. More explicit statements affirming that the client is fully in charge of whether or how to follow the plan would strengthen this criterion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evoked the client’s own motivations and solutions rather than supplying reasons for change. This could be strengthened by linking the small study experiment more directly to the client’s values, future hopes, and sense of competence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance by asking for the client’s perspective and following the client’s own ideas, such as the parts framework and five-minute timer. More transparent power sharing and explicit acknowledgment of the client as the expert would improve this score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited meaningful change talk through open questions and small-step prompts, leading the client to articulate a doable plan. It could be stronger with more active amplification, summarization, and explicit eliciting of confidence and ability language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist softened the client’s sustain talk by reframing fear as protective and using balanced reflections rather than arguing or minimizing. More active double-sided reflection and summarization around the client’s worries about “cheating” or not working hard enough would improve this criterion.
