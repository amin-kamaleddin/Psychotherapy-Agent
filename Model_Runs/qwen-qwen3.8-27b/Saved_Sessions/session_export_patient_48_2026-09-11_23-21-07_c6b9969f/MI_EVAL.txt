# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, warm, and repeatedly removes shame or pressure. Acceptance is strong but can become slightly corrective or performance-focused instead of fully validating Farid’s broader struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at a surface level and support rapport, but they do not consistently explore the deeper emotional meaning beneath Farid’s frustration. Responses sometimes remain generic rather than richly empathic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on a concrete target behavior and next step. Direction is helpful, though it becomes somewhat task-focused before fully exploring the client’s stated agenda of understanding his stalling.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and accepts some client modifications, which keeps the interaction non-directive in tone. However, the psychiatrist still generates most of the plan and structure, limiting the depth of collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is well supported through choice invitations, confidence rulers, and explicit no-pressure language. The main limitation is that the psychiatrist still supplies much of the plan and advice rather than waiting for the client to generate it first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative techniques are used, especially confidence rulers and a reflective check-in question. The process is limited because the psychiatrist does not deeply elicit Farid’s own reasons, values, beliefs, or ideas about change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is visible collaboration, including asking for consent and incorporating the client’s preferences into the plan. The interaction remains largely therapist-led, especially in generating the behavioral experiment and structuring the next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through commitment questions and affirmations. Change talk is not consistently amplified, summarized, or expanded using Farid’s own language and deeper motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced or challenged directly, which helps avoid confrontation. The psychiatrist does not sufficiently use advanced MI techniques to explore or resolve ambivalence, so the underlying sustain talk remains largely intact.
