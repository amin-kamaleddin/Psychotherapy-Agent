# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental, normalizing stance and validates skepticism without shame. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and could be warmer or more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers accurate reflections of Daniel's ambivalence, practical reasoning, and discomfort with tracking. Empathy is largely cognitive/behavioral and could explore deeper emotional or identity-related conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps clear focus on alcohol use in relation to marathon training and moves toward a specific plan. Could more consistently tie drift in sleep/energy/coach feedback back to drinking itself and reinforce the shared target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-creates a low-burden plan around Daniel's preferences, using options and checking his input. The psychiatrist still largely sets the agenda, introduces the experimental frame, and occasionally offers expert suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy through invitations, choice-based language, and respect for Daniel's decisions. There is little pressure, and Daniel clearly owns the plan and check-in.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses the importance ruler and asks for a small doable step, but also provides several reasons/suggestions for change. This limits focus on Daniel's intrinsic motivations, values, and confidence in reducing alcohol.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adopts a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and lets Daniel's choices shape the plan. Partnership is strong but not fully client-led because the psychiatrist still frames and introduces the experiment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk around the marathon, pacing, and a concrete experiment. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or reflect this change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue or moralize when Daniel minimizes risk or questions the survey. Sustain talk is often accepted rather than gently redirected or double-sided toward health goals, autonomy, or values.
