# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and consistently validates Tariq’s frustration and effort. Acceptance is strong but somewhat tied to compliance and release, limiting unconditional individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of core emotions such as frustration, feeling targeted, and uncertainty. However, the reflections become repetitive and miss emotionally significant disclosures, such as his father’s birthday.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on managing frustration, compliance, phone loss, and protecting his release date. The direction is somewhat circular and could more consistently move toward a specific behavioral strategy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites Tariq’s perspective and responds to some of his themes, such as respect and communication with staff. The collaboration is partial because the psychiatrist still guides with repetitive questioning and misses deeper client disclosures.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Language such as “within your control,” “what might feel safer,” and “if at all” supports choice and reduces pressure. Some repeated compliance and release framing subtly implies that Tariq should manage himself to maintain release.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks several open-ended questions that elicit Tariq’s coping ideas, fairness concerns, and values around respect. The evocation is not consistently strategic because it does not deepen or expand Tariq’s own reasons for protecting his release.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and avoids a strongly expert-driven stance, which supports partnership. However, the session lacks a fully co-created agenda and remains steered by the psychiatrist’s repetitive release-focused agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq offers several change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist rarely amplifies, scales, or strategically reinforces them. The responses often return to coping or control questions without deepening his reasons or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct arguing and occasionally redirects toward agency with useful MI questions. More strategic double-sided reflections would better soften his sustain talk about staff and being targeted.
