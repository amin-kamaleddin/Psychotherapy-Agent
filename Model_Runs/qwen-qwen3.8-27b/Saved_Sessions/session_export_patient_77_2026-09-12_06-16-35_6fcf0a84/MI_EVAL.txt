# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently expressed nonjudgmental respect and positive regard, even when Liyana voiced fear, self-doubt, or possible failure. The stance avoided labeling or shaming and normalized setbacks while preserving self-worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used good reflective listening to capture frustration, isolation, fear of bingeing, and concern about undoing progress. A stronger response would have more explicitly reflected the deeper shame and internal conflict behind Liyana’s self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target concern and Liyana’s stated goal of feeling less stuck and more energetic. The work narrowed effectively to a specific, manageable behavior and ended with a clear follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with the psychiatrist inviting Liyana to choose timing, follow-up format, reminder preferences, and what to ask at check-in. The final plan was co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, avoiding directives, and removing pressure with statements such as “that’s okay” if Liyana did not follow through. The plan was presented as an option that Liyana refined through her own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some useful change talk, including Liyana’s desire for more energy and increased confidence. However, the session moved too quickly into problem-solving before fully exploring her values, motivations, and identity-based reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treated Liyana as the expert on her own body and preferences, and her choices meaningfully shaped the plan. Partnership could be stronger by allowing Liyana to generate more options before the psychiatrist presented structured suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used effective scaling and commitment questions that helped Liyana increase confidence and commit to a small experiment. Change talk could be further deepened by exploring what success would mean to her and why these goals matter.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk without arguing or dismissing, normalizing fear and reframing the task as a low-stakes experiment. The response could be stronger by using more double-sided reflections or client language to actively balance ambivalence.
