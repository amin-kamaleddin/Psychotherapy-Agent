# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with positive regard and normalization. Warmth is occasionally polished or generic and does not fully convey unconditional acceptance independent of disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture key themes such as staying light and avoiding stigma. Deeper emotional exploration of anxiety and readiness would further strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior and maintains momentum. It becomes somewhat task-like toward the end, which could feel premature if readiness is not fully explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the patient’s ideas directly shaping the session content. The psychiatrist acts as an equal partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice, ownership, and voluntary action. Language consistently avoids directive advice or pressure to disclose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the patient’s own values, preferences, and solutions. More exploration of why change matters, confidence, and readiness would elevate evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the patient as the expert on her own friendships and social world. Patient contributions clearly shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently draws out the patient’s own change language and motivations. Systematic use of scaling, summaries, or importance/confidence/readiness work would strengthen this domain.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist refrains from arguing against concerns and reframes them as boundaries or safety conditions. Occasional language suggests the ambivalence is already resolved, which could be softened with double-sided reflections.
