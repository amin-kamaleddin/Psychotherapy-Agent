# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained warm, nonjudgmental respect throughout. Hopelessness and avoidant statements were validated without criticism, impatience, or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured both content and emotion and tracked ambivalence well. Some interpretations could be verified, and the deeper emotional weight under the client’s fatigue and shame could be explored more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session narrowed broad financial stress into a concrete focus on the water bill and balance checking. The target was still somewhat broad, and occasional multi-layered questions made the direction feel slightly diffuse.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor worked as an equal partner rather than driving the agenda. Jordan’s own ideas about the water bill, balance app, and fridge note shaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently invited choice and avoided pressuring the client to make calls or change plans. It could more explicitly and repeatedly reinforce Jordan’s right to choose and ownership of both action and nonaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited the client’s own goals, barriers, and small possible steps rather than providing external reasons for change. Deeper values-based evocation and confidence/commitment questions could strengthen this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally treated Jordan as the expert and built on client-generated ideas. Occasional interpretive frames and suggested options slightly reduced the sense that agenda ownership rested fully with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited and amplified small-step change talk about paying the water bill and feeling less stuck. A higher score would require more deliberate rollout of change talk, including confidence, commitment, values, ability, and need statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk such as “I can’t get a break” and “nothing changes” was met with validation and reframing rather than argument or minimization. More strategic MI moves, such as double-sided or paradoxical reflections and a clearer pivot toward what might still be worth trying, could improve it.
