# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used respectful, nonjudgmental language and acknowledged Viktor's defensiveness without challenging him directly. Acceptance was somewhat procedural and occasionally guided by leading questions, which limited deeper warmth and unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected Viktor's ambivalence well, especially the pull between wanting peace at home and not wanting to be labeled or changed. Empathy was strong but mainly cognitive/behavioral, with less exploration of Viktor's deeper emotions or identity-related concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on reducing evening drug use in relation to conflict at home and moved logically toward a small, practical step. Direction was slightly weakened when the conversation shifted toward influencing the wife's response rather than staying on Viktor's own behavior and self-efficacy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used joint language and incorporated Viktor's practical ideas, creating a sense of working together. However, repeated counselor-led small-step prompts and suggestive options made the collaboration somewhat guided rather than fully client-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Viktor's autonomy by using low-pressure language and honoring his preference for a simple, low-drama approach. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming Viktor's right to choose, including the choice not to follow through.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked practical client-generated solutions through open-ended questions, which was useful for planning. Evocation was limited because it focused more on behavior than on Viktor's deeper values, confidence, reasons, or internal motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Viktor as the expert on his own life and followed his stated goals of practicality, low drama, and peace at home. Partnership was strong but could be deepened by more explicit agenda negotiation, pace-setting, and power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change talk by inviting Viktor to generate his own ideas and reflecting a small step toward reducing evening tension. The development of change talk was not consistently deepened, particularly around desire, ability, need, reasons, importance, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled resistance well by reflecting both sides without arguing or becoming confrontational. The response would be stronger if the strongest sustain talk was more consistently connected to discrepancy and change.
