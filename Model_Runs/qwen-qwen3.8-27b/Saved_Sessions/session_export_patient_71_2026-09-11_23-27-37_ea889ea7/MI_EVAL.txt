# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing, but the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and transactional. It lacks deeper, individualized affirmations of the patient as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes an effort to understand the patient’s perspective and asks relevant questions, but reflective listening remains shallow and infrequent. Important emotional cues such as “failing” and “making excuses” are acknowledged briefly rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior, with a logical progression from concern to pattern to small intervention. The direction is effective, though it is more clinician-led than co-owned by the patient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative surface, as the psychiatrist invites choices and adjusts the plan, but the psychiatrist still controls the agenda, framework, and menu of options. Power-sharing is present but uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the patient’s decisions in practice, such as skipping tracking and simplifying the plan. However, frequent advice, structured options, and prescribed experiments undercut the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist’s dominant mode is explanatory and directive rather than evocative, providing reasons, frameworks, and solutions instead of drawing out the patient’s own values and motivations. The patient’s change-relevant language is rarely reflected, amplified, or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and follows the patient’s preferences, but the interaction remains largely expert-driven. The patient selects from options presented by the psychiatrist rather than leading the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers several change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist does not effectively reflect, amplify, or explore them. Change talk is left underdeveloped, and the session leans toward behavioral problem-solving instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and neutralizes sustain talk without reinforcing it, and reframes slips in a nonjudgmental way. However, the sustain talk is not used therapeutically to deepen ambivalence or build momentum.
