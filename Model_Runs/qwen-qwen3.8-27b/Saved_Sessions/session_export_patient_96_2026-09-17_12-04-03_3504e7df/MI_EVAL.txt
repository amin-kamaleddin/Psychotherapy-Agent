# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and non-judgment throughout, never labeling Marisol or making her feel judged. While warm and measured, it remains somewhat professional rather than deeply affirming of her as a person beyond her choices, lacking explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate captures of Marisol's frustration and subtle emotional undercurrents (e.g., the 'I guess' reflection). Could have gone deeper on the underlying theme of autonomy and feeling talked down to by authority figures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with a well-structured arc from exploring perspective to arriving at a practical step. The opening question about self-care was slightly broad but overall the direction is purposeful and well-paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Marisol's input and works within her stated constraints, making the session feel like a conversation between equals. Could be stronger with more explicit co-creation, such as asking Marisol to summarize key takeaways rather than delivering a psychiatrist-led closing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout, consistently framing suggestions as optional and within Marisol's control. Never pressures or implies she should change her decision, with phrases like 'you're the expert on your own body and your own choices' clearly centering her agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses Marisol's own language and framework effectively to elicit her motivations, particularly leveraging her 'something's actually wrong' line as a directional guide. The evocation is skillful but could have been more deliberate in drawing out her personal values around self-care.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong partnership stance with clear power-sharing statements and collaborative check-ins. The agenda could have been more explicitly negotiated at the outset, and the final summary was somewhat psychiatrist-led rather than co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some genuine change talk around the behavioral commitment but does not strategically amplify or expand on it. Misses opportunities to reflect and deepen statements like 'I guess I can do that' or to use scaling questions and values exploration to generate more robust change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles Marisol's dismissiveness without arguing or getting drawn into debates about the piercing. Could have used more explicit double-sided reflections when she expressed resistance to walking out like she did at the dentist's office.
