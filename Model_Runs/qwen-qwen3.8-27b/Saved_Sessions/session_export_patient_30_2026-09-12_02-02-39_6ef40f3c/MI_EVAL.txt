# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but repeatedly corrects the client’s concerns with medical explanations and safety directives. This makes the interaction feel more instructive than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are helpful reflections of David’s ambivalence and fear, such as feeling torn between medication and side effects. However, the counselor often shifts to factual explanation before deeply exploring the emotional meaning behind the client’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cholesterol management, medication hesitation, diet, exercise, and follow-up. The conversation moves from vague concern to a concrete, manageable plan, though safety information slightly dilutes the momentum at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates better later by narrowing the plan after David rejects 150 minutes of exercise and a checklist. The earlier parts remain more counselor-led, with medical explanations and plan details coming before full client co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, asking David’s preference, and not forcing medication initiation. Repeated medical directives and safety instructions, however, reduce the consistency of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks a few open questions, but the session is driven more by medical explanation and suggestions than by eliciting David’s own motivations. The missed follow-up on the confidence scaling question represents a lost opportunity to evoke self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of partnership, such as asking David what he wants and adapting to his limited goals. Overall, the psychiatrist remains the primary guide, providing much of the direction, content, and framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offers some change talk, such as trying the diet, swapping a veggie meal, and walking to the mailbox. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen these statements into stronger reasons or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with David’s sustain talk and validates waiting on medication as reasonable. Repeated emphasis on side effects and safety warnings, however, can keep the client’s attention fixed on reasons not to change.
