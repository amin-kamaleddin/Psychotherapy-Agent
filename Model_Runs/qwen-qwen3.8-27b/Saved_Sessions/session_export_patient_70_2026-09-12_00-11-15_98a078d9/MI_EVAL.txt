# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Lars's goals without shaming. However, acceptance remains somewhat surface-level and generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is adequate but inconsistent, with some accurate reflections followed by a quick shift to problem-solving. Deeper emotional themes such as fear of reoffending, shame about forms, and ambivalence are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on reducing reoffending risk through boredom and alcohol management, narrowing to a concrete polytech step. It falls short of a 5 because the direction is partly maintained through counselor-driven structure rather than a clean MI-consistent process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial collaboration by following Lars's stated priorities and inviting input. However, the interaction is more counselor-managed than co-created, with frequent checklists, scripts, and directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy support is inconsistent; the psychiatrist asks for preferences but also gives many specific instructions. This directive style may weaken Lars's sense that the plan is fully his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with an open question, but after that the psychiatrist mostly supplies advice, scripts, and logistics rather than eliciting Lars's own motivations. There are few evocative questions that deepen his change talk or personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial partnership, as the psychiatrist follows Lars's stated focus on cars and polytech rather than forcing a broader safety plan. However, the counselor often takes on an expert-driven role by managing the process and supplying the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lars expresses clear change talk, and the psychiatrist occasionally affirms it, but does not consistently amplify it. The session would benefit from more evocative questions that help Lars strengthen his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly reinforce sustain talk and redirects toward practical anchors. However, there is limited use of MI-consistent techniques to explore ambivalence or soften sustain talk into change talk.
