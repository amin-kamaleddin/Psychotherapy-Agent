# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and validating but often shifts quickly into problem-solving, limiting deeper emotional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic reflections are accurate but brief, and they are often followed by questions or psychoeducation rather than deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear behavioral focus on exam avoidance and small-step practice, though it is more CBT-oriented than MI-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements such as choices and shared language, but the therapist largely defines the framework and direction of work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and invitations, but also gives specific protocols that can feel directive rather than fully autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some open questions are used, but the psychiatrist often supplies interpretations, coping strategies, and next steps instead of evoking Hana’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session includes some invitations for input, but it is largely expert-driven with preset CBT tasks rather than being co-created with Hana.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Hana produces some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or reinforce her reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed rather than disputed, but softening relies more on clinician reframing than on exploring ambivalence in an MI-congruent way.
