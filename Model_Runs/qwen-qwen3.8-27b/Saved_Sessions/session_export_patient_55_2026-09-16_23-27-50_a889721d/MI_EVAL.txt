# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, with little pressure or shaming. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and could be more warmly individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially around the gap between the screening label and the client’s self-understanding. Empathy could go deeper by exploring underlying emotions more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with some behavioral focus on alcohol, school, and music, but then drifts toward the fairness of the screening tool. The conversation does not consistently return to the target behavior or change-related focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses a collaborative tone and treats the client as an expert on her own experience. The collaboration remains fairly narrow, focused more on interpreting the screening than on broader alcohol-related goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s choices, offers options rather than directives, and affirms the client’s self-knowledge. Support for autonomy is strong but could more explicitly affirm the client’s freedom and ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used to elicit values, self-perception, and the client’s view of the screening. Evocation is limited in consistently drawing out the client’s own motivation for change or risk reduction.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor clearly communicates that the client is the expert on her own life and invites collaborative exploration. The partnership is strong, though power-sharing and co-creation around broader alcohol-related planning could be more explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative openings, but the psychiatrist does not systematically elicit desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. Much of the session focuses on the validity of the screening rather than the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is a major weakness, as repeated sustain talk is not effectively redirected or softened. At times, the counselor’s responses validate the client’s framing in ways that may reinforce the status quo.
