# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts Kevin’s ambivalence without moralizing. Acceptance could be deeper by more explicitly validating his shame, social fear, and internal conflict.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Kevin’s goals, regret, and concern about peer pressure. Empathy would be stronger with more nuanced exploration of feelings such as embarrassment, shame, and fear of being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing injury risk while preserving social connection. The conversation moves clearly from the incident to goals, options, confidence, and a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin’s ideas clearly shape the plan, and the final strategy is co-created rather than imposed. Collaboration could be stronger by inviting more client-generated solutions before offering multiple options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Kevin’s choice not to quit drinking and repeatedly asks which option feels most doable. Autonomy support would improve with more explicit emphasis that Kevin remains fully in control of his decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and confidence ratings successfully draw out Kevin’s own reasons for change and confidence. Evocation is limited by the frequent introduction of therapist-generated strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Kevin’s input and avoids an overtly directive stance. The repeated provision of menus of options makes the interaction somewhat clinician-led despite a collaborative tone.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits change talk through confidence ratings, affirmations, and questions about doable steps. Change talk could be further expanded with more evocative questions about values, importance, and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, and concerns about peer pressure are normalized. The counselor could more consistently use double-sided reflections to reframe sustain talk toward safer choices.
