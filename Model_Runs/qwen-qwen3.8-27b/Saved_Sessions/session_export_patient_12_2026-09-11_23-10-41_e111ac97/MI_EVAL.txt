# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Amira’s distress without shaming her for drug use. Warmth is present but remains somewhat procedural and does not fully affirm Amira’s worth or strength.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some surface-level feelings but misses deeper ambivalence, shame, fear, loneliness, and disappointment. Complex reflections about relapse fear and being let down would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on avoiding getting lost again and managing cravings through small, concrete steps. The focus is practical and skill-based rather than strategically directed toward Amira’s own change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira chooses grounding, a ritual, music, and asks the psychiatrist to stay, showing partial collaboration. The psychiatrist supplies most of the plan content rather than co-creating the plan with Amira as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and low-pressure language that supports Amira’s sense of control. However, there are also directive moments, and the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce that Amira is in charge of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks an open initial question and some preference questions, but the session mostly relies on providing skills, options, and instructions. There is limited effort to elicit Amira’s own reasons for change, values, hopes, or sense of ability.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some client input, which supports partnership, but the session becomes largely therapist-driven. Amira contributes choices, yet her expertise and ideas do not substantially shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amira offers several change statements, but the psychiatrist does not reflect, amplify, or follow up to deepen them. Tools such as importance rulers, confidence questions, values-based questions, and commitment language are absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk and acknowledges Amira’s pain. However, sustain talk is often met with coping suggestions rather than skillful double-sided reflections that honor the pain while opening room for change.
