# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without shaming the relapse. Some language becomes procedural or generic, and the safety check briefly interrupts emotional flow.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects the patient’s ambivalence, fear, shame, and protective role with accuracy. Could go deeper into identity conflict and fear of repeated self-betrayal before shifting to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on relapse prevention, safety, triggers, and concrete coping. Pacing could be slightly better by allowing more exploration of values before problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs a realistic plan around the patient’s preferences and adapts well to his choices. The counselor still initiates much of the structure and drives toward a specific behavioral plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by asking permission, offering options, and avoiding pressure. Some plan elements remain clinician-suggested rather than fully client-originated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks useful questions and draws out some coping ideas and motivations. Evocation is mixed with directive problem-solving and could be more value-based and less practical.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and shares power by letting the patient choose plan components. At times the counselor adopts a more expert-driven role with structured suggestions and if-then planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Highlights strengths, progress, and the importance of the kids as motivators. Could do more to deepen desire, need, ability, and commitment language before moving into practical steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accepts ambivalence without arguing or reinforcing sustain talk. Misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections that both validate sustain talk and pivot toward change.
