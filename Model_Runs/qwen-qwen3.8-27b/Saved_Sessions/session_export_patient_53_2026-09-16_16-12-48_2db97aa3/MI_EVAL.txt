# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and safe stance while normalizing Isabella's avoidance. Acceptance was solid but could be warmer and more individualized in affirming her as a person beyond her difficulties.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections that captured feeling, meaning, and ambivalence, such as the tension between connection and safety. Empathy could deepen by more explicitly reflecting underlying shame, fear of judgment, or self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on a small, manageable step toward social connection and community engagement. The psychiatrist effectively narrowed the topic from broad isolation to a concrete grocery store plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked as a partner, following Isabella's leads and helping her co-create the plan. Her ideas substantially shaped the session, including boundaries, the list, the exit, and modifications to the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized Isabella's choice, ownership, and control over the plan. Language such as “on your terms,” “your pace,” and “your call” strongly supported her agency without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked Isabella's own ideas and problem-solving through open questions, especially around practical safety steps. Evocation would be stronger if it more deeply elicited values, identity, confidence, or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Isabella as the expert on her own limits, needs, and preferences. Questions and responses consistently reflected power sharing and client-led direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabella offered some change talk around small steps, ownership, and practical plans, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen it. More explicit reflections of ability, confidence, and personal values would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responded to sustain talk without arguing, minimizing, or moralizing, reframing avoidance in terms of safety and self-regulation. The approach was strong but could use more explicit MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections, to turn ambivalence into change.
