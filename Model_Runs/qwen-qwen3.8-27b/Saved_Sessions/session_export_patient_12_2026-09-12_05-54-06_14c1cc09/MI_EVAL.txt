# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, consistently validating Amira's struggles without moralizing. However, the warmth was sometimes repetitive and procedural, with less emphasis on affirming Amira as a whole person beyond her behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated genuine effort in understanding Amira's perspective through accurate surface-level reflections. However, the reflections often missed deeper emotional nuances such as fear, shame, and the meaninglessness behind her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation remained consistently focused on the target behaviors of reducing drug use and addressing helplessness, avoiding unrelated topics. However, the repeated cycling through options and safety questions occasionally fragmented the session's momentum and progress consolidation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive language and the willingness to participate in tasks like counting with the patient showed collaborative elements. However, the collaboration was uneven, as the psychiatrist supplied most of the structure, resources, and problem-solving ideas rather than primarily eliciting them from Amira.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported Amira's sense of choice by avoiding commanding language, asking permission, and offering multiple options for next steps. The score is not a 5 because the frequent prompts to choose and act from a therapist-generated list can feel slightly leading for a passive client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and confidence scaling were used effectively to initially evoke Amira's change talk and motivations. However, the session frequently shifted toward therapist-led problem-solving and providing resources, which limited the consistent and deep development of Amira's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated partnership by inviting Amira's input, moving at her pace, and following her stated preferences for micro-steps. However, the session remained largely clinician-led, with Amira primarily selecting from a therapist-provided list rather than directing the overall strategy through her own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evoked and reinforced some change talk, particularly around Amira's desire for less use and less loneliness. However, the evocation was not consistently developed, as the psychiatrist did not systematically explore Amira's deeper values, abilities, or reasons for change before moving into tasks.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately normalized Amira's sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it, redirecting toward small, manageable actions. However, the softening was not strategic enough, as the psychiatrist did not use complex or double-sided reflections to fully explore the fears and truths behind Amira's feelings of futility.
