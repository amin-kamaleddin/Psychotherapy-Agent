# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using supportive statements without pressure or disapproval. However, acceptance is more task-focused and formulaic than deeply warm toward the person as a whole.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some acknowledgment of Richard’s concern, but reflections remain surface-level and repetitive. The emotional significance of staying active with his wife is not deeply reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on cholesterol medication, fatigue, and maintaining energy for retirement. The main limitation is that the direction is more problem-solving and psychoeducation than MI-guided motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard is involved in choosing the focus, saving the script, and rehearsing, which supports some collaboration. However, the psychiatrist remains the main source of information, structure, and suggested language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids obvious coercion, which supports Richard’s decision-making. The frequent provision of scripts, lists, and medical advice reduces the emphasis on Richard’s own ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Richard offers meaningful change talk about walking and hiking with his wife, but the psychiatrist does not consistently draw out or expand these motivations. The counselor relies more on providing information than eliciting Richard’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard’s preferences influence the session, especially in narrowing the focus and practicing a statement. Still, the psychiatrist maintains a fairly expert-driven role by supplying detailed medical content and prepared questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Richard expresses clear change talk about medication, walking more, and not missing retirement activities, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or deepens it. The counselor often shifts to advice or practical preparation instead of strengthening motivational statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Richard’s minimization or sustain talk and responds in a low-pressure way. However, MI-consistent techniques such as double-sided reflections or linking minimization to values are not consistently used.
