# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance; validates social difficulty of refusing drinks. Acceptance is strong but not fully unconditional, as it often ties to Alexei’s goals, insight, or performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections capture both content and emotional meaning, including ambivalence about fitting in versus protecting soccer goals. The psychiatrist is consistently attuned to Alexei’s values, fears, and social concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on alcohol use in social situations and the specific challenge of refusing drinks. The session progresses strategically from goals to ambivalence, refusal phrases, backup plan, and a concrete cue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invitational language and co-construction of a plan from Alexei’s own ideas demonstrate strong collaboration. The psychiatrist still holds the primary conversation structure, with limited explicit shared agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language is non-controlling and supports Alexei’s self-direction and choice. The final plan is framed as client-owned and optional rather than prescribed or rule-based.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly elicits Alexei’s own motivations, values, boundaries, and the “one breath” cue. Some psychiatrist-generated interpretations slightly reduce the purity of evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Alexei as the expert on his own life, goals, and social context. Client-generated ideas drive the session rather than counselor directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively evoke client-generated change talk, including boundary phrases and self-awareness. It lacks more explicit confidence or commitment evocation, such as scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fitting in and not “killing the vibe” is reflected and normalized without argument. Could use more advanced double-sided reflections to hold and explore both sides of the ambivalence more explicitly.
