# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect and nonjudgment, affirming Wiremu’s progress without moralizing. However, acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and is mixed with rapid problem-solving, reducing the sense of deep, individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects content accurately but remains largely factual and surface-level. There is limited emotional resonance with Wiremu’s shame, fear of scrutiny, and parenting concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing alcohol use, family reunification, and concrete next steps. Some overemphasis on logistics and scripts slightly narrows the discussion, but overall direction remains strong.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and adapts to some resistance, allowing Wiremu to shape parts of the plan. However, the therapist-driven agenda and supplied frameworks make the interaction feel more coached than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice at times and reduces pressure by saying actions matter more than proof. Nevertheless, frequent advice-giving, scripts, and prescriptive planning weaken the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to elicit Wiremu’s inner motivations, with much of the session driven by therapist-generated plans. Core values, such as being a good dad and reconnecting with whānau, are not deeply evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership language is present, but the session is largely expert-driven and agenda-setting. The psychiatrist continues to supply structure and scripts even when Wiremu signals that the approach feels like a test.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Wiremu produces some change talk, but it appears largely self-generated rather than strategically evoked. The psychiatrist relies more on suggestions and scripts than on drawing out the client’s own reasons and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced, but it is also not consistently softened or redirected. The therapist stays within a problem-focused frame, missing opportunities to use double-sided reflections or values-based reframing.
