# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and non-judgmental throughout the session. The psychiatrist normalizes ambivalence, removes performance pressure, and never lectures or expresses disappointment, even when Samuel expresses self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture the emotional core of Samuel's experience with precision and nuance. Examples include synthesizing the coffee break insight ('it's about that moment itself') and identifying what Samuel is protecting in his fear of resentment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on the target behavior with a logical progression from journaling to patterns to goal-setting. A clearer closing with a collaboratively identified next step would strengthen the sense of direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and invites Samuel's perspective throughout, treating him as an equal partner. Collaboration could be deepened by more explicitly co-constructing next steps or inviting Samuel to shape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Samuel's ownership of his choices, never imposes a plan or pressures him toward a particular decision. The final question presents both options without favoring either, leaving the decision entirely with Samuel.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative questions draw out Samuel's own motivations and perspectives rather than providing external reasons for change. The opening question and explorations of the coffee break ritual effectively elicit Samuel's own insights.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-directive stance using inclusive language and inviting Samuel's perspective throughout. The partnership is strong though somewhat therapist-guided rather than fully client-directed in setting the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk but responses to it are somewhat passive, missing opportunities to affirm effort or amplify Samuel's statements of desire and ability. More strategic reflections that build on change talk would strengthen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with appropriate care, validating concerns without arguing or dismissing them. Could be slightly more proactive in pivoting away from sustain talk around failure toward alternatives or reframes.
