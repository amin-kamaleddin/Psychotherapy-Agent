# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, validating, and nonjudgmental stance while normalizing the client's experience. It is not a 5 because the expressions of unconditional positive regard could be slightly deeper when addressing the client's fear of being watched or out of control.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and often capture the cognitive–bodily loop and physical discomfort. Empathy could be stronger by exploring deeper shame, self-judgment, and fear of being exposed when the client mentions failing or freezing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the client's central concern and ends with a concrete, behavior-specific plan. It is not a 5 because parts of the session become technique- or psychoeducation-focused before the client's motivation is more fully evoked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's preferences and adapts to her choices, such as choosing the strap anchor and reducing the practice plan. Collaboration could be stronger by relying more on the client's own ideas rather than offering a menu of expert-driven options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers options, and respects the client's decision to reduce the plan. Autonomy support could be slightly stronger by more explicitly affirming that the client owns the entire change process without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and goals but does not consistently elicit or deepen the client's own change talk. More DARN-C-style questions would help strengthen motivation before moving into psychoeducation and practice planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choices clearly shape the session, and the psychiatrist follows her lead in important moments. Partnership is not a 5 because the clinician still leads with a fairly expert-driven menu of techniques and explanations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some evocation of change language, and the client commits to a small experiment. The psychiatrist could do more to reflect, amplify, and expand the client's own reasons, desires, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and reframes concerns as understandable and workable. Softening could be improved by using more double-sided reflections that connect resistance with the client's willingness to try.
