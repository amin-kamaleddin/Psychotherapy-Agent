# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive while normalizing the client’s concern about staying safe without giving up friends. The acceptance is sometimes formulaic and checklist-driven, which limits its depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are brief empathic reflections, but much of the session stays task-focused rather than emotionally attuned. The psychiatrist does not consistently explore the client’s ambivalence, embarrassment, or tension between fun, safety, and education.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target issue of alcohol-related social drinking and avoiding another incident. Direction is more behavioral planning than exploration of values or change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates client preferences into ride, text, and exit-plan details. However, most of the plan and wording is counselor-led, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and asks the client to choose, which supports autonomy. Repeated scripts, checklists, and direct action steps can feel directive and reduce the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies heavily on advice and practical steps rather than eliciting the client’s own reasons for change. The psychiatrist does not deeply explore how safer choices connect to the client’s education, relationships, or self-respect.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often takes an expert, advice-giving role and drives the agenda. The client is treated more as a recipient of a safety plan than as the primary source of his own wisdom and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change-relevant language, such as caring about education and not wanting another incident. The psychiatrist rarely asks follow-up questions that expand these statements into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not argued against, but the psychiatrist mostly redirects to planning instead of using reflections or discrepancy-based questions. The client’s view of drinking as normal college fun is not effectively softened or resolved.
