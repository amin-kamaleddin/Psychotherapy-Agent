# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates David’s experience without shaming. Acceptance is strong but occasionally reads as more praise or coaching than deep unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence, stress, and coping concerns. It could be stronger by exploring affective fears such as embarrassment, loss of control, or relapse more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on smoking cessation, stress, and social triggers. Each question and reflection advances the client toward a concrete, workable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats David as an equal partner and co-creates the coping plan from his own ideas. The client’s suggestions are integrated rather than replaced by the counselor’s strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language consistently supports choice, pace, and ownership of the plan. The counselor avoids directive or controlling statements and emphasizes that the plan depends on David’s own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within David through open, client-centered questions. The counselor helps him articulate his own change talk rather than supplying external reasons to quit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions David as the expert on his own life and experience. He invites David’s perspective and collaborates rather than taking over the counseling role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully elicits strong change talk around desire, ability, need, and commitment. David offers specific coping ideas and expresses confidence in his own chosen strategy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and redirects toward practical coping. Some worries, especially about medication timing and effectiveness, are only partially reframed rather than fully softened.
