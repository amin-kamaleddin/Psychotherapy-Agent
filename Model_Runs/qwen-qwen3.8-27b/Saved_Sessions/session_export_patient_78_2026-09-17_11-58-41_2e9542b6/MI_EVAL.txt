# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, with specific, genuine affirmations and zero labeling or shaming. Resistance was fully accepted without pushback, and the psychiatrist humbly accepted correction from the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with reflections that build on each other and become increasingly precise. Key examples include capturing the functional meaning of the smoking break and validating why the client had minimized her own insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains focus on the target behavior while allowing organic movement to the underlying relational issue, with clear momentum from frustration to action step. The final question ('Can you tell me more about that?') is somewhat generic and slightly disrupts the natural conclusion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplifies full collaboration with the client's contributions directly shaping the session's direction. The psychiatrist accepts correction gracefully and adjusts framing in real-time, confirmed by the client's statement that 'here it just stayed mine.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout feature of the session, with consistent reinforcement of the client's ownership and right to not act today. The closing 'Fair?' frames follow-up as a mutual agreement rather than a clinical expectation, and no prescribing or pressure is present.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No external reasons for change are supplied; the key insight ('permission to stop') and action plan ('stairwell') emerge entirely from the client. The psychiatrist's role is purely to create space for evocation rather than supply content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within the client, never positioning as authority on what the client needs. The power balance is genuinely equitable, with the psychiatrist following the client's lead throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies the client's own reasoning without inflating it. Change talk emerges organically and is consistently noticed, named, and gently reinforced by the psychiatrist.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledges the validity of the client's resistance without making it the focus. Score is limited to 4 because the session's structure provided limited opportunity to demonstrate active softening techniques such as double-sided reflections.
