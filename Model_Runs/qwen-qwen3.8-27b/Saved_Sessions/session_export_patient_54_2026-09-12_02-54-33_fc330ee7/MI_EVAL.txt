# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client's values, autonomy, and practical steps. Warmth is somewhat procedural, with fewer explicit affirmations of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening is used to understand ambivalence around the baby, ritual, and need for evidence. However, some emotional content, such as fear about brain development, is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use during pregnancy and remains practical, with a clear plan around substitution and partner discussion. It becomes somewhat administrative toward the end and could better use MI-focused direction such as commitment building.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to co-create the handout and plan, and her preferences clearly shape the final product. The psychiatrist still leads much of the structure, so collaboration is strong but not fully equal throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated permission-seeking, options rather than directives, and respect for the client's right to decide. The client's need to maintain control is consistently honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open-ended questions that elicit values around the baby, ritual, and autonomy. However, the session relies heavily on information and planning rather than fully evoking the client's own reasons, confidence, and motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats the client as the expert on her own life and invites her input and choices. Still, the psychiatrist holds much of the frame and structure, so the client's expertise is not always made fully central.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses important change talk about wanting a healthy baby and being willing to try non-alcoholic wine. The psychiatrist notices some of this but does not consistently expand, amplify, or strategically develop it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or dismissing the client's doubts about whether one glass is harmful. However, responses are mainly informational rather than using MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to soften sustain talk.
