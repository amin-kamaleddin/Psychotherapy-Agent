# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts the patient’s guardedness without shame or argument. Acceptance is somewhat procedural, with repeated phrases and limited deeper affirmation of the patient’s strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is good reflective listening, especially around the patient’s reasons for attending and desire for calm evenings. Empathy remains more content-focused and does not consistently explore deeper feelings such as defensiveness, fear of control, or guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts away from the stated target behavior of reducing drug use and centers on marital tension, evening downtime, and communication scripts. The psychiatrist does not explicitly address substance use, its frequency, consequences, or connection to the patient’s values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and co-creates a short, direct phrase that fits the patient’s communication style. However, the counselor still dominates problem-solving by offering multiple scripts, options, and follow-up plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient is consistently reminded that he is in charge, and his refusal to plan, write things down, or role-play is accepted without pressure. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicit affirmation of the patient’s right to choose and capacity to direct his own life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated goals, such as calmer mornings and trying the plan for a week. However, evocation is not consistently directed toward the central goal of reducing drug use and quickly shifts into offering solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for permission and invites input, showing some collaborative stance. The session still becomes more expert-led, with the psychiatrist generating options, scripts, and follow-up steps rather than fully following the patient’s lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, including willingness to try one week and desire for peace at home. These statements are not strongly amplified, summarized, or connected to deeper values or the target behavior of reducing drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids confrontation and rolls with the patient’s minimization and resistance. However, sustain talk is mostly tolerated rather than strategically softened through double-sided reflections or discrepancy development.
