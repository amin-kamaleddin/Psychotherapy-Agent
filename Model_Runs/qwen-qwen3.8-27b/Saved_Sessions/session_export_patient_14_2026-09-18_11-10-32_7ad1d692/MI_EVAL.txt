# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, warm, and unconditional positive regard throughout. Takes accountability for the wait, affirms the client's directness, and explicitly honors his autonomy boundaries without judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking that captures the core theme (time as bottleneck), the emotional layer beneath the business-like surface, and the nuanced distinction between 'it won't work' and 'I don't trust myself.' Reflections are varied, precise, and consistently on-target.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with a logical, progressive structure moving from resistance to concrete self-generated action plans. No topic drift; pacing is well-calibrated to the client's time sensitivity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of full collaboration where the client's ideas shape every aspect of the session. The psychiatrist uses the client's exact language ('less fragile,' 'non-negotiable,' 'embedded') as the foundation of the work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's ownership and freedom to choose, explicitly rejecting a directive approach. All reframes are phrased as invitations rather than corrections, keeping the locus of control internal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every concrete strategy in the session emerges from the client's own thinking, evoked through skilled, targeted questions. The psychiatrist never provides answers, gives advice, or explains why the client should change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fosters power sharing with explicit statements like 'I'm turning it back to you' and 'I'm not going to hand you a program.' The session plan is entirely client-generated, with the psychiatrist positioned as a collaborator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently evokes, reflects, and amplifies change talk, including reframing self-deprecating language ('stupidly small') into recognition of the client's own achievement. Skillfully reinforces a pattern of competence and agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively with gentle reframes that distinguish concerns (e.g., 'you didn't say it won't work; you said you don't trust yourself'). Could improve by using more double-sided reflections to explicitly honor both sides of ambivalence before reframing.
