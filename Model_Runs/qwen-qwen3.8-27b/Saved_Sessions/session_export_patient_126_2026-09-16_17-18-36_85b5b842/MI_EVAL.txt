# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepting of the patient's guardedness. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with limited affirmation of strengths and a slight tendency to reinforce the patient's minimal-risk framing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's need for brevity, control, and closure. The main limitation is that many reflections remain content-focused rather than exploring deeper emotions such as fear, responsibility, or defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on obtaining a blood pressure checkpoint and does not wander. However, direction is insufficient because the referral concerns of tobacco use and alcohol use are not directly explored as modifiable targets.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and invites the patient's input at each step. The session is clearly shaped by the patient's preferences, making it a strong example of collaborative interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is very strong, with repeated emphasis on the patient's right to choose, stop, and set the decision rule. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, advice-giving, and the imposition of a treatment agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions that elicit the patient's own goals for clarity, finality, and putting the issue to rest. Evocation is limited because it does not consistently draw out deeper motivation related to smoking cessation or alcohol use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong partnership stance by positioning the patient as the decision-maker. The session is guided by the patient's preferences rather than by a predetermined clinician-driven agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some effort to evoke change-relevant language related to family, work, and not being sick. Change talk is limited because it is not consistently connected to smoking cessation or alcohol use, and existing change talk is not strongly amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or correcting the patient's sustain talk and uses some acknowledgment and redirection. Softening remains moderate because the session stays anchored to the patient's status-quo framing without more skillful use of double-sided reflections or pivots.
