# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a polite, nonjudgmental tone with courteous closing phrases. However, repeatedly re-delivering safety information after the client had already said goodbye multiple times undermines genuine acceptance of the client's expressed boundary and time constraint.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The interaction contains essentially no reflective listening or perspective-taking. The psychiatrist does not mirror or explore the client's feelings, concerns, or time pressure, instead moving directly into didactic safety information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is a clear clinical focus on the citalopram-phenelzine interaction and safety. However, the direction is repetitive and one-directional, restating the same information without advancing the conversation toward goals, next steps, or a collaborative plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session is structured as one-way expert instruction rather than shared decision-making. The client's input and plan to discuss the issue with her prescriber are not meaningfully incorporated or built upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly provides additional instructions after the client clearly and repeatedly signals that she needs to leave. Although some autonomy-supportive language is used, the overall pattern fails to respect the client's choice to end the conversation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supplies all the reasons for safe medication management without eliciting the client's own perspective, concerns, or motivations. The client's change-relevant statements are not reflected, amplified, or explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist operates from an expert, directive stance rather than a collaborative relationship. There is no co-creation of a plan and little acknowledgment of the client as the expert on her own situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not elicit, affirm, or expand the client's change-relevant statements. Information is provided rather than drawn out or deepened through questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's main barrier is time pressure rather than resistance to change, and the psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk. However, the repeated instructions unintentionally add to the client's time burden instead of acknowledging and working with it.
