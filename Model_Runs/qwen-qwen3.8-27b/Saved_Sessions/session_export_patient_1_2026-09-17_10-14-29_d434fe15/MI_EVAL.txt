# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or direct confrontation. However, acceptance is sometimes generic or overly conciliatory, limiting deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures the client's ambivalence but often remains cognitive/behavioral rather than emotionally deep. Empathy is inconsistent in accuracy and misses opportunities to explore underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on mini-cigar use and related symptoms, with relevant follow-up questions. Momentum is limited because the conversation does not consistently move toward a concrete, autonomy-supportive next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is responsive and reflective, with the client's input shaping next questions. Collaboration is strong but incomplete because the psychiatrist still leads the structure and asks a sequence of leading questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client's choice, pace, and ownership without pressure, direct advice, or imposed plans. This is a clear strength, though at times it borders on over-accommodation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions evoke some client-generated ideas, but evocation is more often directed toward the client's current rationale for not changing. It does not consistently elicit or expand internal motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as an expert and avoids an authoritarian stance. The agenda remains provider-led, with limited explicit co-construction or negotiation of priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few questions elicit small pieces of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently recognize, reflect, or amplify it. Change talk remains weak and underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's minimization is frequently validated rather than gently redirected, reinforcing sustain talk. A few mild redirects occur but are not consistent or skillful enough to soften sustain talk effectively.
