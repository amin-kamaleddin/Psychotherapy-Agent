# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally nonjudgmental and validating, using statements such as “You’re juggling a lot” and “You’re not failing.” However, acceptance often felt procedural and quickly shifted into task-giving, limiting the sense of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered surface-level reflections of stress but missed much of the emotional complexity, especially shame, fear of failure, and the deeper meaning of the dog. More accurate and affective reflection of Marcus’s ambivalence and sense of loss would strengthen the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although asthma was the stated target, much of the session drifted into rent, work, and general problem-solving. A stronger focus would have kept asthma as the center while acknowledging financial stress as a barrier.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated on practical items, such as message wording and payment details, and incorporated Marcus’s preferences. However, the psychiatrist still supplied most of the structure, priorities, and problem-solving approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some support for choice and preference, including written messages and allowing Marcus to select an option. This was undermined by directive language, specific scripts, and prescribed actions that reduced the emphasis on client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly asked logistical or choice-based questions rather than evoking Marcus’s own reasons for change. The client offered change talk, but it was not consistently amplified, reflected, or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often adopted an advisory, problem-solving role rather than a true partnership stance. More explicit power sharing and acknowledgment of Marcus as the expert on his own life would strengthen the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offered useful change talk, such as choosing a safer option and wanting something manageable at home, but the psychiatrist responded mostly with practical steps. These opportunities were not consistently amplified to build motivation for asthma management.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided reinforcing sustain talk but tended to bypass ambivalence with practical workarounds, such as redirect scripts and symptom-focused messages. A more MI-consistent approach would use double-sided reflections to explore both the importance of asthma care and the meaning of the dog.
