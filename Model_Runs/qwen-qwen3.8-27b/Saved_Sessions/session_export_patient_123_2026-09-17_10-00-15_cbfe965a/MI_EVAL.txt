# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard by normalizing Sofia’s confusion and affirming her strengths. However, some affirmations feel slightly interpretive or premature, and her ongoing ambivalence could be acknowledged more explicitly without quickly reframing it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Sofia’s concerns about fairness, social discomfort, and fitting in. Empathy could be stronger by reducing occasional over-interpretation and exploring the underlying emotional impact of being singled out more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, coherent focus on the referral concern, moving from why Sofia was flagged to her social boundaries and low-key strategy. Direction could be strengthened by stating the alcohol-related target behavior earlier and more consistently linking her answers to a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with Sofia’s answers shaping the session and the psychiatrist avoiding lectures or prescriptions. Collaboration could be further enhanced with more explicit co-construction language and frequent check-ins on what Sofia wants to do with the ideas discussed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by inviting Sofia to define the focus, emphasizing her expertise, and respecting her low-key preference without imposing a more confrontational narrative. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing Sofia’s freedom to choose and by avoiding reflections that presume a values-based frame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Sofia’s own reasons for maintaining a low-key approach, such as feeling better the next day and preserving friendships and grades. Evocation remains somewhat surface-level and could more deeply explore values, confidence, readiness, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a clear partnership stance by positioning Sofia as the expert on her own life and inviting her perspective on focus and boundaries. The collaboration could be stronger with more explicit shared decision-making and co-construction of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk around Sofia’s desire to keep things low-key, feel better, and maintain her friends and grades. However, this change talk is not consistently amplified, summarized, or developed using MI strategies such as scaling, commitment, or readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with or reinforcing Sofia’s sustain talk and keeps the interaction safe. However, the sustain talk is not consistently redirected, and the final minimization is left largely unaddressed rather than strategically softened.
