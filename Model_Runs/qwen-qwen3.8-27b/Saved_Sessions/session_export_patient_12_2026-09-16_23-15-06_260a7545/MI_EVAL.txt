# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently engages Amira with warmth, respect, and nonjudgment despite her self-blame and hopelessness. Responses validate her experience and convey unconditional positive regard without correcting or disapproving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections show good perspective-taking and often capture Amira’s ambivalence and pain with useful specificity. Empathy is not a 5 because the style becomes somewhat repetitive and does not always deepen the strongest feelings of shame, fear, or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a coherent emotional and relational focus on loneliness, urges, and the need for support. Direction could be sharper by more consistently tying the discussion to a concrete target such as coping with urges or reducing use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a collaborative stance and follows several of Amira’s own ideas, such as texting, listening, and staying present. Collaboration is not a 5 because the session remains somewhat agenda-led and includes visible prompt language that can feel less naturally co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly supports Amira’s autonomy by emphasizing that nothing is required and by using soft, optional language. Autonomy support could be stronger by pausing longer before offering specific suggestions that partly shape the client’s next step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes client-generated statements about being heard, less alone, and needing someone to stay. Evocation is not a 5 because counselor-supplied ideas sometimes preempt Amira’s own formulation and the change talk is not fully deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms Amira as the expert in her own history and avoids imposing a rigid plan. Partnership could be stronger by relying more on a single client-led thread rather than presenting multiple counselor-framed directions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates space for change talk despite Amira’s passivity, drawing out hopes for listening, staying, and not being judged. The change talk is not fully deepened, especially in connecting it to confidence, meaning, or a broader recovery goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by reflecting it without arguing and gently broadening Amira’s attention toward hope and connection. It is not a 5 because the dominant belief that she is a burden and that no one cares is not fully softened or reframed.
