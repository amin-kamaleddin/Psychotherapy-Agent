# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, warm, and supportive, with affirmations and serious attention to the client’s concerns. It is slightly task-oriented and occasionally assumptive, preventing full depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and capture practical/experiential concerns well. Important emotional content—fear of a heart issue, embarrassment, and pressure for a clear medical answer—is underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on smoking cessation, moving logically from motivations to barriers, medication, and medical evaluation. It ends with clear, actionable direction and little topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint framing and incorporates client contributions into the plan and doctor note. The psychiatrist still generates much of the structure rather than consistently inviting the client to lead the content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client choice is respected through permission-seeking, date selection, and medical clearance before starting medication. Autonomy could be reinforced more continuously with stronger ownership and flexibility language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions effectively elicit motivations, past attempts, and confidence. Evocation weakens later as the session shifts toward information-giving, logistics, and note drafting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s input meaningfully shapes the plan, including quit date, medication concerns, and NRT preference. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary driver, making power-sharing somewhat uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes clear change talk early by exploring reasons, success, and past attempts. Change talk is not sustained, and the confidence statement is not fully explored with scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts and sustain talk are validated without arguing and redirected into practical problem-solving. Use of double-sided reflections could further strengthen how ambivalence is held and resolved.
