# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s ambivalence. The warmth is steady but remains somewhat measured, without fully explicit individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and nuanced, tracking both the client’s social-identity concerns and practical stressors. Empathy could be stronger by more consistently exploring the deeper shame, fear of invisibility, or identity loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session moves toward reducing weekday use and making Friday use safer, but the target behavior remains somewhat diffuse. A clearer shared focus on the specific behavior change would improve direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client’s ideas and uses his suggestions to shape the conversation. However, the therapist still offers categories and directs much of the sequence, limiting fully collaborative agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the client’s sense of choice and avoids pressure to “quit everything.” Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client’s ownership of both the decision and its consequences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client’s own motivations, values, and concerns rather than supplying reasons for change. Evocation could be stronger by more consistently deepening these into explicit confidence, values, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative, non-expert stance and invites client input. The stance is not fully client-led, as the therapist still sets the agenda and offers structured options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes several useful change statements but does not consistently amplify, summarize, or strategically develop them. Missed opportunities include exploring pivotal statements such as “I can’t buy silence forever.”

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk. However, the therapist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to reduce the dominance of sustain talk.
