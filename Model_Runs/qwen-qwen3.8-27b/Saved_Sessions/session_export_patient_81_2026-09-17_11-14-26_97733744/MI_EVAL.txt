### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently normalizes Lukas's experience, avoids labeling or moralizing, and validates his defensive stance without shaming. Could be elevated to a 5 by more explicitly affirming Lukas's worth independent of his choices or directly acknowledging the broader social stigma he faces.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, including sophisticated reflections of Lukas's self-concept and coping strategies. A few reflections lean toward interpretive depth that borders on analytical rather than purely empathic, and the psychiatrist could go deeper into the loneliness or grief beneath the surface.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from defensive framing through the mirror moment to audition callbacks and social exclusion fear, with appropriate pacing. The transition between the career and social stakes could be more explicitly connected to maintain a tighter strategic thread.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the session as a joint exploration, matching Lukas's style and following his contributions rather than redirecting. Could be strengthened by more explicit co-creation of the session's direction, such as asking Lukas to set or adjust the agenda mid-session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Lukas's autonomy through language like 'You don't have to perform a problem for me' and a non-directive stance that never implies a required outcome. Could be elevated by more explicit affirmations of Lukas's capacity and agency, particularly when he articulates fear of social exclusion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of open-ended questions that position Lukas as the source of insight, successfully eliciting the powerful audition callback story. After Lukas articulates 'the window isn't infinite,' the psychiatrist reflects on self-concept rather than evoking further change talk around that specific fear, representing a missed opportunity for deeper evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Lukas as the expert on his own experience, inviting rather than directing, and the session flow is largely shaped by Lukas's responses. Could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language and by reducing the occasional sense of directional steering.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes meaningful change talk, particularly around the audition callback, but does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or strategically reflect this change talk back to Lukas. Missed opportunities to ask evocative follow-up questions that would deepen and strengthen the client-generated change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully throughout — the psychiatrist acknowledges Lukas's perspectives without challenging or arguing, and never amplifies or reinforces resistance. The session ends without offering a double-sided reflection or gentle pivot on the fear of social exclusion, which could have been more strategically addressed.
