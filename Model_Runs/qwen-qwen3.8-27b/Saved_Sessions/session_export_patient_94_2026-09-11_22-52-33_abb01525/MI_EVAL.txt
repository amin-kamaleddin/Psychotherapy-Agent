# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Maria’s caution and choice. The stance is supportive, though somewhat procedural and not always deeply validating of her frustration with the medical system.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some awareness of Maria’s practicality and caution, but reflections remain fairly surface-level. The psychiatrist misses deeper emotional themes, such as feeling pressured, distrust, and the meaning of the inhaler to her work life.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on asthma, inhaler use, and a written plan, but the direction is inconsistent. It drifts into repeated safety instructions and administrative steps rather than guiding Maria toward a clear motivational goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and allows Maria to adjust wording, which shows some collaboration. However, much of the session is still driven by the psychiatrist’s prepared script rather than being fully co-created with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely respects Maria’s autonomy and avoids pressuring her to use the daily inhaler. However, repeated directives about bringing the note, using rescue medication, and red flags can feel somewhat controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some open questions, but the evocation is brief and does not follow up deeply enough on Maria’s values, research, or self-efficacy. More scaling or values-based questions would help draw out motivation from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial partnership by asking Maria to choose among options. The interaction is uneven, however, because the session still feels more clinician-led than truly shared or equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria offers some change-oriented statements, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or expands on them. There are missed opportunities to strengthen her own reasons for considering more proactive asthma care.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Maria’s distrust of the daily inhaler, which is helpful. However, the session does not effectively reframe or redirect that sustain talk into a gentle opening for change.
