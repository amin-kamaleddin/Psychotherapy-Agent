# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance while avoiding pressure or argument with the client’s minimizing statements. Acceptance is strong but could be deepened by more explicitly affirming the client’s strengths, values, or difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections to capture ambivalence and the client’s perspective without judgment. Empathy remains largely behavioral and surface-level, with limited exploration of deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on a specific, client-selected target behavior related to smoking in the truck. The direction is appropriate but becomes somewhat narrow and repetitive without connecting more explicitly to broader health goals or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a collaborative interaction in which the client’s ideas shape the plan and the session avoids directive advice. Collaboration could be strengthened by allowing the client to guide pacing and next steps more fully.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes the client’s freedom to choose and avoids controlling or coercive language. This is a particularly strong area, as the counselor works within the client’s stated limits without pressure to quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites the client to generate ideas, cues, and success indicators, but mostly at a practical behavioral level. Evocation is limited because deeper personal reasons, values, fears, or confidence for change are not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly conveys that the client is the expert and follows his agenda rather than imposing a standard plan. Partnership is strong but not perfect because the counselor still drives the structure with repeated question sequences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some useful change talk around small practical steps, such as not smoking in the truck or using gum. Cultivation is limited because the counselor does not consistently explore desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment using broader DARN-CAT strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument, not correcting the client, and redirecting toward choice and small steps. It falls short of exemplary because stronger sustain talk is not actively reframed into change-compatible language.
