# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepted the patient's ambivalence without blame. However, the warmth was somewhat generic and occasionally undercut by a problem-solving stance, with fewer explicit affirmations of strengths or capacity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used frequent accurate reflections of ambivalence and the patient's concerns. However, reflections were mostly content-focused and did not fully capture the emotional depth, identity-related fear, or existential doubt behind the patient's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking cessation and gradually narrowed to a specific, manageable target behavior. Direction was occasionally interrupted by generic coping menus before settling on a concise, client-centered goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by offering choices, inviting input, and adapting the plan based on the patient's feedback. However, much of the collaboration remained framed within therapist-generated options rather than being fully co-created by the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was strong support for autonomy through nonjudgmental language, optional steps, and a small, reversible experiment. The psychiatrist could more explicitly affirm the patient's complete ownership of both the decision to change and the decision not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed limited evocation of the patient's internal motivation, values, reasons for change, and confidence in coping. This was a major missed opportunity to strengthen change talk, especially given the patient's expressed concern about future health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invited the patient's input and allowed her preferences to shape the plan, moving toward a simple, patient-selected experiment. The psychiatrist still assumed an expert, advice-giving role by repeatedly offering structured options and coping strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was very limited active cultivation of the patient's own reasons for change, benefits of quitting, or confidence in her ability to cope. The session focused more on barriers and coping options than on eliciting and reinforcing the patient's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not reinforce or argue against sustain talk, which was appropriate, but the conversation remained heavily focused on reasons to maintain the status quo. There was inconsistent redirection of sustain talk toward change talk or use of double-sided reflections.
