# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard and respect without judgment. Statements normalized the client’s perspective and avoided pressure or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used accurate reflective listening to capture the client’s practical and guarded frame of reference. However, the empathy remained more cognitive than deeply emotional, with limited exploration of underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the referral concern, especially alcohol use and practical indicators like work, sleep, and spouse concerns. It ended without a stronger shared change goal or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, framing the conversation as a joint exploration rather than expert-led advice. The client’s preferences clearly shaped the session’s direction and outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforced the client’s right to choose and ownership of the process. There was no pressure, advice-giving, or covert persuasion, even when the client resisted further exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician made some evocative efforts by asking what would feel like a shift and reflecting the client’s own sense of ability. However, the questions were often agenda-driven and did not deeply elicit the client’s values, confidence, or motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed that the client was the expert on his own life and priorities, supporting a collaborative stance. The score was limited because the clinician still set much of the agenda and framed the discussion around hypothetical shifts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produced some change talk related to ability and self-efficacy, especially around past success with smoking. The psychiatrist did not consistently develop this into stronger importance, desire, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue or challenge the client’s sustain talk and instead softened it with validation and reflective statements. More advanced strategies, such as double-sided reflections, were not used.
