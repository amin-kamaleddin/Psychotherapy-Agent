# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Maria's skepticism and time pressure. It is strong but not fully warm enough for a 5 because some statements sound formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects key concerns about mornings, inhaler uncertainty, dependency, and distrust of reassurance. It stops short of 5 because deeper feelings such as frustration and helplessness are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the inhaler safety and dependency concern and adapts to Maria's time limits. It does not fully guide her toward a concrete behavior change plan for asthma self-management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and adjusts the message format to Maria's preferences, creating some joint problem-solving. However, the clinician still generates most of the content and options rather than fully co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes Maria's freedom to choose, no promise, no pressure, and ownership over the decision. This is the strongest MI component in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Maria's specific dependency worry and preferred brief message, but does not elicit deeper values, goals, or intrinsic reasons for change. Evocation remains moderate rather than strong.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Maria as the decision-maker and asks for her input on what would be useful. It is not a 5 because the clinician remains the primary agenda-setter and content provider.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria offers only weak, conditional change talk, and the psychiatrist does not actively expand it into stronger reasons, benefits, confidence, or commitment. The session manages the barrier more than it builds movement toward change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes safety worries and avoids arguing or escalating the resistance. It is not a 5 because it does not more consistently pivot the language toward change or resolve ambivalence.
