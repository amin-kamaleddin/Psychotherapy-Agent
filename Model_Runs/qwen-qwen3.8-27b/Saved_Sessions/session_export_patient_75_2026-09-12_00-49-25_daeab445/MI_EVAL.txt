# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers supportive statements. However, the acceptance feels somewhat clinical and task-focused rather than consistently warm or deeply validating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is reasonable awareness of Linda’s worry, fear, and discouragement, with some reflection of emotional content. The empathy remains surface-level because the psychiatrist often shifts quickly into psychoeducation and problem-solving instead of exploring underlying ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management, inhaler use, side-effect concerns, and practical next steps. It would be stronger if the focus were more consistently tied to Linda’s personal values, goals, and confidence in change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally invites Linda’s input and accepts her preferences, such as choosing a script or checklist. However, the interaction is often expert-driven and didactic rather than a true co-creation process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some respect for choice by offering options and asking what would help most. Autonomy support is limited by frequent directive advice and a lack of explicit language reinforcing Linda’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies heavily on providing information, scripts, and practical solutions rather than eliciting Linda’s own motivations. There is limited use of open-ended questions, rulers, or evocative reflections to deepen her internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership is present through questions and acceptance of Linda’s preferred alarm wording and checklist. The overall stance remains quite expert-driven, with Linda’s input influencing choices but not substantially shaping the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Linda offers several change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently notice, amplify, or build on them. The session would benefit from evocative follow-ups that help Linda expand her own motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or blaming and acknowledges concerns about side effects and dependence in a non-punitive way. However, the responses often remain focused on risks and practical steps rather than strategically exploring ambivalence to open space for change.
