# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains respectful, nonjudgmental language, which helps keep the alliance alive. However, the stance can feel procedural and managerial when safety and consequences are introduced quickly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes a reasonable effort to understand Tyler's frustration and resistance to being forced into treatment. Empathy becomes less emotionally resonant when Tyler shares meaningful experiences about belonging and identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear, sustained focus on the target behavior and change goal. The conversation shifts among multiple topics and becomes more about ending the session than moving toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative gestures, such as inviting Tyler to choose options and avoid direct advice. However, the repeated menu of therapist-generated options limits the sense that the session is truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Tyler's right to choose, slow down, and decide what to share. This is a strength, though it weakens slightly when options and scripts are offered again after he declines them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions that invite Tyler to share what matters to him. These evocations are not followed up deeply enough to develop his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an overtly authoritarian tone and invites Tyler's preferences. The partnership remains limited because the psychiatrist often reoffers structured options even after Tyler rejects them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is little effective cultivation of change talk regarding substance use, risk, or safer choices. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect and amplify statements that could be redirected toward change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Tyler's sustain talk but does not effectively soften or explore it. Statements about safety and consequences can increase defensiveness rather than help Tyler examine his own ambivalence.
