# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids labeling, which helps reduce defensiveness. However, the warmth is somewhat formulaic and can become procedural rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates good reflective listening by accurately capturing Marek’s values and ambivalence. However, the empathy is often content-focused and does not fully explore the deeper emotions behind his defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on alcohol use and guides toward a concrete next step involving bloodwork and a potential small change. However, the direction is sometimes diluted by repeated medical education and options without clear summarization.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and invites input, creating a collaborative atmosphere. However, the interaction is not fully equal in idea generation, as the counselor frequently supplies concrete options and a suggested plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently affirms Marek’s freedom to choose and avoids coercive language. However, some questions may feel slightly pushing before Marek has fully explored his ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor draws out meaningful values and concerns, but also provides many external reasons for change and specific options. This reduces the opportunity for Marek to generate more of his own internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marek’s input and avoids an authoritarian stance. However, the interaction can feel more counselor-driven due to the frequent presentation of a menu of options and specific experiments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some meaningful change talk, such as wanting to stay sharp and protect his ability to earn. However, the counselor often stops after these statements rather than deepening or amplifying them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and normalizes Marek’s perspective, but does not consistently use skillful techniques to soften sustain talk. Some reflections may inadvertently reinforce the idea that change is unnecessary without objective medical proof.
