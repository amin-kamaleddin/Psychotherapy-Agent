# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment through consistent affirmations and avoids blaming the client for missed opportunities. Acceptance is strong, though it would deepen by more explicitly normalizing the emotional strain of single motherhood and systemic obstacles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening, especially in naming frustration and the conflict between self-belief and childcare barriers. Empathy would be stronger if it explored deeper emotions such as exhaustion, disappointment, fear of failure, and post-role-play relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior: arranging childcare so the client can attend the interview. Each step moves the conversation toward concrete action without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting the client into planning, problem-solving, and role-play. Collaboration would be more fully realized if the client co-authored scripts and messages rather than receiving therapist-drafted language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly affirms the client's decision-making authority and uses optional, non-pressuring language. Autonomy support would be stronger if the therapist elicited the client's own wording more often before providing scripts or reminders.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes the client's plans, confidence ratings, and insight that feeling lighter came from questioning helplessness. Evocation would improve by reducing therapist-generated solutions and asking for more client-originated language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the decision-maker, and her ideas clearly shape the session. Partnership would be stronger if the therapist asked for more client-generated wording instead of supplying scripts, tips, and reminder lines.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits clear change talk through planning questions, confidence ratings, and affirmations of progress. The session would deepen change talk by exploring the broader values, meaning, and strengths behind the client's motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barrier-focused sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and redirected into practical planning. More advanced softening techniques, such as double-sided reflections, would help turn barrier talk into stronger change language.
