# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids moralizing about the client’s struggles. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper through more individualized affirmation of the client’s worth and resilience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate perspective-taking and reflects the client’s ambivalence about health, downtime, and past failures. Empathy is limited by not fully exploring the fear, disappointment, or vulnerability behind the client’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity in a sustainable way. The psychiatrist guides the client from ambivalence to a specific, actionable plan with clear follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively and the client’s preferences substantially shape the plan. Collaboration is somewhat reduced at times when the psychiatrist provides structured options and specific planning steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supports the client’s sense of choice, ownership, and self-determination. Autonomy support is limited by some directive suggestions and moments where confidence is placed more in the psychiatrist than the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own reasons, goals, and ideas for change. Evocation is less strong when the psychiatrist supplies specific plan components rather than fully eliciting the client’s own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly treats the client as the expert on his own life and invites his preferences into the process. Partnership is slightly weakened when the psychiatrist shifts into a more advisory or expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses effective questions to draw out client language supporting change. Some change talk could be amplified more deeply by exploring what the goals mean to the client rather than relying on menu-like questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it, and helps the client create flexible alternatives. The response to sustain talk is more problem-solving than classic MI-style softening or reframing of ambivalence.
