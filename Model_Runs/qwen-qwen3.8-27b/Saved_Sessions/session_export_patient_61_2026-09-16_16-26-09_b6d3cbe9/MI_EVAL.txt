# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, nonjudgment, and respect, even when Jonathan is resistant or noncompliant. The counselor avoids labeling or expressing disappointment, maintaining an affirming stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures fatigue, uncertainty, and the emotional weight of being told what to do. It falls short of a 5 because some reflections are interpretive and deeper emotions are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on diabetes self-management but lacks a clear, specific target behavior or concrete next step. The conversation drifts into general fatigue and avoidance without developing a focused action goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and allows Jonathan’s final request to shape the closing agreement. However, the session remains largely driven by the counselor’s questions and interpretations rather than fully mutual agenda control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent support for choice, pace, and ownership, with little pressure or implicit control. The counselor explicitly honors Jonathan’s request to avoid lists, worksheets, or report expectations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out important client themes such as fatigue, judgment, and lack of bandwidth, but does not deeply evoke values, reasons, or confidence for change. More scaling or importance/confidence questions would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership language acknowledges Jonathan’s expertise and invites his perspective in defining engagement. The session, however, remains largely counselor-led in structure and pace, so partnership is strong but not fully equalized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some open, change-oriented attempts, but the counselor does not affirm or build on Jonathan’s small change statements. There is little exploration of DARN-C, and the client remains mostly in sustain talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and gently redirects resistance toward autonomy and safety, but does not consistently move the conversation toward change. The ending largely preserves the status quo, which may entrench avoidance.
