# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating Camila’s concerns. Acceptance is slightly limited by a task-focused, advice-heavy style.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several accurate but surface-level reflections of Camila’s perspective. There is limited exploration of deeper feelings, values, or the meaning of her barriers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on increasing physical activity and moves toward a concrete plan. Direction is strong, though the planning becomes somewhat procedural at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices and helps co-construct a realistic plan with Camila. Collaboration could be stronger by giving Camila more time to generate her own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes Camila’s choice and avoids pressuring her to commit. Autonomy support is somewhat reduced by the amount of structure and prescriptive advice offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are evocative questions and affirmations early in the session. The psychiatrist often moves quickly to practical advice, limiting the development of Camila’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a collaborative stance but frequently takes on an expert or problem-solver role. More partnership would require waiting for Camila to generate ideas before offering solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and affirms motivation, but does not consistently amplify it. More systematic use of desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment language would strengthen this skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally acknowledges concerns without judgment or argument. The response to sustain talk is reasonable but could be more strategically softened using double-sided reflections.
