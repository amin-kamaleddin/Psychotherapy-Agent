# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids arguing with Jordan’s stated reasons. The delivery is generally warm but can feel somewhat procedural rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Jordan’s ambivalence between feeling healthy and worrying about his grandparents. They remain mostly cognitive and repetitive, with limited exploration of deeper emotions such as guilt, fear, or discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays related to vaccination and protecting family, but it branches across data, scripts, masks, testing, and visit plans. It lacks consistent summarization or goal clarification to build toward a clearer decision.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, which supports a partner stance. However, much of the collaboration is procedural because the counselor supplies many structured options, scripts, and data points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan is consistently reminded that no one can force him and is given multiple options to choose from. Autonomy support is strong, though the frequent provision of detailed recommendations can subtly shape the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions about what matters and what would feel right, but often follows them with information and practical options. This limits consistent evocation of Jordan’s own values, concerns, and readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly asks Jordan for input and adapts to his requests for scripts and risk numbers. The session is collaborative, but it remains somewhat counselor-led by structured information and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan expresses meaningful change talk, such as not wanting to harm his grandparents and willingness to text his grandmother. The psychiatrist invites this talk but does not consistently amplify, affirm, or expand on it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged without confrontation, and some double-sided reflections are used. The psychiatrist does not consistently soften resistant statements into bridges toward change, instead often shifting into information or options.
