# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, but acceptance is sometimes tied to Rosa’s performance rather than unconditional regard. There is limited explicit acceptance of her identity as a smoker.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers good reflections of ambivalence, identity, and practical concerns. Reflections could be stronger by deepening the emotional weight of anxiety, fear, or loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation and a concrete one-hour target. Multiple questions and a broad range of strategies can dilute focus and pacing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan and follow-up process are largely co-created with Rosa. However, frequent therapist-generated solutions make the collaboration less fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports Rosa’s choice, control, and self-directed follow-up. Nonpressuring language and acceptance of her boundaries make this the strongest area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some evocative questions to draw motivation from Rosa. Frequent advice and limited deepening of her change talk weaken this domain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites Rosa’s input and allows her to shape the plan, showing a collaborative stance. However, substantial therapist-generated advice reduces the sense that expertise resides primarily with Rosa.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some change talk through scaling and open questions, but does not consistently develop or amplify it. Frequent advice-giving limits further client-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges Rosa’s concerns without agreeing with them, but often responds with problem-solving options. More double-sided reflections would better soften sustain talk and pivot toward change.
