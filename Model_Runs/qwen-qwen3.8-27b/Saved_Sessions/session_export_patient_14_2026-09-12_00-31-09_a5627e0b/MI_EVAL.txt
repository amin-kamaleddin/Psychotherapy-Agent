# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepts the client's constraints without moralizing. It could be stronger by affirming the person, not only the practical behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but mostly focus on logistics rather than emotional weight. There are missed opportunities to reflect ambivalence, worry, and frustration more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on blood pressure, breathlessness, and feasible action, with a clear plan and safety guidance. The approach is somewhat advisor-driven and could better anchor on the client's own goal first.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is adapted well to the client's limits and preferences, showing genuine collaboration. It becomes less co-constructed when the psychiatrist leads summaries and problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, respects the client's boundary, and avoids pushing unrealistic demands. It could further emphasize that the client is fully in charge of starting, continuing, or stopping.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences early, but quickly shifts into advice and plan building. More probing of values, concerns, and confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the client's practical objections and adjusts the plan accordingly. The stance is not fully equal at times, as the psychiatrist takes on an advisory role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some open questions and brief affirmations, but the psychiatrist rarely deepens or amplifies the client's change talk. Missed opportunities include exploring commitment, ability, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's time pressure and practical limits. It could do better by reflecting the underlying ambivalence and turning barriers into a bridge toward change.
