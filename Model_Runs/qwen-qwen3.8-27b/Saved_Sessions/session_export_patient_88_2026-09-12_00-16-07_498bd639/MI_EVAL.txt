# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or labeling. Acceptance is somewhat pragmatic and could be warmer or more affirming of Marcus as a whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects annoyance, confidentiality concerns, and self-consciousness, but misses deeper emotional meaning in key statements. Moments such as the “catch-22” and “I don’t love feeling like garbage” are under-reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on adolescent alcohol use, peer pressure, consequences, and a practical plan. It could more consistently link back to Marcus’s stated goal of not feeling like “trash on Monday.”

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaboration toward the end when Marcus and the psychiatrist co-create a plan. Earlier portions are more clinician-led, with advice and information provided before fully exploring Marcus’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, respects Marcus’s boundaries, and avoids pushing abstinence. The amount of specific advice somewhat reduces the sense that the plan is entirely client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open-ended questions but relies heavily on providing tips, scripts, and harm-reduction information. Important change talk about mornings, focus, and not wanting to feel like garbage is not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, but the interaction becomes fairly expert-driven. More consistent acknowledgment of Marcus as the expert on his own life and social world would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus offers meaningful change talk, especially about worsening mornings and not wanting to feel like garbage. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or expand this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Marcus’s sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, the responses often default to practical advice rather than reflective or double-sided responses that gently turn sustain talk toward change.
