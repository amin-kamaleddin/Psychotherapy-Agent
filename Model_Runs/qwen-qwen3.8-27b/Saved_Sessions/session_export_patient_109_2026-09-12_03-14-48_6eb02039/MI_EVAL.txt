# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and generally accepted Rafael’s ambivalence without pressure. Affirmations were helpful but sometimes focused on task completion rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, especially around exhaustion, barriers, and small actions. Some deeper self-critical and ambivalent feelings were acknowledged more summarily than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on returning to the gym and building a realistic routine. The conversation moved clearly from identifying barriers to concrete steps, timing, and follow-through.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well by inviting input and adapting to Rafael’s preferences. However, the psychiatrist supplied many options and plan elements, so the session was less fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice through open invitations and allowed Rafael to make decisions about timing, reminders, and fallback actions. Autonomy could be strengthened by giving more space for Rafael to generate his own options first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some effective open questions and a confidence scale to draw out client-generated ideas. Overall, the session relied more on the psychiatrist generating solutions than fully evoking Rafael’s motivations, values, and sense of ability.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist took a collaborative, non-expert stance and invited Rafael’s input throughout. The interaction was still heavily guided by psychiatrist-generated options, which limited some true co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk, including commitment to packing the bag, getting in the door, and choosing an earlier alarm time. More systematic eliciting and amplifying of desire, ability, need, and meaning would have strengthened this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided arguing with Rafael’s all-or-nothing beliefs and acknowledged that a sweaty workout matters to him. More MI-style softening, such as double-sided reflections, would help reduce the binary rule that only a full workout “counts.”
