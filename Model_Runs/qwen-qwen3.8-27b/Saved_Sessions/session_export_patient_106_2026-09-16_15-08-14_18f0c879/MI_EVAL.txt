# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes the client's experience without moralizing. The main limitation is that some supportive phrases feel formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's ambivalence, exhaustion, and fear of being short-tempered with her son. Deeper affective validation of guilt, loneliness, and loss of self would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking, stress, and parenting, but lacks a clear target behavior or progressive structure. More agenda-setting and connection to a specific smoking-related goal would improve direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and respects her constraints, maintaining a collaborative stance. Greater power-sharing through agenda negotiation and co-created goals would make the collaboration stronger.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids advice, pressure, and coercive language, supporting the client's right to choose. An explicit autonomy-supportive repair when the client said the change did not feel like a choice would be optimal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help generate some client ideas, such as noticing tightness or taking a pause. The session does not sufficiently evoke the client's own values, reasons for change, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a non-expert stance and repeatedly asks what the client thinks. Explicitly acknowledging the client's expertise in her own life and co-creating a shared goal would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers tentative change talk, including concern about being short-tempered and curiosity about a pause. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or strategically develop this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's reasons for continuing to smoke. More double-sided reflections and reframing would better acknowledge the function of smoking while connecting it to the client's values.
