# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used warm, nonjudgmental language and validated the patient's concerns about the scale and sweets. Acceptance could deepen further with more explicit acknowledgment of the patient's emotional burden and exhaustion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the patient's ambivalence between control, comfort, guilt, and self-trust. The empathy would be stronger if it more consistently addressed underlying emotions such as shame, fear, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on moving from restrictive, control-based eating toward a more sustainable and non-punishing relationship with food. Direction could be strengthened with more summarization and a clearer consolidated next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used inclusive language and allowed the patient's ideas to shape the experiment and boundary-setting process. Collaboration could be enhanced by more explicitly co-creating the agenda or shared goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided giving direct advice and supported the patient in defining her own experiment and standards. Autonomy support would be stronger with repeated, explicit statements about the patient's freedom to choose and own the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively drew out the patient's own motivations, values, and concrete ideas for change. Evocation could improve by deeper exploration of importance, confidence, past successes, or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the patient's perspective and acknowledged her expertise in her own experience. Partnership could be stronger by more explicitly sharing power in setting the agenda and negotiating priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited meaningful change talk, including a three-day experiment, non-scale indicators, and a boundary around sweets. Cultivating change talk would be stronger with more amplification, summarization, and explicit exploration of commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without judgment, but it was not consistently reframed or softened toward change. The psychiatrist could more actively turn concerns about proof, control, and the scale into strengths, values, or change-oriented questions.
