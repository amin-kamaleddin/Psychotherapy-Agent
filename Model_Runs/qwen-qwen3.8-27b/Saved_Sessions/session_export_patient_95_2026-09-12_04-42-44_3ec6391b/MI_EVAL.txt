# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Maria without judgment and affirms her worth, even when addressing acute risk. Some repeated safety prompts begin to feel mildly pressuring, which slightly limits the sense of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician tracks Maria's metaphors and emotional intensity well, such as the buzzing, stones, and exhaustion. However, some vulnerable statements about shame, loneliness, and feeling “stupid” are met with strong but somewhat generic reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the urgent concern of suicidal ideation and immediate safety, which is clinically appropriate. The direction becomes somewhat circular with repeated risk questions and overlapping safety options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, but the session remains heavily structured around risk assessment and safety tasks. Maria explicitly experiences the interaction as “checking boxes,” which limits the depth of true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids commanding language and repeatedly frames options as optional, supporting Maria's right to choose. The repeated safety prompts still subtly feel controlling to a client who is resistant to tasks and checklists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several good evocative questions that invite Maria to identify what has kept her going, including her friend, survival, and dog. The session ends at a powerful change-talk moment about the dog without fully developing or amplifying that resource.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Maria's perspective and avoids being purely authoritarian, but the agenda remains strongly clinician-driven. Maria's statement that she feels “checked off” signals that the partnership needs more explicit co-creation and fewer rapid-fire options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some important reasons for staying, including Maria's friend, wanting the buzzing to stop, and her dog. These change-talk moments are not consistently reflected, amplified, or expanded into stronger hope or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Maria's hopelessness and avoids arguing directly with her statements that “nothing works.” However, the response could better soften sustain talk by using double-sided reflections that hold both her pain and her continued presence in the same frame.
