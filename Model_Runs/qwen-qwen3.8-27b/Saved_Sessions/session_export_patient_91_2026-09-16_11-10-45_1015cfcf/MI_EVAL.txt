# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a generally warm and nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the client's fears around the needle and high numbers. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client's worth and capacity beyond the specific task.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic reflections are present, especially around the needle fear and pacing requests, but they remain somewhat procedural in emotional moments. Deeper reflections of anxiety, self-doubt, and the meaning of the first reading would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of finger-prick self-monitoring and what to do with a high number. There is clear guidance without major wandering, supporting a coherent and actionable focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is evident through choices and shared pacing, but the instructional segments become more provider-led. More co-creation of the plan, especially in sequencing and personalizing steps, would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers options, avoids pressure, and respects the client's choice to stop after one attempt. Autonomy support could be further strengthened by having the client generate more of the plan independently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative prompts about the hardest part and how the first check felt, but the session relies more on instruction than eliciting the client's own reasons for change. More questions about values, confidence, and personal importance would deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership is clear in the early choice-based interactions, but the educational and instructional sections shift toward a more expert-led role. Explicitly positioning the client as the expert on their own routine would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is supported through affirmations and validation of effort, but the provider does not consistently deepen or expand the client's reasons for change. More targeted eliciting of importance and confidence would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct argument and reframes some resistance in a balanced way, but sustain talk is mostly managed through practical redirection rather than strong MI-specific softening. More double-sided reflections would better address ambivalence and fear of failure.
