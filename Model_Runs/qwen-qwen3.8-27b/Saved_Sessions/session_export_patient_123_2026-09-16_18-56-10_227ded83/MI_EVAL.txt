# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and conveys acceptance. Slightly formulaic, with one mild evaluative comment about panic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects ambivalence and captures both sides of the dilemma. Misses opportunities to build on new ideas, with a minor wording inaccuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session lacks a clear, clinically relevant target behavior. The focus on school routine is not connected to the alcohol-risk referral.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and invites input, but the interaction is uneven. The clinician frequently introduces specific options rather than following the client's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports autonomy by framing choices, respecting readiness, and avoiding pressure. Could be strengthened by more explicit statements that decisions are Sofia's.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical ideas but does not deeply draw out values, identity, or intrinsic motivation. Questions remain logistical rather than motivational.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positions Sofia as the expert, but the clinician still drives much of the agenda. The power dynamic is uneven because of repeated therapist-led options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia offers meaningful change talk, but the clinician does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen it. Use of summaries, scaling, and motivational questions is limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or reinforcement. The response remains within the problem rather than pivoting toward values or change.
