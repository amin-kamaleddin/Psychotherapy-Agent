# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids labeling or shaming around Milan's alcohol use. Acceptance is strong but could be more explicit in affirming Milan's worth beyond the drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Milan's ambivalence between being himself and avoiding social awkwardness. Empathy could deepen by exploring underlying emotions such as embarrassment, fear, shame, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on Milan's alcohol use at gatherings and the goal of being himself without alcohol getting in the way. The target behavior is consistently clear and relevant throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Milan's ideas, such as a buddy, one can instead of three, and staying engaged in the party, are taken seriously and shape the discussion. The interaction feels collaborative rather than directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes that Milan's choice is his, avoids pressure or advice, and validates his desire to avoid labels and big plans. Autonomy is strongly supported without undermining the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections draw out Milan's own language, such as wanting to be in the conversation and being more into the party. Evocation could be stronger by deepening values, confidence, ability, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently asks for Milan's perspective and treats his input as important to the work. Partnership could improve with more explicit language acknowledging Milan as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects and amplifies emerging change talk, including wanting to be in the conversation and naming a smaller option. It could be more strategic in developing desire, ability, reason, need, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with minimization and instead reflects ambivalence while redirecting toward workable options. Softening could be stronger by more explicitly connecting Milan's sustain talk to deeper values or self-efficacy.
