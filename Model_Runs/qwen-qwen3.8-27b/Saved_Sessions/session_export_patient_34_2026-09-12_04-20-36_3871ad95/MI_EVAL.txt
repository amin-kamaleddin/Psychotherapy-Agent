# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful, nonjudgmental stance, but repeatedly labels Dylan as angry rather than fully accepting his self-description. This limits the depth of acceptance and can maintain some defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses adequate reflective listening but becomes somewhat formulaic. Reflections miss Dylan’s more nuanced feelings of annoyance, embarrassment, and concern about lasting consequences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to the referred incident and immediate consequences. However, it does not consistently anchor the conversation to the target behavior, values, or discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a collaborative stance by inviting Dylan to choose and using his own language. Still, the session is led by counselor-generated options rather than fully co-creating Dylan’s agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice, including offering a no-action option. However, continued offering of structured options when Dylan prefers to just talk can feel subtly pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some open-ended evocative questions but does not deeply access Dylan’s values, goals, or change motivation. The conversation often shifts quickly toward practical scripts and consequence management.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some power-sharing through questions asking what feels most helpful. However, repeated counselor-generated option menus can feel directive when Dylan resists planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a few evocative questions but does not effectively elicit Dylan’s own reasons for responsibility or change. The focus quickly becomes script-building rather than change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Dylan but often normalizes his minimization of responsibility. There is limited use of double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk such as “I didn’t do anything wrong.”
