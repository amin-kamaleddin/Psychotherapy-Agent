# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental, respectful language and avoids shaming Jason for the violation or minimizing it. Acceptance is present but feels somewhat task-focused rather than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate complex reflections about fairness, identity, and the perceived penalty. Empathy is limited by repetition and by not going deeper into shame, fear, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and the conduct violation, but the questions become repetitive and circular. Direction could be improved by moving more deliberately through MI stages and addressing the conduct record concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the conversation as collaborative and lets Jason’s ideas shape a concise boundary. Collaboration is limited by repetitive prompts and incomplete co-creation around the conduct process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Jason’s autonomy, avoids pressure, and acknowledges his boundary against monitoring. However, the counselor could more explicitly affirm Jason’s full right to choose, including maintaining current behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits a self-stated plan to avoid on-campus drinking. The evocation remains superficial because it does not deeply explore values, confidence, future self, or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a non-expert stance and frequently signals that Jason is the expert on his own life. Partnership is strengthened by collaboration but weakened by repetitive questioning and limited agenda negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jason offers useful change talk, including a concrete off-campus plan, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen it. More scaling, importance, confidence, and values questions would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or reinforcing defensiveness, but Jason’s sustain talk remains dominant. Double-sided reflections, discrepancy, or gentle reframing could better soften the focus on the rule, record, and status quo.
