# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, normalizing Marcus's anxiety and affirming effort. Falls short of 5 because acceptance is sometimes quickly paired with practical problem-solving rather than deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflects key feelings and facts accurately, but often remains surface-level. Needs more complex, nuanced reflections of fear, judgment, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, behaviorally specific focus on requesting a demo and calling at a set time. Could be stronger by tying the action more to Marcus's values and self-management identity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and adjusts based on Marcus's preferences, but often supplies fully formed scripts and tools. More co-creation of Marcus's own language would make it more equal-partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly invites choice and avoids pressure, supporting Marcus's sense of control. Score reduced by repeated provision of clinician-generated scripts and reminders rather than first evoking his own wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses scaling and open questions to raise confidence and identify a small step, but does not consistently draw out Marcus's values, reasons, and self-efficacy. More evocative questions would make motivation more internal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborates and allows Marcus to shape the plan, yet frequently steps into an expert role with ready-made solutions. More explicit client-ownership language would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces several change statements and increased confidence, but quickly shifts to logistics. More amplification and expansion of Marcus's own change talk is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Normalizes doubt and avoids arguing, but often responds with contingency planning instead of softening sustain talk toward change. More double-sided reflections or gentle reframing would improve ambivalence resolution.
