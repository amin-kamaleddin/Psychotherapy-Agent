# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally warm, nonjudgmental, and validating. Some subtle clinical/labeling language slightly limits the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Perspective-taking is accurate and nuanced, reflecting both feelings and values. The therapist consistently tracks ambivalence and emotional texture.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a specific target behavior and value-congruent goal. Progress is purposeful and consistently directed toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist reflects, asks questions, and co-constructs ideas with the client. The interaction is collaborative, though the clinician still sets much of the conceptual framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through open questions and validates self-directed process. Some gentle normative steering prevents a top score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client generates strong motivations, values, and coping ideas that are then reflected back. Evocation is strong, though occasionally supported by therapist-supplied framing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert on her own life and her ideas shape the session. The balance is strong, but the therapist still guides direction quite heavily.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes meaningful change talk and supports it with reflections. The process could be more systematic in exploring confidence, importance, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met without argument, using normalization and double-sided reflections. Some responses remain slightly fixated on guilt or uncertainty without an immediate active pivot.
