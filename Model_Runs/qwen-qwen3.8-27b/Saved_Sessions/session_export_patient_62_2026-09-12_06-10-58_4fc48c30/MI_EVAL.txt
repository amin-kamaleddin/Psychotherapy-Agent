# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental stance with clear positive regard. Frequent direct safety checks were clinically appropriate but occasionally made the interaction feel slightly mechanical.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate and normalizing reflections of Jordan’s exhaustion, stuckness, low energy, and fear of rejection. Empathy was somewhat surface-level and did not fully capture deeper ambivalence or underlying meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on Jordan’s immediate stressors and moved toward a concrete next step within 48 hours. Direction was therapist-led and practical, but did not consistently link actions back to Jordan’s values or reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by involving Jordan in choosing options, gathering details, and drafting the landlord message. Collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist performed much of the cognitive and logistical work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices, respected Jordan’s limits, and emphasized low-effort steps. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing Jordan’s ownership over sending the message and pursuing next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked helpful questions to identify pressing concerns and move toward a concrete action. Deeper evocation of values, hopes, and confidence was limited, and the session shifted quickly into practical problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Jordan to choose and respected his preferences, creating a generally collaborative stance. Partnership was limited by the psychiatrist’s expert/manager role and Jordan’s occasional passivity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change talk and affirmed Jordan’s concrete step of sending the text. There was limited follow-up to expand Jordan’s own change language or explore what desired outcomes would mean to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Jordan’s sustain talk and validated his low energy and fear of rejection. MI-specific reframing or complex reflection of global negative statements was not consistently used.
