# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly asks permission before probing. The warmth is somewhat efficient and task-oriented, and the session moves into planning before fully affirming the client’s whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s ambivalence, benefits, and costs of use. Empathy remains more functional and summary-like, missing deeper identity-related emotions such as loneliness, shame, and fear of losing “the vibe.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear, realistic target behavior and a concrete one-week experiment. Pacing is occasionally too plan-focused before the client’s ambivalence is fully consolidated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is genuinely shaped by the client’s preferences, boundaries, and self-chosen goals. A more fully collaborative approach would have invited the client to generate more of their own ideas before introducing the psychiatrist’s options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through permission-seeking, choice emphasis, and respect for the client’s boundary against monitoring. The repeated menu of practical options still carries a mildly directive tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions invite the client to identify values, barriers, and reasons for change. The session becomes too quickly advice- and plan-oriented, limiting deeper evocation of the client’s internal motivation and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, respects boundaries, and offers choices, showing partial partnership. The expert role remains prominent when presenting a long list of strategies before fully exploring the client’s own perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some values-based change talk and affirms client statements that support change. There is limited follow-up, scaling, or amplification to expand and consolidate the client’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, judging, or confronting the client. It is not consistently redirected into change language, especially around identity, belonging, and “the vibe.”
