# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally respectful and validating of the client's pain and fear, but occasionally framing reactions as stress felt minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequately reflects surface distress and being dismissed, but does not consistently explore deeper fears, identity loss, or relational strain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on pain, tests, and communication without wandering, but is more procedural than strategically MI-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly co-creates scripts and role-play, adapting wording to the client's preferences and tone.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequently offers choices about wording and next steps, but medical advice and scripts are not always explicitly framed as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mostly supplies reasons and biomedical plans rather than eliciting the client's own values, motivations, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Allows the client to shape practical details, but the psychiatrist remains the primary expert driving content and recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rare action-oriented statements by the client are not systematically evoked, amplified, or consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoids direct confrontation but largely reinforces test-seeking rather than gently redirecting the need for certainty.
