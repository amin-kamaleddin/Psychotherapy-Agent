# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating tone and avoids labeling or correcting Ryan. Acceptance is consistent but remains somewhat transactional rather than deeply personal or affirming of Ryan’s broader worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several accurate surface-level reflections of Ryan’s stress, embarrassment, and unfairness. However, the psychiatrist misses key opportunities to explore deeper emotions, especially the fear that the incident will define him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session frequently shifts away from alcohol use and ambivalence toward legal/academic appeal preparation. As a result, the target MI behavior receives only peripheral attention rather than sustained exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist assumes an expert-advisor role by providing checklists, drafting language, and prescribing a tracking plan. Ryan’s input does not meaningfully shape the direction or content of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes choice-giving questions and respects Ryan’s preference to keep tracking simple. However, autonomy is limited by the directive prescribing of specific limits and harm-reduction steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supplies information and solutions rather than drawing out Ryan’s own motivations, values, or change talk. Key moments for evocation are met with checklists or binary choices instead of open, client-centered exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor quickly adopts an advising stance focused on conduct procedures and practical next steps. There is limited evidence of a genuine partnership in which Ryan’s perspective drives the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ryan offers several statements of desire and need for safety, but the psychiatrist does not reflect, amplify, or ask follow-up questions to deepen them. Change talk is largely missed or reduced to logistical next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Ryan’s minimization and sustain talk. However, the session mostly sidesteps this sustain talk rather than using reflections or discrepancy work to soften it.
