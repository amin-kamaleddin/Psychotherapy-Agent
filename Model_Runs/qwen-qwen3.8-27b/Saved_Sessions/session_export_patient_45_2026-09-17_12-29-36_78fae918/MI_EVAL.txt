# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and non-judgment throughout, with strong validation of Erik's experience. The acceptance occasionally reads as procedural rather than deeply individualized — Erik's specific identity as a shift supervisor could have been affirmed more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic understanding with several excellent complex reflections, particularly capturing the emotional function of the beer pattern. Missed a brief but meaningful emotional disclosure when Erik said 'I know how that sounds,' which signaled vulnerability and self-awareness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with strong session momentum, skillfully narrowing from broad concerns to the specific Tuesday night pattern. Could have more explicitly linked the specific behavior change to the broader diabetes management goal in a way Erik could see the connection himself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treats the session as joint exploration with inclusive, non-hierarchical language. The psychiatrist remains the primary question-asker and direction-setter, and could have more explicitly acknowledged Erik's unique expertise in areas where the psychiatrist genuinely does not have answers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout — consistently returns decision-making to Erik without ever being directive. The powerful reflection 'You're not resistant to change, you're resistant to being told' and the final framing of the plan as Erik's own are particularly strong.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong evocative questions that successfully draw out Erik's own motivations, values, and plan. Could have gone one level deeper by explicitly eliciting Erik's self-efficacy language and amplifying what his autonomy statement reveals about his own capacity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a genuinely collaborative stance, deferring to Erik's self-knowledge and shaping the session around his contributions. Slightly guided by the psychiatrist's choice of focus rather than fully co-created — could have more explicitly invited Erik to co-structure the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully creates space for Erik to generate meaningful change talk through well-crafted evocative questions. Does not fully amplify or reinforce Erik's strongest change talk moments, such as when he says he wants to be 'the one calling the shots.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by acknowledging it without arguing and gently pivoting forward. Could have used more deliberate double-sided reflections (e.g., 'you don't want to give up the beer — and that makes sense. And you're also curious about where the line is') to actively soften rather than merely manage it.
