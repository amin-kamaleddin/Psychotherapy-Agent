# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoided arguing with the patient's sustain talk. However, acceptance was somewhat practical rather than deeply emotionally affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few accurate content reflections, but emotional meaning was not consistently explored. Frustration, anxiety, and concern about having an unconfirmed problem were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on coffee consumption, blood pressure, and next steps. The direction was clear and stable, though more logistical and risk-management oriented than change-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributed meaningfully to the plan, especially the choice to use a home blood pressure cuff. However, the psychiatrist supplied most of the structure and options, limiting deeper co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honored the patient's choice, asked permission, and respected his reluctance to change his routine. It was not a 5 because the plan remained somewhat therapist-led and directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was limited effort to draw out the patient's own motivations, values, or self-efficacy. The conversation moved quickly into education and monitoring rather than eliciting internal change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and allowed the patient's preferences to shape the plan. However, the psychiatrist remained the primary driver by providing detailed options, instructions, and thresholds.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some early attempts were made to evoke change-oriented language, but change talk was not actively developed or amplified. The session focused more on practical monitoring than on strengthening reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was tolerated and gently redirected toward lower-risk actions without confrontation. However, more advanced MI strategies such as double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance were not used.
