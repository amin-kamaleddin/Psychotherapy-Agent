# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with repeated affirmations of effort and honesty (e.g., "that's okay," "glad you picked something simple"). Falls slightly short of a 5 due to a somewhat procedural quality that could have included deeper, individualized emotional validation of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Cognitive and practical reflections are present ("sounds really frustrating," "practical and honest") but remain mostly surface-level. Misses opportunity to emotionally explore distress, fear, shame, or the symbolic role cigarettes play in helping him feel calm and competent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, focused momentum throughout—from broad asthma concern to specific triggers, cues, day/time, confidence ratings, and concrete plan. Minimal drift; consistently guided toward a tangible target without losing sight of the presenting issue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as joint problem-solving and the final plan is clearly co-constructed with his input. Slightly diminished by repeated lists of clinician-supplied strategies rather than fully waiting for his own solutions to emerge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** "You're in charge," invites choices, accepts his decision to keep cigarettes, and avoids quit-pressure. Minor deduction for a few rapid narrowing questions ("What one small step…?") that felt mildly directive before deeper exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes some open questions ("What's on your mind?", confidence scaling, "which feels most doable?") and he offers some self-generated motivation. Lacks deeper probing of values, hopes, or future consequences; multiple clinician-supplied options limit further client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong expert-in-his-life stance—asks him to choose, values his taxi knowledge, and lets his contributions shape the session. Occasionally softened by supplying multiple specific intervention options, making it feel slightly more menu-driven than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some practical change language (confidence 6→8, "simple enough that I won't make an excuse") and he identifies the water bottle cue himself. Missed opportunities to ask what breathing easier would mean for him or what his ability says about him, keeping evocation somewhat superficial.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental acceptance of his sustain talk ("not quitting right now, and that's okay") avoids confrontation. Could be stronger with double-sided reflections or strategic pivots (e.g., "on one hand it helps you stay calm, on the other your chest isn't okay") to redirect his words toward change.
