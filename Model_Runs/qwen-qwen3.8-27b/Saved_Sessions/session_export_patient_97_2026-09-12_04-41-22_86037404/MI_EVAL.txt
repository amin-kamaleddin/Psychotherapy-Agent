# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, affirming Mateo's values and strengths. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not fully resonate with the deeper loneliness, fear, and futility he expresses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial reflections capture Mateo's central ambivalence and pain, but later emotional statements are acknowledged rather than deeply explored. Multiple questions at once may reduce the depth of perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of avoiding old circles and managing high-risk urges. The main limitation is an overemphasis on practical tactics, which can narrow the broader values-based direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is present through client choices and joint problem-solving, but it is uneven. The psychiatrist often supplies many options and scripts, shifting toward an expert-advisor role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice and avoids coercive language, respecting Mateo's decisions. The volume of suggestions, however, can create subtle pressure to implement a plan during the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk and strengths, but frequently moves into advice and problem-solving. More reflective listening and strategic follow-up would strengthen client-driven motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo is invited to choose and his input shapes some decisions, but the frequent presentation of multiple strategies creates an uneven dynamic. The session would benefit from fewer clinician-generated options and more client-led planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms Mateo's change talk and draws out important motivations, such as protecting his daughter. However, the move toward practical advice limits the expansion and amplification of his own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Mateo and normalizes his doubts, but often responds with additional tactics rather than MI-consistent softening. More double-sided reflections would better address the sustain talk.
