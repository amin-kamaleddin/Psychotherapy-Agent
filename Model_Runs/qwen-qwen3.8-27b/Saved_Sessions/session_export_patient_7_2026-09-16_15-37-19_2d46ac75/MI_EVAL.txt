# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, nonjudgment, and support without blaming or pressuring the client. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth is somewhat formulaic and a few interpretive statements could impose meaning on a guarded client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reflects the client's feelings of being pressured, judged, and tired, including the shift toward needing quiet. Empathy remains mostly surface-level and repetitive, missing deeper exploration of shame, fear, exhaustion, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on making the interaction less pressuring and eventually narrows toward quiet. It is repetitive rather than strategically progressive, lacking a clear summary and a small collaboratively chosen next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through inclusive language and adjusting toward less pressure and silence. The interaction remains more clinician-led than truly negotiated or mutually shaped by the client's limited responses.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports choice by offering options, asking permission, and honoring the request to sit quietly. It is not a 5 because questions continue after signaling a non-push stance, and some meanings are interpreted before fully ceding control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician makes some effort to evoke the client's own preferences through questions about safety, usefulness, and quiet. The evocation remains superficial and does not develop into deeper client-owned motivation or meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on her own experience and avoids a purely expert-driven stance. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still largely controls the agenda and question structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little genuine change talk elicited or built from the client's own language. The session produces minimal desire, need, ability, values, or confidence language, and useful MI moves are often clinician-generated rather than client-evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and appropriately softens statements with validation such as “I hear you” and “That makes sense.” However, sustain talk is mostly validated rather than redirected through double-sided reflection toward change, values, or a preferred direction.
