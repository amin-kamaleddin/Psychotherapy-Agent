# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and encouraging. Warmth is strong but somewhat formulaic, so acceptance is not fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Lars’s concerns, practical conflicts, and realistic risks. Deeper emotional states such as fear, excitement, and ambivalence are only partially reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on target re-entry behaviors and moves efficiently toward a concrete plan. There is no meaningful drift away from the core goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Lars’s ideas and co-constructs much of the action plan. However, the counselor often supplies specific scripts, timing, and options, which reduces the sense of equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lars is regularly given choices, his preferences are respected, and he retains ownership of the plan. The counselor’s structured suggestions can still subtly steer the session toward a preferred pathway.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence scaling help draw out commitment and a small first step. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist relies more on practical suggestions than on exploring Lars’s deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction conveys that Lars is the expert on his own life and decisions. It is strong but not fully equal because the counselor frequently supplies detailed options and contingency plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some commitment and action language around calling the garage and following through at 9 AM. There are fewer opportunities to deepen change talk by exploring Lars’s values, hopes, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging concerns without arguing or blaming. It is effective but not maximally strategic, as it does not use stronger MI techniques such as double-sided reflections to turn resistance into motivation.
