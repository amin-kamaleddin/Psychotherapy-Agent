# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes potential slips. However, validation is often brief and quickly moves into advice, limiting deeper affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of the client’s concerns about weekends, contraception, and family history. The empathy is limited by insufficient exploration of shame, self-doubt, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability with concrete next steps. The direction is weakened by too many clinical recommendations that dilute the motivational focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates practical tools such as the check-in text, “need out” code, and cash-stashing plan. Collaboration is uneven because the clinician often adopts an advisory, checklist-driven stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects the client’s preference for reduction rather than abstinence. Autonomy support is reduced by frequent directive language and prescriptive instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session includes some client input and task-oriented questions. Evocation is limited because the client’s values, reasons, and confidence are not consistently drawn out or strengthened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s choices influence the final plan, such as using sober friends and selecting a check-in time. The partnership is weakened by an early expert-driven stance and frequent clinical suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses useful change talk about cutting down and using her sober social network. The psychiatrist does not systematically amplify, summarize, or use scaling to strengthen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s ambivalence and accepts her preference not to make a big deal of it. Sustain talk is not actively softened through double-sided reflections or deeper ambivalence work.
