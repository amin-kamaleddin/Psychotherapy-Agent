# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with clear affirmations and acceptance of the client's reduction-based goals. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not fully deepen engagement with the client's shame, fear, or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects several core ambivalent themes, such as weekend use, infection worry, and the desire to fit in. However, reflections often remain at a surface level and do not consistently capture underlying emotions such as shame, fear of rejection, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains strong and consistent focus on the target behavior: reducing weekend methamphetamine use. The psychiatrist effectively narrows the client's broad concern into concrete, testable steps without drifting into irrelevant topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes meaningful plan elements, and the psychiatrist builds on them, showing collaborative intent. However, the psychiatrist frequently supplies many strategies and scripts, making the interaction feel more like guided planning than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice, respects the client's preference for reduction over abstinence, and does not pressure decisions such as booking the gym. This is occasionally weakened by the large number of therapist-generated strategies and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence ratings help the client articulate motivation and increase self-efficacy. However, the psychiatrist often moves quickly to advice before fully evoking the client's own values, reasons, and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows the client's choices to shape the session, but does not consistently position the client as the primary expert on his own life. The density of therapist-generated advice can make the interaction feel more guided than truly power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently creates opportunities for the client to express his own change talk, such as leaving cash at home, skipping Saturday, and prebooking the gym. However, there are missed opportunities to further deepen, amplify, and expand that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce the client's sustain talk, which is a strength. However, there is limited use of advanced strategies such as double-sided reflections to gently orient the client's ambivalence toward change.
