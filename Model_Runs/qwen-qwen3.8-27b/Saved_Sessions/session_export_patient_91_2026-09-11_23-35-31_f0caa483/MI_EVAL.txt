# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly acknowledges Marcus’s distress. Acceptance is slightly limited by frequent advice-giving and fewer explicit affirmations of the client’s worth or strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some of Marcus’s overwhelm and anxiety, but the empathy is often brief and quickly shifts to practical instructions. Deeper feelings such as fear of failure or daily strain are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target issue of blood glucose self-monitoring and practical next steps. However, the focus becomes more procedural and educational than MI-oriented at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marcus’s input and adjusts to his stated limits, such as choosing a video and script. Collaboration is present but uneven because most of the content and structure is clinician-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and explicitly allows Marcus to pause or stop, which supports autonomy. Autonomy is somewhat undercut by the large amount of directive advice and clinician-provided options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most questions are logistical, and the session relies more on education and resources than on eliciting Marcus’s own motivation. There are clear missed opportunities to explore his reasons, confidence, and values related to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus’s preferences influence the session, including pacing and chosen supports. The partnership remains partially clinician-led, with the psychiatrist functioning more as an educator than an equal collaborator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offers small signs of change talk, such as wanting to start small and using a script. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen these statements through MI-style questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing Marcus’s reluctance and accepts that the task feels overwhelming. However, there is limited use of MI-specific strategies to reframe ambivalence or pivot the client toward change.
