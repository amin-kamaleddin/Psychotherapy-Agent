# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is nonjudgmental and supportive, using phrases like “you won’t waste my time” and “be kind to yourself.” However, the acceptance feels somewhat generic and task-focused rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few empathic acknowledgments, but the psychiatrist mostly clarifies and problem-solves. Samuel’s ambivalence, fear of failure, and concern about his boss’s perception are not deeply explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking reduction, afternoon triggers, and follow-up logistics, with little topic drift. The direction is strong but remains largely clinician-led rather than collaboratively MI-guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration, as Samuel’s constraints influence details like using a pen, shorter delays, and follow-up timing. However, the psychiatrist still drives most of the agenda and prescribes the overall plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and adjusts the plan to Samuel’s practical limits. Yet much of the language remains directive, so client ownership of choice is not consistently emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes only limited efforts to draw out Samuel’s own reasons, values, confidence, or strengths. Many motivations and solutions are supplied externally instead of being evoked from Samuel.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist proposing the taper, slip script, log, and follow-up structure. Although Samuel’s input changes details, he is not positioned as the central expert about his own change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes brief, weak affirmations such as “nice work noticing the pattern” but does not actively develop Samuel’s change talk. There are few questions or reflections designed to amplify his reasons for reducing smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes slips and avoids judgment, which helps reduce defensiveness. However, responses remain mostly corrective and behavioral rather than using MI strategies like double-sided reflections or pivots from sustain talk.
