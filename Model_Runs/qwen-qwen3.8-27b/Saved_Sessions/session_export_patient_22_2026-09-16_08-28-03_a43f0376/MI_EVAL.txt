# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and accepts Camila’s goal to reduce rather than quit. Acceptance is less consistently unconditional and does not fully validate the identity-related fear behind her statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Camila’s drinking pattern, consequences, and ambivalence about control versus social connection. Empathy is limited by moving quickly into problem-solving and not deeply exploring the emotional/identity meaning of her fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing alcohol use and its impact on school, work, sleep, and social life. The conversation moves logically from assessment to a concrete plan for Thursday, reinforced by a clear summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a practical plan and repeatedly incorporates Camila’s preferences and edits. Collaboration is strong but somewhat counselor-led, since the psychiatrist generates most of the options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids coercion, invites choice, and does not impose abstinence or rigid commitments. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly returning all decisions to Camila rather than offering many specific strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses useful open questions, scaling, and rehearsal to elicit some change talk and confidence. However, the counselor frequently offers suggestions before allowing Camila to generate her own motivations, reasons, and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, asks permission, and meaningfully invites Camila to shape the plan. The interaction is strong but not fully equal because the counselor remains the primary source of ideas and strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits statements of desire, ability, and commitment, such as wanting more control and agreeing to try a plan. These change statements are not consistently amplified, reflected, or expanded to deepen Camila’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with, dismiss, or reinforce Camila’s sustain talk, responding in a nonjudgmental and accepting way. Softening is less strategic because it relies more on acceptance than on double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of statements like “I’m not an alcoholic.”
