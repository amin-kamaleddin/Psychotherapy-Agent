# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the patient's concerns without arguing. Acceptance could be slightly warmer and more individualized to reach full unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures the patient's central worry about protecting his ability to work. Deeper emotional reflections about identity, control, and fear of job loss would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on the medication decision, side-effect concerns, and practical adherence. A clearer final summary or concrete next step would improve strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and generally follows the patient's lead on his main concerns. Repeatedly suggesting options the patient rejects slightly weakens the co-constructed nature of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports the patient's choice and right to control the medication decision. Minor leading statements prevent full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses helpful questions to elicit the patient's own decision criteria but does not deeply explore values, hopes, or personal reasons for change. More targeted evocative questions would strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a partnership stance and acknowledges the patient's expertise in his own life. The therapist occasionally proposes solutions before fully letting the patient shape the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk around conditions for trying the medication, but does not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen it. More strategic focus on the patient's own reasons for change is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by reflecting and normalizing concerns rather than arguing. More consistent redirection from sustain talk toward change talk would improve this area.
