# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepted Ingrid's time constraints without labeling her as resistant. The main limitation was that repeated offers to prepare a note began to feel slightly persistent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used accurate reflections of Ingrid's practical goals, such as wanting a clear and quick answer. Empathy was generally good but remained more logistical than deeply values- or emotion-based.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the citalopram-phenelzine interaction and the 14-day washout question. Direction became less effective when the counselor repeatedly returned to note offers and restated safety information instead of guiding the conversation toward a collaborative change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and offered options, showing some collaborative intent. However, much of the session was unilaterally informative, and Ingrid's repeated priorities did not fully reshape the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices, respected Ingrid's stated limits, and did not pressure her to make a medication change. The repeated re-offering of the note after multiple declines slightly weakened the autonomy-supportive tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor made a few early evocative moves by asking what brought Ingrid in and how important change was. Afterward, the session shifted into information-giving without exploring Ingrid's own values, intentions, or language about being prepared and safe.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor showed some partnership by asking what Ingrid wanted to address and offering choices. The relationship became less collaborative when the counselor cycled back to the same note offer rather than positioning Ingrid as the decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were appropriate early attempts to evoke change talk, such as asking about the most important issue and using an importance scale. These were not developed, and the session did not consistently draw out Ingrid's own reasons, goals, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with or challenging Ingrid's stated limits and occasionally acknowledged her need for time. However, the counselor did not consistently use skillful double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk or help resolve ambivalence.
