# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes the patient’s concerns. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not consistently deepen into explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of the patient’s ambivalence and practical concerns. However, the reflections mostly stay at the content level and do not consistently capture deeper emotion, shame, or identity-level conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains broadly focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and social pressure to drink. However, there is no clearly negotiated target behavior or coherent MI agenda, so the conversation drifts into problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites patient input and uses some collaborative language. In practice, the psychiatrist supplies most of the content, leaving the patient mostly evaluating preselected options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses autonomy-supportive phrases such as “whatever you choose” and “if you’d like.” Autonomy support is weakened by repeatedly introducing options and gently pressing for a step to try this week.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions early in the session. The evocation remains superficial because the psychiatrist often provides reasons, scripts, and solutions instead of drawing out the patient’s own values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are partnership elements, including inviting the patient to choose among options. The psychiatrist often assumes an expert/problem-solver role by generating lists of scripts and plans rather than fully co-creating direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expresses clear change talk, such as wanting to protect the baby and commit to stopping if needed. The psychiatrist does not consistently capture, amplify, or expand this change talk before shifting to practical options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and uses normalization. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections or skilled pivots to convert sustain talk into change talk.
