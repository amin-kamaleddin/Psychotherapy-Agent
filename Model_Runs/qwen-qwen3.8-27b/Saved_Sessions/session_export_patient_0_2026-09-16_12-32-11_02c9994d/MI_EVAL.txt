# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Adrian’s frustration without arguing. Acceptance is not a 5 because the tone becomes more instructional and the opening includes a clinical conclusion before fully hearing Adrian’s perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of ambivalence, especially early, but empathy becomes more surface-level as the session shifts to psychoeducation. Deeper feelings such as shame, fear of control, and embarrassment are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on methamphetamine use, infection risk, contamination, and practical harm reduction. The psychiatrist consistently guides the conversation toward actionable, behavior-specific steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adrian contributes meaningfully at points, such as choosing test strips and preferring nasal naloxone. However, the psychiatrist often moves into expert-led education before fully co-creating the plan with Adrian.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly supports Adrian’s choice, avoids coercion, and respects privacy boundaries. This is slightly limited by an information-heavy approach that can reduce Adrian’s sense of being the primary author of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opening questions elicit some client-led goals, but the psychiatrist often shifts to providing reasons, options, and instructions. More scaling, values, and confidence questions would better elicit Adrian’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and adapts to some preferences, but frequently presents lists and instructions in a clinician-led way. A more balanced partnership would involve more shared agenda-setting and client-generated problem solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adrian offers useful change-compatible statements, such as wanting to avoid another infection and being open to testing. The psychiatrist acknowledges this but does not consistently amplify, deepen, or reinforce the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Adrian’s sustain talk without judgment or argument. However, the responses mostly acknowledge these statements and then move forward, rather than using strategic reflections to redirect them toward harm-reduction action.
