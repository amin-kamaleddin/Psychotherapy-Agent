# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Camila's shame and fear without dismissing or labeling. Zero instances of judgment, disapproval, or conditional acceptance — treats her fears and self-criticism as legitimate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Goes beyond surface-level reflection to capture emotional nuance, such as the complex reflection on self-minimization versus external validation and holding contradictory truths (fear of becoming her uncle AND determination to stop). Consistently asks permission to explore deeper and distinguishes feeling from thinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use with a logical progression from presentation through consequences to emotional core and motivation, without drifting into unrelated topics. The session ends somewhat abruptly, missing an opportunity for a collaborative summary or next-step planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the conversation as a joint exploration between equals, using language like 'we'll figure it out together' and 'I'm curious' to signal a guest stance. Reflections are offered as hypotheses rather than statements of fact, and the session feels like a genuine dialogue rather than a clinician-led assessment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never tells Camila what to do, gives advice, or implies obligation — consistently positions her as the owner of her own change process. Questions like 'What made you think now was the right time?' and 'What do you think that something might be?' respect her agency and invite self-definition.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws out Camila's own motivations rather than providing external reasons, producing powerful change talk like 'I want to be the one who stops it.' Could be strengthened with scaling questions, more exploration of values, and more systematic targeting of specific change talk types (DARN-C).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong partnership stance by seeking Camila's perspective and avoiding advice or authority positioning. Could be elevated by more explicitly acknowledging Camila's own insight and expertise in her life (e.g., 'You've already figured out a lot of what needs to happen').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively picks up on and builds with Camila's change talk, particularly affirming 'I want to be the one who stops it' and exploring what brought her in. Missed opportunities to evoke deeper ability talk and commitment language, such as asking what that would look like on a specific Tuesday or using scaling for self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles the uncle parallel well with a double-sided reflection that honors the fear while redirecting to her agency, and never pushes abstinence when she says she doesn't want to stop altogether. The final sustain talk about forgetting her motivation during bad weeks was acknowledged but not actively softened or redirected toward problem-solving.
