# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout, validating Anya's preferences without criticism or moralizing. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and repetitive, with less individualized affirmation of her strengths, values, or self-efficacy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows adequate reflective listening and accurately tracks Anya's surface-level concerns and practical worries. Empathy remains mostly content-based and formulaic, missing deeper emotional or identity-level meanings behind her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays relevant to alcohol-related safety and practical goals, such as refusals, rides, and a backup text plan. Direction becomes too narrow and task-focused, with less exploration of values, ambivalence, or motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anya's choices clearly shape the final plan, including the ride, refusal style, and text wording. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist initially provides a list of expert-led options rather than consistently co-creating solutions with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Anya's choices, avoids pressure, and supports her preference to keep things low-key and optional. Some subtle steering toward action, such as asking if she is ready to send the text, limits a higher score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes some open and client-centered questions that invite Anya's own preferences and ideas. The overall approach remains more advice-providing than evocative, with limited effort to elicit deeper motivation, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Anya's input and often asks her to choose among options, allowing her input to influence the final plan. Partnership is limited by early advice-giving and a more expert-driven stance before fully eliciting her priorities and ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Captures some client change talk, such as wanting to stay safe, protect schoolwork, and reduce pressure to say yes. These statements are not consistently reinforced, expanded, or developed into stronger motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Anya's ambivalence and sustain talk well by normalizing reluctance and reducing pressure without arguing or confronting her. Could use more advanced MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to further transform ambivalence into change talk.
