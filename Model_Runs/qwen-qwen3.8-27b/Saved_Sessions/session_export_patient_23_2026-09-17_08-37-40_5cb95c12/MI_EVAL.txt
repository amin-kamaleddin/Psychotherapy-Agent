# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes ambivalence around rest, health, and drinking. Acceptance is strong but somewhat behavior-focused and occasionally steers toward the clinician's framing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections of the wine's function and the patient's language, showing good perspective-taking. Could go deeper by reflecting the loneliness, fear, or vulnerability behind being alone with her thoughts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on reducing evening wine and building a safer wind-down routine. Direction could be strengthened with an explicit early target and periodic summaries linking the plan back to the broader alcohol-reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Builds the plan closely around the patient's own ideas, such as measuring, a smaller bottle, and leaving the room. Collaboration could become more co-created by checking whether the direction is useful or inviting her to propose the next focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options without obvious pressure and respects her preference for a two-drink approach. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming her freedom to choose any option, including doing nothing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws out the patient's own goals, strategies, and body cues through effective open questions. Evocation could be deeper by exploring values, confidence, importance, and identity-related reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the patient as the expert on her own evenings and follows her lead in shaping the plan. Partnership could be made more explicit by inviting her to set priorities and steer the session more directly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits useful change talk, including wanting a clear head and concrete steps like measuring two drinks. It is limited in depth and frequency because it does not consistently amplify, deepen, or expand change talk with rulers, values, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating the need for a wind-down and reframing drinking as protective rather than resistant. It could be stronger with more deliberate double-sided reflections and strategic summaries to pivot sustain talk toward change talk.
