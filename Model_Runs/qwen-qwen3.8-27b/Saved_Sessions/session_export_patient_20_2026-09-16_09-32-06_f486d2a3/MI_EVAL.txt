# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while avoiding shaming or moralizing. Acceptance is generally supportive but somewhat task-focused and lacks deeper unconditional regard for the client’s ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture the client’s practical concerns, stress, and need for low-effort solutions. Empathy remains somewhat cognitive and problem-focused, with less exploration of deeper emotions such as shame, exhaustion, or fear of loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on stress-related drinking and develops a specific, testable behavioral step. The main limitation is excessive attention to practical logistics and a missed opportunity to respond to the client’s final concerns about Tuesday.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and adapts the plan based on his feedback, including dropping the timer and using the shower pause. The collaboration is strong, but the clinician still drives much of the agenda and follow-up structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, low pressure, and the idea that the attempt is not a test or failure. Some directives in planning and follow-up slightly reduce the fully client-driven quality of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses importance/confidence scales and asks what would make the step easier, eliciting some client-led language. However, the session moves quickly into advice and planning without sufficiently exploring the client’s own values, reasons, or discrepancies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and shares power by asking for preferences and adapting the plan to the client’s practical reality. The session is not fully client-led, as the clinician still introduces a specific coping plan and structures the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk by asking about importance, confidence, and small steps, and reinforces the client’s own statements. Change talk is not deepened or amplified consistently enough before shifting into concrete planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and acknowledges the short-term benefits of drinking. However, the responses do not consistently use double-sided reflections or discrepancy to gently redirect sustain talk toward the client’s values.
