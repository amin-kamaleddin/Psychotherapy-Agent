# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Rowan’s resistance and concerns. Acceptance could be stronger by showing more individualized unconditional positive regard rather than relying on formulaic phrases.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Rowan’s ambivalence, exhaustion, and fear that the device will feel like another job. Some reflections become repetitive or mildly leading, with less exploration of deeper emotions such as shame, loss of control, or being monitored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on the target behavior and moves from ambivalence to a concrete low-pressure plan. At times, the focus becomes too narrow on device features rather than linking Rowan’s preferences to the broader goal of sustainable health management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner throughout, using collaborative language and inviting joint exploration. The resulting plan is co-constructed rather than prescribed, showing no authoritarian or clinician-led dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a strong area, with consistent support for Rowan’s choice, no coercion or pressure, and clear validation of his boundary about putting the device back if it feels pressuring. The psychiatrist also normalizes imperfection, strongly reinforcing autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, client-centered questions to draw out Rowan’s own ideas and preferences. Evocation could be stronger by more explicitly connecting those ideas to deeper values, long-term health goals, and Rowan’s own capability for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Rowan as the expert on his own life and routine. Language such as “from your side” and “on your terms” clearly supports shared decision-making and power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps Rowan express useful change talk, such as plugging in the device when he sees it and keeping it simple. These statements could be more effectively amplified, consolidated, and connected to Rowan’s values to strengthen their motivational impact.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without direct challenge, and some of it is skillfully reframed into a workable boundary. There are moments where a double-sided reflection or gentle pivot toward values could better redirect resistance into change-focused exploration.
