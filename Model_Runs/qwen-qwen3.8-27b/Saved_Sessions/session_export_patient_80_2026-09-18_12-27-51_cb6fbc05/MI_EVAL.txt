# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout, with natural woven affirmations and no moments of disapproval. Occasionally reads as slightly scripted rather than deeply felt, preventing a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic attunement with reflections that capture the emotional and existential weight of the client's experience. Masterful use of the client's own metaphors and a final complex reflection that names the deeper fear beneath the surface concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors without drifting into unrelated topics. Lacks strategic momentum toward a concrete goal or next step, remaining exploratory without progressively narrowing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model-level collaboration with no power imbalance; the psychiatrist builds on the client's metaphors and follows his lead. The interaction feels like two people thinking together, with the psychiatrist contributing structure and the client contributing substance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support that never suggests what the client should do or implies cutting down is insufficient. All questions are oriented toward the client's own evaluation without pressuring toward commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation skills that successfully drew out rich, self-generated insight including the bridge metaphor and the realization about answering differently. Could push deeper at key moments to reach the fullest level of self-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates that expertise and wisdom reside within the client, using open-ended questions that invite his own interpretation. The session is genuinely shaped by the client's contributions and metaphors, with no imposed agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully creates conditions for change talk to emerge, successfully eliciting desire, ability, and recognition statements. Could have more explicitly amplified and reinforced change talk when it appeared for a higher score.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk respectfully without dismissing or arguing against it, validating concerns like fear of relapse. Could use more double-sided reflections and active reframing to strategically redirect sustain talk.
