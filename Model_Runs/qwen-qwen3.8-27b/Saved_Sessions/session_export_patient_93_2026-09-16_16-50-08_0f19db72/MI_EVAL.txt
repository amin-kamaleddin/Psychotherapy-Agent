# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Margaret’s experience without judgment and respects her independence. There is no pressure to quit alcohol and no disapproval of her continued connection to the pub or wine.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening and captures Margaret’s ambivalence well. Some reflections are somewhat formulaic, and deeper emotional themes such as fear, embarrassment, or loss of independence could have been explored further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol consumption, especially in the pub context. The conversation consistently moves toward concrete, manageable actions such as waiting before the first glass and choosing to leave the glass down.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Margaret to co-create solutions rather than prescribing a plan. Margaret’s own ideas, such as the one-minute pause and using conversation to stay present, strongly shape the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes that Margaret is in charge of her own decisions. Language and questions consistently support choice without coercion, advice, or pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn out from Margaret’s own values rather than supplied by the psychiatrist. The session successfully evokes statements such as “I decide it, not the wine,” reinforcing her internal sense of control.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Margaret as the expert on her own life and follows her lead throughout. The interaction clearly positions her as the decision-maker rather than a passive recipient of recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits, reflects, and strengthens Margaret’s own change talk. Her statements about safety, self-efficacy, and autonomy are repeatedly reinforced in a supportive way.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled well without argument or pushing abstinence. However, moments such as minimizing being “an alcoholic” and the final statement “I just like my wine” could have been explored more actively.
