# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, with clear affirmations of the client’s effort. Some warmth becomes evaluative or coaching rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are strong reflective statements that capture ambivalence and emotional themes. Deeper feelings such as shame, fear, grief, and rejection are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on reducing alcohol use, continuing the Stopping Violence program, rebuilding trust, and clarifying living arrangements. Movement from goal-setting to rehearsal is clear and strategically guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows Wiremu to choose the rehearsal direction and adjust language. Pre-supplied scripts and responses reduce the collaborative process at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly emphasized, and the client is allowed to modify the plan and wording. Exact scripts and specific language suggestions can limit client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions bring out important client motivation and change talk. The psychiatrist often supplies scripts, responses, and advice instead of drawing out more client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative, with client input shaping the role-play and plan. Providing ready-made lines and multiple responses can feel coach-like and reduce equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses strong change talk, and the psychiatrist asks some useful action-focused questions. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, summarized, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s concern about independence is acknowledged without argument. The response is limited because ambivalence is moved past too quickly, with limited double-sided reflection or exploration of the status quo.
