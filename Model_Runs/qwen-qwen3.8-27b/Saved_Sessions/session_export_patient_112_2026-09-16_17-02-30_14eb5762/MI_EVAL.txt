# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a respectful, validating tone that normalized ambivalence and reduced shame. Acceptance was strong but slightly structured, with fewer explicit affirmations of Rosa’s courage or strength.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Rosa’s health concerns, stress management, identity fears, and practical worries. Some reflections remained concise and could have explored deeper emotional meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking cessation and moved progressively from values to ambivalence to a concrete plan. There was little topic drift, and questions consistently advanced change toward a small actionable step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Rosa to generate and refine ideas rather than prescribing solutions, allowing her concerns and preferences to shape the plan. The collaborative stance was strong but could be made more explicit with shared decision-making language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rosa’s right to choose was consistently respected, with repeated emphasis that she did not have to decide today. The psychiatrist avoided pressure, imposed plans, or guilt, while still supporting exploration of options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively drew out Rosa’s own motivations, values, and possible solutions. Evocation could be stronger by more explicitly exploring importance, confidence, and readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided an expert-driven stance and treated Rosa as the best source of what might work. The partnership could be strengthened with more explicit acknowledgment of Rosa’s expertise and co-created decision-making language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections encouraged Rosa to express reasons for change, such as protecting her health and trying a small step. Change talk was not explicitly evoked across all domains, such as confidence, importance, or a future without smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing, including concerns about weight, stress, identity, and the ease of smoking. The psychiatrist gently redirected toward possibility but could more consistently minimize sustain talk and pivot toward change language.
