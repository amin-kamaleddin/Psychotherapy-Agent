# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance, validating Elias's effort and physical limitations. Some interpretive statements, such as "you're ready to try," slightly reduce the depth of acceptance by assuming his position.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening to accurately capture Elias's mixed feelings of meaning, pain, and fear of being a burden. Empathy is occasionally limited by a quick shift toward problem-solving before fully exploring his emotional concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on increasing physical activity and moves steadily toward a concrete, manageable action step. The psychiatrist effectively guides the conversation from general ambivalence to a specific, realistic plan without losing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively on practical details while inviting Elias's input and preferences. However, the clinician often introduces specific options and script choices before allowing Elias to generate them independently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and avoids overt pressure by using open questions and respecting Elias's preferences. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by moments that feel directive, such as offering a structured script and stating that Elias is ready to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling questions to draw out Elias's motivations and confidence, but the evocation is not consistently deep. The session moves too quickly toward logistical planning, limiting further exploration of Elias's values, meaning, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and invites Elias's input throughout the session. However, the provision of specific plans and scripts can shift the interaction toward clinician-led problem-solving rather than being fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through scaling and questions about safety and small steps. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently catch, amplify, or deepen Elias's change talk before moving into concrete planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk respectfully and offers helpful reframes, such as normalizing stopping for pain. The session remains somewhat anchored in limitations and contingencies, with less consistent use of reflections to pivot toward change.
