# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, validating language and avoids judgment, especially around pain and slowness. Acceptance could be more individualized and emotionally explicit about the burden of chronic pain.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence and reframe “slowing down” as staying engaged with the plan. Empathy would benefit from more curiosity and verification of deeper inferences, such as proving worth by enduring pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on rehab participation and pain management without drifting into irrelevant topics. The counselor guides the conversation strategically toward concrete, behaviorally relevant goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo actively contributes ideas, including the PT conversation and next-morning check. Collaboration could be stronger if the counselor relied more on co-creation and less on supplying scripts and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by using permission language and framing change as optional rather than required. Some immediate scaffolding of next steps slightly reduces the sense that Mateo is fully driving the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently draws out Mateo’s own reasons, values, and motivation rather than supplying external persuasion. This results in strong client-led language about staying engaged and protecting his ability to function.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Mateo as the expert on his body and repeatedly invites his perspective. Partnership could improve with more explicit power-sharing and less counselor-generated sequencing of ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and evocative prompts effectively elicit Mateo’s own reasons for change and confidence language. The counselor also amplifies change talk by reframing caution as protecting progress.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Mateo’s pain or limitations but reframes them in a non-judgmental, goal-consistent way. This helps convert sustain talk into practical self-regulation and continued participation.
