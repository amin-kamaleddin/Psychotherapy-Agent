# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirms the client's expertise and ambivalence. Slightly deeper warmth or acknowledgment of the emotional intensity could elevate this score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately tracks the client's fear, ambivalence, and need for privacy. Some reflections are accurate but could explore deeper emotional meaning and concerns about bodily autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on contraception after the pregnancy scare, moving from broad worry to a concrete, manageable next step. There is no drift, and each question supports the target behavior purposefully.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering choices, adapting to the client's stated needs, and proposing a low-pressure plan. Slightly more explicit co-creation of the agenda or next step would make the collaboration stronger.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated emphasis on the client's freedom to choose, decide privately, and avoid pressure. The client's statement that she appreciated not being pushed reflects effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively draw out the client's own values, preferences, and reasons for protecting herself. The evocation remains somewhat practical and could more deeply explore self-efficacy or confidence in future decision-making.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on her body, life, and preferences while inviting input on focus and format. The partnership could be stronger with more explicit power-sharing or co-constructed agenda summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's own reasons, values, and preferences, and the client does express change-relevant language. However, the counselor does not consistently deepen, amplify, or build stronger commitment through scaling or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client resistance or hesitation is handled well by avoiding argument or pressure and reframing toward a low-pressure, private exploration step. More active use of double-sided reflections could further integrate her "not ready" stance with small forward movement.
