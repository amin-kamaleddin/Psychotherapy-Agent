# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive while normalizing ambivalence. Slightly formulaic and not yet fully individualized in its expression of regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate and useful reflections, including a strong double-sided reflection of boredom, peer pull, and fear of reoffending. Less developed in naming deeper emotions such as shame, regret, and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing reoffending risk and managing boredom and peer pressure. Each step moves toward concrete, actionable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works with the client’s ideas to co-construct a practical relapse-prevention plan. Somewhat clinician-led in offering options and problem-solving prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice by asking questions and avoiding pressure or moralizing. Frequent solution menus reduce the sense of client-generated ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk and confidence but relies heavily on therapist-generated options and scripts. Needs more emphasis on the client’s own values, reasons, and identity-based motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites client input and builds on Lars’s chosen ideas throughout. Could more explicitly position the client as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reflects change-oriented language, including desire, ability, and commitment. Could deepen change talk with more values-based and DARN-C-style questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accepts sustain talk without argument and turns it into useful planning information. Does not consistently reframe the appeal of the status quo or deepen the ambivalence.
