# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoided judgment or coercion and respected Marisol’s autonomy. Some repeated risk advice created a slightly procedural or paternalistic tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but mostly cognitive and surface-level. The psychiatrist did not deeply explore the meaning, values, or feelings tied to the piercing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the piercing-related risks and practical monitoring. Direction was clear but remained centered on the clinician’s risk-reduction agenda rather than Marisol’s personally meaningful plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered choices, which showed some collaboration. However, the clinician supplied most of the agenda, terminology, and options, leaving Marisol largely reacting to clinician-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support was strong, with repeated affirmations that keeping the piercing was Marisol’s choice. Repeated detailed recommendations still created subtle pressure toward clinician-approved behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were occasional evocative questions, but the session quickly shifted to didactic explanation. The psychiatrist provided many reasons for risk reduction instead of helping Marisol articulate her own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Marisol’s preferences and offered options, showing some partnership. The interaction remained heavily clinician-driven, with expertise and agenda primarily controlled by the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marisol produced modest change-related statements, but the psychiatrist rarely expanded on them. There was limited use of scaling, reflective amplification, or values-based questions to strengthen her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Marisol’s sustain talk without arguing, which prevented escalation. However, repeated return to risk-reduction advice limited more consistent softening and redirection toward client-generated change motivation.
