# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout the session. Although consistent, the warmth was somewhat formulaic and did not fully convey deeper individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections to capture the client’s ambivalence and practical concerns. However, the reflections stayed somewhat surface-level and missed deeper emotional nuances such as the intensity of the client’s internal “noise” and fear of falling apart.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stayed focused on marijuana use and stress coping, and it ended with a concrete small step. Pacing was somewhat scattered, with shifts among harm reduction, safety reminders, and alternative strategies that reduced strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked with the client as an equal partner, using permission-seeking language and inviting the client’s input. The client’s preferences directly shaped the session, and the psychiatrist adapted rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforced the client’s right to choose, ownership, and low-pressure participation. Refusals of ratings, homework, and extra steps were respected without pushback or persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made some attempts to evoke client motivations by asking what the client enjoys and what concerns them, and by building on the music idea. The evocation remained superficial and did not deeply explore values, confidence, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invited client input and avoided an expert-driven stance, asking how to support the client and offering choices. The later structured harm-reduction content and planning felt somewhat clinician-led rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported the client’s own suggestion to try music before smoking and reinforced that it felt doable. However, there was limited amplification of change talk, with little exploration of ability, confidence, or deeper change motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk but did not actively soften or redirect it. The client’s minimization and justifications for use remained largely unchallenged and continued to dominate the conversation.
