# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent, warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, validating self-harm as a functional response without judgment or disapproval. Phrases like 'Both are you' and 'That's a really hard place to be' communicate acceptance without any conditional regard or implicit pressure to perform better.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist delivers highly accurate empathic understanding, consistently going beyond surface content to emotional and relational meaning. Masterful complex reflections such as 'when the one thing that works starts to feel like who you are' and naming the specific fear of hope followed by letdown demonstrate exceptional empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from understanding the experience through evoking a picture of change, without wandering. However, it could benefit from a tighter strategic arc toward a collaboratively defined next step rather than ending somewhat open-endedly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the session as a joint exploration using inclusive language and co-constructs meaning with Liora. The collaboration could be elevated by more explicit agenda negotiation and by more actively incorporating Liora's ideas into the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports autonomy through phrases like 'no pressure,' 'at whatever pace feels right,' and 'without it being a promise,' never imposing recommendations. It could be strengthened by more explicit affirmations of Liora's right to make her own choices in direct language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits motivation from within Liora through questions like 'What do you think that other version might look like?' and 'What do you make of that?' The evocation could be more systematic, for example by explicitly exploring confidence or using amplifying summaries of Liora's own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, partner-like stance with exploratory questions and avoids positioning as the expert. It could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language that reinforces Liora as the expert on her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and reinforces change talk, particularly the powerful move of noticing Liora described a specific picture while calling it a fantasy. The change talk could be maximally developed by pushing further on confidence and readiness language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates the function behind sustain talk without agreeing with hopeless conclusions, avoiding any adversarial dynamic. However, the final sustain talk statement ('I don't want to get excited about something that's probably not gonna happen') is reflected but not fully softened or pivoted toward change.
