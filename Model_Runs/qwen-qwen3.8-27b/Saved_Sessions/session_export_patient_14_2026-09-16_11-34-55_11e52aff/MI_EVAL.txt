# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, normalizing Andrei’s stress and time pressure. The warmth is somewhat procedural and could more explicitly acknowledge his broader frustration with the medical system.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially around Andrei’s need for practicality and dislike of extra obligations. The empathy remains mostly cognitive and task-focused, with less exploration of emotional or health-anxiety meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on weight, blood pressure, and a small actionable health behavior. Summaries help keep the plan anchored without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts well to Andrei’s feedback and narrows the plan to practical, client-preferred actions. Collaboration could be stronger if more options were co-created from Andrei’s ideas before being supplied.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported by asking preferences and avoiding forced commitments, including respecting Andrei’s rejection of reminders and a diary. Some mildly directive language remains, such as suggesting a “1-week experiment.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions, such as scaling importance and asking what has worked before. However, counselor-provided advice and structured options limit the depth of client-owned evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan is collaborative, but the psychiatrist still takes a relatively expert-driven role by offering multiple options and protocols. More explicit positioning of Andrei as the expert on his own schedule and routines would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Andrei produces relevant change talk, such as wanting control and concern about blood pressure, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen it. The conversation often moves quickly to practical options instead of elaborating client reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging resistance and reframing it toward smaller, realistic action. The initial offering of multiple steps and limited exploration of the deeper function of Andrei’s time pressure keep this from being a 5.
