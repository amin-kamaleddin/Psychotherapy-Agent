# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and avoids harsh labeling of the client. Some characterizing language and repeated insistence on a medical check slightly undermined acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic reflections were present but often brief and surface-level. Important emotional themes, such as social pressure and fear of being seen negatively, were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and DUI risk. However, the direction was somewhat rigid and driven by the clinician’s agenda rather than the client’s lead.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently acted as the primary planner by adding options and structuring the client’s steps. This limited true co-creation and made the power dynamic feel more expert-driven than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening invited client choice and some options were offered. However, repeated additions to the plan and repeated safety push weakened the sense of client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician provided more solutions than they elicited from the client. Rich change talk related to values, independence, and past sobriety was not sufficiently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a directive, menu-driven approach rather than a true partnership stance. The client’s pushback about “running a business” was not fully addressed by slowing down or renegotiating the pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered some change talk and developed concrete strategies. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, explore, or deepen this motivation to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided reinforcing the client’s denial or minimization. However, sustain talk was largely left neutral rather than skillfully reframed or explored.
