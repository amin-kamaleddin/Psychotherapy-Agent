# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while validating Erik's concerns. The warmth is somewhat procedural and lacks consistently explicit affirmations, which limits the rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures Erik's ambivalence and practical need for a simple plan. The empathy does not fully explore deeper emotional undercurrents, such as defensiveness or fear of being told to change.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol, diet, and diabetes management while narrowing to practical first steps. The guidance is incomplete because it does not clearly summarize or consolidate the emerging plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language, offers choices, and invites Erik's input throughout the session. The collaboration remains somewhat clinician-structured rather than fully client-led in agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directive advice and supports Erik's sense of choice by asking what would be realistic and doable. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming Erik's ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions, but they do not consistently elicit strong internal motivation from Erik. The session does not sufficiently explore values, confidence, future concerns, or stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Erik as the expert on his own life and invites his perspective on next steps. The partnership would be stronger if the psychiatrist more explicitly reduced Erik's reliance on the doctor's authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective efforts are made to evoke change talk, including willingness to skip a beer or pause the chips. The clinician does not consistently amplify or deepen Erik's internal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to sustain talk softly without arguing or moralizing. However, the session does not consistently pivot the conversation away from reasons for maintaining the status quo.
