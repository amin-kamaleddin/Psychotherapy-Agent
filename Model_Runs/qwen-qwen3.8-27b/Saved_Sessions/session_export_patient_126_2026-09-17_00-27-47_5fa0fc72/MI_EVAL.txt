# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard. There was no moralizing, pressure, or invalidation, even when the client minimized risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client’s ambivalence and family-related values. It could be stronger by more explicitly naming underlying fears, identity concerns, or defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a generally clear focus on alcohol use and related ambivalence, moving from concern to possible next steps. It would be improved by more clearly defining the target behavior and moving from conditional planning to a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked with the client to develop a reasonable, shared plan rather than imposing one. Collaboration was strong, though the clinician still structured much of the conversation before the client fully led it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client’s freedom of choice was consistently respected without pressure or control. The psychiatrist supported evidence-based decision-making while allowing the client to define acceptable risk.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited the client’s values, preferred evidence, and concrete ideas for change. Deeper internalized motivation could have been evoked through scaling or discrepancy questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s perspective was repeatedly invited, and the psychiatrist avoided imposing a fixed plan. Partnership could be stronger by more explicitly co-creating the agenda and naming the client as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively brought out client-generated reasons for change, including family protection and possible reductions in drinking. The change talk remained somewhat conditional, tied to future lab results rather than fully autonomous commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument, using balanced reflections to avoid confrontation. The psychiatrist could more actively redirect or reduce the weight of sustain talk when the client normalized drinking.
