# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates the client’s ambivalence and self-insight without judgment or moralizing. Responses normalize pressure, rest, and fluctuating motivation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses highly accurate and nuanced reflections that track the client’s emotional and practical experience. The reflections are specific, supportive, and grounded in the client’s own language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on building a low-pressure routine and moves toward a concrete next step. However, the target behavior of physical activity is not always explicitly anchored throughout the middle of the conversation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as an equal partner and co-creates the check-in, pause language, and next step. The final plan is client-generated rather than expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes the client’s freedom to choose, pause, and define success in her own terms. There is no pressure, persuasion, or implicit expectation to meet a performance standard.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Most questions draw out the client’s own solutions, values, and motivation rather than giving advice. The client generates the flexible routine, self-check-in, pause statement, and tiny next step.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert on her own life and experience. The session is collaborative, with the client’s contributions shaping the agenda and final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits practical change statements and client-generated solutions. It could be stronger by more explicitly amplifying the client’s DARN-C change talk, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through reframing concerns about pressure, guilt, and failure in autonomy-supportive ways. There is less opportunity for more active pivots or advanced double-sided reflections.
