# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and affirming throughout, reframing client's self-perceptions positively (e.g., 'not passivity, that's a kind of quiet clarity'). Never judges or expresses disapproval; normalizes avoidance before gently exploring it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that captures both content and underlying emotional significance, often extending the client's own metaphors to deepen self-understanding. Reflections are progressive rather than repetitive, each building on the previous.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Follows a coherent arc from values clarification through gap exploration to a small commitment, with each question building logically on the last. Could benefit from slightly more active summarization at key transitions and a brief closing summary of accomplishments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's metaphors ('the hum,' 'two rooms,' 'a door, a crack') become the shared therapeutic language, with the psychiatrist extending rather than redirecting. All decisions about focus, framing, and timing are left to the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly honors the client's timeline and right to self-determination at every point, from opening ('whatever's on your mind') to closing ('no pressure, no deadline'). Even the action step is framed entirely as the client's own discovery, not a suggestion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws out motivation and solutions from within the client through well-crafted open questions, resulting in nearly all therapeutic content being client-generated. Never provides reasons for change or suggests solutions directly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions herself as a partner rather than expert, with the client's contributions substantially shaping the session's direction and framework. Never tells the client what to do, when to do it, or how to feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk through open-ended questions that draw out the client's own solutions and commitments. Slightly constrained by the client's already-motivated maintenance stage, limiting opportunities for deeper evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates and normalizes sustain talk without dismissing or arguing against it, then gently explores underlying assumptions through reframing. Fewer opportunities to demonstrate advanced softening due to the client's low ambivalence.
