# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, normalizing Nadine’s exhaustion and self-doubt without blame. However, frequent shifts into clinical instructions make the acceptance feel more functional than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is present but often remains at a surface level, with accurate reflections followed quickly by advice. Deeper feelings, such as guilt, fear of failing as a mother, and emotional exhaustion, are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear, relevant target: helping Nadine manage infant care strain through a realistic break plan. There is some drift into safety screening and broader recommendations, but overall direction remains coherent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration, as the clinician adapts to Nadine’s preferences such as texting and using a softer script. Nevertheless, the interaction is largely clinician-directed, with the provider offering most of the structure, options, and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges choice by asking what feels doable and allowing Nadine to select breathing, a short break, and texting. However, many directive instructions and scripts reduce the sense that the change process is fully owned by the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is more driven by advice-giving and problem-solving than by drawing out Nadine’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change. Opportunities to explore what a break would mean for her relationship or sense of identity as a parent are missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and follows some of Nadine’s preferences, indicating a degree of partnership. However, the psychiatrist frequently takes the lead with advice and behavioral steps, limiting the impression that Nadine is the primary expert on her own needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nadine expresses some change-oriented language, but the clinician does not consistently elicit or expand on it. The session would benefit from questions that help her articulate why change matters to her and what being calmer would allow her to do.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is often reframed in a normalizing, nonjudgmental way, which reduces defensiveness. However, more advanced MI strategies are used only sparingly, and sustain talk is not consistently transformed into movement toward change.
