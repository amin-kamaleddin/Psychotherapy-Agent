# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirms the client without pressure. The warmth is somewhat professional and formulaic, with more counselor judgments than deep individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence, especially the value of wine and the pub for unwinding. Empathy remains mostly summary-level and misses some deeper emotional meanings, such as embarrassment, fear, or the significance of “just one.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use and moves consistently toward a small, achievable reduction plan. The work remains connected to the client’s concerns about the fall and her son’s worry without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively and incorporates the client’s choices into the plan. It remains somewhat therapist-guided because the counselor introduces much of the structure and options before the client generates them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by emphasizing choice, using permissive language, and respecting the client’s boundaries around monitoring. The client clearly owns the experiment, follow-up format, and plan details.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling, open questions, and the client’s own reasons to build motivation. Evocation could be deeper by eliciting more values, ability, and identity before moving quickly into planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s perspective and lets her shape key decisions, supporting a partner stance. The session is still more counselor-guided because the therapist provides much of the agenda and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits importance, confidence, and concrete change talk through scaling and a small experiment. Change talk could be further strengthened by amplifying the client’s values, abilities, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and respectfully redirects sustain talk toward a low-risk experiment. It could more consistently transform sustain talk into deeper change-relevant reflections and bridges to the client’s values.
