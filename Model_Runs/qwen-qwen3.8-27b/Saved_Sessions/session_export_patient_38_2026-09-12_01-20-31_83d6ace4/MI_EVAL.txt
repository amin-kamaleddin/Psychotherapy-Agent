# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the patient's ambivalence and emotional reactions. Some responses were slightly evaluative or formulaic rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected some of the patient's feelings, such as relief and overwhelm after the scare. However, empathy often remained surface-level and shifted quickly toward practical problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed generally focused on contraception and reducing the risk of another frightening near-miss. There was some drift into general anxiety management, but it remained clinically relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the patient to choose among options and incorporated her preferences into the final plan. However, much of the agenda and wording remained therapist-led, especially through detailed lists and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoided pressuring the patient to make a concrete contraceptive decision and used low-pressure language. Some prescriptive suggestions still made the interaction feel more guided than fully client-directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made limited efforts to evoke the patient's own motivations for change. The session relied more on practical options and advice than on exploring the patient's values, desires, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited patient input and avoided a purely directive stance. The balance shifted toward therapist-led guidance when multiple concrete options and scripts were offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offered some clear change talk about avoiding another pregnancy scare. The psychiatrist made minimal effort to amplify or expand this language, often responding with practical steps instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk without arguing or pressure, reducing defensiveness. However, there was limited use of strategic MI techniques to gently pivot resistance toward change talk.
