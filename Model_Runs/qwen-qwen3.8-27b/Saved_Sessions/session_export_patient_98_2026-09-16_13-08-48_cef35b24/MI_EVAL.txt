# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, patience, and nonjudgmental support, validating Mateo’s pain and caution. The warmth is occasionally procedural or plan-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflective listening about pain, stiffness, and frustration. Empathy is limited because deeper ambivalence, emotional meaning, and values are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on rehab progress, morning stiffness, and a concrete low-risk behavior change. Direction is somewhat rigid because the psychiatrist leans into detailed problem-solving and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering choices and adapting the plan to Mateo’s preferences and need for brevity. Collaboration is not fully realized because the psychiatrist generates most of the ideas, steps, and trial structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, pacing, and the right to stop, including permission to skip the walk and stop for sharp pain. Autonomy support is slightly limited by a fairly prescriptive plan and check-in structure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes an initial meaningful goal and some willingness to try, but then shifts quickly into suggestions and planning. Evocation is limited by fewer questions exploring Mateo’s personal reasons, values, confidence, and importance of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers choices, and avoids a purely directive stance. The collaboration remains limited because the psychiatrist primarily supplies the structure and content of the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some initial change talk and willingness to try small steps, especially around the kitchen-walk goal. Change talk is not consistently deepened or amplified with questions about importance, confidence, values, or future self.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or criticizing Mateo’s doubts and acknowledges the realism of his concerns. However, sustain talk is not often softened with double-sided reflections or redirection toward change and personal motivation.
