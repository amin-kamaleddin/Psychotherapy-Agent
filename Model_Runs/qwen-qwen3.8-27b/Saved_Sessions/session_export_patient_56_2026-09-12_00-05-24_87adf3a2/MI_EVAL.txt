# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the patient's concerns. The tone supported collaboration, though it did not reach the profound warmth of a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist understood the client's perspective but often reflected facts and logistics rather than deeper emotional concerns. There were missed opportunities to explore the patient's ambivalence and fear of performance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed strongly focused on aligning daily actions with the value of family happiness. It concluded with a clear, practical next step and showed little topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction was partially collaborative, but the psychiatrist supplied many strategies rather than co-constructing the plan. The power balance remained uneven despite the patient's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and respected the patient's decision to drop the scorecard. However, the frequent prescriptive scripts and homework made the approach somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful content about family, values, and autopilot routines, but mostly in a task-focused way. The patient's internal reasons for change and confidence were not fully evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance while often acting as the primary expert. The plan was shaped by clinician suggestions more than shared exploration of the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expressed clear goals, but the psychiatrist frequently moved toward concrete planning without amplifying or reinforcing that motivation. The client's own change language was not consistently developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled the client's resistance effectively by dropping the rating requirement rather than arguing. This redirection helped preserve momentum and reduce defensiveness.
