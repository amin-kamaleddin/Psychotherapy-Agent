# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Claudia's ambivalence without moralizing. The warmth is consistent but somewhat restrained, with few deeper statements of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist offers strong, accurate reflections of Claudia's perspective, especially the tension between wine as relaxation and a trigger for drinking. Some reflections remain more cognitive than emotional and do not fully explore affective undertones.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing wine use during Claudia's evening wind-down and moves coherently toward a concrete plan. There is no drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist invites Claudia's input and builds the plan around her ideas, making the session feel like joint problem-solving. Collaboration is strong but sometimes implicit rather than fully explicit or co-created in every moment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist supports choice, frames the plan as Claudia's experiment, and respects her ambivalence about specific drinking limits. Some language still subtly frames change as expected rather than fully emphasizing Claudia's freedom to modify or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively elicits Claudia's own motivations, ideas, and strategies through open questions rather than giving advice. The evocation is strong but could go deeper into values, identity, confidence, and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist generally treats Claudia as the expert on her own life and lets her input substantially shape the session. A few leading or identity-framing phrases subtly place the therapist in a guiding expert role rather than a fully neutral partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and affirming reflections draw out substantial client-generated change talk, practical plans, and commitment language. The focus is strong on next steps but could more explicitly elicit deeper desire, ability, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with Claudia's sustain talk and reflects the benefits of wine rather than correcting her. However, the session spends considerable time exploring those benefits and does not consistently pivot away from them with double-sided reflections or balancing language.
