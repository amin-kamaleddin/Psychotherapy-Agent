# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids labeling or condescension throughout. Acceptance is present but slightly more intellectual than emotionally warm, lacking explicit affirmation of Julian's worth as a person separate from his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong complex reflections that accurately capture compounded frustration and emotional toll (e.g., the wait-then-crisis loop). Could go deeper into underlying shame or self-disappointment at times, occasionally staying one level above the deepest affect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a generally clear focus on completing community service hours while following Julian's natural flow of concerns. The middle segment on the 'too late' pattern drifts slightly from specific logistics, though the psychiatrist pulls back to the concrete to stay grounded.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine collaboration with a genuinely equal power balance. Julian's hesitations, minimizations, and pattern-recognition all shape where the session goes, and the psychiatrist never dictates the agenda or corrects him.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Julian's ownership and freedom of choice without ever using directive language like 'you should' or 'you need to.' Every question is open-ended and every reflection returns agency to Julian, even validating his right to be skeptical.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never provides a single reason for change or appeals to external consequences; every piece of motivation is evoked from Julian himself. Reflections serve to amplify what Julian is already thinking rather than implant new thoughts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly names the power dynamic ('That's you figuring it out, not me handing it to you') and consistently positions Julian as the expert on his own experience. The psychiatrist never assumes the expert role or gives advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully isolates and amplifies change talk (e.g., reframing 'I don't know if I want to keep doing that' as authentic self-motivation). Could more explicitly elicit commitment language or readiness rulers to push the score to a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles minimization and skepticism without arguing or creating a righting reflex, gently reframing rather than dismissing (e.g., 'You said it's not a big deal—but the fact that you're turning it over says something different'). Sustain talk was relatively limited in this session, so the skill was demonstrated but not heavily tested.
