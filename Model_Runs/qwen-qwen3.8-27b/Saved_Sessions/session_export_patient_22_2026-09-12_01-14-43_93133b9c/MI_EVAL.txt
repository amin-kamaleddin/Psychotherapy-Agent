# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful and nonjudgmental, using affirming statements such as “that makes total sense.” However, acceptance felt somewhat formulaic and did not deeply validate Camila’s values, courage, or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Camila’s social pressure and anxiety, but empathy remained surface-level. Deeper feelings, such as fear of being a “buzzkill” and regret about wasting Friday, were not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of alcohol use in social situations. Direction was mostly effective, though it became more procedural and advice-based than MI-focused on motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaboration was present, including asking how Camila preferred to be supported and using her photographer idea. However, the psychiatrist remained the primary source of solutions and did not fully co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for preferences but then offered many specific strategies, scripts, and instructions. This directive approach reduced Camila’s sense of ownership over her choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** An initial open question was appropriate, but the session quickly shifted into advice-giving rather than evoking Camila’s own motivations, values, and hopes. More open-ended questions about her reasons for change were needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopted an expert-driven stance, frequently providing lists of tactics and scripts. A stronger partnership would require inviting Camila to lead more and positioning her as the expert in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Camila offered some change talk, such as considering a two-drink limit, using the photographer role, and texting Sarah. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen these statements to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk, such as fear of slipping and not wanting to quit completely, was often met with harm-reduction advice rather than gentle MI-style softening. Double-sided reflections and value-based redirection would have been more effective.
