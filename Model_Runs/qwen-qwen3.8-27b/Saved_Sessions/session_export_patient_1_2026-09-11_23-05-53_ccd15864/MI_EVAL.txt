# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, accepting Ahmed's refusal to quit. However, repeated checklist reminders and oral cancer screening emphasis can feel scripted and less person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are basic reflections and accurate tracking of Ahmed's perspective. Empathy remains surface-level, missing deeper themes such as autonomy, work pressure, and irritation at being corrected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cigar use and oral health, but the direction is inconsistent. It cycles among dental preparation, harm reduction, and reassurance without a clear change-focused goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and offers optional supports, showing partial collaboration. However, Ahmed's stated preferences do not consistently reshape the interaction or session content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Ahmed's choice and avoids directly demanding cessation. Autonomy support is weakened by prescriptive, repetitive instructions that can feel controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some open questions, but they are mostly informational rather than evocative. The psychiatrist provides more external information than strategic questions to draw out Ahmed's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership is shown through invitations to choose, but the stance is largely expert-driven. Ahmed's boundary against being lectured is not fully honored, limiting true collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk are rare and weak. The psychiatrist does not consistently use evocative techniques or amplify Ahmed's own reasons, ability, or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct argument but does not effectively soften Ahmed's minimization or sustain talk. Repeated risk information and checklists keep the focus on the problem rather than redirecting toward ambivalence.
