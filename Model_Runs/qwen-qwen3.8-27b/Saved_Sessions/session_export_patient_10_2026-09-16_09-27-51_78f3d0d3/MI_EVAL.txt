# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgmental support, and validation of Amina’s burden. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming Amina’s worth, resilience, and emotional experience beyond practical problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Amina’s situation and concerns accurately, such as childcare barriers, frustration, and fear of sounding like an excuse. Empathy could be deeper by exploring underlying feelings such as guilt, shame, disappointment, or fear of being seen as unreliable.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target issue: securing childcare so Amina can attend the interview and take a concrete step toward steady work. The counselor consistently guides the conversation toward actionable steps, timing, backup options, and follow-through.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Amina in joint planning, adapts to her ideas, and supports a collaborative problem-solving process. Collaboration could improve by inviting Amina to generate more of the language and plan before offering templates or structured options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking what Amina wants to focus on, what step she will try, and how confident she feels. Autonomy support could be strengthened with more explicit affirmations of Amina’s ownership and right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Amina’s motivation, confidence, and problem-solving ideas, leading to increased confidence scores and a concrete plan. Evocation could be deeper by drawing out more of Amina’s own values, reasons for change, and self-efficacy before providing scripts or advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and treats Amina as the expert on her life, inviting her input on focus, steps, and sequencing. The session is sometimes more guided than fully client-led because the counselor offers scripts, timing adjustments, and structured suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits strong change talk through importance, confidence, and next-step questions, with Amina generating much of the plan. Change talk could be further amplified by summarizing Amina’s strengths, motivation, and capability rather than moving quickly into practical directives.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully validates barriers without arguing or reinforcing resistance, such as concerns about overburdening her sister or sounding like an excuse. Softening could be more explicitly MI-based by using double-sided reflections or reframing the ambivalence around both the barrier and the desire for the job.
