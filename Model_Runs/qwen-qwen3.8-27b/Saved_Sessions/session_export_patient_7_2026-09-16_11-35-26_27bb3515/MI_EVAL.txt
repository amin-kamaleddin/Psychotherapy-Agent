# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, validating stance and appropriately apologizes after an intrusive safety question. Acceptance is slightly reduced by repeated procedural reminders and new plans that can feel mildly pressuring.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates solid reflective listening and accurately captures Amina's exhaustion and need for quiet. Empathy remains mainly at the content level and does not fully explore the emotional texture beneath her minimal statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on household conflict and Amina's need for space, returning to the target after side moves. Direction is modest because the work remains exploratory and ends without a clear collaboratively identified change focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses choice language and honors Amina's final preference to simply listen. The session remains largely clinician-directed, with repeated menus and prescriptive options rather than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently affirms Amina's choice, avoids pushing the caseworker, and acknowledges her right to say no. Autonomy support is limited by early and repeated offers of concrete suggestions before the client is ready.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician makes several evocative attempts by inviting Amina to describe what would feel helpful or different. Evocation is often interrupted by suggestions and does not deeply access values, motivation, or importance of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses partnership language and respects Amina's choice at the end of the session. Partnership is limited because the clinician repeatedly sets the agenda and offers multiple-choice prompts rather than inviting the client to lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks useful questions and notes Amina's desire for space, but does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen that change talk. There is little elicitation of confidence, ability, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to resistance respectfully and avoids arguing with Amina's statements. The skill is limited because the clinician does not actively use double-sided reflections or subtle pivots to resolve ambivalence.
