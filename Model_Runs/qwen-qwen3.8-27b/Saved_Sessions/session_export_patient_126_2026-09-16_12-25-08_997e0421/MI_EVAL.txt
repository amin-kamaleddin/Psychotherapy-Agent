# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect and avoids judging or criticizing Thomas for minimizing alcohol use. Acceptance is strong but remains somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized or unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable efforts to understand Thomas's perspective and ambivalence. Empathy is often surface-level and misses deeper feelings such as irritation, defensiveness, or pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use and moves toward a concrete, behaviorally specific plan. Direction is somewhat limited by an overemphasis on logistics rather than deeper motivation, and the smoking concern is not addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Thomas and allows his choices to shape the session. Collaboration is uneven because the clinician often supplies menus, scripts, and options, which keeps the power balance less equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Thomas's autonomy by inviting his choices, respecting rejections, and avoiding pressure. Autonomy support is slightly limited by continued structured suggestions that can subtly steer the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions and the confidence ruler to draw out some change talk. Evocation is moderate because the clinician does not consistently deepen the exploration of Thomas's values, confidence, or internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Thomas's input and allows him to make several key decisions. Partnership is uneven because the clinician frequently provides plan components and options, maintaining a more expert-guiding role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk through open questions and reflections. The session could strengthen change talk by more consistently amplifying, exploring, and building on Thomas's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Thomas's minimization and acknowledges his framing without escalation. Softening sustain talk is moderate because the clinician mostly accepts it rather than consistently reframing it toward change while preserving autonomy.
