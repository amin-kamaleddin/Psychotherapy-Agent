# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates the patient's concerns and parenting values in a respectful, nonjudgmental way. The score is not higher because the acceptance language becomes somewhat repetitive and could be more personalized to her emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of her history, risk concerns, and desire for balanced information. Deeper emotional themes such as guilt, fear of making a mistake, or internal conflict were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerges, especially around leaning toward the shot if local risk is high. However, the psychiatrist does not actively develop, amplify, or summarize this change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is respected rather than challenged or argued against. The psychiatrist does not consistently soften or strategically redirect it toward change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as the expert on her family and child, and her concerns clearly shape the session. However, the psychiatrist still maintains much of the question structure and agenda, limiting fuller co-creation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the MMR/measles decision and narrows effectively toward the daughter's individual risk. Progress is limited by a lack of movement toward a concrete next step or shared plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the patient's autonomy without pressuring, persuading, or implying one choice is morally superior. This is a strong demonstration of autonomy support throughout the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint problem-solving language and builds on the patient's own ideas. The interaction is collaborative but remains largely counselor-question-led rather than fully co-constructed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes the patient's own values, concerns, and decision criteria rather than giving advice. The evocation is less strong because it does not consistently elicit deeper change talk, confidence, or commitment.
