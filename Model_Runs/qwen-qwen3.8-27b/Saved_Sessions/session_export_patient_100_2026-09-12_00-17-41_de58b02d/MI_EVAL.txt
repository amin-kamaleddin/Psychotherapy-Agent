# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, avoiding blame or shame. The tone is warm but occasionally scripted, and full unconditional acceptance of the client’s ambivalence is not consistently shown.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic statements are present but mostly stay at the surface or practical level. The client’s underlying feelings of pressure, shame, fear of being labeled, and ambivalence are not fully reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays connected to drinking and school performance, but focus is diluted by repeated safety checks and broad coping suggestions. Momentum toward a single negotiated target is weakened by frequent topic shifts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and the final plan reflects the client’s preferences. However, much of the session remains counselor-led and expert-driven rather than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy-supportive language appears, especially when the client resists tracking or role-play. The early part of the session is still quite directive, making autonomy support uneven overall.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open-ended questions elicit client statements and a small plan, but the counselor frequently offers advice and strategies instead of evoking the client’s own motivations. Important client statements about barriers and confidence are not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership language is used and the client’s preferences are incorporated into the final plan. The session is still more expert-driven than truly equal, limiting consistent partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses some change talk and agrees to a small step, such as finishing homework and swapping a drink for soda. The counselor does not deeply cultivate importance, confidence, values, or desired outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reduces pressure when the client feels overwhelmed and avoids direct argument. However, client sustain talk and ambivalence are not consistently reflected or redirected toward change language.
