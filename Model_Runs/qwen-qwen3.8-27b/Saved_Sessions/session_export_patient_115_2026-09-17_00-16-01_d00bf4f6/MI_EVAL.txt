# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent respect, nonjudgment, and validation of the client's difficult work and symptom burden. It falls short of a 5 because acceptance feels somewhat structured and tied to coping efforts rather than unconditional regard regardless of choices or setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, capturing practical and motivational aspects of the client's experience. It does not fully reach the emotional core, such as the fear of losing control while driving, preventing a top score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, consistent focus on asthma control, inhaler use, smoking reduction, and workday-specific strategies. The progression is logical, purposeful, and consistently guided toward actionable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds effectively on the client's ideas and treats planning as joint problem-solving. It is not a 5 because the counselor still drives much of the structure and sequence, limiting fully equal client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and repeatedly invites the client to define realistic options, strongly supporting choice and lived expertise. It could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client's ultimate freedom to choose differently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes the client's own practical plans and motivations through open questions. Evocation is less deep in tapping values, identity, or longer-term meaning, so it does not reach a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as an expert on his life and driving patterns, and his input shapes the plan. Partnership is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still leads the session structure and prioritization rather than fully co-creating every step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful change talk, such as reducing cigarettes, earlier inhaler use, and concrete cues. It falls short of a 5 because change talk is not consistently deepened or amplified through values, confidence ratings, or stronger reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without confrontation, and partial progress is validated to keep the conversation change-compatible. It is not a 5 because ambivalence is not consistently explored, especially around fear of failure and ongoing drinking.
