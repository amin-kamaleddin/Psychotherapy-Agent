# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, with genuine affirmations that go beyond formulaic praise. The opening tone of safety and willingness to understand from Rosa's perspective is maintained from start to finish.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional reflective listening that consistently captures both content and emotional meaning. Reflections like 'that pause is really precious to you' show deep perspective-taking into Rosa's inner experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation and follows a logical progression from ambivalence to insight about the pause and guilt pattern. However, the session ends at a point of deeper understanding without actively building momentum toward a concrete next step or decisional balance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration where Rosa's ideas (the permission concept, guilt pattern, walk around the block) directly shape the session's trajectory. The psychiatrist uses inclusive exploratory language and treats Rosa as the expert on her own life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Rosa's autonomy with language like 'I'm not here to push you toward anything' and 'you know your own rhythms better than anyone.' Never prescribes a plan or pressures Rosa to decide; the weight of choice is acknowledged without pushing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes Rosa's own motivations through questions like 'what is it doing for you that feels so hard to let go of?' and 'what actually makes them feel like yours?' Could have made a stronger move to evoke Rosa's ability or confidence language before the session concludes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise about Rosa's life resides within her, using open questions to let Rosa define what matters. Rosa's contributions substantially shape the session's direction, and the psychiatrist follows her lead rather than a pre-planned agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk (e.g., 'I know I've got to stop, honestly') and helps Rosa articulate her own reasons for wanting change. The session could have more explicitly amplified Rosa's emerging insight about permission or asked a direct evocative question to elicit stronger ability or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by reflecting overwhelm without arguing or dismissing any of Rosa's concerns. Could have used more explicit double-sided reflections to simultaneously validate sustain talk while expanding toward change, particularly around the 'I can't imagine life without smoking' statement.
