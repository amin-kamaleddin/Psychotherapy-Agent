# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a nonjudgmental stance through affirmations like 'there's no judgment in this room' and normalizing self-care efforts. Affirmations are warm but occasionally feel scripted rather than deeply personal, and there is limited explicit normalization of Matteo's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with accurate captures of Matteo's frustration and the social nature of his drinking ('a social thing, not just a willpower thing'). However, reflections tend to stay at the surface level, missing deeper emotional undercurrents like social identity fears and the powerful insight about automaticity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear strategic arc toward change, with no summary of themes, collaboratively identified focus, or concrete next step. The conversation remains exploratory and ends without a clear direction or commitment, despite the ankle-to-alcohol transition being handled naturally.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language ('Let's take a look,' 'we can figure out') and consistently invites Matteo's perspective through open questions. Collaboration is somewhat uneven, being stronger in the ankle portion and slightly more guided in the alcohol portion rather than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never directs or prescribes, consistently framing choices as Matteo's own and affirming his expertise in his own social world. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Matteo's right to make any choice and more clearly separating the psychiatrist's role from decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions that elicit some self-generated insights (middle-ground strategy, 'doc said no' line), but does not systematically draw out values or long-term goals. The most significant missed opportunity is Matteo's final insight about automaticity, which is not engaged with before the session ends.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear collaborative stance is maintained throughout with consistent use of open questions and invitations for Matteo's input. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing, such as asking Matteo to set the agenda or more consistently acknowledging his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes several instances of change talk (practicality, avoiding re-injury, pacing strategies) but does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or deepen these moments with follow-up questions. Opportunities to build Matteo's commitment language (e.g., 'How confident do you feel?') are missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled non-confrontationally but with limited active redirection, and key moments like 'that's not me' and 'I'm not an alcoholic' are not directly engaged. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections, which would be the gold standard for validating sustain talk while gently opening space for change.
