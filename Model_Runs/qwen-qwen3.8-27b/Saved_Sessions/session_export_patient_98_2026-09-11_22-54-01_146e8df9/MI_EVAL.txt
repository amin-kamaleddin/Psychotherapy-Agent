# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental. Warmth is sometimes generic, and approving comments imply a preferred course of action.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledge frustration and exhaustion but remain largely surface-level. The client's deeper emotional ambivalence is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on pain, rehab, and contacting the care team. It lacks tighter alignment with a specific behavioral change target such as rehab engagement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and joint planning, but the psychiatrist steers the session with recommendations. The client's priorities do not fully shape the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects the client's preference for brevity. Evaluative approvals and frequent instructions reduce the sense of client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist gives many external reasons and practical solutions. There is limited effort to elicit the client's own values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but the session becomes expert-driven. The psychiatrist provides detailed instructions and drafts messages, limiting shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is little effort to explore the client's own motivation, goals, or confidence. Client change talk is present but not amplified or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not strategically redirected toward change. The focus remains on barriers and problem-solving rather than agency or possible benefits.
