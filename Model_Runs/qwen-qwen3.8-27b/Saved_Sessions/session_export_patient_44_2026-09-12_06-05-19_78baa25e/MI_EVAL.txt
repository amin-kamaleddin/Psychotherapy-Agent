# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, normalizing, and generally nonjudgmental. Acceptance is more tied to progress and task completion than to unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are solid reflections of Erik’s practical concerns and cognitive concerns. However, the psychiatrist often stays at a surface level and misses deeper emotions such as shame, fear, and discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on a specific, measurable behavior. The psychiatrist effectively guides Erik from goal clarification to a concrete, step-by-step plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently builds on Erik’s own ideas and invites him to choose components of the plan. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still directs the structure and supplies much of the strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik is given choices and is asked to commit, but the session feels more guided than fully autonomous. The psychiatrist often prescribes specific steps without consistently emphasizing Erik’s freedom to revise or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurs when Erik is asked about past routines, confidence, and his preferred prompt. However, the psychiatrist often supplies the rationale and solutions before fully eliciting them from Erik.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Erik’s input and offers options, which supports some collaboration. Nevertheless, the approach is still quite expert-led and clinician-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits important change talk around importance, confidence, and commitment. It could be deeper by exploring Erik’s values and expanding his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is often addressed through normalization and planning. It is softened, but usually in a more cognitive, advice-driven way rather than through reflective, MI-consistent responses.
