# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard without judgment or disapproval. Responses affirm the client’s competence, effort, and values while respecting her ambivalence about drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client’s ambivalence, stress, need for control, and fear of failing as a mother. It is slightly less than full because the empathy remains somewhat structured and does not fully explore underlying shame or self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on evening alcohol use and the transition from work and school to parenting. The discussion moves clearly toward a concrete, client-led plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner, reflecting the client’s ideas and inviting her to choose wording and next steps. The support plan emerges collaboratively from the client’s own statements.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasizes the client’s right to choose and her ownership of the process. There is no pressure to commit to abstinence or accept monitoring; support is framed as a client-controlled tool.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client’s own motivations, boundaries, and strategies, allowing her language to drive the session. It could be stronger by drawing out deeper intrinsic values, confidence, or future-oriented reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on her own life and avoids imposing solutions. The client’s ideas substantially shape the direction, content, and plan of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits change talk about reducing drinks, setting boundaries, and using a backup plan. It could be stronger by more actively amplifying and consolidating the client’s reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without arguing or challenging the client’s concerns about quiet, control, and possible relapse. It could improve by more actively reframing barriers and pivoting toward change intentions.
