# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and non-judgmental, with multiple affirmations of the patient’s effort and goals. Acceptance was generally warm but somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured the patient’s complex feelings of fear, frustration, and responsibility. Empathic depth decreased later as the session shifted toward behavioral advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on alcohol refusal and the patient’s soccer/scholarship goals. The psychiatrist successfully guided the conversation from the incident to practical application and role-play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although input was requested, the psychiatrist largely supplied the ideas and plan rather than co-creating it with the patient. The power balance was uneven, with the psychiatrist doing much of the cognitive work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Providing specific scripts, rules, and tactics reduced the patient’s sense of choice and ownership. The approach implied the patient could not generate effective strategies independently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked open questions to elicit motivations and strategies, but often answered them immediately with expert advice. This limited the patient’s opportunity to develop their own change talk and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently assumed an expert/coach role by driving the agenda and supplying specific tactics. This undermined a true collaborative MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient generated useful change talk related to his goals, but the psychiatrist often interrupted with advice instead of amplifying or deepening it. This limited the patient’s ownership of motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk such as peer pressure was acknowledged without argument. However, the psychiatrist tended to bypass ambivalence with problem-solving rather than using MI techniques to soften it.
