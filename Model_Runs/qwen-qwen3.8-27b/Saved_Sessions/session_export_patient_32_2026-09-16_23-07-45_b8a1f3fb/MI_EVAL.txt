# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental while accepting Derrick’s framing without arguing. It is not a 5 because some statements feel slightly interpretive and there is less explicit normalization of ambivalence or difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong reflections of Derrick’s practical concerns, emotional burden, and own language. It would be stronger with deeper exploration of underlying feelings, values, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on avoiding a repeat DUI and developing a practical exit strategy. The conversation stays relevant without drifting or becoming overly directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Derrick’s own ideas, such as the envelope, short exit phrase, and pre-night cue, to create a coherent plan. The interaction is co-created rather than didactic or expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Derrick to decide, emphasizes his agency, and avoids pressure or moralizing. The plan remains framed as something Derrick chooses rather than something being imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out Derrick’s own reasons for change, such as sleep, work, and avoiding legal consequences. More explicit evocation of confidence, readiness, and deeper values would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports a collaborative stance and lets Derrick’s ideas shape the session. Occasional interpretive statements place the psychiatrist slightly ahead, reducing the sense of shared direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps Derrick articulate benefits, plans, and a tentative commitment, including “I can try that.” It could be stronger by deepening the commitment and using scaling or readiness questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or correcting Derrick’s minimizing and sustain talk, keeping the focus on safety and planning. More active double-sided reflections would help work with that sustain talk more fully.
