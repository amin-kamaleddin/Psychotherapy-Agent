# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard without judgment or condescension. Affirmations are specific and tailored to what Timur just expressed, such as validating both the temptation and the desire for change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex, accurate, and go beyond simple restating to capture unspoken emotional meaning. The psychiatrist skillfully identifies cognitive shifts and tracks emotional tone throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a coherent focus with progressive deepening from surface-level comparisons to deeper identity-level concerns. However, it could have benefited from a clearer endpoint or summary tying the exploration back to concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses 'we' framing, offers reflections as hypotheses rather than facts, and invites Timur to confirm or correct. The session's direction is genuinely shaped by Timur's contributions, including following unexpected leads.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently removes pressure to decide, reinforces that the timeline is Timur's, and respects his 'not yet' stance. No attempt is made to push toward a specific course of action or decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes motivation from within Timur rather than providing reasons for change, using selective attention, gentle hypotheses, and reflecting back the client's own words. Timur arrives at insights that are entirely his own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Timur as the expert on his own life through open-ended questions and never lectures or assumes. The conversation moves organically based on Timur's contributions, with each shift driven by his words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully identifies and explores change talk moments as they emerge, such as the 'mine, not stolen, not shady' statement. Could have more systematically elicited change talk through scaling questions or deeper exploration of the porch/tea imagery.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled by validating rather than arguing against, while opening space for the other side. Could have been more strategic with double-sided reflections on moments like 'I'm just talking myself out of it again.'
