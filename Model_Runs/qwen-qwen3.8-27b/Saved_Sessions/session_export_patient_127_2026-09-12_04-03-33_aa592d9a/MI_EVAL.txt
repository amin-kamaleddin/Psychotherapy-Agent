# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the patient's 22 days and ambivalence. The acceptance is strong but can feel somewhat formulaic when quickly moving to problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's torn feelings between staying clean and needing money. They are helpful but often remain surface-level rather than exploring deeper shame, identity, or self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of taking a concrete employment step while protecting abstinence. It would be stronger if the action plan were more explicitly tied to the patient's broader values and identity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds a plan with patient input but provides most of the cognitive work, including options, scripts, and strategies. More eliciting of the patient's own ideas would make the collaboration more equal and MI-consistent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonpressuring language, offers optional scripts, and respects the patient's preference for a flexible check-in. The structured provision of content and plan still somewhat limits the patient's sense of full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions and elicits a practical plan, but much of the session is content delivery rather than eliciting. Deeper evocation of the patient's values, meanings, and internal sources of motivation is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites input, but the session is driven by a pre-structured agenda. The patient selects from the psychiatrist's options rather than more fully co-creating the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces the 22-day progress and helps the patient plan a concrete action step. However, change talk is mostly limited to the immediate step rather than deeper personal values and identity-level motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with self-deprecating statements but does not strategically soften them with double-sided reflections. Sustain talk is often bypassed by moving quickly into scripting and planning.
