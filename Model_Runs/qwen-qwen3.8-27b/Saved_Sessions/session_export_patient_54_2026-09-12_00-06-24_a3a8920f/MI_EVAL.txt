# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, acknowledging frustration and validating Isabella’s intentional step. However, acceptance becomes less individualized when the counselor repeatedly shifts into directive medical guidance and planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some attempts to reflect frustration, but the listening remains shallow. The counselor does not deeply reflect Isabella’s core ambivalence between family tradition/independence and concern for her baby’s health.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of alcohol use during pregnancy and practical next steps. However, the direction is sometimes overly directive and task-focused rather than fully guided by the client’s priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and allows Isabella to choose parts of the plan, showing partial collaboration. Nonetheless, the counselor supplies much of the content and continues adding tools and specific instructions, keeping the session expert-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive language is present, such as asking permission and offering options. The prescriptive advice, supplied scripts, and instruction to “use your line exactly” undermine true autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few initial open questions, but then relies heavily on providing external medical reasons and planning tools. There is limited effort to draw out Isabella’s own values, reasons for change, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are collaborative phrases and offered choices, but the session is largely clinician-driven. The counselor supplies most of the plan, reducing the sense of an equal partnership in decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Isabella generates much of the change talk herself, and the psychiatrist affirms it at times. The counselor, however, does not consistently evoke, amplify, or build upon the client’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly argue with Isabella and responds to her request for facts. However, sustain talk is not fully softened with MI-consistent reflections, double-sided reflections, or autonomy-supportive reframing.
