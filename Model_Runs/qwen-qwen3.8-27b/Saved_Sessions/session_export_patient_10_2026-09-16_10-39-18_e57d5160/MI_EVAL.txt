# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental language and validates Amina’s caregiving pressures. Acceptance is strong but occasionally tied to “brave” behavior rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Amina’s frustration and fear, but often shifts quickly into planning. Deeper emotional concerns about burdening her sister and fear of rejection are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the interview and childcare barrier with clear, logical momentum. Direction is partly directive, but it effectively maintains behavioral focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina makes meaningful choices, such as using text and removing payment language, showing active involvement. However, the psychiatrist supplies many scripts and plans, making the collaboration feel counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites Amina to choose and respects her decisions, including sending the text immediately. Autonomy support is solid but could be strengthened by more emphasis on her own expertise.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some motivation and readiness by asking what Amina wants and which steps feel doable. However, much of the session is driven by practical suggestions rather than drawing out Amina’s deeper values and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and allows Amina to shape key decisions, such as the text message and interview answer. The role still leans toward expert consultant, limiting a fully partnership-based stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina expresses clear change talk, and the psychiatrist supports it with affirmations and questions. Change talk is not consistently amplified, deepened, or connected to her values and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges barriers without arguing, normalizing Amina’s fear of asking for help. Sustain talk is softened but not consistently resolved using more MI-based reflective strategies.
