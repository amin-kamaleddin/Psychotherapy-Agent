# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental, validating, and respectful, normalizing Aisha’s feelings without blame or shame. Slightly limited because it occasionally moves quickly into planning before fully validating body-image distress and fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate but mostly surface-level reflections of Aisha’s exhaustion, self-consciousness, and fear of failure. More complex emotional reflection of her body-image distress, shame, and social withdrawal is needed before shifting to action.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, concrete focus on initiating exercise through a specific and actionable plan. The direction is strong but somewhat narrow, not fully integrating her broader goals around body image, fatigue, confidence, and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha contributes to the final plan by choosing calls, timing, a phone vibration, and a follow-up focus on sleep/caffeine. However, the psychiatrist drives much of the structure and content, making the collaboration only partial.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and includes choices, which supports autonomy somewhat. The language remains directive, and Aisha’s statement that she is doing it because the psychiatrist asked suggests autonomy support is not fully effective.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers reasons for change rather than evoking Aisha’s own values, hopes, and confidence. More questions about what exercise would mean to her, what has worked before, and how important this is would have strengthened evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Aisha’s input at several points, showing some collaborative stance. Much of the session remains expert-driven, with the psychiatrist prescribing specific behaviors, timing, and tracking methods rather than fully partnering.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aisha offers clear change talk, but the psychiatrist does not systematically expand or strengthen it. The session moves quickly into behavioral planning instead of eliciting her reasons, importance, confidence, and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges doubts and uses some normalizing, non-judgmental language around slipping. However, Aisha’s sustain talk remains largely unresolved because MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or scaling are not used.
