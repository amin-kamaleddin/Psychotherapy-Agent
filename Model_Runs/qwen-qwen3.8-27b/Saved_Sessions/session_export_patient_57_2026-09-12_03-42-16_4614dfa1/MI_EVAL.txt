# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and frequently normalizes Jamal's feelings. However, the acceptance is somewhat generic and often paired with problem-solving rather than deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate and helps organize Jamal's ambivalence about drinking and belonging. Yet the reflections remain surface-level and repetitive, missing opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of alcohol use in team settings and the practical challenge of peer pressure. The direction is clear but becomes somewhat repetitive and tactical, cycling through similar options without moving toward deeper change exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, as the psychiatrist asks for input and presents choices rather than commands. However, the psychiatrist frequently supplies lists of strategies, making the interaction more helper-and-advised than truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by rejecting a rule-based approach and repeatedly asking Jamal's preference. The repeated provision of practical options can subtly steer Jamal toward a behavior plan rather than full ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with the dominant process being the psychiatrist offering options and practical solutions. There is insufficient effort to elicit Jamal's own motivations, values, confidence, or vision for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids laying down rules, creating a collaborative tone. Partnership is uneven, however, because the psychiatrist repeatedly becomes the problem-solver and leads the structure and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk are rare and weak, with little amplification of Jamal's own reasons for change. Important change talk is not consistently reflected, affirmed, or connected to values or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal's sustain talk about belonging and avoiding attention is acknowledged but not effectively softened or reframed. The conversation remains focused on social management and blending in, leaving the underlying ambivalence unresolved.
