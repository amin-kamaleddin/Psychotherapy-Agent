# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, with validating statements such as 'you’re not a kid.' However, repeated cautionary and corrective language about risk and legality makes the stance less fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is one strong early reflection and some recognition of embarrassment, but much of the empathy remains generic or surface-level. The counselor does not fully explore the deeper emotional conflict around fitting in, shame, and peer acceptance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on the target behavior of alcohol use in the team context, with little drift. However, direction is more compliance- and risk-management-oriented than MI-style guidance toward the client’s own values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor often leads with scripts, tactics, and safety steps rather than allowing the client’s ideas to shape the plan. Some collaborative moments occur, but the interaction remains largely expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Choice is occasionally acknowledged, but frequent directive advice and prescriptive limits undermine autonomy. The counselor adds structured controls despite the client’s pushback, which may increase resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor starts with open-ended questions but quickly moves into advice-giving rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations and values. This limits the client’s opportunity to generate intrinsic motivation and ownership of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language, but the overall stance is expert-driven and corrective rather than truly collaborative. The client is often positioned as being guided or corrected, especially around safety measures.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, but the counselor does not consistently evoke, amplify, or build on it. Little use of DARN/CRAFT-style questions limits development of intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses substantial sustain talk, and the counselor acknowledges some of it, but often responds with alternative tactics or safety warnings. This can entrench sustain talk rather than gently redirecting it toward change.
