# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, validating the client’s burden and small progress. Acceptance is slightly weakened by agenda-driven advice and repeated framing of the jacket trial as protecting her son.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers useful reflections of ambivalence, burden, and the client’s need for a quiet pause. Empathy remains mainly cognitive and does not deeply explore underlying guilt, fear, or the emotional meaning of the quiet house.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking after work and its impact on her son, narrowing to a concrete low-effort jacket trial. It does not fully develop broader motivation to reduce or quit smoking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client meaningfully shapes the plan through choices about timing, trigger, effort, and limits. Collaboration is weakened by counselor-supplied solution lists and a tendency toward guided selection rather than equal partner problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice, reversibility, and no pressure, respecting the client’s preferences and boundaries. Some subtle pressure remains in the repeated advice and emphasis on protecting her son.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open questions and a readiness ruler are used, but the psychiatrist often fills the space with practical advice. There is limited effort to draw out the client’s own reasons, values, confidence, or desired change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative questions invite client input and allow her to select plan details. Power sharing is limited by frequent expert-driven recommendations and practical solution lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, especially around protecting her son and trying a small test. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand this change talk, often shifting to advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directly arguing with sustain talk but often bypasses it by moving to harm-reduction suggestions. There is little active softening, double-sided reflection, or redirection toward values and change.
