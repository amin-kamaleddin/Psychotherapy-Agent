# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with frequent normalizing statements. The warmth is helpful but can feel somewhat formulaic and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate and acknowledges some cognitive and somatic distress. However, reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper fears, shame, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on exam preparation and reducing avoidance. Direction is strong, though it becomes somewhat narrow and task-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative invitations such as making a checklist together and offering choices. However, most of the structure and plan are clinician-generated rather than jointly developed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges choice and reduces pressure with supportive statements. Autonomy is undermined by a directive structure and moments where the clinician makes choices for the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to draw out the client's internal motivation, values, or confidence in change. The session relies more on instructions and problem-solving than on evoking change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stance is partly collaborative but remains expert-driven. The psychiatrist frequently takes decision-making away from the client, reducing the sense of true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk are rare and weak. The focus shifts quickly to concrete tasks instead of exploring the client's reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not amplify sustain talk and offers some reassuring statements. However, core fears of failure and judgment are not consistently explored or skillfully redirected.
