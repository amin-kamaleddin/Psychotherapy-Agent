# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and used appropriate affirmations such as normalizing slips and encouraging self-kindness. The warmth was generally consistent but remained somewhat task-focused rather than deeply attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered several reflective responses to the client’s experience but tended to pivot quickly to practical problem-solving. Deeper empathic exploration of ambivalence, self-criticism, and underlying emotions was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on reducing alcohol use to two drinks per night and produced a specific behavioral plan. Minor drift into sensory substitutes remained tied to the target behavior and did not weaken the overall focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporated some client input by modifying the plan in response to concerns about tracking, stop times, and activities. However, the overall structure remained largely counselor-led rather than mutually co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the client’s choices and respected boundaries when she rejected the hard stop time and journaling. Nonetheless, the repeated detailed instructions and added plan elements were directive and weakened autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was little effort to draw out the client’s own reasons for change or explore values and motivations. The psychiatrist primarily provided strategies rather than evoking change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative invitations were present, such as asking what the client wanted to focus on and offering choices about the alternative activity. The overall dynamic remained more expert-driven than equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made few efforts to amplify or build on the client’s change talk. Client statements showing motivation to try the plan were not consistently reflected, expanded, or used to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with the client’s sustain talk and occasionally validated it in a nonjudgmental way. However, sustain talk was not actively explored or softened through double-sided reflections.
