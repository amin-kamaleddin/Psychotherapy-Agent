# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the client's ambivalence and pace. It is strong but somewhat procedural, with fewer explicit individualized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responses accurately reflect the client's concerns about social connection, stress, and not wanting to lose a routine. Empathy is solid, though moments like the generic 'I understand' could be deeper.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is well-structured, moving from information gathering to a concrete, optional trial. Guidance is clear but offered as options rather than instructions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently asks for the client's perspective and co-creates the note, trial, and check-in. One brief, less responsive exchange prevents it from being fully collaborative throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker, with repeated emphasis that the plan is optional and not a test. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, rules, or judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions elicit the client's own ideas about tracking, social versus stress smoking, and self-checks. Evocation is effective but not always sustained in every response.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The exchange generally feels shared, with the psychiatrist treating the client as an expert in her own experience. Partnership is strong, though some reflections are brief rather than deeply reciprocal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist highlights the client's emerging intentions, such as trying a smoke-free break on Friday. Change talk is supported and elaborated, though not maximally amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's reluctance to give up the break is acknowledged, and the psychiatrist reframes the goal as separating the break from smoking. Sustain talk is softened without confrontation.
