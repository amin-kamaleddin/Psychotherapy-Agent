# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, repeatedly normalizing Linda’s ambivalence without criticizing her smoking. Acceptance is strong, though the warmth remains somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are effective reflective listening responses that accurately capture Linda’s ambivalence. However, the reflections often stay cognitive and behavioral rather than exploring deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on a relevant target behavior: reducing children’s exposure to seeing Linda smoke and making a small change easier. The focus could have been summarized more explicitly early in the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Linda’s lead and adapts to her practical concerns, allowing her ideas to shape the plan. The repeated use of structured lists keeps the interaction from feeling fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda’s choice is consistently honored, including her refusal to make a firm plan. The frequent offering of many suggestions creates subtle pressure toward selecting one of the clinician’s options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk but often moves quickly into offering options. Deeper evocation would require more follow-up on Linda’s values, motivations, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session includes invitational language and invites Linda to choose among options. However, the psychiatrist often adopts a problem-solving, expert role that limits the collaborative MI stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda identifies some reasons and plans for change, and the psychiatrist affirms this. The session leans more toward problem-solving than actively deepening Linda’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing or pressuring. The psychiatrist does not consistently use more advanced MI moves to redirect sustain talk toward change or explore values-based discrepancy.
