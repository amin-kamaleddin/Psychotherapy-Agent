# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly framing the space as non-judgmental and validating Samir's experience of being referred. He affirms Samir's honesty, agency, and self-awareness repeatedly without ever labeling or expressing disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist's reflections consistently capture the emotional essence beyond surface-level content, such as identifying that the wife's smile—not the boxes—is what undoes Samir because it makes it harder to be angry. No instance of misreading Samir's perspective occurs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a logical progression from surface resistance to deep emotional exploration, with each response building on the previous one. However, the emotional work could have been more explicitly tied back to the specific target behavior of medication adherence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is structured as a joint exploration shaped by Samir's disclosures rather than a predetermined agenda, with the psychiatrist using collaborative language and following Samir's lead throughout. His reflections are offered as interpretations rather than statements of fact.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently and explicitly supports Samir's autonomy, never pressuring him to change or imposing a timeline, and repeatedly highlights his agency (e.g., 'You didn't say I can't. You said could'). He validates the complexity of Samir's guilt while maintaining the thread of his own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and reflections to help Samir articulate his own motivations and barriers rather than providing his own reasons for change. He consistently uses Samir's own words back to him, helping him recognize his own agency and the connection between his values and behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Samir as the authority on his own experience, avoiding giving advice or prescribing solutions. The session is clearly a collaborative process where Samir's contributions substantially influence its direction and depth.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes ability talk and need/importance talk, particularly highlighting Samir's statement that he 'could' stop letting his wife do it. However, the session could have been more systematic in building momentum toward change through scaling questions or deeper values exploration, and ends without a concrete next step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Samir's sustain talk effectively by never arguing against it, reframing practical focus and guilt as evidence of agency or change motivation. The session doesn't present many strongly dominant sustain talk moments, so the skill isn't tested to its full extent.
