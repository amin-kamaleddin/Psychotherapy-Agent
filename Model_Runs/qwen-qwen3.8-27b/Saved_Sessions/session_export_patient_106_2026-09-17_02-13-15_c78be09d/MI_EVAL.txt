# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating, and respectful stance without pressuring the client. Warmth could be strengthened with more explicit affirmations of the client’s worth outside the smoking issue.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures the client’s stress, fear of irritability, and identity tied to the after-work break. Some reflections are slightly interpretive and could deepen further into shame, failure, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and narrows toward the after-work cigarette and smaller change. A clearer summary, agenda, or smallest next step would strengthen strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is clearly collaborative, with the psychiatrist framing the work as joint exploration. The client’s own language and concerns guide the session rather than an imposed treatment framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through open invitations, client-driven decision making, and avoidance of directive language. There is no visible pressure to quit or override the client’s ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively draw out the client’s values, meanings, and possible alternatives. The evocation could be stronger by more consistently eliciting confidence, past successes, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert on her own life and choices. There is no lecturing, prescribing, or assumption of authority over the client’s decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions intended to evoke change talk and possible next steps. There is limited visible evidence of amplifying, summarizing, or reinforcing the client’s own pro-change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s reasons for smoking and acknowledges the cigarette’s function. More strategic double-sided reflections or gentle pivots would better soften sustain talk while opening change language.
