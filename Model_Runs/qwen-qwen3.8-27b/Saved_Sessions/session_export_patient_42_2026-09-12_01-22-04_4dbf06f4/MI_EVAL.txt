# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect and nonjudgment through statements like 'totally normal' and 'valid worry.' However, acceptance can feel procedural when the conversation quickly moves from validation to practical planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflections of the client's ambivalence about belonging versus health. The empathy is limited by frequent movement from reflection to advice before fully exploring the client's deeper feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of smoking cessation within the client's social context. The direction is clear and practical, though sometimes overly focused on strategies rather than the motivational process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and offers choices, which supports collaboration. However, the counselor frequently supplies the content, such as lists, scripts, and plans, making the interaction feel counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is offered options and avoids direct pressure, supporting moderate autonomy. Directive moments, such as suggesting specific scripts and a one-round commitment, can reduce the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative techniques, such as asking for importance ratings. The evocation is limited because the counselor quickly supplies pros/cons and strategies rather than eliciting the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts partnership by asking for preferences and offering choices. The partnership is uneven because the counselor often takes the role of problem-solver and shapes the agenda with structured options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited efforts to elicit the client's own change talk, and some client statements are not sufficiently amplified. The psychiatrist frequently supplies reasons and strategies instead of drawing out the client's values and motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against the client's reasons for continuing to smoke and acknowledges their strength. Sustain talk is softened somewhat, but it is not consistently reframed using double-sided reflections or explored in a way that moves the client toward change.
