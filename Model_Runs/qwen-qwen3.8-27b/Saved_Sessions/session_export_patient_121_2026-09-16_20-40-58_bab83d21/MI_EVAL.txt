# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, even when Sofia is defensive or blame-focused. She validates Sofia's protective behavior without correcting, arguing, or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong perspective-taking and accurate reflections of ambivalence, especially around safety, connection, and fear of rejection. Some reflections could more fully capture the deeper emotional hurt of repeated blaming, isolation, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the target issue of peer conflict, avoidance, and isolation at school. It narrows effectively from the broader problem to a specific, workable next step without drifting or becoming rigid.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by following Sofia's ideas and jointly exploring options such as the math walk, phone/headphones strategy, and "cool, ok" plan. The collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still sets the overall sequence of questions rather than fully co-creating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia is frequently invited to choose, and the psychiatrist respects her boundary about not putting the phone away. A perfect score would include even more explicit space for Sofia to simply hold her choice without the gentle persistent agenda toward trying the math walk.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Sofia's own ideas, confidence, and coping strategies rather than providing advice. The session could go deeper by exploring Sofia's values, meaning, and confidence in managing a "no" more explicitly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally positions Sofia as the expert on her own experience and follows her lead rather than imposing a plan. A slightly more balanced partnership score is warranted because the psychiatrist still leads with a structured question sequence and briefly uses third-person language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes and builds on Sofia's own change talk, especially around asking Maya to walk to math. The session could be stronger by more systematically developing DARN-C change talk, including commitment, confidence, values, and what success would mean.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Sofia's sustain talk well by acknowledging its protective function without challenging or reinforcing avoidance. Some of Sofia's persistent limits could be more fully pivoted toward change talk about connection, safety, and self-protection without isolation.
