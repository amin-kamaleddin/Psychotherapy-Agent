# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, normalization, and nonjudgment toward Elise’s ambivalence and slip worries. Acceptance is strong but sometimes more formulaic than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Elise’s conflict, health concerns, and social worries. Empathy is useful but does not consistently deepen the emotional meaning of her fear of being “the odd one out.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on smoking reduction in social settings and moves efficiently toward a concrete plan. Direction is excellent, with little wandering and clear momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Elise to build a doable plan and invites her to choose among options. Collaboration is present but less equal because the counselor frequently generates the menu of strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise is repeatedly given choice, and the counselor avoids controlling language such as “should” or “must.” Autonomy could be stronger by allowing more space for Elise to generate her own options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses rulers and targeted questions to draw out importance, confidence, and intention. However, deeper evocation of values, self-image, and personal meaning for change is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise is invited to choose next steps and preferred coping strategies, showing some partnership. The interaction still tilts toward expert guidance because the psychiatrist frequently offers concrete scripts and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise expresses desire, concern, ability, and increased confidence, especially through ruler use. Change talk is present but not consistently expanded into richer, values-based reasoning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise’s sustain talk about fitting in and fear of rejection is normalized without argument or shame. The counselor could more strategically reflect and redirect those reasons rather than moving quickly into problem-solving.
