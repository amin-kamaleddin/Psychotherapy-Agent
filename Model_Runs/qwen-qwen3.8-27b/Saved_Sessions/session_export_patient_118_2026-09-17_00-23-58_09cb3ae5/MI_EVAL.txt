# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive even when Seamus resists. Some affirmations are slightly generic, but positive regard is clear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Seamus's frustration, practical concerns, and fear of pain. It remains somewhat task-focused and misses deeper emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session quickly drops the smoking-cessation focus after Seamus resists and becomes centered on dental logistics. A thin through-line toward smoking would improve MI direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor co-creates the session and lets Seamus's preferences directly shape the plan. The interaction is strongly client-led and collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly emphasizes Seamus's ownership, choice, and right to avoid unwanted lectures. It avoids pressure and reinforces freedom without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively draw out practical dental preferences and self-management. Evocation of smoking-related motivation, values, or confidence is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treats Seamus as the expert on his own body, limits, and schedule. Power is shared through collaborative language and client-led decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only limited smoking-related change talk emerges, and it is not substantially amplified. More evocation of reasons, need, or commitment to reduce smoking is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor handles resistance without arguing and avoids reinforcing avoidant sustain talk. It could more strategically redirect sustain talk toward smoking-related change.
