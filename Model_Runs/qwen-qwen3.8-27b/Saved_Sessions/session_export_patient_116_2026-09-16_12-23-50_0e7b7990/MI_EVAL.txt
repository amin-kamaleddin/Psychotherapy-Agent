# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes Samir’s concerns. However, early agenda-focused risk information and some reflections of nonadherence slightly weaken acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening of Samir’s view that medication feels like another rule. The main limitation is that reflections often stay at content level rather than naming deeper emotions and values such as independence, competence, and control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on medication adherence and its impact on daily functioning. The main limitation is occasional drift toward practical planning instead of consolidating change talk and a clearly agreed focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Samir’s input and adapts when he declines options. However, the interaction is not fully equal-partnered because the psychiatrist frequently supplies structured menus and problem-solving frameworks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice with noncoercive language and accepts Samir’s repeated refusals. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated suggestions after declines and early medical risk framing that can feel controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks about hopes, past successes, and thresholds for seeking help. The main limitation is that too much content is supplied, reducing the depth and emergence of Samir’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, preferences, and choices, showing some collaborative stance. However, the psychiatrist often generates the content and options rather than positioning Samir’s expertise as central.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms one small step and elicits a modest change-relevant behavior. The main limitation is insufficient development of importance, desire, need, and confidence language to build Samir’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct argument and accepts Samir’s sustain talk without challenge. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to transform resistance into change-relevant thinking.
