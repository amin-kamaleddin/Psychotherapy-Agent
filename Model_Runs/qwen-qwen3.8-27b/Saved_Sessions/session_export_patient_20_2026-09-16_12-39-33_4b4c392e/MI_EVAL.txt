# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language that avoids blaming or shaming the client. Acceptance is present but often centered on the plan rather than warm affirmation of the client's worth or courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective statements accurately capture the behavior and consequences, but they remain somewhat factual. The therapist misses emotionally significant concerns about appearing weak, performing, failing, and the ambivalence about using beer to avoid yelling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays connected to after-work drinking, evening irritability, and work stress as triggers. There is minor drift into general work strategies and social scripts, and periodic summaries would better clarify progress toward the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses inclusive language and lets the client's preferences materially shape the plan. However, the therapist still retains much of the agenda and offers structured options rather than fully co-creating from the client's own concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice and client control are consistently supported, including no logging, no mandatory check-in, and flexible limits. Mild directive framing around steps and scripts slightly reduces the autonomy emphasis.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist uses open questions, examples, confidence ratings, and obstacle prompts to draw out the client. Evocation remains surface-level because values, motivation, and the client's own change talk are not consistently deepened or reflected back.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist invites input and accepts the client's modifications, showing genuine power-sharing. The consultant-like offer of menus of options means the client is not fully positioned as the sole expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits useful change talk through planning prompts and confidence questions. The therapist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or connect that change talk to the client's values to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with the client's reasons for maintaining drinking. However, double-sided reflections are limited, and the key ambivalence about using beer to prevent yelling is underexplored.
