# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Mateo's frustration and dignity. It falls just short of full acceptance by not consistently normalizing emotional pain or explicitly expressing unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflective listening that accurately captures Mateo's perspective and ambivalence. Some deeper emotional themes, such as feeling like a burden, fear of collapse, and loneliness, are not explored fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity and managing pain-related barriers. Summaries and recaps keep the conversation practical and on track.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with the plan co-constructed and Mateo's modifications incorporated. The psychiatrist sometimes leads by proposing specific steps, which slightly reduces the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through flexibility, optional steps, and asking what feels doable. However, the session is somewhat proposal-heavy and could more explicitly emphasize Mateo's full control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes internal motivation by exploring confidence, doability, and past helpful strategies. A stronger rating would require more client-generated ideas before offering therapist-generated options and more follow-up on change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's input is invited and meaningfully shapes the plan, reflecting respect for his expertise. The psychiatrist occasionally feels directive when introducing detailed options early in the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk through small goals, confidence questions, and reinforcement of Mateo's commitments. The approach could be strengthened by exploring values, identity, and quality of life more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by reframing barriers such as pain and the couch as risks to plan around rather than reasons to stop. The psychiatrist could use more double-sided reflections for stronger statements of sustain talk.
