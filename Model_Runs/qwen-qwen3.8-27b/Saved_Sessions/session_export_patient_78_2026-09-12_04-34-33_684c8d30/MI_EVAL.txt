# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard, even when the client is not ready to quit. The language is strong but somewhat formulaic and could include deeper, individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture much of the client’s stated frustration but often remain at the content level. They miss deeper emotional experiences such as feeling policed, judged, or treated as a problem rather than an autonomous adult.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to smoking and the client’s frustration with being told to quit, but it lacks a consistently narrowed focus. It ends without a clear shared next step or small, workable goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and avoids imposing a one-size-fits-all quitting plan, creating a collaborative tone. However, the collaboration is somewhat menu-like rather than deeply co-constructed with the client’s expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by repeatedly affirming that the client is in control and that quitting is her choice. He respects the client’s stated boundary against pressure and does not attempt to override it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are helpful for engagement, but they mostly address logistics, preferences, and comfort rather than internal change motivation. The client’s own recognition that smoking may not be good for her is present but not sufficiently drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a strong sense of partnership by inviting the client to direct the conversation and using inclusive, nonauthoritarian language. It remains somewhat passive and option-based rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers subtle change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely affirms, reflects, or builds on it. Standard MI evocation strategies to strengthen motivation for change are largely missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or invalidating the client’s sustain talk, which helps reduce defensiveness. However, he does not actively soften or pivot sustain talk toward change with double-sided reflections.
