# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental throughout, using supportive language that reduces shame. Acceptance is somewhat task-oriented and could more explicitly honor Linda’s ambivalence, fear of dismissal, and personal values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture Linda’s concerns about inhaler use, steroids, dependence, and being brushed off. Empathy could deepen by exploring the emotional core of her ambivalence and using more complex two-sided reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and produces a concrete, practical plan for discussing dose reduction with the clinician. Direction is clear but more task-oriented than motivationally strategic, with limited connection between the plan and Linda’s personal change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Linda to co-create the plan and lets her choose the list length, opener, and fallback sentence. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist remains the primary source of content and could elicit more of Linda’s own ideas before offering options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by emphasizing Linda’s right to choose, offering options, and leaving the final decision to her. There is no pressure, coercion, or implication that she must accept a specific course of action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins by evoking goals but then shifts toward providing information, questions, and script options. More skillful evocation would involve drawing out Linda’s own values, concerns, and reasons for change before supplying language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates high-level partnership by inviting Linda’s input and allowing her preferences to shape the session and final product. Linda’s choices directly influence the questions, opener, and fallback sentence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda expresses change talk about wanting peace of mind and avoiding long-term reliance, and the psychiatrist supports her initiative. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or systematically elicit change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and reframes Linda’s sustain talk without arguing or dismissing her concerns. Softening is effective, but the ambivalence is not fully resolved and could benefit from more double-sided reflections.
