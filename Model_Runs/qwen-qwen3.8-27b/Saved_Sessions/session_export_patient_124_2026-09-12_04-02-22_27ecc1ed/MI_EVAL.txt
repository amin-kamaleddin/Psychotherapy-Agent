# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Sofia’s concerns. It fell short of a 5 because strengths and willingness to consider change were not consistently and explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used reflective listening to capture Sofia’s ambivalence and mixed concerns. Empathy was sometimes surface-level, with limited deeper exploration of fear, anxiety, identity, and the meaning of marijuana as a coping tool.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a specific target behavior: reducing or limiting marijuana use, especially by not replacing what she already has. The focus was occasionally broadened by introducing multiple strategies, making it feel more like problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia contributed important choices, but the psychiatrist frequently offered options, techniques, and structured experiments. This made the collaboration feel uneven and less equal-partnered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice with low-pressure language and accepted Sofia’s reluctance to make promises. Autonomy support was slightly weakened by the counselor’s tendency to propose plans, time limits, scripts, and tools.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked some evocative questions and drew out concerns about cost, stress, and social fit. However, the session often relied on counselor-provided reasons and tools rather than deeply evoking Sofia’s internal motivations, values, and future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered choices, supporting partnership. The interaction felt more expert-led because the counselor generated much of the agenda and provided many options, scripts, and coping tools.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited, particularly around cost and a small behavioral experiment. The counselor did not consistently amplify Sofia’s own reasons for change or reflect them as personal values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and acknowledged that marijuana helps Sofia relax and socialize. However, there were not enough double-sided reflections or reframes to hold benefits while moving toward discrepancy with broader goals.
