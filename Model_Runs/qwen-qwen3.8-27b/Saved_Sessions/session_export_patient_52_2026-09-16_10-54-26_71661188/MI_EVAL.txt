# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming. However, some affirmations endorse the client's plan as the right or good option, which makes the acceptance feel slightly performance-based.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects the client's ambivalence about drinking, guilt, and limited recovery time. The main limitation is moving quickly toward problem-solving rather than deeper exploration of affect and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of alcohol use and coping. It moves clearly from assessment to a concrete, brief experiment with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's preferences clearly shape the plan, including tea, music, texting her sister, and timing. The collaboration is somewhat limited by frequent structured option menus and a fairly detailed clinician-led sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client's control, pace, and choice, and respects her request to handle reminders independently first. There is no pressure, coercion, or moral persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician asks effective open questions that draw out readiness, confidence, and the client's own change talk. However, substantial practical advice and option lists reduce the emphasis on deeper values-based evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is invited to choose and her ideas repeatedly guide the work. The partnership is slightly weakened by the clinician offering multiple prepackaged options and a fairly structured plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates clear opportunities for the client to express change talk about trying one drink, waiting five minutes, and proving she can keep this up. Some menu-style questions limit fuller elicitation of the client's own motivations and definitions of success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician responds to sustain talk with normalization, reframing, and non-argumentative language. It would be stronger with more consistent double-sided reflections that pivot sustain talk toward agency and change.
