# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Elise’s concerns without arguing with her sustain talk. Acceptance was slightly task-focused and less individualized in affirming Elise beyond her struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used reflective listening to recognize ambivalence and social fears, such as feeling odd or excluded. However, empathy often remained surface-level and quickly shifted into problem-solving rather than exploring emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing or quitting smoking during social breaks and was guided toward a concrete next step. There was little drift, and the plan ended with a clear target behavior and follow-up intention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options, asked Elise to choose, and adjusted the plan based on her concerns and preferences. Collaboration was evident, though the psychiatrist supplied much of the content, including specific lines and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise’s choice was frequently acknowledged, and she was allowed to reject rehearsal and select lower-pressure options. Autonomy support was strong, though the plan remained somewhat prescriptive through repeated suggestions and the "tiny experiment" framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opening questions explored what matters about the break and what might be easiest to try, but evocation was superficial and often replaced by advice-giving. Deeper values, confidence, and change talk were not consistently elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise’s input was invited, and the psychiatrist adapted when she declined rehearsal. However, the psychiatrist often acted as planner by offering multiple specific options and scripts, which limited client ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited, such as health consequences and willingness to try a small step, but it was not consistently followed up, amplified, or expanded. The conversation moved quickly into practical scripts and behavioral options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with or reinforce Elise’s sustain talk, but the session heavily focused on her reasons to remain connected through smoking breaks. More rolling with sustain talk and redirection toward change would have strengthened this area.
