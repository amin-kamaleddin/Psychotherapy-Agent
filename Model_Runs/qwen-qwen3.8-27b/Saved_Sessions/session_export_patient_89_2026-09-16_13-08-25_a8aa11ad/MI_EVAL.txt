# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, supportive, and affirming while normalizing the difficulty of fitting activity into a busy life. It is not a 5 because the warmth is sometimes formulaic and does not deeply validate the emotional frustration behind past diet failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects practical constraints, realistic expectations, and the need for a manageable plan. The reflections remain somewhat surface-level and do not fully explore deeper feelings such as frustration, fear of failure, or excitement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior with clear momentum from identifying a small activity to creating a concrete plan and check-in. There is no drift into unrelated topics, excessive psychoeducation, or abstract discussion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist and patient co-construct a realistic plan, and the patient’s preferences clearly guide many decisions. However, the therapist remains the primary agenda-setter and supplies many structured options and practical suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Marcus’s sense of choice by asking him to select activities, supports, timing, and check-in format. A few moments, such as targeting a specific confidence score and providing exact calendar wording, are slightly therapist-managed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist frequently uses evocative questions to help Marcus generate his own strategies, reasons, and commitments. The evocation is mostly action-oriented and does not deeply explore underlying values, identity, or stronger motivational themes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marcus’s input throughout and frames the work as collaborative rather than authoritative. It is not a 5 because the therapist still supplies substantial structure, options, and an exact calendar plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist elicits useful change talk and affirms Marcus’s emerging confidence and interest. The main limitation is that the therapist often moves quickly to the next logistical step rather than deepening or amplifying the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Marcus’s barriers and uses flexible if-then planning to make sustain talk less immobilizing. It is not a 5 because the therapist does not frequently use double-sided reflections or explicitly pivot sustain talk into change talk.
