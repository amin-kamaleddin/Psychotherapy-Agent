# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive without shaming the patient. Acceptance is adequate but somewhat procedural, with limited personalized affirmation of her specific values and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some empathy for the burden of the two-week gap, but responses often shift to information and problem-solving. Deeper reflection of fear, control, and work-related worry is limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safe medication transition, diet, sleep, symptoms, and prescriber communication. Direction is clear but more medical-procedural than tied to the patient's personal goals and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates the patient's concerns and preferences into the plan, but still supplies much of the structure and wording. Interaction is collaborative but more guided than truly equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options and prescriber confirmation are offered, supporting informed choice, but many directive medical statements remain. It would be stronger to more clearly affirm that final decisions and acceptable outcomes belong to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly factual and logistical, with little effort to draw out the patient's values, hopes, fears, or confidence. The patient's existing change talk is not consistently expanded or elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often acts as the medical expert, providing explanations, lists, and draft messages. There is limited explicit recognition that the patient is the expert on her own values, tolerance, and decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers clear change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently affirm or build on it with importance, confidence, or values questions. More strategic amplification of her reasons for change is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Concerns are addressed through practical information without arguing or invalidating the patient. More double-sided reflection and gentle reframing would better soften ambivalence.
