# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, and normalizing, with clear positive regard for the client’s experience. Falls short of a 5 because some affirmations assign positive meaning before the client fully articulates it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects the cycle, somatic fear, and desire for a nonjudgmental space accurately and supportively. Deeper shame, fraudulence, and exhaustion are not explored with enough depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior and the exam-related anxiety. Could better connect the small step to the client’s broader values and long-term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offers options, invites revisions, and co-creates the script, task size, and response preference. The process is strongly shared rather than clinician-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client’s right to choose, stop, and define the task in her own terms. Avoids pressure and normalizes quitting without it proving weakness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses open questions and a confidence ruler to elicit the client’s own words and motivation. Could deepen evocation by exploring values, hopes, and past successes before moving into the experiment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client meaningfully shapes the wording, task size, and in-session response preference. Some guiding and script-providing moments make the stance less egalitarian.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits desired change and a small behavioral step, but does not consistently amplify the client’s own change language. The session moves into practice before change talk is fully developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Normalizes fear and failure without arguing, and reframes stopping as acceptable and informative. Could use more double-sided reflections and fewer clinician-supplied interpretations.
