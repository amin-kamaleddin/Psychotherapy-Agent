# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and nonjudgmental tone throughout, with explicit validation of the client's directness and frustrations. Never scolds or labels resistance negatively; reinforces the client's value as a person, not just a case to manage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and nuanced reflections that go beyond surface statements to name emotional undercurrents (e.g., exhaustion, frustration at automaticity). Each reflection adds something new and tracks the client's shifting emotional state precisely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on weight management as the target behavior with a well-structured arc from engagement to a concrete next step. Briefly validates tangential concerns (waiting room, blood pressure) without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine joint exploration, with the client's contributions (accounting metaphor, food identification, timeline) driving direction and content. Suggestions are offered as possibilities for the client to evaluate, not prescriptions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent reinforcement of the client's ownership, freedom, and right to set his own pace throughout the session. No pressure tactics, no 'you should' or 'you need to' language; all suggestions are framed as the client's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation and solutions from within the client rather than providing external reasons. Uses the client's own language and professional framework to guide him toward his own insights and plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions the client as the expert on his own life and leverages his professional expertise as the source of the solution. The psychiatrist functions as a mirror and catalyst, never as the one who has the answer.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively amplifies and reinforces the client's emergent change talk using his own words and highlights the shift from resignation to a concrete plan. Could be strengthened by more explicit elicitation of confidence/ability statements (e.g., 'How confident are you that you can...?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (lack of time, no plan, knowing isn't doing) is consistently acknowledged, validated, and gently worked around rather than argued against. Could benefit from more explicit double-sided reflections in moments where sustain talk was more pronounced.
