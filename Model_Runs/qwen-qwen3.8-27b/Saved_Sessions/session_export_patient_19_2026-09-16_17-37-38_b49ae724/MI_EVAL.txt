# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout the session. Slight deduction for occasionally formulaic, less individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong complex reflections to capture ambivalence and key concerns, often accurately tracking the client's experience. Some interpretations are imposed, and the emotional texture is not explored deeply enough.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear progression from broad concern to a concrete, client-led plan. It stays in practical planning and does not move toward explicit commitment or summary tied to values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels collaborative, with client ideas shaping the agenda, plan, and language. The psychiatrist still does much of the structuring, so co-construction is not fully balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently invites client choice, affirms ownership, and avoids directive language. A few subtle constraints on goals or options reduce the score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits internal motivation and client-generated ideas without external pressure. It does not deeply tap values, identity, future vision, past success, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the client as the expert, avoids advice, and lets the client choose focus and plan. Minor expert-driven framing at times, such as pre-specifying low-energy/no-cost parameters.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits concrete behavioral change talk and affirms client-generated steps. It remains practical and misses opportunities to deepen DARN-CAT dimensions, values, ability, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reflects sustain talk without arguing, including concerns about friends and 'surviving.' It could use more strategic double-sided reflections or active pivoting to resolve ambivalence.
