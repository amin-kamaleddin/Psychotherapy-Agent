# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, affirming language and normalizes imperfect progress without blaming past quit attempts. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with less individualized acknowledgement of Linda’s identity, shame, or repeated failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Linda’s main tensions, including health, money, and the social importance of smoking breaks. However, reflections remain surface-level and do not deeply explore underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or identity tied to smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking reduction/cessation with a clear, practical near-term plan for Tuesday. Direction is strong, though it becomes heavily behavioral and could better connect the plan to Linda’s values and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the plan around Linda’s choices, including the patch trial, refusal phrase, and mint as a behavioral support. Collaboration is slightly limited by frequent provision of pre-made options and scripts before fully eliciting Linda’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Linda’s right to choose and frames all steps as optional and low-pressure. Linda is allowed to set a non-quitting goal, choose a small step, and decide how much to do without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions about motivation, past attempts, and doable steps. However, evocation is inconsistent because advice, options, and scripts often interrupt the process of drawing out Linda’s own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, asks for input, and invites Linda’s choices into the plan. Partnership is not fully optimal because the psychiatrist introduces many concrete options early, which can feel more like offering a menu than co-creating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda expresses some change talk about health, cost, and readiness to try a patch. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk, often moving quickly into options and behavioral planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk, such as the importance of the social break and fear of losing connection, without arguing. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic redirection to soften sustain talk and turn it toward change.
