# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids framing past dieting struggles as failure. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly normalizing the emotional pain behind body dissatisfaction and mirror-based concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The reflections accurately capture the client's frustration, ambivalence, and desire for sustainable change. The empathy could deepen by exploring the stronger emotional undercurrents, such as shame, fear, and the weight of cultural body ideals.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on sustainable eating, movement, and self-monitoring rather than drifting into unrelated topics. Even tangential topics such as the inner critic and mirror-based evaluation are kept connected to the target behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation feels like a joint exploration, with the client's language, metaphors, and ideas shaping the work. The psychiatrist builds on the client's suggestions rather than imposing a counselor-defined plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice by asking what would feel useful, sustainable, and within the client's control. There is no visible pressure, persuasion, or directive advice that would undermine autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits client-generated motivation, skills, and a concrete self-monitoring practice. The score is not a 5 because some counselor-introduced frames guide the formulation and the evocation of deeper values and commitment could be stronger.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert on her own goals, values, and experience. The psychiatrist invites the client to identify what matters and what would feel doable, demonstrating genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions effectively generate change talk around sustainability, skills, self-monitoring, and a small practice. The counselor could strengthen this by more consistently amplifying change talk into explicit DARC language and firmer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully normalizes the client's mixed concerns without arguing against the desire for weight change. The score is limited because the final sustain talk about mirror-based validation and body change is not fully addressed in the visible transcript.
