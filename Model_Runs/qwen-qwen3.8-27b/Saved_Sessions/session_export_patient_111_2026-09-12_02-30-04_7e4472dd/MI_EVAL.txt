# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows nonjudgmental respect, accepts the client's ambivalence, and avoids moralizing or coercion. Acceptance is strong even when the client is resistant.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client's reasons for attending, routines, and irritation with intrusive suggestions. It could deepen further by reflecting the relational ambivalence around his wife and changing habits.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays organized around smoking and the wife's worry but drifts toward peripheral odor management. It could maintain clearer focus on the primary target of tobacco cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative, avoiding taking sides and adapting when the client rejects options. More client-generated ideas before counselor suggestions would make it fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently respects choice, asks permission, and accepts the client's limited decision without pressing for more. Slight pressure emerges around trying a specific plan and confidence rating, but autonomy support is strong.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks open questions and evokes practical preferences, but does not deeply evoke internal motivation, values, or ambivalence about changing smoking. Follow-up on brief change talk such as helping his wife was not expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor collaborates by inviting input, asking permission, and letting the client choose the fan option. It becomes somewhat leading when the counselor introduces practical suggestions before fully eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is limited to a minor environmental compromise rather than smoking cessation itself. The counselor does not strategically elicit or amplify the client's reasons, benefits, or confidence for changing smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids argument and respects the client's refusal, but often accepts resistant statements as final. More double-sided reflections and exploring costs or benefits of maintaining the status quo would soften resistance.
