# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates unconditional positive regard without judgment, even when the client minimizes drinking. Responses are warm, respectful, and affirming of her right to enjoy wine while considering health.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s ambivalence, dignity, steadiness, and concern about being perceived as “wobbly.” It could be stronger by exploring deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or the meaning of the fall.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior—reducing alcohol use and building a concrete plan for safer drinking. Each question builds momentum toward a doable action step without major drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids lecturing, diagnosing, or prescribing a plan. The client’s ideas—such as skipping one Tuesday, using sparkling water, and planning what to say to Sarah—direct the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change is consistently framed as self-directed, using language like “your choice, not a loss” and “on your terms.” The client’s ownership is respected, such as “I’m the boss of my schedule.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes the client’s own motivations rather than providing external reasons to change. Open questions and reflections help her articulate steadiness, dignity, calm with her son, and being sensible.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports Evelyn’s expertise over her own life and choices, and her ideas shape the session. The partnership could be stronger by more explicitly negotiating the agenda and affirming her primary role in setting direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits and strengthens change talk through open questions and reflective amplification. The client produces desire, ability, reasons, and commitment language such as “I want to feel steady on my feet.”

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by validating the value of wine, the pub, and connection while linking it to health. The psychiatrist avoids arguing, amplifying barriers, or becoming adversarial.
