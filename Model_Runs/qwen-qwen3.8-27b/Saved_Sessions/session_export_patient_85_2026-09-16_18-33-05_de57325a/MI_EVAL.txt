# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, affirming, and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s capability and normalizing imperfection. Acceptance is somewhat more process-focused than deeply relational, with limited deeper emotional validation of repeated weight cycles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture both meaning and some affect, showing genuine perspective-taking. Empathy remains largely cognitive and strategic, with less exploration of disappointment, embarrassment, or the emotional weight of giving up a favorite drink.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing fruit juice and building a sustainable water routine. Each response advances concrete, behavior-specific steps without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and client co-construct a practical plan using the client’s language and ideas. Collaboration is strong but shaped by a counselor-led sequence of questions and summaries rather than more open agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites the client to choose and avoids direct advice, supporting self-determination. Some narrowing or binary questions subtly channel choices toward counselor-identified options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions draw out the client’s own practical strategies and realistic success criteria. Evocation is less strong for deeper values, desire, or identity-based motivation beyond the immediate behavior plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the expert on his mornings, evenings, and kitchen. Partnership is good but somewhat controlled by a steady sequence of narrowing questions rather than fully open client-directed flow.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently affirms and asks questions that elicit client change talk, especially action and confidence. It could more broadly elicit desire, importance, need, and values-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing attachment to juice and gently redirects toward easier alternatives. Sustain talk was limited, so advanced softening strategies were not fully demonstrated.
