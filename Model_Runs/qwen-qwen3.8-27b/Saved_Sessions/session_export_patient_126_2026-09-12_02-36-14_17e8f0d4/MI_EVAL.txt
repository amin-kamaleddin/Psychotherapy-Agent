# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirms the client's right to choose. Acceptance is sometimes generic or overly agreeable, which can endorse the client's minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician makes several reflective attempts about relief, frustration, and the importance of health for his son. Reflections are sometimes surface-level or over-interpretive and do not fully explore deeper feelings or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session touches on alcohol use and health risks, but the target behavior is not clearly established or maintained. The conversation remains somewhat diffuse and client-led without clear MI direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses collaborative language and invites input, but still functions as an adviser offering options and concrete strategies. The interaction is not fully co-constructed as a true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician strongly supports autonomy by emphasizing the client's freedom to choose and avoiding pressure. Offering advice and concrete plans slightly limits the client-as-self-determining stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses some evocative questions, including an importance ruler and questions about what 'sensible' would look like. Evocation is not consistently used to develop the client's own motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites preferences and avoids a purely directive stance, but the client is not fully positioned as the expert on his own life. Power-sharing is present but not fully developed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk about quitting smoking and staying healthy for his son, but the clinician does not consistently amplify or build on it. The clinician often moves quickly to advice before strengthening change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client uses substantial sustain talk about drinking not being a big deal, and the clinician mostly responds with agreement and reassurance. There is little active softening, such as double-sided reflections or exploration of underlying concerns.
