# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment or condescension even when the patient minimizes or dismisses. Phrases like 'I'm not going to dismiss it' and 'you don't owe anyone more than you gave today' demonstrate deep respect regardless of the patient's choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional perspective-taking with specific, nuanced reflections that capture multiple levels of meaning. The psychiatrist accurately names the deeper fear ('the cigarette isn't just the break, it's the thing that keeps your head off whatever's under there') and validates it without pushing further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression from surface tension to specific triggers to underlying needs to deeper meaning, maintaining consistent focus on smoking. Slight drift toward broader existential themes near the end, though handled well by respecting the patient's boundary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative stance throughout, with the session structured as a joint exploration using language like 'I'm curious' and 'what do you think?' The psychiatrist builds on the patient's own language and accepts his terms of engagement without negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support; the psychiatrist never pressures to commit and fully respects the patient's decision to end the session. Statements like 'whatever feels right to you' and 'the door's open, no timeline' consistently place ownership in the patient's hands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful use of strategic questions, reflections, and reframes that draw out the patient's own motivations and insights at every stage. Every insight—walking, the two o'clock marker, being present—originates from the patient, elicited through skillful evocation rather than external suggestion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise about the patient's life resides within him, explicitly stating 'I'm not trying to hand you a script.' The session ends with the patient setting his own boundaries, which the psychiatrist fully respects, demonstrating true power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful reframes and questions (e.g., 'what would make them feel like a real break?') successfully elicit growing change talk, including the profound 'being present' insight. Could have more explicitly reinforced or amplified key change talk moments such as the 'two o'clock' marker as a strength or capability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively by acknowledging without challenging, then gently pivoting to curiosity (e.g., the 'not addicted' and 'just dust' statements). The sustain talk around age ('I'm 34, not 64') and the doctor being a 'worrier' could have been more strategically redirected, though the psychiatrist handles it adequately by not engaging directly.
