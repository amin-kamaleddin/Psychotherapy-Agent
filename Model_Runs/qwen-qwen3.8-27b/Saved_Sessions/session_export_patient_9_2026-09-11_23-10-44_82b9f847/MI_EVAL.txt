# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance. Warmth is generally present but can feel somewhat functional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some accurate reflective listening, especially early in the session. However, empathy is inconsistent because the counselor often shifts into problem-solving before fully exploring the client's feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on reducing co-parenting conflict and supporting a concrete goal for the child. The direction is strong but sometimes becomes overly narrow on texting mechanics rather than broader MI change processes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and adapts to the client's input. Collaboration is limited by the frequent provision of scripts, plans, and contingency strategies rather than fully co-created direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly affirms choice and avoids overt pressure. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by the amount of prescriptive advice and structured behavioral planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some motivation and helps the client identify a small, doable step. However, much of the session relies on advising and pre-made solutions rather than consistently drawing out the client's own reasons and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the psychiatrist asks for input, the overall stance is more expert-driven than partnership-based. The counselor frequently provides ready-made scripts and plans, reducing the sense of shared power and client-led exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are positive moments where the client expresses willingness and agency. The counselor does not consistently elicit, amplify, or expand change talk through deeper motivational questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk and redirects toward practical change. Softening is uneven because responses often rely on additional scripts and contingency plans rather than reflective MI-consistent techniques.
