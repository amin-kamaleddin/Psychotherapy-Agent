# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Daniel’s routine without shaming him for drinking. The stance is strong but somewhat formulaic, relying heavily on repeated phrases like “it makes sense” and offering less individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses complex reflections that accurately capture Daniel’s ambivalence, defensiveness, and desire for autonomy. These reflections track both cognition and emotion without over-pathologizing or distorting his experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to alcohol use and recovery but lacks a clear strategic direction toward the target behavior. It moves through several client-selected topics without consolidating a decisional balance, brief summary, or concrete next step toward reducing or modifying alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively on concrete signs such as sleep, leg feel, and the 20-miler. The session could be more collaborative by explicitly negotiating the agenda and inviting Daniel to choose the next direction after summaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong and consistent, with repeated emphasis on Daniel’s choice, self-direction, and control over the process. The psychiatrist avoids advising him to stop drinking and reassures him that he is not being managed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions draw out Daniel’s own standards and monitoring ideas, but evocation is not consistently directed toward motivation for change. There are fewer questions targeting importance, confidence, or readiness to try a small change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions Daniel as the expert on his own body, training, and goals. Daniel’s responses actively shape the session, and the language consistently reinforces that he is in the driver’s seat.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-relevant language about feeling sharp and protecting training, but does not consistently deepen it into explicit change talk. There are limited follow-up questions amplifying desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing and acknowledges the normalcy and usefulness of Daniel’s routine. However, he does not consistently use double-sided reflections to hold both the value of the ritual and the possible costs to training.
