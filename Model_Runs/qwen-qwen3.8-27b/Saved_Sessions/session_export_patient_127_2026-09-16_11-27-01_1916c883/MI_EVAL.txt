# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Timur’s courage, dignity, and practical grit. Acceptance could be slightly stronger by normalizing the stigma, shame, and identity conflict around his record more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate reflections of Timur’s situation, concerns about dignity, and ambivalence about work and rejection. Deeper emotional resonance with his fear of rejection, shame, and identity conflict would elevate this area.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on building a stable, crime-free life and reducing recidivism through concrete action steps. The conversation moves purposefully from importance to a specific, actionable plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting Timur to choose options, preferences, and language for his plan. A stronger sense of equal partnership would come from relying less on therapist-generated options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, as the psychiatrist repeatedly honors Timur’s choices, boundaries, and desire to act on his own terms. The counselor avoids coercion or excessive direction while still supporting him to move forward.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Timur’s own motivations, steps, resources, and preferred approach. Evocation could be stronger by drawing out more of his internal values, identity, and self-confidence rather than partially scripting the response.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and lets Timur’s experience shape the session. A more fully client-led partnership would involve fewer counselor-generated options and more explicit acknowledgment that Timur is the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes Timur’s own reasons, desires, and commitments, resulting in a concrete action plan. The evocation is more task-oriented than motivation-oriented, with less systematic use of deeper DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing, judging, or dismissing Timur’s concerns. More deliberate double-sided reflections and strategic pivots could reduce the weight of the old ways and repeated focus on rejection.
