# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-judgmental and respectful, explicitly validating Milan's right to his own perspective multiple times. Affirmations are well-placed but occasionally feel procedural rather than deeply personal or organic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening that captures perceived injustice, social motivation, and the gap between 'having a good time' and 'just there.' Some reflections remain surface-level and could probe deeper into the emotional texture of Milan's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior throughout with a logical progression from context to self-perception to social dynamics to probing alignment with preferences. Pacing is appropriate without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with genuine invitations for Milan's input and no prescribed solutions. However, the conversation follows a fairly linear planned path, and Milan's responses do not substantially reshape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never imposes a goal or suggests Milan should stop drinking, consistently framing the conversation as exploration rather than persuasion. Could be strengthened by less frequent use of 'I'm not going to [x],' which can subtly imply a default impulse to control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently uses open questions to elicit Milan's own language, values, and criteria rather than providing external reasons for change. Could be deeper by exploring underlying values beyond fitting in that might provide stronger internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Milan as the authority on his own experience and avoids an expert-driven approach. The psychiatrist still steers heavily, keeping Milan somewhat reactive rather than truly co-directing the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk but Milan's remains minimal throughout and is not consistently amplified or built upon. Missed opportunities to deepen evocation after Milan offers small seeds of change language, such as 'if it was something I wasn't into, maybe I'd quit it eventually.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully validates Milan's minimization without challenging or moralizing, using techniques like 'I'm not going to inflate it' and naming the gap between 'most of the time' and 'every time.' Could incorporate more strategic double-sided reflections that explicitly honor the status quo while pivoting toward change.
