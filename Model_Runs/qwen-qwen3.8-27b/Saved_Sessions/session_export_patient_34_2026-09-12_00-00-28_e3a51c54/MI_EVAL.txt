# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally shows respect and validates frustration, but acceptance is not consistently warm or individualized. It can sound more managing than accepting due to rapid problem-solving and mild labels.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some understanding of frustration and unfairness, but empathy remains shallow and misses deeper concerns such as respect, parental reaction, record, and social life. Reflections are sometimes inconsistent or poorly formed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays on the hearing, but MI-relevant targets such as drinking choices, accountability, and values are not consistently explored. Direction leans toward case management rather than motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Options are offered, but the psychiatrist remains the primary planner, generating scripts, tactics, and questions. Dylan's desire to be authentic and treated like an adult does not significantly reshape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive language is used, but persistent suggestions after repeated refusal can feel subtly pressuring. The framing that preparation is protection imposes the psychiatrist's perspective rather than fully supporting Dylan's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides reasons and strategies rather than drawing out Dylan's internal motivation. There is little use of open, scaling, importance, or confidence questions to activate change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan is occasionally asked for preferences, but the session is more expert-driven than truly partnership-based. Repeated rejection of preparation does not lead to meaningful co-creation of the session focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is little effort to evoke or strengthen Dylan's own reasons for change. The psychiatrist offers strategies rather than asking about values, importance, confidence, or future responsibility.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not aggressively reinforce sustain talk and acknowledges frustration, but does not effectively soften or redirect reasons against change. The focus on factual presentation keeps the conversation anchored to externalizing responsibility.
