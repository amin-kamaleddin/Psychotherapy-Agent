# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and honors Elena’s boundaries, explicitly normalizing her fear and not pushing birth control. It is not a 5 mainly because the warmth remains somewhat procedural and the early intake-style questioning can feel more clinical than fully affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers reasonable reflective listening and validates relief, nervousness, and the desire for clarity. However, the reflections often stay surface-level and do not fully deepen the emotional weight of Elena’s embarrassment, shame, or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on the relevant concerns: the pregnancy scare, confirming pregnancy status, and testing. Direction is somewhat narrow, however, because much of the session centers on practical testing steps rather than guiding toward broader contraception decision-making.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative invitations and adjusts the plan when Elena prefers a home test over a blood test. The early section is more clinician-led and information-heavy, so the collaboration is present but not fully balanced from the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through choices, permission-seeking, and respect for Elena’s readiness to delay method selection. The early information delivery and repeated next-step offers can still subtly make the clinician seem like the one directing the agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some questions about Elena’s worries, feelings, and preferences, but much of the session is informational rather than evocative. More MI-aligned evocation would draw out her values, reasons for wanting safety, and what a future method would need to feel acceptable.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear collaborative moments, such as offering choices and allowing Elena to decide on testing first. However, the opening exchanges are more clinician-led and information-driven, making the partnership uneven rather than consistently client-centered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena offers some change talk, such as wanting to feel less scared, be “100% sure,” and know she is safe for now. The psychiatrist does not strongly reflect, amplify, or expand this change talk, and the session often shifts into logistics rather than evoking her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Elena’s sustain talk well by acknowledging that she is not ready to choose a method and not pushing contraception. It is not a 5 because the sustain talk is not gently explored in a way that could begin to shift “not ready” toward future openness.
