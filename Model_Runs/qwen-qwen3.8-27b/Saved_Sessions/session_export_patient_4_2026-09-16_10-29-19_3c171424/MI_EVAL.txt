# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Aizada’s exhaustion, fear, and need for a clear diagnosis. Acceptance is strong but somewhat focused on supporting her medical-testing request rather than broader unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Aizada’s fear, frustration, and mistrust of prior medical explanations. Empathy is good, but it could be deeper in exploring the emotional impact of dismissal by her husband and fear of cancer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Aizada’s immediate goal of obtaining tests and a referral. The psychiatrist effectively steers the conversation back to practical next steps when it briefly drifts to other topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates Aizada’s constraints and preferences when planning next steps. However, the collaboration becomes limited when the psychiatrist generates specific scripts and medical test lists rather than co-creating solutions with Aizada.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Aizada’s choices and preferences at several points. Autonomy support is weakened by drafting exact emails and scripts, which may reduce Aizada’s sense of ownership over her own communication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Aizada’s desire for medical clarity but misses a key opportunity to explore her own insight about the pain being less during her son’s wedding. This missed reflection limits the evocation of intrinsic motivation and broader coping insights.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially offers choices and invites Aizada’s input, demonstrating some partnership. The balance shifts toward an expert role when the psychiatrist drafts scripts and test lists, positioning them as the problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change talk related to obtaining tests, reducing suffering, and returning to work. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or deepen Aizada’s change talk, especially when she offers an insight about stress and coping.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aizada’s sustain talk centers on a biomedical explanation and distrust of psychological factors, and the psychiatrist largely validates this stance. There is little use of double-sided reflection or exploration of ambivalence, which limits softening of the rigid focus on medical certainty.
