# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers a respectful, validating tone with phrases like 'that makes sense' but feels procedural rather than deeply warm, overshadowed by persistent information delivery. The repeated checklist/check-in offers after clear refusals slightly undercut the supportive stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial reflection captures some ambivalence but empathy remains surface-level, missing David's deeper functional need (stress-management break) and irritation about extra burden. Key cues like 'not doing some homework assignment' were not reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Stays topically on nicotine replacement but lacks a clear MI-consistent focus, oscillating between cessation support, patch dosing, brands, and safety warnings. No effort to help David clarify a specific, personally meaningful change target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early open questions invite collaboration but quickly become information-delivery without a shared agenda. David's repeated signals not to give more input ('I'm good,' 'just tell me which box') are not fully honored, making the power balance uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges David's right to choose ('not just a scare tactic') and frames future help as optional, but repeatedly offers additional structure (checklist, check-in, repeated summaries) after clear refusals. The effect can feel like gentle pressure rather than full respect for his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few appropriate opening and exploratory questions, but no sustained follow-up to deepen David's emerging language about cost, smell, and wanting to get through shifts. The session becomes mostly didactic rather than evocative, missing opportunities to draw out his own motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few early partnership gestures, but the overall stance becomes expert-driven with rapid product options, dosing, and safety warnings. David's stated preferences (no gum, no program, no check-in) are acknowledged but not consistently honored in the session's pace and focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offers a genuine change-talk cue about cost and smell early on, but the psychiatrist does not pick up or amplify it. No scaling, importance/confidence questions, or reflective follow-up to expand his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David expresses strong sustain talk ('not quitting,' 'not a program') and the psychiatrist normalizes some of it but does not use double-sided reflections or strategic pivots. The repeated focus on patch-plus-smoking safety inadvertently keeps the conversation centered on his continued smoking rather than opening space for change.
