### Acceptance

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes Andrei’s choice and avoids pressure, especially around alarms or apps. Autonomy support is strong but less explicitly centered on Andrei as the expert in his own life beyond immediate decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and confidence scaling to evoke client-generated motivation. Evocation remains somewhat superficial, focusing on practical follow-through rather than deeper values or broader reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a clear partnership stance and invites Andrei to direct the conversation and choose options. The session is still partly clinician-led because the psychiatrist sets much of the structure and offers many options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk around buying a monitor, keeping a log, and following through. The change talk remains mainly about practical adherence rather than broader health values or alcohol reduction.

**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against Andrei’s minimizing statements and redirects toward small steps. Sustain talk is mostly bypassed rather than skillfully softened or explored, especially around alcohol and the cost of maintaining the status quo.
