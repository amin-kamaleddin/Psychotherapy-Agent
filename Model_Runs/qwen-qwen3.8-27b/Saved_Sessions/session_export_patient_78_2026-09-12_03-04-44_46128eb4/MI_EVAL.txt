# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and remains nonjudgmental, including when the client is resistant or ambivalent. Relapse is normalized and framed as meaningful without shame or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s frustration, anxiety, and ambivalence using clear and relevant reflections. Empathy could be stronger by exploring deeper feelings such as loss of control, loneliness, or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on smoking cessation and quickly narrows to a concrete, behavior-specific plan. The final plan is clear, low-intensity, and aligned with the client’s stated needs.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s preferences directly shape the session, and the counselor respects her rejections and choices. Collaboration is slightly limited because the counselor still generates many of the specific options and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client’s right to choose, avoids coercion, and treats any attempt as optional and non-punitive. The response to possible failure strongly reinforces ownership and freedom of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit some client-generated motivation, such as the goal of being less winded and the self-statement about taking a pause. However, the counselor shifts too quickly into option-generating and plan-building rather than consistently deepening internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor adopts a collaborative stance and allows the client’s ideas to guide the direction of the session. The partnership score is limited by the counselor’s tendency to propose multiple preformed solutions rather than fully deferring to the client’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes the client’s own change-related goal, willingness to try, and self-generated statement. The score is limited because the counselor does not consistently amplify change talk by exploring values, importance, confidence, or what change would make possible.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through normalization, acceptance, and avoiding argument or counter-argumentation. The counselor could use more advanced MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections, to help the client explore ambivalence more deeply.
