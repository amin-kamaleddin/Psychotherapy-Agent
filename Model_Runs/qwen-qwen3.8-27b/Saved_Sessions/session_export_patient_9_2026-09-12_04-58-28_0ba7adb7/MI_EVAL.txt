# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, reflecting the client's care and protective motivation. However, frequent shifts into problem-solving can make the acceptance feel somewhat procedural in moments of strong fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture the client's ambivalence and fear. Later emotional disclosures, such as not wanting to fail him next Saturday, receive more strategic than deeply empathic responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear target by narrowing the concern to a calm pickup and no-argument zone. The counselor steadily guides from broad concern to concrete plan and rehearsal, though occasional broader options slightly dilute focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor collaborates by co-constructing and rehearsing a pickup plan. However, much of the structure, wording, and contingencies are counselor-generated, making the power balance uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports autonomy by inviting the client to choose what feels doable and emphasizing that she cannot control the father. Specific scripts and behavioral instructions make the support feel more directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes motivation through questions and confidence scaling, and the client produces her own plan. However, heavy reliance on advice, examples, and scripts interrupts deeper evocation of the client's values and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership, as the counselor invites client input and preferences. Frequent supply of agenda, options, and scripts makes the interaction more counselor-driven than fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open and scaling questions elicit meaningful change talk, such as 'I can do that part' and increased confidence. The counselor's therapist-generated options can partially interrupt the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing and reframed into safety planning and lower-risk wording. Some resistance is managed more through practical options than deeper exploration of ambivalence.
