# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates honesty without blaming or moralizing. Warmth is generally present, but it can feel formulaic and more process-focused than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist captures the core ambivalence and practical concerns around drinking and mornings, but the reflections remain mostly factual. There are missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings, such as defensiveness, family pressure, or the meaning of drinking as stress relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior and moves strategically from goal selection to a concrete 9 PM plan. The direction is strong, though it becomes somewhat narrow around confidence scaling and behavior management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is clearly co-constructed, with the client choosing the alarm, ginger ale, five-minute rule, and follow-up method. The psychiatrist still supplies many options and leads the structure, so collaboration is strong but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client's choices, avoids pushing abstinence or emotional probing, and supports a modest, realistic plan. The repeated push to raise confidence from 5 to 7 or 8 subtly reduces autonomy by implying a preferred outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some motivation through importance, confidence, and next-step questions, but often substitutes client-generated reasons with counselor-generated options. There is limited exploration of the client's own values, benefits of change, or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and the client's choices shape the session, but the counselor frequently supplies structured lists of options. This creates a helpful yet uneven partnership that can feel more expert-driven than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps the client articulate a concrete plan and affirms progress, such as avoiding rough mornings and moving confidence up. Change talk is evoked more around action and confidence than around deeper personal reasons or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge sustain talk and acknowledges the perceived benefits of drinking. However, MI-specific softening strategies are underused, and the session often moves quickly to alternatives rather than fully processing why alcohol feels protective.
