# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but the warmth can feel procedural. Frequent instructions and practical guidance subtly reduce the sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some empathic reflections, but they are often shallow and infrequent. The deeper relational and identity concerns are not consistently tracked or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on drinking and social situations. However, the direction is inconsistent and becomes more structured intervention than guided MI.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and uses role-play, creating some collaboration. However, the counselor often leads the content and structure, making the interaction only partially collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client is occasionally given choices, but the counselor frequently provides prescriptive guidance. This can make the session feel more directive than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers more external advice than evocative questioning. The client's own motivations and values are not systematically explored or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some partnership behaviors, such as asking for choices and role-play. Overall, however, the counselor remains fairly expert-driven and directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is one useful affirmation after role-play, but the counselor rarely builds on the client's spontaneous change talk. Opportunities to expand motivation are missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against the client's concerns but does not meaningfully explore or reframe them. The client's ambivalence and sustain talk remain largely unprocessed.
