# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Linda's ambivalence and slip concerns. Acceptance is slightly weakened when the stance becomes quickly corrective or instructional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is present, especially in recognizing identity, habit, and the common social-cue slip pattern. It often remains cognitive rather than deeply reflecting Linda's fear, shame, frustration, or sense of loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays on tobacco but moves across many topics without a clear, mutually selected priority. Direction could be stronger by consolidating focus on one high-risk situation or target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone and respect for Linda's preferences, but the clinician frequently leads with clinical options and structured recommendations. Linda's own ideas do not consistently shape the session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices, respects refusals, and emphasizes Linda's pace and control. Some directive language and multiple practical instructions slightly weaken the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses evocative tools such as open questions and a confidence ruler to explore Linda's experience. However, much of the session relies on advice and recommendations rather than deeply evoking Linda's values, reasons, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership is evident through options and shared choice-making, but the conversation remains largely expert-driven. The client responds to the clinician's menu more than leading from her own priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda offers several change-related statements, and the clinician highlights some progress. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or deepen this change talk with follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is prominent, and the psychiatrist sometimes acknowledges identity and social meanings of smoking. Responses are often corrective or advisory, which can increase resistance rather than gently soften it.
