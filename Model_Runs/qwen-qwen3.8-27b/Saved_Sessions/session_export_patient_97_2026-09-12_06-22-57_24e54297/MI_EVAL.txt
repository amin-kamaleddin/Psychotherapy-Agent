# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respectful, nonjudgmental positive regard, including normalizing statements and affirming practical efforts. The main limitation is that the stance feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply relational or empathic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses adequate content reflections but does not fully capture the emotional depth of Mateo’s experience, such as fear, shame, or ambivalence. More complex, emotion-focused reflections would be needed for a stronger MI stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the target behavior and repeatedly connects planning to Mateo’s goal of staying out of trouble. The main weakness is an overemphasis on concrete logistics rather than sustained direction toward values and internal motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration because Mateo makes choices, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the options and details. The session would benefit from more consistent client-generated problem solving rather than selecting from a counselor-provided menu.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids coercion and allows Mateo to choose among options, supporting decision-making power. However, the frequent provision of specific scripts and detailed steps can weaken the sense that the motivation and plan belong primarily to Mateo.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to evoke Mateo’s own motivations early on, including open-ended questions and rulers. This is limited by a quick shift toward counselor-provided solutions rather than sustained evocation of Mateo’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo is invited to choose and shape some aspects of the plan, but the psychiatrist remains the primary generator of ideas. A stronger partnership stance would involve more open-ended questions and less counselor-led planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo offers meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it appropriately, but there are missed opportunities to deepen or amplify his own reasons. The session shifts too quickly into behavior planning without consistent development of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk and redirects toward coping strategies, but does not actively reframe or soften the sustaining thoughts. More skillful double-sided reflections would better reduce the power of sustain talk.
