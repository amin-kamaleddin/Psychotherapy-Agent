# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained nonjudgmental, respectful, and supportive of Linda’s ambivalence, caution, and prior quit attempts. Acceptance was strong but somewhat plan-focused rather than deeply affirming of her as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Linda’s practical concerns, ambivalence, and desire to avoid a large commitment. They could go deeper by addressing the emotional burden of repeated failure, self-doubt, and fear of relapse.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on tobacco use and moved from broad ambivalence to a concrete two-day reduction plan. The direction was clear, behaviorally relevant, and maintained throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options, invited Linda’s preferences, and incorporated her ideas into the plan. The amount of clinician-provided advice and structure slightly reduced the sense of fully client-led co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda’s right to choose was strongly reinforced through open-ended questions, optional support, and respect for her low-key, small-step approach. The psychiatrist avoided pressure while clearly affirming her ownership and control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited Linda’s own motivations, prior experiences, and preferred language without imposing external reasons for change. Evocation could be stronger by exploring values, confidence, hopes, and deeper internal motivation beyond the immediate plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative, with Linda’s input clearly shaping the plan and the psychiatrist avoiding an expert-driven stance. It was slightly less fully co-created because the psychiatrist still provided considerable information and structured suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively invited and affirmed Linda’s change talk, such as counting cigarettes, swapping the lunch cigarette, and calling if stuck. Change talk could have been further amplified by exploring confidence, values, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without challenge and was redirected toward a manageable, low-pressure step. More active MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or deliberate reframing, could further soften and pivot this ambivalence toward change.
