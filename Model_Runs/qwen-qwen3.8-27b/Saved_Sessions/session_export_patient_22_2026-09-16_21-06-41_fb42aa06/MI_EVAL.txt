# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and validates the patient’s ambivalence without judgment or pressure. Statements normalize her fear and social concerns while maintaining a warm, respectful stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Empathic reflections accurately capture the emotional meaning behind the patient’s statements, including ambivalence, anxiety, and desire for authenticity. The responses are nuanced and track emotional shifts throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing problematic drinking in social situations, but the focus narrows around one specific scenario and one friend. A slightly broader connection to overall alcohol reduction would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner, co-constructing the plan rather than prescribing it. The session follows the patient’s ideas and avoids imposing a therapist-driven agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the patient’s freedom, ownership, and choice throughout the conversation. There is no persuasion or directive pressure, reinforcing that the decision is entirely the patient’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws motivation from the patient’s own values, concerns, and goals rather than supplying reasons to change. Open questions help her articulate and clarify her internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the patient as the expert on her own life and follows her lead in developing practical steps. The collaborative tone consistently positions the patient as the author of her plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces the patient’s own change talk about wanting to feel more present and less wiped out. The session could be strengthened by adding more explicit scaling, readiness, or commitment questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully by validating concerns without arguing or reinforcing resistance. The response could be enhanced by more explicitly redirecting those concerns into language that supports change.
