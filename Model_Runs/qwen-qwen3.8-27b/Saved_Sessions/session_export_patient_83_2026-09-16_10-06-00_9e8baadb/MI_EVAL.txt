# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows respect and nonjudgment while validating Mai's concerns. Acceptance is clear but somewhat procedural, making it less than unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture frustration, need for clarity, and practical safety concerns. Some reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on understanding and deciding about the new medication, ending with practical options. It occasionally drifts into detailed medical explanation, reducing MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai's requests shape the session and the counselor co-creates scripts and options. Taking the physician role at the end slightly limits equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are repeatedly offered and pressure to start immediately is avoided. The counselor's expert recommendations and prescriber role can subtly shift decision authority away from Mai.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks about feelings, hopes, and preferences but relies heavily on providing information. There is limited deep elicitation of Mai's personal values, goals, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites Mai's input and collaborates on next steps and scripts. The dynamic becomes less balanced when the counselor assumes the doctor role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai generates several change statements, and the counselor affirms them. The counselor does not consistently deepen or expand her change talk toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with validation rather than argument or dismissal. The counselor often gives more information instead of strategically redirecting doubt toward change.
