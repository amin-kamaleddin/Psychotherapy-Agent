# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session. However, the stance sometimes shifts quickly from acceptance to advice, making the warmth feel more professional than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial reflections accurately capture exhaustion, worry, and guilt. As the session continues, reflections become shallower and miss deeper emotional themes such as fear of inadequacy, partner-related guilt, and ambivalence about resting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on helping the patient gain rest and create a practical nighttime plan. It is less than a 5 because the focus sometimes narrows into directive problem-solving rather than consistently following the patient’s own motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently provides the plan, details, and scripts, which limits true co-creation. While the patient’s input influences the plan, the interaction is more clinician-led than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites patient input, supporting some sense of control. However, the highly directive instructions and supplied scripts reduce the patient’s ownership of the decision-making process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited effort to draw out the patient’s internal reasons for change. Questions are mostly logistical rather than exploring values, hopes, confidence, or identity-based motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a fairly expert-driven stance by supplying solutions and detailed guidance. Although the patient contributes, she is not consistently positioned as the primary expert on her own life and preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers meaningful change talk, such as wanting to sleep and trying the 2 AM feed. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or expand this change talk with evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or dismissal, which reduces escalation. However, MI-consistent softening strategies such as double-sided reflections, normalization with pivot, or reframing are used inconsistently.
