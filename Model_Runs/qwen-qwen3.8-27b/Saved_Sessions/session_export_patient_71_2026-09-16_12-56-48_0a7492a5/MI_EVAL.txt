# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and normalizes setbacks without judgment. Praise sometimes ties the client’s worth to plan adherence, keeping the rating below perfect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s health values, readiness tension, and automatic failure response. It could go deeper into shame, exhaustion, and self-concept before moving into implementation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on aligning health values with a small, manageable after-work behavior. It consistently builds toward a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and helps the client co-create the plan. The therapist contributes a large share of the concrete content, making the collaboration slightly uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice, affirmation, and nonjudgment are strong, including permission to adjust the plan and separate identity from missed actions. Mild pressure appears in nudging readiness upward and in therapist-generated scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates useful values, readiness language, and coping choices. However, the psychiatrist often offers scripts and solutions before fully eliciting the client’s own motivations and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client chooses days, alarm wording, scripts, and backups, showing genuine partnership. The therapist supplies many concrete suggestions, making the role more like a planner than a fully equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Values, readiness, and self-love are effectively evoked, and the client offers meaningful change talk. The session moves too quickly to planning, with fewer deepening importance, ability, and need questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about work stress and fear of failure is accepted, normalized, and reframed without argument. It could use more double-sided reflection to explore the function of the concerns before offering fixes.
