# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while normalizing the client's struggle. Deeper body-image and shame-related pain receives less individualized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture frustration, exhaustion, and concern about being judged. Emotional depth could be stronger by naming underlying shame, ambivalence, and fear of doing it wrong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on a specific, actionable eating-related experiment. The plan moves clearly from concerns to concrete steps, timing, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes ideas and chooses some plan elements, but the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure and content. More client-led problem solving would strengthen the partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers options, respects preferences, and reduces pressure to comply. The detailed clinician-generated plan can subtly make the solution feel prescribed rather than fully client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces useful change talk through open questions and self-generated ideas. The clinician could do more by evoking and amplifying the client's own motivation before moving into planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some preference-sharing and flexibility, but the psychiatrist takes a leading role in shaping the experiment. More power-sharing would make the collaboration feel less expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits useful change talk and small-step motivation. Change talk is not consistently reflected, reinforced, or consolidated throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about weight, rules, and fear of failure is handled without argument and reframed as data. The plan remains heavily food-focused, which may keep attention on the triggering domain.
