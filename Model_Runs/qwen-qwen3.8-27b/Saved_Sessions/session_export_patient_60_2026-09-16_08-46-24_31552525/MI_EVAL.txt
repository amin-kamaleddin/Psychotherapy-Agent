# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Jason’s frustration and concerns about his record. Acceptance feels somewhat procedural and not fully person-centered, as it often validates minimization rather than the whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate reflections of Jason’s stated concerns, but empathy stays mostly at a surface level. It does not consistently explore or name the deeper emotions and values beneath his defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the alcohol violation and immediate record concerns, but direction is more tactical than MI-consistent. It steers toward consequence management rather than linking the behavior to Jason’s values, goals, or change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments, especially in revising the statement and asking Jason to choose between options. However, the counselor remains the primary problem-solver and advocate rather than fully co-creating the approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks for Jason’s preferences, supporting some autonomy. Directive advice and scripted instructions, however, weaken autonomy by positioning the counselor as directing Jason’s behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks some practical preference questions but does not strongly elicit Jason’s internal reasons for change. The session shifts too quickly into advice and strategy, limiting evocation of change-related language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and invites Jason’s input, showing partial partnership. The interaction remains uneven because the counselor often takes a strategist or advocate role rather than treating Jason as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to invite Jason’s goals or change-relevant language, but change talk is not consistently recognized or amplified. The counselor does not effectively explore desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** This is the major weakness: Jason’s sustain talk is repeatedly reinforced through non-admitting scripts and defensive strategy. The counselor does not effectively soften, reframe, or redirect justification, avoidance, or resistance.
