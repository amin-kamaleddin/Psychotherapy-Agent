# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental, warm tone and frequently affirms the client’s experience. A direct medical correction of the dependence fear slightly tempers full acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers accurate early reflections of ambivalence and fear. Later reflections become more formulaic and miss deeper feelings of loneliness or being dismissed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stays consistently focused on asthma management and inhaler use. The session has clear momentum, though the shared focus is not explicitly summarized or confirmed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites client input and offers choices, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the plans and tools. True collaboration is limited because the client mostly selects pre-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports client choice with permission-giving, nonjudgmental language. Some prescriptive planning slightly reduces the client’s sense of ownership over solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open questions evoke the client’s concerns, but the session quickly shifts to information-giving and problem-solving. Deep exploration of values, confidence, or internal motivations is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses partnership language and invites the client to choose options. In practice, the psychiatrist leads the content and planning, making the interaction more expert-guided than equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, such as trying a short test. These moments are not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened with MI-specific questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about dependence and being dismissed is acknowledged but often answered with correction or practical advice. The client’s ambivalence is not fully explored or softened using MI-consistent techniques.
