# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated respect, nonjudgmental support, and validation of Mateo's effort and honesty. Support was strong but somewhat task-focused, with limited deeper individualized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy was present and often accurate, especially in reflecting frustration, caution, and practical concerns. It tended to remain factual and logistical, missing opportunities to reflect deeper emotion, fatigue, or the meaning behind Mateo's pain and control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear and consistent focus on increasing physical activity, specifically a short walk within rehab. There was strong momentum toward concrete action planning with no significant topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's preferences and practical ideas were visibly incorporated into the plan, reflecting a strong collaborative stance. The interaction remained somewhat therapist-guided due to frequent structured options and proposed next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Mateo's autonomy by offering choices, respecting his pain thresholds, and honoring his decisions about monitoring and plan details. Some therapist-generated suggestions and scripts kept the process from being fully client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-generated motivation was evoked, particularly around goals, small steps, and supports. Evocation was often surface-level and did not consistently draw out Mateo's values, personal meaning, or stronger reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Mateo as a partner by asking for his input and using his ideas to shape the plan. However, the session sometimes felt more therapist-directed than fully co-created because of menu-style questions and structured choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited change-oriented language about movement, normal activities, and confidence. Change talk was often behavioral and logistical rather than deeply values-based or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's sustain talk about pain, safety, and avoiding extra check-ins was accepted and incorporated without challenge. More skillful softening strategies could have helped convert ambivalence into stronger change talk.
