# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or arguing about nonadherence. Warmth is good but remains somewhat efficient and would benefit from deeper affirmation of the client's worth independent of adherence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture Samir's tiredness, privacy needs, and fear of being tested. Deeper empathy is limited because it does not fully name embarrassment, loss of control, or family role fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a small, realistic medication-taking goal, using confidence scaling, barriers, and a clear recap. Focus is somewhat logistical and checklist-like rather than connecting the behavior to personal goals and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-created, with Samir choosing the bathroom anchor, revising wording, and setting session boundaries. Collaboration is solid but not fully client-designed because the psychiatrist offers several concrete options first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices, permission, and the client's boundary against making it a 'test' are consistently respected. It would be stronger with more explicit affirmation that Samir is in charge and can change or stop the plan without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and preference questions elicit some client input about difficulty, ease, and confidence. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist quickly moves to practical planning rather than exploring Samir's own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys collaboration by offering options and respecting Samir's choices, which shape the final plan. It is not fully client-led because the psychiatrist still generates much of the structure and details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and preference questions elicit mild desire and commitment, with confidence rising from 4 to 6. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to amplify or deepen change talk by exploring values, meaning, and personal goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Resistance is acknowledged without challenging or arguing, and the plan is adjusted to reduce burden. It remains more problem-solving than MI-style softening, lacking double-sided reflections or exploration of what sustain talk protects.
