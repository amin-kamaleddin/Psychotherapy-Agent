# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the patient without blaming. Warmth is somewhat task-focused, and the safety check slightly interrupts the empathic flow.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflections early in the session, but the psychiatrist quickly shifts to advice and practical planning. Deeper emotional themes such as shame and fear of failure receive limited reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and moves toward concrete next steps. At times, information overload dilutes the clarity of direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and helps simplify the plan, which supports collaboration. However, the session often feels more like expert guidance than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and asks for preference, supporting some autonomy. Frequent direct suggestions and premature affirmations may reduce space for the patient to own the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few evocative questions are asked early, but the session shifts quickly into advice and planning. There is limited effort to draw out the patient’s values, strengths, or confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers alternatives, but the interaction remains largely expert-driven. The patient’s own perspective is not consistently centered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient offers meaningful change talk about her daughter and improved well-being. The psychiatrist affirms this but does not consistently deepen or amplify the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some sustain talk is normalized, and the plan is simplified when the patient feels overwhelmed. Important concerns such as feeling “weirder” or replacing one addiction with another are not fully addressed.
