# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor began respectfully but remained mostly procedural, focusing affirmations on the academic task rather than the client's core alcohol risk concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor provided adequate reflections on the client's stress but lacked emotional depth, never deeply exploring how the alcohol flag impacted her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** While focused on the History essay, the counselor completely drifted away from the clinically relevant target behavior of alcohol risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely counselor-driven, frequently supplying plans, sources, and thesis statements instead of inviting the client to generate her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor used some choice-giving language for the study plan but provided heavy direction that subtly undermined the client's ownership of her own life decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor made a brief evocative opening but largely failed to draw out the client's internal motivation, values, or reasons for change regarding her drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session quickly became superficial and expert-driven, with the counselor supplying most of the structure and content rather than allowing the client to shape the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial openings to evoke change were weak and quickly abandoned as the session shifted entirely to academic task planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor missed a key opportunity to soften the client's sustain talk about her drinking by bypassing her ambivalence and immediately moving to the essay.
