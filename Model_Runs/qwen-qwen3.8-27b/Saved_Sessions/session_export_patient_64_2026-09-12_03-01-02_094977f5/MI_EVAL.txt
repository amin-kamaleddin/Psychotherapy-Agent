# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and normalizes Jordan’s ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but somewhat generic and could more explicitly affirm his autonomy and personhood beyond the vaccine decision.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates solid reflective listening and accurately captures Jordan’s core conflict between feeling healthy and wanting to protect his grandmother. Deeper emotional themes, such as self-doubt and discomfort with needing his mother, could have been explored more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and moves logically from ambivalence to a practical plan. Some question clustering and mixing of information with planning make the pacing slightly fragmented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan to choose and modify options, and Jordan actively contributes to the final plan. The collaboration is strong but partly scaffolded by clinician-generated scripts and checklists rather than more client-led solution-building.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Jordan’s sense of choice and avoids forcing a decision. Repeated offers of suggestions and drafts can subtly nudge the client toward compliance, reducing full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling and other evocative questions to help Jordan articulate his motivation, especially protecting his grandmother. The main limitation is that the psychiatrist sometimes supplies options or scripts rather than fully evoking Jordan’s own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction conveys that Jordan is the expert on his own choices, and his decisions clearly shape the session. However, the psychiatrist’s multiple options and scripts can make the interaction feel more advisory than fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through scaling and planning questions, and Jordan moves toward a concrete plan. The cultivation is not consistently skillful because the psychiatrist often supplies change language and solutions rather than fully evoking Jordan’s own reasons and abilities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Jordan’s sustain talk and reframes resistance in a nonconfrontational way. At times, direct questions and medical information risk shifting the interaction toward debate rather than softer MI-style handling.
