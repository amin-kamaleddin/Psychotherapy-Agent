# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes Erik’s concerns and lapses. It falls short of a 5 because warmth is often embedded in practical problem-solving rather than more explicitly affirming Erik’s strengths and personhood.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Erik’s ambivalence, stress, and desire for changes that meaningfully affect his health. However, reflections tend to be brief and do not fully explore the deeper emotional or identity-based meaning of the evening beer or fear of being told what to do.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a specific, clinically relevant target behavior: reducing alcohol as part of diabetes management. It occasionally broadens into medical education, and more frequent summaries tying the plan back to Erik’s values would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting input, adapting suggestions, and co-creating a simple slip rule. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still generates much of the structure and many practical options rather than more fully eliciting Erik’s own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik is repeatedly given choices, permission is sought, and the decision is framed as his own. The autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly stating that the plan is only one option and that Erik is the expert on what fits his life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used, but the session relies more on practical options and medical rationale than on eliciting and expanding Erik’s own motivations. There are missed opportunities to deepen change talk through values, confidence, and the meaning of protecting his personal time.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and allows Erik to shape the final plan. The score is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still drives a substantial portion of the structure by offering multiple options and practical suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik does express some desire and ability statements, such as trying to cut back and keeping a paper log. The psychiatrist does not consistently develop, amplify, or follow up on this change talk to build stronger internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and responds with acceptance and normalization. However, the response is often neutral, and there is less strategic use of double-sided reflections or reframing to pivot sustain talk into change talk.
