# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warm, nonjudgmental acceptance and validates Liyana’s self-critical worries without shame. Statements like “That’s not stupid—it’s a real worry” show strong MI acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate and emotionally responsive reflections that capture Liyana’s burden and ambivalence. Empathy could be slightly deeper in explicitly reflecting her underlying self-worth fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to Liyana’s concerns but becomes less focused on a specific change target. A clearer agenda check and summary linking values to a concrete self-care step would improve direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well in generating possible requests and follows Liyana’s lead. More explicit co-construction of the session goals would make the partnership more fully client-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Liyana’s sense of choice and avoids controlling language or direct advice. Her options are framed as hers to define rather than as obligations or tests.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Liyana’s own goals, values, and preferred next steps. The main gap is not consistently expanding those evoked motivations into deeper change talk, ability, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Liyana as the expert on her experience and invites her to define what help would look like. The session would be stronger if the agenda and structure were more explicitly negotiated with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions evoke Liyana’s own motivations for self-care and feeling heard. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or develop that change talk into stronger confidence or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged rather than challenged or reinforced. More active double-sided reflections or reframing would better soften barriers and redirect them toward change.
