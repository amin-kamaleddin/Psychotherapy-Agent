# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honors the client's choice, though the repeated safety/crisis statements make the language feel somewhat scripted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but remain surface-level, such as “that makes sense,” and miss the possible irritation, feeling policed, or deeper meaning of the piercing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to the piercing and dental concern, but the psychiatrist does not clearly shape a collaborative goal or strategic focus toward a specific change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements like asking about focus and offering choices, but the client is not deeply involved in co-creation and the psychiatrist delivers repeated practical advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a strong criterion; the psychiatrist consistently asks permission, offers options without pressure, and leaves the decision clearly with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative attempts are made by asking how the client feels and offering to explore meaning, but the questions remain informational and do not draw out deeper motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer asks what the client wants to focus on and offers multiple options, but the interaction is uneven because practical information is given without full co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few brief invitations to explore risks or meaning are present, but the psychiatrist does not actively elicit, amplify, or develop the client's reasons for possible change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interviewer does not argue or challenge, but also does not use double-sided reflections or strategic reframes to gently shift attention toward oral health or future concerns.
