# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with strong acceptance statements like 'I'm not here to take that from you.' The warmth remains somewhat procedural and doesn't reach deep, individualized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captures emotional nuance and goes beyond simple paraphrasing. Occasional tendency to hit the same note rather than progressing into deeper emotional territory.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Logical flow from understanding to contributors, but lacks a clear strategic trajectory toward a specific change goal. The session ends without a defined target behavior or next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative behaviors and invitations for the patient's perspective, but the psychiatrist largely drives the conversation and fills silence with questions rather than letting the patient set direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects the patient's choices with explicit autonomy-supportive language and avoids pressure or directive advice. Could more explicitly affirm the patient's capacity and frame suggestions as entirely his choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Weakest domain; questions are largely assessment-oriented rather than evoking the patient's own motivations, values, or vision for the future. No questions tapping into long-term health concerns, relationships, or personal reasons for considering change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative language and multiple invitations for the patient's perspective signal a genuine partnership stance. However, the psychiatrist retains directional control and doesn't fully let the patient's contributions shape the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to evoke change talk; the session remains in the understanding phase without strategically building toward the patient's own change language. Multiple opportunities missed to explore values, future self, or personal motivations for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without arguing or contradicting, and the reflection 'There's a quiet I know who I am' is a good move. However, the psychiatrist doesn't actively work to soften or redirect the sustain talk with double-sided reflections or strategic pivots.
