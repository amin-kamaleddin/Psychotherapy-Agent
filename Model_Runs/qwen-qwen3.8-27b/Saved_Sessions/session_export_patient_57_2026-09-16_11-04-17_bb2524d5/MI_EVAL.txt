# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Jamal’s disclosures without shaming. Mildly evaluative language such as “smart” or “solid move” and limited normalization of teen athlete ambivalence prevent a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The reflections accurately capture Jamal’s tension between fitting in with the team and protecting his playing future. Empathy could be strengthened by more deeply reflecting emotional and social concerns such as embarrassment, being labeled, or fear of social exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on reducing alcohol involvement in team-related social settings. The clinician consistently guides the conversation toward a concrete, low-risk plan for Thursday.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-created, incorporating Jamal’s preferences, realistic constraints, and his own ideas. It falls short of a 5 because the clinician still supplies many options and drives much of the structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly leaves choices with Jamal, respects his privacy, and supports a client-controlled “not tonight” exit. The detailed plan and multiple clinician-generated options subtly reduce the sense that Jamal is fully leading the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness rulers and evocative questions elicit some motivation and help Jamal move from 3 to 4. Deeper exploration of his values, player identity, and internal reasons for change is limited before moving into practical planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician asks for Jamal’s input and allows his preferences to shape the plan, including the drink, ally, line, and exit strategy. A fully client-led partnership would have waited longer before offering a menu of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some change talk, including Jamal’s care about playing and increased readiness. However, the session moves quickly into concrete options without consistently amplifying or expanding Jamal’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician accepts Jamal’s sustain talk without arguing or invalidating his need to fit in. There is limited active softening through double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to hold ambivalence and redirect it toward change.
