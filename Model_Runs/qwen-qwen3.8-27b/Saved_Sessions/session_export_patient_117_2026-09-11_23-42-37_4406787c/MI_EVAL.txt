# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized cravings and slips. Acceptance was solid but remained somewhat procedural rather than deeply affirming Samuel’s worth beyond his behavior change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist recognized Samuel’s practical concerns, stress, and fear of failure, but often moved into advice rather than fully reflecting his frustration or ambivalence. Empathy was adequate but not consistently nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking reduction, the post-meeting cigarette, and practical implementation. Direction was clear but more behavior-focused than motivation-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporated Samuel’s preferences and simplified the plan based on his input. However, many strategies were clinician-generated rather than fully co-created from Samuel’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used optional language, respected Samuel’s boundaries, and avoided pressuring him to adopt complex steps. Autonomy support was strong, though not consistently framed as Samuel’s full ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently provided strategies, reasons, and solutions instead of eliciting Samuel’s own motivations and values. Questions were mostly logistical, limiting development of deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Samuel’s input and adjusted to his preferences. Partnership was present, but the degree of directive advice kept the interaction from being fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforced Samuel’s progress and specific weekly goal, which supported some change language. Change talk was not deeply cultivated because there were fewer questions drawing out Samuel’s own reasons and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Slips and worries were reframed as normal data rather than failure, which reduced shame. The session still centered heavily on obstacles and did not consistently turn sustain talk into an opportunity for self-directed change.
