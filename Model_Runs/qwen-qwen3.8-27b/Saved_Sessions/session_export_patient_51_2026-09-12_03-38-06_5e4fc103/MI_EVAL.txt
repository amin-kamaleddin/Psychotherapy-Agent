# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, validating the patient’s concern and initiative. The regard becomes slightly conditional at times because of repeated directives and affirmations tied to doing the "right thing."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The response recognizes worry and time pressure, but remains largely surface-level and content-focused. It misses deeper feelings, ambivalence, and the emotional complexity of the situation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the medication-safety issue and offers a concrete, usable question for the GP. The main weakness is a circular tendency rather than efficient movement toward client-owned closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and accepts the patient’s plan to use her own notes, but the interaction often becomes expert-driven. More equal partnership would involve using the patient’s brief, direct style to guide closure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some autonomy support through language like “at your pace” and respect for the patient’s notes. However, repeated directives and continued offers after the patient repeatedly asks to leave weaken autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions but provides more safety information than space for the patient to articulate her own motivations. More evocation of her values, confidence, and reasons for safe use would strengthen MI.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient’s perspective and respects her preference to use her own notes, but much of the session remains provider-led. A stronger partnership would rely more on the patient’s own plan as the organizing framework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the patient’s safe decision-making and GP follow-up plan. However, it does not consistently expand, summarize, or deepen the patient’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges time pressure and avoids arguing with the patient’s need to leave. Still, continuing to add reminders and offers after repeated exit statements keeps focus on the barrier rather than a cleaner close.
