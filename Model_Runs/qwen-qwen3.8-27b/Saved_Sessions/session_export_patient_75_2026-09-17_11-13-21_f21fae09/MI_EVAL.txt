# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent validation of Linda's experience. Slightly formulaic in its warmth without a moment of deeper, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate and nuanced reflections that capture emotional meaning beyond surface paraphrasing, including the cognitive mechanism behind Linda's anxiety. Evidenced by Linda's spontaneous insight that she didn't previously recognize.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on asthma medication adherence with a coherent progressive arc from daily struggle to specific actionable insights. Slight drift to doctor communication but clinically reasonable as a secondary barrier.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue between equals with the psychiatrist positioning as a partner in exploration rather than an expert providing guidance. Linda's contributions are acknowledged as the valuable knowledge driving the session forward.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports Linda's right to choose and never pressures toward a decision. Lacks an explicit statement affirming Linda's ownership and right to make any choice she wants.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook MI evocation with strategic questions that draw out Linda's own motivations, insights, and solutions. Linda generates nearly all key insights and solutions herself, which is the hallmark of exceptional evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance with language like 'I'm curious' and 'What do you think' that respects Linda's expertise on her own experience. Could be slightly stronger with more explicit power-sharing language acknowledging Linda's self-determination as an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits multiple change talk statements across desire, reasons, and ability categories. Strong but not maximally systematic in developing DARN-CAT categories or explicitly asking for commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully acknowledges the validity of Linda's fears while consistently redirecting toward exploration of what would help. Reframes sustain talk into problem-solving opportunity rather than a reason to avoid treatment; could be more explicit in normalizing ambivalence.
