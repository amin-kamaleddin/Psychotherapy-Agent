# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, affirming Marco's right to his own life and never judging or pressuring. Acceptance is specific and individualized, normalizing skepticism without treating resistance as a problem to solve.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture the emotional and meaningful core of Marco's statements rather than simply paraphrasing, naming underlying feelings, values, and identity themes. The psychiatrist accurately tracks subtle shifts in language and emotional state with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a logical progression from resistance to the specific physical symptom to the possibility of action, maintaining clear focus on smoking cessation. Direction is slightly less strategic in the later portion, as the conversation could have been more purposefully guided toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine dialogue between equals, with the psychiatrist consistently using inclusive, non-hierarchical language and borrowing Marco's own metaphors. Both parties are treated as contributors of useful knowledge, and the power dynamic remains consistently even.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is explicit and consistent, with the psychiatrist never prescribing a plan or implying Marco 'should' do anything. The interaction is structured around options and Marco's right to maintain his current position while creating space for change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never provides a single reason for change; every insight and emotional articulation emerges from Marco's own mouth through precisely calibrated questions. Evocation is strategic and progressive, building Marco's change talk without any direct prompting or suggestion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist immediately positions Marco as the expert on his own experience and consistently uses phrases that level the power dynamic. The session direction is clearly shaped by Marco's contributions, with reflections returning to his language rather than imposing clinical frameworks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marco produces increasingly significant change statements, moving from initial resistance to 'I'm not saying no,' with each shift evoked rather than directed. The psychiatrist skillfully names and holds Marco's shifts, making him aware of his own movement without pushing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Marco's sustain talk without reinforcing it, and effectively reframes the contrast between the dramatic 'can't' scenario and the gradual shift already happening. Could have more actively used double-sided reflections, and the final 'Don't get too excited' is acknowledged but left slightly unaddressed.
