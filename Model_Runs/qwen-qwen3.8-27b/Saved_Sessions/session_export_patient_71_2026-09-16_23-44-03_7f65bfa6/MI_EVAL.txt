# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warm, nonjudgmental regard and normalizes fatigue, forgetting, and inconsistency. Rest and difficulty are reframed as acceptable parts of the process rather than failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and nuanced, capturing meaning, affect, and underlying concerns. The psychiatrist skillfully reflects tensions between wanting health alignment and fearing added obligation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior: a small, value-congruent after-work practice. The conversation stays connected to specific actions, language, and a soft return plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient's participation and builds on her ideas rather than imposing solutions. Collaboration is strong but remains somewhat guided by the therapist's question sequence, limiting it from a perfect 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exceptional, with repeated emphasis on choice, flexibility, and permission to modify or abandon the practice. The psychiatrist preserves patient ownership while gently suggesting an optional one-week trial.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes motivation by asking open-ended questions that help the patient generate her own meaning, wording, and plan. Evocation could be deeper by more systematically eliciting and amplifying desire, ability, need, commitment, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely conveys that expertise resides within the patient and invites her to evaluate her own ideas. Partnership is strong but could be more fully equal through explicit agenda negotiation and invitations for the patient to steer the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits change-oriented language about benefits, self-ownership, and practical commitment. It is slightly below a 5 because key forms of change talk are not all explicitly drawn out and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk and ambivalence are handled very well without argument, dismissal, or defensiveness. Concerns are reframed as invitations, permissions, and realistic parts of a sustainable process.
