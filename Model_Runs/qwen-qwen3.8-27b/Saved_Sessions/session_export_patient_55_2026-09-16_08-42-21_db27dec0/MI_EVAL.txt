# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, with no blaming or shaming language. However, the opening feels more administrative than deeply warm, so acceptance is positive but somewhat functional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Isabelle's content and preferences, such as her desire for a simple, low-drama exit. Empathy remains mainly at the content level and misses opportunities to explore underlying emotions, identity, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target issue of alcohol-related lateness and its impact on band and grades. Each exchange consistently moves toward a concrete, behaviorally relevant plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabelle's input shapes the final plan, especially the alarm, sax case, vibration, and visible placement. Collaboration is limited because the counselor generates much of the content and presents many options for her to choose from.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally respects Isabelle's choices, avoids pressure, and accepts her preference against extra steps. It could be stronger by more clearly emphasizing that she is in charge and no additional step is required.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some useful MI questions, including an initial focus question and a confidence ruler. However, the session moves too quickly into problem-solving, limiting deeper exploration of Isabelle's values, motivations, and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Isabelle to choose among options and builds the plan around some of her ideas. Partnership is uneven because the counselor remains the primary agenda-setter and problem-solver rather than following Isabelle's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some change talk related to protecting band, grades, and avoiding lateness. Deeper exploration of Isabelle's values, identity, confidence, and reasons for change would strengthen this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue with or amplify Isabelle's minimization, and acknowledges her preference for a low-drama approach. However, there is limited strategic reframing or use of double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk.
