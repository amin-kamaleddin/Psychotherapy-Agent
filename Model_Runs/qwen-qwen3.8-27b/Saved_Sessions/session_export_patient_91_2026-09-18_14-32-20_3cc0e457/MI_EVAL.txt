# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, never shaming or labeling. However, acceptance occasionally leans toward strategic reframing (e.g., quickly pivoting to a reframe rather than fully sitting in the pain of an identity shift), preventing a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptionally accurate and deep empathic understanding, consistently identifying the emotional and meaning-level content beneath Marcus's words. Each reflection is generative, opening new doors for deeper disclosure rather than merely mirroring surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical focus on blood sugar monitoring, progressing from overwhelm to a concrete, owned plan. The session occasionally broadens into identity-level exploration that, while therapeutically valuable, briefly dilutes the specific behavioral focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuine collaborative dynamic where decisions are co-constructed and all major insights are generated by Marcus. The psychiatrist never positions as an authority—there is no information-giving, no expert advice, no top-down guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports Marcus's autonomy and ownership, with all plans and solutions generated by the client. The psychiatrist never uses directive language and explicitly frames suggestions as questions, reinforcing that the plan is 'yours, not something I'm assigning.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every major insight—the 'walk for ten minutes' plan, the 'one check before bed' commitment, the device placement strategy, the accountability idea—is elicited from Marcus rather than provided. The psychiatrist never fills in information that Marcus could generate himself, making this exemplary evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Marcus, explicitly ceding authority and never assuming the expert role. The session is structured as Marcus leading the content while the psychiatrist facilitates by reflecting, amplifying, and asking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully cultivates escalating change talk, guiding Marcus from 'I can't do this' to 'not fixed, but maybe not impossible' to 'I could try just one check before bed.' Each piece of change talk is explicitly named, amplified, and attributed back to Marcus's own agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles all sustain talk with remarkable skill—validating the feeling first, then gently redirecting toward change without arguing, minimizing, or dismissing. Sustain talk is consistently met with acceptance followed by a change-oriented question, never with persuasion.
