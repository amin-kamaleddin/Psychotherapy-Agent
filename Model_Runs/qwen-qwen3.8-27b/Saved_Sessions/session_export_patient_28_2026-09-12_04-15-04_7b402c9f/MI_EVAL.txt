# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids blame or pressure. Acceptance could be deepened by explicitly affirming the client’s loss or identity struggle around the old exercise routine.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and captures the client’s confusion, ambivalence, and self-doubt. More emotionally focused reflections on loss, fear of failure, or value conflict would make the empathy stronger.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity. It moves purposefully from vague ambivalence to a concrete, low-pressure plan with little topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor collaborates effectively by building on the client’s ideas, such as the 3-minute walk, shoes by the door, and partner nudge. The session would be more fully client-led if the counselor generated fewer options before inviting the client’s own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses choices, low-pressure language, and respects the client’s need to avoid overcommitting. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicit reinforcement of the client’s ownership over the decision and outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client-generated motivation and confidence, but often shifts into advising and structured problem-solving. More elicitation of the client’s values, reasons, and self-efficacy would improve MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and incorporates the client’s contributions into the plan. The partnership is slightly limited by the counselor’s frequent introduction of options, ratings, and structured steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers some meaningful change talk, such as recognizing that a smaller step feels less pressuring. The counselor could more consistently evoke, reflect, and amplify the client’s own desire, ability, reasons, and commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges concerns without arguing or reinforcing sustain talk, but sometimes responds with reassurance or advice. More double-sided reflections and ambivalence-based responses would better soften sustain talk in an MI-consistent way.
