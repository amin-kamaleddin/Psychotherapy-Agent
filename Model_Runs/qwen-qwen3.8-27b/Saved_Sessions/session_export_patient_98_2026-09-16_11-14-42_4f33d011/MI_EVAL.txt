# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally conveys respect, appreciation, and a nonjudgmental stance toward Mateo’s effort and ambivalence. However, frequent evaluative praise such as “sensible,” “practical,” and “wise” can make acceptance feel more approving than fully nonjudgmental.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reflects Mateo’s goals and concerns accurately, showing adequate understanding of the content. Empathic responses remain mostly surface-level and do not consistently explore deeper feelings such as fatigue, fear, or loss of independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of increasing short walks and rehab progress. The main limitation is that the focus becomes quite procedural and narrow, more like action planning than MI-guided change work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Mateo’s input and builds the plan around his choices, such as walking with Maria, using the cane, and the 30-second pause. Collaboration is slightly limited by the clinician frequently offering pre-selected options and driving the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports Mateo’s choice, control, and ownership of decisions around the plan. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by repeated suggestions and evaluative endorsements of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses some evocative techniques, including scaling and questions about what matters most and what would help. Evocation is limited because ideas are often supplied before fully exploring Mateo’s own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a reasonable collaborative stance and incorporates Mateo’s ideas into the plan. Partnership is weakened by an advisory tone and repeated labeling of the plan as sensible, practical, smart, or wise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo expresses desire, ability, and commitment, and the clinician elicits some of it through scaling and planning questions. The clinician does not consistently deepen or amplify Mateo’s change talk or explore the personal meaning of the goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges Mateo’s pain, discouragement, and doubts without arguing or dismissing them. Sustain talk is not strongly softened using MI-consistent techniques such as double-sided reflections or deeper ambivalence exploration.
