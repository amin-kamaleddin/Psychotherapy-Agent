# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, using language that reduces defensiveness. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic and not deeply individualized to Viktor’s ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture surface feelings and practical concerns, especially skepticism about being sent by his wife. The psychiatrist misses deeper emotions and underlying ambivalence about control, pride, and conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins on the referral issue but shifts mainly to managing fights with a timeout word. The target behavior of examining or reducing drug use is not maintained as the central MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and builds on Viktor’s preference for a simple timeout plan. However, much of the plan is supplied by the psychiatrist, making the collaboration moderate rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Viktor did not choose to come and offers choices rather than demanding change. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated detailed directives and specific behavioral instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only limited questions elicit Viktor’s own values, motivations, or reasons for change. The session moves too quickly into practical advice, leaving Viktor’s internal reasons for change underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative gestures, but the psychiatrist quickly assumes a consultative, plan-giving role. Viktor is not consistently positioned as the expert on his own marriage, values, and preferred approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few questions probe the “not a big deal” stance, but they risk sounding challenging. Small change talk is not consistently reflected, amplified, or developed into stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Viktor offers strong sustain talk, and the psychiatrist avoids arguing but often reflects it in ways that reinforce the status quo. Opportunities to use double-sided reflections or gently pivot sustain talk are missed.
