# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonblaming, but the interaction feels procedural rather than warmly accepting of Kieran as a person. Acceptance is shown through tolerance of disengagement rather than clear unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately name frustration, boredom, and a sense of obligation, but they remain surface-level. They do not consistently deepen understanding of ambivalence, resentment, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays focused on forms, scheduling, and safety compliance without a clear motivational direction toward engagement or meaningful participation. It lacks summaries, agenda-setting, or goal-focused momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some inclusive language and choices are offered, but the psychiatrist defines the available options and controls the administrative flow. Kieran’s role remains largely passive rather than genuinely collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options, preferences, and “you can pause anytime” provide some autonomy support, but implementing a plan after the client says not to choose reinforces a controlling environment. Support for autonomy is moderate but uneven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial open question is not followed by deeper motivational exploration, and most questions are administrative. Small statements such as “I’ll be there” are not used to elicit internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician offers practical choices and avoids confrontation, but quickly shifts to a clinician-led plan rather than treating the client as an equal partner. Kieran is not clearly positioned as the expert on his own goals or values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to elicit, reflect, or amplify client-generated motivation for engagement. “I’ll show up” is treated as logistical confirmation instead of being developed as possible change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but does not effectively pivot sustain talk toward change, values, or exceptions. Responses reduce the session to paperwork and accommodation, prolonging focus on why things are pointless.
