# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental validation of the client’s work, family, and adherence challenges. This is held at 4/5 because acceptance relies somewhat on familiar MI phrases and could include broader, more individualized affirmations of the client’s worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Empathy is highly accurate and nuanced, capturing both the practical demands and emotional meanings of the client’s statements, including guilt, fatigue, hunger, and fear of another chore. The reflections remain specific and developmentally attuned throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on a specific, manageable target behavior: improving lunch choices to reduce afternoon hunger and impulsive eating. The conversation progresses logically from identifying the hardest routine component to a concrete, client-defined behavioral plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a strongly collaborative stance, using team-oriented language and inviting the client’s input at every step. The final plan is clearly co-constructed rather than prescribed by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through optional, non-punitive framing and respect for the client’s stated limits, including no tracking and no pressure to succeed perfectly. The client remains clearly in charge of whether and how she tries the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client’s own motivations, values, and preferences rather than supplying reasons for change. Client-generated language drives the formation of both the action plan and the definition of success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Partnership is consistently expressed through invitations for the client to define the problem, identify barriers, and choose what is doable. The psychiatrist avoids an expert-authority stance and treats the client as the authority on her own life and constraints.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully cultivated through open, evocative questions that elicit the client’s own desires, abilities, needs, and commitments. The client moves from ambivalence to a concrete, self-endorsed trial goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argumentation, moralizing, or escalation. The psychiatrist softens the emotional weight of doubt and anticipated failure by reframing the goal as low-pressure, guilt-free, and optional.
