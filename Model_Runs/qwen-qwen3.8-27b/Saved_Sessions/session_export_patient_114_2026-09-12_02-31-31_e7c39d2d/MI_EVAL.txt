# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided labeling, which reduced shame. Positive regard felt somewhat procedural rather than deeply affirming of Ryan’s values or strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used accurate and complex reflections of ambivalence and worry, especially early in the session. Empathy became more summary-like and task-focused, missing opportunities to explore deeper fears, shame, or social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor kept the session focused on the conduct incident and academic consequences, preventing drift. However, the MI target of drinking behavior was not consistently maintained when Ryan declined to explore it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor incorporated Ryan’s feedback while drafting the statement and requested his preferences on tone and next steps. Collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist supplied most of the plan and strategic direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ryan was regularly given control, choices, and the ability to skip questions, which was a meaningful strength. Autonomy support was limited by the counselor proposing the main plan and drafting the response.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor used some open questions but relied heavily on closed, factual questions related to the conduct process. Core evocation of Ryan’s reasons, values, and motivations for change was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited choices, asked permission, and offered options, creating some collaborative structure. The session became expert-driven as the counselor supplied the plan and practical recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor made only weak attempts to evoke change talk and did not follow up when Ryan minimized or declined to explore drinking. Ryan’s own change-related statements were not consistently reflected, amplified, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing but often accommodated Ryan’s minimization, such as agreeing to avoid implying a drinking problem. There was little use of double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance to move sustain talk toward change.
