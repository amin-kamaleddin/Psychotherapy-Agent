### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early portion shows appropriate collaborative behavior with open questions and exploratory framing. The three consecutive identical responses to Linda's frustrated feedback demonstrate a failure to adapt and respond to the client as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes autonomy-supportive language early ('I'm not here to convince you otherwise,' 'No pressure, just curiosity'). However, ignoring Linda's explicit boundary and continuing to push the same direction is a significant autonomy violation in practice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocative questions elicit meaningful self-insight, particularly around the cigarette as 'the one thing that's just mine.' The psychiatrist fails to follow up on this insight with deeper exploration and the repeated prompting in the final exchanges is mechanical rather than genuinely evocative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriate partnership language early ('No pressure, just curiosity'). The dynamic shifts to an interrogation-like posture in the final exchanges, which Linda explicitly perceives as pressure, and the psychiatrist does not acknowledge or address this feedback.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk around Linda's concern for being a role model and her self-insight about what the cigarette represents. The psychiatrist does not consistently build on these moments with follow-up questions that deepen or amplify the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges ambivalence with 'Both of those are real, and neither one makes you wrong.' The repeated identical response to Linda's sustain talk is mechanical and actually reinforces her defensive posture rather than strategically redirecting or reframing it.
