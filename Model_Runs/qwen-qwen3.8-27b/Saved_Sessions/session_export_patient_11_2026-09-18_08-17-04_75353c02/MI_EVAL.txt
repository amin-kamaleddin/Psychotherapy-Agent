# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment through multiple affirmations and normalizations (e.g., 'That's not nothing,' 'you don't have to figure it all out at once'). Falls just short of a 5 because the warmth is closely tied to specific behaviors and insights rather than maintaining a consistent, overarching tone of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional complex reflections that capture the emotional and meaning-level content with remarkable accuracy, going far beyond surface paraphrases. Key reflections (e.g., 'the jar of salsa became this symbol of being fragile,' 'the walk isn't really about the body getting stronger — it's about the act of choosing itself') demonstrate genuine understanding rather than performance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior (increasing physical activity) throughout the entire session with purposeful pacing. Each intervention moves the conversation one step closer to concrete action while remaining entirely client-paced, without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuine collaborative experience structured as joint exploration rather than an interview. The psychiatrist builds on Amira's own language and metaphors, uses reflective questions that invite co-construction of meaning, and never interrupts or redirects before Amira has finished developing an idea.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit autonomy support woven into the fabric of every interaction, from removing pressure ('you don't have to figure it all out at once') to affirming ownership ('it's genuinely yours'). The psychiatrist never uses advice, persuasion, or implicit pressure, and validates Amira's right to not follow through on some days without trying to fix it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation throughout; the psychiatrist never states a single reason why Amira should exercise, and all benefits, rationales, and coping strategies are generated by Amira herself. Strategic open questions and future-oriented prompts elicit vivid imagery and self-generated motivation without any external input.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Amira as the expert on her own life and change process, with plans and solutions emerging entirely from the client. The psychiatrist's role is strictly to reflect, validate, and elicit — never to direct, instruct, or offer external expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces rich change talk across multiple dimensions (desire, reasons, ability, commitment) through strategic questions and amplifying reflections. Falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist could have more systematically explored Amira's confidence and ability (e.g., using a confidence ruler) and more deliberately elicited additional change talk dimensions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk without reinforcement by validating the inner voice's presence, reflecting Amira's own solution back to her, and normalizing imperfection without redirect. Could have pushed slightly further by using a double-sided reflection on the perfectionism trap or exploring the inner critic more deeply.
