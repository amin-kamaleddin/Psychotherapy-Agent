# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows unconditional positive regard and honors Claudia’s ambivalence without judgment. The tone is warm, respectful, and individualized, with no moralizing about her wine use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures Claudia’s values, ambivalence, and emotional texture. It could be deepened by exploring underlying feelings such as safety concerns, low mood, loneliness, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing evening alcohol use and identifying specific high-risk moments. The work moves strategically from ambivalence to concrete transition points and a client-defined success signal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative, with Claudia shaping key ideas such as the cup strategy, morning clarity, and being present. A higher score would require more explicit shared agenda-setting and less clinician-led sequencing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Claudia’s right to choose and avoids pressure toward clinical guidelines. Language like “if you decide to,” “you’re not promising perfection,” and “it’s yours, not the doctor’s” reinforces ownership and autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within Claudia rather than supplied by the psychiatrist. Open-ended questions help her articulate her own reasons for change, especially around mornings, presence, and self-awareness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Claudia as the expert on her own life and invites her to define meaningful signals and strategies. More explicit power-sharing, such as asking what she wants to prioritize first, would strengthen partnership further.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and reinforces Claudia’s change talk around trying a pause, using the cup idea, and noticing mornings. It would be stronger if the psychiatrist waited longer for client elaboration and more explicitly evoked confidence, importance, or readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully, with strong double-sided reflections that honor the value of wine while linking to change-compatible meaning. It could improve with more consistent and immediate pivots toward Claudia’s own values.
