# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids labeling or challenging the client. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic, with less explicit warmth toward Kenji as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures ambivalence and the client’s low-pressure concerns effectively. Some deeper emotional experiences, such as feeling stuck, tired, or lonely, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on after-work drinking and a small initial pause. Direction becomes repetitive at times and does not clearly consolidate one agreed-upon target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas drive much of the conversation, and the psychiatrist generally builds on his suggestions. Multiple consecutive counselor turns and several generated options reduce the sense of full co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms choice, avoids pressure, and frames the plan as a fair experiment. Autonomy support would be stronger if ownership of both trying and not trying was made even more explicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated change ideas and uses open questions. Evocation remains mostly behavior-focused and does not deeply explore values, importance, confidence, or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and lets Kenji’s ideas shape the plan. The interaction becomes somewhat therapist-led when several suggestions are offered in sequence without enough client response.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively supports practical change ideas such as a five-minute pause or using an existing habit. Change talk could be deepened by exploring importance, values, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and responds with balanced, autonomy-affirming reflections. Some sustain talk is not fully pivoted into a double-sided reflection that honors both resistance and emerging change.
