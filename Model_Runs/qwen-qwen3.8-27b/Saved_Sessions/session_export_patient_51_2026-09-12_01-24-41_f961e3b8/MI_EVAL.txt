# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist opened with an unnecessary psychiatric screening that did not match Ingrid's stated need for a brief medication question. The tone was not overtly judgmental, but it failed to show individualized respect or acceptance of her time-pressed situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not reflect or validate Ingrid's repeated statements about stress, time pressure, or the need to leave. Responses were purely informational and lacked emotional attunement or perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began with a major directional mismatch by launching into a crisis screen for a specific drug-interaction question. After the redirect, the psychiatrist stayed on topic but focused on delivering information rather than supporting the patient's clear, narrow goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The interaction was one-way and expert-driven, with no meaningful invitation for the patient to participate. The psychiatrist did not build on patient input, share control, or engage in joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relied heavily on directives and repeated warnings rather than affirming the patient's choice and ownership. There was limited deference to the prescriber, but the overall approach remained controlling rather than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked no evocative questions to elicit the patient's own motivations, values, or reasons for safety. The session was almost entirely counselor-provided information rather than patient-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There was no genuine sense of a shared agenda or collaborative relationship. The psychiatrist continued adding information even as the patient repeatedly expressed the need to leave, undermining partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not affirm, reflect, or amplify Ingrid's proactive concern about taking the medications together. No effort was made to elicit or strengthen her own reasons for safe medication use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** When Ingrid said the food restrictions felt like too much to keep track of, the psychiatrist did not reflect or explore the tension. Instead, the response added an even longer list of restrictions, which likely increased the perceived burden.
