# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, non-punitive stance and accepts Thomas’s perspective and choices. Minor evaluative language and formulaic “I hear you” responses keep it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at the content level, capturing Thomas’s view that the issue is not serious. Empathy remains limited because it does not deeply explore his underlying self-image, autonomy, or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and ends with a clear safety plan: a full blackout triggers not driving and contacting support. Direction is strong, though at times it narrows into pushing external structure rather than exploring the client’s own priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and co-constructs the final plan, allowing Thomas’s choices to shape the outcome. Collaboration is slightly uneven because the clinician frequently supplies specific options and strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly supports Thomas’s right to choose and respects his decision to rely on his own judgment. Frequent suggestions of limits, alarms, and check-ins create some subtle pressure despite the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks about readiness, possible changes, and warning signs, eliciting some client-generated commitment language. Evocation is adequate but does not consistently draw out Thomas’s deeper values, self-concept, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Thomas is invited to contribute and the session is framed as exploratory, supporting a collaborative stance. Partnership is limited by the psychiatrist’s repeated advice-giving and tendency to set much of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are attempts to elicit change talk, including questions about possible change, readiness, and warning signs. Change talk is intermittent and not deeply developed, especially regarding Thomas’s own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and acknowledges Thomas’s preference to wait and see. However, repeated suggestions of external controls may inadvertently strengthen his sustain talk around autonomy and self-reliance.
