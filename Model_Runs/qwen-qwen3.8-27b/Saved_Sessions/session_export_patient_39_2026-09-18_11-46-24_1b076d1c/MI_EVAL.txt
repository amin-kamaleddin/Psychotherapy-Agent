# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and unconditional positive regard throughout; reframes Elias's self-labels as adaptive rather than deficient (e.g., 'You're not being lazy; you're protecting yourself'). The acceptance is genuine and individualized, specifically addressing Elias's fears and self-perceptions without minimization or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate complex reflections that capture both desire and frustration simultaneously, as well as nuanced self-deprecation and quiet self-worth in a single sentence. The psychiatrist demonstrates deep attunement to emotional shifts and goes beyond surface content to capture underlying meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains impeccable focus on the target behavior (physical activity) with a clear strategic arc from past experience through planning to commitment. Each exchange builds logically on the previous one without drift, pacing is patient but purposeful toward a concrete, actionable goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-created with Elias generating the ideas and the psychiatrist reflecting, amplifying, and refining. However, the session structure (exploration → planning → commitment) remains therapist-guided, and there is less explicit negotiation of agenda or shared decision-making about direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question is framed in terms of Elias's own judgment, and the psychiatrist never prescribes, directs, or implies a particular action is required. The entire plan (driveway, note, soreness check) is generated by Elias, with autonomy reinforced at every step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Elias rather than providing external reasons for change, using strategically designed open questions throughout. The closing question is particularly powerful, leading Elias to articulate his own insight: 'I'm not waiting to be good at something first. I just... go.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Elias as the expert on his own life and uses open, exploratory questions rather than directive ones. Could be deepened by more explicit acknowledgment of shared uncertainty or by co-creating the agenda more explicitly at the outset.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently evokes and reinforces change talk, amplifying Elias's commitment statements and reinforcing ownership of progress. Could have pushed further on confidence and ability statements, such as exploring what would happen if he felt a little off the next morning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully validates concerns without reinforcing them as reasons to remain inactive, using double-sided reflections that acknowledge sustain talk while gently framing it within a context that allows for change. The relatively limited sustain talk in this session reduces the opportunity to demonstrate the full range of softening skills.
