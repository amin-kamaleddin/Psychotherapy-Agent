# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Hana’s ambivalence, coffee-related smoking, and self-consciousness. It falls just short of 5 because the acceptance remains somewhat clinical rather than deeply affirming her strength in managing a long-standing habit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Hana’s worry, habit, and ambivalence, including the coffee-smoking link and concern about ruining her morning. The main limitation is that the reflections stay somewhat cognitive rather than fully exploring the emotional weight of losing a familiar ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation and narrows the conversation to the coffee trigger and a small experiment. It is not yet a 5 because of some redundancy and incomplete consolidation into a fully client-owned plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with Hana’s choices guiding the focus, strategy, and alternatives. It remains somewhat instructive when the psychiatrist provides multiple options and factual information, limiting a fully equal-partner dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through open invitations, confidence ratings, and low-pressure experimentation. It is held back from 5 because the prescriptive menu of strategies and brief psychoeducation can reduce emphasis on Hana’s own agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and self-evaluation through confidence ratings and questions about what would make trying easier. However, frequent advice-giving and education reduce the overall evocative quality of the interview.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Hana’s input and allows her preferences to shape the session, such as focusing on coffee, delay, and gum or lozenge options. It is strong but not yet fully egalitarian because the counselor remains somewhat expert-driven in offering suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including curiosity, willingness to test a small change, and the idea that regretting trying is better than not trying. More evocative questions about values, importance, future self, and ability would be needed for a higher score.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged as a real barrier rather than reinforced, particularly around the coffee-smoking ritual and long-standing habit. The response is mostly reassurance and problem-solving rather than active softening through double-sided reflections or gentle pivots.
