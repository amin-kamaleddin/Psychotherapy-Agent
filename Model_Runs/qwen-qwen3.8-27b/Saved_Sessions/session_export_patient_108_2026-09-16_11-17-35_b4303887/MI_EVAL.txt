# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers affirming statements that validate Oliver’s experience. Some affirmations feel generic or mildly interpretive, preventing a higher rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Oliver’s ambivalence, guilt, and fear of damaging the friendship. There are missed opportunities to go deeper into the emotional meaning of his self-critical beliefs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of asserting a boundary and setting a move-out date. The pacing is practical and goal-directed, moving from exploration to role-play and a concrete message.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on problem-solving, role-play, and plan-building while incorporating Oliver’s preferences. The counselor remains somewhat the designer of options and the final message, limiting full collaborative equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Oliver is repeatedly invited to choose wording, date, role, and next steps, supporting his sense of agency. Providing specific scripts and coaching cues is helpful but can subtly reduce his ownership of the final language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some motivation through role-play and choice-making, especially in maintaining the 15th. Much of the change language is guided by counselor-provided scripts rather than fully drawn out from Oliver’s own values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver influences the session by choosing the date, role-play format, and support offer. The repeated structured options and counselor-generated scripts make the interaction feel more counselor-led than fully partner-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver produces useful change talk during the role-play, such as “I need the 15th” and “the date has to stay.” However, much of this is supported by scripts provided by the psychiatrist, limiting the depth of self-generated change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Oliver’s guilt and fear without arguing or dismissing them. The sustain talk is redirected effectively, though less sophisticated techniques such as double-sided reflections are not consistently used.
