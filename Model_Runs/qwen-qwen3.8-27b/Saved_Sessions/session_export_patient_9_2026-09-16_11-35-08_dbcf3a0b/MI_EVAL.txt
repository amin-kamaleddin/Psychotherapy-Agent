# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating Amina's stress and protective parenting. Acceptance is less than exceptional in moments of deeper self-doubt, when the counselor quickly moves into problem-solving and scripts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Amina's perspective, including the tension between being available and protecting her son's calm. Empathic depth could increase by staying longer with the emotional cost of her ambivalence before shifting to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing phone-related evening anxiety and building a concrete, actionable plan. It is less than ideal because early discussion is broad and some prompts feel slightly directive rather than fully client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works interactively with Amina and incorporates her ideas, such as the 7 PM cutoff, phone drawer, alarm, and text wording. Collaboration is uneven because the counselor frequently generates plan content and options rather than fully co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports choice through permission-giving, alternatives, and noncoercive language. Autonomy support is limited at times by the frequent provision of specific scripts and counselor-generated options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative, future-oriented questions to elicit Amina's own motivations, confidence, and plans. Evocation becomes less consistent when the counselor supplies texts and reply scripts instead of inviting Amina to generate her own responses.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Amina's input and allows her preferences to shape the plan. However, frequent expert-driven suggestions and scripts can make the counselor appear more solution-oriented than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and builds on Amina's change talk, including her desire for her son to feel safe and calm. This is somewhat limited when the counselor shifts from evoking client language to offering specific solutions and scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without judgment but does not consistently use MI-consistent strategies to soften it. Responses often move toward planning, scripts, or reassurance rather than exploring ambivalence with double-sided reflections.
