# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, reducing shame while discussing the drinking incident. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures the client’s fear, ambivalence, and concern for future goals. It could be deeper by more fully reflecting shame, vulnerability, and fear of rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and its impact on soccer, scholarships, and safety. Direction is clear but could more consistently move toward a concrete commitment step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts role-play to the client’s preferences, adjusting scripts and scenarios. Collaboration is generally strong, though the early strategy menu is more clinician-driven than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is given choices and can reject suggestions, supporting some autonomy. Autonomy support is weakened by early prescriptive options and ready-made scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling elicit some change-oriented goals and motivations. Evocation is limited by premature advice-giving and insufficient follow-up to deepen values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites client input and lets preferences shape practice. Partnership is uneven because early strategy suggestions are more expert-driven than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses clear goals and willingness to protect soccer, and the psychiatrist reflects some of it. Change talk is not consistently amplified, deepened, or summarized.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about minimal use and overkill is acknowledged without judgment. The psychiatrist does not strategically soften or redirect it with double-sided reflections or exploration of its function.
