# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance toward the patient’s pain, inactivity, and missed medication. Some language remains close to the patient’s burden/worth theme, preventing a fully exemplary rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses strong reflective listening to track ambivalence, fear, pain, and self-criticism. However, some reflections stay at the thematic level rather than fully exploring deeper grief, shame, or dependency.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session consistently stays focused on the target behaviors of physical activity and medication adherence. The discussion of values, guilt, and fear is kept tied to concrete steps such as the mailbox, cane, and standing at the door.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses inclusive language and builds on the patient’s ideas and metaphors, such as the cane as a railing. The counselor still leads much of the structure and could make joint problem-solving more explicit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports the patient’s choice and avoids pressuring compliance with activity or medication expectations. The patient’s smaller, self-defined plan is accepted without being replaced by a stronger mantra or goal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor draws out the patient’s own motivations, leading to client-generated ideas like using the cane and reframing the step as “for my legs.” There is room to deepen values, identity, confidence, and commitment further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys a collaborative stance and the patient’s ideas substantially shape the session. The counselor could more explicitly position the patient as the expert on his own body, values, and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicits and reflects the patient’s change talk about the mailbox, cane, dry ground, and standing at the door. The session could more systematically strengthen change talk using DARN-C categories and confidence amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor normalizes the patient’s safety concerns and avoids arguing with statements that the couch is safest or the cane feels like defeat. More explicit double-sided reflections could better balance safety with the cost of staying on the couch.
