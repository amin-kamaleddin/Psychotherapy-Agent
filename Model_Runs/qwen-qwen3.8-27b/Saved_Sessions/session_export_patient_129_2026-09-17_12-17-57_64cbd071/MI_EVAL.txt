# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with a warm, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Tyler's developmental stage. However, the warmth feels somewhat generic and scripted, lacking deep, individualized positive regard beyond surface-level respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some decent reflective statements but tends to summarize rather than emotionally penetrate, often reframing Tyler's direct accusations into more positive framings. The repeated 'Can you tell me more about that?' responses are thin and don't demonstrate deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session is fundamentally off-target: the presenting concern is substance use, yet the psychiatrist never once addresses drugs or the target behavior after the opening. The entire session drifts into the mother-son conflict without returning to the actual reason for the referral.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language and asks for input, but consistently steers the conversation toward a predetermined narrative about boundaries and self-knowledge. Tyler's contributions do not substantially shape the session direction—the psychiatrist keeps returning to the same themes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately acknowledges Tyler's choice to be there and his right to withhold information. However, the psychiatrist subtly pressures Tyler to see himself as someone who 'knows what he needs,' which may undermine genuine autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Asks open questions but they are directed at the mother-son relationship rather than the target behavior (substance use). When Tyler expresses resignation, no evocation techniques are used to draw out his own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and avoids an expert-driven approach, but the partnership is largely superficial. Questions are leading and steered toward the psychiatrist's preferred direction rather than genuinely negotiating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal cultivation of change talk throughout the session, with no strategic use of scaling questions, importance/confidence rulers, or other techniques. The session never circles back to substance use as the target behavior, leaving no opportunity to evoke DARN categories.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk, which is positive, but also fails to actively soften or redirect it. Tyler's externalization of blame is largely accepted at face value without helping him examine how his own choices interact with the family dynamic.
