# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent, warm unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming Aisha's worth and effort multiple times without ever judging her struggles. Self-critical thoughts are reframed with warmth and strength rather than gentle correction, and the closing statement 'I'm here with you in this' establishes a foundation of genuine acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate, nuanced reflective listening that consistently goes beyond surface content to underlying meaning. Key reflections include naming the 'distance from yourself,' articulating the catch-22 precisely, and elevating Aisha's distinction between decision difficulty and doing difficulty into a clearer formulation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from emotional exploration through barrier identification to concrete planning and internal cue identification, with every question tied to the target behavior. The session ends with Aisha having a specific, actionable plan for tomorrow without any drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every concrete solution (shoes by the door, podcast app, two blocks, no commentary) is generated by Aisha herself, with the psychiatrist's role limited to reflecting, affirming, and building on her ideas. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with the psychiatrist consistently validating Aisha as the authority on her own experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently removes pressure, explicitly affirming that Aisha is not being pushed to do more than she wants and that the plan is entirely her own idea. Aisha's permission to do a 'really bad walk' is framed as her own rule-setting, and the psychiatrist never imposes a plan or pressures for greater commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is client-generated — the psychiatrist provides zero advice, health statistics, or reasons for change, instead using evocative questions to draw out desire, reasons, ability, and commitment from within Aisha. The session progresses naturally through the stages of change talk, with Aisha's final statements representing strong commitment language she generated herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions expertise and wisdom within Aisha, opening by asking what feels most important and never providing solutions. The session direction is substantially shaped by Aisha's contributions, with the psychiatrist acting as a fellow explorer rather than an expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk throughout, affirming Aisha's own change language, asking open-ended questions that elicit further elaboration, and naming her discoveries to build momentum. The session naturally progresses from desire to reason to ability to commitment, guided by evocative rather than directive questioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged as valid without dismissal, then gently explored to uncover the underlying barrier (the decision, not the ability) and helped Aisha reframe the narrative. The psychiatrist never reinforces sustain talk or engages in debate, consistently helping Aisha discover her own solutions to her fears.
