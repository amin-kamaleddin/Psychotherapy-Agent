# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and affirming, but the warmth can feel task-focused. Positive regard is present yet not consistently deep enough when Elias expresses shame or fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers useful reflections but often moves quickly to problem-solving. Deeper emotions such as shame, self-doubt, and fear of relapse are not fully explored or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target behavior of reducing nighttime alcohol use. It could be stronger by more consistently linking planning to motivation and change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias is included in planning and some of his choices are incorporated. However, the psychiatrist generates much of the structure, which limits full collaborative co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids direct commands, but also gives many prescriptive suggestions. More explicit support for Elias’s right to choose or modify the plan would strengthen autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-driven motivation is elicited early, especially around staying clear for his kids. The session shifts too quickly to advice and concrete planning, limiting further evocation of internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves Elias in decisions, creating a partially collaborative process. The interaction remains somewhat expert-driven because the psychiatrist supplies many options and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk appears but is not consistently or strategically developed. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to ask evocative questions that would deepen Elias’s reasons, confidence, and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and offers helpful reframes that reduce some self-criticism. The conversation still stays close to obstacles and risk, with less deliberate redirection toward change language.
