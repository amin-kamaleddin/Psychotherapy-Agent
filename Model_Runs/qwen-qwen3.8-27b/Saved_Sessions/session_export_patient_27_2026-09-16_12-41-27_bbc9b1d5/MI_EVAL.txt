# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses validating, nonjudgmental language and respects Daniela’s fears without shaming. The stance is generally warm but occasionally becomes mildly advisory, keeping it below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Daniela’s concerns about being judged, made a target, or losing normalcy. Empathy is strong but does not always stay long enough to fully explore deeper fears before moving toward solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is well focused on the target behavior, moving clearly from concerns to a specific friend, text draft, and next step. It stays purposeful but becomes heavily centered on disclosure wording rather than broader disclosure goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates the message and invites Daniela to choose, edit, and adjust wording. Collaboration is strong, though the clinician still generates much of the actual script and procedural suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniela’s pace and preferences are respected, with clear emphasis on her control over when and how to disclose. Some mildly directive suggestions, such as practicing a specific line, slightly reduce pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions about Daniela’s concerns and desired outcome, but the session moves quickly into practical scripting. There is limited evocation of her values, confidence, abilities, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is collaborative, with Daniela’s input clearly shaping the final message and plan. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still supplies much of the language and guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela offers meaningful change talk, including wanting to be honest without hiding from Sarah. The psychiatrist uses some of this language but does not consistently amplify, summarize, or strategically expand it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniela’s hesitations are accepted without argument and redirected into manageable next steps. The response is effective, but it does not often use double-sided reflections or explicitly link her concerns to her own values.
