# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, normalizes difficulty, and affirms client efforts without judging alcohol use. Acceptance is strong but does not deeply validate underlying shame or fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the push–pull and some concerns, but often remain surface-level. There are missed opportunities to explore deeper anxiety, vulnerability, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol and preventing violent evenings, moving logically from concern to a concrete plan. Direction and goal focus are excellent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s chosen cue, tea replacement, and script are clearly co-created with the psychiatrist. The psychiatrist also supplies multiple options and advice, making collaboration less fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, invites choices, and supports the client’s pace and confidence. It would be stronger with explicit reinforcement that the client owns the final decision and can change the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence scaling elicit useful client-generated material, but advice-giving and supplied strategies limit deeper evocation. The client’s own values and resources could be explored more before solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas shape the session and the psychiatrist avoids imposing a distant expert stance. The balance leans slightly toward clinician direction when multiple strategies and scripts are offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits desire, ability, and commitment, tracking confidence increases and client statements of readiness. Powerful change talk is sometimes not fully amplified or consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s desire for an immediate drop and goal of reducing rather than quitting without argument. Softening is neutral rather than strategically using double-sided reflections to bridge toward change.
