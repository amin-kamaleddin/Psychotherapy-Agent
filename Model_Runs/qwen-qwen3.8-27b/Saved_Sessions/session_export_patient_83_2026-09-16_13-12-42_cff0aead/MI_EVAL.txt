# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently respected Mai’s concerns without judgment. Acceptance could be deeper by normalizing her ambivalence and difficulty starting an unfamiliar medication.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflected Mai’s need to understand the medication and fit it into her routine. Empathy remained mostly cognitive and did not fully capture her anxiety, frustration, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on initiating and adhering to atorvastatin. The counselor consistently guided the conversation toward practical adherence steps such as timing, side effects, missed doses, and follow-up labs.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor incorporated Mai’s preferences, such as her 10 PM routine and use of a pill tray. The interaction was still counselor-driven, with more information-giving than co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai was allowed to make her own choices about starting the medication, calling her doctor, and managing reminders. Some directive clinical recommendations slightly reduced the emphasis on full autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asked some open questions early but quickly shifted to education and problem-solving. There was limited use of evocative techniques to elicit Mai’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited Mai’s input and adapted parts of the plan to her needs. The session was still heavily influenced by the counselor’s clinical knowledge rather than shared expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai expressed several change statements, and the counselor acknowledged them. The counselor did not fully deepen or expand this change talk with values-, confidence-, or ability-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoided arguing with Mai’s concerns about the medication. However, she often responded with clinical explanations rather than reflecting or reframing the concerns in MI-consistent ways.
