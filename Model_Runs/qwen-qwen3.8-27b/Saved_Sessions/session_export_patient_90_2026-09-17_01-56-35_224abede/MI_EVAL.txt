# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and individualized unconditional positive regard throughout. Explicitly validated the client's practical constraints and protected his attachment to the dog.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Contains several accurate, complex reflections of the core conflict and emotional risk. Occasionally reflected meaning cognitively without deeper emotional exploration of the fear/shame underneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Remained clearly focused on asthma self-monitoring even when the dog and finances came up. Lacked a final consolidated summary tying together target behavior, success criteria, and follow-up timing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative; never imposed a protocol and adopted the client's own language ('dashboard,' 'circus') as shared framework. The final plan was explicitly co-created rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** One of the session's strongest features—consistently positioned Marcus as decision-maker and removed pressure around reporting numbers. Final language fully reflects client ownership ('trying the basics').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used strong non-suggestive questions to help Marcus articulate his own goals and methods without lecturing. Occasionally labeled or assumed the client's 'decision' or 'plan' slightly prematurely.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear collaborative intent ('figure that out with you than hand you a script') and repeated invitations for Marcus to define the work. Treated Marcus as expert on his own life, home environment, and limits.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicited and affirmed genuine change talk about inhaler use, nighttime monitoring, and feeling 'normal.' Could have more systematically developed and consolidated it using DARN-CAT themes (e.g., confidence scale, deeper reasons).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Neatly handled all resistances (dog, finances, journaling, fear of grading) without argument or invalidation. At times accommodated sustain talk without a stronger double-side reflection that would transform it further into change talk.
