# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and validating, but frequently shifts into corrective reframing or advice. This limits the depth of acceptance despite a warm tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are genuine but brief empathic statements that mainly transition to problem-solving. Deeper, complex reflections of the client’s ambivalence and emotions are missing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the exercise goal with a logical progression to a concrete plan. There is minor drift when extra strength and nutrition content is added.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and incorporates some client ideas. However, most of the plan is therapist-generated, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects the client’s pushback on added intensity. Autonomy is occasionally undermined by directive scripts and unsolicited plan additions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open-ended questions are used, but the psychiatrist relies heavily on providing rationale and solutions. The client’s own motivations and reasons for change are not deeply elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and the client’s input shapes the final plan. In practice, the therapist does most of the cognitive work, so partnership is more nominal than substantive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses strong change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely amplifies, reflects, or deepens it. The change talk is largely client-generated rather than skillfully cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s worries are acknowledged without being reinforced. However, the psychiatrist does not use MI-specific techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to work with ambivalence.
