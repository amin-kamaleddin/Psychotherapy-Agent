# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirming reflections that de-escalate defensiveness. The warmth is somewhat formulaic, and the opening 'stuck' reflection feels assumptive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's ambivalence and practical concerns about work, energy, and smoking. The empathy remains more cognitive/behavioral and does not fully explore underlying emotions, identity, or relational strain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking and moves logically toward a small, concrete behavioral idea. Repetitive use of similar 'low-effort' and '10%' formulations reduces forward momentum and consolidation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites the client's expertise in shaping the conversation. The agenda and plan still feel largely guided by the counselor rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choice is strongly respected, and the psychiatrist avoids pressure to quit. The repeated focus on trying a pause can subtly imply that trying is the expected next step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client's own concerns about work, stamina, and coping with the 'loud head.' Evocation remains fairly surface-level and does not deeply explore core values, identity, or future self.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert and invites his perspective. The interaction remains somewhat guided by the counselor's repeated strategic framework rather than being fully equal-power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers small change ideas, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or consolidate them. There is no use of importance/confidence rulers or clear DARN-C elicitation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids lecturing and uses some double-sided reflections that are MI-consistent. The conversation remains heavily weighted toward reasons for maintaining smoking and does not consistently pivot toward change.
