# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, consistently acknowledging the client’s burden and maternal values. However, acceptance remains somewhat implicit, with fewer direct affirmations of the client’s strengths and worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflections that accurately capture the client’s practical concerns and emotional strain, including mirroring the client’s own language. The main limitation is that deeper feelings such as loneliness, fear, and grief are not consistently named or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on improving co-parenting communication and moves toward a concrete, child-centered action. Periodic summarizing of the target behavior and its link to the client’s parenting values would strengthen the direction further.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting the client’s ideas and working with her to draft a message rather than giving direct advice. The session remains somewhat therapist-led because the psychiatrist still largely chooses the sequence and focus of the work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy through open-ended, non-directive questions that invite the client to generate and refine her own solutions. The main gap is a lack of explicit affirmation that the client is free to choose and own any decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently draws out the client’s own values, concerns, and possible next steps rather than providing reasons for change. The evocation could be strengthened by more deeply expanding the client’s emotional and values-based motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and repeatedly positions the client as the expert on her relationship and family situation. However, power sharing is not fully explicit, since the psychiatrist still structures the session quite directly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client change talk through questions about goals, small steps, and practical communication. The main weakness is that the client’s motivation, ownership, and intention are not consistently reflected, amplified, or consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles doubt and status-quo statements well by acknowledging them without arguing and redirecting toward manageable, client-driven options. More consistent transformation of global negative statements into change talk would be needed for a higher score.
