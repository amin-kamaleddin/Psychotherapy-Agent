# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and helps lower defensiveness. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural and lacks stronger explicit affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence, social concerns, and mixed motives. Emotional depth could be stronger by more clearly validating feelings such as embarrassment, fear, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, peer influence, and safety while narrowing toward a practical plan. It would be stronger with a clear closing summary and collaboratively defined next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows the client’s topics and treats him as a partner rather than imposing expert advice. Collaboration would be stronger with more explicit co-creation of the session agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice and avoids pressuring the client to quit or promise change. Repeated “protect your health” language can subtly shift toward a therapeutic agenda rather than full autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions effectively elicit the client’s own reasons, limits, and safety ideas. The evocation could be stronger by affirming, summarizing, or scaling the emerging change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s perspective and allows his concerns to guide the conversation. Partnership would be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment that the client is the expert on his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes desire, ability, and need talk, including limits and a ride-home plan. The score is limited by inconsistent amplification, affirmation, or consolidation of that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects sustain talk without arguing or escalating resistance. One reflection reinforces a detection-avoidance frame rather than consistently pivoting toward values, safety, or autonomous choice.
