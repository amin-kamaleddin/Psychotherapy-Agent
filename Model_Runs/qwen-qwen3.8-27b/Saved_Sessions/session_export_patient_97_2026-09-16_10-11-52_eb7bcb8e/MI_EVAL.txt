# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, validates Mateo's ambivalence and practical constraints, and avoids judgment or pressure. The acceptance is strong but could be warmer and more explicitly person-centered rather than focused only on the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and demonstrates good understanding of Mateo's goals, pressures, and preferences. There is less emotional depth, particularly around uncertainty, shame, fear of failure, or the weight of mandated participation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear and appropriate focus on building stability, financial security, and a small concrete behavior. It could be strengthened by more consistently connecting the micro-step to Mateo's broader values, future self, and risk reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Mateo's input and allows his preferences to shape the plan, such as the cash box, reminder wording, check-in format, and label language. Collaboration is effective but remains somewhat therapist-led in structure, with the psychiatrist presenting options before fully co-creating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, reducing pressure, and accepting Mateo's preference for a simple check-in and flexible fallback. The language could more explicitly emphasize Mateo's ownership and decision-making, especially when he expresses uncertainty or obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some of Mateo's own motivations early on and helps him generate his own ideas, such as saving $10 per week and involving his cousin. The evocation is limited because the session shifts quickly into planning and logistics rather than consistently drawing out Mateo's deeper reasons, values, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites Mateo's input throughout the session. The partnership is strong but not fully equal at all points, as the psychiatrist sometimes moves quickly into structured plans and specific logistics before fully exploring Mateo's perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk and reinforces Mateo's intentions, such as saving $10 per week and doing it for his future child. The development of change talk is not consistently deep or skillful, and powerful statements like 'it's something I built' are not fully explored or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Mateo's resistance and reduces pressure by accepting flexibility and acknowledging mandated participation. The softening could be more strategic by using double-sided reflections and exploring the underlying meaning of his obligation and frustration.
