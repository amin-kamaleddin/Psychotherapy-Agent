# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and appropriately de-escalates resistance. Acceptance is strong but sometimes generic and could better normalize the patient's frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate reflective listening, especially around the dental concern and desire to avoid lectures. However, empathy remains surface-level and misses deeper feelings of frustration, disrespect, and autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear practical focus on dental staining, cost, durability, and appointment logistics. However, it lacks a stronger MI direction toward linking the dental goal to possible ambivalence about smoking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's preferences help shape the session, and the psychiatrist invites input on priorities and logistics. Collaboration is good, though the psychiatrist still supplies much of the structure and content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports autonomy through permission, optional suggestions, and explicit respect for the patient's choice. The amount of information and scripting is mildly directive but still autonomy-respecting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful open and value-based questions, but quickly shifts into information-giving and problem-solving. More evocative questioning would have been needed to draw out deeper motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient's preferences and follows the dental agenda. However, the interaction remains somewhat expert-driven rather than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited efforts to cultivate change talk, and the session mainly moves into practical options. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect, amplify, and build on the patient's own values and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and appropriately accepts resistance to smoking advice. However, sustain talk is managed mainly through avoidance rather than strategic MI skills such as double-sided reflection or reframing.
