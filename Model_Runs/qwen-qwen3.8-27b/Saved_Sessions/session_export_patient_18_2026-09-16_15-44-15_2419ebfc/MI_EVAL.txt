# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is consistently nonjudgmental, supportive, and normalizing. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth is somewhat procedural and does not explicitly affirm the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client's confusion and desire for reassurance. The empathy remains mostly surface-level and does not deeply address the underlying fear or emotional impact of being flagged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation stays relevant to the screening concern but becomes repetitive. It does not move productively toward a clear behavior-related or change-related goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally invites the client to shape the conversation and follows her topic shifts. Collaboration becomes uneven when the counselor continues asking for preferences instead of giving the direct reassurance the client requested.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly emphasizes choice, nonjudgment, and lack of pressure. Autonomy support is weakened by repeatedly asking what would help after the client has clearly stated what she needs.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses evocative questions, but they mostly focus on process and reassurance. There is little effort to evoke the client's own values, hopes, or motivation for healthier choices.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor adopts a collaborative, non-lecture stance and offers options. Partnership is not fully realized because the counselor still appears to steer the session rather than fully follow the client's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are early attempts to open space, but the session does not develop actual change talk. The counselor does not build on the client's concerns about records, parents, or future choices as motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing the client's minimization and reduces the sense of being labeled. However, the counselor does not use the sustain talk to create a pivot toward balanced thinking or future decision-making.
