# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, unconditional positive regard, and nonjudgment. They avoid labeling, shaming, or moralizing while normalizing Marcus’s social world.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing Marcus’s values, respect needs, and frustration. The score reflects that the empathy is somewhat cognitive/behavioral rather than deeply exploring underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol use at parties and related risks. The psychiatrist guides the conversation toward concrete change without drifting or forcing an agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner, inviting Marcus to co-construct goals and strategies. Marcus’s own examples shape the session rather than the counselor directing it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marcus’s choice and personal authority are consistently emphasized throughout. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, directives, or implied mandates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Marcus’s own motivation, values, limits, and strategies. Marcus articulates clear change talk rather than receiving external reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marcus is consistently positioned as the expert on his own life, experiences, and choices. The session follows his language and concerns rather than an imposed counselor agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and reinforces Marcus’s change talk, including ability, need, and strategy. It does not reach a 5 because the change talk is not fully consolidated into a stronger commitment or coherent plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well with nonarguing, validating, double-sided reflections. It remains a 4 because some sustain talk, especially near the end, is not fully softened or reframed.
