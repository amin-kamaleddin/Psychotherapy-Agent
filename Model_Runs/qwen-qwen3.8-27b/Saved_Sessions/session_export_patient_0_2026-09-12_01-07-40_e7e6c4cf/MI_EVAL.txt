# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, calmness, and nonjudgmental acceptance without shaming or arguing. The tone can feel procedural, and the harm-reduction education adds a mildly didactic element.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections track stated content and perspective, but remain mostly at the surface level. The emotional subtext—embarrassment, fear of being labeled, and ambivalence—is not consistently named or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear starting focus on the infection and use, but drifts into broad harm-reduction education and script preparation. It lacks a strategic, motivational direction toward a personally meaningful change or harm-reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options and preferences are offered, which improves collaboration compared with a directive stance. Much of the session, however, is one-directional advice and scripting rather than joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly respects choices and avoids pressuring the patient to quit. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicit affirmation that any decision about use or care belongs to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial open questions are appropriate, but the psychiatrist quickly shifts to informing rather than eliciting. The key patient concern about not knowing what is in the drug is not used to draw out his own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership through choices and invitations, but the session remains largely clinician-driven. The patient is not consistently positioned as the authority on his values, limits, and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist notices some change-related material, such as concern about contaminants and being more careful. However, there is little systematic evocation of desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but does not strategically soften the patient's strong minimization and sustain talk. The conversation accepts the 'not addicted/not a problem' narrative without exploring ambivalence or safer-use options.
