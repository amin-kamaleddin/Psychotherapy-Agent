# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was nonjudgmental and repeatedly normalized the client’s experience. However, validation often shifted quickly into advice, limiting deeper acknowledgment of emotional conflict.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured some of the client’s perspective but remained surface-level. Key themes such as guilt, role-model concerns, and the rupture of feeling unheard were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stayed clearly focused on smoking and child exposure with little topic drift. Direction favored concrete behavior management over MI-driven motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaborative wording was present, but the client mostly responded to the counselor’s menu of suggestions. The interaction remained counselor-led rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy was acknowledged at key moments, especially when the client expressed resistance. Repeated prompts to choose options, however, felt pressuring and reactive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor provided many external suggestions and reasons rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations. There were few evocative questions about values, readiness, or meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session was largely expert-driven, with repeated structured lists and specific behaviors. The client’s contribution influenced the session only because she insisted on it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited effort was made to build on the client’s change talk about her children and the car smoke-free step. Most questions focused on choosing external steps rather than eliciting internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided direct challenge and normalized ambivalence about smoking. However, the work on sustain talk stayed neutral rather than strategically exploring discrepancy or possible change.
