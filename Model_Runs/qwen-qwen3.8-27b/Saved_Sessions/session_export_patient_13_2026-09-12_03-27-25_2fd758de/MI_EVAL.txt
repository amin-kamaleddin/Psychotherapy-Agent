# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, nonjudgment, and support for the client's fear and hesitation. The tone is accepting, though sometimes more procedural and task-focused than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects several surface emotions accurately and helps the client feel heard. However, deeper emotions such as fear of inadequacy, shame, and the need for reassurance are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally moves toward a clear, manageable target behavior and practical plan. At times the direction is diluted by multiple options, repeated scaling, and added interventions that fragment focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts the final plan to the client's preferences, showing genuine collaboration. Earlier parts are more clinician-driven because the psychiatrist offers structured menus rather than fully client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by asking preferences and respecting the client's stop rule. Some repeated prompts and options still create subtle pressure toward action rather than fully affirming inaction as an option.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling questions to evoke some change talk and self-assessment. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist often supplies rationales and suggestions instead of eliciting the client's own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some partnership language and invites the client to choose among options. The interaction can feel uneven because the psychiatrist frequently provides preselected menus rather than allowing the client to set the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses several change-related ideas and the psychiatrist asks questions that draw out confidence. These moments could be strengthened by reflecting, affirming, and expanding the client's own change talk more consistently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or dismiss the client's resistance, and often normalizes it. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently use strategic MI moves such as double-sided reflections to transform sustain talk into motivation.
