# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance without labeling Derrick’s second DUI as moral failure. Acceptance is slightly limited by repetitive language and less explicit affirmation of his worth beyond his choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Empathic reflections accurately capture ambivalence about friendship, pressure, and protecting the license. Deeper exploration of shame, fear, and loss would have made the empathy more consistently strong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Direction stays focused on reducing alcohol-related driving risk and managing peer pressure. It would be stronger with clearer summaries and tighter linking to specific high-risk moments such as shots at the bar or preparing to drive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaboration is strong because Derrick’s suggestions drive the plan, and the psychiatrist avoids taking over problem-solving. It is not fully co-led, with limited explicit shared ownership or priority negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is respected through non-controlling language and no pressure to quit, cut friends off, or enter treatment. The counselor could more explicitly affirm Derrick’s ownership of decisions and freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocation is effective in bringing out Derrick’s goals, ideas, and tentative willingness to act. It would be stronger if it more deeply accessed values, identity, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses open questions and lets Derrick’s own ideas shape the plan, reflecting a collaborative stance. Partnership would benefit from more explicit power-sharing and agenda co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session evokes useful practical change talk, including cab money, a backup driver, and willingness to test the plan once. It could be stronger by consistently deepening change talk with confidence/importance scaling and explicit commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward alternatives. However, double-sided reflections and exploration of consequences were used only modestly.
