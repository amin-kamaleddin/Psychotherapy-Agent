# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepted the patient’s boundaries without pushback. Warmth was generally supportive but felt somewhat scripted and slightly mismatched at the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured ambivalence and the importance of the break. Deeper emotional reflection on fatigue, resistance, and the experience of being pressured was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking, the cigarette break, and a low-pressure experiment. There was no drift into unrelated topics, and the direction fit the patient’s request to keep it simple.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input, offered options, and adapted to the patient’s choices. Collaboration was strong, though the use of structured options prevented it from being fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized choice, ownership, and the patient’s authority over the process. There was no coercion or pressure, and the plan was framed as an optional experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked about values, past attempts, and confidence to evoke motivation from within. Evocation was helpful but limited because the session moved quickly into planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned the patient as the expert and invited her input to shape the session. Partnership was clear, but structured options and suggestions still guided parts of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient offered some change talk, including concern for her daughter, willingness to try water, and viewing Tuesday as a potential win. The psychiatrist affirmed this but did not consistently deepen the patient’s own reasons, hope, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk skillfully by rolling with resistance and avoiding argument or risk reinforcement. It could have been stronger with more double-sided reflections or pivots back into change talk.
