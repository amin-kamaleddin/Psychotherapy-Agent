# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with clear affirmations of the client's efforts and strengths. However, the warmth is somewhat formulaic at times and does not fully normalize systemic barriers such as poverty and lack of support network.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, including a powerful final reflection on loneliness. However, reflections are occasionally summarizing rather than deeply exploring the emotional nuance behind the client's language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and logical arc from identifying the biggest stressor to mapping first steps, following the client's lead throughout. The session could have more explicitly tied back to a concrete next step or summarized progress at the close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites the client's input at each step and builds on her ideas in a natural dialogue. Slightly undermined by moments where the psychiatrist offers interpretive frames rather than staying in a purely reflective, client-led space.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's autonomy and ownership, with explicit statements like 'You didn't need me for that' and 'I want this space to be about what matters to you.' The strongest criterion in the session; the psychiatrist never pressures or prescribes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits the client's own motivations and reasons for change through open-ended, future-oriented questions. Could be deepened by more explicitly evoking the client's values and identity to strengthen the motivational base.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance positioning the client as the expert on her own life, with explicit acknowledgment of her competence. A slight role shift occurs when the psychiatrist says 'I want to be that person who helps you figure out the steps,' subtly moving toward an expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes multiple forms of change talk (desire, reasons, ability, commitment) through well-timed questions and affirmations. Could more explicitly target all DARN categories with equal depth and elicit more confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully using curiosity-based questions that allow the client to examine and soften her own framing. Could more explicitly use double-sided reflections or actively reframe barriers by linking them back to the client's own practical solutions.
