# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges frustration and hurt, but acceptance is limited by a procedural, task-driven focus. Mildly evaluative praise and a problem-solving stance reduce unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflection of the immediate frustration about phone loss and the father’s birthday is present. However, empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore fear, loneliness, family values, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session maintains some focus on the immediate goal of getting a phone call. It drifts into paperwork, grievance language, and procedural steps without clearly connecting to broader behavioral or motivational goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences but quickly takes an expert-led, solution-providing role. The interaction feels more like supplying a problem-solving package than working jointly as equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is offered some choices about scripts and documents, and explicit commands are avoided. However, repeated directive pushes toward grievance, appeal, and procedural steps limit true ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly practical rather than motivational and do not effectively draw out the client’s own values, hopes, or reasons for change. The counselor supplies many external solutions, reducing space for internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are occasional invitations for client input, but the counselor rapidly assumes the role of advocate, script-writer, and procedural guide. The client is not consistently treated as the primary source of wisdom about his own goals and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes few and weak efforts to elicit the client’s own change talk, confidence, need, or commitment. The session focuses more on external plans than on the client’s own motivations for constructive action.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s blame toward the officer and grievance-focused stance are not effectively softened or balanced. Helping draft grievance and appeal language may reinforce an adversarial frame rather than redirecting toward self-management.
