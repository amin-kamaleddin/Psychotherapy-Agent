# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly validates the client’s frustration. An opening statement that the client "reached out" was slightly misattuned because the client said he was pressed by his mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s annoyance, feeling dragged in, and desire for privacy. They remain mostly surface-level and do not consistently reach deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with an attempt to focus on concerns or change, but quickly narrows to administrative logistics. The target behavior and presenting risk are not consistently addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and accommodates the client's preferences, creating a somewhat equal interaction. Collaboration is stronger for process and logistics than for the therapeutic content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers multiple choices, avoids coercion, and respects the client's request to leave. The main limitation is that autonomy support centers on logistics rather than choices about substance use or help.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few open-ended questions attempt to draw out the client's goals, but the counselor does not consistently evoke, amplify, or expand the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and uses collaborative language, but the session remains largely clinician-managed. He does not consistently treat the client as the expert on his own life and decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are minimal opportunities to elicit change talk, and small pro-change statements are not amplified or expanded. The session does not meaningfully cultivate motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing or judging the client's reasons to disengage, which helps prevent escalation. However, he rarely uses strategic reflections or double-sided responses to soften sustain talk toward autonomy or change.
