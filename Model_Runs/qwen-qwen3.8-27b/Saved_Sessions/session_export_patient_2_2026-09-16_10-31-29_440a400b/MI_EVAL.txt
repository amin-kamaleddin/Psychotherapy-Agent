# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone while normalizing Aisha’s stress and respecting her alcohol boundary. Positive regard is strong but can become somewhat formulaic, with less explicit emotional validation of her ambivalence and caregiving burden.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflections of exhaustion and the function of smoking, including Aisha’s own language. However, reflections often stay at the content level and do not fully capture the emotional complexity of her ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of reducing evening cigarette use and appropriately honors the client’s boundary around alcohol. The opening is broader than necessary, and multiple suggestions slightly diffuse the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician treats Aisha as the expert in her own life and allows her choices to shape the final plan. Frequent clinician-generated options and strategies make the interaction feel somewhat clinician-led rather than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invites choice, accepts Aisha’s rejections without pressure, and normalizes slips. Autonomy support is strong, though it could be more explicit about Aisha’s complete right to choose not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits Aisha’s own motivations, confidence, and change talk, especially around her values as a mother. However, the clinician moves quickly into concrete strategies and does not consistently deepen or consolidate the client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Aisha’s input and co-constructs a realistic plan around her preferred strategies. The advisory style, with many specific suggestions, slightly limits the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician evokes and affirms Aisha’s change talk, including her goal to cut down and her motivation to stay healthy for her son. There are missed opportunities to deepen the change talk and explore the meaning behind Aisha’s confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with Aisha’s minimization and respects her boundary around alcohol. However, sustain talk is largely left unexplored, with fewer strategic reflections or gentle pivots to help her examine it from both sides.
