# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses warm, affirming, and nonjudgmental language throughout (e.g., 'I love that you've actually tested one of these already'). There is no condescension, minimization, or disapproval at any point—genuine unconditional positive regard is maintained.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is precise and layered, capturing nuanced distinctions like the difference between asking and being picked up, or the cognitive-emotional gap between knowing and feeling ready. Reflections go beyond generic paraphrasing to demonstrate deep understanding of Mei's internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session progresses logically from ambivalence assessment through strategy development to a concrete next step, with each question building on the previous one. The alcohol-contraception link is maintained as a consistent through-line without the session drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation functions as genuine joint problem-solving, with the psychiatrist acting as a facilitator who builds on Mei's ideas rather than imposing solutions. All key strategies (codeword, daytime text, exit plan) are client-generated and drive the conversation forward.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently delegates decision-making back to Mei through open questions and framed suggestions that return choice to the client. No directive language is used; even offers like the low-stakes hint are immediately followed by a question returning the decision to Mei.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change talk is client-generated—Mei produces the tipsy threshold, codeword system, and daytime script herself in response to strategic questions. The session ends with spontaneous self-efficacy ('I'm starting to sound like I actually believe I can do this'), the gold standard of evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mei is positioned as the expert on her own life, with the psychiatrist consistently asking for her perspective rather than providing answers. The power balance is genuinely equal, and the session is shaped entirely by Mei's contributions and ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strategically uses questions that elicit solution-focused language and reinforces emerging change talk with affirmations like 'That's proof you can do the hard part' and 'You've turned a moment of vulnerability into a system.' No external reasons for change are provided—motivation is drawn entirely from within the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of being 'the buzzkill,' embarrassment of initiating, worry about seeming weird) is consistently acknowledged and normalized before being gently reframed or redirected toward solutions. The psychiatrist never argues against or minimizes sustain talk, always opening space for resolution.
