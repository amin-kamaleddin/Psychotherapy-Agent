# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout. Acceptance is task-focused rather than deeply relational, with less explicit affirmation of the client's worth beyond the immediate plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects exhaustion, fear, and the impact of being dismissed by Dr. Brown. However, the empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore the deeper meaning of her cancer fear, isolation, or need to be believed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear and consistent direction, moving logically from presenting the problem to tests, scripts, a written note, and rehearsal. The focus is narrow and primarily medical-advocacy oriented rather than connecting to broader MI-relevant change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by allowing the client's preferences to shape the script, note, and practice approach. However, the interaction is not fully equal throughout, especially in the early provision of an expert-driven biomedical checklist and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers options and avoids overt pressure, supporting the client's ability to choose scripts and approaches. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly affirming the client's ownership of the change process and right to make different choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some open-ended questions, especially early in the session. There is limited use of deeper evocative questions to elicit the client's values, confidence, reasons for change, or inner motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites input and the client's preferences clearly guide the session. The partnership is effective but not fully realized, as the psychiatrist still assumes a considerable expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's motivation to seek scopes and written referrals is acknowledged and affirmed. The psychiatrist does not skillfully expand this into broader change talk about values, self-management, coping, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** This is the weakest MI component in the session. The psychiatrist largely aligns with and amplifies the client's biomedical sustain talk, reinforcing the need for certainty and testing rather than gently exploring alternative explanations or self-directed management.
