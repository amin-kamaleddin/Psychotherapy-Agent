# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Farid’s self-doubt about being lazy or failing. It is not a 5 because personal affirmation of Farid’s effort, honesty, or self-awareness is limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Farid’s ambivalence, time pressure, exhaustion, and concern about skipping the plan. They are less than perfect because deeper emotional or identity-related meaning is not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on increasing physical activity despite fatigue and time constraints. The progression from barriers to a small, concrete plan is clear and purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as a partner rather than an expert, using Farid’s schedule, preferences, and rejections to shape the plan. The final plan is co-constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Farid is repeatedly offered choices and given permission to keep the plan small. The psychiatrist respects his decision to reject the fallback and avoids pressuring him to add more commitments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions about what matters, the biggest barrier, least intrusive option, and confidence level. However, the psychiatrist often leads with options and plan language, so client-generated motivation is not strongly drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The language is collaborative and invites Farid to choose among options throughout the session. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still generates most of the intervention ideas and maintains a clear agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Farid expresses values, desire, and a small intention to try the plan once. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or expand Farid’s own change language before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates sustain talk without arguing, such as Farid’s fear of being lazy or finding reasons not to do it. However, there are few double-sided reflections or strategic pivots that help resolve ambivalence.
