# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Daniela’s concerns about disclosing her diabetes. However, the acceptance felt somewhat formulaic and could have shown deeper, more individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate reflections of Daniela’s fear and discomfort, but often moved quickly to problem-solving. More depth was needed in exploring the emotional complexity and meaning of disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior of disclosing Daniela’s diabetes to a friend. The psychiatrist effectively guided each step toward a concrete, manageable disclosure plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements, such as offering choices and asking Daniela to pick wording that felt authentic. However, the psychiatrist’s repeated provision of scripts made the process feel less collaborative and more like homework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally respected Daniela’s choices and avoided coercion, including supporting her preference to draft alone. Autonomy was somewhat reduced by frequent therapist-provided scripts and specific suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some of Daniela’s own ideas, such as using a storytelling format and choosing a safer friend. However, the session often shifted from evocation to direct suggestion, supplying external language instead of deeper self-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempted to position Daniela as the decision-maker by asking for input and offering options. Partnership was weakened by the psychiatrist’s frequent introduction of exact wording before Daniela had fully explored her own approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela produced some change-oriented language, including deciding to type the message and preferring the story-style option. The psychiatrist could have more consistently reflected and amplified Daniela’s own reasons for disclosure instead of supplying ready-made phrases.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Daniela’s hesitation without judgment and allowed her to pause. However, the response to her sustain talk remained somewhat problem-solving-heavy and did not consistently use double-sided reflections to connect disclosure with her values.
