# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong nonjudgmental frame established early and maintained throughout, with consistent validation of Ethan's perspective. Falls short of a 5 because acceptance remains somewhat surface-level and doesn't explicitly affirm Ethan's worth as a person beyond his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective complex reflections that accurately track Ethan's emotional shifts and capture the ambivalence without judgment. Misses opportunities to deepen into the emotional experience, particularly the vulnerability beneath the bravado about social exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful pacing that moves strategically from broad opening through values, behavior, consequences, and future self without wandering. A 5 would require a brief collaborative summary that ties emerging themes together and previews the conversation's direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue with curiosity about Ethan's internal world; the psychiatrist follows his lead and does not impose an agenda. Collaboration is strong but somewhat one-directional, with the psychiatrist driving the thematic progression rather than co-constructing it visibly with Ethan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent non-directive stance with open questions that invite Ethan's own perspective; never prescribes or implies obligation. Autonomy support is implicit and well-maintained but not explicitly reinforced at key decision points with language affirming Ethan's ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions in key moments (e.g., 'if you weren't trying to prove it's fine, what would you actually notice?') successfully draw out admissions. Lacks systematic use of different question types to evoke various forms of change talk (confidence, need, desire, ability) and scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear collaborative language and shared ownership of the process; the session is client-led in content. Could be strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language and agenda negotiation to make Ethan's influence on session direction more visible.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges naturally but is acknowledged rather than actively expanded, amplified, or built upon. No attempts to evoke confidence, ability, or commitment statements; Ethan's 'I don't like it' and 'maybe I'm being too hard on myself' are recognized but not deeply mined.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk around peer belonging is handled skillfully without argument or challenge, using validation before gently introducing discrepancy. The peer belonging theme is not fully addressed with a double-sided reflection that honors friendship AND opens the possibility of belonging without drinking.
