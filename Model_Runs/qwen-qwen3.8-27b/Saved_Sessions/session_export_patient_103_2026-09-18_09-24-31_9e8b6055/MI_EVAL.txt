# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, validating Nabil's anger, defensiveness, and vulnerability without pathologizing or minimizing. Warmth is individualized (referencing the white coat, 'tight ship' language) rather than generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, progressively deepening reflections that capture both content and emotion, moving from surface frustration to identity threat to fear of not knowing. Particularly skilled at identifying underlying feelings Nabil hadn't fully articulated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression from surface frustration through identity threat to the 'not knowing' trigger without wandering. Slightly weakened at the end by the absence of a forward-looking consolidation or explicit next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is genuinely driven by Nabil's disclosures, with inclusive language and no imposed agenda or framework. Nabil's own insights are treated as the central material rather than being overlaid with the psychiatrist's interpretations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never tells Nabil what to do, never suggests specific interventions, and explicitly names autonomy violations in the system. Consistently affirms Nabil's right to decide on his own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions consistently draw out Nabil's own reasoning and change talk without providing external justifications. The elicit-draw-respond pattern is applied skillfully, progressing from surface goals to deeper values and insight.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and repeatedly refuses the expert role, conveying that expertise resides within the client. Power-sharing is genuine, with the psychiatrist contributing through reflections and questions while content and direction come from Nabil.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently and skillfully evokes change talk, with Nabil generating substantial desire and need statements throughout. Could have pushed further into confidence/ability talk and more explicitly elicited commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids arguing with sustain talk and instead rolls with it while gently redirecting toward the client's own language. One instance (Nabil emphasizing coercion and lack of choice) could have been more actively softened with a double-sided reflection.
