# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client’s ambivalence, financial stress, and attachment to the dog. The warmth was strong but somewhat structured, and there was limited explicit normalization of deeper emotional burden.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used strong, accurate reflections of the client’s conflicting priorities and practical concerns. However, some emotionally charged material, such as fear, shame, exhaustion, or emotional reliance on the dog, was underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on improving asthma management within the client’s real-life constraints. The discussion progressed from general overwhelm to a concrete, behaviorally specific one-week plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed around the client’s constraints, values, and preferences rather than imposed by the psychiatrist. Full collaboration would benefit from more explicit joint decision-making and client-led organization of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported the client’s autonomy, choice, and low-pressure stopping rule. The score is not a 5 because some summaries and suggested structures still subtly steered the direction of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited the client’s own criteria, constraints, and practical plan. The score is limited because the evocation focused more on feasibility than on deeper intrinsic motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned the client as the expert on his own life and allowed his contributions to shape the plan. Partnership could be stronger with more explicit negotiation of agenda, priorities, and decision-making power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk around willingness to try a small one-week step. Change talk cultivation was limited because the client’s statements were not consistently amplified, expanded, or connected to deeper values, confidence, or ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully avoided arguing with sustain talk and reframed resistance as protective of the client’s life. Some sustain talk was accepted but not fully pivoted into change, especially around cost, effort, and the dog’s dander.
