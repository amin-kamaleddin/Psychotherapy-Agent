# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, frequently affirming Erik’s choices. However, the acceptance feels more task-focused and transactional rather than consistently expressing deep, unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Erik’s practical and emotional concerns. Some emotional nuances, especially ambivalence and frustration about past failed attempts, could have been explored with greater depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains well focused on the target behaviors, moving clearly from broad concerns to specific, manageable steps. It falls short of a 5 because the focus sometimes prioritizes logistics over connecting actions to Erik’s deeper values or reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik meaningfully shapes the plan, and the counselor adapts to his preferences rather than imposing a fixed solution. However, the counselor provides a large amount of specific content, which slightly limits the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor regularly supports Erik’s sense of choice and avoids direct pressure or commands. The main limitation is the amount of prescriptive guidance offered, which can subtly shift the interaction toward counselor-led problem-solving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor makes reasonable attempts to elicit importance, confidence, and possible first steps. However, the session quickly moves into advice-giving and concrete planning, missing opportunities to draw out Erik’s deeper personal motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik is involved in decision-making, and his choices influence the direction of the session. The counselor nonetheless takes on a fairly expert role by supplying product details and behavioral scripts, which weakens the emphasis on Erik as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor helps Erik generate some change talk and acknowledges practical steps. However, he often shifts quickly into planning before reflecting, elaborating, or strengthening Erik’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor handles sustain talk adequately but does not consistently soften or redirect it using MI skills. Resistance is often accepted at face value rather than reflected, explored, or gently connected to Erik’s own values.
