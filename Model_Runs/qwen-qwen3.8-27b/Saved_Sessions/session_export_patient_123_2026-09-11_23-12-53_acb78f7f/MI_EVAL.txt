# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone. However, acceptance feels more task-oriented than deeply person-centered and does not fully validate the client’s underlying confusion or stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses are limited and mostly surface-level. The client’s ambivalence and attachment to phone reading are not meaningfully reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session lacks clear focus on the referral reason, never addressing alcohol-related risk. It shifts to sleep hygiene without a coherent motivational direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some structured choices are offered, but the interaction remains largely directive and one-directional. The client’s own ideas are not used to co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is supportive language emphasizing choice and lack of pressure. However, prescriptive plans and scripts undermine genuine autonomy by guiding the client through counselor-determined steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few evocative questions early in the session, but then shifts into advice-giving. The client’s own motivations and reasons for change are not drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some collaborative elements, such as topic choices. Overall, the session remains expert-driven rather than reflecting a true partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client identifies “sleeping more” as a small change, but this is not expanded or deepened. There is little effort to amplify desire, need, ability, or reason change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly but not effectively softened or reframed. The psychiatrist responds with more information and instructions rather than exploring the client’s ambivalence.
