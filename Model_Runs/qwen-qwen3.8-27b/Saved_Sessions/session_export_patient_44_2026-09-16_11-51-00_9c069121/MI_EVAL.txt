# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and positive regard by affirming Erik’s experience and progress without judgment. Acceptance is strong but occasionally moves quickly into problem-solving rather than consistently affirming Erik as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Erik’s practical barriers and self-critical beliefs, such as the 3 PM slump and fear of all-or-nothing thinking. Empathy could be deeper by exploring the emotional meaning behind statements like wanting to avoid numbers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on target behaviors such as gym frequency, realistic exercise, diet, and craving management. The psychiatrist keeps the conversation moving toward concrete, actionable change without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik and the psychiatrist build a plan together, but many key ideas and structures are supplied by the psychiatrist rather than elicited from Erik. Collaboration is present but not fully equal because the counselor frequently leads the planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids coercive language and affirms Erik’s choices, including making one session count and framing setbacks as data. Autonomy support is weakened by the frequent offering of detailed plans and specific options before Erik fully develops his own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out useful client-generated ideas, such as the wall plank, desk almonds, sticky note reminder, and self-compassion phrase. Evocation is limited because solutions are often offered before fully exploring Erik’s own preferences and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative language is used, and Erik is invited to choose what feels doable. Partnership is undermined by frequent expert-driven suggestions, creating an imbalance in which the psychiatrist supplies much of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik produces meaningful change talk, including wanting the gym to not feel like punishment and creating “One bar doesn’t undo the walk.” The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Erik’s concerns without harsh dismissal, such as normalizing the possibility of getting upset after eating candy. However, sustain talk is often shifted quickly into practical planning rather than fully explored or redirected toward change talk.
