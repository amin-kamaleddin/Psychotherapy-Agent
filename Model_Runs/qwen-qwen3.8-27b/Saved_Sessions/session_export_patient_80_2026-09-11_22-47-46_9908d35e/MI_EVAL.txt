# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, affirming, and nonjudgmental overall. Acceptance sometimes feels procedural or risk-focused, and the client briefly reacted to being framed as a "lost cause."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers adequate reflections and acknowledges the client's worry about slipping. Empathy remains mostly surface-level, missing deeper feelings such as being tired of "the old me" or not wanting to burden his partner.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Keeps a general focus on anger, alcohol, and the coping plan. The MI agenda is diluted by safety planning language and lacks progressive movement toward values, goals, or change reasons.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client and psychiatrist co-create a short coping note, with the client making meaningful edits. Collaboration is uneven because the therapist frequently introduces strategies, examples, and plan language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices, respects the client's decisions, and avoids pushing abstinence. Prescriptive safety instructions reduce the degree of client-led decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some open questions and allows the client to shape the plan. Evocation stays shallow and does not deeply draw out values, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and adapts to client preferences, such as removing the texting step. The psychiatrist still drives much of the content and structure rather than fully centering the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports the client's progress and action statements with affirmations. Change talk is not consistently deepened through values, goals, confidence, or commitment questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accepts partial change and helpfully renames "relapse plan" as a coping note. Sustain talk is not argued against, but the responses are not strategic enough to redirect ambivalence into change.
