# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect, warmth, and non-judgment without labeling the patient’s missed doses. However, the acceptance remains somewhat formulaic and is frequently overtaken by directive, solution-focused advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflections and normalizes the patient’s frustration and limited energy. However, the empathy remains surface-level and does not deeply explore the patient’s fear of failure, self-doubt, or emotional burden.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on medication adherence and moves toward a concrete action step involving pharmacy contact and medication delivery options. Some drift occurs when broader lifestyle suggestions are introduced, and repeated safety checks slightly interrupt the flow, but the overall direction is clear and purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely one-way, with the psychiatrist providing pre-generated options, scripts, and plans rather than truly co-creating solutions with the patient. The patient is asked to choose among counselor-generated ideas rather than contribute her own expertise or ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive invitations are offered, such as choosing the easiest step or a small task. However, the counselor imposes a high level of structure by drafting messages and creating multi-step plans, which can reduce the patient’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to elicit the patient’s own motivation, values, or reasons for change. The session quickly shifts from open questions to advisory problem-solving, with minimal use of scaling, importance, or deep motivational exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely assumes an expert role by generating solutions, scripts, and reminders rather than partnering with the patient as a co-creator. The patient’s input is considered only in the narrow sense of choosing among counselor-provided options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers clear statements of motivation and desire for change, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or builds on this change talk. The counselor tends to pivot quickly to practical planning instead of helping the patient explore and strengthen her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes some of the patient’s sustain talk, such as “I’ll start tomorrow” and low energy. However, the counselor does not actively redirect or reframe the sustain talk, nor does the patient’s ambivalence between feeling fine and the importance of adherence get explored in depth.
