# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the client’s feelings. However, acceptance is somewhat procedural and at times reinforces the client’s defensive stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects content and some emotions accurately, helping the client feel heard. Empathy remains mostly surface-level and does not fully capture deeper ambivalence, fear, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the conduct incident and hearing preparation. Direction is clear but becomes more procedural than MI-oriented, emphasizing strategy over deeper behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates some client input and avoids imposing a single solution. However, the psychiatrist largely controls the agenda, drafts, and scripts, limiting true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects the client’s boundaries. Autonomy support is limited because the options are largely therapist-generated rather than client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few early evocative questions that elicit some client acknowledgment. Overall, the psychiatrist supplies many answers, scripts, and strategies rather than drawing out the client’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows choices to shape the session. The interaction is more advisory than equal partnership, with the psychiatrist guiding much of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session opens some space for the client to acknowledge parts of the incident. Change talk is not consistently elicited, amplified, or connected to values or future consequences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s resistance. However, sustain talk is sometimes reinforced and not effectively redirected toward change or discrepancy.
