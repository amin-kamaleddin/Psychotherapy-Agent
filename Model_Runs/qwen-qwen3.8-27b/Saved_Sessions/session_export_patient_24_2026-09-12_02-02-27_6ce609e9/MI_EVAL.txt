# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance while honoring Daniel's choices and boundaries. However, the warmth remains somewhat procedural and could be more individually personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate reflective listening and understands Daniel's ambivalence and perspective. However, the responses remain mainly cognitive and miss deeper emotional or identity-based nuances.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use in relation to marathon training without significant topic drift. The direction is effective but becomes somewhat plan-centered when detailed health advice is introduced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by adapting to Daniel's preferences, tracking needs, and boundaries. The collaboration is uneven because the clinician supplies much of the structure and content of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks permission, offers choices, and respects Daniel's autonomy and weekend boundary. The score is limited because the session still moves quickly toward a structured, clinician-provided experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses several open-ended questions and evocative prompts to draw out Daniel's perspective. Overall, the session relies more on clinician-provided information and planning than on eliciting Daniel's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids an authoritarian stance while adapting to Daniel's preferences. The interaction becomes increasingly expert-led and plan-driven rather than fully client-guided.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and reinforces some of Daniel's change talk, such as willingness to test a two-day experiment. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deeply explore Daniel's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Daniel's sustain talk and redirects toward a low-risk experiment. Some responses still validate the status quo and miss opportunities for double-sided reflections that would more effectively reduce sustain talk.
