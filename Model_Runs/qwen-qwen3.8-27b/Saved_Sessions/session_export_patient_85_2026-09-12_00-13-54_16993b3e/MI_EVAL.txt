# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids criticizing the client’s past choices. However, acceptance feels somewhat practical and tied more to the client’s plan than to unconditional regard for the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathy is limited and mostly surface-level, with little reflection of the client’s frustration, pride, or ambivalence about orange juice. Responses remain largely task-focused rather than exploratory.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session has a clear and consistent focus on reducing juice intake and building a concrete action plan. There is strong momentum toward specific, behaviorally focused steps without significant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes to several decisions, such as the trial length and alternatives. However, the frequent use of checklists, scripts, and structured advice makes the interaction feel more directive than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the clinician occasionally asks for preference, the session is heavily guided with repeated suggestions, scripts, and reminders. This can make the plan feel imposed rather than fully client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful commitment and planning language through targeted questions. However, evocation is limited because the clinician often supplies reasons and strategies instead of deeply drawing out the client’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to lead in several areas and makes key decisions throughout the session. The partnership is weakened by the clinician’s repeated expert-driven advice and checklist-style structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces strong change talk, including confidence and commitment statements. The psychiatrist affirms this language but does not consistently use open questions or reflections to deepen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk and offers a helpful MI-consistent reframing about slipping. Still, there is limited active exploration or softening of the client’s ambivalence around giving up something he enjoys.
