# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent unconditional positive regard and respects Tyler’s boundaries without judgment. The opening is warm, and resistance or guardedness is met with acceptance rather than argument.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflective listening that captures Tyler’s concerns about being judged, forced to attend, and losing control. Some reflections are slightly inferential, and deeper emotional themes are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays safely on Tyler’s chosen topics but lacks a clear strategic thread toward the referral concern. The psychiatrist does not summarize themes or gently connect autonomy and boundaries to substance-use engagement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works in a highly collaborative way, letting Tyler choose topics and shape the conversation. The interaction avoids lectures, expert positioning, or taking over the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Tyler’s sense of choice, pace, and control over what is discussed. There is no pressure, advice-giving, or implied obligation to change or perform.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Tyler’s preferences for safety and manageable topics, but does not elicit his own reasons or values related to substance use. More evocative questions could better connect his autonomy to the referral concern.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong partnership stance by following Tyler’s lead and inviting his input on the session. Shared ownership could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging Tyler’s expertise in his own life and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very little change talk about substance use is elicited, and Tyler’s limited autonomy language is not amplified toward change. The focus remains on conversation comfort rather than motivation for safer behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds well to sustain talk by reflecting it without argument or confrontation. However, it is not strategically softened or pivoted toward ambivalence or engagement with the target behavior.
