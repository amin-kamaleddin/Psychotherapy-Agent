# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard. Diego’s ambivalence and current use are normalized without pressure to stop.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Diego’s ambivalence about relief and risk. However, empathy could be deeper by exploring the emotional meaning behind stress, fear, and safety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on heroin-related harm reduction. The conversation progresses logically from general risk to a concrete safety plan for Narcan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as joint problem-solving and incorporates Diego’s preferences. Collaboration could be stronger by more fully positioning Diego as the expert on his own life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Diego’s freedom, choice, and pace are repeatedly emphasized. The psychiatrist avoids directives and supports Diego’s ownership of the safety plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical intentions, especially around Narcan and safer injection. Evocation remains somewhat surface-level because it does not deeply draw out Diego’s values, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Diego’s perspective and lets him shape the plan. Partnership could improve by offering fewer structured options and allowing more client-led decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego offers some clear change talk, such as being ready to be safer and confident in two specific steps. The psychiatrist affirms this but does not consistently amplify or expand Diego’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respectfully reflects the appeal of heroin without arguing or dismissing it. Sustain talk is softened effectively, though deeper double-sided reflections could strengthen the shift toward safety.
