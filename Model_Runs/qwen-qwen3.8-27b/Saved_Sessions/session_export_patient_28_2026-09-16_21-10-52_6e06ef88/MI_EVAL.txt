# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed warmth, nonjudgmental regard, and validation without pressure or labeling. Dante’s hesitation, fatigue, and fear were treated as acceptable and understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both practical and emotional meanings, including ambivalence, embarrassment, and fear of relapse. The psychiatrist demonstrated nuanced understanding beyond simple paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior: building a small, realistic exercise step. The conversation moved logically from identifying a step to reducing barriers and clarifying meaning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked as an equal partner, building the plan around Dante’s own ideas such as the shoes, sticky note, brushing teeth, and porch step. The client’s contributions clearly shaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Dante’s choice, ownership, and self-knowledge were emphasized throughout without advice, coercion, or “should” language. The psychiatrist helped normalize skipping while preserving Dante’s sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within Dante through open-ended questions, reflections, and meaning-based prompts. He articulated his own values and identity-based reasons for change rather than receiving external persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positioned Dante as the expert on his own life and followed his suggestions. The interview was co-constructed rather than clinician-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked and reinforced desire, importance, and identity-based change talk. The score is limited by the absence of more structured elicitation of confidence, readiness, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by avoiding argumentation and responding with acceptance and curiosity. Some statements about failure and embarrassment could have been softened further with more explicit double-sided or self-compassion-focused reflections.
