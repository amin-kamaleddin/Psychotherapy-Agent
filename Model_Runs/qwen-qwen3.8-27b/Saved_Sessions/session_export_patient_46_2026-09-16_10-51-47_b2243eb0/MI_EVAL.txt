# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, using validation without shaming or moralizing. The warmth is sometimes procedural rather than deeply individualized, which prevents a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Ethan’s ambivalence and main concerns, especially around fitting in and avoiding trouble. The empathy is somewhat cognitive and does not fully explore deeper emotions such as fear, embarrassment, or autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on alcohol use in a social context and related risks. The conversation moves clearly from exploration to a concrete low-risk plan with little drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting Ethan to choose options and build a plan around his own ideas, such as using a soda, going with Tyler, and staying over. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still supplies many suggestions and options themselves.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ethan is frequently invited to choose and his boundaries are respected, including not pushing confidence higher than he wants. Repeated offering of tips and options somewhat dilutes the emphasis on his ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some of Ethan’s own motivations and solutions are elicited, especially through scaling and questions about moving confidence from 7 to 8. Frequent advice-giving and practical suggestions reduce the emphasis on his internal expertise.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some power-sharing when Ethan chooses parts of the plan, but the psychiatrist often takes an expert role by providing many harm-reduction tips. More open questions would better emphasize Ethan as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits concrete change talk, confidence increases, and a specific plan for the weekend. Deeper values or identity-based change talk is limited, and evocation is sometimes interrupted by suggestions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging Ethan’s sustain talk and acknowledges his reasons for maintaining the status quo. However, advanced softening skills are used inconsistently, and the session often moves quickly into planning rather than resolving ambivalence.
