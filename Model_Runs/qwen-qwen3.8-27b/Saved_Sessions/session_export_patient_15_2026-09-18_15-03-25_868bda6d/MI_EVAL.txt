# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with multiple affirmations of Andrei's perspective and choices. However, the acceptance remains somewhat formulaic and doesn't go deeper into individualized positive regard or acknowledge underlying vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking on Andrei's need for simplicity and trust in authority. Reflections tend to be repetitive in formulation and miss the deeper emotional undercurrents of defensiveness and anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on hypertension and alcohol use with logical progression from understanding perspective to exploring willingness. The transition to the alcohol topic could be more explicitly linked to the prior discussion of diet and medication.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some collaborative language and maintains a conversational rather than didactic tone. However, reflections subtly frame Andrei's responses in a particular way, and the overall structure remains counselor-driven rather than truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports Andrei's sense of choice, never pressures commitment, and respects his boundaries and standards. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Andrei's capacity for self-direction at key moments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes genuine evocation attempts with open questions but lacks depth and consistency. No use of scaling questions, no systematic DARN exploration, and missed opportunities to deepen Andrei's conditional willingness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative moves such as asking Andrei what 'on track' looks like and whether testing feels within his comfort zone. However, the session feels more like a guided conversation than a true partnership with co-created goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-oriented language (e.g., 'I guess I could do it') and highlights existing change talk, but does not consistently build on or amplify it. Lacks strategic follow-up, scaling, or confidence rulers to deepen the change talk that emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and uses one effective double-sided reflection. However, responses are largely neutral reflections that partially validate the sustain talk before pivoting, rather than consistently and skillfully redirecting toward change.
