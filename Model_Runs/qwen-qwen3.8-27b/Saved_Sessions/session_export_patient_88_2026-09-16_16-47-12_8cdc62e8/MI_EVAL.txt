# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while taking responsibility when the client feels judged. Some leading or problem-focused questions slightly reduce the acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening and captures the client’s social concerns well. Deeper identity-related emotions and the final “normal” statement could have been explored more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on alcohol use in the client’s weekends and social context. Some broader exploration moves away from the target behavior without a clear link back.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a collaborative partner and invites the client’s perspective. The client could have shaped the questions and agenda even more consistently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the client’s choice and avoids direct advice or pressure. Some hypothetical questions about not drinking subtly steer toward a preferred behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help the client describe his own experience, but several questions are leading. The client’s strong final change talk is not reflected or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong partnership stance and repairs the relationship when the client feels pressured. The psychiatrist still remains the primary agenda-setter in many questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or explore it. More follow-up on desire, confidence, and ability would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing and uses one effective double-sided reflection. Sustain talk is not consistently softened or redirected toward ambivalence resolution.
