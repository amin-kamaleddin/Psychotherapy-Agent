# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clinician is respectful and nonjudgmental with supportive phrases but warmth remains procedural and does not deeply affirm the courage of disclosing a stigmatizing behavior or fully normalize the fear around consequences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited brief reflections ('that sounds stressful,' 'Totally reasonable to worry') fail to capture the client's underlying fears of being labeled or his coach's reaction; shifts quickly to scripts and problem-solving instead of exploring emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session consistently maintains focus on alcohol use and its impact on soccer eligibility, scholarships, and future goals; slightly task-focused on scripting rather than guiding deeper change motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative interaction during role-play where the client tries lines and makes adjustments, but clinician supplies most content including plans, scripts, and responses; partial collaboration rather than truly equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client is allowed to stick with his chosen script line and not pressured, but early directive instructions ('stop drinking, avoid risky situations, don't drive') and consistently prescriptive approach reduce his sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opens with some evocative questions but quickly shifts to advice, education, and scripting; limited use of scaling questions, values-based exploration, or amplification of the client's own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client input is invited and options offered, but overall stance is largely expert-driven with rapid provision of plans, rules, and scripts; uneven power-sharing with clinician controlling session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client offers spontaneous change talk about wanting to keep playing and get a scholarship, but clinician doesn't consistently reflect, amplify, or expand on it; instead frequently introduces external reasons like health risks and eligibility.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Doesn't aggressively challenge sustain talk like 'not saying I'm an addict,' but also doesn't skillfully soften, reframe, or redirect toward change; focus on avoidance scripts may inadvertently support maintaining the status quo.
