# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and nonjudgmental positive regard throughout — Dante's inactivity, self-criticism, and hesitation are never met with judgment or premature praise. Validation feels genuine and individualized (e.g., 'That's not nothing,' 'I respect that') rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Empathic reflections are precise and go beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture emotional depth — e.g., naming the loneliness Dante hadn't articulated and connecting his current self-doubt to his earlier stated pattern. Reflections are specific, non-repetitive, and progressively deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session progresses logically from barriers → emotional cost → social support → specific plan → implementation trigger, with each question building on the last. Focus on the target behavior is maintained without rigidity, and the session moves from contemplation toward a concrete action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist functions as a thinking partner rather than an expert — the implementation plan is entirely Dante's creation, co-constructed through dialogue but never prescribed. Meta-awareness of the collaborative process reinforces it ('You came in bracing for someone to tell you what to do, and instead you just figured it out yourself').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and consistently reinforced — the psychiatrist honors Dante's boundaries, attributes ideas to him ('That's your own thinking doing the work'), and never uses directive language. The entire session is structured around Dante's own choices of what 'enough' means and what his trigger would be.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every insight in the session is Dante's own, elicited through skilled open questions and strategic reflections rather than provided by the psychiatrist. The evocation is consistent and progressive, moving from general desire to specific ability to concrete planning without ever telling Dante what to want or believe.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Dante as the expert on his own life — asking rather than telling, attributing the plan to Dante, and never offering an external solution. Dante himself confirms the partnership was felt: 'I came in thinking I'd just sit here and be told to go for a walk.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk moves through desire, ability, and need, with the psychiatrist consistently catching and amplifying each instance ('I'm hearing that in your voice — not as a guess'). Open questions like 'What would make you think that one counts' directly elicit Dante's own commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated first, then gently explored for its underlying function rather than challenged — the 'all or nothing' belief is examined as a pattern, and Dante is helped to define his own threshold of 'enough.' The psychiatrist never argues against Dante's stated barriers but helps him see their cost.
