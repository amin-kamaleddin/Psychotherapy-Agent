# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and normalizing stance without being judgmental. However, the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper, personalized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides adequate, surface-level reflections of his concerns about time pressures and past failures. It misses deeper emotional themes such as his shame and grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays connected to the target behavior but lacks consistency. The initial response dilutes the focus with multiple lifestyle suggestions, and later activity options feel scattered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts collaboration by offering a menu of options but mostly acts as a clinician leading the session. She converts the patient's contributions into counselor-designed plans rather than exploring his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and reinforces flexibility without overt pressure. However, the support is mixed with a directive style and lacks explicit reinforcement of the patient's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Demonstrates limited evocation by asking preference questions but does not deeply elicit the patient's internal motivations or values. She frequently provides practical suggestions instead of drawing out his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Uses some collaborative language but remains largely expert-driven and counselor-led. She frequently generates structured plans rather than letting the patient co-construct the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited efforts to cultivate change talk by asking which small action feels least disruptive. The psychiatrist fails to use core strategies to elicit, reflect, or expand on the patient's emerging motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Acknowledges barriers without arguing but quickly shifts into offering alternatives. The response lacks strategic double-sided reflections to gently soften and resolve the patient's ambivalence.
