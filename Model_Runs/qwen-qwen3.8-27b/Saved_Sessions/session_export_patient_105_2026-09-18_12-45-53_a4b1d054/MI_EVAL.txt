# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental stance throughout, with validating language and no labeling of Naomi as broken or weak. Affirmations are well-placed but occasionally feel formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, capturing emotional nuances like the 'courtroom' metaphor and the 'annoying' statement. Reflections sometimes stay at the cognitive level rather than reaching the deeper emotional vulnerability beneath Naomi's words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on social anxiety and its impact but lacks a clear strategic arc or progressive focus. The conversation meanders through relevant topics without building toward a specific change goal or using summaries to create momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with language positioning Naomi as the expert on her own experience, actively building on her metaphors and contributions. However, the collaboration is slightly one-directional in practice, with Naomi's input not visibly redirecting the session's trajectory.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports Naomi's choice and self-direction without pressuring or prescribing solutions, reinforcing that she doesn't need to have answers yet. Autonomy support could be stronger with more explicit affirmations of her right to choose and ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses open questions to elicit meaningful self-exploration, but misses key opportunities to deepen change motivation from statements like 'I used to be okay with things' and the frustration in 'it's annoying.' Evocation is present but superficial relative to the rich material Naomi provides, with no use of scaling questions or strategic building on change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-directive stance with language like 'I want to sit in that uncertainty with you' and 'I'd be curious.' However, occasional framing subtly positions the psychiatrist as the one who 'sees' or 'notices' what's happening, creating a slight hierarchy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some work to evoke change talk through open questions, and Naomi does articulate change-related language, but the psychiatrist doesn't consistently reinforce, amplify, or strategically build on these moments. Key change talk seeds (past ability, frustration with the stuck narrative) are reflected but not deepened or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcing it, and the psychiatrist reflects the weight of statements like 'I've been doing this for so long' without arguing. However, the psychiatrist does not actively use double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to create tension between the sustain narrative and the change evidence Naomi herself provides.
