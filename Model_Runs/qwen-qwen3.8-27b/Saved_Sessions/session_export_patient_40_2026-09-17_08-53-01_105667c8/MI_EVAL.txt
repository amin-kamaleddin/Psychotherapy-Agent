# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validated Elias’s experience with respectful, nonjudgmental language. Affirmations of his strength and worth were strong, but could be more explicit about his inherent value independent of recovery outcomes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Elias’s ambivalence, fear, and desire to be present for his children. Deeper reflections on shame, relapse fear, and the meaning of three months of sobriety would increase emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a concrete, recovery-relevant goal: being steady and present during weekend time with his children. The connection between this behavioral target and broader alcohol/pill recovery could be made more explicit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation was built around Elias’s own ideas, including the walk, question, body cues, and self-phrase. The psychiatrist consistently reflected and expanded client-generated solutions, making collaboration central to the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided pressure and repeatedly positioned Elias as the author of his own plan. Explicit permission to modify or set aside the plan would further reinforce autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively elicited Elias’s own goals, actions, body awareness, and confidence. Evocation would be stronger if it more fully explored values, identity, and long-term recovery motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited Elias to lead the content of the plan. More explicit agenda negotiation or invitations for Elias to choose the next focus would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out meaningful change talk such as “I can do it,” “that’s a win,” and “That’s enough.” More deliberate elicitation across values, identity, and future hope would deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubt and sustain talk were acknowledged without argument or reinforcement. Use of stronger double-sided reflections or strategic reframing would make the softening more active.
