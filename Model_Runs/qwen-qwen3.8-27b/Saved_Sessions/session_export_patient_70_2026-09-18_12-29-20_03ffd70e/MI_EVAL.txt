# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, warm unconditional positive regard with no judgment or labeling. Key phrases like 'You don't owe anyone an explanation' and 'You're not a project to be tracked' affirm the client's identity and choices without condition.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Shows highly accurate and complex reflective listening that captures both content, meaning, and emotion. Creative metaphors like 'the forms have two locks' and temporal insights about the 'nah' disappearing after the first beer demonstrate deep understanding of the client's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, purposeful arc from exploration of ambivalence through to a concrete action plan, with each step building logically on the last. Consistently ties every conversation thread back to the target behavior of reducing reoffending risk without ever losing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration with genuine power sharing, where the client's ideas shape the entire direction. The closing frame — 'a plan you built yourself' — explicitly positions the session as client-led rather than clinician-delivered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides exceptional autonomy support by explicitly removing accountability pressure and never prescribing or implying the client should do anything. Statements like 'no strings, no expectation' and 'the schedule is yours' reinforce self-determination at every turn.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every insight in the session — the video solution, the timing/alcohol connection, the gym idea, the kit in the bag — originates from the client, elicited through skillful open questions. The psychiatrist never provides information or solutions, purely creating conditions for the client to generate their own motivations and plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on their own life, with explicit statements like 'You know yourself better than I do.' The psychiatrist functions as a container rather than an authority, never providing recommendations or taking the lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through questions that elicit the client's own motivations and solutions, then reinforces it by naming it back. The final summary amplifies change talk and attributes ownership directly to the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without arguing or minimizing, instead validating the weight of the belief and gently pivoting toward change. The closing exchange where the client anticipates doubt is met with acceptance rather than correction, trusting the process.
