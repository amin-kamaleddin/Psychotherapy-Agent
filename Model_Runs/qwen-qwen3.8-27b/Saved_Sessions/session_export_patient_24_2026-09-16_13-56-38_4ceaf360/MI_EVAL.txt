# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates Daniel’s ambivalence without lecturing or blaming. The stance is safe but somewhat procedural and could be warmer or more affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures key themes such as health, marathon energy, and wanting to stay in charge. Empathy is often content-level and misses deeper feelings like defensiveness or fear of being pathologized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear target behavior or specific change goal related to alcohol use. It stays in a low-pressure monitoring frame without building consistent momentum toward a defined change step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Daniel as an equal partner and consistently invites his input to shape the process. No agenda is imposed, and Daniel’s preferences clearly guide the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through nonpressuring language and repeated emphasis on Daniel’s right to decide. There is no advice-giving, pressure, or insistence on a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Daniel’s own definitions for useful signals and gut-based noticing. However, it does not deeply draw out his values, reasons, or confidence related to reducing or reconsidering alcohol use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces that Daniel is the expert on his own body, judgment, and readiness. The client’s contributions substantially direct the session without a prescriptive stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit, reinforce, or expand Daniel’s change-relevant statements. Opportunities to build motivation using values, marathon goals, or discrepancy are largely missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully without argument or blame, and some language avoids the righting reflex. The psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or pivot skepticism toward change.
