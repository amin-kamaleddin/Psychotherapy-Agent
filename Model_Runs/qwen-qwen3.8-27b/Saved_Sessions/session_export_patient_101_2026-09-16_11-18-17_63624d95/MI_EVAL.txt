# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Mei’s ambivalence and effort. The warmth is helpful but somewhat task-oriented, with room for deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Mei’s concerns about family history, alcohol, and contraception. Empathy is strong but often cognitive rather than emotionally nuanced, missing deeper fear, guilt, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol risk and improving contraceptive reliability. Momentum is maintained by repeatedly tying options to doability and confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames work as shared problem-solving and co-creates practical details with Mei. Collaboration is strong, though the clinician often initiates multiple options before fully ceding agenda control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mei is offered choices, pacing is respected, and she decides among backup options. The interaction can become solution-focused, with clinician-generated suggestions slightly overriding self-directed planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses confidence scaling and questions to draw out some motivation, but leans heavily on structured options and fixes. More open-ended elicitation of Mei’s values, past successes, and own ideas is needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites Mei’s input and lets her preferences shape the plan. A more fully balanced partnership would emphasize Mei as the expert and reduce expert-driven option menus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and affirmations effectively evoke and reinforce Mei’s reasons, confidence, and momentum. The psychiatrist sometimes moves quickly from evocation to advice rather than amplifying change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or dismissal, and hesitation is normalized or adjusted. More double-sided reflections and value-based pivots would make softening even more MI-specific.
