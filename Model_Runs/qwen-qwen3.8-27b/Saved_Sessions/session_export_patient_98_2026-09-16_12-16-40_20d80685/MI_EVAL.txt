# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, validating stance toward Mateo’s pain, discouragement, and plan choices. A few mildly evaluative phrases (“strong, realistic,” “wise”) slightly reduce the unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening is shown for pain, discouragement, and concern about burdening Maria, with some acknowledgment of ambivalence. It remains partly surface-level and returns quickly to problem-solving rather than exploring deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has clear focus on the target rehab behavior and consistently returns to the 5-minute plan and mailbox/counter goals. Direction occasionally becomes overly logistical, reducing motivational depth.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a manageable plan incorporating Mateo’s preferences and keeps it collaborative overall. Still, many options are psychiatrist-generated rather than elicited from Mateo, limiting fuller client-generated alternatives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo’s choices are respected through options, acceptance of the 5-minute commitment, and no pressure to add tasks or tracking. The autonomy support is strong but mostly behavioral rather than explicitly stated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful client-generated content is elicited, including the goal, confidence ratings, and plan details. However, the session relies heavily on counselor-generated options and does not deeply evoke Mateo’s values, self-efficacy, or motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Mateo’s input and allows his choices to shape the plan in a non-authoritarian way. A more explicit stance would include more space for Mateo to propose next steps without being guided through a structured checklist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several attempts to elicit and affirm change language, including goals, interest, and confidence increases. The development remains limited because the psychiatrist frequently interrupts with suggestions and task-focused questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo’s sustain talk is handled nonconfrontationally and normalized, with redirection toward manageable options like stopping if pain increases. A stronger double-sided reflection could have more explicitly acknowledged both sides while moving toward change.
