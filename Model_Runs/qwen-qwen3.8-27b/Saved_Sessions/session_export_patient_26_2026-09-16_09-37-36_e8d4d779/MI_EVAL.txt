# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, validating Daniela's stress and efforts. Acceptance is strong but slightly imperfect because some responses are formulaic or momentarily center another person rather than fully unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Daniela's practical stress accurately and compassionately. Empathy is limited by a tendency to focus on the problem rather than naming deeper emotions such as shame, fear of failing as a mother, or ambivalence about alcohol use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the main concern of childcare and its related impacts on work, school, stress, and coping. Direction is clear and organized, though it is more clinician-led than collaboratively constructed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Daniela's input and adapts to her preferences, such as shortening the message or changing the thank-you offer. However, much of the practical content is counselor-generated, making the collaboration advisor-heavy rather than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and respects Daniela's limits, including not pressuring her to stop drinking immediately. Autonomy support could be stronger by encouraging Daniela to generate her own options and wording rather than supplying specific scripts and plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies heavily on practical problem-solving and choice among pre-generated options rather than evoking Daniela's own motivations. Important change talk and ambivalence are present but not consistently deepened with MI-style questions, rulers, or values exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela is invited to participate and her preferences influence the conversation, which supports some partnership. The psychiatrist often remains the primary generator of solutions, limiting the extent to which Daniela is positioned as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Daniela expresses some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently affirm, expand, or deepen it. The counselor mostly shifts into practical problem-solving rather than eliciting Daniela's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Daniela's sustain talk but often responds with advice or harm-reduction options. The session does not consistently use MI-consistent techniques such as double-sided reflections to explore ambivalence and shift sustain talk toward change.
