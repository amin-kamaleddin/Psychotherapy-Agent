# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and consistently normalizes Diego's ambivalence without labeling his drug use. Warmth is strong but can feel slightly formulaic, and more explicit normalization of repeated relapse would strengthen it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Diego's ambivalence, fear of his housemate's reaction, and underlying anxiety. Some moments summarize the practical content more than the deeper emotional pain or loneliness beneath it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on harm reduction and concrete safety planning around heroin use. There is occasional drift into broader emotional discussion that could more consistently tie back to specific safety actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Diego's input and builds on his practical ideas rather than imposing expert advice. Collaboration could be stronger when the psychiatrist preframes an interpretation before fully following Diego's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Diego's ownership, pacing, and right to choose are consistently reinforced throughout the session. The psychiatrist never pressures him toward abstinence or a bigger plan and honors his decisions as valid.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Diego's own motivations, values, and agency using open-ended and double-sided questions. Evocation could be deepened with more follow-ups exploring confidence, past successes, and underlying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance and treats Diego as central to the session. Partnership could improve by more explicitly negotiating the agenda and acknowledging Diego's right to steer the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego's change talk is strong and is supported through reflection and questions that elicit his own motivations. The psychiatrist could further develop this by eliciting confidence language and connecting it to past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument or dismissal, using reflections that honor both the relief heroin provides and Diego's desire for safety. More active redirections toward smaller, concrete steps would make this even stronger.
