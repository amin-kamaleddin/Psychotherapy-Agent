# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer is respectful and nonjudgmental, with statements that validate the patient’s frustration and right not to quit. However, the warmth feels somewhat formulaic and procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer reflects key themes such as the importance of the break and annoyance with the new rule. The reflections are repetitive and often interrupted by advice, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the workplace smoke-free policy and a practical trial using lozenges and a window break. The direction is more directive than MI-consistent, moving quickly toward a specific intervention and repeated safety instructions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient chooses the lozenge and window break, but the interviewer functions more as an expert providing options and instructions. Greater collaboration would involve eliciting the patient’s ideas and preferred solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer supports the patient’s right not to quit and frames the plan as work management rather than cessation. Autonomy is supported in the broad goal, but repeatedly undermined by prescriptive dosing, tracking, and behavioral instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly practical rather than evocative, with little eliciting of personal values, confidence, or self-efficacy. The patient’s change talk is not systematically drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interviewer invites input in places, but the session is largely expert-driven with multiple options and instructions supplied. The patient’s contributions often respond to the interviewer’s menu rather than shaping the session from the patient’s own perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are several moments of client movement, such as agreeing to try the lozenge and window break. The interviewer does not sufficiently amplify, reflect, or deepen this change talk with importance, confidence, or values-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interviewer does not argue with sustain talk and acknowledges the patient’s unwillingness to quit. However, the interviewer rarely pivots toward ambivalence or change, leaving the focus on maintaining smoking rather than exploring conflicting motivations.
