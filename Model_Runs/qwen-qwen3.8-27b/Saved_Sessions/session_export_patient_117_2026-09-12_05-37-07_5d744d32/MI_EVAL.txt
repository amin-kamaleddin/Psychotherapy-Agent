# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, affirming, and nonjudgmental tone throughout. Positive regard is strong, though it is more tied to progress than to unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is clear and helps the client feel understood. Empathy remains largely cognitive/behavioral and could explore deeper emotions more consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking reduction, urges, and practical coping at work. Later plan details could be more explicitly linked back to the goal of reducing to five cigarettes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s preferences meaningfully shape the plan, and the therapist responds supportively. Some therapist-generated structure and examples make the collaboration feel more guided than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects choice, avoids pressure, and frames tools as options rather than demands. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client’s right to change or decline the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client-generated ideas and confidence statements are elicited through scaling and open questions. The therapist introduces strategies before fully exploring the client’s values, motivations, and own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist invites input and positions the client as an expert on his own preferences. The interaction is collaborative, though the fairly complete plan supplied by the therapist makes it somewhat directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and scaling effectively elicit client-generated reasons, confidence, and plans. Change talk could be deepened and amplified further before moving toward problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument, and slips or ambivalence are reframed constructively. More consistent use of double-sided reflections would better honor both sides of ambivalence.
