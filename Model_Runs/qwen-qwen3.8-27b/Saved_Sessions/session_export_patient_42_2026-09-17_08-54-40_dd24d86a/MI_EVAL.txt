# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently uses nonjudgmental, normalizing language and shows respect for the client’s ambivalence. A limitation is subtle leaning toward the clinician’s health agenda, which can feel slightly directive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening around the social meaning of smoking and fear of exclusion. Reflections become repetitive and do not always deepen emotional meaning such as shame, identity, or loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation during social breaks. However, the conversation becomes circular and lacks strategic movement toward values, readiness, or a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works with the client’s experience and uses collaborative language to explore competing needs. Repeated similar questions and problem-solving prompts make the collaboration feel somewhat guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice and avoids pressure to commit or make a decision. Could strengthen the client’s ownership and capacity by more explicitly affirming that change is her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses open questions but focuses mainly on behavior replacement rather than deeper change talk. Evocative momentum is reduced by repetitive questioning and limited exploration of values or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites the client’s perspective and avoids directive advice. Therapist-led patterns, including consecutive reflections and suggested options, slightly limit a fully co-created stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks some evocative questions about motivation and change, and uses client-relevant concerns such as energy. The client’s change talk is not consistently amplified or expanded, leaving the session more problem-focused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and normalizes the social benefits of smoking without arguing. The discussion remains heavily centered on difficulty and exclusion rather than pivoting toward values or alternative belonging.
