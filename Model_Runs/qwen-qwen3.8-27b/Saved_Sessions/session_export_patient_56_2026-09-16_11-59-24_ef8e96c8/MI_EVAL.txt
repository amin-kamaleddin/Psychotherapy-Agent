# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Isolde's concerns about family, guilt, and work demands. Acceptance could become stronger by more explicitly affirming her values and character rather than mainly staying procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflections that capture surface-level meaning and often summarize the client's experience accurately. However, empathy is frequently brief and shifts quickly into practical options, missing deeper emotional and meaning-level reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target behavior of protecting family time, reducing phone checking, and involving her partner. Even when Isolde raises work, guilt, or partner concerns, the psychiatrist keeps the discussion connected to the main goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Isolde to choose among options, which supports collaboration. However, much of the planning is structured and supplied by the psychiatrist, limiting how central Isolde's own ideas and expertise become.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and avoids pressuring Isolde to commit, supporting her freedom to decide. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming that she is the expert on her own family and that the plan is entirely her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions early in the session, such as the scaling and recent-example questions. However, the session quickly moves toward options and scripts rather than consistently eliciting and expanding Isolde's own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Isolde's preferences and asks her to choose among options, which creates a collaborative stance. Yet the frequent use of pre-made scripts and structured options makes the session feel more like counselor-led planning than a balanced partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isolde expresses meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it at times. The psychiatrist could do more to reflect, amplify, and deepen this change talk so Isolde more fully owns her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Isolde expresses several forms of ambivalence, including guilt, fear of missing work, and worry about burdening her partner. The psychiatrist normalizes these concerns but mostly moves into problem-solving rather than using MI responses to soften and redirect the sustain talk.
