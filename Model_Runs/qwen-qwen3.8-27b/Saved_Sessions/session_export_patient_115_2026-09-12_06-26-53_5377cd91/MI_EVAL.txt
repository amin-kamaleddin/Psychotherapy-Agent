# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained unconditional positive regard and nonjudgmental support throughout. They validated the client’s difficulty with quitting while reinforcing effort and progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** They used reflective listening to capture the client’s goals around work, family, and breathing. Deeper exploration of fear, exhaustion, and ambivalence would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma-related flare-ups and practical behavioral changes. It moved efficiently from broad concerns to a specific morning-shift trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-created the plan with the client rather than prescribing solutions. The client’s ideas shaped the visor note, alarm timing, and success criteria.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client’s right to choose was consistently respected, including trying gum without promising full abstinence. Language emphasized ownership, no judgment, and doable commitments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling helped draw out the client’s motivations and readiness. More evocation of deeper personal meaning and values would further strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the client as the expert on his own life and situation. The client’s preferences clearly shaped the final plan and follow-up approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited desire and reasons for change related to work, children, and breathing. More explicit reflection and amplification of values could deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled without arguing or reinforcing avoidance. More explicit double-sided reflections could better honor ambivalence while guiding toward change.
