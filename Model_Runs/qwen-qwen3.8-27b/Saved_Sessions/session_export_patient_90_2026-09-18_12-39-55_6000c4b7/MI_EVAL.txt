# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and affirming without judgment or dismissal. The psychiatrist validates Marcus's concerns about his dog, finances, and exhaustion using his own words, creating a genuine sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional reflective listening with complex, accurate reflections that capture emotional meaning beneath Marcus's words. Examples include reflecting 'it's not just breathing, it's everything that breathing threatens' and recognizing the protective function behind 'at least then I'm not failing at it.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear strategic progression from general overwhelm to a concrete first step (the gate). However, the session allows some drift into broader themes and ends with Marcus still in sustain talk territory, suggesting tighter directional focus in the final exchanges.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuinely equal partnership where the psychiatrist's process expertise and Marcus's life expertise are both respected. The session feels like collaborative exploration rather than a clinical interview, with the psychiatrist actively following Marcus's lead while contributing meaningful reflections.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Marcus's ownership of choices through questions that elicit his own goals and explicit attribution of agency ('That's your thinking, not someone else's plan'). Never prescribes, advises, or pushes, and explicitly contrasts this approach with the directive model Marcus is used to.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of evocative questions like 'What would a win look like for you?' that lead Marcus to articulate his own goals and generate the gate idea independently. Could be stronger in the latter portion by evoking the readiness and confidence Marcus demonstrated mid-session when he slipped back into sustain talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise about Marcus's life resides within him, never taking an expert stance or providing solutions. The psychiatrist's vulnerability ('That stuck with me, and I'm glad it landed') humanizes the interaction and reinforces equality throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies change talk, particularly the powerful 'not fighting for air in my own bedroom' image and the shift from 'nothing feels like a win' to the gate idea. Could have more deeply explored confidence/ability change talk moments like 'I'd figure it out' to build further momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully validates sustain talk without arguing or minimizing, such as affirming the attachment to the dog and the 'back to square one' feeling. However, the session ends with Marcus still in sustain talk territory, suggesting missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or gentle pivots at key moments.
