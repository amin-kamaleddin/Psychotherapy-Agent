# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, reducing stigma by framing the session as being about the client rather than a label. However, the warmth feels partly scripted and does not fully validate the client as a whole person or normalize adolescent ambivalence and alcohol risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate reflective listening to the client’s academic and musical demands, showing effort to understand her experience. However, the reflections remain mostly cognitive and surface-level, with limited exploration of underlying anxiety, stress, shame, or avoidance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear, sustained focus on the referral concern of elevated alcohol risk. The conversation drifts into schoolwork, band rehearsals, and practice time without consistently linking these topics to drinking or risk reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is relatively collaborative, using open questions and following the client’s lead through topics such as quiet practice and academic pressure. However, collaboration could be strengthened by more explicitly co-creating the session agenda and treating the client as the primary expert on her own life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by emphasizing choice, avoiding pressure, and respecting the client’s limited motivation. This is a clear strength, though it is not fully connected to the alcohol-related target behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several open-ended questions draw out the client’s perspective and allow her to identify the value of quiet practice time. However, the evocation is superficial and not sufficiently tied to the client’s values, goals, or motivation to reduce alcohol risk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of collaboration, but the psychiatrist still drives the direction of the conversation more than the client. The session does not fully establish power-sharing or a co-created agenda centered on the client’s own priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some weak attempts to evoke choice and self-care, but the session does not meaningfully cultivate change talk related to alcohol use or risk reduction. The psychiatrist does not consistently evoke desire, ability, reasons, or need for change around the target behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing against the client’s reasons for staying the same and reflects both sides without persuasion. However, the response does not consistently help the client see the cost of staying stuck or the possibility of change.
