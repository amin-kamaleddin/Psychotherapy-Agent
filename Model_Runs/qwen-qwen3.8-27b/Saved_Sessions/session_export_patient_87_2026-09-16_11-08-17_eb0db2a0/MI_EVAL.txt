# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers nonjudgmental, supportive, and affirming responses. It is not a 5 because the warmth occasionally feels task-focused rather than deeply individualized to Marco’s emotional burden.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Marco’s ambivalence, family pressures, and fear of conflict. It is not a 5 because empathy often stays at summarizing the problem and plan rather than exploring deeper emotions such as guilt, shame, or self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on a concrete weight-management behavior change. The discussion moves efficiently toward a specific two-week plan with a defined start date, supports, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently works as a partner, incorporating Marco’s preferences and shaping the plan around his home life. It is not a 5 because the counselor still relies somewhat on pre-made scripts and options rather than fully co-creating from Marco’s own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco is given meaningful choices and control over the plan, including timing, snack money, and backup support. Autonomy support is not perfect because the counselor still offers many specific scripts and directives that could reduce client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Marco’s motivation, confidence, and plan through scaling and reflective questions. It is not a 5 because the counselor also supplies many solutions and scripts rather than consistently helping Marco generate his own reasons and ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative stance, but the balance is uneven because the counselor frequently generates options and scripts. This can position the therapist as the main idea generator rather than fully eliciting Marco’s own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk by asking about importance, confidence, and what would help the plan succeed. It is not a 5 because the counselor often supplies options and next steps instead of consistently inviting Marco to generate deeper change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes and refrains from arguing with Marco’s concerns about failure, family resistance, and relapse. It is not a 5 because some relapse-focused language keeps attention on slipping, and the counselor could more consistently redirect toward change talk.
