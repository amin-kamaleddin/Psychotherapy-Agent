# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive while reducing shame and pressure. Acceptance is strong but could be more deeply individualized around regret and self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening and perspective-taking capture the client's ambivalence, isolation, and relational pain. Some reflections are slightly leading or interpretive before fully following the client's stated experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear target around drug use, regret, and isolation, but then drifts toward loneliness and feeling stuck. Focus is thematically connected but not sharply maintained on a specific substance-use behavior or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and frames the conversation as a joint exploration. Collaboration is effective but remains therapist-guided, with less clear co-creation of the agenda or mutual decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives, advice, and pressure while allowing the client to identify what matters. Autonomy is supported but would be stronger with more explicit affirmation of the client's right to choose and ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently uses open, client-centered questions that attempt to elicit motivation from within. Evocation is present but not fully effective because the client's change talk is not consistently developed or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on her own experience and avoids an authoritarian stance. Partnership is strong, though the client's voice does not fully shape the session's focus or structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several evocative questions draw out change talk, such as wanting to stop feeling alone and needing support. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or connect this change talk to a concrete drug-use goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and reflects the client's ambivalence. However, the session remains centered on helplessness and negative beliefs without consistently redirecting toward curiosity about possibility or change.
