# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental, with frequent normalization of Anya's experience and feelings about the referral. Slightly performative at times (repeated phrasing) and lacks an explicit statement of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures Anya's emotional state and perspective, including a sophisticated observation about her finding language for a previously unarticulated insight. Could have gone deeper on emotional undercurrents, such as the self-doubt in comparing her role to her mother's nursing career.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session drifts entirely from its stated purpose (supportive conversation about early alcohol exposure) into a general identity and career exploration. The alcohol screening is mentioned once at the opening and never revisited; the target behavior is never reconnected to the conversation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and a non-directive tone, but the power dynamic remains heavily tilted toward the counselor. Anya functions as a respondent rather than a co-creator; she never steers the conversation or is explicitly invited to set the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports Anya's autonomy with no prescriptive language, validating her right to be uncertain and framing all insights as her own. Could be stronger by more explicitly affirming her ownership of decisions and her expertise in her own life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively evokes Anya's own insights and language (e.g., 'make someone's day less terrible'), but directs this skill entirely toward identity and purpose rather than the clinical target. No evocation of Anya's values in relation to substance use or motivation for any specific behavior change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance with consistent invitations for Anya's perspective and no lecturing or advice-giving. However, the partnership is one-directional in practice—Anya never redirects the conversation or takes the lead, and the psychiatrist does not explicitly acknowledge her expertise in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No questions are designed to elicit change talk related to alcohol or health-protective behaviors, and there is no DARN-C elicitation at all. The session never connects Anya's values or goals to any specific target behavior, making the MI change process essentially absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Very little sustain talk appears in the session, so the criterion is largely untested. The psychiatrist handles the limited sustain talk (confusion about referral, feeling it doesn't match her life) with gentle normalization but does not demonstrate specific softening techniques such as double-sided reflections.
