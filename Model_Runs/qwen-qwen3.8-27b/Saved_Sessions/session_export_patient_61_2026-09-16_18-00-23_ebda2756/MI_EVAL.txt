# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Jonathan’s overwhelm. A few subtle prompts toward action slightly temper the warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects ambivalence, autonomy, and fear of being nagged. A stronger rating would require deeper exploration of underlying emotions such as shame or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on diabetes self-management and narrows the problem to a manageable medication step. It becomes somewhat repetitive and could consolidate progress more tightly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frames the work as a joint exploration and builds on Jonathan’s stated preferences. Collaboration is strong, though the counselor still leads the process rather than fully sharing agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated emphasis on choice, control, and avoiding external monitoring. It could become stronger by even more explicitly affirming Jonathan’s right to choose whether or not to act.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some client-generated ideas, especially around a simple counter placement. Evocation remains mostly logistical rather than motivational, with limited exploration of values, confidence, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently invites Jonathan’s input and positions him as the expert on what would fit his life. A higher score would require more explicit acknowledgment of his expertise and shared ownership of the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits a concrete change statement about placing the bottle on the counter. Change talk is not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened using DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is reflected neutrally without argument or reinforcement. The counselor could more actively pivot concerns toward change, such as through double-sided reflections or explicit holding of both sides.
