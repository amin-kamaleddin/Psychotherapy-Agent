# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, validation, and nonjudgment toward the client’s behavior. This strong acceptance helps normalize the session and reduces defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate and helpful reflections of the client’s concerns about basketball, school, and fitting in. However, some reflections remain more cognitive than feeling-focused, missing deeper emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on the target behavior and its connection to school, basketball, and the client’s plan. Direction could be stronger by linking early administrative concerns back to values and change more efficiently.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats the client as an equal partner rather than an authority figure. The client actively co-creates the plan and direction of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure, commands, or prescriptive advice, and treats the client as the decision-maker. The client’s own ideas are respected and reflected throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits client-generated change talk, especially around sober performance and confidence. Deeper exploration of values, identity, and motivation could strengthen this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong collaborative stance by inviting the client to direct the conversation and clarify priorities. The client’s agenda and preferences shape the session in meaningful ways.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully reinforces and builds on the client’s own change statements about playing better sober. More systematic development of change talk into stronger commitment, ability, or values would improve this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client and validates the social and practical pulls toward continued use. More consistent use of double-sided reflections or gentle pivots could better balance sustain talk with change talk.
