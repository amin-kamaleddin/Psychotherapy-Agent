# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance throughout, validating Samuel’s concerns without labeling or scolding. However, the acceptance remains somewhat task-focused and does not fully express deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture Samuel’s ambivalence and fear of self-judgment. A limitation is that the reflections stay largely at the content level and do not fully explore the deeper emotions or both sides of ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking reduction, especially using the journal to identify triggers during stress, work, and driving. A stronger direction would more consistently connect journal mechanics back to Samuel’s broader smoking-reduction goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a workable plan with Samuel, incorporating his preferences for brevity, neutrality, and weekly check-ins. However, the psychiatrist still initiates several options and structures rather than allowing the plan to emerge entirely from Samuel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported by framing the journal as Samuel’s choice and emphasizing that he is “in the driver’s seat.” There are a few mildly directive moments that slightly reduce the emphasis on complete client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Samuel’s practical ideas about how to use the journal and what would make it workable. The evocation is less strong in drawing out Samuel’s deeper personal motivation, values, and confidence in reducing smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a partnership stance by inviting Samuel’s input and affirming his expertise on his own life. However, the psychiatrist still offers specific suggestions and structures, so the session is collaborative but not fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encourages Samuel to generate his own ideas and elicits some change talk, such as wanting a simpler process and reviewing high-urge moments. However, there is limited amplification, reinforcement, or scaling of Samuel’s change language and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Samuel’s concerns and reframes sustain talk in ways that keep the session moving forward. The main limitation is that the response to sustain talk could more consistently use double-sided reflections that hold both concern and change goals.
