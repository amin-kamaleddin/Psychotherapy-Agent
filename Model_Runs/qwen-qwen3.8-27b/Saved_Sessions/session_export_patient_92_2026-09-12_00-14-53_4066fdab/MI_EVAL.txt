# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids labeling the client while affirming his honesty and willingness to try. Acceptance is solid but remains more procedural and safety-oriented than deeply affirming of Marek’s values, identity, or strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects surprise, defensiveness, and some emotional meaning early in the session. However, empathy does not consistently explore the deeper tension between Marek’s desire to protect his health and his need for pleasure, social belonging, and autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing risky alcohol use and builds toward a clear, concrete behavioral experiment. The direction is generally appropriate, though it moves somewhat quickly into planning before fully exploring Marek’s ambivalence and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Marek to help shape the plan, including choosing limits, monitoring targets, and social strategies. Collaboration is strong, but the psychiatrist still provides much of the structure rather than fully co-constructing every element with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by asking permission, offering options, respecting refusal of a stricter plan, and framing slips as information rather than failure. Marek is consistently treated as the decision-maker about what he is willing to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-led change by asking open questions and accepting Marek’s own “no two nights in a row” idea. However, the session shifts quickly to practical advice and plan details instead of repeatedly eliciting Marek’s values, confidence, reasons for change, and perceived ability.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an expert stance, asks permission, offers choices, and lets Marek’s input meaningfully shape the final plan. Partnership is strong, though it could be deepened by more explicitly affirming Marek as the expert on his own life and social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces emerging change talk such as Marek’s willingness to try a flexible plan and monitor sleep. More deliberate DARN-C-style evocation would be needed to consistently deepen the client’s own motivation and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Marek’s sustain talk and uses harm reduction to lower pressure. Advanced MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or strategic pivots, are not consistently used to address the meaning of drinking and the pull of social pleasure.
