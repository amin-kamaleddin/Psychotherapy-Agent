# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive without moralizing about alcohol use. Warmth is generally strong but could be more emotionally validating, especially around feeling monitored or judged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking on Margaret’s ambivalence and priorities. Reflections are frequent but remain somewhat surface-level and miss deeper feelings such as fear, irritation, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol-related risk, particularly around the fall and pub drinking. Momentum is maintained through concrete planning, though brief expansion into communication and follow-up logistics slightly stretches the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Margaret’s preferences clearly shape the session, and the plan is co-constructed around her “low-drama” approach. The psychiatrist still introduces more structured content than a fully client-driven exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Margaret’s freedom to choose and avoids pressure to quit or follow a rigid plan. Client ownership could be affirmed even more explicitly, especially around setbacks and future choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and planning questions help Margaret articulate a plan and increase her confidence. Deeper exploration of her values, hopes, and intrinsic motivation is limited, as the session moves quickly into practical advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and repeatedly invites Margaret’s preferences. Some expert-advisor moments emerge when providing safety steps, scripts, and if-then strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret produces useful change talk, particularly around confidence, the water plan, and “just safer.” The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or expand this change talk to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Margaret’s resistance and validates her preference to keep pub nights while focusing on safety. Sustain talk is handled well, though it could be more strategically pivoted toward change.
