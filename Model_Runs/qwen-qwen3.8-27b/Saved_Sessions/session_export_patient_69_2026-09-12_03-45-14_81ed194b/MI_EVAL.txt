# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids blaming the client for wanting to finish quickly. However, acceptance remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply affirming of the client's underlying fatigue or sense of meaninglessness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate surface-level reflections about low energy and wanting to move quickly. Empathy is limited because the client's more meaningful statement about repetitive, meaningless compliance is not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear operational focus on completing forms and signatures, which prevents wandering. However, the direction remains too task-focused and does not sufficiently guide the conversation toward broader MI goals such as engagement or change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and offers choices, such as how to review forms and whether to read aloud or point. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist still controls the agenda, sequence, and level of detail despite the client asking for a simpler process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly affirms choice by offering options, following the client's lead, and avoiding pressure or coercion. Some repeated procedural explanations may add burden, but the client's autonomy is consistently respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is an initial attempt to evoke the client's perspective by asking what is on their mind and whether to explore deeper. Evocation remains weak because the psychiatrist does not later draw out the client's values, motivations, or reasons for engagement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership by offering choices and inviting the client's preference. This is undermined by repeatedly listing forms and instructions despite the client clearly asking for a more minimal, point-only process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is only a brief opening to explore the client's feelings, and the session quickly shifts to paperwork. The psychiatrist does not effectively elicit the client's reasons, values, or benefits for more meaningful engagement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the client's disengagement without reinforcing it, but does not use MI strategies to soften or redirect sustain talk. Sustain talk remains dominant, with no double-sided reflections or gentle pivot toward change or engagement.
