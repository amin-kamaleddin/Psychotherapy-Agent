# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing with the client. The repeated safety instructions add a mildly directive tone that slightly weakens unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist accurately reflects that the client feels pushed, wants to fit in, and does not want judgment. The reflections remain somewhat surface-level and miss deeper emotional themes like feeling unseen or controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays tied to substance use and immediate safety but lacks a clear strategic focus after the client becomes resistant. The repeated safety checks create a circular, checklist-like feel.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist uses collaborative language and allows the client to end the session. However, the safety portion becomes prescriptive rather than co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client's choice and respects his decision to leave. The repeated safety directives subtly reduce the sense of client-led autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist asks some open questions but does not consistently draw out the client's values, motivations, or self-efficacy. Missed opportunities include exploring what fitting in or not falling behind means to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist offers options and invites the client's input, showing a collaborative posture. However, the safety segment becomes clinician-led and the session does not fully co-create a shared agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few attempts to evoke change talk, but the client's statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or built on. As a result, motivation for change remains underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but does not effectively soften the client's resistance or reframe it toward change. The offer to help the client keep using without getting into trouble is a notable concern.
