# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout; never reacts with surprise or disapproval to client's self-dismissal. Validates goals as meaningful ('That's not small, Elias. That's your life') and closes with unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, nuanced reflections that capture emotional texture beyond surface paraphrasing. Checks accuracy with 'Am I close to how it feels?' and is specifically tailored to client's language and metaphors.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with logical pacing from initial experience through to decision to engage. Falls short of a 5 because the session ends without a brief summary or collaboratively identified concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine, consistent collaboration with a clearly equal power dynamic; observations framed as invitations ('I'd like your take on it') rather than directives. Session content and direction shaped almost entirely by client's contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces client's agency and ownership; never pressures to commit to a specific action or frequency. Holds space for client to generate his own next step (moving the breakfast table closer) without jumping in with a prescription.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivating content is entirely self-generated by the client—never information, advice, or listed benefits. Exemplary evocation: the clinician creates conditions for client's own motivation to surface without ever telling him what to think or do.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions client as the expert on his own life; explicitly invites client's perspective and defers interpretation. The clinician never tells the client what to do, think, or what his goals should be.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates change talk through strategic questions and amplification of client-generated imagery. Could have pushed slightly further on confidence talk or future-oriented language to reach a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk with double-sided reflections (e.g., 'you said you'd rather not think about it—and yet here you are, thinking about it'). Slight gap in not explicitly addressing the 'doesn't seem like enough to matter' sustain talk with a double-sided reflection.
