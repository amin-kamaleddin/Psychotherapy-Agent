# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard is maintained throughout the session. The psychiatrist validates the patient’s minimization and concern about being labeled a smoker without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the patient’s ambivalence, need for evidence, and desire to stay in control. Empathy could be deepened further by exploring underlying emotions, values, or the meaning of stress and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the relevant behavior and health concern while narrowing toward a concrete monitoring plan. A repeated question briefly interrupts the forward momentum of the conversation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient to co-construct a next step and integrates the patient’s ideas into the plan. Collaboration is strong but slightly limited by a structured, interview-like sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient is consistently positioned as the decision-maker, with clear respect for their need for visible proof and self-control. There is no pressure, coercion, or implied obligation to commit to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical, patient-generated next steps while avoiding direct advice. Evocation remains limited because it does not deeply explore the patient’s values, reasons, confidence, or importance of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that the patient is the expert on their own body, work, and habits. The interaction is collaborative but somewhat script-like, especially with repeated questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-related language is elicited, such as cutting back if visible evidence appears. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify change talk or explore desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without argument, correction, or direct confrontation. The psychiatrist effectively reframes resistance into a small, patient-controlled check-in, though the patient’s sustain talk remains prominent.
