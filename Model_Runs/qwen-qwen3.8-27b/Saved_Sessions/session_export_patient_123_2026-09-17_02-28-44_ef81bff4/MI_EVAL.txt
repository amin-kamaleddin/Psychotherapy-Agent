# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client's strengths. However, acceptance is not consistently warm or individualized throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses reflective statements to understand the client's perspective on stress and drinking. These reflections are often accurate but remain somewhat surface-level rather than deeply nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor keeps the session focused on the client's life, stress, and alcohol use without wandering aimlessly. The specific target behavior could have been made more explicit to keep the discussion more anchored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative tone is established through open-ended questions and co-constructing small steps with the client. However, the counselor's multiple concrete suggestions can make the interaction feel more expert-guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively supports autonomy by emphasizing that the client is the expert on her own life. Repeatedly introducing specific alternatives can subtly imply preferred behaviors and weaken full autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor attempts to elicit the client's own motivations by asking what feels doable and using the client's own language. This could be strengthened by consistently deepening the client's change talk regarding values or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats the client as a collaborator and builds on her own ideas. The interaction occasionally shifts toward an expert-guided approach when the counselor introduces several specific strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some change-related language by asking what helps and what feels doable. These change statements are not consistently amplified or built upon to further strengthen the client's motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor normalizes the client's sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it. More consistent use of double-sided reflections would help redirect these statements toward the client's values and goals.
