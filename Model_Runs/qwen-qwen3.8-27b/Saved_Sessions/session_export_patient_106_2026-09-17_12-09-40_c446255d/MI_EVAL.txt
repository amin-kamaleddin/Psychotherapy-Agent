# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard from the opening statement through to the end. Never moralizes, dismisses difficulty, or makes the client feel like a problem to be fixed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate complex reflections that capture the emotional core, such as naming the cigarette as the client's only 'permission to stop.' Consistently reflects meaning and feeling rather than just content, and the client herself confirms the accuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression from ambivalence to a concrete, self-generated action step without drift. Each question builds on the previous answer and consistently returns focus to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conducted as a genuine collaboration with the client's ideas (balcony, texting sister) shaping the session's direction and outcome. The final plan is entirely the client's, with the psychiatrist only helping to clarify and reduce the perceived magnitude.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces ownership and choice through language like 'you don't have to solve all three today' and 'I don't want to hand you a script.' Never uses directive language and validates doubt rather than dismissing it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation emerges from the client's own mouth — no external reasons, statistics, or persuasion are introduced. Strategic questions about values, tiny versions, and what a break would need to include naturally elicit change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise about the client's life resides within her, ceding the floor and using her contributions as primary material. Never positions themselves as the expert or provides unsolicited information about cessation strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes multiple forms of change talk (desire, need, ability, commitment) through strategic reflections and amplifying statements like 'that's not nothing.' The client's change language increases in specificity and confidence over the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively redirects sustain talk without reinforcing it, and the overall trajectory moves the client away from reasons to maintain the status quo. Could be improved by using more explicit double-sided reflections to actively honor the sustain talk while maintaining momentum toward change.
