# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicated a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without criticizing Alessia's uncertainty or caution. Affirmations were generally positive but occasionally generic, so individualized validation could be deeper.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate reflections of Alessia's concerns, caution, and relief at having a clearer plan. Deeper emotional resonance could be added around fear of instability, loss of control, and high-stakes decision-making.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on the medication transition, safety monitoring, and prescriber call. Pacing was practical but could be more strategic in moving from information to a concrete commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was highly collaborative, with Alessia's requests shaping the agenda, materials, and next steps. The counselor co-constructed scripts, checklists, and monitoring plans rather than imposing a fixed structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly supported Alessia's sense of choice by offering options and honoring her preference to avoid new medications. Some clinically appropriate directive statements slightly shifted the stance away from explicit client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used appropriate open and preference-based questions early, but the session shifted heavily toward information-giving and problem-solving. More evocative follow-up questions about values, confidence, and self-efficacy would strengthen MI depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative tone by inviting input and adapting to Alessia's needs. The prominent expert role in medical safety information slightly limited the sense that wisdom resided primarily within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged when Alessia stated readiness to call her prescriber and preference for non-drug supports. The counselor could have more consistently reflected, amplified, and explored these readiness statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled doubts about the washout, daily log, and safety rules by normalizing concerns and reframing them into manageable actions. More explicit MI-style pivots could further transform fear into agency.
