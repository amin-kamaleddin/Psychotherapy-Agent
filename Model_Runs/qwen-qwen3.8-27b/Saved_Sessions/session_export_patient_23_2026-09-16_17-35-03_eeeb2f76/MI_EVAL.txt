# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, non-judgment, and positive regard, normalizing Claudia's evening routine without moralizing. It is slightly procedural and could more explicitly affirm her courage, insight, or values to feel warmer.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tracks Claudia's ambivalence with accurate reflections about wine as a wind-down versus next-day discomfort. Empathy is task-focused and could go deeper into emotional undertones such as worry, feeling grey, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing evening alcohol use and moves logically from routine to function, cue, and a realistic morning-based metric. There is no drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed around Claudia's ideas, including the pantry cue, breath pause, and morning function metric rather than a prescribed agenda. Therapist-generated menus are used as invitations rather than directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by emphasizing small steps, fitting the routine, and respecting Claudia's boundary not to add another job. Avoiding pressure or abstinence reinforces client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Claudia's own ideas, preferences, and solutions rather than advising. Evocation is stronger for behavior than for deeper motivational change talk, such as importance, need, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Claudia to guide the conversation and avoids a directive posture, but still offers multiple therapist-generated options. Clearer power-sharing statements and letting her set the agenda would improve this criterion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, but it is mostly reflected neutrally rather than amplified or connected to values and confidence. More deliberate development of importance, ability, and commitment would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against sustain talk, accepts Claudia's realistic limits, and redirects toward small steps. More double-sided reflections could better pivot both sides toward ambivalence resolution.
