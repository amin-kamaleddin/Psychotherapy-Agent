# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment, lecturing, or moral pressure. The psychiatrist explicitly normalizes the client's perspective and reduces fear of being reported.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective reflective listening captures the client's irritation, ambivalence, and concern about privacy. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on cannabis use, peer context, and parental conflict. Focus becomes somewhat diffuse later when it drifts into broader family conflict rather than a specific target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the session as a joint process and invites the client to help shape the conversation. The client's ideas about privacy, fairness, and autonomy directly guide the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on the client's freedom to choose without pressure or coercion. The psychiatrist respects the client's limits, including not quitting and not changing friends.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is encouraged to generate his own motivations and goals rather than being given reasons for change. Evocation could be stronger by more systematically developing discrepancy, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life and social context. The client's contributions shape the direction and content of the interview.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk around spacing out use and reducing pressure at home. However, the DARN-C spectrum is not fully developed, especially ability, reasons, need, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's resistance and effectively rolls with statements like not quitting. A stronger response would more actively use discrepancy to weaken the sustain talk.
