# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows warmth, nonjudgment, and validation of ambivalence, including normalizing setbacks as useful data. Acceptance is strong, though some praise is tied to the client's effort and plan adherence rather than fully unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Amina's ambivalence, sleep concerns, class impact, and discomfort with rule-heavy strategies. Empathy is solid but remains somewhat surface-level, with limited exploration of the deeper emotional meaning behind scrolling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused on the target behavior of evening phone use and its effects on sleep and class performance. The psychiatrist maintains clear purpose and guides the conversation toward a specific, workable experiment with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and incorporates Amina's preferences, such as the kitchen drawer, light off, and water instead of tea. However, the therapist remains the primary generator of options and plan structure, so collaboration is present but uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasizes choice, ownership, and an experimental approach, while reframing potential lapse as data rather than failure. Autonomy support is strong, though the detailed plan and repeated suggestions subtly center the therapist's preferred strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and questions elicit some client-generated language about sleep, class energy, and willingness to try. Evocation is functional but somewhat limited, as the session focuses more on feasibility and plan details than on Amina's deeper values and internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina's input is valued and used to shape the plan, but the conversation remains largely clinician-led. The psychiatrist could strengthen partnership by more consistently inviting Amina to generate her own ideas before offering structured options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through questions about goals, doability, and confidence, and Amina expresses willingness to try the experiment. There are fewer efforts to amplify deeper desire, need, ability, or commitment language before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and softened by reframing possible failure as useful data and allowing a small 2-minute check. The session could more actively transform lingering sustain talk into forward-looking change language.
