# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses validating language and maintains a nonjudgmental stance, especially regarding the patient’s fear of cancer and distrust of the GP. However, validation sometimes functions as a bridge to advice rather than as a fully supportive MI stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges distress but often remains at a surface level, missing deeper reflections of loneliness, grief, and feeling like “the sick one.” Empathic responses are more factual than emotionally attuned.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting GI concerns and preparing for the upcoming GP visit. The direction is clear, but it is rigidly medical and does not flex toward broader values or psychosocial goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying lists, scripts, and recommendations. There is little evidence of shared decision-making or a side-by-side working relationship.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy is undermined by directive language and pre-determined solutions, such as insisting on referrals and using a provided script. The patient is positioned more as a recipient of instructions than an active agent in her care.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies heavily on closed questions and supplies the motivational content rather than eliciting the patient’s own reasons, fears, and values. There are few open-ended questions designed to draw out client-centered change motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts an authority role by directing the conversation, drafting scripts, and specifying tests. The patient has limited opportunity to co-create the agenda or negotiate priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opportunities to build on the patient’s values and desires, such as missing gardening, walking, and wanting agency, are not sufficiently reflected or amplified. Change talk is not consistently developed or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces the patient’s biomedical focus by expanding the search for an organic cause and facilitating requests for tests. This amplifies sustain talk rather than gently exploring alternative meanings, stress-symptom links, or functional goals.
