# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental acceptance, affirming Erik's efforts and normalizing struggle. It falls short of a 5 because acceptance is sometimes framed through evaluative interpretations rather than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Erik's ambivalence, practical concerns, and fears. A 5 would require deeper exploration of the emotional undercurrents, such as shame, discouragement, or identity threat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behaviors of weight loss, exercise consistency, and dietary choices, moving coherently toward a practical, client-generated plan. Pacing is strong, with no meaningful drift from the goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist functions as an equal partner, allowing Erik's ideas to substantially shape the session and final plan. The interaction is co-constructive rather than expert-driven, with Erik actively contributing to the solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Erik's sense of choice and avoids controlling language or prescribed rules. Erik is repeatedly invited to create his own flexible, self-directed approach, reinforcing his ownership and freedom.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Erik's own ideas, motivations, and solutions rather than giving reasons for change. However, some therapist-supplied labels and interpretations could have been replaced with deeper evocation of values and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites Erik to take ownership of the process, avoiding a purely expert-driven approach. A 5 would require more explicit power sharing, such as having Erik set the agenda or direct next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates space for Erik to generate change talk and build his own plan, with multiple moments of client-driven motivation. However, the psychiatrist could more consistently amplify, label, and strategically reinforce this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Erik's sustain talk without arguing or dismissing it, normalizing the difficulty of setbacks. A 5 would involve more active use of double-sided reflections to explicitly reframe sustain talk into motivation.
