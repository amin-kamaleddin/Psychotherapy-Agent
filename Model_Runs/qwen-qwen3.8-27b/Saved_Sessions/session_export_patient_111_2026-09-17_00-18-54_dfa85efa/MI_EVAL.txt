# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, patience, and nonjudgment, even when Richard is resistant. It falls short of a 5 mainly because the warmth remains somewhat formulaic and could be more individually affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses good reflective listening and captures mixed feelings and the function of smoking. Empathy could deepen by more explicitly reflecting underlying loss, fear, or love, and following up on unanswered emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on smoking and related concerns without wandering. Direction could be stronger by more clearly linking the discussion to possible next steps or change decisions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor collaborates by inviting Richard’s perspective and adapting to his request for a simple conversation. It is not a 5 because the counselor still controls much of the agenda rather than fully co-creating the next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with repeated respect for Richard’s pace, choices, and control. The counselor avoids pressure and frames change as optional rather than required.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks evocative questions and elicits some internal motivations. Evocation is limited because emerging change talk is not consistently amplified or developed into stronger self-motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys a collaborative, client-centered stance and respects Richard’s preferred way of working. It is not a 5 because shared decision-making and co-created agenda could be made more explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, especially around his wife, grandkids, and not wanting to be a burden. The counselor does not consistently reflect, amplify, or consolidate these reasons for change strongly enough.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or reinforcing resistance and rolls with sustain talk appropriately. It could be stronger by using double-sided reflections more consistently to link resistance with change-relevant values.
