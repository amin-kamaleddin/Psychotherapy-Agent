# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client’s feelings. Affirmations are encouraging, though they sometimes focus more on performance than unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture the client’s ambivalence and social fears. Reflections remain somewhat surface-level and could go deeper into underlying shame, identity, and loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is focused on a concrete target behavior: reducing gambling at the pub by leaving early. The main limitation is that the immediate plan is not consistently linked to broader goals such as debt reduction and future well-being.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes practical ideas, including the phone alarm, lock-screen note, and exit line. However, the psychiatrist often adds new options after the client has chosen, which can make the process feel more clinician-led than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking what feels doable and respecting the client’s rejections. The frequent offering of suggestions can still subtly steer the client toward the clinician’s preferred solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence rulers are used effectively, but the session relies heavily on external suggestions rather than evoking the client’s own reasons and values. More open-ended questions could strengthen internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows her preferences to shape the plan. However, the role of problem-solver with multiple sequential options can reduce the sense of true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses several concrete intentions to change, and the psychiatrist affirms some of these. The psychiatrist does not consistently expand or deepen the client’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates the client’s reasons for maintaining the current behavior without arguing. There is limited use of MI techniques to actively soften sustain talk or connect it with the client’s longer-term goals.
