# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, normalizing ambivalence without shame. Acceptance is somewhat professional and could include warmer, more explicit affirmations of the client's worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and shows good understanding of the client's ambivalence and practical concerns. Empathy is often more cognitive than emotional, with missed opportunities to reflect deeper shame and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on sustaining recovery and connects ambivalence to shame, triggers, and a concrete coping plan. The link to avoiding alcohol and pill use could be made more explicit during high-risk moments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively and builds the plan around client ideas, such as picturing his daughter and sitting down. The clinician still supplies much of the structure, such as the if-then plan and phone-note format.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options, avoiding pressure, and inviting the client to select what feels helpful. Autonomy support could be stronger by relying less on therapist-generated strategies and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client motivations, especially around family, his daughter, and the desire to preserve calm. Evocation is not consistently developed, as the session shifts to therapist-provided coping strategies rather than deeper confidence or ability.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and lets the client influence the session direction. The approach could become more client-led by reducing therapist-introduced techniques and increasing explicit power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk about the client's reasons to stay connected and calm. The clinician does not consistently deepen, summarize, or amplify this change talk before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles ambivalence well by normalizing it rather than arguing or invalidating the client's doubts. The sustain talk could be softened more actively through double-sided reflections or stronger MI-consistent pivots.
