# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental language and respects Alexei’s concerns and preferences. It falls short of a 5 because the opening is slightly leading and does not fully affirm Alexei’s worth beyond his goals and behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and helpful, especially around consequences, soccer, and not wanting to be the “buzzkill.” They remain mostly cognitive and surface-level, with less depth on shame, fear, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol-related risk and building a concrete refusal plan. It moves logically from assessment to goals, planning, role-play, and backup strategies with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist works jointly with Alexei through choices, role-play, and adapting to his ideas and tone. It is not a 5 because the therapist still supplies much of the structure, scripts, and plan content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses invitational language and respects Alexei’s choice not to tell his parents or involve a friend immediately. Slight reduction in autonomy support occurs when providing frequent scripts and step-by-step options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence ratings help Alexei articulate some motivation, but the therapist does not consistently explore his values, hopes, or self-efficacy in depth. There is overreliance on therapist-generated suggestions before fully eliciting change motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alexei influences key decisions, such as choosing a line and role-play, showing some partnership. However, the therapist frequently supplies options and scripts, making the interaction more expert-driven than client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist evokes some change talk through readiness, confidence, and goal-related questions. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, summarized, or deepened through more effective MI questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist appropriately avoids arguing with Alexei’s minimization and sustain talk. Advanced softening strategies, such as double-sided reflections or exploring the pull behind his statements, are not consistently used.
