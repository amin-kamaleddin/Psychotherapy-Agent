# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance. Ambivalence and partial progress were accepted without criticism or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and responsive, especially around the tension between smoking for calm and health concerns. Empathy could deepen further by exploring the emotional weight and values behind the patient’s fear of hospitalization and nighttime distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target goal of reducing smoking and dust exposure to improve asthma control. The conversation moved clearly from barriers to practical steps, slip management, and a measurable sign of progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-created from the patient’s own practical ideas. The clinician framed the work as a joint experiment rather than an expert-driven instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently emphasized choice, experimentation, and the absence of perfection. The patient was supported in setting his own realistic goal rather than being pressured toward complete abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions were drawn from the patient rather than provided by the clinician. The patient generated strong change talk, including gum use, a five-cigarette limit, and an evening breathing outcome.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was consistently positioned as the expert on his own work, habits, and stress patterns. The clinician built on the patient’s knowledge rather than imposing an external plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and affirmations effectively elicited concrete change talk and commitment-like statements. The patient’s own language was amplified to support a realistic behavior plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument and redirected toward manageable steps. A perfect score would require more consistent explicit rolling-with or reframing of remaining ambivalence.
