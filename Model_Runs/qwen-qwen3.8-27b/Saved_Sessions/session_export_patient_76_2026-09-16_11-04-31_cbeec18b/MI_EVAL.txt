# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and warm, without moralizing about self-harm. The main limitation is that acceptance sometimes becomes repetitive and focuses more on safety behaviors than Liora’s broader ambivalence and exhaustion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflective listening and accurately captures fear, exhaustion, the “noise,” and the function of cutting as relief. Reflections could be deeper, especially around burdening her roommate, feeling trapped, and underlying hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on self-harm reduction, means restriction, and immediate safety. The focus is largely procedural and crisis-oriented rather than progressively moving toward longer-term values and reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the safety plan collaboratively and incorporates Liora’s practical contributions. However, the psychiatrist still supplies many options and scripts, so Liora does not fully shape the overall direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice with open invitations and respects Liora’s boundaries around writing and not burdening her roommate. Some safety language remains directive, especially around calling 988/911 if the urge rises.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some evocation of Liora’s focus and small coping choices, but the psychiatrist frequently provides options rather than eliciting Liora’s own solutions. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, past successes, and hope is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and adapts to Liora’s preferences and constraints. The interaction remains clinician-led due to safety planning, and Liora is not fully positioned as the expert on her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes moderate efforts to elicit change talk and affirms small safety-oriented behaviors. There is limited follow-through to deepen, amplify, or expand Liora’s change talk toward a stronger change goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk, and appropriately acknowledges that cutting works. Responses could be more MI-consistent by using double-sided reflections and pivoting toward the conflict between relief and exhaustion.
