# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance, validating progress and normalizing setbacks. Acceptance is somewhat tied to performance or plan completion rather than fully unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Samuel’s smoking patterns, triggers, and worries in a supportive way. Empathy remains largely cognitive and behavior-focused, with limited deeper exploration of ambivalence or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing smoking and narrows the discussion into a clear, concrete plan. At times, the direction becomes overly logistical rather than linking action steps to motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Samuel’s input and co-constructs the plan, adjusting based on his feedback. Collaboration is limited by the counselor’s frequent offering of specific solutions and structured interventions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses choice-focused language and respects Samuel’s preferences when simplifying the plan. Some prescriptive instructions, however, may reduce the client’s sense of self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits practical plan elements such as gum, timer, water, and journaling. Evocation remains largely logistical and does not deeply draw out Samuel’s values, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samuel’s input is invited and his ideas are incorporated into the plan. The partnership is uneven because the counselor often takes on an expert problem-solving role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit some change talk related to goals, effort, and feasibility. The counselor does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk in connection with values, ability, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged without arguing or reinforcing the reasons to continue smoking. The counselor could more consistently use MI-specific strategies to pivot sustain talk toward change.
