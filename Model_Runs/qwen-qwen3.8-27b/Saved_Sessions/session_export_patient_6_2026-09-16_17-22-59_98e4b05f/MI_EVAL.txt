# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently showed warmth, validation, and nonjudgment, even when risk and consequences were discussed. This created a safe, respectful environment and supported Alexei’s willingness to engage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflected ambivalence and practical concerns, but some empathy remained surface-level. Deeper emotional undertones, such as shame, fear of disappointment, or anxiety about labels, could have been explored more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use and Alexei’s goals around soccer, recovery, and family trust. Each question logically built toward a concrete, client-driven plan without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor actively co-constructed the conversation and plan with Alexei rather than directing it. Alexei’s ideas, such as using water, texting a friend, and keeping the team app visible, clearly shaped the outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used non-controlling language and emphasized Alexei’s choice and ownership. However, some suggested strategies may have subtly guided the plan, so explicit optional framing could strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited internal motivation by helping Alexei connect his behavior to identity, control, and future goals. Deeper exploration of values, confidence, and self-efficacy could have made evocation even stronger.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Alexei as the expert in his own life. The session progressed through Alexei’s own language and ideas rather than through counselor-driven advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections helped Alexei articulate identity-based change talk, such as wanting to “make smart calls.” A more systematic evocation of different forms of change talk, such as desire, ability, reasons, and commitment, would improve this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handled resistance without arguing, correcting, or moralizing, and used reflections to accept Alexei’s concerns. Stronger double-sided reflections could more effectively pivot statements like “I don’t want this to become a big deal” toward intrinsic motivation for change.
