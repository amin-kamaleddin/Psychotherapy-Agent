# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, explicitly countering Marcus's self-deprecation and affirming his progress. The warmth is consistent but occasionally feels slightly scripted, which slightly reduces the sense of genuine in-the-moment positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate complex reflections that capture both sides of ambivalence. However, reflections occasionally lean toward summarizing content rather than exploring the deeper emotional experience (e.g., the shame of repeated failure or vulnerability of being a beginner at 42).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively invites Marcus to articulate his own change reasons, and his change talk escalates naturally from ambivalence to excitement and commitment. Could have pushed slightly further on confidence/ability (e.g., readiness scaling) or tapped into deeper health values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without arguing or dismissing, using it as a springboard toward change. Could have used more double-sided reflections to explicitly acknowledge both sides before pivoting, making the softening even more nuanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marcus as the expert on his own life, using open-ended questions to let him generate his own solutions and set the pace. The plan that emerges is entirely his, framed as self-built rather than prescribed, with the closing 'no pressure' reinforcing full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical focus on the target behavior from problem identification through concrete action planning, ending with a specific behavioral commitment. The direction is strong but more implicit than it could be, lacking explicit signposting or summarizing to make the progressive structure more visible.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Marcus through open-ended questions, never providing external reasons for change such as statistics or warnings. The evocation is effective but operates mostly at the level of preferences and practicality rather than tapping into deeper values and identity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Behaves as an equal partner throughout, using collaborative language like 'I'm noticing' and 'I want to make sure I get them' rather than an expert-to-patient dynamic. The session is genuinely co-constructed, with Marcus providing the content and the psychiatrist providing the structure.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise about the right path forward resides within Marcus, never assuming the expert role or prescribing a specific intervention. The session is genuinely shaped by Marcus's contributions, with the psychiatrist's role limited to eliciting, reflecting, and affirming.
