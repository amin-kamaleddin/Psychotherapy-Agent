# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming even when Dylan minimizes his role. A limitation is that the acceptance sometimes over-validates his minimization rather than also holding space for accountability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Dylan’s perspective, frustration, and desired outcome. Empathy remains mostly content-based and could better explore underlying feelings, values, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session focuses heavily on procedural outcomes, such as getting the matter dropped or avoiding a paper trail. It does not consistently guide the conversation toward the target behavior of alcohol-related conduct, responsibility, or safer decision-making.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites Dylan’s input and adapts when he declines scripts, emails, or rehearsal. However, collaboration is partly framed around the counselor’s practical problem-solving strategies rather than a fully equal exploration of values and motivations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently affirms Dylan’s right to choose, control, and decide what to do next. This strong noncontrolling stance helps preserve ownership and reduce resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some initial curiosity, but the counselor does not effectively draw out Dylan’s own reasons for change. More evocative questions about values, consequences, and future goals would be needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor makes a strong initial partnership statement and asks for permission, but quickly becomes more expert-driven. The introduction of multiple practical approaches limits the sense that Dylan is fully leading the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening MI question is appropriate, but it is not followed up in a way that develops Dylan’s own reasons for change. Most questions remain procedural rather than eliciting motivation for accountability or safer alcohol-related decisions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids confronting Dylan’s sustain talk but also does not effectively soften or redirect it. Suggesting a non-admitting statement may unintentionally reinforce his minimization instead of exploring its implications.
