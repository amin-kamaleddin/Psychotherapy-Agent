# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, validating the client's concerns without moralizing. It falls short of a 5 because the affirmation of the client's broader strengths and self-awareness is not consistently individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrates accurate and nuanced reflective listening, capturing both the client's emotional and cognitive experience. A deeper exploration of the identity-level distress of being seen as 'risky' would have strengthened the empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the screening process and the client's specific concerns without drifting into unrelated topics. The strategic momentum toward a concrete, change-relevant goal or agenda was somewhat limited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician behaves collaboratively by inviting the client's perspective and following their stated needs for clarity and low stress. The collaboration remains somewhat clinician-guided, as the client is not fully positioned as an equal co-author of the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician supports autonomy by avoiding coercion and pressure, while acknowledging the client's authority over their own experience. The support could be further strengthened by more explicit statements about the client's freedom to choose and clearer collaborative information on confidentiality limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses appropriate evocative questions to invite the client to reflect on their own values and personal definitions of risk. However, the evocation remains superficial and does not sufficiently develop the client's change-relevant language or internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician conveys a partnership stance by treating the client as the expert on their own life and using collaborative questions. A 5 would require more explicit evidence of shared decision-making and equal power-sharing in the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is an initial effort to evoke client-centered language regarding values like grades and social functioning. The clinician missed the opportunity to reflect, amplify, and expand this language into stronger, more developed change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician handles sustain talk effectively without arguing or dismissing, using balanced double-sided reflections. The responses were not sufficiently strategic in redirecting the client's reasons for maintaining the status quo toward their own values.
