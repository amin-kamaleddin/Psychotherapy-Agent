# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, with language that validates Marcus’s realistic approach and avoids shame. However, the early “you’re clearly motivated” comment is slightly premature, and the acceptance remains more practical than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses competent reflective listening and captures Marcus’s practical concerns well. However, the reflections stay mostly at the content level and do not fully explore Marcus’s emotional burden, past failures, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior and moves effectively from vague intention to a concrete, actionable plan. There is clear direction, momentum, and consistent attention to specific steps such as timing, cues, barriers, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the psychiatrist acting as a co-planner and incorporating Marcus’s choices into the final plan. It falls short of a 5 because the therapist still structures the agenda and introduces many options rather than fully letting Marcus lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Marcus’s sense of choice by inviting his input, respecting his limits, and emphasizing voluntary participation. Autonomy support would be stronger if the therapist paused more before offering options and allowed Marcus to set the direction first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative techniques by eliciting confidence, cues, fallbacks, and self-generated problem-solving. However, evocation remains procedural and does not deeply explore Marcus’s values, personal reasons for change, or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong partnership stance by inviting Marcus to choose among options and following his decisions. This is not a 5 because the therapist still leads much of the agenda and drives the session toward a concrete plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk, especially around commitment, confidence, and practical action. However, there is limited amplification or deepening of change talk, with less exploration of values, reasons, or meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk in a nonjudgmental, practical way by validating caution and building in fallbacks. It is not consistently skillful, however, because the therapist does not actively redirect sustain talk with more advanced MI techniques.
