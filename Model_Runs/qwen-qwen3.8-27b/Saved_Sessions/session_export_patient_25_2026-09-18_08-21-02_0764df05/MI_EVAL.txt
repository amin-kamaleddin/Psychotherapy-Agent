# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming language throughout. No negative labeling or disapproval is expressed, and the client is treated as capable and whole.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and specific reflective listening captures both content and emotional weight. Reflections are consistently precise rather than generic, including complex dualities in the client's ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use in social contexts with logical pacing from identification through exploration to consolidation. The opening could have been slightly more directed toward the target topic, though letting the client lead is arguably appropriate.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session reads as a genuine conversation between equals, with the client's ideas and language consistently shaping direction. The psychiatrist builds on the client's own metaphors rather than imposing clinical terminology.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Zero pressure or implied expectation throughout, with explicit reinforcement that any change is entirely the client's choice. The psychiatrist validates the client's right to pace himself and offers availability without obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is sourced from the client through strategic questions and reflections that prompt self-generated insight. The psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change, advice, or information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within the client, with an egalitarian power dynamic throughout. The psychiatrist never assumes the expert role or positions themselves as having superior knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits change talk through strategic questioning and reflective amplification, including self-generated solutions. Could be slightly stronger in the mid-session where ambivalence is most pronounced, with deeper value-based or scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges the client's concerns about rules and restriction without reinforcing them as reasons to maintain the status quo. Offers alternative reframes that honor the concern while still supporting change, though one or two moments could have been more strategically targeted.
