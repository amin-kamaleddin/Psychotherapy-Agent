# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warm, unconditional positive regard without judgment or condescension. Mixed feelings are explicitly validated, and affirmations feel genuine and individualized rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections move beyond surface content to capture deeper emotional and existential meaning (e.g., the autonomy violation behind the morning struggle). Reflections are varied, non-repetitive, and progressively deeper throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows a clear, logical progression from rapport-building through barrier exploration to solution co-creation and commitment. There is no topic drift, and each question or reflection moves the session forward purposefully toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine joint exploration, with Jamal's ideas shaping the direction and content throughout. The solution (5:15 + five quiet minutes) is entirely Jamal's own, with the psychiatrist serving as a reflective partner rather than a guide.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly transfers decision-making authority to Jamal ('I don't get to decide if it's enough—you do') and never pressures or implies he 'should' do anything. When asked 'Is that enough?', the psychiatrist returns the judgment to Jamal rather than validating or invalidating the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out Jamal's own values and intrinsic motivation rather than providing external reasons for change. This results in a clear, observable shift from compliance-based motivation ('to make my mom stop yelling') to value-based motivation ('I'm doing it for the five minutes').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly names Jamal's expertise in his own life and never assumes the expert role or provides advice. Language like 'what does it look like from where you're sitting?' consistently positions Jamal as the authority on his own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully amplified through strategic reflections of Jamal's own words and affirmations of his decision-making process. The session culminates in a meaningful evolution from external to internal motivation, which the psychiatrist facilitated through evocative questioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** External pressures (mother, school) are effectively depersonalized and redirected toward Jamal's own experience. However, when Jamal anticipated difficulty ('I might still hit snooze'), the psychiatrist did not explicitly acknowledge the setback before affirming progress, which was a minor missed opportunity.
