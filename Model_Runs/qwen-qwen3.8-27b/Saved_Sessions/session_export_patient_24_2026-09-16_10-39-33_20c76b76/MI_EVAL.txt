# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client’s experience. However, acceptance feels somewhat procedural and lacks deeper, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflective statements about Daniel’s routine and concerns. Empathy remains mainly content-focused and does not fully explore his ambivalence, defensiveness, or fear of being labeled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use in relation to training, sleep, and recovery. The counselor moves the discussion toward a concrete one-week tracking plan, though with limited motivational depth.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and adjusts the plan based on Daniel’s preferences. Collaboration is uneven because the counselor still supplies most of the structure, framing, and recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and asks permission before proceeding. However, multiple specific directives and advice reduce the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks a few initial questions about goals and concerns. Much of the session is didactic and advice-based rather than eliciting Daniel’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel’s input is invited and shapes some decisions, such as tracking and phone notes. The psychiatrist still occupies an expert role, so the session is not fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few brief attempts to evoke change talk, but the counselor does not expand on Daniel’s own reasons or values. Systematic use of rulers, affirmations, or values-based exploration is missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally does not argue with Daniel’s sustain talk and uses some reflections that reduce resistance. Double-sided reflections and strategic pivots toward discrepancy are not consistently used.
