# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout, without blame or moralizing. It is not a 5 because some acceptance statements feel slightly formulaic or anticipatory rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections generally capture Dante’s ambivalence, fatigue, and fear of failure accurately and supportively. Some reflections are slightly interpretive or lead ahead of his stated experience, and the shame/self-critical theme could be explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on increasing physical activity and develops a concrete, workable plan. It could benefit from a clearer mid-session summary or agenda statement to strengthen strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates strongly by building on Dante’s own ideas and treating the shoes-by-the-door plan as client-designed. The interaction reflects genuine partnership, with the client actively contributing the core intervention content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported through emphasis on flexibility, choice, and freedom from guilt or obligation. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, rules, or “you should” language while validating Dante’s control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections evoke Dante’s internal reasons and help him generate a practical change plan. Evocation could be strengthened by more deliberately exploring confidence, values, past successes, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Dante’s perspective and supports a collaborative stance, but the session remains somewhat therapist-led through a sequence of questions. More explicit power sharing and agenda negotiation would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk emerges naturally, including Dante’s self-generated shoes-by-the-door strategy and statements about sticking with the plan. The psychiatrist could do more to amplify, summarize, and consolidate this change talk toward clearer commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by reflecting barriers without challenging or minimizing them and redirecting to smaller, flexible options. The final uncertainty about whether the plan will stick could be addressed more explicitly with curiosity or double-sided reflection.
