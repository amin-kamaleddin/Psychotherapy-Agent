# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming while normalizing the client's ambivalence and self-criticism. Minor risk of sounding overly polished in a few affirmations, but overall strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurately reflects both content and underlying emotion, including fear, guilt, and the aftermath of the conversation. Could deepen by occasionally reflecting the client's humor or self-critical identity more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, purposeful focus on assertive boundary-setting with the flatmate. The session progresses logically from identifying an acceptable arrangement to a concrete plan and supports for follow-through.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively co-constructs the client's language, plan, and phrasing rather than imposing advice. The client's ideas clearly drive the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently returns ownership to the client and respects ambivalence without pressure. Could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client's freedom to choose, including the option not to act now.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses evocative questions to draw out the client's own values, motivations, and concrete change ideas. Could improve by more systematically eliciting confidence, ability, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and invites the client's perspective throughout. Could be stronger by more explicitly acknowledging the client as the expert in his own life and co-creating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies useful change talk, including concrete action statements and agency. Could be stronger by intentionally eliciting all DARN-C types, especially confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles guilt, fear, and conflict concerns respectfully without arguing or dismissing them. Could be stronger by more clearly pivoting sustain talk toward the client's values, such as through double-sided reflections.
