# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with consistent positive regard for Mai's concerns and fears. Acceptance is genuine but operates at a professional-warmth level rather than a deeply relational one.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate, nuanced reflections that capture Mai's ambivalence well. Reflections occasionally lean toward summarizing rather than fully inhabiting the emotional undercurrent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on medication adherence with logical progression from confusion to a manageable plan. Session ends somewhat abruptly without a clear closing summary or collaborative next-step formulation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the conversation as joint exploration and builds on Mai's ideas with inclusive language. Could more explicitly co-create plans and acknowledge the collaborative nature of the process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never pressures or implies guilt, and reinforces Mai's ownership of her health decisions. Slightly implies the 'right' answer is to take the medication, though the framing remains non-coercive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Draws out some of Mai's own motivations around understanding and control through evocative questions. Evocation is limited to practical/logistical aspects and misses deeper values, identity, and ruling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites Mai's input and positions her as the expert on her own experience without lecturing or assuming motivations. Occasionally steps slightly into an advisory role, shifting subtly toward expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evokes some change talk around the decision to take the medication and plan to ask her doctor. Change talk is largely practical/logistical rather than value-based, with missed opportunities for DARN-CAT elicitation and scale questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles skepticism and fear of seeming difficult effectively by validating and exploring without arguing or dismissing. Could more explicitly use double-sided reflections to fully honor the ambivalence.
