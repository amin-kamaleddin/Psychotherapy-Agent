# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and affirms the client's strengths. Acceptance is solid but somewhat procedural and does not fully validate the emotional meaning behind the client's minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate, respectful reflections of the client's perspective, preferences, and practical concerns. Empathy is strong but mainly cognitive, with less attention to the emotional weight beneath the client's minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the relevant issues of smoking cessation, alcohol use, and the client's health concerns. However, the conversation becomes circular and does not consistently move toward clear findings, concrete next steps, or resolution.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and invites the client's input on how to proceed. Collaboration is limited because the client's request for direct findings is not fully addressed and does not strongly shape the next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the client as the decision-maker and avoids direct pressure to change. Some questions about steps or additional evidence may feel mildly directive to a client seeking closure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few attempts to elicit motivation, but the client remains largely in sustain talk. The psychiatrist does not effectively use MI-consistent methods to develop the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and avoids a purely expert-driven stance. However, the psychiatrist continues to set the agenda and frame the conversation, limiting the sense of true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions, but the client rarely expresses change talk. The session lacks consistent use of scaling, importance/confidence questions, or reflections that deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to sustain talk with acceptance and avoids arguing or reinforcing resistance. The response is supportive but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic redirection to resolve ambivalence.
