# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and avoids labeling, but the stance often feels more managerial than deeply accepting. Unconditional positive regard is present but not consistently warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture surface-level needs to unwind and not be judged, but often miss deeper emotions like guilt, exhaustion, and fear of losing the beer as a coping tool. Empathy is adequate but not consistently penetrating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to work stress, decompression, and marital tension, but drifts into text scripts rather than strategic MI focus on ambivalence and discrepancy. It is relevant but not fully change-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers structured options and adapts wording, but the session is largely driven by therapist-generated scripts and experiments. Viktor’s deeper concerns about control and the root of the problem are not meaningfully co-explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers choice and avoids direct pressure to stop drinking, but frequently introduces specific scripts, logs, and boundary lines that can feel prescriptive. Viktor’s sense of being scolded or controlled is not sufficiently countered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few exploratory questions, but much of the session is spent offering practical reasons, options, and scripts instead of evoking Viktor’s own motivation. Change-related feelings are not consistently amplified or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids an authoritarian tone, but often positions themselves as the problem-solver and script provider. Viktor is not consistently positioned as the primary expert on his own direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Viktor offers meaningful change talk about fights being draining, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand it. The session often shifts to practical tactics before developing Viktor’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Viktor’s sustain talk is strong, and the psychiatrist avoids direct argument but often validates it without creating movement. Strategic double-sided reflections, roll-with-resistance, and discrepancy development are insufficient.
