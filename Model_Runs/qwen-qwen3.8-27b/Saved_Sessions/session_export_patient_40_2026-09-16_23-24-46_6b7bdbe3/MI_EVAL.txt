# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance. The counselor affirms the client's persistence and reduces shame without moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate double-sided reflections that capture the client's ambivalence. However, reflections could be more emotionally deep, especially around shame, fear, and loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safer recovery and high-risk moments with a clear, coherent progression. Each question supports relapse prevention and practical planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the plan from the client's own ideas and uses joint language. The interaction remains therapist-guided, so the client could be invited to set or adjust the agenda more explicitly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports client choice and ownership without pressure or directives. The client is positioned as the decision-maker throughout the planning process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's own motivations, coping ideas, and concrete plan. Deeper evocation could come from stronger summaries and amplification of change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and shapes the session around the client's responses. More explicit power sharing, such as inviting the client to lead parts of the session, would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session elicits useful change talk, concrete steps, and value-based motivation. The counselor could more actively label, summarize, and amplify this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument and redirected into problem-solving. More strategic double-sided reflections could further reduce the pull of the status quo.
