# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while helping Amira create a safety plan. However, the tone often shifts quickly from acceptance to practical problem-solving, making the validation feel less sustained and more instrumental.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several accurate reflections of Amira’s shame, overwhelm, fear of being alone, and doubt that others will respond. Yet the counselor often moves too quickly to concrete steps and does not fully explore the deeper emotional meaning behind statements such as always failing, talking to a wall, or not caring anymore.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of reducing drug use and coping with urges tonight. The main weakness is that the direction is somewhat directive, with the therapist leading the agenda and providing many concrete options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Amira to choose among options and offers to write the text together, which supports collaboration. However, the therapist supplies most of the ideas, structure, and decision-making framework, so the process remains more therapist-directed than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes Amira’s choice by asking which options feel possible and avoiding direct commands. The score is limited by the frequent provision of exact plans, wording, and structured choices, which can subtly reduce Amira’s ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks useful questions about what Amira wants to change, what small step feels possible, and how confident she is. These moves elicit some client-driven language, but the therapist often shifts to advice and planning before deeply exploring Amira’s values, hopes, reasons for change, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments, such as inviting Amira to select options and offering to build a plan together. However, the counselor frequently shifts into an expert-driven problem-solving stance, which weakens the sense that Amira is the primary expert in her own change process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including Amira’s willingness to try a one-day goal, texting, and using a one-hour delay. However, the counselor does not consistently expand, amplify, or explore the client’s own change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amira repeatedly expresses sustain talk about helplessness, isolation, fear of being ignored, and the sense that her efforts are pointless. The counselor acknowledges some of this but does not effectively use reflections, reframing, or ambivalence exploration to soften or redirect these concerns.
