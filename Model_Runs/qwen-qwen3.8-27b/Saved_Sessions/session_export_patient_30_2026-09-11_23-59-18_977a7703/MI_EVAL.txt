# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a generally nonjudgmental and respectful stance, but acceptance was formulaic. Repeated advice, scripts, and homework weakened the sense that David's preferences were fully honored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Validation was present but mostly surface-level. The psychiatrist missed deeper layers of David's fear, including his uncle's experience, his ability to work, and his frustration with scripts and being pushed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on cholesterol medication, side-effect concern, and the upcoming appointment. However, direction narrowed around script preparation rather than strategic MI guidance toward David's own motivation and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative invitations were present, but the psychiatrist often acted as the expert by supplying information, scripts, and homework. David's repeated preference for simplicity and his own words was not fully incorporated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged David's choices at times, but repeatedly offered scripts, memorized lines, and fallbacks after David declined. This made the interaction feel subtly pressuring rather than fully autonomy-respecting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few initial open questions were asked, but the psychiatrist shifted into information-giving and script-building. There was limited exploration of David's values, hopes, fears, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session included some partnership behaviors, but was often expert-driven. The psychiatrist's repeated suggestion of specific language undermined the sense that the work was being co-created with David.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offered useful change talk about diet, exercise, walking, and rechecking later. The psychiatrist did not consistently notice, reflect, amplify, or expand this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged David's reasons for avoiding medication but did not consistently soften or pivot them toward change. The session often remained centered on the medical debate rather than gently exploring David's values and goals.
