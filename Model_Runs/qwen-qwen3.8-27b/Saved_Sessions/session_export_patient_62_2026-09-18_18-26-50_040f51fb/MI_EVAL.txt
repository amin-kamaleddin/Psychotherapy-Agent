# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance throughout, with multiple moments of genuine validation. Occasionally feels slightly performative in phrasing and could be deeper in specificity to Jordan's lived experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and perspective-taking, particularly in capturing the pain of the hoping and the ambivalence. Some reflections remain surface-level, missing deeper emotional layers such as repeated rejection and identity threat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session flows naturally but lacks a clear strategic focus toward a specific target behavior or concrete change. The conversation follows Jordan's lead passively without re-establishing a clear target after deflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Jordan as an equal partner, asking for input and validating his perspective without imposing an agenda. However, the collaboration is somewhat one-directional, with Jordan's ideas not substantially reshaping the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-pushy, with clear statements affirming Jordan's right not to resolve things now and not owing a grand reason for being there. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming his right NOT to change and full ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some evocative moves (e.g., noticing 'I get up, I try to make something happen') but largely reflects back what Jordan already said rather than actively drawing out new motivation. Misses opportunities to evoke ability and confidence talk around the effort he already put in.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance with no lecturing or advice-giving, using language like 'I'm wondering' and 'I'm curious.' Occasionally asks questions that feel slightly leading, and could more explicitly acknowledge Jordan's expertise in his own situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively notices and honors spontaneous change talk, with good evocative reflections like 'you're still here, still saying you want it to be different.' Could have more consistently and explicitly amplified change talk, such as asking Jordan to elaborate on what 'different' looks like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk reasonably by not reinforcing it and redirecting curiosity toward specific barriers, but misses opportunities for double-sided reflections. The 'rigged game' narrative is validated heavily without a gentle pivot toward agency or possibility.
