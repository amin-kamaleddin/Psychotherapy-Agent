# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive. However, warmth remains somewhat surface-level before quickly shifting into task planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some early emotional experience and normalizes concerns. Deeper shame, fear, and self-punishing thoughts are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of resuming exercise. The direction is clear, though heavily therapist-driven and action-planning focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and incorporates Aisha’s schedule and knee concerns. However, the clinician supplies most of the plan, structure, and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha is offered some choices and is allowed to select days and routines. Autonomy is undermined by the highly prescriptive plan, scripts, and details.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers few open-ended questions to elicit Aisha’s values, reasons for change, or confidence. Motivation is largely shaped by clinician-provided plans rather than client-generated statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts details based on Aisha’s preferences. Still, the psychiatrist remains the primary expert designing the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aisha expresses some readiness and motivation, but the psychiatrist rarely asks evocative questions to expand it. The session quickly moves into routines and instructions, limiting development of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Setbacks are normalized and reframed with statements such as a missed day being data, not failure. The response is supportive, though it remains somewhat prescriptive and does not consistently use MI-consistent methods.
