# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance. Warmth is somewhat task-focused and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows limited reflective listening and mostly focuses on practical content. Responses to emotional or ambivalent statements are often advice-based rather than experientially validating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on increasing physical activity, a short walk, and exploring a flexible gym option. Direction is maintained through prescriptive advice rather than MI-style guided exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Leila to shape some choices and scripts. However, much of the session is still counselor-directed, with the clinician supplying much of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is explicit no-pressure language and support for Leila’s decision to avoid signing a contract. Autonomy support is uneven because many directives and scripted steps are offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers solutions early instead of drawing out Leila’s own motivations, values, and confidence. Open questions are mostly practical, with limited effort to elicit personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not strongly position Leila as the expert on her own life and readiness. Leila contributes to the plan, but the psychiatrist remains the primary problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Leila offers strong spontaneous change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke, amplify, or develop it. There is limited use of MI-style evocative questions or reflection of desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue against sustain talk or barriers, which is positive. However, responses rely mainly on direct problem-solving and scripts rather than MI-based softening strategies.
