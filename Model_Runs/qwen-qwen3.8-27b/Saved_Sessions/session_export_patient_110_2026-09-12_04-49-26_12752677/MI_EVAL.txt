# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoided criticizing the client’s concerns. Acceptance was slightly task-focused and could have more explicitly affirmed the client’s values and commitment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client’s practical concerns about medication, side effects, and staying active with his wife. Empathy remained more cognitive than emotional and could have explored the deeper fear of losing future time with his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed highly focused on medication adherence and creating a practical plan for the doctor. There was little drift, and the conversation effectively moved toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and client co-created and refined a simple script for talking to the doctor, showing strong collaboration. The psychiatrist remained the primary source of medical information, which slightly limited a fully collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices about what to discuss, how to word the message, and whether to practice or simplify. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement that the decision is fully the client’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session began with helpful evocative questions and used the client’s own language about hiking with his wife. The conversation then shifted heavily into information and problem-solving, reducing continued elicitation of the client’s internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited the client’s input throughout. The strong expert role in providing detailed medical information reduced the extent to which the client was framed as the primary expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered clear change talk, including willingness to try a statin and wanting to protect his hiking with his wife. The psychiatrist could have asked more evocative questions to deepen his own motivations, ability, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s hesitations were met respectfully and not argued with, using balanced medical information. The response was more informational than MI-specific, with less use of double-sided reflections or reframing to turn sustain talk into motivation.
