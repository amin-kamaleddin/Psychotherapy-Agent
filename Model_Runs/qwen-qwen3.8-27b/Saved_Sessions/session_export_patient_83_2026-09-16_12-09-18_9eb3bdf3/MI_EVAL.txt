# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, using statements that acknowledge Mai’s need for clarity and safety. Acceptance could be stronger with more explicit normalization of her confusion, disappointment, and anxiety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is present but often remains at a surface level, capturing basic feelings and needs. Deeper emotions such as feeling blindsided, dismissed, or isolated while managing medication alone are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on understanding and safely starting atorvastatin, with little topic drift. Direction could be improved by more clearly connecting practical steps to Mai’s own reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts the plan to Mai’s preferences and offers choices, making the interaction feel collaborative. However, the counselor often leads the planning process rather than inviting Mai to generate more of the solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai is frequently asked for preference and given options, which supports her independence. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing that she is the decision-maker and expert on her own routine.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some motivation and readiness, especially toward the end, but relies heavily on providing medical information rather than drawing out Mai’s internal reasons for change. More evocative questions about her values and goals would strengthen this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai’s input shapes the plan, and the psychiatrist adapts to her preferences, showing some collaboration. The session remains more expert-driven, with the counselor taking the lead on information-gathering and problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai offers meaningful change talk, such as viewing the medication as an “insurance policy” and committing to a routine. The psychiatrist does ask about worries and confidence, but could do more by amplifying and expanding on Mai’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai’s concerns are acknowledged rather than dismissed, and the psychiatrist avoids arguing or reinforcing resistance. However, the counselor often moves quickly into practical problem-solving instead of using MI strategies to address ambivalence.
