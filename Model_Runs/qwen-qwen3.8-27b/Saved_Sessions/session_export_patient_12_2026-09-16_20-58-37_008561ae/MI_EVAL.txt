# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating. To reach a higher level, they could more explicitly affirm the client's worth beyond her substance use, fears, and perceived failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate reflective listening of the client's ambivalence, shame, and fear. The empathy could deepen further into identity-level shame and helplessness, and it becomes somewhat repetitive around missed check-ins and failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains coherent around making support feel reliable and low-pressure. However, it does not consistently connect the support plan to concrete substance-use reduction goals, coping strategies, or specific next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the client's ideas shaping the plan. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still guides the session through repeated structured questions and options about missed check-ins.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client's choice, expertise, and ownership. Stronger autonomy support would include more explicit affirmations that the client can proceed differently and that ownership is central even amid ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions that elicit some client language about hope and small steps. The evocation remains largely logistical and does not deeply draw out values, strengths, confidence, or internal motivation to reduce drug use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that the client is the expert and invites her to shape the process. The stance becomes somewhat directive when repeatedly returning to the missed-check-in issue and offering specific formats.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-oriented language about reliable support, shorter check-ins, and re-engagement. However, they do not consistently reinforce, expand, or build this into stronger motivation for reducing drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client's fears and helps them feel heard. The session could better redirect helplessness by using double-sided reflections or summaries that move toward hope, strengths, and change.
