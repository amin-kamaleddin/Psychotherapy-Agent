# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and non-judgmental tone throughout, with affirmations tailored to each specific disclosure. The psychiatrist normalizes Mei's struggles and reframes self-doubt as self-awareness without ever expressing disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently go beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture emotional nuance, such as extending her 'momentum' metaphor and reaching the identity-level fear beneath her words. The accuracy is high enough that each reflection captures the experiential quality of Mei's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains good thematic coherence and logical progression from concern identification through to commitment building. Could benefit from more explicit signposting of session progress and tighter linkage between identity exploration and concrete behavioral targets.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session consistently feels like a conversation between equals, with decisions co-constructed and the psychiatrist's role clearly that of a skilled listener and questioner. Mei's ideas consistently shape the direction, and the psychiatrist's contributions always serve to help her articulate her own thinking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is woven naturally throughout rather than being perfunctory, with the consistent message that this is her choice, her pace, and her plan. The psychiatrist validates her self-determined plan ('two glasses, that's it') rather than pushing for more, and honors her uncertainty without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire session is structured around questions that draw out Mei's own motivations, with no external statistics, warnings, or clinician-provided reasons for change. The final question about the version of her at 10pm that set the number is a masterful evocative prompt that pulls forward a vivid personal image of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Mei as the expert on her own experience through open-ended questions and language like 'what do you think?' The psychiatrist never prescribes solutions or says 'you should,' always returning the ball to Mei with curiosity rather than authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategically designed questions elicit substantial change talk from Mei, and the psychiatrist effectively amplifies it with affirmations like 'that's not a vague wish; that's a direction.' The evocation progressively deepens from initial ambivalence to concrete imagery of the desired future self.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and validated with care, and the identity-level concern is explored deeply without being reinforced as a barrier. The psychiatrist could have been slightly more active in using double-sided reflections to help Mei explicitly see the tension between her identity concern and her stated goals.
