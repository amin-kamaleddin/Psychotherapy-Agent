# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance, accepting David’s caution and skepticism without pressure. Acceptance is slightly less than top-level because it is more cognitive and procedural than deeply emotionally individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture David’s ambivalence, priorities, and practical concerns, including the side-effect worry and value of staying able-bodied. Empathy is strong but remains more cognitive and strategic, with limited deeper exploration of possible fear about aging, decline, or loss of physical independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on cholesterol management and the medication decision, moving logically from side-effect concerns to information needs, warning signs, and a six-month monitoring plan. Direction could be stronger by linking the ending plan to a concrete next step directly tied to the prescribed medication decision.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently follows David’s lead, allowing his concerns to shape the agenda and using inclusive, joint language. The conversation functions as a shared exploration of risk, values, and decision-making rather than an expert-driven explanation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David’s choice and control are strongly supported throughout, with repeated language about pace, ownership, and staying “in the driver’s seat.” The psychiatrist avoids coercion and respects the client’s “not yet” stance while helping him clarify what would make him feel ready.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites David to articulate his own priorities, values, and concerns without giving direct advice or imposing external reasons for change. However, much of the evoked material remains conditional or sustain talk, and the psychiatrist does not consistently develop stronger change talk around confidence, desire, reasons, or need for medication adherence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist centers David’s perspective and follows his lead, signaling that his knowledge about his body and life matters. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly negotiating the agenda and stating David’s role as the expert on his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks helpful questions that bring out some change talk, such as reconsidering if risk information is concrete or the number remains high. These moments are underdeveloped because there is limited use of DARN/CRAFT and little amplification or summarizing of David’s own arguments for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without confrontation and reframes some of David’s avoidance as practical weighing of tradeoffs. However, the session still validates the status quo for a considerable time and does not consistently use double-sided reflections, strategic summarizing, or evocative questions to pivot toward change.
