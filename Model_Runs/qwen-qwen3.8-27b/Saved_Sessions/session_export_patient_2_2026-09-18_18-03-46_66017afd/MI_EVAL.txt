# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with examples like 'I appreciate you being honest' and 'we're not going to judge you.' Occasionally feels slightly scripted, and the closing 'I'm proud of you' borders on evaluative rather than purely accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate captures of Aisha's emotional landscape and validates her feelings well. Could go deeper into emotional undercurrents such as grief or fear beneath her stated concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior throughout with a logical flow from symptoms to a concrete plan. Never loses momentum or allows the session to drift, even when Aisha minimizes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a teamwork dynamic with 'let's figure it out together' and the safety net framing. Could be more balanced by sharing the psychiatrist's own perspective or co-creating the plan more visibly as a joint effort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and skillfully supports Aisha's autonomy by eliciting her own solutions, offering choices, and affirming ownership without ever prescribing or pressuring. Woven naturally into every interaction with language like 'it's yours' and choice-giving questions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation where every insight, motivation, and plan comes from Aisha herself rather than being provided by the psychiatrist. The psychiatrist's role is to create space and ask the right questions, allowing Aisha to generate all the substance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance positioning Aisha as the expert on her own life and letting her decisions shape the session. The 'I'm proud of you' language and guiding posture slightly shift toward a hierarchical rather than equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and skillfully highlights and amplifies Aisha's own change talk, using targeted questions that elicit concrete commitments and solution-generating language. Change talk is not just elicited but reinforced throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating without agreeing and redirecting to the change talk Aisha already offered, without creating debate. The alcohol-related sustain talk at the end is left unaddressed, which could be seen as a missed opportunity to gently soften it.
