# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive while normalizing Thomas’s stress and boundaries. However, the acceptance was somewhat formulaic and at times over-accommodated Thomas’s minimizing of his risks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used reflections and showed some effort to understand Thomas’s perspective, but much of the empathy remained surface-level. He missed or underexplored important feelings of frustration, surveillance, and concern about being nagged or medicalized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session had minimal steady focus on the target behaviors of smoking cessation maintenance and alcohol use. It drifted toward administrative closure and note wording rather than concrete behavior goals or risk exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited Thomas to define what would make the session useful or complete. However, the broader agenda around testing, closure, and documentation remained largely clinician-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respected Thomas’s right to decide and avoided imposing a lifestyle lecture. It fell short of a 5 because he could more explicitly reinforce Thomas’s capacity and freedom to make balanced health decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist’s questions mainly elicited practical criteria for medical confirmation and closure rather than internal motivation for change. He did not effectively draw out Thomas’s values, hopes, fears, or confidence related to health behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed some partnership by inviting Thomas’s input and acknowledging his expertise about his own life. However, the collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist largely directed the session toward tests, note wording, and closure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were only rare or weak efforts to elicit Thomas’s own reasons, desires, or confidence for healthier behavior. The psychiatrist did not effectively amplify Thomas’s positive statements, such as quitting smoking or caring for his child, into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided direct argument, but he often reinforced Thomas’s reasons for maintaining the status quo. He did not use effective MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections, to gently broaden or soften sustain talk.
