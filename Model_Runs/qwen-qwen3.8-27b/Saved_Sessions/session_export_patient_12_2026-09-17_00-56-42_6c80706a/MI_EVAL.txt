# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming. Some reflections are formulaic or slightly presumptive, which limits the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, loneliness, regret, and the weight of silence. The empathy is good but does not consistently deepen the disclosed fear of being seen or underlying shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session opens with clear focus options and stays relevant to use, loneliness, and silence. It becomes circular without a summary, client-chosen target, or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Amira to shape the work. The session remains somewhat therapist-led and does not fully co-construct a shared plan or next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites choices, avoids advice or pressure, and positions Amira as the expert. It would benefit from stronger explicit affirmations of Amira’s ownership and fewer presumptive statements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit speech about safety, silence, and what would help in the moment. The evocation is limited by not consistently exploring values, strengths, confidence, or Amira’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly states a partner role and invites feedback on how to respond to Amira’s silence. The interaction is still guided more by the psychiatrist than by Amira actively leading.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are brief moments of regret and small-step language, but the psychiatrist does not systematically amplify or develop change talk. Some change language is inserted on Amira’s behalf rather than evoked from her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and acknowledges the pull of continued use. Sustain talk remains dominant, with limited skillful pivoting toward strengths, double-sided reflections, or small steps.
