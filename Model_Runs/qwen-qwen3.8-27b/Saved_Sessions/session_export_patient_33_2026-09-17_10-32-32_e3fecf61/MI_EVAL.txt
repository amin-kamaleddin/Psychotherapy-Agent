# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and explicitly normalizes Diego’s ambivalence without pressuring abstinence. Acceptance is strong but more practical than deeply warm, with fewer explicit affirmations of his worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Diego’s perspective on using heroin as a way to disappear and his fear of being seen as a “lost cause.” Some responses are accurate but generic and could better explore the emotional weight behind his fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear harm-reduction focus, moving from Narcan planning to safer use practices while staying on concrete safety goals. Direction could be stronger with a brief closing summary that consolidates the agreed-upon next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego’s ideas drive the intervention, such as marking the Narcan box and using a phone timer, which supports a collaborative working style. The psychiatrist could more explicitly invite Diego to co-create the session agenda and emphasize his expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Diego’s statement that he is not ready to quit and frames safety steps as choices that fit his current situation. A few comments risk subtly steering or assuming the plan is already completed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits Diego’s own harm-reduction ideas rather than giving direct advice. Evocation is mainly practical and could go deeper into values, identity, and confidence in following the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Diego as the expert on his own life and invites his perspective to shape the conversation. The session is collaborative but remains somewhat therapist-led in structure and topic transitions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego generates strong change talk around safety actions, and the psychiatrist affirms this language effectively. The counselor could further strengthen change talk with scaling, commitment, or a summary of Diego’s reasons and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or pushing for abstinence, and the conversation is redirected toward a manageable safety step. The psychiatrist could use more advanced reflections to balance the desire to escape with his fear of harm.
