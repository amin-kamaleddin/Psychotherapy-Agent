# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgmental regard, and acceptance of Margaret’s preference to reduce rather than quit. The warmth is strong but somewhat procedural and could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflective listening and accurately captures Margaret’s perspective, practical concerns, and preferences. Empathy could be deeper by reflecting underlying emotions such as loneliness, fear, and pressure from friends.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing alcohol use and moves from exploration to a specific, testable plan. There is no significant drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and lets Margaret’s choices shape the plan. Collaboration could be stronger by exploring Margaret’s own ideas more before supplying concrete suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoids pressure, supports Margaret’s choices, and respects her right to define success. Language such as “progress, not perfection” strongly supports client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk, including confidence, goals, and a workable plan. Evocation could be stronger by exploring Margaret’s values, reasons, benefits, and deeper motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys collaboration, seeks permission, and treats Margaret as the expert on her life. The session could feel more client-led if fewer clinician-generated options were offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively evokes and reinforces change talk, including confidence increases and specific intentions. The client’s change language could be further amplified by reflecting values and benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately accepts and refrains from arguing with sustain talk while keeping the focus on reduction. Softening could be more strategic using double-sided reflections that honor sustain talk while gently moving toward change.
