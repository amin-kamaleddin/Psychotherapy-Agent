# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers minimally respectful language but does not consistently demonstrate warm unconditional regard. Continuing to offer help after the client repeatedly asks to be left alone undercuts acceptance of his boundary.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are basic and often paraphrase the client's frustration without deeper understanding. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to reflect feeling pressured, disrespected, or fatigued by repeated advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear MI direction toward smoking cessation or related risk reduction. The focus remains on dental logistics, pain relief, and practical scripts rather than the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative phrases, but the interaction remains largely clinician-driven through repeated options and menus. The client's perspective does not meaningfully guide the session in a partnership way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist sometimes acknowledges choice but repeatedly prompts for help after the client sets a clear boundary. This persistent prompting makes the support feel pressuring rather than autonomy-respecting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is only one broad open-ended question at the start, with no real exploration of the client's motivations, values, or ambivalence. Instead of evoking internal reasons for change, the psychiatrist offers external suggestions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some partnership language but positions themselves as the solution provider rather than an equal. The client is not clearly treated as the expert on his own life or priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is no effective effort to evoke or expand change talk about smoking cessation. No affirmations, values-based questions, or client-driven reasons for change are developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct arguing but does not skillfully reflect or normalize the client's resistance. Repeatedly offering misaligned practical alternatives maintains the impasse rather than softening sustain talk.
