# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently uses a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and affirms the client with statements such as “you’re not doing it wrong” and “you’re not failing.” Acceptance is generally strong, though it occasionally shifts toward reassurance and safety management before fully exploring the client’s emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges the client’s exhaustion and burden, but the reflections remain more surface-level than emotionally deep. There is limited exploration of the client’s fear of failing, guilt about “abandoning” the baby, and the pain behind “I feel like I’m losing my mind.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains well organized and focused on the main concerns: infant sleep, feeding, parental exhaustion, and safety. However, the direction is more problem-solving and medically oriented than guided toward the client’s own goals, values, or change intentions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments, such as co-creating a nurse-line script and a brief plan for when the baby cries. Overall, the interaction is uneven because the clinician often provides long sequences of advice before checking in with the client, making it less like a true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician supports autonomy by asking which small change feels doable and allowing the client to choose a manageable step. At the same time, the session includes many directive recommendations that can reduce the client’s sense of ownership over her decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses some evocative questions, including the opening question about what the client wants to focus on and which small change feels doable. Evocation remains limited because the clinician does not deeply explore the client’s values, hopes, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with an open-ended, client-led question and adapts to the client’s fatigue by offering a simple script and short plan. Partnership is inconsistent because much of the session remains expert-driven, with the clinician leading the direction through detailed guidance and problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client produces some change talk when identifying a small step, such as keeping the night dim and boring. The clinician does not consistently amplify, reflect, or build on the client’s own reasons for change, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged, such as the client’s sense that routines feel like too much work and that she lacks support. The clinician uses few double-sided reflections and does not effectively soften the ambivalence around needing rest while fearing she is failing or abandoning her baby.
