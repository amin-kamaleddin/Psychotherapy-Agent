# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while normalizing the client’s ambivalence. However, some praise feels contingent, and deeper self-critical statements are not accepted with the same warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several accurate reflections but often remains at a cognitive level. Responses frequently shift toward advice before fully exploring shame, fear, or feelings of invisibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on relapse reduction and building a stable, prosocial lifestyle. Direction would be stronger if the plan were more explicitly tied to the client’s values and identity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting input and incorporating the client’s preferences into the plan. However, the psychiatrist still supplies much of the agenda and coping toolkit, limiting fully equal collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s choices and avoids imposing a rigid treatment plan. Repeated prescriptive coping strategies and praise can create a mild directive or performance-focused frame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions and reflects back useful client insights. Evocation is limited by narrow, multiple-choice questions and a tendency to introduce strategies rather than elicit the client’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and accepts pushback, showing some shared power. At key moments, however, the psychiatrist becomes expert-driven by offering numbered plans and coping tools.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and affirms some useful change talk, such as the Saturday plan and the importance of staying present. Change talk is not consistently deepened through DARN/CRAAR-style questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the pull of the old life without arguing or taking sides. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened or redirected using double-sided reflections or reflective pivots.
