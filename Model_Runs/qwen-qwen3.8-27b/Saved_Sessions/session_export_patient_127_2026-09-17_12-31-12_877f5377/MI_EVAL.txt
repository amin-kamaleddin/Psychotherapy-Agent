# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, unconditional positive regard with zero judgment or labeling throughout the session. Notably accepts Timur's self-criticism and ambivalence without pushback, and offers an alternative perspective at the close while explicitly refusing to impose it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses complex, multi-layered reflections that capture both sides of ambivalence simultaneously and name emotions beneath the content (e.g., identifying grief in 'I'm not that kid anymore'). Each reflection adds a new dimension rather than repeating or paraphrasing at a surface level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear logical arc from exploration through values to a concrete next step, with each stage building on the previous one. Minor drift at the end toward identity-level exploration without an explicit tie-back to the concrete behavioral plan before closing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's trajectory is entirely shaped by Timur's spontaneous contributions (the truck story, the repair shop, the porch), which the psychiatrist builds on without directing. Uses shared language ('we,' 'together') while ensuring all substantive content—goals, steps, definitions—originates from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides explicit, unambiguous autonomy support including directly stating Timur's right not to commit and the remarkable meta-level guarantee: 'If I have to push it, it's mine, not yours. So I won't.' Never uses directive language; every question is framed as exploration rather than instruction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits motivation from within Timur through varied open-ended questions and strategic follow-ups, producing a clear progression from vague desire to concrete self-directed commitment language. Never provides reasons for change, fills silence, or introduces external options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Timur as the expert on his own motivation, using 'I'm curious' as a recurring frame and explicitly ceding authority over his self-concept. The session's direction is entirely shaped by Timur's contributions, with the psychiatrist never introducing external options or advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates conditions where Timur generates his own motivation, steps, and commitments, progressing from vague desire to a specific behavioral intention ('I'll pick a time… I need to go there with my eyes open'). Uses the client's own metaphors (trucks, repair shop) as vehicles for evoking future-directed thinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without argument, dismissal, or counter-evidence—instead acknowledging the underlying truth while redirecting or opening space for Timur to find his own counter-argument. The self-label 'I'm lazy' is reframed as honesty rather than met with a direct correction.
