# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and specific unconditional positive regard throughout, with zero judgment or moralizing even when the client was hostile. Affirmations were individualized and earned rather than formulaic, and the client's boundary was respected without pushback.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional reflective listening that accurately captured complex emotional content, such as the double bind of coerced attendance combined with feeling invisible. Reflections went beyond simple mirroring to genuinely engage with the meaning behind the client's words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Remarkably clear and coherent progression toward a specific target: a concrete tool for managing anger in medical waiting rooms. Each question built logically on the previous one, and the session ended with a clear, actionable next step identified by the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multi-layered collaboration with joint framing throughout ('we work on what matters to you'), shared perspectives as contributions rather than verdicts, and a session structure that was dynamically co-constructed by both parties. The psychiatrist consistently followed the client's lead in determining direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent and explicit support for autonomy, including asking permission, matching the client's speed, working within stated boundaries, and never prescribing or using directive language. The final tool and next step were entirely self-generated by the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every meaningful insight was drawn from the client through strategically placed open questions rather than provided by the psychiatrist. The client self-generated his own solution, articulated his own trigger, and directed the next step, demonstrating the psychiatrist's skill as a 'midwife' of the client's own wisdom.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positioned himself as an equal partner, never lecturing or assuming the expert role—even when directly asked what would work, he reflected the question back to the client. Language like 'I'll try to match your speed' and 'both can be true at the same time' reinforced a genuine collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions consistently elicited the client's own change statements, from articulating what a good experience looks like to identifying concrete criteria for success. The session ended with the client self-generating the next step in his own words, demonstrating full ownership of the change process.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple layers of resistance were handled without argument, dismissal, or reinforcement of the sustain talk. The psychiatrist held the tension between both sides (e.g., 'I've been heard before and it went nowhere' AND 'maybe this could be different') and let the client move through it himself.
