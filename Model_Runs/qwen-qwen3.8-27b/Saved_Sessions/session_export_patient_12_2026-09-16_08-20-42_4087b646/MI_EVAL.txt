# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Amira’s pain and avoids blame for her drug use or regret. It falls short of 5 because acceptance of her shame and self-blame could be more explicitly affirming of her worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Amira’s feelings and tracks her emotional state throughout the session. However, reflections are often followed quickly by practical options, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains focus on immediate safety, isolation, and small manageable steps. It lacks clearer, consistent movement toward a larger target behavior such as reducing drug use or strengthening recovery.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently uses collaborative language and invites Amira into decisions. However, the interaction often becomes expert-driven through multiple scripted options and structured plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Amira’s autonomy by repeatedly offering choices and avoiding coercion. It does not reach 5 because the prescriptive options and scripts can subtly reduce Amira’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some open questions to begin evoking Amira’s goals and coping history. The session shifts too quickly to advice and coping tools before fully drawing out her values, confidence, and internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choice and uses collaborative phrases such as “you choose” and “at your pace.” The session is weakened by a menu of clinician-generated interventions that limits genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist opens with change-focused questions and affirms Amira’s agency, but does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand her tentative change statements. More OARS would be needed to deepen Amira’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or dismiss Amira’s statements of helplessness, isolation, and futility. However, these are often redirected into practical coping options rather than gently pivoted toward values, hope, or agency.
