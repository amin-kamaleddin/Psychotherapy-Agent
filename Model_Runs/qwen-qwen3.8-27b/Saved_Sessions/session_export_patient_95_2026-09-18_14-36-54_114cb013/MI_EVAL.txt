# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist validates and never dismisses, even when the patient engages in harsh self-devaluation. The opening statement establishes a non-threatening, accepting container that is maintained for the entire session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture the emotional core and underlying self-dismissal pattern with remarkable accuracy. The psychiatrist names the patient's ambivalence and self-sabotage precisely without being preachy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and logical arc from initial pain expression through the 3am experience to ambivalence and the self-dismissal pattern, with each reflection building on the last. The explicit connection back to broader therapeutic goals or safety planning could be slightly stronger.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine joint exploration, with the patient's words, rhythms, and resistance directly shaping the conversation. The psychiatrist frames observations as curiosities rather than conclusions and follows the patient's lead consistently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the patient's ownership and right to make her own choices without pressure or implied judgment. Statements like 'I'm not going to ask you for a small step' and 'You don't have to perform hope for me' explicitly protect autonomy from coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful open questions draw out the patient's own language and ambivalence without imposing external motivations. The evocation could be more sustained with additional follow-up questions to deepen and develop change talk further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that wisdom about change resides within the patient, never assuming the expert role or providing unsolicited solutions. The session is entirely shaped by the patient's contributions and led by her needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and protected from the patient's own self-dismissal through gentle curiosity and non-judgmental follow-up. Explicit amplification or summarization of the change talk (e.g., reflecting back the parts that matter) would strengthen this further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with validation of its reality rather than argument, while gently highlighting the self-dismissal pattern that undermines the patient's own resources. More explicit double-sided reflections could help the patient see the full landscape of her ambivalence.
