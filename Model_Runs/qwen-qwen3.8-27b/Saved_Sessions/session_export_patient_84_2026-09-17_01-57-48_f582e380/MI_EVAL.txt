# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance, with no moralizing or blame around Malik’s cannabis use. Acceptance is strong but could be deepened by more explicitly affirming Malik’s worth beyond his peer choices or substance use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is good and captures Malik’s frustration with being judged, caught between his mom and peers, and wanting less tension at home. Empathy could be stronger by exploring deeper emotions such as shame, fear of losing belonging, or feeling controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to the referral through family tension and Malik’s desire to be taken seriously, but it does not consistently focus on cannabis use itself. More direct attention to use patterns, risks, or concrete change goals would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and invites Malik to shape the conversation, reflecting a collaborative stance. Collaboration could be more fully client-led by explicitly negotiating agenda priorities such as peers, mother tension, or cannabis use.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, with consistent emphasis on Malik’s choice, ownership, and self-direction. The therapist avoids pressure, does not take sides, and invites Malik to find his own honest solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated motivation, such as wanting less stress, normalcy, and proof of being in control. Evocation could be stronger by more consistently drawing out intrinsic motivation tied to Malik’s values and cannabis-use choices.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist treats Malik as the expert on his own life and invites him to define what would make the conversation feel less forced. Partnership could be further strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging Malik’s peer expertise and co-creating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk appears around calmer home, keeping grades up, and feeling in control, but it is not strongly developed. More amplification, elaboration, and direct linking to cannabis-use change would improve this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Malik’s minimization and reflects the ambivalence between his friends and his mother’s concern. Softening could be stronger by using double-sided reflections to connect sustain talk with Malik’s larger goals for respect, calm, and control.
