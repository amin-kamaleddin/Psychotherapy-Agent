# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental stance with normalizing language. However, the warmth sometimes feels formulaic or premature, with reassurance and problem-solving slightly outweighing deeper acceptance of the client's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic understanding is limited, with most responses focused on factual clarification and practical next steps rather than reflecting the client's deeper ambivalence and social concerns. The counselor rarely captures the push–pull of wanting to cut back while fearing she will seem boring, weird, or less connected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on reducing alcohol use, especially weekday drinking and social situations. A limitation is that the focus becomes quite narrow and practical, with less sustained attention to broader values, social anxiety, and reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration because the counselor incorporates some of the client's ideas, such as the roommate inside-joke, avoiding tracking, and using a one-week trial. However, the psychiatrist still supplies most of the scripts, rules, and plan structure, keeping the client more responsive than co-constructive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor shows moderate autonomy support through low-pressure language, options, and normalization of slips. Nonetheless, many directive and prescriptive moves, such as setting rules and suggesting specific texts, make the support uneven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides many external suggestions rather than consistently eliciting motivation from within the client. Evocative questions about importance, confidence, values, or reasons for change are limited, so the client's own change language is not deeply developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stance is largely expert-driven and solution-focused, with frequent lists of scripts, rules, and coping moves. Although some input is requested, the client is not consistently positioned as the expert on her own life, relationships, and motivations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some spontaneous change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke, reflect, or amplify it. Questions and responses tend to move quickly toward logistics and planning rather than deepening the client's own reasons and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor occasionally softens sustain talk by normalizing low-pressure experimentation and reframing slips as not failure. However, there is limited strategic use of double-sided reflections or value-based redirection, and much of the session remains focused on social scripts and barriers.
