# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and nonjudgmental tone throughout, with normalization of Andrei's minimizing. Falls short of a 5 due to reliance on a limited set of standard MI warmth phrases rather than deeper, individualized acceptance of his specific situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections that capture Andrei's cognitive disconnect between feeling fine and being told something is off. Does not consistently probe the affective layer beneath his flat, minimizing tone or explore the deeper reluctance implied by his passive compliance framing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks clear forward momentum toward the clinical target of alcohol and blood pressure management. The conversation circles around the same themes without introducing key topics or building toward any concrete focus or action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present but the interaction structure remains largely therapist-led with Andrei in a passive, responsive role. The psychiatrist missed a clear opportunity to co-create focus when Andrei asked what they would look at today.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent and natural autonomy support throughout, including reframing compliance as a choice and affirming Andrei's right to make his own decisions. This is one of the stronger areas of the session, though a 5 would require evidence of deeper empowerment in decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to evoke Andrei's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; questions are primarily information-gathering rather than designed to elicit change talk. No exploration of values, scaling, or importance/confidence — a critical gap for a client at the precontemplation stage.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Non-authoritative stance and collaborative tone are present, but the psychiatrist does not explicitly position Andrei as the expert on his own life or negotiate the agenda. The session remains therapist-led in pacing and direction despite gentle language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes minimal effort to elicit or expand on change talk, missing opportunities to follow up on seeds like 'not completely ignoring it.' No use of open questions targeting values, goals, or motivations, leaving the session in a flat compliance-based register.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing Andrei's minimization and picks up on the 'not ignoring' piece rather than the 'I feel fine' piece. However, no MI-specific techniques like double-sided reflections or reframing are used to actively pivot from sustain talk toward change.
