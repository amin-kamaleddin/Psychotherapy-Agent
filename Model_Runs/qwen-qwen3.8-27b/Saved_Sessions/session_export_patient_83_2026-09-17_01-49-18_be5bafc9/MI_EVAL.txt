# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated the patient's concerns without labeling resistance. It was not a 5 because the validation was somewhat formulaic and did not fully normalize the emotional difficulty of trusting a new medication after unclear information.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate reflective listening and captured the patient's desire for clarity, control, and a meaningful reason for the medication. Empathy remained mostly cognitive and did not deeply explore underlying frustration, fear, or feelings of being dismissed by the prescriber.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation stayed focused on the target behavior but remained largely exploratory. It did not consistently move from clarification into a concrete, consolidated next step for adherence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the conversation as joint problem-solving and let the patient's priorities shape the discussion. Collaboration did not reach the highest level because the psychiatrist still controlled the overall structure and did not fully co-construct a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positioned the patient as the decision-maker and avoided pressuring her to take the medication. Language affirmed her right to choose and her capacity to make a trustworthy decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly elicited motivation from the patient rather than providing external reasons for taking the medication. Evocation could be stronger by using more deliberate MI questions to deepen change talk, such as confidence or values-based questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the patient's expertise about her routine and preferences, reducing an expert-driven stance. Partnership did not reach a 5 because the psychiatrist did not fully negotiate the agenda or visibly co-lead the interview.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expressed desire, ability, and commitment, and the psychiatrist elicited some change talk. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or summarize the patient's readiness to start the medication.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged barriers without arguing and reframed concerns as a need for trustworthy information. It was not a 5 because more explicit double-sided reflection or strategic pivots were needed to fully transform sustain talk into change language.
