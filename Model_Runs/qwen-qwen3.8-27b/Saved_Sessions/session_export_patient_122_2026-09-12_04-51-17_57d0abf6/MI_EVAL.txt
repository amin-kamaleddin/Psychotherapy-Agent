# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided moralizing or labeling. Acceptance was solid but somewhat procedural, with fewer moments of deeper, individualized affirmation of Sofia’s worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening and perspective-taking were accurate and helpful, especially around exhaustion and the stress-eating cycle. A key limitation was that some reflections remained cognitive rather than fully emotionally empathic, particularly around her fear of failure and longing for relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, relevant focus on reducing the stress-related cycle in a small, realistic way. Direction was good, though the counselor moved quickly into a specific behavior plan without fully exploring Sofia’s broader concern about feeling overwhelmed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely collaborative, with Sofia influencing the plan by choosing the fridge cue, water backup, and sticky note. Collaboration was somewhat uneven because the psychiatrist still supplied much of the content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was well supported by respecting Sofia’s desire to avoid diet, homework, and rigid programs. This was further strengthened by offering options rather than directives, though some suggestions remained therapist-generated and subtly encouraged trying the plan today.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some client ideas and preferences through questions, but often moved into solution-focused suggestions. Evocation was limited in depth and consistency, especially in drawing out Sofia’s own motivations, values, and confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally took a collaborative stance and invited Sofia’s input in choosing realistic options. Partnership was not fully explicit, as the counselor still provided many specific ideas and sequencing decisions, making the interaction somewhat therapist-guided.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged, and the counselor affirmed parts of it, but the session became more solution-focused and therapist-driven. Strong change-oriented statements were not consistently reflected, amplified, or expanded to deepen Sofia’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled skepticism and fear of failure well by normalizing concerns and reframing the plan as useful information rather than proof of failure. The score was not a 5 because the counselor did not consistently use more strategic MI moves such as double-sided reflections.
