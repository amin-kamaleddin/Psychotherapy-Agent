# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and genuine unconditional positive regard throughout, reframing client's fear as strength and validating without judgment. The psychiatrist explicitly rejects an expert-driven posture and never minimizes Rosa's concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking that consistently captures the emotional and relational significance beyond surface content, such as the identity-level fear of not belonging and the relational meaning of the coffee-and-cigarettes bond. Reflections are vivid, specific, and consistently precise rather than generic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent momentum from ambivalence through identity exploration to a concrete action plan (pick a day, tell Marta, try one morning). The target behavior remains the consistent thread even when adjacent topics like money and social identity arise.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is shaped almost entirely by Rosa's contributions—her metaphors, her ideas, her chosen friend. The interaction feels like a genuine dialogue between equals, with the psychiatrist as a skilled facilitator rather than a director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly stated and reinforced throughout, from establishing 'this is your space' to validating Rosa's right to choose her level of commitment. The psychiatrist never pressures, persuades, or implies an expectation of follow-through.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never states a single reason why Rosa should quit; every question is designed to draw out Rosa's own reasoning, values, and self-observation. This results in Rosa arriving at her own plan and commitment organically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly rejects the expert posture ('I'm not going to tell you what to do') and consistently positions Rosa as the expert on herself. Rosa's ideas drive the session direction, and the psychiatrist builds on them rather than redirecting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategically and consistently evokes change talk through open questions that draw out Rosa's own reasoning, then explicitly names and amplifies her growth (e.g., 'That's not what I told you to say. That's you.'). The session progresses from initial ambivalence to a concrete client-generated commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged and validated using double-sided reflections, but the pivot away from it is not always as strategic as it could be. One moment briefly reinforces the validity of the money concern before redirecting, representing a minor area for refinement.
