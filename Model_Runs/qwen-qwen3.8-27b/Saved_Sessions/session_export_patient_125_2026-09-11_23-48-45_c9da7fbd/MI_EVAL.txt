# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and non-judgmental, using validating statements such as “I hear you” and “It makes sense.” However, the tone is more task-oriented and occasionally corrective rather than deeply affirming or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects frustration and anxiety accurately but often stays at a surface level. Deeper feelings about being targeted, appearing guilty, or fearing consequences are not consistently explored or emotionally attuned.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the immediate problem of contacting his mother and following facility rules. The direction is clearer than the MI process, as it emphasizes problem-solving rather than connecting the issue to broader goals such as compliance and release readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some initial collaboration through offering options and asking for preference, but the interaction becomes counselor-led as the psychiatrist keeps introducing scripts and backups. Greater partnership would require consistently following Tariq’s lead and co-creating language that feels authentic to him.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges limits and offers choices, but repeatedly supplies scripts, delivery tips, and rehearsals that reduce Tariq’s ownership of the process. Autonomy support would be stronger by following Tariq’s preferred wording and asking what feels realistic for him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative questions early in the session, but the counselor mostly supplies advice and scripts rather than drawing out Tariq’s own motivations. Stronger evocation would involve asking what talking to his mother means to him and how staying in line connects to his goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with collaborative language but quickly takes a leading stance by providing multiple scripts and strategies. Tariq’s discomfort with scripted language is not fully addressed through power sharing or co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq provides change talk about staying in line and doing the right thing, but the psychiatrist does not amplify or build on it to evoke motivation. The responses shift quickly to scripts and problem-solving rather than exploring his values, goals, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq’s sustain talk about conflict, being targeted, and fearing a file is not effectively softened or redirected. The counselor sometimes reinforces problem-solving around refusal rather than using double-sided reflections to move the conversation toward Tariq’s own goals.
