# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, nonjudgmental, and consistently affirms the client’s effort, self-awareness, and plan-making. Acceptance is strong but remains more tied to performance and progress than fully unconditional regard for the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses reflective listening to accurately capture ambivalence, pride, frustration, and the desire to maintain dignity. A higher score would require deeper reflection of the emotional sting of literacy shame and fear of being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reentry risks and moves toward concrete targets: asking Donna for help with forms and managing Friday-night peer pressure. Direction could be stronger by more explicitly connecting each step back to the overarching goal of avoiding reoffending.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive, exploratory language and allows the client’s ideas to shape the plan. Collaboration remains at a 4 because the psychiatrist still largely controls the agenda and does not explicitly invite the client to lead or restructure the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and ownership by asking for the client’s perspective and highlighting that the client created his own plan. Autonomy support could be strengthened with more explicit language about the client’s right to decide when, whether, and how to use the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits the client’s own motivations, metaphors, and strengths rather than providing reasons for change. Evocation is effective but somewhat action-focused and could go deeper into values, identity, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on his own life and avoids adopting an expert problem-solving role. Partnership is strong but not fully exemplary because the session structure and agenda remain clinician-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reinforces the client’s own statements of desire, ability, and need while expanding them into a concrete plan. The process would reach a 5 with more development of deeper change talk, such as importance/confidence rulers and values-based reinforcement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges boredom, peer pull, and embarrassment without arguing, and supports the client’s “leaky tap” reframe. Softening sustain talk could be more strategic through double-sided reflections and clearer redirection toward protective goals.
