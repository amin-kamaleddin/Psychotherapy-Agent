# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respectful, nonjudgmental regard and honors Maria’s request for silence. However, the repeated safety directives make the stance feel somewhat more crisis-management than fully affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Maria’s pain, exhaustion, and desperation. The empathy is somewhat formulaic and misses deeper ambivalence, fear, and conflict between wanting relief and staying for her sister.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on acute suicidal risk, access to pills, and immediate safety. The direction is strong but somewhat repetitive and does not move smoothly toward an MI-focused change pathway.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and asks for Maria’s preferences. However, the repeated safety checklist and clinician-driven recommendations make the collaboration feel partly procedural rather than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Maria’s sense of choice by offering options, respecting her request for silence, and avoiding forced participation. Imperative safety instructions are clinically appropriate but slightly reduce emphasis on Maria’s ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening question is evocative, but the session quickly becomes dominated by safety assessment. The psychiatrist does not sufficiently draw out Maria’s values, strengths, reasons to stay, or emerging hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is use of partnership language such as asking what Maria would prefer and offering choices. The overall stance remains quite clinician-led due to repeated safety checks, directives, and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria offers meaningful change talk, including wanting to keep going for her sister and breathing for a minute. The psychiatrist only briefly affirms this and does not actively expand, elicit, or amplify it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Maria’s hopeless statements. However, the response rarely uses MI techniques to soften sustain talk by exploring exceptions, values, or alternative meanings.
