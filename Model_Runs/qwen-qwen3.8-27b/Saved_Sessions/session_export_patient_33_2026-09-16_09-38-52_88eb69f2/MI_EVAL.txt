# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally nonjudgmental, respectful, and privacy-conscious, with no clear moralizing or pressure. Acceptance was somewhat procedural and task-oriented, so it was not consistently warm or individualized enough for a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and helpful for tracking Diego’s practical concerns, such as wanting safer use without quitting now. They remained mostly cognitive rather than emotional, missing some of the deeper stigma, fear, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear harm-reduction target and moved toward concrete, time-bound steps. Early information delivery broadened the agenda somewhat, but the overall direction was strong.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well later in the session, especially when co-creating a short privacy-friendly phone script. The earlier phase was more therapist-led, which limited fully balanced collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Diego’s choices, respected his decision not to pursue buprenorphine, and avoided pressure to stop using. Repeated menus of therapist-generated options may have subtly narrowed Diego’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions and confidence rulers effectively elicited Diego’s own plans, preferences, and problem-solving ideas. Evocation was more behavioral than motivational, with limited exploration of deeper values or reasons for safety.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were clear partnership behaviors, including permission-seeking, privacy respect, and shared planning. The psychiatrist often acted as an expert problem-solver by supplying options before Diego had more opportunity to generate his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked practical change talk and reinforced concrete safety steps. It was not a 5 because the change talk was mainly logistical rather than values-based.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego’s sustain talk was not challenged or amplified, and the psychiatrist kept focus on harm reduction rather than abstinence. The sustain talk could have been further reframed with double-sided reflections to more actively support the shift toward safer use.
