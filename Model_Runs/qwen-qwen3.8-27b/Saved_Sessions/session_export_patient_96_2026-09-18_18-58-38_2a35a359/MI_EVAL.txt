# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, with explicit affirmations of Marisol's autonomy and right to her own choices. However, validation occasionally becomes formulaic, with 'You know yourself well' repeated three times, giving it a scripted quality.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening with accurate summaries of Marisol's stance and emotional reality, particularly regarding the referral context. Some reflections remain surface-level and miss the deeper emotional nuance of feeling invalidated by being sent to a session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains topical relevance but lacks strategic momentum, moving through several topics without a clear arc or progression. It ends without a concrete next step or summary, feeling more like an open-ended conversation than a directed MI session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and invites input, but the psychiatrist controls the pace rather than genuinely following Marisol's stated preference to simply hear the dentist's concerns. Marisol's lead is not picked up until her second expression of frustration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout — explicit ownership language ('That's your call and your body'), no persuasive pressure, and consistent framing of all decisions as Marisol's to make. This is the strongest domain of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes moderate evocative efforts, eliciting a small but real piece of self-monitoring language and a thread of curiosity about the dentist's concern. However, the evocation is limited in depth and does not systematically connect Marisol's strong autonomy theme to self-care as self-ownership.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive language and explicitly positions herself as not arguing or convincing. However, the partnership is somewhat one-directional in practice, with the psychiatrist leading every turn rather than following Marisol's stated direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to cultivate change talk; the session ends with Marisol in her original position with no new change talk elicited. One evocative question produced a small sliver of change-adjacent language, but opportunities to deepen it were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Correctly avoids reinforcing or arguing against sustain talk, but responses are largely neutral affirmations rather than strategic softening techniques. Misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance to open space for forward movement.
