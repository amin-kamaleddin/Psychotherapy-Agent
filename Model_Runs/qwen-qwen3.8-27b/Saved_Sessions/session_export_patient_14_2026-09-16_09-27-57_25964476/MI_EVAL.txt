# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s time constraints and practical focus. The tone is efficient but sometimes procedural, with only mild warmth toward the client’s frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist understands the client’s practical pressures but largely reflects cognitive concerns rather than emotional impact. Deeper empathy is limited by minimal exploration of fear, stress, or health worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on weight, blood pressure, and practical lifestyle change without drifting off topic. The main weakness is repetitive recapping at the end, which reduces efficiency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences are incorporated into the plan, but the psychiatrist still drives much of the content by generating options and a structured protocol. More client-generated solution-building would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids coercive language, supporting the client’s decisions about snacks, reminders, and monitoring. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicitly framing the plan as the client’s own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides rationales and logistical questions rather than consistently eliciting the client’s own motivation, values, or confidence. This is the weakest area, as the client’s deeper reasons for change are not sufficiently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and uses his preferences, but much of the planning is therapist-generated. A stronger partnership would involve asking the client to generate and shape the plan first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk by asking what feels doable and inviting plan details. However, the client’s motivation, confidence, ability, and values are not consistently deepened or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles the client’s time-related resistance well by agreeing to keep changes practical and low-effort. The approach avoids arguing or lecturing, though more sophisticated ambivalence-handling could be used.
