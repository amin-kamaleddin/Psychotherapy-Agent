# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes several of Erik’s concerns. However, the warmth is somewhat formulaic and the frequent advice-giving makes the interaction feel more coach-like than fully affirming of Erik’s personhood.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges comparisons, doubt, and hesitation, but the empathy remains mostly cognitive and surface-level. Several emotional themes, such as frustration with slower progress and fear of being a failure, were not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target behavior: consistent gym attendance and reducing the likelihood of skipping. The focus is somewhat narrow, though, and broader goals such as weight loss or diet are not addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and modifies the plan based on Erik’s preferences, such as dropping the texting idea and removing the rest-day cap. However, the psychiatrist remains the main generator of strategies, so the client is more often responding to therapist-generated options than co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive moments, including respecting Erik’s choice to keep the plan simple and avoid involving a friend. At the same time, many directive statements and specific behavioral prescriptions make the process feel prescriptive rather than fully client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with an open, evocative question, but it quickly shifts into advice-giving and behavior planning. The psychiatrist supplies many reasons for change and scripts rather than consistently drawing out Erik’s own internal reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership by asking for preferences and adapting to Erik’s responses. A more equal stance would be needed because much of the session is driven by the psychiatrist providing strategies, scripts, and rules.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Erik produces some change talk spontaneously, and the psychiatrist affirms parts of it. The psychiatrist does not consistently use open questions or affirmations that would draw out and expand Erik’s own desire, ability, reasons, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and often normalizes Erik’s doubts and tiredness. The responses could be more MI-consistent by using double-sided reflections, pivots, and connecting these concerns back to Erik’s own values and change goals.
