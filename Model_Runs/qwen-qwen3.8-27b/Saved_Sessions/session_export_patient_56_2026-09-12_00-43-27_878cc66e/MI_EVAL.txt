# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly affirms the patient's priorities and choices. The warmth is sometimes generic and could more explicitly affirm the patient's deeper struggles with perfectionism and fear of feeling like a fraud.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows reasonable attunement to the patient's concern that interventions may feel mechanical or performative. Empathy remains uneven and shallow because the response often moves quickly into problem-solving without fully exploring the patient's underlying shame or perfectionistic pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on reducing work-related intrusion during family time and building a concrete plan for being present at dinner. The focus occasionally broadens into work anxiety and memory concerns, but the core MI target remains clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's input helps shape the final plan, especially around putting the phone away and using a thought label. The interaction remains unevenly collaborative because the psychiatrist frequently generates and introduces multiple strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by inviting the patient to choose what feels doable and respecting her refusals or modifications. Autonomy support is somewhat limited by the continued introduction of structured tasks and additional steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that elicit the patient's values and preferences, such as wanting genuine presence and trusting herself later. Evocation is limited because it mostly involves selecting among therapist-generated strategies rather than deeply drawing out the patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient to choose among options, and the patient's preferences influence the final plan. The session remains more clinician-led than fully collaborative, with many solutions generated by the therapist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk about being present for family and trusting herself to reconstruct work details later. The development of change talk is limited because it does not systematically explore desire, ability, reasons, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument and removing rigid structures such as timers and fixed check-in times. The response could be more strategically MI-consistent by using double-sided reflections and reducing the introduction of new potential sources of resistance.
