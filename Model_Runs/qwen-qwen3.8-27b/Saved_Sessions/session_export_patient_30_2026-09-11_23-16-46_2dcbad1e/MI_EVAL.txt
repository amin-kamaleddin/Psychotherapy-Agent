# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirming and normalizing statements. The interaction can feel somewhat mechanical and safety-driven, limiting deeper acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mostly responds to factual/medical content rather than underlying fear, fatigue, or ambivalence. Lacks complex reflections of emotional and motivational meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays generally focused on cholesterol, diet, walking, and medication. The conversation repeatedly drifts toward medical safety and paperwork rather than a client-led change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some collaborative language and invites input, but much of the session is clinician-led information and options. Collaboration is limited by repeated suggestions for notes, monitoring, and lists.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms the client's preference for simplicity and does not force medication or a detailed plan. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated directive medical advice and task suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly logistical rather than motivation-eliciting. The clinician does not effectively draw out values, hopes, confidence, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and asks for input, but repeatedly drives the conversation toward lists, notes, scripts, and monitoring. This pattern can feel more directive than partner-like.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges some client-generated change talk about walking and diet. The clinician does not consistently expand or strengthen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reassures that the client is not in immediate danger and focuses on warning signs, which can reinforce avoidance. Does not use MI-consistent strategies such as double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to change.
