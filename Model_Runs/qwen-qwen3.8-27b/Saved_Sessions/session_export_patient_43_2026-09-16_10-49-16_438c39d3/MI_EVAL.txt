# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirmed Eric’s choices. Acceptance was slightly limited by repetitive clinical offers and health warnings that made the tone feel more managerial than purely accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected several surface-level themes, including work stress, feeling fine, and pressure from his wife and doctor. However, the reflections stayed mostly content-focused and did not deeply explore Eric’s underlying emotions or identity-related concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking, health, and follow-up planning without significant drift. Direction was limited because the psychiatrist did not strategically steer the conversation toward values, ambivalence, or change-relevant goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements, such as asking Eric for preferences and inviting his input on next steps. Collaboration was limited because the psychiatrist repeatedly presented clinician-generated options and medical information rather than fully letting Eric’s concerns lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported Eric’s autonomy by respecting his refusal to quit, reducing, or schedule follow-up. Autonomy support was slightly reduced by repeated offers of tips, warning signs, and future check-in options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made a few early attempts to elicit Eric’s priorities, but the session became largely driven by clinician suggestions. Several key evocation opportunities, such as exploring the cough, stamina, toughness, or family concerns, were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some partnership language and invited Eric to choose what he wanted from the session. Partnership was limited because Eric was not fully positioned as the expert on his own life, and the agenda remained partly counselor-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few weak attempts to invite Eric to consider what matters to him, but these were not effectively expanded into change talk. The psychiatrist did not use scaling questions, benefit exploration, or affirming follow-ups to draw out Eric’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoided arguing or directly challenging Eric’s reasons for not changing. However, sustain talk was repeatedly reflected back without using double-sided reflections, reframing, or gentle pivots toward future health, values, or possible experiments.
