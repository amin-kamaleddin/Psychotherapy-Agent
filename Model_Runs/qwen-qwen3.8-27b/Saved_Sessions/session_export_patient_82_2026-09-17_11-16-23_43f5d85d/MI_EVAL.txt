# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, non-judgmental stance with strong validation and no stigmatizing language. Falls just short of a 5 because acceptance is slightly conditional on the client's insight and self-reflection rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic reflections that capture not just content but the underlying emotional meaning, exemplified by the 'folder' metaphor and complex double-sided reflections. The client repeatedly confirmed the accuracy of these reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is largely exploratory and reflective, which is appropriate for a first session, but lacks consistent strategic direction toward a specific target behavior or change goal. The conversation wanders between themes without deliberate summarizing or collaboratively identifying a concrete focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding collaboration with the session consistently framed as a joint exploration between equals, using inclusive language and co-creating shared understanding. The psychiatrist never assumes expertise or drives toward a predetermined conclusion.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's autonomy with a non-directive approach, never pressuring decisions or imposing a course of action. Could more explicitly affirm the client's ownership of the change process and capacity for self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws out the client's own reasons and motivations using open questions and reflective listening rather than imposing interpretations. Could be more deliberately aimed at eliciting and expanding change talk in a more targeted and strategic way.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with clear power-sharing moves that put the client in the driver's seat, such as inviting him to shape the session's purpose. Could be more explicit in acknowledging the client's expertise in his own life and negotiating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited but the psychiatrist does not consistently or strategically amplify, reflect back, or expand on these moments. Tends to validate and reflect rather than deliberately pivot to explore the client's ability, values, or direction.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively handles sustain talk using double-sided reflections that acknowledge the client's concerns while keeping the door open for change. Could more explicitly and strategically pivot away from sustain talk in a few key moments.
