# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client’s fear of being trapped, watched, or making a scene. It is not a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat task-oriented and lacks richer unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence, safety needs, and fear of freezing when watched. It is not a 5 because the reflections do not consistently go deeper into underlying shame, fear, or the cost of avoidance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a target community-engagement behavior and progresses logically from a small step to specific safety conditions. It is not a 5 because the target behavior is not explicitly re-anchored or tied together in a clear closing summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a plan using client-generated ideas such as the library, Thursday lobby, and sticky note. It is not a 5 because the collaboration is not fully explicit and the counselor still leads through structured questions and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is supported in choosing her own step and is reminded that leaving is allowed without making a scene. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist does not explicitly state that the client has full choice to modify or not go at all.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes practical ideas from the client using open-ended questions. It is not higher because evocation is mostly logistical rather than motivational, with limited eliciting of internal reasons, values, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert on her own safe spaces and doable steps, and her ideas shape the session. It is not a 5 because the counselor-led structure and leading questions can subtly narrow the client’s role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encourages small, doable actions, but does not deeply develop the client’s own desire, ability, reasons, or confidence for change. The client’s sparse change talk is acknowledged but not strategically amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s excuses and acknowledges her ambivalence without judgment. It is not higher because there is limited use of double-sided reflections or reframing to shift the balance toward change.
