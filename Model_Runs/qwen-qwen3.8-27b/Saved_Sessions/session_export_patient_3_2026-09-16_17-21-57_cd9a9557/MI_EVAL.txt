# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s efforts without reinforcing shame. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly naming the client’s worth independent of progress or body image.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence, practical constraints, and self-critical voice. Empathy could deepen by exploring the underlying shame, sadness, or body-image pain more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on resuming exercise in a manageable way that supports confidence and self-image. Questions steadily move the conversation from broad motivation to a specific action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids giving expert-driven advice and instead builds on the client’s own ideas. The plan is clearly co-created through the client’s suggestions about timing, reminders, shoes, and definitions of success.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly invited to define her own goals, choices, and what counts as a win. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming that she is in charge and may choose differently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits the client’s internal reasons for change rather than providing external reasons. The client articulates her own values, motivations, and confidence about making a small but meaningful change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction reflects collaboration and positions the client as the expert on her own life. The partnership stance could be more explicit by directly stating support for the client’s choices and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes strong change talk about not skipping, feeling less trapped, and choosing actions that support herself. Client statements of motivation are repeatedly reinforced through reflective summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or amplifying the client’s fears of quitting or talking herself out. The score is not a 5 mainly because sustain talk was limited, but when present it was addressed effectively.
