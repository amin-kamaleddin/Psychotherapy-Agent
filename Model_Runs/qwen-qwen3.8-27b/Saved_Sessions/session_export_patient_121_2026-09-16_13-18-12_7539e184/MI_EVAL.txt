# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, validates Sofia’s experience, and avoids blame or judgment. However, the warmth is slightly reduced by continuing to offer strategies after Sofia asks them to stop.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses clear, accurate reflections and shows effort to understand Sofia’s perspective. The main limitation is that empathy often remains surface-level and shifts quickly into problem-solving rather than exploring deeper shame, fear, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing the experience of being publicly pulled out of class. Although several concrete strategies are introduced, they remain connected to the target behavior and do not lose the session’s direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates Sofia’s preferences into the plan, especially the note and washroom approach. However, collaboration is limited because the counselor supplies much of the specific content rather than co-constructing the plan from Sofia’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites Sofia to make choices and avoids commanding language. Autonomy support is weakened when additional strategies are offered after Sofia says she has enough and asks for the list to stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with strong open-ended questions that invite Sofia to identify her own goals and resources. However, much of the session shifts into advice-giving and prescriptive suggestions rather than eliciting Sofia’s own motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership through shared choice-making and follow-up on Sofia’s selected options. The interaction becomes uneven when the psychiatrist repeatedly generates options and scripts, creating a more expert-driven dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia offers some change talk, such as considering the note and wanting less embarrassment. The psychiatrist does not consistently evoke, amplify, or deepen this change talk, relying more on counselor-generated options and scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia’s sustain talk is acknowledged without being reinforced, and the psychiatrist gently redirects toward alternatives. However, the responses often use additional suggestions rather than MI-skilled double-sided reflections or evocative pivots to resolve ambivalence.
