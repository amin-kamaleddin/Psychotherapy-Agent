# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent respect, validation, and nonjudgment toward Sofia’s concerns and choices. The support is genuine but somewhat procedural, with room to affirm her worth and ambivalence more deeply.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflections capture key parts of Sofia’s experience, but empathy is often shallow and inconsistent. Important feelings such as guilt, fear of harming the baby, social identity, and anxiety about being “the weird one” are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target behavior of alcohol use during pregnancy, especially in weekend social situations. The counselor effectively narrows the issue to specific, actionable steps without significant topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia is given some space to choose options and use her own language, such as the NA beer and “I’m good, thanks.” However, the counselor frequently introduces multiple scripts and plans, making the interaction feel more expert-driven than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some autonomy-supportive language and avoids commanding Sofia to act. However, early planning, multiple options, and scripted suggestions can undermine Sofia’s sense of choice and self-directed pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks useful open questions and uses a confidence scale to elicit some ability and intention talk. Evocation is limited by a quick shift into advice and tactics before deeply exploring Sofia’s own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Sofia’s input and adopts some of her preferred language, showing partial collaboration. Yet the frequent provision of options, scripts, and drafts can make the psychiatrist seem more like a problem-solver than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia offers spontaneous change talk, but the counselor does not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen it. Responses often move quickly into practical tactics rather than reflecting and expanding Sofia’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or dismissing Sofia’s concerns about weekends, friends, and Jess. However, there is less active use of double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence and redirect toward her values and goals.
