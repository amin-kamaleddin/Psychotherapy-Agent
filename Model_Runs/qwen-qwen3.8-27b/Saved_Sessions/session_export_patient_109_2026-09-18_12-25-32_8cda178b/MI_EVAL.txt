# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, genuine, and non-judgmental throughout. The psychiatrist reframes self-doubt as strength (e.g., 'That's not weakness, that's wisdom') and validates without inflating or dismissing the client's small goals.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that capture Rafael's ambivalence, emotional nuances, and psychological process rather than just content. Particularly strong in naming the shift from intrinsic motivation to perceived burden and the distinction between tiredness and laziness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful progression from exploring barriers to identifying obstacles to narrowing toward a concrete, client-defined action. No topic drift; each question builds logically on the previous while maintaining focus on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine dialogue between equals, with Rafael's ideas consistently shaping the direction. The plan that emerges is entirely co-constructed through the psychiatrist's strategic questions rather than suggestion or direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly separates external pressure from internal motivation ('not because you should, but because something in you would want to'). The entire plan is framed as Rafael's choice, with his right to not follow through implicitly honored throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every intervention is designed to elicit Rafael's own motivations, solutions, and insights rather than providing information or reasons for change. The progression from vague intention to concrete, self-generated plan is entirely driven by the client's own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Rafael through non-expert, client-centered language. The psychiatrist's reflections deepen Rafael's own thinking rather than providing the psychiatrist's perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk that progresses naturally from acknowledging his membership to committing to a specific action and imagining himself in the scenario. The psychiatrist never injects reasons for change but uses strategic questions and reflections that guide Rafael to articulate his own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively with appropriate validation and gentle redirection, never amplifying reasons to stay the same. Sustain talk is relatively limited in this session (mild discouragement rather than strong resistance), so there is less opportunity to demonstrate this skill.
