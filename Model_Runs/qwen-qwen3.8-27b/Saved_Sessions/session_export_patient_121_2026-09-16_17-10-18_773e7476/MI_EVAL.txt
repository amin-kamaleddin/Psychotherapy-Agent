# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental and respectful stance, validating Sofia’s boundaries and need for safety. However, the warmth remains somewhat procedural, with fewer deep person-centered affirmations of her worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Sofia’s ambivalence and practical concerns. However, the empathy stays largely cognitive and does not fully name or validate deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing the feeling of being trapped and building a small step toward trust. The conversation is progressively guided toward a concrete, testable behavior without drifting into abstract complaint.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with Sofia’s specific ideas shaping the emerging plan. However, the therapist still leads the structure through guiding questions and summaries, with limited explicit co-creation language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids advice, pressure, and directives, repeatedly emphasizing Sofia’s control over timing, boundaries, and exit. However, autonomy support is mostly implied rather than explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is highly effective at evoking motivation from within Sofia using open, future-focused, and scaled questions. This allows Sofia to generate her own concrete change talk and self-directed plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Sofia as the expert on her own experience, and her responses directly shape the session. However, the therapist maintains a structured agenda without explicitly negotiating focus or pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses open questions and reflections to elicit client-generated motivation and planning language. Sofia moves from general distrust to specific, actionable change-oriented statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue or minimize Sofia’s sustain talk and reframes it as protective and reasonable. However, the session could benefit from more deliberate double-sided reflections to balance ambivalence in the moment.
