# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, validation, and nonjudgmental respect. Statements such as “I’m glad you’re here” and “no promises, no pressure” create a safe, accepting space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflections of ambivalence, such as “holding two true things at once.” However, empathy does not always go deep enough into underlying emotions such as identity loss, fear, or financial stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking-related concerns and moves toward a concrete, manageable coping step. However, the broader goal of smoking cessation could have been more consistently foregrounded.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rosa’s expertise and co-creates a plan with her, using collaborative language such as “we” and “draft it together.” The counselor still guides much of the structure, so collaboration is strong but not fully balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated affirmation of Rosa’s right to choose. The psychiatrist avoids pressure and keeps the plan small, client-generated, and non-threatening.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to elicit Rosa’s own ideas and coping plans. However, evocation is more task-focused and does not deeply elicit intrinsic values, confidence, or motivations for quitting smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance by inviting Rosa’s input and allowing her ideas to shape the plan. The counselor still sets much of the agenda, so the partnership is effective but not fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through questions that invite Rosa to generate her own ideas. However, change talk is not systematically developed or amplified using DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or minimizing Rosa’s concerns. Some sustain talk remains central, and there are fewer double-sided reflections or explicit pivots toward change.
