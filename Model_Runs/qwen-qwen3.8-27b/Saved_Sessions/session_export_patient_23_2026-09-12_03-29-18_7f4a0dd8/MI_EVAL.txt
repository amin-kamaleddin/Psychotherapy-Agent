# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming, especially with the self-compassion lapse plan. Could more deeply affirm Claudia’s values, strengths, and personhood beyond the alcohol-reduction goal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Claudia’s observations, the meaning of wine, and worry about stressful nights. Deeper exploration of underlying emotions such as loneliness, loss of control, fear, or ambivalence would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused and moves coherently from ambivalence to goal-setting, confidence, planning, obstacles, and follow-up. It remains clinically appropriate and avoids irrelevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally co-creates the plan and allows Claudia’s choices to guide the session. However, the counselor’s frequent content suggestions and structuring can make it feel more guided than fully mutual.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission-seeking, noncoercive language, and emphasis on choice support Claudia’s autonomy well. The psychiatrist could more explicitly state that Claudia is free to modify, simplify, or abandon any part of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful open questions and confidence-based prompts, but often moves quickly to suggestions and drafted language. This limits the depth and client-ownership of evoked motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Claudia is treated as the expert, with meaningful choice in goals, strategies, and timing. The counselor’s active role in generating options and wording reduces the fully client-led partnership needed for a higher score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits useful change talk, including reduced drinking goals, confidence ratings, and planned actions. The counselor could do more to elicit and amplify Claudia’s own DARN-C reasons instead of supplying strategies early.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled gently without argument, with validation and compassionate framing. More strategic redirection of status-quo reasons into values or change talk would strengthen this criterion.
