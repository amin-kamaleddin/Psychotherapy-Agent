# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth, nonjudgment, and respect for the client's perspective. He avoided moralizing or correcting the client's minimizing statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client's ambivalence, defensiveness, and fear of being judged. Some underlying concerns, such as pressure at school, were not explored deeply enough.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stayed focused on the target concern of alcohol use at social gatherings. However, repeated offers to end the session limited the opportunity to build momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client's input and used shared decision-making language throughout. The collaboration was less strong when specific suggestions were mainly supplied by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was repeatedly reminded that choices were his own and that he controlled the pace of the session. The psychiatrist avoided pressure, guilt, or imposed advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked some appropriate questions to draw out the client's own perspective. He did not go far enough in exploring the client's deeper values, fears, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a generally non-authoritative stance and asked permission before sharing information. The session was still somewhat shaped by the psychiatrist's menu-style options and suggested focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change-oriented language, including a small plan to keep water. He did not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it. He could have used more double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to help the client examine both sides.
