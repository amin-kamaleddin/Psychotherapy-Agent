# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and uses normalizing, affirming statements, but the warmth is somewhat formulaic and interrupted by prescriptive advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some accurate emotional reflections, but engagement is limited because the counselor quickly shifts to practical strategies.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol at social events and linking it to a concrete goal, such as better sleep.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's ideas influence the plan, but the counselor often leads with detailed options and summaries rather than co-creating as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor includes nonjudgmental language, but much of the advice is directive and does not consistently emphasize the client's ownership or choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks useful opening questions but tends to supply strategies and reasons rather than deeply eliciting the client's internal motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert role and frequently structures the session, limiting a genuine power-sharing, collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor affirms progress and elicits a concrete commitment, but does not consistently deepen or amplify the client's own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcement, and the counselor offers low-effort alternatives, though active MI softening techniques are used only modestly.
