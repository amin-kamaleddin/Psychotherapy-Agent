# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, acceptance, and positive regard without judgment or pressure. Leila’s experiences, concerns, and efforts are affirmed in a warm and supportive way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Leila’s practical perspective and coping strategies. A 5 would require more consistent exploration and validation of her underlying emotions rather than mostly cognitive content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on developing a sustainable exercise plan. The conversation progressively narrows from general goals to concrete, actionable steps without losing momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with Leila’s ideas shaping the resulting plan. The psychiatrist contributes structure and momentum while remaining an equal partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Leila’s freedom to choose, set boundaries, and own the process is repeatedly emphasized. There is no controlling language, coercion, or pressure to commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Leila’s own ideas, preferences, and practical solutions. More consistent evocation of deeper motivations, values, and self-efficacy would elevate the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Leila is consistently positioned as the expert on her own life and needs. The psychiatrist avoids direct advice and allows Leila’s contributions to guide the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Leila offers clear statements of desire, ability, reasons, and commitment. More deliberate use of DARN-C follow-ups could further amplify and deepen her own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Leila’s barriers and concerns are acknowledged without argument or minimization. They are reframed as protective boundaries and workable criteria that support rather than block change.
