# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates nonjudgmental, respectful attention through validating and permission-seeking language. Acceptance is strong but occasionally slips into mild one-sided framing when characterizing the parents' reaction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates solid reflective listening and accurately captures both Marcus's social framing of drinking and his concern about being mistrusted. Some reflections feel formulaic and do not consistently deepen the emotional or identity-based meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins by connecting to the presenting concern but quickly shifts toward parent conflict and communication scripts. There is limited sustained focus on the target behavior of alcohol use and client motivation for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering choices and allowing Marcus to select approaches. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently supplies the options and language rather than fully co-creating them with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms Marcus's freedom to choose, seeks permission, and respects his boundary about not rehearsing scripts. Later suggestions become somewhat directive, limiting the score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses several open, preference-focused questions that elicit client-owned language. Evocation remains limited because the conversation often shifts toward therapist-generated options rather than exploring Marcus's deeper values, motivations, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marcus's preferences and avoids an authoritarian tone, showing a generally collaborative stance. Partnership weakens when the psychiatrist supplies structured scripts and choices, making Marcus more of a selector than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some useful questions that elicit brief statements about Marcus's limits and safety behaviors. These statements are not consistently affirmed, amplified, or developed into stronger change talk about alcohol use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk such as the drinking being normal and harmless. However, the session does not consistently use MI-consistent double-sided reflections or reframing to gently tilt the conversation toward change.
