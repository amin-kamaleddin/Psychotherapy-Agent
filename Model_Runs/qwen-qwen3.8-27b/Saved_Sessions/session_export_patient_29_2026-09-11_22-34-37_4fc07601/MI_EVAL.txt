# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and encouraging, but the warmth was often brisk and formulaic. The clinician moved quickly into information-giving rather than fully sitting with the patient’s struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was some surface-level understanding, but the psychiatrist often responded to emotional statements with practical advice. Important emotional cues such as stress, anxiety about the prescription, and self-doubt were not sufficiently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking cessation and relevant high-risk situations, such as the upcoming dinner. The direction was effective, though it was achieved more through directive planning than collaborative MI-consistent goal-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tailored the plan to the patient’s specific details and concerns, showing partial collaboration. However, the interaction remained more counselor-driven than truly equal or shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy was supported by offering medication options, but the psychiatrist frequently used directive and prescriptive language. Statements such as setting a drink limit or telling the patient to remove cigarettes may have reduced the patient’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist primarily provided medical information and behavioral strategies rather than eliciting the patient’s own motivations. There were missed opportunities to explore the patient’s values, confidence, reasons for change, and internal barriers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently assumed an expert role and led the session with structured clinical content. Although the patient was invited to contribute, the overall interaction was more instructive than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offered several clear statements of change, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk. The session shifted quickly into medical information and behavior planning rather than exploring the patient’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with the patient, but responses to sustain talk and barriers were largely practical. Limited use of reflections, normalization, or strategic softening made it harder to reduce resistance.
