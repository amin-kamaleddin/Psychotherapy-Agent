# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Marco’s boundaries without defensiveness. It fell short of 5 because the warmth remained somewhat polished and did not deeply address the emotional weight of his health scares, aging, and vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking around daily impact and pattern recognition. Deeper feelings such as embarrassment, resentment, fear of irrelevance, or grief about aging were not consistently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking-related daily consequences, especially breathlessness and coughing. However, it did not clearly connect the “engine check” process to concrete smoking reduction or cessation behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly used Marco’s preferred language and co-created the “engine check” metaphor to guide the conversation. The counselor still introduced some structure and categories, so collaboration was strong but not fully equal in agenda setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported Marco’s pace, choice, and noncommitment with low-pressure language such as “driver’s seat” and “no report required.” This strongly reinforced his ownership and reduced the risk of resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited Marco’s own values, privacy, and practical self-monitoring approach. However, it did not consistently evoke smoking-specific desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited Marco to set the focus and influenced the session with his own language and metaphor. The counselor still led the sequence and introduced some content, so power sharing was present but not fully explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited small change-oriented statements such as “maybe I think” and “I might look,” but did not frequently amplify or deepen them. There was no use of importance/confidence scaling or clear commitment language related to smoking change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided challenging Marco’s resistance and used low-pressure acceptance to prevent escalation. However, the counselor rarely used double-sided reflections or other strategies to redirect sustain talk toward change.
