# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive positive regard without being dismissive or pressured. Even when the patient expresses fear or skepticism, the response remains respectful and validating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses reflective listening effectively to capture the patient's ambivalence, fear, and self-doubt. Empathy is accurate and helpful, though it could go deeper in reflecting the stronger emotional meaning behind thoughts such as 'faking it.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on anxiety, avoidance, self-doubt, and confidence, with a clear and relevant structure. It could be stronger if the calming and thought work were more explicitly tied to a specific behavioral target or follow-through plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance by offering options, using inclusive language, and inviting the patient's input throughout. It is somewhat therapist-guided, as the psychiatrist still designs much of the intervention content and sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice by inviting preferences and reducing pressure, such as allowing the patient to shorten the exercise or stop anytime. Autonomy support is strong, though it could be more explicit about the patient's authority and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does elicit some of the patient's own thoughts, evidence, and preferences, but also provides many suggestions, examples, and a structured plan. More open-ended questions about the patient's own reasons, confidence, and desired change would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers multiple choices and allows the patient's decisions to shape the session in a genuinely collaborative way. The session is still fairly structured and expert-led, so partnership is strong but not fully equal co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and responds to some change talk, such as finishing the Tuesday run-through and wanting to do more than just breathe. The session does not consistently amplify or expand the patient's own change talk with deeper questions about values, competence, and motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating the need for safety and avoiding argumentation or pressure. It could be improved by using more advanced MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or turning reflections, to gently pivot toward change.
