# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, non-judgmental, and respectful throughout; never labels or implies disappointment. Language is tailored to Marcus's specific concerns and feels genuine rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections capture deep emotional undercurrents and accurately track subtle shifts in Marcus's affect. Shows progressive deepening without repetition.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session drifts toward the father-son dynamic at the expense of maintaining alcohol use as the central target. Would benefit from periodically redirecting to the drinking behavior itself (e.g., exploring what a different party might look like).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative tone with joint language and shared inquiry throughout. Minor weakness where the psychiatrist occasionally interprets feelings for Marcus rather than inviting him to name them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly removes pressure and expectation ('no script,' 'you're not performing for anyone'). No sense of obligation or directive push for change at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open evocative questions are used, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies interpretations rather than drawing feelings out from Marcus. Misses opportunities to evoke values-based change talk (e.g., 'What does going back to your life look like for you?').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marcus as the expert on his own life with explicit acknowledgments and open questions. Genuinely follows his lead rather than pulling toward a pre-planned agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One or two moments of subtle evocation around change, but does not consistently elicit or amplify Marcus's own change language. Misses the opportunity to reflect back and expand on 'it's exhausting' as recognition of need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating and de-escalating rather than arguing (e.g., 'that's not happening'). Could have used more double-sided reflections to gently hold both perspectives simultaneously.
