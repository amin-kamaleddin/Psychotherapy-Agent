# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids escalating resistance. However, acceptance is somewhat formulaic and lacks stronger affirmations of the client’s strengths or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects frustration and time pressure, but stays mostly at a surface level. An inaccurate reflection of “relief” limits the accuracy and depth of empathic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session remains focused on completing paperwork and managing probation rather than exploring the target behavior, risks, or values. MI direction toward behavior change is weak.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction is cooperative and procedural, but collaboration does not deeply engage the client’s values, concerns, or change goals. Client input shapes the administrative task more than the therapeutic direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, control, and brevity, offering options without pressure. However, supplying specific scripts reduces the consistency of client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited evocation of the client’s internal motivations, values, or confidence for change. The psychiatrist provides answers and scripts instead of drawing out the client’s own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and respects the client’s pace, but shifts into a helper/problem-solving role by supplying form language. This weakens the client’s role as the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist misses opportunities to expand client statements such as “I’m done with it” into client-owned change talk. The session relies more on scripts than on eliciting reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue, but also does not effectively redirect the client’s minimizing sustain talk. There is little use of double-sided reflection or gentle exploration of broader consequences.
