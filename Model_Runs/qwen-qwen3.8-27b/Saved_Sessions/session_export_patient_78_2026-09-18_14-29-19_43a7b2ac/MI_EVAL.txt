# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and non-judgment, validating Lucía's ambivalence and protective resistance without disapproval. Falls short of a 5 because the acceptance is more about validating specific statements than expressing deeper, individualized unconditional positive regard of Lucía as a whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong empathic understanding with accurate reflections of emotional weight (e.g., 'being called stupid must be exhausting'). Occasionally stays at the surface level of what Lucía says rather than reaching the underlying emotional meaning, particularly around her guilt and the daughter dynamic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a general focus on smoking cessation but lacks a clear strategic trajectory or sense of building momentum toward a specific target. The session feels exploratory without a clear destination; transitions between topics (daughter, work stress, alternatives) are not consolidated with summaries or strategic pivots.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the conversation as a joint exploration with clear collaborative framing ('I'd like to work with you on this'). Could be deeper by more explicitly acknowledging Lucía's own expertise and inviting her to co-create the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support throughout — explicitly rejects an authoritative stance, reframes resistance as wisdom, and removes pressure to commit to a direction. The response to Lucía's fear of being 'held to' something is textbook and directly addresses her history with authority figures.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks good open-ended evocative questions but does not consistently build on or amplify the change talk that emerges. Misses opportunities to reflect back the implied recognition in statements like 'it helps me function' and the buried desire for change in her protective resistance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong partnership stance with inclusive language and explicitly relinquishes power ('You don't owe me a direction'). Occasionally still positions the psychiatrist as slightly more in control of the conversation's direction rather than fully co-creating it with Lucía.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lucía offers change-relevant statements (e.g., 'I don't want to get excited and then have another person say you wanted to change'), but the psychiatrist does not consistently capture, reflect, or amplify this change talk. The evocation is present but not strategically developed or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully without arguing or dismissing, acknowledging the function of smoking while gently pivoting. Could be more active in rolling with the most persistent sustain talk — the belief that change is impossible or life is too full for any change.
