# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout—no judgment, correction, or disapproval at any point. Dante's ambivalence, fear of being soft, and self-criticism were all met with equal warmth and affirming language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, specific, and progressively deeper, capturing emotional and identity-level meaning beneath surface statements. Examples include naming the paralyzing mechanism ('doing nothing feels safer') and the paradox of self-criticism shutting down the engine.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, logical MI arc from understanding loss → exploring meaning → identifying barriers → examining identity → eliciting a plan → challenging the 'deciding in advance' pattern. Pacing is patient but purposeful with no circling or drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-person collaboration—the psychiatrist uses tentative 'I' statements, exploratory questions, and follows Dante's lead (dog, dad, 'it counts'). The final reframe is offered as a question for Dante to decide on, not a recommendation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No directives, prescriptions, or 'you should' statements anywhere. Every intervention is a question or reflection inviting Dante to find his own answer, and his capacity is explicitly affirmed ('a plan that's actually yours').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No external reasons for change are ever provided; all motivation is drawn from within Dante through questions like 'What would make something feel like it counts to you?' The session culminates in Dante articulating his own insight about not allowing himself to feel okay after a small thing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Dante is consistently positioned as the expert on his own life—the agenda, criteria, plan, and reframe all originate from him. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges this: 'You just described your own path without me having to point you there.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked through targeted questions and amplified when it emerges. The session builds progressively toward Dante generating his own concrete plan and articulating his own cognitive reframe about checking in after rather than deciding in advance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged, reflected, and gently examined rather than argued against—e.g., reframing 'easier to not do anything' as a protective function and asking 'whose voice is that?' to make the internal standard visible and examinable.
