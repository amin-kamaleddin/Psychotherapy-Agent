# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and generally validating of Mateo’s pain, fatigue, and uncertainty. Warmth is somewhat procedural and lacks deeper, individualized unconditional regard, especially around self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects ambivalence, including the tension between wanting progress and needing pain as proof. Reflections are sometimes repetitive and more cognitive than emotional, with less attention to fear, self-criticism, and feeling hard on himself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on rehab progress and physical activity despite pain, narrowing the discussion from broad concern to pace, movement, and tracking. Direction is strong but would benefit from a clearer collaborative summary and concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, reflects Mateo’s language, and builds on his ideas about short walks, standing, and tracking stiffness. The interaction is partly counselor-led and could be strengthened with more shared agenda-setting and co-created next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo is consistently positioned as the expert on his body, goals, and limits, with non-directive questions supporting his choice. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming his freedom to define progress, including rest.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions but sometimes offers interpretations or options before fully exploring Mateo’s own motivation. More evocative use of values, importance, confidence, and future hopes would deepen the process.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys partnership by treating Mateo as the primary authority on his body and choices. The approach is somewhat structured by the counselor, so shared agenda-setting and mutual collaboration could be more evident.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo expresses change talk about short walks, standing, noticing less stiffness, and writing down activity. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen this change talk, leaving important motivational statements underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about pain, fatigue, and needing pain to count is handled without argument or dismissal. The conversation could do more to pivot Mateo’s language toward change rather than remaining centered on pain and doubt.
