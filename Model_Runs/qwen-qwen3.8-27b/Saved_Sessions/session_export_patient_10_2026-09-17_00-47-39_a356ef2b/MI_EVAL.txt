# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment throughout. The client's feelings are normalized, including shame, parenting demands, and the difficulty of seeking help.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures the client's ambivalence and burden. It could go slightly deeper in exploring the pain, exhaustion, or fear behind the client's internal "spinning."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on improving employment-related action while addressing childcare barriers. The conversation moves steadily from general tension to concrete, relevant next steps without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as a partner rather than an expert directing the plan. The client's ideas, language, and preferences consistently shape the action plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's choice and control are consistently respected through open questions and low-pressure language. The therapist avoids directives and supports the client's sense of doing things "on her terms."

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's own reasons for change, confidence, and motivation. Questions elicit the client's vision of relief, self-efficacy, and concrete first steps rather than supplying external reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert on her own life, routines, and needs. The therapist follows the client's lead and invites her to make decisions about language, timing, and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently strengthens the client's own statements of desire, ability, and reasons for change. Affirmations and questions help the client notice her agency, competence, and readiness to take small steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by reflecting the conflict and preserving the client's perspective without arguing. Some barriers, such as concerns about leaving her children with a stranger or rigid office hours, are acknowledged but not deeply reframed.
