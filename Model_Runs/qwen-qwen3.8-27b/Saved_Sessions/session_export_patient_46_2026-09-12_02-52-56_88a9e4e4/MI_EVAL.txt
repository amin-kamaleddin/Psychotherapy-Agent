# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental validating language throughout ('appreciate your honesty,' 'that makes sense,' 'totally reasonable'). Acceptance is somewhat task-focused and occasionally framed around problem-solving rather than deeper individualized unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides good reflective listening and accurately captures ambivalence ('sounds like drinking helps you fit in,' 'you sound torn'). Empathy stays largely at surface-level social convenience without exploring deeper emotions like fear of rejection, belonging, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in social settings without wandering into unrelated topics. Repeatedly returns to the specific behavior and guides toward concrete, testable steps (next hangout strategy, confidence scaling).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Builds the plan around Ethan's preferences and his ideas clearly shape the outcome (soda in glass, skipping rounds, texting a friend). Multiple structured option lists make the interaction feel somewhat counselor-driven rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids pressure, asks Ethan to choose among options, and respects his right to decide ('it's okay to forget sometimes'). Could be stronger by more explicitly affirming Ethan's ownership of the decision-making process and his right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Ethan's own ideas, particularly practical coping strategies and confidence statements. Often introduces therapist-generated options before fully eliciting Ethan's personal values and deeper motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks for direction, offers choices, and asks permission ('follow your lead,' 'which feels most useful?'). Still generates many options and leads much of the planning rather than fully co-constructing from Ethan's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits multiple examples of change talk including practical commitments and confidence statements. Does not consistently amplify by reflecting back the importance, desire, or ability embedded in Ethan's own statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue or reinforce sustain talk and uses some useful double-sided reflections. Sustain talk remains a major organizing theme throughout, with responses focused on practical workarounds rather than strategic pivots toward discrepancy, health, or long-term consequences.
