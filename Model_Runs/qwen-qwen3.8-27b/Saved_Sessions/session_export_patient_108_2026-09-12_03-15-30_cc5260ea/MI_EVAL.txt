# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client's emotional and physiological reactions. Acceptance is strong, though it is sometimes tied to performance rather than consistently expressing unconditional regard for ambivalence and guilt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are effective reflections of the client's conflict between caring for the friend and needing personal space. However, the responses often remain behavioral and do not consistently explore the deeper emotional meaning of guilt, loyalty, identity, and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused on the target behavior of asserting a firm move-out date and making concrete steps toward it. Direction remains clear throughout, moving from defining the outcome to rehearsal and planning next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses role-play, invites input, and tailors the rehearsal to the client's concerns, creating a teamwork approach. Collaboration is strong, though the clinician still introduces much of the content, such as scripts and structured plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices, checks preferences, and avoids pressuring the client to accept the suggested scripts or plan. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming that the decision belongs to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions and elicits useful statements of need and commitment. However, the session relies more on clinician-provided scripts and plans than on drawing out the client's deeper personal reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and avoids a purely expert-driven stance, allowing the client's choices to shape the session. Partnership is good, but the clinician still supplies much of the content and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some change talk, such as commitment to the date and desire to stop overthinking. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify and elaborate this change talk by connecting it to the client's values, ability, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing with it and redirects toward boundary maintenance and practical action. More nuanced, double-sided reflections could further acknowledge both the client's care for the friend and need for space.
