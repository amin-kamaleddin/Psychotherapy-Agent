# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but acceptance feels somewhat generic. It is undercut by frequent advice-giving and prescriptive language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful empathic reflections, but they remain fairly surface-level. The counselor misses opportunities to deeply reflect Lukas’s fears about identity loss and belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts toward money management and social spending rather than maintaining a clear focus on substance use. A specific target behavior is not clearly identified or sustained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates at a basic level and uses some of Lukas’s ideas. However, the counselor supplies most of the structure, making the process more expert-led than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive phrases are used, but directive language frequently reduces Lukas’s sense of choice. The counselor does not consistently emphasize that Lukas owns the decisions and pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies heavily on advice and problem-solving rather than evoking Lukas’s own motivations. Key material about values, identity, and meaning is not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input at times but largely controls the agenda and methods. The interaction feels more counselor-directed than a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lukas generates some practical change talk, and the psychiatrist occasionally reinforces it. However, the counselor does not skillfully deepen or connect this change talk to Lukas’s values or goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids direct argument but does not effectively soften sustain talk. Responses often trigger stronger sustain talk or remain focused on problem-solving rather than ambivalence.
