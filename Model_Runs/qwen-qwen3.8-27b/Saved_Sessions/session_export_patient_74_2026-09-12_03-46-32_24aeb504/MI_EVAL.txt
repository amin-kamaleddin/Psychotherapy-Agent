# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful language and validates Linda's ambivalence, but acceptance is sometimes inconsistent and blended with advice. Unconditional positive regard is not consistently explicit when she resists change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of ambivalence, but empathy is often brief. It misses deeper emotions such as anxiety, embarrassment, tiredness, or fear of looking weird.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays fairly focused on smoking around her daughter and a specific target behavior at car drop-off. Direction is clear but could be strengthened by linking the plan more explicitly to Linda's values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction includes collaborative elements, such as asking which option feels doable and co-creating a plan. However, the psychiatrist often leads with prepackaged choices, limiting the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms choice and avoids forcing quitting, but repeated suggestions, scales, and planning questions can subtly pressure Linda. Autonomy is supported, yet not consistently emphasized in ways that make her feel fully in control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some motivation through questions about importance, confidence, and willingness. However, evocation is limited because many responses are counselor-generated rather than emerging from Linda's own values and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are partnership statements, such as telling Linda she is in charge of what to focus on. The psychiatrist frequently supplies options and a structured plan, which can feel expert-driven and does not consistently highlight Linda's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions and Linda offers some change talk, such as trying a week and a confidence shift. Change talk is not consistently deepened, and much of the session focuses on problem-solving rather than intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without direct confrontation, but the psychiatrist repeatedly returns to the reasons Linda wants to keep smoking and offers substitutes. This can keep attention on what she may have to give up rather than consistently softening sustain talk.
