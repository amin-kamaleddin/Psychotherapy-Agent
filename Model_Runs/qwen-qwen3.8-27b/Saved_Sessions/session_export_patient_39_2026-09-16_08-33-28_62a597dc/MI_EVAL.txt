# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while validating Elias’s limitations and ambivalence. The warmth is real but can feel somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects frustration, fear, and concern about being a burden, helping Elias feel understood. Empathy is strong but occasionally remains at the surface, missing deeper shame or emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and moves from vague concern to a concrete, realistic plan. Direction is strong but becomes narrow around medical clearance, safety warnings, and documentation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor collaborates well by offering choices and incorporating Elias’s preferences into a one-page plan. The interaction is not authoritarian, though the clinician still supplies much of the content and framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor regularly supports choice, offers options, and respects Elias’s right to decline or stop. Autonomy support is strong but can become directive through structured scripts, safety rules, and supplied options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor makes some effort to elicit motivation by asking what matters and inviting Elias to identify feasible steps. However, the session shifts too quickly toward external rationale and structured guidance instead of deepening Elias’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats Elias as an active contributor, and his input directly shapes the final plan. Partnership is effective in practice, but the counselor still holds considerable agenda control and provides many of the specifics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias produces meaningful change talk about not feeling stuck or useless and about doing a small feasible step. The counselor does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or deepen that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with Elias’s concerns and keeps the plan small and cautious. However, the conversation remains heavily anchored in barriers and safety, without fully reframing sustain talk toward values, agency, or possible wins.
