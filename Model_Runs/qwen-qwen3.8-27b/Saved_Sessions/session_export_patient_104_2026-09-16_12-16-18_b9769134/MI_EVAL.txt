# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, patience, and nonjudgmental support, validating Nadine’s fear and exhaustion. It is slightly formulaic and could more deeply normalize her parental fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections track exhaustion, worry, and fear, but remain surface-level and repetitive. Deeper empathy would capture parental guilt, internal conflict, and the exhausting checking loop.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on exhaustion, checking, and a manageable safety/rest plan. It occasionally shifts into psychoeducation and text drafting, which reduces MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts the plan to Nadine’s needs, including breathing, tracking, home visitor contact, and shorter messages. The balance is collaborative, though the psychiatrist remains the primary source of information.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices and invites Nadine’s judgment, respecting her limits. More directive instructions for rest and emergency guidance reduce full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some initial questions about concerns and helpful options. Much of the session is driven by information and practical solutions rather than evoking Nadine’s deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nadine’s choices influence the session, and the psychiatrist offers options. The expert role and detailed instructions still limit full collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked through choices and affirming Nadine’s plan. The session relies more on practical problem-solving than expanding her own reasons, confidence, and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and redirected toward practical safety supports, but not strongly MI-based. More double-sided reflections or pivoting fear into change talk would strengthen this criterion.
