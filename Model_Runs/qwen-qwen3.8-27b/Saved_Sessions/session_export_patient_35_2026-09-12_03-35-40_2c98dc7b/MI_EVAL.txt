# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, warmth, and unconditional positive regard despite defensiveness and minimization. There was no shaming, labeling, or judgment, and pressure was explicitly repaired when perceived.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used reflective listening and perspective-taking to capture the client's ambivalence and stress. Some deeper emotional themes, such as shame, helplessness, and fear of losing a coping tool, were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remained relevant to smoking, secondhand smoke, and the child's ear infections, but the focus drifted from quitting/reducing smoking to exposure management and medical care. A clearer agenda and explicit connection to a target behavior would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative moments, especially when the psychiatrist followed the client's concern about her son's ears. However, the interaction was often clinician-led, with many options and advice supplied rather than co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through repeated choices, noncoercive language, and respect for the client's pacing. It was slightly limited by continued suggestions after reluctance, though the psychiatrist generally avoided pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk by asking about priorities, first steps, and past coping, but did not consistently deepen or reflect the client's own reasons for change. More scaling questions and deeper exploration of values, fears, and self-efficacy would improve evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client's priorities and honored her request to focus on her son's ear infections. Partnership was limited by a clinician-led style, with many concrete options offered rather than building on the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expressed some change-oriented statements, such as thinking about quitting and possibly trying a small step. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, reflect, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk respectfully and avoided arguing with the client. However, repeated focus on secondhand-smoke reduction strategies kept the conversation partly anchored in the status quo rather than consistently moving toward change.
