# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Naomi’s anxiety without correction or disapproval. Warmth was generally strong, though the language became somewhat formulaic and could have included more explicit affirmation of Naomi’s strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Naomi’s physical symptoms and worries about being judged. Empathy could deepen further by reflecting the shame, embarrassment, and professional identity concerns underneath her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on a specific target behavior: managing social anxiety during photography shoots. Each question supported Naomi’s goal of staying present, avoiding freezing, and completing the shot.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation was co-constructed from Naomi’s own ideas, symptoms, and practical suggestions. The psychiatrist functioned as a partner rather than an authority prescribing a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invited Naomi’s perspective and avoided pressuring her to adopt any strategy. Autonomy support would be stronger with explicit statements affirming Naomi’s freedom to choose whether and what to try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked Naomi’s own ideas about practical in-the-moment strategies. Evocation could be broadened to elicit deeper change talk around values, identity, confidence, and meaningful goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance was evident through repeated open questions and minimal direct advice. Some therapist framing language subtly guided the conversation, slightly limiting the sense of full client-led partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Naomi produced useful change talk about practical ways to stay grounded, pause, and make the behavior look normal. The psychiatrist could deepen this by summarizing, amplifying, and exploring confidence, commitment, or identity-related change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Naomi’s doubts without arguing or dismissing them. More deliberate double-sided reflections could help gently pivot sustain talk toward Naomi’s agency and resources.
