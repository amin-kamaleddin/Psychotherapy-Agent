# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes the client’s concerns, and avoids judgment even when he doubts medication or alcohol change. Affirmations about his dignity, self-reliance, and strength maintain a warm, nonjudgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate and nuanced reflections, including double-sided reflections that capture both skepticism and openness to change. The responses track the client’s feelings, self-concept, and practical concerns rather than simply summarizing content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the referral targets of alcohol use and late-night eating, with clear movement toward concrete, testable steps. Guidance is practical and grounded in the client’s shift work, morning functioning, and real-life constraints.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client’s perspective and builds a plan from the client’s own ideas, such as water first, waiting until after dinner, and using morning functioning as a marker. The session feels like a shared process rather than a counselor-directed intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes the client’s choice, avoids pressure, and frames the plan as optional and client-led. The client is treated as the decision-maker who can adjust or stop the experiment if it does not work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out the client’s own goals, preferences, and practical change statements. However, the evocation is stronger at the behavioral level than in eliciting deeper values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on his own life and capacity for change, inviting him to define what is manageable and meaningful. Questions about the physician relationship further reinforce power sharing and collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps the client articulate specific change talk around testing two alcohol-free nights, delaying the beer, and using morning function as a marker. The limitation is that the change talk is mostly practical rather than deeply value-based, and some counselor affirmations may supply meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk, using reflections that soften shame and reframe continued experimentation as strength. The final doubt about whether the plan is “too little” is not fully addressed, which limits the score.
