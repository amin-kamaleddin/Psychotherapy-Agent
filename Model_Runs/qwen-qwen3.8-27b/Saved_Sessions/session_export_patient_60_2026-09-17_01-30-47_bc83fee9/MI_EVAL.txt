# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session. Acceptance is strong, though it occasionally remains tied to Jason's preferred framing rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses solid reflective listening to capture Jason's frustration, fairness concerns, and desire to feel normal. The empathy could be deeper by exploring more subtle emotions such as stigma, shame, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on the conduct issue and Jason's alcohol-related behavior. It would be stronger if the target behavior and longer-term goals were more consistently and explicitly framed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Jason as an expert on his own life and consistently builds on his ideas. The conversation feels genuinely collaborative, with Jason's input shaping the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jason is supported in identifying his own plans and choices through open questions and nonjudgmental responses. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming his right to choose, including the possibility of choosing differently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Jason's own motivations, limits, and reasons for change. Deeper evocation would connect these behaviors more explicitly to Jason's values, identity, and future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Jason's perspective and treats the work as a joint exploration. Jason's contributions meaningfully guide the session, demonstrating a strong partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jason articulates several change-oriented statements about his limits, future choices, and commitments to safer drinking. The psychiatrist could further develop this by using more advanced MI techniques to deepen and amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Jason's sustain talk and instead reframes his statements in a nonconfrontational way. Some parts remain focused on minimizing consequences, so more deliberate work could better link his values and long-term goals to behavior change.
