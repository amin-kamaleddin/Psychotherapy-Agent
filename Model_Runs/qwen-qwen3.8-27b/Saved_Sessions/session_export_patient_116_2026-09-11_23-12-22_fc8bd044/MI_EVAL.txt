# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows general respect and nonjudgment by validating Samir’s frustration and medication distrust. Repeated medical warnings and initial triage-style questioning slightly undercut deeper warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides some accurate reflections of the annoyance and "static in my head" experience. However, empathy remains somewhat superficial and does not deeply explore the underlying meaning of his distrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays largely on medication adherence without wandering off-topic. It feels somewhat diffuse and task-driven due to repeated menus of options and safety cautions rather than a consistently narrowed MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative language and offers choices while respecting Samir’s refusal of some interventions. However, frequent predefined options and structured interventions make it feel more counselor-directed than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly respects Samir’s right to choose, including statements like "I won't push" and "it’s okay to stick with what feels tolerable." Repeated prescriptive medication cautions partially weaken the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Uses only limited, mostly informational questions and does not deeply draw out Samir’s personal motivations, values, or reasons for change. Greater evocative questioning would have been needed to uncover latent motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some partnership by inviting input and offering choices, but the interaction remains largely clinician-led. Samir is not consistently framed as the expert on his own life or invited to drive the session more fully.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few brief moments of change talk are acknowledged, but they are not amplified, deepened, or expanded skillfully. The psychiatrist mostly responds with options, reassurance, or cautions rather than actively eliciting or strengthening change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids directly arguing with Samir’s sustain talk and accepts his decision to continue current behavior. However, double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to soften sustain talk are not used consistently or effectively.
