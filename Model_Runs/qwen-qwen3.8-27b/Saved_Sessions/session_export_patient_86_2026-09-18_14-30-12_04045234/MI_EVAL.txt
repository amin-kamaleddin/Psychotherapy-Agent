# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist consistently affirms Marco's right to be where he is without judgment, disappointment, or subtle pressure. The final statement 'You don't owe anyone an announcement' is a masterful acceptance that honors his self-concept without requiring performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and specific empathic reflections that land precisely, attending to subtle linguistic shifts and capturing complex emotional distinctions (e.g., 'The not-knowing is what's heavy, not the knowing'). The use of Marco's own metaphors demonstrates deep attunement rather than generic empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on smoking with logical progression from rapport through cost exploration to identifying an accessible framework. Slightly tighter anchoring to the target behavior at transitions would elevate this to a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative practice where Marco's ideas genuinely shape the session direction, metaphors, and pace. When Marco asked what the psychiatrist was exploring, the response 'I'm not thinking of a plan' returned authority to him rather than seizing the opportunity to lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with no unsolicited advice, predictions, or implicit obligations. Every statement reinforces Marco's freedom to choose—including the freedom to choose not to change, to change slowly, or to change on his own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change-relevant content is generated by Marco himself; the psychiatrist never provides information, statistics, or reasons for change. The progression from 'I can't see myself changing now' to 'I'd probably do it' is entirely Marco's own evolution, drawn out through evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently rejects the expert/director role and hands the agenda to Marco, using his own tradesman language rather than clinical framing. Power is explicitly shared throughout: 'You don't have to do anything with this,' 'Or maybe that's a question for another time.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions effectively draw out Marco's own change talk organically, from 'I'm always open to listen' to 'I'd probably do it.' Could be stronger by more explicitly affirming and amplifying Marco's strongest change talk moments (e.g., 'I'd probably do it') and exploring confidence or ability statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged, validated, and set alongside emerging change talk without confrontation or minimization. The statement 'I'm not going to try to turn your maybe into a yes' explicitly refuses to challenge resistance, allowing the cracked tool metaphor to integrate both positions.
