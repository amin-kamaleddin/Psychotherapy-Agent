# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental, validating language and avoids labeling Maria as resistant. The stance is respectful and supportive, though somewhat procedural rather than deeply humanizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers accurate, complex reflections of Maria's ambivalence, values, and concerns. However, the empathy focuses more on content and standards than on the deeper emotional texture of her experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on asthma management and inhaler use without drifting into unrelated topics. The session could benefit from more movement toward a concrete next step or actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Maria to define key terms and co-construct standards for trustworthy evidence and tracking. The collaborative stance is strong, though the clinician still maintains much of the agenda and pacing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressuring Maria and uses open questions that preserve her freedom to choose. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit affirmation that she is fully in charge of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently elicits Maria's own values, standards, and conditions for acceptable change. The evocation is effective but somewhat narrow, focusing mainly on making the inhaler acceptable rather than broadly exploring her motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maria is treated as the expert on her own life, concerns, and standards. A stronger partnership stance would include more explicit agenda negotiation and invitation for her to direct the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked around breathing more freely and being present for her child. The psychiatrist does not consistently capture, amplify, or summarize Maria's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and validates Maria's safety concerns, with some useful reframing. However, much of the session remains focused on sustain talk and does not consistently pivot toward change.
