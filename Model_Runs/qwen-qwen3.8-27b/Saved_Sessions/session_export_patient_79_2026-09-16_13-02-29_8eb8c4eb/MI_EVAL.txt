# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite Luis’s sarcasm and boundary-setting. Acceptance was strong but somewhat formulaic, with repeated phrases that felt scripted rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used reflective listening to capture Luis’s conflict, preference for control, and desire for low-effort solutions. Empathy was mostly cognitive and logistical, with less attention to deeper feelings such as irritation, fear of burden, or the protective function of sarcasm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma management and moved toward a concrete plan involving the inhaler by the coffee cup. Direction was clear but somewhat repetitive, with similar options and safety reminders repeated multiple times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited Luis’s input and adapted to his preferences, such as rejecting alarms and check-ins. Collaboration was evident but remained somewhat provider-guided, with the clinician generating most of the options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a major strength, with consistent emphasis on Luis’s choice, control, and boundaries. The clinician respected his minimal plan, avoided pressure, and framed follow-up and safety information as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician used some evocative questions to help Luis articulate a plan and confidence rating. Evocation remained superficial and logistical rather than exploring deeper values, motivations, or personal meaning behind change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a collaborative stance and allowed Luis to choose among options. Partnership was somewhat procedural, as the clinician still controlled much of the direction and repeatedly re-presents similar ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some change talk through questions about what would feel different and what small change Luis might try. However, the clinician did not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen Luis’s change-relevant statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing with Luis’s resistance and accepted many of his concerns without judgment. However, sustain talk was not consistently softened using more strategic MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or reframing.
