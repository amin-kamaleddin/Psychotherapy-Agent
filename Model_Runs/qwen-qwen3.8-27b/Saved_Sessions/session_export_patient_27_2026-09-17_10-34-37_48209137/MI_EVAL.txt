### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and normalization of Daniela’s ambivalence and care for friendships. Acceptance is strong, though some affirmations focus on her competence or strategy rather than her worth independent of disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Daniela’s core conflict, fear of being seen as fragile, and worry about losing her “loud, funny” identity. Empathy is often summarizing rather than deeply emotional, leaving some identity-level fear underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on disclosing diabetes in a manageable way and narrows the goal from full disclosure to one friend and then a text. Direction could be stronger by clarifying exact timing, wording, and a more concrete if-then plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently builds on Daniela’s ideas, such as casually mentioning it and texting one friend, without replacing her plan. The interaction feels genuinely collaborative, with Daniela as the primary problem-solver.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports Daniela’s choice and control, emphasizing “smallest way,” “driver’s seat,” and whether the plan feels manageable. Daniela’s “Thanks for not pushing me” indicates autonomy was well supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits motivation from Daniela through open questions and reflections rather than giving advice or external reasons to disclose. Daniela generates her own change talk, including “I’ll try,” “I just stopped lying,” and “I’m not broken.”

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Daniela’s lead, invites her input, and builds on her contributions, creating a collaborative process. Partnership could be stronger by more explicitly naming Daniela as the expert on her friendships and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk related to desire, reason, and commitment, helping Daniela move toward a small plan. Change talk could be deepened by exploring her values, authenticity, ability, and future benefits of disclosure.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging the protective function of secrecy and using balanced reflections without arguing. The session could more consistently pivot Daniela’s fear back to her values, agency, and evidence of resilience.
