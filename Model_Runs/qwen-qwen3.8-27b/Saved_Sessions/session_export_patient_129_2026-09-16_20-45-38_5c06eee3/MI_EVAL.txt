# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids labeling or punishing Tyler’s resistance. The main limit is that the warmth is somewhat procedural and could more explicitly normalize Tyler’s fear or identity threat.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and accurately captures Tyler’s key themes of autonomy, freedom with peers, and fear of surveillance. Empathy could deepen by responding more directly to the emotional weight underneath Tyler’s resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on substance use, peer influence, parental conflict, and the autonomy-risk tension. Momentum is weakened by repeated looping around confidentiality and safety before moving decisively toward change discussion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Tyler’s perspective, follows his request to discuss friends first, and adapts to his stated boundaries. Collaboration could be stronger by reducing consecutive therapist questions and giving Tyler more control over the next session focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice and avoids directive or coercive language. It would be even stronger if he more explicitly affirmed Tyler’s right to decide whether, how, or when to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, evocative questions that help Tyler connect his behavior to values such as autonomy and being himself. The main gap is not consistently drawing out or developing Tyler’s own deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and treats Tyler as the expert on his own life. Partnership could improve with more visible power-sharing, such as co-creating the agenda or inviting Tyler to choose what to explore next.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler offers some change-relevant statements, such as going to friends’ houses less or hanging out at the park. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or expand this change talk using confidence, values, or DARN-C-style evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Tyler’s sustain talk and does not reinforce resistance. However, the session does not actively use double-sided reflections, decisional balance, or strategic reframing to help Tyler resolve ambivalence.
