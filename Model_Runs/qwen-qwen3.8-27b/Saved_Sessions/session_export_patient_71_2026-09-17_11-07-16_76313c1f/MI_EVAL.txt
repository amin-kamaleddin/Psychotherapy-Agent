# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming tone throughout, validating Laura's self-awareness and precision without judgment. Occasionally reads as slightly formulaic and lacks a clear closing affirmation to fully round out the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate captures of emotional weight and perspective-taking. Some reflections remain surface-level and the repeated use of 'Can you tell me more?' suggests occasional default to safe prompts over deeper empathic responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, coherent arc from values exploration through specific behavioral patterns to future-oriented planning without drifting. The session ends slightly open-ended without a clear consolidation or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently uses Laura's own language and exploratory questioning, with genuine co-construction moments like the 'polite avoidance' framing. Occasionally asymmetric with interpretive reflections that could have been offered as questions for greater symmetry.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-directive, never prescribes actions, and returns authority to Laura (e.g., 'who gets to say ten minutes is enough?'). Could more explicitly affirm Laura's power to choose her own path regardless of the session's outcomes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws out Laura's own motivations through strong evocative questions that access internal reasons for change. Could push deeper on individual insights (e.g., the friend analogy, 'boring' goal) before moving on to the next topic.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive language and consistently positions Laura as the expert on her own experience. Could be strengthened by offering summaries as questions rather than statements and explicitly negotiating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura offers genuine change talk (less reactive, ten minutes on the mat, wanting it to be boring) but the psychiatrist doesn't consistently reinforce or amplify these statements. Tends to move forward rather than staying with and deepening the change language that emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating fears without dismissing and reframing patterns positively. Could employ double-sided reflections more systematically to further soften the 'waiting for the other shoe to drop' narrative.
