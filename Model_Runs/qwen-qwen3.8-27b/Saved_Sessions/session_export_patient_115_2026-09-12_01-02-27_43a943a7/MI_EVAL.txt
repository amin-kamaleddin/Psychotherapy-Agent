# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and avoids blame, but praise is often plan-focused rather than person-focused. The repeated lists create a somewhat instructive tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate perspective-taking statements about the taxi job and asthma burden, but reflections remain brief and miss deeper feelings. Empathy is practical rather than deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management, smoking reduction, and safety, but feels checklist-driven. It lacks a clear MI progression from ambivalence to goals, discrepancy, and commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites choices and eventually co-constructs a small plan, but frequently introduces multiple options before eliciting the patient's own ideas. Collaboration is partial and uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the patient's limits and does not pressure him to quit entirely, offering open check-in options. However, repeated advice and reminders can feel directive rather than fully client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies mainly on practical options and logistical questions rather than eliciting values, fears, hopes, and reasons for change. There is little use of importance/confidence rulers or evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership is shown when the patient chooses among options, but much of the session is expert-driven with repeated lists of recommendations. The overall balance remains tilted toward clinician-led advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or evocate it. Questions are mostly about feasibility rather than importance, desire, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient repeatedly defends current habits and reduced smoking, and the psychiatrist often accepts these in a neutral way. It misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections and explore discrepancy between smoking and better breathing.
