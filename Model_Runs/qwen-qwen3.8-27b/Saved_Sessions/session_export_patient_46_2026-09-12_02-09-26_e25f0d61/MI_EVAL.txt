# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with validating language. However, the boundary statement about underage drinking and problem-solving tone slightly reduce acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong complex reflections of ambivalence and the social meaning of drinking. Some responses miss the deeper emotional pain around shame and fear of exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and its social consequences with little topic drift. The direction becomes somewhat overly focused on behavior management rather than internal motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts to the client's chosen options and avoids a fully lecturing approach. However, menus and scripted solutions create a guided, problem-solving dynamic rather than equal collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks what feels doable, supporting some client control. Prescriptive scripts and plans weaken explicit ownership and collaborative freedom.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions encourage the client to describe experiences and consider change. The session does not fully draw out deeper values or internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and responds to the client's choices. Frequent counselor-generated options and planning make the process feel more directed than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk about hangovers and wanting to fit in. Change talk is often not expanded or deepened before moving into advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated but not consistently redirected toward change. Some framing treats drinking as a given to manage rather than exploring discrepancy.
