# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided shaming or arguing with Ahmed’s minimization. Acceptance was strong, though warmth was more tied to practical planning than deeper affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The provider used good reflective listening, especially around Ahmed’s ambivalence between protecting his throat and preserving his evening routine. Empathy was strong but mostly stayed at a cognitive level rather than exploring deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remained focused on the presenting throat and gum concerns and did not wander off topic. Direction was less consistent toward the broader MI target of reducing mini-cigar use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited Ahmed’s input and made the monitoring plan feel jointly constructed. Collaboration was practical, but remained more procedural than equal partnership on the core smoking issue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported Ahmed’s right to decide and avoided pressure to quit or change his routine. Autonomy support was slightly reduced by some language that kept the provider more in charge of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The provider used helpful open-ended questions, but most of the evocation was aimed at symptom management and check-in details. There was less evocation of Ahmed’s own motivation, values, or confidence about reducing mini-cigar use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently treated Ahmed as the expert on his own body and habits, and his preferences shaped the session. Partnership was strong, though the clinician still kept the main clinical agenda centered on the exam findings.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was a clear opportunity when Ahmed expressed openness to cutting back, but the provider did not build on or strengthen that change talk. The session shifted away from evoking motivation toward symptom monitoring.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The provider handled resistive statements well without arguing, lecturing, or moralizing. Sustain talk was softened effectively, though it was not consistently used as a bridge toward discrepancy or change talk.
