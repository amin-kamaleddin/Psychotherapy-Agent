# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming tone while validating the client’s ambivalence and social concerns. Acceptance could be strengthened with more explicit, individualized unconditional positive regard beyond the client’s roles and accomplishments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and capture the client’s ambivalence, identity concerns, and desire to stay clear without seeming “too precious.” Some reflections feel repetitive and do not fully deepen feelings such as isolation, uncertainty, or craving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains largely focused on alcohol use in social settings and practical ways to stay connected without drinking. Direction could be improved by more explicitly naming the target behavior and using brief summaries to prevent drift into broader identity topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and allows the client’s ideas to shape the conversation and next steps. Collaboration could be more egalitarian by offering the client more explicit choices about focus, pace, and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported by avoiding pressure, not imposing a plan, and using language such as “I’m here to work alongside you, not to push you.” Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client’s right to make his own choices, including choosing no change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own values, motivations, and alcohol-free enjoyable activities such as music and dancing. Evocation could be deepened by more consistently amplifying change talk and consolidating motivations with questions or scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on his own life and invites his perspective rather than giving expert-driven advice. Partnership could be stronger by more explicitly inviting the client to set the agenda and control the session’s focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits change talk about protecting his future, staying sharp, and trying a small step such as showing up without bringing drinks. Change talk could be amplified more systematically with importance, confidence, and future-oriented questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully by validating concerns about fitting in and seeming performed without arguing or dismissing them. Softening could be more advanced through double-sided reflections and exploring exceptions when the client felt good without alcohol.
