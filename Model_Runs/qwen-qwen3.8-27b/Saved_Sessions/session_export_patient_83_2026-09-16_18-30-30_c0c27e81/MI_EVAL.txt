# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Mai’s concerns. However, acceptance feels somewhat procedural and could more explicitly affirm her worth and agency.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Mai’s perspective and practical concerns about the medication. Deeper emotional attunement to her frustration or fear would strengthen the empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of understanding and accepting the statin. It would be stronger if it moved toward a concrete commitment or action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction invites Mai’s input and respects her need for a clear explanation. More visible co-creation, such as letting Mai set the agenda, would improve collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the decision as Mai’s and avoids pressuring her. More explicit affirmation of her full decision-making ownership would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions, but they mainly focus on information needs rather than values, confidence, or commitment. More deliberate eliciting of Mai’s internal motivation would be needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Mai’s lead and treats her as the expert on her own experience. A slightly more equal power-sharing stance would make the partnership more visible.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai offers meaningful conditional change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or expand it. More targeted reflections and evocative questions would help develop commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Mai’s concerns and normalizes her uncertainty. More active use of double-sided reflections to gently reconnect her concerns with her values would improve this area.
