# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoids arguing or labeling David. The main limitation is that the warmth is somewhat structured and task-focused rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening and perspective-taking are strong, including acknowledging the impact of the 'what if' question. A few pacing misses occur, especially when the therapist asks for a percentage threshold after David says he is not playing with numbers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target issue of cholesterol management, medication use, and small lifestyle changes. The focus narrows somewhat heavily around side-effect odds rather than consistently linking back to David's broader health goals or a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and adapts well when David rejects the idea of a plan. Collaboration is not fully equal in all moments because the therapist still guides the conversation toward medication risk information and occasional consecutive questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David's autonomy is generally respected, with emphasis on information, control, and no forced commitment. The final question asking for a percentage threshold feels slightly pushy given his statement that he is not playing with numbers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist frequently asks open questions that draw out David's own concerns, preferences, and possible steps. Evocation remains somewhat narrow and conditional, with less exploration of confidence, values, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David is positioned as the expert on his own priorities, and his concerns shape much of the session. At times the therapist leads the agenda toward medication risk information more than David leads the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes willingness, such as starting small, considering diet changes, and maybe trying the pill if risk is low. The change talk is not deepened through scaling, confidence exploration, or broader values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed without rebuttal or moralizing, which helps keep the conversation collaborative. The session still spends considerable time on medication odds rather than more consistently using double-sided reflections tied to David's goals.
