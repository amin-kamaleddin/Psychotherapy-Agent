# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Tyler's feelings and autonomy. The warmth was sometimes procedural, and the final safety check felt slightly alarmist for a resistant adolescent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used effective reflective listening to capture ambivalence and frustration. Deeper exploration of shame, fear, and peer-related feelings would further strengthen empathic connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session focused on the conflict with Tyler's mother but lacked clear strategic direction toward the target behavior of substance use. A stronger collaborative summary and concrete next step would improve momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist sought Tyler's input but often led with specific scripted options, making the interaction feel uneven and expert-driven. More open-ended questions would better support collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honored Tyler's choices and avoided coercive language. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming his self-direction and ownership of his decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made limited efforts to evoke internal motivation, often shifting to practical scripts instead of exploring Tyler's own values. More evocative statements and reflections could help him articulate change reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and respected Tyler's preferences but frequently offered multiple scripted options. This made the session feel more like a multiple-choice test than a true collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was limited effort to evoke Tyler's own reasons for change, and his change talk was not effectively reflected or amplified. More focus on values, concerns, and hopes could strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing, but the psychiatrist did not actively soften it or redirect it toward change. Double-sided reflections could have helped Tyler resolve ambivalence.
