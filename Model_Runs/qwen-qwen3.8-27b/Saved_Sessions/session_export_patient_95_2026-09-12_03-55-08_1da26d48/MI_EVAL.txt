# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, though the repeated safety questions occasionally interrupt the emotional attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening of the client's pain and meaningful self-other dynamics, but some reflections were repetitive and did not fully explore deeper feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remained focused on safety and emotional pain, but it lacked a clearer collaboratively defined change goal or direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were collaborative offers and invitations to joint work, though the psychiatrist still led much of the session through repeated structured choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of choices, willingness language, and respect for the client's preference not to do an exercise, with only minor directive overtones from repeated safety checks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions were used to draw out experience, but the counselor did not consistently deepen or amplify client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, non-expert stance and offered options, but the repeated safety agenda made the session less client-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was briefly affirmed but not meaningfully expanded, deepened, or summarized, leaving several important client statements underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided challenging hopeless statements and sometimes redirected, but did not consistently soften sustain talk or pivot toward alternatives.
