# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warm, unconditional positive regard and validation throughout. Even when the client expresses self-doubt, the responses remain respectful and nonjudgmental.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and often accurately captures the client's ambivalence and values. The empathy occasionally moves too quickly toward reframing or solution-building before fully exploring the emotional pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior of protecting evenings and reducing work-related guilt. It moves coherently from values and concerns to a concrete behavioral experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively through questions, reflections, and follow-up of the client's ideas. Some interpretive statements and agenda-setting moves reduce the sense of fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is allowed to generate the action plan and is not given direct advice. Autonomy is occasionally undermined by therapist-suggested experiments or evaluative statements that supply direction before the client fully endorses it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's own motivations, values, and indicators of success. Some key reframes are supplied by the therapist rather than fully elicited from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on her life, and her ideas shape the session. However, the psychiatrist sometimes takes a leading interpretive role rather than explicitly sharing power or inviting the client to choose the next focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and reflections help the client articulate reasons for change and confidence. The therapist could further strengthen client ownership by asking the client to explain, summarize, or rate her own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worries, guilt, and critical self-talk are acknowledged, normalized, and gently redirected without argument. Sustain talk is effectively softened by connecting self-care to the client's existing values and proposed evidence-based experiment.
