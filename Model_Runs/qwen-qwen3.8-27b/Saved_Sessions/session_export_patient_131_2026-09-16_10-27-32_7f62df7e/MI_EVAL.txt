# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms Wiremu’s effort and the complexity of his situation. The warmth is somewhat formulaic and does not go deep enough into shame or fear to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Wiremu’s main conflict between protecting his relationship with his children and preserving independence. The empathy is strong but mostly cognitive rather than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on alcohol reduction, family goals, and practical planning. It could be stronger by more explicitly connecting each step to Wiremu’s values and long-term goal of being a present father.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and uses Wiremu’s choices to shape the final plan, creating a collaborative problem-solving tone. However, the clinician still drives much of the agenda and supplies the initial structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Wiremu’s independence is repeatedly respected, and he is allowed to choose days, coping strategies, reminders, and tracking. The session would reach a 5 if the clinician offered fewer exact recommendations and let the client lead more fully.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions that elicit some client motivation, especially around his children. Evocation is limited because the clinician does not consistently draw out deeper values, hopes, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation conveys a collaborative stance, with phrases inviting input and adapting the plan to Wiremu’s preferences. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still structures the session and offers a fairly complete plan before fully letting the client lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify it. The work remains more logistical than strategically evocative.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well without arguing or reinforcing resistance. The skill would be stronger if sustain talk was more consistently redirected into change talk.
