# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while normalizing the client’s concerns. However, occasional mildly directive affirmations such as “You’re doing the right thing” make the acceptance less purely client-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence about support, monitoring, and alcohol-impaired follow-through. The empathy is often surface-level and does not deeply explore underlying fears, shame, or the emotional weight of avoiding her father’s pattern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol-related risks to contraceptive reliability and moves toward a concrete next step. The direction is occasionally diluted by extensive practical options, logistics, and task completion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences clearly shape the session and the final plan reflects her input. However, the psychiatrist often leads with lists, practical solutions, and drafted messages, making the interaction more expert-driven than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices, low-pressure steps, and respects the client’s decision not to send a clinic message herself. Autonomy support is slightly limited by evaluative endorsements that subtly steer the client toward a particular course of action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance and confidence scaling elicit some client-generated motivation and increase the client’s stated confidence. However, evocation is frequently interrupted by advice, factual information, and practical problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative quality, and the client’s choices guide the session. The partnership is weakened when the psychiatrist acts as the primary source of options, logistics, and drafted messages rather than emphasizing the client’s own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses meaningful change talk, including wanting to avoid her father’s pattern and increasing confidence. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk before shifting to practical options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s barriers and offers low-pressure alternatives. Sustain talk is often met with practical fixes rather than deeper MI reflections of the tension between safety and autonomy.
