# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgment, and validation for the patient's ambivalence and need for autonomy. Some responses are somewhat formulaic or coaching-like, preventing a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides strong reflective listening and accurately captures some of the patient's emotional and practical experience. Several reflections remain content-focused and miss deeper emotional or identity-level material.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target concern and moves toward a specific, manageable change around weekend drinking. Direction could be stronger by better connecting the issue to the patient's broader values and briefly addressing the smoking concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting the patient to choose among options and allowing his preferences to shape the plan. However, the rapid succession of counselor suggestions makes the agenda visible and less fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports choice, frames options as optional, and respects the patient's stated comfort with a 6 confidence level. The score is limited by subtle pushes toward additional improvement or strategy after the patient expresses contentment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some patient-generated statements, such as knowing limits and being willing to try the water plan. Evocation is limited because the counselor frequently supplies concrete strategies rather than expanding the patient's own motivations and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and the patient's choices meaningfully influence the final plan. Partnership is not fully exemplary because the counselor repeatedly presents multiple pre-selected options, which can feel coached.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk and affirms the patient's stated willingness to try a small change. The change talk remains relatively thin and is not consistently deepened through values, identity, or ability-based reflection.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with the patient's sustain talk and redirects toward modest, low-effort options. Softening is not consistently effective because the counselor at times pushes to increase confidence after the patient says 6 is enough, and the patient's preference to avoid check-ins is not fully explored.
