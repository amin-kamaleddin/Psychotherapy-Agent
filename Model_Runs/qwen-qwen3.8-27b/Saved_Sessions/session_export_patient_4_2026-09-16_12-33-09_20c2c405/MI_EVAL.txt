# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Aizada's fear, exhaustion, and frustration. Acceptance is strong but remains somewhat procedural, with limited deeper unconditional regard for the person behind the symptoms.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflective listening and accurately summarizes Aizada's experiences, including her fear of being dismissed. Empathy stays mostly at the surface level and misses opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as shame, helplessness, grief, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Aizada's gastrointestinal symptoms and practical next steps, such as the letter, script, and scan request. However, the direction is more medically problem-solving than MI-consistent, with less exploration of her ambivalence and personal change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates in a practical way by asking for input and adjusting the letter and script based on Aizada's preferences. The interaction is collaborative in task completion, but the psychiatrist remains the primary expert guiding the medical plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported by offering choices and avoiding overt pressure, such as choosing between a letter, script, or both. However, the prescriptive nature of the plan and limited reinforcement of Aizada's decision-making role weakens stronger autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some evocative moves, such as asking what feels most important and inviting practice. Evocation is limited because the session relies more on providing medical recommendations and problem-solving than drawing out Aizada's own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes Aizada's input and adapts the plan to her concerns, showing partial collaboration. Partnership is limited because Aizada mainly edits the clinician-led plan rather than being positioned as the expert on her own goals and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aizada offers several moments that could have been developed into change talk, such as her pain limiting work and her inability to keep waiting. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify these statements to explore her values, ability, hope, or commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not soften Aizada's sustain talk and instead reinforces her focus on biomedical certainty, urgent testing, and fear of being told it is stress. Responses validate and amplify the medical-confirmation frame rather than exploring ambivalence or the possible role of stress and coping.
