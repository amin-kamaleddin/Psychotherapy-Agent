# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoided moralizing. The tone was supportive, though occasionally procedural and risk-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the client's situation accurately and acknowledged her enjoyment of wine. Deeper emotional themes, such as feeling judged or defensive, were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use, safety, medication interaction, and possible reduction. The overall direction was relevant and practical.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative moments, especially around the client's own idea of having one glass and water. However, much of the session remained clinician-led, with the psychiatrist guiding the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices, respected the client's refusal of a formal plan or GP script, and supported self-determination. Some gentle pressure remained through repeated recommendations and safety information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open-ended questions and evocation were used, but much of the session was informational and advice-based. The client's own reasons for change were not consistently elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited choice and avoided an authoritarian stance, but the session was often rapid and clinician-led. The client's perspective was not fully centered as an equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encouraged the client's plan to reduce and affirmed it as practical. Change talk emerged naturally but was not consistently amplified or explored further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk, such as 'seven drinks is not that much,' was not effectively softened or reframed. The response often returned to risk and harm information rather than motivational redirection.
