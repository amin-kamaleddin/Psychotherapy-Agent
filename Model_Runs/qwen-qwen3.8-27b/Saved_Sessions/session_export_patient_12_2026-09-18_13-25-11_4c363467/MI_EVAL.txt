### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, never labeling, judging, or minimizing Amira's experience. Particularly strong when she corrects the psychiatrist's focus — he immediately accepts her redirection without defending his previous framing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflects go beyond paraphrasing to capture the felt meaning and emotional texture of Amira's experience, such as naming the exhaustion of 'hoping and watching it get snuffed out.' Each reflection adds new understanding without being repetitive or formulaic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a logical progression from broad ambivalence to the concrete image of a single day and the park across the street, with each question building on the previous response. Slight opportunity to provide strategic summaries at key junctures to consolidate progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is framed as a collaborative exploration between equals, with the psychiatrist crediting Amira as co-creator of insights ('That's a real idea that came from you'). Session direction is shaped by her responses rather than driven by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is explicit and woven naturally throughout — never using 'should' or 'you have to' language, explicitly removing pressure ('You don't owe me a blueprint'). When Amira resists, the psychiatrist meets her where she is rather than pushing past her stated limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out Amira's own reasons and ideas rather than imposing external motivation, asking questions like 'What would working look like?' and 'What does your body say about it?' The spark, the day-to-day problem, and the park all emerge from Amira, not the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise and wisdom about change are consistently positioned as residing within Amira, with language like 'I'm curious what you think' and 'You don't owe me a blueprint.' The psychiatrist invites her insight rather than providing answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully amplifies Amira's own change-relevant statements, such as reflecting 'I really shouldn't have done it' as evidence of her position and highlighting that she named the park rather than saying she had nowhere to go. Could push further in consolidating and reinforcing this emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with appropriate care — validating the truth in statements like 'it's not gonna matter anyway' without reinforcing or getting stuck in them. Could be more strategic with double-sided reflections to pivot sustain talk toward change while honoring it.
