# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, normalizing Samir's struggles without pushing against his sustain talk. Occasionally feels structured and formulaic rather than deeply spontaneous; lacks explicit affirmation of Samir's worth as a person separate from his behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong complex reflections that capture functional meaning and emotional weight (e.g., dashboard moment, being the person his family can count on). Could go deeper in moments of vulnerability, and the final creative reflection slightly veers into interpretation rather than pure empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, logical focus on smoking and drinking in relation to asthma management with good pacing and momentum. Could benefit from more explicitly narrowing focus to a primary target and giving the session a concrete landing point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates a genuinely collaborative dynamic, treating Samir as an equal partner and naturally integrating his contributions. The psychiatrist still holds slightly more structural control over the flow and could invite Samir to direct the conversation more explicitly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support—never pressures toward a specific decision and consistently returns decision-making to Samir. Explicitly resists the pull toward advice-giving even when Samir directly asks, making this about as clean an example as one can get in MI.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws motivation from within Samir using open-ended questions and amplification of his own emerging language. Some reflections carry so much insight that they begin to interpret on Samir's behalf rather than letting him arrive at insights independently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Samir as the expert on his own life through phrasing like 'I want to understand this from your side' and questions that assume he holds the answer. Could extend partnership into a more explicit collaborative planning phase, as the session ends with Samir still asking for direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes rich change talk about identity, family protection, and driving safety through well-timed questions and strategic affirmations. Does not consistently amplify or consolidate strong change talk when it appears, missing opportunities to fully develop it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by not arguing or countering, instead using reflections and reframing to allow Samir to examine his own language. Could have more explicitly explored the meaning behind statements like 'I don't see it as a big deal anymore.'
