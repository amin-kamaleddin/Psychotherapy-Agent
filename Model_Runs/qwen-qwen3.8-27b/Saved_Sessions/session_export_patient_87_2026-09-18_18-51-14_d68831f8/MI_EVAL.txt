# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with multiple genuine affirmations of Marco's honesty and effort. Slightly formulaic in places (repeated 'that's not nothing' constructions) and one moment borders on a mildly corrective tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with skillful reframes, particularly the insight that 'it's not the slip that undoes you, it's the story you tell yourself after.' Could hold moments of vulnerability (e.g., 'I'm probably gonna mess up') a bit longer before pivoting to strategy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical momentum from opening through identification of the specific barrier to a concrete behavioral strategy. Lacks a brief collaborative summary or explicit follow-up plan to reinforce forward momentum at the close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely egalitarian power dynamic where Marco's ideas shape the entire session trajectory. The psychiatrist has no visible agenda beyond facilitating Marco's own thinking, and all key concepts (spare room, cold water, 'body moves first') emerge collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never imposes advice or uses directive language; consistently frames the conversation in terms of Marco's own choice and capability. All solutions are generated by Marco, with the psychiatrist creating conditions for his autonomy to emerge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never states reasons for change or provides external information; every key insight is evoked through well-timed questions. The final line 'my body moves first' is a powerful example of organically evoked change talk that could not have been prescribed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marco as the expert on his own experience through questions like 'What do you think would actually work for you?' The session direction is entirely shaped by Marco's contributions rather than the psychiatrist's recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A masterclass in eliciting rich, varied change talk that progresses from vague hope to specific, embodied action. The psychiatrist skillfully amplifies change talk without being sycophantic and systematically builds toward concrete behavioral commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframes self-doubt ('I don't know if that's enough') and anticipates relapse productively. The sustain talk 'I've tried willpower stuff before' is acknowledged but not deeply explored as an opportunity to differentiate this approach from past attempts.
