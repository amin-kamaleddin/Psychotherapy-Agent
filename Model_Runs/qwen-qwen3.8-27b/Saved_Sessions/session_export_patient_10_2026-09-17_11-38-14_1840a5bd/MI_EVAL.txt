# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, immediately validating Amina's effort and explicitly countering her self-criticism. No moment of judgment, labeling, or conditional regard in the entire session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides highly accurate, nuanced perspective-taking that captures the emotional complexity of Amina's double-bind (guilt + anxiety, protecting a relationship vs. her own future). Reflections go well beyond surface-level paraphrases to name the specific pain points.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, progressive focus on the target behavior (securing employment) and the specific barrier (childcare reliability), with each exchange building logically on the last. Pacing is well-calibrated—patient enough for exploration but with strategic momentum toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of MI collaboration where Amina's contributions directly shape the session's direction and each response is proportional, allowing her space to think. The tone communicates genuine partnership rather than clinician-as-expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never once suggests, pressures, or imposes a plan on Amina, explicitly removing urgency with statements like 'I'm not asking you to solve it tonight.' The entire session is structured around Amina's own words and solutions, reinforcing her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question is designed to surface what is already within Amina—her motivations, partial solutions, and self-awareness—rather than providing what the clinician thinks is needed. Textbook high-level evocation that progressively draws out concrete, self-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within Amina with explicit statements ('You know your situation better than I do') and curiosity-driven, open-ended questions. The power balance is genuinely egalitarian, with the psychiatrist positioned as a collaborator rather than an advisor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits and amplifies change talk, building on Amina's own statements (e.g., 'the gap is in the logistics, not in you') and strategically asking questions that draw out her own solutions. The session moves progressively from barrier identification to concrete self-generated action plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk by redirecting toward strengths and framing barriers as external and solvable. However, misses a couple of moments where Amina's self-deprecating language ('That's stupid, I know') could have been met with a more explicit softening reflection before moving forward.
