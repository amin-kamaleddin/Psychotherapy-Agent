# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, unconditional positive regard, and nonjudgmental acceptance. Validation of fatigue, rest, and limitations remains respectful throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, with accurate acknowledgment of frustration, shame, exhaustion, and feeling stuck. Empathy could deepen further by more consistently exploring ambivalence and the emotional complexity behind the client’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on physical activity, fatigue, barriers, and the presenting concern. The work moves toward a small, concrete behavioral target, though the connection to personal values could be clearer.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites the client’s input and adapts to his preferences and limits. Collaboration is generally strong, though occasional therapist-led options and explanations make the interaction feel less co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client’s right to choose is consistently emphasized, with no pressure, coercion, or expectation to comply. Rest is repeatedly framed as acceptable rather than failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several open-ended questions invite the client to describe his experience and barriers. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist often supplies options, explanations, and interpretations before fully eliciting the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and follows the client’s lead in several ways. The stance is sometimes undermined by frequent use of structured options and expert content, which can position the psychiatrist as the authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on it. Responses to statements of motivation are often brief or procedural rather than reinforcing the client’s own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, and the psychiatrist offers smaller, low-pressure options. The session could more consistently use MI-specific strategies such as double-sided reflections or exploring discrepancy to transform resistance.
