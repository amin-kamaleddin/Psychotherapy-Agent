# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer conveys respect and non-judgment, but warmth remains somewhat polished. Unconditional positive regard could be stronger against the client's shame and self-blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's perspective and coping complexity. Deeper empathy for shame, fear, and social loss would strengthen the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing gambling and related consequences. It progresses coherently toward a concrete, client-led plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer works with the client as an equal partner rather than giving advice. The client's own metaphors and ideas shape the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer consistently supports choice without pressuring abstinence. The client is encouraged to identify the smallest version she can try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer effectively draws out the client's motivations and plans. Deeper, values-based evocation could further strengthen the client's reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer maintains a strong non-expert stance and follows the client's leads. The session feels collaborative and client-driven rather than prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client generates concrete change talk, such as cutting cards and using a post-it anchor. More systematic evocation of DARN-C change talk would make this stronger.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or judgment. More consistent use of double-sided reflections could better convert ambivalence into small change steps.
