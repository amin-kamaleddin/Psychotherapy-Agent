# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Jordan’s ambivalence without moralizing. Warmth is strong but somewhat procedural, with less emphasis on affirming worth or courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Jordan’s conflict between peer pressure, safety, and desire to avoid trouble. Deeper emotional impacts such as shame, guilt, or fear are only lightly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on shoplifting in peer situations and moves toward a concrete first-step plan. The small exit strategy could be more explicitly linked to Jordan’s broader goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Jordan’s ideas and treats the conversation as joint problem-solving. The pace of guiding questions may make the session feel more counselor-steered than fully co-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan is consistently framed as the decision-maker, with language that respects his uncertainty and choice. Some counselor-suggested scripts and cues are helpful but mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions draw out Jordan’s own values, concerns, and possible solutions. Evocation could go deeper into intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, and future identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s input and affirms that Jordan knows his own life best. A more fully egalitarian stance would involve fewer counselor-generated behavioral options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan’s change talk is reinforced and connected to protecting his future. More systematic evocation of importance, confidence, and values-based reasons for change would strengthen this.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with acceptance and double-sided reflections rather than argument. The psychiatrist could more consistently pivot from sustain talk into change-focused questions.
