# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes effort without shaming weight, progress, or partial success. Could more explicitly affirm the client’s worth beyond the scale or productivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections of frustration, exhaustion, and fear, helping the client feel understood. Some reflections remain summary-level and could go deeper into shame, self-doubt, and fear of repeated failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on the target concern and moves logically from identifying the problem to selecting a small behavior change. Becomes somewhat task-oriented too quickly and could periodically reconnect the plan to the client’s value of feeling less self-conscious at school.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as a collaborative partner by adjusting the plan based on the client’s constraints, fatigue, and preferences. Could create more space for client-generated ideas before offering specific options and supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses permission-seeking language, choice questions, and low-barrier options to reduce pressure and support client control. Repeated confidence ratings and many suggestions could subtly create a sense of performance; explicitly reinforcing client decision-making would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strongly evokes motivation early by connecting change to confidence and asking about signs of improvement. The session later becomes more practical, with less exploration of the client’s internal values, abilities, and reasons for continuing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites client preferences and allows her choices to shape the session and final plan. The psychiatrist still leads the agenda and provides much of the structure and content, limiting fully client-led exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk through value-based questions and positive support for the client’s willingness to try. Does not consistently reflect, expand, or deepen the client’s change talk, and at times provides reasons for change rather than evoking them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and effectively reframes partial success as data rather than failure. Could use more strategic double-sided reflections to hold the client’s ambivalence, especially when she expresses doubt at the end.
