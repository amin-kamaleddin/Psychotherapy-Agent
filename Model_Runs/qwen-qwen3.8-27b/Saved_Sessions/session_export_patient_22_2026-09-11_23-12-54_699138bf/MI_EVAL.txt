# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect and nonjudgment with supportive statements about stress, ambivalence, and possible lapses. However, acceptance is somewhat procedural and could be deeper in affirming the client’s effort and worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers understanding and relevant questions, but reflections remain fairly surface-level. The core emotional push-pull between wanting less drinking and protecting her social life is not fully captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol use and narrows to a specific, realistic goal. Direction could be stronger by exploring motivation and ambivalence before moving into planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes client preferences and adapts the plan to a quiet, low-pressure goal. However, the interaction is often shaped by provider-generated options rather than emerging primarily from the client’s own thinking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are autonomy-supportive phrases and the client chooses the target night and check-in format. The psychiatrist could more explicitly emphasize that the client is in charge and that choice remains hers at every step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening question allows some client concern to emerge, but the session quickly moves into advice and planning. There is limited use of questions, reflections, or rulers to draw out the client’s own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and adjusts the plan based on the client’s responses. The early delivery of multiple concrete strategies creates a more expert-led than equal-partner stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses some change talk, especially wanting to feel less out of control, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand it. The session moves too quickly into behavioral strategies before strengthening the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or amplifying the client’s concerns about social life, boredom, and feeling weird. However, the sustaining themes are not fully softened, reframed, or integrated with the client’s change goals.
