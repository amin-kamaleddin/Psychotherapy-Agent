# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is largely respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, but the warmth can feel procedural and risk-management-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is good reflective listening and tracking of ambivalence, though the reflections remain somewhat surface-level and repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safety and immediate coping, but it feels fragmented across many microtasks rather than a coherent change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to the client’s low energy, but still generates most of the structure and problem-solving content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are autonomy-supportive phrases and respect for the client’s unwillingness to promise, but the frequent clinician-generated options limit client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few early open questions and the willingness scale are evocative, but the session quickly becomes more advice-driven than client-driven.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and offers choices, but often shifts into a directive expert problem-solver role rather than consistently sharing power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some client change language is elicited, but the psychiatrist does not consistently develop, amplify, or strategically reflect it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges helplessness, but does not consistently or strategically redirect the conversation toward change.
