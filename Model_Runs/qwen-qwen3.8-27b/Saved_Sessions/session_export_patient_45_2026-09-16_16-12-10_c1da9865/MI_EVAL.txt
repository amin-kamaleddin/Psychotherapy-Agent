# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing or shaming the patient. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the patient’s values and normalizing his fear of a lifelong diagnosis.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses reflective listening well to capture ambivalence and underlying concerns. Empathy falls short of a 5 because reflections are somewhat repetitive and do not fully explore emotional weight, shame, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on identifying a manageable first step for diet and alcohol-related lifestyle change. Direction is limited by a narrow focus on soda, bread, and fries, with alcohol reduction and a final commitment step underexplored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting the patient’s input and using his preferences to shape the plan. Full collaboration would involve less therapist-selected framing and more patient-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and supports the patient’s agency in choosing a small step. Autonomy support could improve by explicitly affirming the patient’s right to choose not to change and reducing subtle steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, evocative questions to draw out the patient’s own motivations and values. Evocation is not fully developed into deeper change talk, confidence, or structured commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys that the patient is the expert on his own routines and constraints. Partnership could be stronger by more consistently acknowledging the patient’s expertise and reducing therapist-framed options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk, such as trying a soda swap and asking the doctor a clear question. Change talk is present but underdeveloped because it is not consistently reinforced, summarized, or turned into commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without arguing or criticizing the patient. The session lacks stronger strategic redirection, such as double-sided reflections that connect sustain talk to change talk.
