# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with strong affirmations of the client's honesty (e.g., acknowledging the courage it took to name the 'performing' concept). Normalizes the experience as a very human response rather than a character flaw.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures emotional nuance, such as the distinction between 'being there' and 'showing up as a version of yourself.' Could be elevated by exploring deeper into the emotional weight of social anxiety and fear of not being accepted.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with a logical, strategically sequenced progression from the immediate incident to broader patterns (what's at stake → what separated this night → what does relaxed mean → what would change if the voice were quieter). Only a slight drift when exploring 'performing' before connecting it back to the drinking context.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the client as an equal partner using collaborative language ('I'm curious,' 'I'm wondering,' 'I get the pushback there') and does not impose an agenda. Could be deepened by more explicitly co-creating the session agenda or inviting the client to set direction more actively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by avoiding pressure to commit, using language like 'I'm not asking you to solve that tonight,' and framing issues in terms of the client's own agency. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client's freedom to choose whether or not to change his drinking behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a masterful evocation sequence that draws out the client's own insights rather than telling him why his drinking is problematic. Skillfully notices and reflects the shift in the client's language ('I was just there' → 'I guess I did'), eliciting key concepts like 'performing' and 'a little concerning' from the client himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance throughout, explicitly stating 'I don't have a simple answer for you' to avoid the expert trap. Could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging the client's expertise in his own life and inviting greater co-creation of next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evokes some change talk, particularly when the client admits to not remembering recent nights and calls it 'a little concerning.' Could have been more effective by further strengthening and elaborating on these emerging change statements to build momentum toward contemplation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk reasonably well by distinguishing between 'everyone's doing it' and 'I did it,' and by reflecting the tension in the client's ambivalence ('a part of you is pulling toward this matters' vs. 'I'll be 21 soon'). Could be improved by more actively rolling with or exploring the underlying needs behind sustain talk statements.
