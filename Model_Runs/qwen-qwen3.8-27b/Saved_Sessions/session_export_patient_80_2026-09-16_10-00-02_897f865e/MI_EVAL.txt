# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, affirming, and nonjudgmental, with strong validation of the client's strengths and concerns. Some emotional expressions, such as shame or embarrassment, are addressed more through quick problem-solving than deeper affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate perspective-taking and reflects ambivalence well, though the empathy is often cognitive rather than emotional. There are missed opportunities to fully reflect the client's underlying fear, shame, or concern about failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is clear, focused, and consistently directed toward reducing Friday alcohol use and preventing aggression. The pacing is purposeful, moving effectively from strengths to planning, strategy selection, and confidence-building.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client meaningfully contributes to the plan and message, and his preferences shape key decisions. However, the psychiatrist often leads with structured options and problem-solving, limiting fully collaborative agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites client choice and respects preferences, such as the refusal script, exit plan, and text message wording. Autonomy is still somewhat limited by the relatively high level of clinician-provided options and direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses useful scaling questions and elicits the client's own motivations and plans. More evocation would involve deeper exploration of the client's values, discrepancy, and intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to the client's preferences, showing some partnership. The interaction is still fairly expert-led, with the psychiatrist providing many strategies and scripts rather than prioritizing the client's own solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits desire, ability, need, and commitment language through questions about strengths, confidence, and doable options. The main limitation is not consistently amplifying or consolidating the client's change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Resistance and sustain talk are handled well through normalization and reframing without argument or pressure. At times, the psychiatrist moves too quickly into planning before fully exploring the client's ambivalence.
