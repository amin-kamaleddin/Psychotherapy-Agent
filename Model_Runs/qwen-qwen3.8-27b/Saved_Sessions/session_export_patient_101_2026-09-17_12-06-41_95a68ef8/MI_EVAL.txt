# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with genuine positive regard and no moralizing about drinking. However, affirmations occasionally feel slightly formulaic and there is no moment of explicit, deep unconditional acceptance that would push this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and consistent reflective listening that moves beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture emotional meaning, underlying patterns, and the felt sense of experience. Key reflections precisely track ambivalence and shifts in Mei's internal dialogue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors with good pacing from awareness through pattern identification to micro-level strategy. The session ends somewhat abruptly without a consolidation summary or next-step recap that would provide stronger closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative throughout, with genuine co-construction where Mei's observations drive the direction and the psychiatrist builds on her contributions rather than steering. The power dynamic feels equal and the session reads as a joint inquiry rather than a guided interview.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy through questions and reflections, never prescribing or using directive language. Could be elevated by more explicit affirmations of Mei's right to choose differently, particularly in moments of self-doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation where all motivation, solutions, and insights emerge from Mei's own mouth through strategic questioning. The psychiatrist never provides a single reason for change or a recommendation, consistently trusting the client's internal resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client and avoids imposing plans or prescriptions. Could be deepened by more active co-construction, such as inviting Mei to set the agenda or acknowledging the psychiatrist is learning from her self-observations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently evokes and reinforces change talk through strategic questions and affirmations, generating substantial desire, ability, and commitment language from Mei. Could be stronger by explicitly naming and labeling types of change talk or evoking a confidence re-rating to consolidate the shift.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk using double-sided reflections and counter-evidence without arguing or dismissing. Could benefit from a more explicit double-sided reflection to more actively pivot the balance toward change.
