# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance while validating Isabella's concerns about autonomy and not being 'overreacted to.' Acceptance could be strengthened by adding more individualized, emotionally resonant affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used strong reflective listening to accurately track Isabella's ambivalence and central tension around family tradition, pregnancy, and personal choice. Some reflections became repetitive and did not consistently deepen into emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained generally focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and moved logically toward a concrete, client-owned next step. Direction could improve with earlier agenda-setting and summaries to consolidate motivation and momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaved as an equal partner and actively co-constructed the conversation with collaborative language and client choice. The final dinner plan was clearly client-generated and collaboratively explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a major strength, with explicit non-directive language and consistent respect for Isabella's right to make her own decisions. The psychiatrist avoided pressure, advice-giving, or controlling language while allowing the client to generate her own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out Isabella's values, criteria, and practical next steps, leading to meaningful change talk. Evocation could be stronger by consistently deepening change talk after it appears, especially around importance, confidence, and self-endorsement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveyed that expertise and wisdom resided with Isabella and framed the session as a joint exploration. The client's contributions substantially shaped the direction, from evidence review to partner communication and a concrete first dinner plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited and reflected change talk, especially around Isabella's values, willingness to try a small step, and practical plan. It could be improved by more consistently eliciting deeper change talk around importance, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not challenge or amplify sustain talk and respected Isabella's concerns about not being lectured or pressured. However, the response to sustain talk was not consistently strategic and could benefit from double-sided reflections or value-based reframing.
