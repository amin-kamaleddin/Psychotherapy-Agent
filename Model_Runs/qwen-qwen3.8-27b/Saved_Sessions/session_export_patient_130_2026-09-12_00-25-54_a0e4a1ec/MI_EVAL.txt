# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful and nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating the client’s reluctance and honesty. Warmth was generally supportive, though it sometimes felt more procedural than deeply affirming of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made adequate attempts to understand the client’s annoyance, pressure, and desire for fewer fights. However, the reflections stayed mostly content-focused and missed deeper emotional layers such as resentment, autonomy, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on reducing conflict at home and limiting weekend drug use. The minor limitation was that the focus shifted too much toward practical scripting rather than connecting the behavior back to broader values or goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client to choose among options and built on the client’s weekend-limit idea. However, the collaboration became uneven because the clinician frequently imposed specific scripts, time limits, and rules.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were moments of autonomy support, especially when the psychiatrist offered options and accepted the client’s decision to end the session. Autonomy was inconsistent, though, because the psychiatrist also introduced multiple specific suggestions and structured solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked a few questions about what the client wanted to focus on, but did not consistently draw out the client’s own motivations. The session relied too heavily on advice and scripts rather than eliciting personal values, goals, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and asked for preferences, showing some partnership. However, the stance became increasingly expert-driven and directive, especially in shaping the plan around scripts and practical steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered several change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or explore them. The session moved quickly into problem-solving rather than deepening the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with the client’s resistance and used nonjudgmental language to acknowledge concerns about drugs, pressure, and social life. However, he did not consistently use MI strategies such as double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy to soften sustain talk.
