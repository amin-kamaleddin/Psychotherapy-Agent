# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the difficulty of fitting a diabetes routine into work and family. The tone is somewhat procedural and could express deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and captures ambivalence, fear of misreading numbers, and concerns about panic. Some emotional content, such as worry about being a bother and feeling overwhelmed, is under-addressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of blood glucose monitoring. It moves logically from general ambivalence to a concrete, workable plan without meaningful topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint language and offers options without imposing choices. Marcus’s preferences are integrated into the plan, making the interaction highly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated invitations for Marcus to choose and avoid controlling language. The session remains within Marcus’s sense of feasibility and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions are consistently drawn out from Marcus rather than supplied by the psychiatrist. The client generates the key steps, including timing, cue, target number, and note format.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Marcus as the expert on his own life and preferred approach. Decision-making is clearly shared, with Marcus directly shaping the session’s outcomes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits concrete change talk and supports client-generated steps. The main limitation is a less consistent effort to amplify change talk into stronger commitment, confidence, or values-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully without arguing or dismissing concerns. The response could be stronger if it used more explicit double-sided reflections, such as addressing the fear of being a bother.
