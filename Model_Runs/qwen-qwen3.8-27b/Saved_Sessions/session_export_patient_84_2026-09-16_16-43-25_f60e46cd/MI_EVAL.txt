# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used nonjudgmental, normalizing language and accepted Malik's ambivalence without blame. It fell short of a 5 because affirmations were relatively professional and brief rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured the client's minimization, peer attachment, and frustration with his mother. Some emotional nuances, such as anxiety, autonomy, and fear of losing peer belonging, were not fully reflected or deepened.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session began with a clear target related to cannabis use and health, but it drifted toward family conflict and parental monitoring. The specific target behavior lost momentum because the therapist did not repeatedly clarify the direction or collaboratively define the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist framed the work as a joint exploration and used Malik's own language to guide the conversation. It was not a 5 because the therapist still led through a sequence of questions, including a somewhat repeated question that reduced natural co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist emphasized Malik's control, choice, and perspective rather than imposing solutions. It was not a 5 because the psychiatrist could more explicitly affirm that Malik was free to choose any option, including not changing at all.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions drew out Malik's values, such as peace, belonging, autonomy, and protecting his 'vibe.' The evoked material was not consistently developed into change talk specifically connected to cannabis use or health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, non-hierarchical stance and allowed Malik's concerns to shape the session. A slightly more directive question sequence and a repeated question prevented a perfect partnership rating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-related statements were elicited, such as keeping use away from home and protecting his peace. The therapist did not consistently use DARN-C strategies to amplify or deepen change talk about reducing or protecting himself from cannabis use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with sustain talk and gently reframed it toward related values. However, sustain talk remained central, and the therapist did not consistently use double-sided reflections or build discrepancy in a way that would justify a higher score.
