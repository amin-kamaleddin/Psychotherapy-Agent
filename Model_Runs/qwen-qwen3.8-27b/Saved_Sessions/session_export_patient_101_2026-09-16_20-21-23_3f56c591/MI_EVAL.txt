# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm, communicating nonjudgmental acceptance of Mei's ambivalence and fear without labeling her drinking. Does not deeply normalize the family-history fear or explicitly affirm her worth beyond acknowledging the difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and consistent complex reflections that capture both values, emotions, and ambivalence (e.g., the push-pull between exhaustion and future safety). A minor missed opportunity in not exploring the family-history fear of 'going down that path.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus with purposeful forward-moving questions that create momentum toward change. Narrowly centers on pill-taking logistics without periodic summaries reconnecting the cue back to alcohol reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully co-constructive stance where every client contribution (friends, pill in purse, shelf, bag, charger) becomes the collaborative next step. No lecturing, fixed protocol, or counselor-dominated agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invites Mei to identify what feels doable and realistic, avoiding coercive or prescriptive language. Lacks explicit reinforcement of her freedom to choose not to try a given strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws out Mei's own change language with open, future-oriented questions. Moves quickly into problem-solving without fully evoking deeper desire, confidence, or meaning (e.g., ruler questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mei's perspective and routines drive the session direction throughout, with the counselor following and building on each idea rather than imposing alternatives. Strong power-sharing that could only be enhanced with an explicit partnership statement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete client-generated change ideas (non-drinking friends, pill cue, phone-plug action) and reinforces her initiative. Could deepen with explicit evocations of desire, meaning, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects worry without arguing or amplifying, gently redirecting toward workable problem-solving. Could have used more deliberate double-sided reflections holding worry and hope together.
