# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental positive regard is clearly expressed throughout, especially after Maria rejects problem-solving ('not a burden,' 'not looking away'). The early push into problem-solving ('small step,' 'one thing you've tried') slightly undercuts unconditional acceptance before the pain is fully validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflections capture both pain and interpersonal fear, including a strong empathic repair after Maria reports feeling invalidated. Some reflections stay at an 'I hear' surface level and miss opportunities to differentiate deeper shame, self-hatred, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays anchored to acute risk, present-moment distress, and immediate safety with focused risk questions and present-oriented prompts. The initial vague 'small step' framing and the drift away from a concrete safety thread (plan/means/intent) prevent a perfect score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maria substantially reshapes the session's direction, and the psychiatrist adopts her stated conditions (silence, no fixing, no scrubbing). However, the first two therapist-led, solution-focused questions delay full collaborative establishment and agenda co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong moment-to-moment autonomy is supported through non-coercive invitations ('if you're willing,' 'what do you need,' 'what feels least scary for me to do'). Slightly limited by fewer explicit statements reinforcing Maria's overarching ownership and freedom as the agent of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite Maria's own words and avoid persuasion, but focus mostly on immediate therapeutic presence and safety management. Evocation could have gone deeper to elicit protective factors, values, reasons to live, or confidence beyond the acute relational need.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance is evident ('follow your lead,' 'expert on what has helped,' 'what do you need'), and the psychiatrist adapts to Maria's directions mid-session. Not a 5 because the initial therapist-led, problem-solving opening and repeated safety-agenda framing delay fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maria offers several meaningful change statements ('maybe I can breathe,' 'can't do this alone'), and the psychiatrist reflects some of them. However, there is no consistent amplification, no systematic exploration of values, hopes, or reasons to live, and no double-sided reflections to deepen the ambivalence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing or arguing against sustain talk ('nothing works,' 'want to be dead'), using containment and validation instead. However, there is little strategic redirection via double-sided reflections or gentle reframing to resolve ambivalence or pivot toward change.
