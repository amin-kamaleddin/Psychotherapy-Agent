# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and supportive stance. Statements such as "That makes sense" and "I'm here with you" validate the patient without moralizing or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses reflective listening to capture the patient's frustration, dental priorities, and back pain. Empathy could be deeper by exploring underlying feelings of powerlessness, exhaustion, and past failed attempts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session keeps a clear focus on dental care and immediate comfort. However, it drifts toward dental logistics and back pain without consistently guiding toward a specific smoking-related change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and co-constructs a simple plan around the patient's preferences. Full collaboration could be strengthened by more explicit mutual agenda-setting and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the patient's freedom of choice and avoids coercive advice. The patient's boundaries are respected, and the plan remains low-pressure and self-directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open-ended questions that elicit practical preferences such as flossing and cue use. Evocation could be stronger by exploring deeper values, motivations, and confidence related to smoking cessation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and lets the patient define priorities and boundaries. The interaction remains somewhat clinician-led in structure, limiting full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change-related language appears mainly around dental care and small steps rather than smoking cessation. The psychiatrist could better evoke and amplify motivation, ability, reasons, and commitment related to quitting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the patient's resistance and validates his desire to keep the appointment brief. Softening could be stronger by using double-sided reflections or gently connecting sustain talk to smoking-related change.
