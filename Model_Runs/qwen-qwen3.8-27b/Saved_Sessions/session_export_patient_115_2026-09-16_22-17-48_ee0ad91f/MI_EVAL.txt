# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used respectful, nonjudgmental language and accepted ambivalence without moralizing. Acceptance was strong but occasionally formulaic, with room for more explicit affirmation of difficulty and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate reflections of practical constraints and conflict, such as needing to drive while wanting steadier breathing. Empathy was effective but mainly behavioral, with opportunity to explore deeper emotional concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma management and narrowed effectively to reducing smoking in the cab and managing dust exposure. Direction was clear, though broader targets like drinking were not consistently revisited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with the psychiatrist following Samir’s ideas rather than prescribing solutions. The plan was co-created from Samir’s own suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a major strength, with repeated emphasis on Samir’s choice and ownership of the plan. There was no pressure, coercion, or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited Samir’s own reasoning, barriers, and self-regulation strategies. Evocation was practical and behavioral, but could be deepened by exploring values, confidence, or future outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treated Samir as the expert in his own life and used open questions to guide the conversation. The session was client-driven, with Samir shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully invited small, self-directed steps and reflected Samir’s change statements positively. Cultivation was effective but could be strengthened with scaling questions or deeper exploration of confidence and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by avoiding argument and reframing “not quitting” into a smaller, autonomy-preserving change. Softening was strong but could be more advanced in reducing the pull of sustain talk.
