# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and avoids blame, labeling, or explicit punishment. Acceptance remains somewhat generic and procedural rather than consistently warm or affirming of Dylan as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflections capture Dylan’s view that the response feels overblown and unfair. However, empathy stays surface-level and misses deeper themes such as embarrassment, shame, fear for his standing, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to the incident but shifts among safety, memory, documentation, texting, grounding, and future consequences. Direction feels practical but scattered rather than focused on a single client-led change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and collaborative problem-solving, which is better than a purely directive approach. Collaboration is uneven because the counselor often acts as the problem-solver and does not fully center Dylan’s stated priority of returning to normal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** This is one of the stronger areas, with repeated respect for Dylan’s right to choose, including permission to do nothing. The repeated presentation of options and risk framing can still feel subtly directive to a defensive client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few open-ended questions early, but the counselor does not consistently follow up by eliciting Dylan’s own values, reasons for change, or confidence. The interaction shifts too quickly into information-gathering, scripts, and practical advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids overtly authoritarian commands, showing partial partnership. However, the counselor frequently controls the agenda and practical process, making the interaction more advisor-and-advisee than equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only rare and weak efforts to elicit or build on change talk. The counselor misses opportunities to reflect or amplify Dylan’s desire to return to class, protect his social life, and move forward.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing Dylan’s sustain talk but does not strategically soften it with double-sided reflections or discrepancy. Statements that he is “basically 21” and the incident is unfair remain unresolved.
