# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys nonjudgmental respect and support, avoiding moralizing when the patient minimizes use. Statements such as “I’m glad you’re here” and “No pressure, just curious” reflect strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate and reflective listening to capture ambivalence, especially around being blamed and weekend use. Empathy is strong but becomes too brief when the patient expresses shame, awkwardness, and self-criticism about drug testing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on methamphetamine use, the infection, substance purity, and weekend safety without drifting into unrelated topics. The target remains somewhat broad, and a more specific collaborative focus could strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient’s perspective and uses the patient’s language to guide the conversation. However, the clinician still maintains a health/safety frame, limiting the degree of fully co-created collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is consistently strong, with repeated affirmations that the patient does not need to quit or follow a strict plan. The psychiatrist returns decision-making to the patient while raising safety concerns without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes the patient’s own motivations, values, and ideas about safety and reduced risk. Evocation would be stronger if it more deeply explored confidence, identity, and preferred next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the patient as an active participant and responds to his concerns about loneliness, testing, and social comfort. The stance could become more collaborative by explicitly acknowledging the patient as the expert on his own life and negotiating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out change talk around safer use, knowing substance purity, and having another plan for Friday. Change talk could be strengthened by more amplification, values-based exploration, or confidence-building.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing or pressuring when the patient minimizes use or says he “manages.” Some responses risk over-normalizing minimization and could more actively redirect sustain talk toward change.
