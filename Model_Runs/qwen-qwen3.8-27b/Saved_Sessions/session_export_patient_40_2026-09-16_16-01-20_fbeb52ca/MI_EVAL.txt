# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently accepted Elias's ambivalence, shame, and self-doubt without judgment or pressure. Responses affirmed his reality, agency, and progress, creating a respectful and non-moralizing stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and emotionally attuned to Elias's mixed feelings, fear of burdening others, and internal conflict. The psychiatrist captured both the content and felt experience behind Elias's ambivalence and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on sustained recovery, relapse prevention, shame management, and building a concrete morning plan. The psychiatrist kept emerging ideas connected to the central goal of staying steady and reducing craving-driven choices.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with Elias's own ideas shaping the session and final plan. The psychiatrist organized and clarified Elias's thoughts rather than imposing advice or a preformed agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Elias's sense of choice and avoided controlling or coercive language. Options were offered only as possibilities, with decision-making returned to Elias throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew motivation from Elias by eliciting his own change language and supporting his self-generated plan. However, some questions were somewhat structured and could have been more open to deepen value- or identity-based change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Elias as the expert on his own life, recovery, and triggers. Elias's contributions substantially guided the focus, planning, and final commitments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was frequently elicited and reinforced, especially through Elias's own examples of past successes and doable commitments. The score is 4 because the session did not always deepen change talk through more explicit desire, ability, need, and commitment exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and softened without argument or dismissal, often through double-sided reflections. It was handled well, but the psychiatrist did not always explicitly pivot the sustain talk into stronger value-based change language.
