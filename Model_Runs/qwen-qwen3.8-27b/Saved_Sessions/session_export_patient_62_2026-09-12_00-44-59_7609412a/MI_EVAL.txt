# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming Jordan's refusals or discouragement. The score is not higher because rapid problem-solving and task orientation sometimes reduce the depth of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers generally accurate reflections of distress and effort to understand Jordan's perspective. However, empathy remains brief and misses deeper ambivalence, hope, and the meaning behind Jordan's belief that he cannot catch up.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on Jordan's financial stress, overwhelm, and difficulty moving forward. Direction could be stronger by consolidating the emerging pattern and building clearer MI momentum toward change rather than repeatedly offering new options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and allows Jordan to make choices. The interaction is limited by frequent therapist-generated menus and problem-solving, which reduces the sense of true equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices and respects Jordan's refusals without pressure. Autonomy support is weakened by prescriptive suggestions and repeated therapist-generated next steps rather than inviting Jordan to define his own move.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions, especially early, and invites Jordan to describe his concerns and sensations. Evocation remains moderate because the session relies on closed or menu-based questions rather than deeply drawing out Jordan's values, reasons for change, and agency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and preferences, showing some partnership. However, the session is largely therapist-guided, with much of the agenda and possible solutions supplied by the psychiatrist rather than Jordan's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some helpful invitations and affirmations of small steps, such as checking one due date. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand Jordan's own change talk, so the development of motivation remains limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Jordan's sustain talk and acknowledges his concerns without confrontation. Sustain talk remains dominant, and the psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections to help Jordan hold his ambivalence.
