# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and generally nonjudgmental tone, but acceptance is limited by a corrective, fact-driven approach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of genuine perspective-taking, but empathy is often brief and quickly shifts to factual or directive content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the health-related targets, but direction is largely informational rather than MI-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative language and options are used, but the psychiatrist remains the primary source of information and direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's freedom to choose is frequently acknowledged, but prescriptive health guidance weakens the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to elicit the patient's own motivations for change, with most questions being information-gathering rather than MI-focused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and shared direction, but the expert-driven style keeps the partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk are rare and weak, with little amplification or exploration of the patient's own change-oriented ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk and patient resistance are largely unaddressed, and repeated factual correction can reinforce the adversarial dynamic.
