# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and acknowledges Marcus’s difficulty and strengths. It stops short of a 5 because unconditional positive regard is mostly implied rather than explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately tracks Marcus’s ambivalence, guilt, and family concerns. It could be deeper by more directly naming the shame, fear of failure, or vulnerability behind his statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on building a realistic, sustainable activity plan. The pacing moves logically from a small step to practical supports and failure-prevention without drifting into medical or dieting topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is joint problem-solving rather than clinician-led. The plan clearly emerges from Marcus’s own ideas, which the psychiatrist then organizes and builds on.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and repeatedly invites Marcus to choose his own steps. It could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing Marcus’s freedom to adjust, pause, or stop the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist elicits Marcus’s own motivations, values, and concrete solutions. Evocation is slightly limited because the therapist sometimes offers examples or options before waiting for the client to generate more of his own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Marcus as the expert on his own life and schedule. Client-generated ideas substantially shape the session, which reflects strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus produces several clear change statements, and the psychiatrist acknowledges and reflects them. The therapist could do more to deliberately amplify, deepen, or scale this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by normalizing concerns and reframing them without argument. The therapist turns barriers into useful information for a safer, more realistic plan.
