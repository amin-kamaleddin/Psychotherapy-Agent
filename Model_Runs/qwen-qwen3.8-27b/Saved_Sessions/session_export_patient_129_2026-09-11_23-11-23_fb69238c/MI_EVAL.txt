# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental stance and validates the client's frustration without shaming, but sometimes over-agrees rather than offering warmer, more individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections of being pressured and exhausted are present but remain somewhat generic and miss deeper themes such as control, false accusation, and fear of being labeled disrespectful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a negotiated agenda and sustained focus on a target behavior, drifting into repetitive problem-solving rather than addressing substance use or a client-centered goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are collaborative gestures, but the psychiatrist acts more like a problem-solver than an equal partner and does not integrate the client's clear objections into a shared direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is frequently reminded that he has freedom to choose and is not pressured, but the therapist-generated options weaken the sense that the plan comes from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is very little effort to evoke the client's own values, concerns, or reasons for change, with the session often shifting to advice instead of eliciting client-driven motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks the client to choose options, but remains expert-driven by repeatedly offering scripts and plans after the client rejects them, so his contributions do not shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Small openings are created by asking what he wants and what feels doable, but there is little follow-through to elicit his own reasons, goals, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but often agrees with sustain talk, such as "I'm not the problem," reinforcing the status quo rather than gently exploring ambivalence or alternative goals.
