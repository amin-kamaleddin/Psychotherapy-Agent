# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided shaming the patient’s resistance. Acceptance could be deeper by fully acknowledging the patient’s frustration at feeling misunderstood.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were effective early reflections about the patient’s fatigue with reminders and the meaning of asthma symptoms. Empathy became more generic later and missed a key opportunity to address the relational frustration of not being understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stayed appropriately focused on a concrete, realistic asthma management step involving inhaler use. The main limitation was becoming repetitive and task-focused near the end despite patient pushback.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient’s ideas shaped the plan, especially the door-handle idea and preference to avoid alarms and texts. Collaboration was uneven because the therapist continued offering options and planning prompts after the patient resisted further direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist supported choice by framing the plan as an experiment and accepting the patient’s preference against check-ins. Autonomy support was undermined by repeated options and continued steering after the patient expressed feeling controlled.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist elicited useful patient-generated ideas and confidence ratings. Evocation remained surface-level because deeper values, concerns, and reasons for change were not consistently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient was invited to choose a small, manageable step and his preferences were respected. The therapeutic alliance was not fully balanced because the therapist retained much of the agenda and kept problem-solving after resistance emerged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expressed meaningful change talk, including using the inhaler when symptoms appear and avoiding later regret. The therapist did not consistently amplify, reflect, or deepen this change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoided arguing and acknowledged some of the patient’s reasons for resisting, such as not wanting to feel nagged. Sustain talk was not strategically softened, and the repeated focus on planning may have increased the patient’s resistance.
