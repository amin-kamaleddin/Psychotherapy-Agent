# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and explicitly avoided labels, shame, or lecture-like language. Acceptance was strong but somewhat efficient, so it did not fully reach the highest level of consistently warm, individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Marco’s goals, autonomy concerns, and planning perspective, including reframing missed days as “information, not failure.” Deeper emotional empathy would have more directly addressed shame, self-criticism, pride, and the meaning behind his statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on a specific target behavior: a short after-dinner walk. Nearly every interaction advanced confidence, planning, problem-solving, follow-up, or commitment without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and incorporated Marco’s ideas, such as the fixed 8:30 time, shoes by the door, brief coworker text, and communication with his wife. Collaboration could be stronger by reducing the counselor-led flow and fewer early strategy suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through options, respect for Marco’s preferences, and repeated emphasis on ownership rather than permission. The counselor avoided pressuring language and committed to nonjudgmental follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, scaling, and exploration of past success helped Marco articulate his own plan and reasons for change. Evocation could be stronger by reducing early advice-like suggestions and more deeply exploring values, meaning, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Marco as the expert on his own life and built the plan around his strengths and preferences. The interaction was collaborative, but the structured agenda and counselor-introduced strategies kept it from being fully equal-partner throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling, strength-based prompts, and planning questions effectively elicited desire, ability, and commitment language. The counselor could have more consistently amplified Marco’s own change talk before moving quickly into advice and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not challenged or reinforced; concerns about snacks and setbacks were reframed in a more MI-consistent way. The counselor could more consistently use double-sided reflections to pivot sustain talk directly into change talk.
