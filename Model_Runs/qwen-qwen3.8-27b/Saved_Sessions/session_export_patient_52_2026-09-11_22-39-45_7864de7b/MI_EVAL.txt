# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone that reduces shame. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural and not consistently rich in individualized, unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adequately acknowledges exhaustion, responsibility, and the need for relief. Yet, the reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional themes such as guilt, depletion, and the conflict between self-care and parenting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and developing a practical coping plan. The primary weakness is that this direction is more behavior-management oriented than MI-process oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and incorporates the client's practical suggestions into the plan. However, the overall structure remains largely therapist-generated, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by avoiding lectures and allowing the client to modify the plan. However, repeated directive instructions and recaps feel prescriptive and may undermine the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the primary weakness, as the session quickly shifts into advice and concrete planning. There is little active effort to elicit the client's internal reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and integrates the client's ideas, such as the 3-minute pause and same-glass substitute. Nevertheless, the interaction remains relatively expert-driven and directive rather than a fully collaborative process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates some relevant change talk, which the psychiatrist reinforces. However, the therapist does not consistently use evocative questions to expand the client's motivation, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against the client's reasons for drinking and adaptively modifies the plan to be less burdensome. More MI-consistent strategies, such as double-sided reflections or strategic redirection, were not consistently used.
