# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, normalizes Elira’s anxiety and self-doubt, and avoids judgment. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, and the final fear of not following through is not directly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s pattern, fatigue, and worry about freezing. Empathy remains mostly paraphrase rather than fully exploring underlying fear, shame, or conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on post-practice avoidance, confidence, and follow-through. It moves clearly from problem identification to a concrete, low-barrier behavior plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses “we” and incorporates client choices into the plan. However, frequent therapist-generated option lists make the interaction more therapist-led than truly equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks permission and invites choice, allowing Elira to select parts of the plan. Autonomy support is weakened by directive prompts like “Decide now what to do with your phone” and limited explicit affirmation of her freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative techniques are used, including open questions, scaling, and barriers questions. The counselor does not consistently draw out the client’s own motivations, meaning, or reasons for change before offering interventions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present and the client’s preferences shape parts of the plan. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist often takes an expert role by offering multiple techniques before eliciting the client’s ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elira expresses desire and willingness, and the psychiatrist affirms some of this language. Change talk is not systematically developed or expanded, as the session shifts toward behavior planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes doubts without arguing or reinforcing them. Softening is not consistently strategic, and the final sustain talk is left unresolved rather than reflected, pivoted, or explored.
