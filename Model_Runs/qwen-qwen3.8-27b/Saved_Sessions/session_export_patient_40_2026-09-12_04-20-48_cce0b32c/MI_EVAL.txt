# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, while avoiding shame around the relapse. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly normalizing relapse as part of recovery and separating Elias’s behavior from his worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate and complex reflections of Elias’s pride, shame, fear, ambivalence, and values. The score is limited by missing deeper emotional moments, such as his fear of being “sober or just hiding” and his final skepticism about the plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the presenting concern: relapse risk, safety, late-night urges, and concrete coping strategies. There is no meaningful topic drift, and safety is appropriately checked throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with Elias actively contributing to the porch plan, 15-minute delay, crisis line, and one-tap access format. The psychiatrist still supplies much of the structure, language, and practical content, which limits a fully collaborative rating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking for preferences, respecting Elias’s boundary about not waking his brother, and inviting edits to the plan. A higher score would require more frequent explicit reinforcement that Elias is the decision-maker and the plan only works if it reflects his own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Elias’s internal motivation and uses client-specific language, especially “awake without the fog,” as the core of the plan. Evocation is somewhat interrupted by psychoeducation and practical problem-solving, and could more consistently build confidence, commitment, and identity change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and allows Elias to shape the plan through his own choices. The score is not a 5 because the psychiatrist remains the primary organizer, offering structured options, scripts, and crisis details.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk is elicited, including Elias’s desire to be “awake without the fog,” his agency in the park trip, and his decision to throw out the beer. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or develop this change talk into stronger commitment, confidence, or self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully and without arguing or dismissing Elias’s doubts. The score is limited by fewer double-sided reflections and by the final skepticism about the porch plan remaining unreflected and unprocessed.
