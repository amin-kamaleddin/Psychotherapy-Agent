# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, warm, unconditional positive regard with zero judgment or shaming. Powerfully affirms the person beyond the problem, e.g., 'Those aren't new. They're already you.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly nuanced reflections capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions, using the client's own language and metaphors. Sophisticated complex reflections build on each other to demonstrate deep attunement to the client's emotional landscape.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical arc from minimization to insight with strong momentum throughout. Minor opportunity to more tightly connect identity exploration back to the specific target behavior (meth use vs. 'the scene' broadly).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session structure emerges entirely from the client's language and concerns rather than a predetermined agenda. The session ends with the client's own realization, which is the hallmark of true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces ownership and freedom — 'You don't have to sort that out yet,' 'You don't have to become someone new.' Never uses directive language; all questions are open-ended and exploratory, inviting the client to discover their own motivations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits motivation from within the client without providing external reasons or psychoeducation. The client spontaneously generates evidence of ability ('I've done it before') by session's end. Could be strengthened with a scaling question to further solidify confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly declines the expert role and frames all insights as originating from the client, not the psychiatrist. Functions purely as a mirror and question-asker, with the session entirely driven by the client's own words and realizations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes multiple types of change talk (desire, ability, reason, intention) through strategic questions and reflections. Could be improved by more explicitly amplifying and reinforcing each piece of change talk as it emerges, e.g., naming the confidence implied in 'I've done it before.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully with double-sided reflections that honor both positions without taking a stance. Could further use reflective pivots to help the client resolve some of the identity-based sustain talk, though the session ends at a natural point of insight.
