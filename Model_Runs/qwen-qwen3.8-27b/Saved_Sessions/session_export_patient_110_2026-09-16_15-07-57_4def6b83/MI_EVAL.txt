# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, affirming Richard’s efforts and preferences. The opening is slightly prescriptive, and acceptance could be warmer by more explicitly validating ambivalence as normal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses strong, accurate reflections of Richard’s ambivalence and values, such as staying active with his wife while avoiding a heavy health process. Empathy could deepen further by exploring underlying fears, such as being a burden or feeling managed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on cholesterol medication adherence and steadily moves from values to practical, client-defined steps. It builds toward concrete action, such as the counter cue and trail-based evidence, without drifting off target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor co-constructs the plan with Richard rather than prescribing it, building directly on his ideas about the counter, a quiet nod from his wife, and trail planning. The interaction reflects genuine partnership and shared ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with repeated framing of the plan as Richard’s own choice rather than an imposed requirement. The counselor avoids pressure and respects Richard’s preference not to be managed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The questions generally draw out Richard’s internal motivations, especially around his wife, independence, and quiet success. Evocation would be stronger with more consistent follow-up questions that deepen confidence and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly invites Richard’s perspective and follows his contributions rather than directing the session. There is no expert-driven tone, and the work stays centered on Richard’s own goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard produces meaningful change talk, and the counselor affirms it, but the counselor does not consistently amplify or deepen it. More strategic use of DARN-C follow-up questions would strengthen change talk further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by acknowledging concerns without arguing or correcting. The counselor smoothly redirects statements like not wanting a big deal toward Richard’s own goals and practical routines.
