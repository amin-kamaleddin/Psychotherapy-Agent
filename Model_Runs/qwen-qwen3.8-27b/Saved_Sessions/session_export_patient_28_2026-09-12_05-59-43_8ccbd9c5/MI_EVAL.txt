# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, validating, and supportive throughout the session. Slightly formulaic with repeated phrases like "That makes sense" and "I hear you," limiting deeper individualized emotional resonance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate complex reflections that captured the client's ambivalence, fear of failure, and shame (e.g., "want it to feel worthwhile and hate adding another promise you might break"). Strong empathic attunement to the client's specific psychological state.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the target behavior throughout. Logical progression from goal to barriers to concrete plan, with every interaction directed toward concretizing the behavioral change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction with ideas offered and adjusted based on client feedback (e.g., alarm rejected, wife's tiredness noted). Client's own ideas were seamlessly integrated into the final co-created plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent agency-emphasizing language and options offered rather than directives (e.g., "Which feels most doable?"). Respects client's low-pressure preference and reinforces ownership over his own change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excelled at eliciting solutions from within the client rather than providing them. Evocative questions led the client to generate the shoe cue, kitchen-standing idea, and fridge note himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong facilitator stance with the client invited to make all key decisions on duration, time, location, and cue. Language like "Which feels most doable?" and "Any small tweak you'd make?" ensured the client's voice drove the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of open-ended questions produced a clear confidence shift from 4/10 to 8/10, with client language evolving from vague desire to specific ability and commitment. Client-driven motivation was consistently amplified without therapist imposition.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframed the 'five minutes feels too small' sustain talk as a 'smart low-barrier move.' Minor early mismatch with the spouse-walking suggestion after wife stated tired, and the final 'weird' concern was left unaddressed.
