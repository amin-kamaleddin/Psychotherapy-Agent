# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing the client’s ambivalence about alcohol and peer belonging. It could be stronger with more personalized warmth and affirmation of the client’s strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture the client’s ambivalence around belonging, practice, and social perception. Some responses remain surface-level and miss deeper identity or fear themes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, peer influence, performance, and a concrete low-stakes plan. It could improve with periodic summaries that connect values, concerns, and chosen steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes to the final plan, especially the camera, soda, and success criteria. However, frequent prepackaged scripts and bullet options make the interaction feel more advisory than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks permission, offers choices, and frames steps as low-stakes experiments. Repeated provision of specific scripts and options somewhat constrains the client’s freedom and sense of being in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions evoke the client’s goals, priorities, and desired win. Too much reliance on counselor-supplied reasons and strategies limits deeper exploration of the client’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaborative language and the client shapes a practical plan. The counselor often takes an expert problem-solving role, making the partnership present but somewhat superficial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk about team priority, wanting to be present without the buzz, and not wanting to make a game of it. The counselor does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand that change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s sustain talk about fitting in and trying the experience. It lacks strategic MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or value-based reframing.
