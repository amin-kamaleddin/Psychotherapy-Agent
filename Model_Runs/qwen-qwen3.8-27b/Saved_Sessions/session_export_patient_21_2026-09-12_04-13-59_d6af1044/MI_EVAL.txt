# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms effort, normalizes concerns, and avoids judgment, conveying respect and positive regard. It falls short of 5 because affirmation is somewhat task-centered and does not fully reassure Camila she would remain valued if the plan fails.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections capture ambivalence, morning blur, and worry about derailment. The score is limited because reflections stay mainly behavioral or cognitive and miss deeper emotional meaning, especially the final guilt concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on increasing physical activity through a small morning routine to address afternoon fatigue. It progresses logically to a concrete, actionable plan without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is substantially shaped by Camila’s choices, showing genuine collaboration. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist sometimes leads with information and menus of options rather than fully letting the client generate the next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids coercion, offers alternatives, and emphasizes that Camila decides and can stop if it feels burdensome. It misses a 5 because suggestions still fill some space and the final guilt or failure worry is not explicitly met with autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and planning questions evoke some client-driven motivation and confidence. However, frequent advice, explanations, and option lists reduce the client’s own articulation of values, reasons, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Camila as the expert and asks for her preferences, producing a client-selected plan. The slight advising role and provided menus prevent a full 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some change talk and confidence, such as intention to start tomorrow and perceive doability. It does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen change talk, often stepping in with options instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and redirected toward smaller manageable actions, avoiding confrontation. The softening is not fully skillful because double-sided reflections are limited and the final guilt or failure worry is not directly addressed.
