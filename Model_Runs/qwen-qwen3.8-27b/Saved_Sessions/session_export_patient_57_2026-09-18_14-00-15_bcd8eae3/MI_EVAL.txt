# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment, normalizing Jamal's experience without moralizing. Occasional scripting keeps it from a 5, and a more explicit affirmation of Jamal's courage in being open could have elevated it further.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate capture of the 'lose-lose' dynamic and the emotional weight of the social interpretation problem. Empathy occasionally stays at the cognitive level rather than fully accessing the deeper emotional vulnerability beneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from safety to consequences to exploring what it would take to protect his standards, with no drift off-topic. The session ends without a collaborative plan or forward-looking next step, slightly weakening the directional arc.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels like a genuine two-way exploration, with Jamal's responses clearly shaping the direction and the psychiatrist following his lead on team culture and social dynamics. Could be elevated by more explicit co-creation language, such as inviting Jamal to set the agenda or naming the collaborative process directly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional in a directive referral context — the psychiatrist fully neutralizes the compliance framing and positions Jamal as the expert on his own life and choices. No pressure, no implied 'shoulds,' and his final statement ('That's not me') is met with validation rather than correction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All 'evidence' for change comes from Jamal himself, drawn out through well-timed questions about performance and his own standards. The closing could have pushed further into exploring his confidence in making a change that feels authentic to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance with open questions and no directive advice-giving throughout. Could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language and occasional positioning as a true equal rather than a gentle guide.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk builds progressively from 'I guess a little bit' to 'I don't want to be the kid who's always tired' through well-timed double-sided reflections and probing questions. Skillful without over-amplifying or pushing; each piece of change talk is acknowledged and gently built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated ('That bond is real') without being reinforced as a reason to continue, and the 'whack-a-mole' concern is acknowledged while gently noting the exhaustion of that path. Could have more explicitly used double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while simultaneously highlighting the change talk within it.
