# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, nonjudgment, and affirmation of the client’s efforts. Acceptance is solid, though it remains more functional than deeply affirming and sometimes shifts quickly into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some accurate reflections of the client’s ambivalence and stress. However, the responses are often content-focused and do not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on recovery-related goals, especially housing, dignity, and being a stable father. Direction could improve by more consistently reconnecting the housing plan to alcohol reduction and violence prevention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and accepts the client’s modifications to scripts and boundaries. Collaboration is strong, though it is partly structured around clinician-generated options rather than fully eliciting the client’s own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports choice and avoids pressuring the client to move in with whānau. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming client control and allowing more time for the client to generate options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some of the client’s motivations, such as being a stable dad and avoiding relapse. However, the session often moves into advice and planning before the client’s internal motivations are fully developed and amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves the client in choosing the focus, boundaries, and scripts. Partnership is present but uneven because the psychiatrist frequently steps into an advisor or problem-solver role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces relevant change talk about stability, sobriety, and being a father his children can look up to. The psychiatrist could better amplify and develop this change talk with follow-up questions and reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges some sustain talk, such as fear of judgment and concern about freedom. However, the sustain talk is not consistently softened using double-sided reflections, value connections, or gentle pivots toward change.
