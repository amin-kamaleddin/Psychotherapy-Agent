# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance with explicit reassurance reducing fear of evaluation. Slightly formulaic in places and could have deepened unconditional positive regard beyond the 'good student' identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captured ambivalence and internal conflict. Missed an opportunity to deeply reflect the shame/fear of being seen as 'the kid who messed it up.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, coherent focus on the referral concern with logical progression from concern to indicators to monitoring plan. Session remained more assessment-oriented and could have more consistently tied indicators back to alcohol-use decisions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client's ideas substantially shaped the agenda and plan, with each choice respected and built upon. Could have included more explicit joint problem-solving language to make collaboration more overt.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support for client's freedom to choose, with multiple preference-eliciting questions and no pressure or lecturing. Autonomy was tied mainly to assessment rather than explicitly affirmed around decisions about alcohol use itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several good evocative questions that drew out values, standards, and preferences. Mostly evoked monitoring preferences rather than motivation for changing alcohol use itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client explicitly positioned as expert with clear invitation to direct the session and agenda shaped by her choices. Still somewhat guided by the counselor's initial data-based framework rather than fully negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client offered relevant values and self-monitoring statements, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or reflect them back as change talk. Session remained assessment-focused rather than building motivation toward behavioral change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk or create an adversarial dynamic, with reasonable nonjudgmental reflections. Lacked strategic redirection or nuanced double-sided reflections to open space for change from maintain-the-status-quo language.
