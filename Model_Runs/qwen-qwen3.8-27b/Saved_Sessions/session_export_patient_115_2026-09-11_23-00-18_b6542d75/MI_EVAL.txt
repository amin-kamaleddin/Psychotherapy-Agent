# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, avoiding blame around smoking, drinking, dust exposure, and asthma control. Warmth is sometimes brief and procedural rather than deeply normalized for Samir’s long-standing struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate and often accurate regarding Samir’s practical job constraints. However, the response stays mostly cognitive and misses deeper emotional themes such as fear, frustration, resignation, and feeling trapped between income and health.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to asthma control, inhaler use, smoking, dust exposure, and follow-up. Direction is less MI-oriented because the conversation repeatedly cycles through practical advice, scripts, and plans rather than moving toward a client-owned change agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through offering options and accepting Samir’s decision to try a spacer. However, the session is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying structured recommendations rather than fully co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Samir’s limits and avoids pushing immediate smoking cessation. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated offers of scripts, plans, videos, and check-ins after Samir declines them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks an initial open question but then mostly supplies reasons, recommendations, and practical steps. There is limited use of evocative questions to help Samir articulate his own motivations, confidence, or preferred next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some collaborative intent by asking which option feels more useful and acknowledging that solutions must fit Samir’s job. The interaction remains largely clinician-led, with Samir not substantially steering the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samir produces some change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms parts of it. However, the psychiatrist rarely uses evocative MI questions to amplify, expand, or build on Samir’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct argument or pressure, which is appropriate, but does not consistently roll with or redirect sustain talk. Some statements may inadvertently validate staying at a lower level of change by reinforcing partial reduction as sufficient.
