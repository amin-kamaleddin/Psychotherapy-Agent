# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, validation, and nonjudgment, especially around David’s stress and social triggers. Acceptance is somewhat task-focused, with less explicit affirmation of David as a person independent of his progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures David’s practical concerns and feelings of being overwhelmed. It could be strengthened by exploring deeper emotions such as fear, frustration, shame, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on managing smoking urges in high-risk situations, such as the bar and work-related stress. The discussion moves clearly from general support to specific, actionable coping plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and works as an equal partner in building the plan. David’s ideas, such as the 15-minute pause and cold water bottle, meaningfully shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David’s role as the decision-maker is respected through preference-focused questions and avoidance of direct advice. The intervention could be stronger by more explicitly affirming his freedom to choose and ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out David’s own ideas, strategies, and practical motivation. Evocation could be deepened by connecting quitting to broader personal values, identity, or long-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David is positioned as the expert on his own experience, and his contributions actively guide the conversation. The psychiatrist still retains some agenda-setting and does not fully invite David to choose the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful client-generated change statements and helps refine them into concrete plans. It could be improved by more actively amplifying and summarizing deeper change talk, such as values, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and refrains from dismissing David’s concerns about relapse, stress, and barriers. More use of double-sided reflections or deliberate pivots toward strengths and reasons for change would balance the sustain talk.
