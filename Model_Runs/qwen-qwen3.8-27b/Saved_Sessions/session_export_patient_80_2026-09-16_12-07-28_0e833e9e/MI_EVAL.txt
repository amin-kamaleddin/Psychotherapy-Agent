# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positive and generally respectful, but repeated praise can feel evaluative and subtly pressure the client. A minor pronoun lapse also slightly reduced attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s practical concerns and risk moments. More explicit validation of the client’s nervousness and emotional ambivalence would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused and moves logically from risk identification to a concrete plan. Each turn advances toward a specific, doable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-created using the client’s own ideas and preferences. The clinician, however, provides much of the structure and scripting, which could be softened to allow more client-led direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and avoids pressuring abstinence. Repeated confidence scaling and emphasis on follow-through could create subtle expectation, so stronger statements of client control would help.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively draws out the client’s own strategies and confidence. Deeper exploration of personal values, hopes, and the broader meaning of staying safe would deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The stance is collaborative, with invitations for client input and respect for his expertise. The psychiatrist still leads by frequently offering options and scripts, which could be reduced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and client-generated steps effectively strengthen motivation and confidence. The work could be deepened by connecting change to personal values and relationship goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the client’s partial reduction without pushing abstinence. More explicit, nonjudgmental acknowledgment of the pull of drinking could further soften sustain talk.
