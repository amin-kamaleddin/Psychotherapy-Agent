# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers a nonjudgmental, validating stance toward Jordan’s mixed feelings about self-protection and family risk. It is strong but could feel warmer and less formulaic by normalizing the stress and ambivalence more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflections of Jordan’s ambivalence, responsibility burden, and need for certainty. A fifth level would require deeper emotional exploration of frustration, resentment, or fear behind the reluctance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear MI focus on the vaccination decision and moves coherently through values, evidence needs, and a possible next step. It would be stronger if the final discussion were consolidated into a concrete client-owned action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s input and works alongside him rather than overriding his clinical relationship with his doctor. It would improve with more explicit agenda negotiation and joint co-construction of a next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Powerfully supports Jordan’s right to choose and repeatedly returns to the decision as his own. It avoids pressure and explicitly respects Jordan’s need to rely on his trusted physician for the final data.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses evocative questions that elicit Jordan’s personal values, threshold for protection, and desired trustworthy answer. It would be stronger if it also more fully elicited broader change talk such as desire, confidence, ability, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats Jordan as the expert on his own priorities and invites his perspective throughout. It would be stronger with more visible agenda co-creation and explicit invitations for Jordan to guide the next step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan produces useful change talk about potentially taking the shot and asking his doctor a specific question. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify, summarize, and deepen this emerging change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges Jordan’s sustain talk without challenging or arguing against it. It would benefit from more strategic double-sided reflections or reframes that actively turn his concerns into change-oriented language.
