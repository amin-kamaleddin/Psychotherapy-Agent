# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and validating while discussing Nathaniel’s coffee use. However, the tone was somewhat formulaic and problem-focused, limiting the warmth of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate, surface-level reflections that showed an effort to understand Nathaniel’s experience. Deeper emotional or ambivalent themes, such as fear of losing his “edge” or the importance of his routine, were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior, especially the after-2 PM caffeine cutoff, symptom tracking, and practical next steps. The direction was clear but more behavior-plan driven than strategically motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Nathaniel’s input and adapted the plan to his preference for a low-disruption approach. However, the interaction remained uneven because the psychiatrist provided many detailed recommendations while co-creating the plan less intentionally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Nathaniel’s autonomy by avoiding pressure, offering options, and acknowledging his choice to start small. Explicit reinforcement of Nathaniel’s ownership could be stronger.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relied heavily on psychoeducation and advice rather than eliciting Nathaniel’s own reasons for change. There were few evocative questions about his values, concerns, confidence, or future-oriented motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked about Nathaniel’s routine and adjusted to his preferences, showing some partnership. However, the psychiatrist often assumed an expert role, which limited the expression of Nathaniel as the primary expert on his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were minimal attempts to evoke and strengthen Nathaniel’s change talk. Statements expressing discomfort with jitters, blood pressure concerns, or sleep impact were not consistently reflected, amplified, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nathaniel’s sustain talk about needing coffee for focus, routine, and his “edge” was frequently validated rather than strategically softened. The psychiatrist rarely helped him explore discrepancy between his current habits and his health or work goals.
