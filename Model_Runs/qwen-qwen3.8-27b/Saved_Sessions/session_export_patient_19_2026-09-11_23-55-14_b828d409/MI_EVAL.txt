# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated respectfully and nonjudgmentally, accepting the client’s decisions and stress without pressure. However, acceptance was more practical than deeply affirming, especially when the client expressed ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the social and enjoyable aspects of gambling and some frustration with its costs. However, empathy remained surface-level and did not fully explore the client’s underlying emotions, values, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on gambling reduction, credit card debt, and practical behavioral changes. The focus was clear, though at times it became overly structured and agenda-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited some client input by offering choices and asking which options felt doable. Collaboration was present but uneven because the counselor frequently guided the agenda with practical plans and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by letting the client select practical steps and text messages. Autonomy was sometimes weakened by repeated suggestions and directive problem-solving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked the client to identify doable steps, supporting some self-directed motivation. However, deeper reasons for change, values, and future goals were not consistently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempted to share control by asking the client to choose options. Partnership was limited by the expert-driven structure and the counselor’s frequent introduction of plans and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirmed the client’s proposed plan and encouraged some change talk. There were limited opportunities to deepen the client’s own reasons for change, confidence, or future-oriented motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided arguing with the client’s reasons for continuing to gamble and acknowledged the fun and social aspects. However, there was limited use of reflections or reframing to help the client resolve ambivalence and move toward change.
