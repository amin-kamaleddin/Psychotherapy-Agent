# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout the session, with specific validation of the client's experience rather than formulaic responses. Gracefully handles the client's irritation about trailing-off silence by immediately owning it rather than defending.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate and consistent understanding of the client's perspective, consistently going beyond surface content to capture the emotional and existential weight. Each reflection adds new depth or a new angle, including sophisticated captures like the 'twist at the end' of trading one unknown for another.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and consistent focus on the target issue with logical progression from rapport to exploration of autonomy, the moving finish line, and the unknown. Could benefit from slightly more explicit strategic movement toward a specific behavioral target, though the direction is well-suited to the client's precontemplative stage.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the session as a joint exploration using language that frames conversation as mutual inquiry and explicitly avoids the expert trap. Could go deeper by more explicitly inviting the client to shape what happens next or set the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports the client's autonomy, never giving advice or implying the client should do something differently. Strong statements like 'you don't owe me more than that today' and 'I'm not here to change your mind' represent the highest level of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws out the client's own motivations and perspectives rather than providing reasons for change externally, using open and evocative questions. The questioning is well-crafted but could push further to deepen the client's own motivational language, particularly around autonomy and 'figuring things out on your own.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance throughout, positioning the client as the expert on his own experience and adjusting immediately when called out for trailing off. Could be deeper with more explicit power-sharing, such as negotiating the agenda or acknowledging the client's perspective takes priority over any clinical framework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk given the highly resistant, precontemplative nature of the client, eliciting ability, desire, and contemplation statements. Could go further by more systematically amplifying the client's change-relevant statements and asking follow-up questions that deepen the motivation language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles significant sustain talk well by acknowledging its validity without arguing or dismissing, and redirecting toward change without negating the sustain content. Could benefit from more strategic use of double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to help the client see a path beyond the sustain talk.
