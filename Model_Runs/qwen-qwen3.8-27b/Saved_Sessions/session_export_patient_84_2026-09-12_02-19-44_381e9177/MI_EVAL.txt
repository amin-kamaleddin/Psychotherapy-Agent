# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and communicates basic positive regard, but the acceptance is somewhat formulaic. It does not deeply affirm Malik’s peer belonging, autonomy, or identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects surface-level views and frustration about the mother, but misses deeper emotional and identity-related meanings. Empathy would be stronger if it explored what peers, respect, and cannabis use represent for Malik.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts from the target issue of cannabis use and ambivalence toward managing conflict with Malik’s mother. The primary target behavior is not consistently addressed or guided toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are attempts to collaborate by offering choices and co-creating a brief plan, but much of the interaction is therapist-driven. The psychiatrist frequently presents solutions and scripts rather than fully exploring Malik’s perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms Malik’s choice, offers options, and does not pressure him to quit cannabis. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by the frequent provision of therapist-generated scripts and plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with few effective attempts to elicit intrinsic motivation, values, or readiness for change. The psychiatrist does not strongly draw out or expand change talk about cannabis use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, but the interaction becomes expert-driven. Providing multiple lists, scripts, and structured plans reduces the sense of a client-led partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only weak attempts to evoke change talk, such as asking about downsides or weighing pros and cons. These are not followed up, and change talk remains mostly about conflict with the mother rather than cannabis use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Malik’s sustain talk, but does not effectively soften it. He does not consistently use double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or discrepancy development.
