# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates nonjudgmental, warm, and respectful regard without pressuring the client. The tone remains affirming even when the client emphasizes reasons to maintain the status quo.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially in capturing ambivalence and the client’s evolving self-narrative. Deeper emotional themes, such as fear of losing competence or identity, could be reflected more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior of coffee use. The counselor guides the conversation in a coherent, graduated sequence without drifting or becoming pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the counselor building directly on the client’s ideas and plans. The session feels co-constructed rather than expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently emphasizes choice, smallness, and self-determination. Language avoids coercion and keeps the decision firmly with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is effectively drawn from within the client rather than supplied by the counselor. Open, value-based questions elicit the client’s own reasons for change and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor positions the client as the expert on their own life and choices. Power is shared, and the client’s contributions shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully invites the client to generate reasons, plans, and confidence statements. Change talk is actively amplified and built upon throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively by acknowledging its validity and redirecting it toward curiosity and experimentation. The counselor avoids arguing or minimizing the client’s reasons for maintaining the habit.
