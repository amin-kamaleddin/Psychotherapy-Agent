# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor consistently validates the client and avoids shaming or labeling, even when she expresses shame or fear of failure. Warmth is supportive but remains somewhat task-focused and slightly evaluative in the closing praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture key behaviors and concerns, such as weekday drinking, unwinding, and fear of losing friends. Empathy is useful but often stays at the behavioral level rather than deeply exploring the underlying loneliness, social anxiety, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing weekday drinking and progresses from concerns to triggers to a concrete plan. Direction is strong, though it narrows quickly into behavioral planning more than linking back to the client's deeper values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites choices and adapts when the client rejects counting or strict rules. However, collaboration is uneven because the counselor supplies many options and structures much of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently supports choice, avoids shaming, and normalizes imperfect attempts. Autonomy support is occasionally weakened by prescriptive details and a fixed sequence of actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks useful questions and uses confidence scaling to encourage the client to articulate a higher level of confidence. Evocation is present but inconsistent because the counselor often provides options and solutions instead of drawing out the client's own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some collaborative language and lets the client choose plan elements. Partnership is limited by the counselor's frequent provision of options and the expert-like pacing of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses some change-related motivation, such as wanting to feel normal and trying the playlist step. The counselor elicits change talk but does not consistently deepen it through values, desires, or abilities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk and validates worries about social inclusion. It does not consistently redirect sustain talk through more complex reflections of ambivalence.
