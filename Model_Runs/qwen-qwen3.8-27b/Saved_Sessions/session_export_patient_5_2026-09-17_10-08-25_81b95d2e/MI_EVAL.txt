# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated respect, nonjudgment, and appreciation for Alessia’s careful, safety-focused approach. Acceptance was professional and supportive, but somewhat formal and behavior-focused rather than deeply warm or explicitly unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate, nuanced, and emotionally attuned, capturing Alessia’s concern, uncertainty, and desire to feel safe. The counselor consistently demonstrated strong perspective-taking and understanding of the functional meaning behind her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior: making an informed, safe decision about starting citalopram after phenelzine. The counselor continually guided the conversation toward clarity, actionable next steps, and the central clinical issue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and allowed Alessia’s ideas to shape the plan. However, the counselor introduced and structured much of the format, making the collaboration strong but still somewhat counselor-guided rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supported Alessia’s freedom of choice and reinforced that the decision was hers to make. There was no coercion, and options were offered in a way that respected her ownership and self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited motivation, concerns, and planning language from Alessia rather than simply providing information. Alessia generated the key content of the safety plan through open-ended, client-centered questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Alessia as the expert on her own decision-making process. Her input shaped the session, and the psychiatrist explicitly affirmed her ownership and collaborative role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited strong change talk about safety, clarity, verification, and self-direction. Some opportunities were missed to further amplify, consolidate, or explicitly elicit commitment/confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled Alessia’s uncertainty well, reframing concerns in an empowering way without reinforcing them as barriers. However, because sustain talk was limited, there was less opportunity to demonstrate the full range of softening skills.
