# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently maintains a nonjudgmental stance with multiple affirmations of the client's experience and graceful handling of pushback. Could be elevated by more explicit normalization of the client's ambivalence and warmer individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening that captures both content and emotional tone, often going beyond surface-level paraphrasing. Some reflections lean toward interpretation rather than pure attunement, and the session lacks moments of deeper emotional connection to underlying grief or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a coherent thread that builds logically from broad themes to specific anchors without wandering. Falls short of a clear strategic arc, as the session lacks a summarizing moment that ties themes together and identifies a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as shared exploration with inclusive language and genuinely follows the client's lead when he resists. Falls short by not negotiating the agenda upfront or explicitly inviting the client's goals, with the psychiatrist still doing most of the steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy through optional framing and never prescribes or implies the client should do something specific. Could reach a 5 by more explicitly affirming the client's ownership of any decisions or changes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes genuine efforts to evoke motivation from within the client with open, evocative questions. However, the evocation is passive and fails to consistently deepen or amplify the change talk that emerges, instead shifting to exploring barriers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with frequent invitations and avoids an expert-driven approach. Could be strengthened by more explicit acknowledgment of the client's expertise in his own life and more visible co-creation of the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some change talk but the psychiatrist doesn't consistently amplify, reflect back, or strategically deepen these moments. Responses to emerging change talk tend to pivot toward exploring barriers rather than reinforcing the client's own evidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without arguing, dismissing, or reinforcing hopelessness, and effectively addresses binary thinking without being dismissive. Could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly honor both sides of the client's ambivalence in a more structured way.
