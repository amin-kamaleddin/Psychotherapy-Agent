# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers supportive affirmations. Some framing feels slightly evaluative or assumes the issue before full disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows accurate reflective listening to Maya’s stress, coping, and fear of failure. Reflections could go deeper into underlying emotions and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stays focused on alcohol-related stress and builds a logical plan. Occasionally shifts toward broader safety/planning language that feels external to Maya’s low-effort preference.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maya influences the final plan, especially the playlist and low-pressure approach. The counselor still provides many options and structures much of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports Maya’s choice to try things her own way and respects her boundary around reminders and check-ins. Repeated invitations to plan or follow up create mild pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks useful questions that elicit coping ideas and desired change. Less effective at consistently evoking Maya’s own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative questions and some client-led choices. Overall remains fairly counselor-driven and directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits meaningful change talk about feeling less stuck and handling stress without alcohol. Does not consistently amplify, summarize, or build on these statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reframes fear of failure with experiment/non-pass-fail language. The added options and structure can make the plan feel like more burden rather than less.
