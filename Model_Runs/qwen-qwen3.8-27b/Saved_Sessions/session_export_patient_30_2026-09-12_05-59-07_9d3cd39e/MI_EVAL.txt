# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates David’s reluctance and competing life demands. The stance is warm but slightly task-oriented and could more explicitly affirm David’s values and emotional discomfort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately tracks David’s concerns and practical barriers with clear reflective listening. However, some reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional nuance such as pride, anxiety, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on David’s cholesterol management and medication decision. The conversation moves purposefully from ambivalence to a concrete plan, follow-up labs, and practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well on the note, meal options, and practical details, and David’s preferences clearly influence the session. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary driver of structure and content, limiting fully equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, respecting David’s decision to delay medication, and avoiding pressure. The plan is advice-rich and quite specific, which may subtly reduce the sense that the plan is fully David’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses rulers and evocative questions to elicit client language and move the session forward. Intrinsic motivation is present but limited because the psychiatrist supplies much of the plan, evidence, and practical details.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites David’s input and respects his preferences throughout the session. However, the early introduction of a detailed clinical plan gives the interaction an expert-driven flavor rather than a fully partner-led approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through questions about importance, evidence, and realistic changes. The session quickly shifts into problem-solving and advice-giving, which limits deeper development of David’s own change motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles David’s sustain talk well by acknowledging his concerns without arguing or framing them as resistance. Using more double-sided reflections could more effectively turn sustain talk into motivation.
