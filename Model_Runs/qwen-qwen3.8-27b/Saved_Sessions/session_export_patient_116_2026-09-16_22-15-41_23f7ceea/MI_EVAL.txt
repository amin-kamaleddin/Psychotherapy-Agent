# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoids judgment and pressure, meeting guardedness and medication dislike with curiosity and respect. Warmth is strong but mostly embedded in reflections rather than explicit positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong perspective-taking and accurately captures ambivalence, including the social meaning behind the client’s language. Deeper emotional concerns such as shame, stigma, and identity threat are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on medication adherence and progressively narrows from general burden to a concrete, client-generated plan. Momentum is maintained by consistently returning to the central issue of making medication fit the client’s life.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist co-constructs a plan around the client’s ideas and avoids imposing a prescriptive medical stance. The interaction could become more fully collaborative through more explicit shared agenda-setting and mutual problem-solving language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly honors the client’s right to choose, preserving uncertainty and limiting commitments to the client’s stated willingness. The stance consistently positions the client as the decision-maker about timing, conditions, and the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively elicits the client’s own ideas, preferences, and practical steps rather than supplying reasons for change. Evocation is strong behaviorally but less developed in drawing out deeper values, confidence, or identity-based motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently treated as the expert on his own life, and his contributions substantially shape the intervention. The therapist maintains a non-prescriptive, client-led stance without adopting an expert-driven posture.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections successfully generate practical change talk and movement toward a workable plan. The therapist could more consistently amplify, deepen, and connect change talk to values, confidence, and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Resistance and sustain talk are handled without argument, validation, or debate. Some language still subtly frames medication as medically necessary, which limits fully strategic redirection toward the client’s own reasons for change.
