# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Daniela’s efforts without blaming her for smoking, drinking, or childcare stress. The warmth is generally supportive but can feel somewhat task-focused and mildly evaluative, such as calling her choices “sensible.”

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate content reflections about Daniela’s stress, budget constraints, and prioritizing childcare. However, the responses often miss the deeper emotional undercurrents, such as feeling trapped, not wanting to sound like she is begging, or ambivalence about her sister.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reliable childcare and connects that goal to reducing smoking and alcohol use. It moves efficiently toward concrete next steps, though it can feel more like case management than a balanced motivational process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela influences the plan by choosing childcare first, suggesting the neighbor barter idea, and editing the message and voicemail. However, the psychiatrist still does much of the organizing by generating lists, options, scripts, and checklists, so the interaction is not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly asks permission, offers choices, and allows Daniela to decide what to do next, which strongly supports her sense of control. Some supportive statements, like “sensible” or “great plan,” are mildly directive and could subtly steer the client toward the clinician’s preferred choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks helpful opening questions and helps Daniela identify priorities and action steps. The evocation is mostly practical and does not fully draw out her deeper reasons for change, values, hopes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is evidence of partnership through invitations, choices, and Daniela’s input shaping the session. The psychiatrist also takes a directive role by introducing structured questions, options, and scripts, which can reduce the sense that the client is the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Daniela offers several important change statements, such as “it changes everything” and “I think I can handle tonight.” The psychiatrist often responds by moving into logistics rather than reflecting, expanding, or strengthening this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and does not reinforce Daniela’s barriers, including financial limits and her decision to keep the sister topic off the table. The responses generally acknowledge ambivalence, but they do not consistently use MI-style softening or double-sided reflections to explore it.
