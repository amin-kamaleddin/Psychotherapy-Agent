# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is not overtly judgmental and offers limited validation, but warmth and unconditional positive regard are inconsistent. The task-focused, prescriptive tone undercuts genuine acceptance of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few surface-level reflections of practical needs and frustration, but deeper emotions are missed. The psychiatrist does not adequately reflect stigma, embarrassment, identity concerns, or the client’s need to be seen as a student rather than an offender.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has a clear logistical focus on paperwork, release, and legal steps, but it is not aligned with the target behavior or change-related goals. It remains fixed on compliance and legal process rather than therapeutic direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The interaction is expert-led and directive rather than collaborative, with the psychiatrist supplying plans, scripts, and legal precautions. The client’s preferences and ideas are not meaningfully integrated, leading to increasing frustration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly directs what the client should ask, say, and document, minimizing the client’s sense of choice and self-direction. The repeated suggestion of reading a script further reduces autonomy and prompts resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is little effort to elicit the client’s own reasons for change, values, goals, or concerns. The session becomes advice-giving rather than drawing out the client’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves more like an authority giving instructions than a partner in exploration. The client’s contributions do not substantively shape the session, limiting genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is essentially no effort to evoke or develop change talk related to the client’s motivations, values, or goals. Relevant client statements are not built on or redirected into change-related discussion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not soften the client’s minimizing or dismissive language and instead escalates legal framing. This reinforces resistance rather than using reflection or reframing to reduce sustain talk.
