# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but warmth and positive regard remain somewhat procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is basic perspective-taking, but reflections often focus on facts rather than underlying feelings and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on safer drinking and harm reduction, though the progression is somewhat checklist-like.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evelyn meaningfully shapes parts of the plan, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the content and does not consistently treat her as the expert in her own life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and accepts some decisions, but continues adding recommendations after Evelyn states her own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is more educational than evocative, with limited use of open-ended or scaling questions to elicit internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evelyn influences the plan, but the psychiatrist remains the primary source of information and recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk appears, especially around the fall and water rule, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or build on it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation and normalizes wine, but does not strategically use reframes or double-sided reflections to connect sustain talk with change.
