# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses validating statements such as “That sounds really hard” and “Freezing makes sense,” but these are often followed quickly by advice or new suggestions. This makes acceptance feel present but somewhat transactional rather than fully sustained.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate empathic reflections about overwhelm, freezing, and the risk of being seen. However, the empathy tends to remain surface-level and is frequently followed by problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on managing social anxiety during photography work. The direction is consistent and relevant, though it is driven more by the clinician’s plan than by the client’s priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites some input, such as asking which option feels least scary. However, the client’s role is mostly to select from pre-defined interventions rather than to co-create the session’s direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Statements such as “No pressure to pick a day” acknowledge the client’s right to choose. Yet the repeated offering of new alternatives after each hesitation creates subtle pressure and can reduce the client’s sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies heavily on advice, tools, and behavioral options rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change. There is limited effort to amplify the client’s change talk or explore her internal resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some collaborative behavior by offering options and asking for preferences. However, the psychiatrist largely holds the plan and agenda, so the partnership remains somewhat superficial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Naomi offers some spontaneous change talk, such as hoping the cloth will help or expressing willingness to try the bag. The psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or explores this language to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses substantial hesitation, worry, and the sense that suggestions feel like homework. These responses are briefly acknowledged but are often met with more alternatives rather than being reflected, explored, or strategically softened.
