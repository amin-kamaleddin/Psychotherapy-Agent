# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids blaming the client for gambling, debt, or social choices. Approval is mostly supportive but slightly tied to progress, preventing a perfect 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are reasonable reflections of ambivalence, urges, and social concerns. However, the psychiatrist does not deeply validate the client's shame, self-criticism, or fear of looking foolish.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on gambling, debt, and concrete harm-reduction steps. It is purposeful and coherent, though somewhat task-driven and less exploratory than an ideal MI session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes ideas and chooses parts of the plan, such as the bank block, café choice, and check-in preference. The psychiatrist still leads with many structured options, making the collaboration less client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supports choice, respects the client's pace, and avoids assuming failure if she does not follow through. Some phrases still subtly emphasize performance or commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, especially around practical steps and the Egypt goal. It does not consistently deepen values, confidence, identity, or hope-based reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and builds on the client's ideas. The session still feels somewhat clinician-directed because of the heavy planning agenda and multiple suggested options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk around travel, debt, and not owing friends. The psychiatrist supports this but moves quickly into logistics without fully deepening the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with the client's reasons for continuing gambling and socializing. Ambivalence is sometimes addressed, but the session often shifts too quickly into problem-solving rather than exploring the sustaining reasons.
