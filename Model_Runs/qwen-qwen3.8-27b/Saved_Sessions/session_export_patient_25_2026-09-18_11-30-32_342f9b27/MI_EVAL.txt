# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, genuine warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout the session. The psychiatrist never judges, labels, or minimizes Daniel's concerns, and the closing exchange demonstrating deep positive regard is particularly moving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with complex, non-repetitive reflections that progressively deepen. Key examples include the 'swap' reflection and the dual-tension reflection that track Daniel's values in real-time.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an excellent, consistent focus on alcohol use in social contexts with a clear progressive arc. Each question and reflection builds on the previous one with clear momentum toward deeper self-understanding and a natural resolution.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative stance with conversational, equal tone and active seeking of Daniel's input. However, the psychiatrist does the majority of the talking while Daniel provides shorter responses, limiting evidence of true co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Daniel's agency and autonomy, explicitly crediting self-discovery ('That's your own insight, not something I gave you'). Never prescribes or tells Daniel what to do, think, or what level of change to aim for.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist's strongest skill, consistently drawing out Daniel's own motivations, values, and insights without providing information or advice. The final exchange where Daniel articulates his own insight is a masterclass in MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong partnership stance with client-centered language and avoids an expert-driven approach. However, Daniel is somewhat more passive, largely responding to the psychiatrist's prompts rather than independently steering the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits meaningful change talk including values, desire, and capability statements. Could be more strategic by systematically exploring confidence, ability, or amplifying key moments of change talk more explicitly using DARN-CAT.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles the mild sustain talk adequately by acknowledging both sides, but does not employ more sophisticated techniques such as double-sided reflections, strategic value exploration, or reframing. The opportunity for a higher score is somewhat limited by the mild nature of the sustain talk itself.
