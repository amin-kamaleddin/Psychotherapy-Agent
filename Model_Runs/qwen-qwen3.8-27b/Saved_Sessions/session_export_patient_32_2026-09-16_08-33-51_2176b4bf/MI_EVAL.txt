# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and used strengths-based affirmations. Acceptance was somewhat task-focused and did not fully convey unconditional positive regard independent of the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and captured multiple layers of ambivalence. Empathy was stronger cognitively than emotionally, with limited exploration of deeper shame, fear, or grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on reducing DUI risk and moved coherently through goals, confidence, barriers, and implementation. There was no significant drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with Derrick selecting and owning the final plan. Decisions were co-constructed rather than prescribed, and the psychiatrist used partnership-focused language and questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Derrick’s choice, self-reliance, and control over the plan. He avoided pushing when Derrick resisted rehearsal and framed the plan in terms of Derrick’s ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out Derrick’s motivations, practical steps, and confidence effectively. At times, however, scripts and tactics were offered before fully eliciting Derrick’s own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a collaborative stance and allowed Derrick’s input to shape the direction and final plan. The psychiatrist occasionally became more expert-driven by offering multiple scripts and tactical suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked practical change talk and helped Derrick’s confidence increase from 6 to 8. Change talk was strengthened with affirmations but could have been deepened further through values, identity, or amplified client language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with sustain talk and generally kept the focus on risk reduction. He did not directly soften or reflect minimization such as “it’s just beer,” nor consistently pivot it into change-oriented dialogue.
