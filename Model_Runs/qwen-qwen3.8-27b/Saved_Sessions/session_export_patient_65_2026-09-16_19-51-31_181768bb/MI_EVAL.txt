# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating language that normalizes Julian's concerns. The stance is supportive but somewhat procedural, without deeper individualized affirmation of his struggle or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Julian's practical concerns, need for structure, and desire to avoid mistakes. Empathy remains focused on logistics rather than fully exploring the emotional meaning behind his anxiety, hesitation, or court-mandated ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior: completing court-mandated community service hours before the deadline. The psychiatrist effectively moves the conversation from vague uncertainty to a concrete next step, sending a text to the case worker.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, but the interaction remains largely counselor-led. The transcript does not clearly show Julian's ideas actively shaping the session or a plan being genuinely co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supports Julian's ownership, choice, and preference for a low-effort plan. However, the psychiatrist still subtly steers toward a specific solution, such as texting tonight from his room, without explicitly emphasizing that Julian is free to choose differently or set his own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks Julian to generate practical next steps rather than giving direct advice. Evocation remains limited because it focuses on logistics rather than drawing out his values, confidence, personal reasons for change, or sense of capability.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Julian as the person who knows his own needs and preferences, using questions that invite his perspective. The partnership is strong but not fully power-sharing, since the psychiatrist remains the primary questioner and agenda-shaper.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session mainly focuses on problem-solving and arranging a concrete action step rather than evoking change talk. There is limited exploration of Julian's desire, need, ability, values, benefits, confidence, or commitment to completing the service hours.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing avoidance, instead framing low-effort preferences as understandable needs for structure and predictability. The score is limited because there is little visible sustain talk to soften and no clear use of double-sided reflections or strategic redirection.
