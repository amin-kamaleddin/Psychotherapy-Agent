# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a warm, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client's ambivalence without pressure. Acceptance occasionally tilts toward gentle approval tied to trying or changing rather than fully unconditional value.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses clear reflective listening and accurately captures the tension between social connection and health concerns. Empathy could deepen by exploring the implied shame, fear of rejection, and need for belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on smoking in the context of social breaks and ends with a specific, relevant plan. Direction would be stronger if the experiment were more explicitly linked to broader change goals or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed and the client's choices meaningfully shape the session. Collaboration would be enhanced by eliciting more of the client's own problem-solving ideas before offering options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly reinforces the client's right to choose, set the pace, and decline check-ins. Autonomy support would be further strengthened by offering fewer behavioral directives and more open invitations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions, but the session moves quickly into suggestions and behavioral experiments. More use of importance/confidence scaling, values exploration, or decisional balance would better develop internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a collaborative surface and asks for input, but also takes on a noticeable advisor/coach role. The session would be stronger if more client-generated ideas were explored before offering solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some mild change talk, but does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen it. There are missed opportunities to expand on the client's own reasons, desires, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges the client's reasons to keep smoking without arguing or reinforcing them directly. More skillful double-sided reflections and reframing would better soften sustain talk and open space for change.
