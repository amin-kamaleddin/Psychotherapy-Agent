# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, including when the client self-labels as 'lazy' and doubles down on sustain talk. The psychiatrist validates rather than corrects, maintaining a warm and nonjudgmental stance at every turn.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that capture emotional nuance, such as hearing the 'for now' in 'we're good' and naming the paradox of simplicity feeling like a cage. Reflections go well beyond paraphrasing to demonstrate genuine perspective-taking and emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target behavior with logical progression from ambivalence to specific barriers to envisioning change. The session could benefit from a stronger movement toward a concrete action step or commitment level by the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue where the client's exact words and ideas shape the session direction, with reflections responsive to what was just said rather than pre-planned. No external models or frameworks are imposed without the client's buy-in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary reinforcement of the client's ownership and right to choose, explicitly honoring the 'not yet' commitment without trying to upgrade it. No directive language ('you should,' 'you need to') appears anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is elicited from within the client through strategic questions rather than externally provided reasons for change. The psychiatrist consistently evokes the client's own resources, vision, and self-knowledge as the primary drivers of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the expert on his own life, with the psychiatrist inviting his interpretations rather than providing them. Explicit acknowledgment that the client's self-knowledge is the valuable resource that no one else can provide.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of strategic questions to elicit and amplify change talk, including the powerful 'What do you make of that contradiction?' intervention. Could be stronger in explicitly drawing out DARN-CAT statements, particularly commitment and confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook handling of sustain talk through double-sided reflections that acknowledge the feeling while gently highlighting contradictions without being adversarial. Every instance of sustain talk is met with understanding and redirection rather than confrontation.
