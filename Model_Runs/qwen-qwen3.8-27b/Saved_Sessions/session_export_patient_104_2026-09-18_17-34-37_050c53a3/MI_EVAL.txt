# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates genuine, specific unconditional positive regard, reframing Nadine's self-critical statements as evidence of dedication rather than deficiency. Warmth is tied to concrete observations and never formulaic or judgmental.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex and multi-layered, capturing both content and emotional subtext (e.g., holding the dual need for reassurance and information simultaneously). Empathic responses are consistently specific, emotionally nuanced, and never generic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session flows logically from emotional validation to information provision to self-awareness to a concrete self-generated action step, with each question building on the last. The transition from information-giving to the action-oriented 'what do you need' question could have been more explicitly framed as a bridge toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses inclusive language, checks in for alignment, and co-constructs the final plan with Nadine. Collaboration dips slightly during information-providing segments where the psychiatrist takes on a more educator role, though this is responsive to the client's request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never prescribes or says 'you should,' and consistently returns authority to Nadine's interpretation and voice. The final five-minute plan emerges entirely from Nadine's own words, and her self-set boundaries are respected without being pushed further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Powerful evocative questions ('what do you need in that moment?', 'if no one was going to judge you, what would you tell yourself?') draw out Nadine's authentic self-observations about her body being braced. Evocation could be more systematic throughout the session rather than concentrating in the second half.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong collaborative stance, explicitly asking for Nadine's input on format, pacing, and direction throughout. The dynamic shifts toward an expert-teacher role during the developmental information and witching hour explanation, though clearly framed as responsive to the client's request.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk, particularly in the later session, drawing out Nadine's own language about needing to sit, drink water, and give herself permission to be tired. Reinforcement and amplification of these change talk statements could be more consistent and systematic across the entire session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk by acknowledging the weight without reinforcing despair, holding dual perspectives (e.g., 'both are true'), and gently redirecting without arguing or dismissing. The sustain talk around 'I'm failing her' could have been addressed with a slightly more active double-sided reflection earlier in the session.
