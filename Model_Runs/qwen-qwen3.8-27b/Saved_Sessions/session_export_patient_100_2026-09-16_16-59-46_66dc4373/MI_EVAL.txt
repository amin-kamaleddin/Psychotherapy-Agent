# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and affirming stance throughout the session. They avoid moralizing the client’s alcohol use and validate her perspective without challenging her statement that she does not want to quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately mirrors the client’s ambivalence and practical concerns. However, some emotionally charged themes, such as shame and fear of being seen as the “girl who messed up,” are only indirectly addressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and appropriate focus on alcohol use and its impact on school performance, varsity running, and future opportunities. The logical progression from broad concern to a concrete behavioral target is a strength, with only a minor loss of momentum when the clinician asks again about setting a cue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with the clinician treating the client as an equal partner and using inclusive, invitational language. The collaboration feels slightly uneven when the clinician asks a similar question after the client already offers a specific plan for a silent alarm.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent and consistent, with the clinician emphasizing the client’s freedom, ownership, and right to make her own choices. The clinician respects the client’s explicit preference not to quit and avoids pushing toward abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently elicits motivation from within the client rather than supplying reasons to change. The client is the primary generator of the plan, which reflects skillful and effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician generally conveys a collaborative, non-expert stance and invites the client’s input throughout the session. The partnership is slightly weakened when the clinician fails to explicitly build on the client’s specific contribution regarding the silent alarm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is one of the strongest parts of the session, with the clinician consistently eliciting and helping the client expand her own change-related language. The client’s statements become increasingly concrete, reflecting desire, need, and ability for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician handles sustain talk well by not arguing with the client’s reasons to maintain the status quo and instead using double-sided language. A stronger response might have more explicitly explored the tension between the client’s desire to “keep the vibe” and the consequences of drinking.
