# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and uses normalizing language, though it is somewhat formulaic and does not fully acknowledge deeper fears.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Erik’s ambivalence and practical tensions, but the reflections remain mostly cognitive and repetitive rather than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused and efficiently moves to a concrete plan, but it largely drops the diet component and relies heavily on labs and external medical proof.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructs a realistic plan around Erik’s own ideas, but still acts as the primary organizer by offering many structured options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently offers choices and avoids pressure, but could more explicitly reinforce Erik’s ownership and shift motivation toward personal values.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some self-generated planning and problem-solving, but often uses multiple-choice options rather than deeply eliciting Erik’s values, confidence, or identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Erik’s input and builds the plan around his preferences, but the agenda remains somewhat clinician-led and prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik produces useful change talk, but the clinician does not consistently deepen it or fully elicit Erik’s own reasons, abilities, and needs.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with sustain talk but does not consistently pivot it toward Erik’s personal values, competence, or relationships.
