# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional, genuine warmth and nonjudgmental stance throughout; the psychiatrist never reacts with disapproval to Jamal's resistance or minimization. Affirmations feel individualized rather than formulaic, including the powerful 'you can be at your worst here too if you ever want to.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong accurate reflective listening, particularly in capturing the school vs. park contrast and the emotional weight of constant performance. Could be elevated by more deeply exploring the loneliness and fear underlying Jamal's pain at school.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on building rapport and self-awareness with appropriate pacing despite resistance. A brief, gentle connection to the specific behavioral target (marijuana) could have slightly strengthened directional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as a joint process between equals, following Jamal's lead and adapting to his boundaries. The closing exchange offers a reflection but explicitly frames it as optional, maintaining balanced power dynamics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support with explicit language of choice, non-coercion, and freedom from obligation throughout. Never pressures or uses covert control tactics; the closing 'if you want to' is a powerful affirmation of future agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions elicit meaningful self-reflection, particularly 'what's the difference between Jamal at the park and Jamal at school?' Could be stronger by using scaling questions and more fully developing the change talk that emerges.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional partnership established from the opening and maintained throughout; the psychiatrist consistently positions Jamal as the expert on his own life. Never assumes expertise or takes over the conversation, even when Jamal pushes for closure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective evocative moves plant seeds for change, and Jamal does produce meaningful change talk. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or build on these moments, and there is limited elicitation of confidence or commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively without confrontation, arguing, or reinforcement; the psychiatrist acknowledges and then gently pivots. Could use more strategic double-sided reflections when Jamal repeatedly pushes for the signing part.
