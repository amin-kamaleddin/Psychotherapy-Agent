# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and avoids judgment, but the stance feels more procedural than warm. Acceptance is limited to compliance-related validation rather than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture some of Kieran’s boredom and emptiness but remain mostly surface-level. There is a mild misattunement when labeling the experience as painful, and strong metaphors are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear MI agenda or target behavior. Discussion drifts into general venting and safety checking without anchoring to a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and respects choices, but still directs much of the session through options and safety checks. There is limited evidence of true equal partnership or co-created focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Kieran’s refusal of tools and allows him to stop, which supports autonomy. However, repeated safety checks and structured options can feel directive and limit explicit client control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open questions elicit some client language, but not change-related motivation, values, or confidence. Evocation focuses more on frustration and numbness than on reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a basic collaborative stance, but the psychiatrist frequently controls the session through safety checks and menus. Kieran is not consistently positioned as the expert on his own experience and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little effort to build client-generated motivation for change. The session mostly sustains resignation without using MI-style questions to explore desire, ability, values, or possible change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with sustain talk, but also does not strategically redirect or explore ambivalence. Kieran’s statements about futility remain dominant rather than being gently balanced.
