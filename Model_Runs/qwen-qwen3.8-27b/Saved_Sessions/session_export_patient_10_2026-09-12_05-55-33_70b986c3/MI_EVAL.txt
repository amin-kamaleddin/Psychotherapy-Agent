# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s choices and constraints. Acceptance is somewhat brief and task-focused, with limited deeper affirmation of the client’s worth or parenting stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist accurately reflects frustration, stress, and the challenge of balancing childcare with job-seeking. However, empathy remains surface-level and often shifts quickly into problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on improving job-seeking behavior despite childcare responsibilities. The therapist consistently guides the conversation toward concrete, actionable next steps with little drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist works collaboratively, and the client’s preferences meaningfully shape the plan. The main limitation is that the therapist introduces many specific tools and scripts before fully exploring the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist strongly supports autonomy by affirming the client’s control, offering choices, and respecting her decisions. Some therapist-generated solutions may reduce opportunities for the client to generate her own options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist begins by eliciting the client’s values, motivations, and first-step ideas. However, the session moves quickly into providing scripts and solutions, limiting further evocation of the client’s own reasons, confidence, and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist demonstrates a genuine collaborative stance and invites the client’s input throughout the session. The main limitation is occasional therapist-led offering of specific options and scripts before fully drawing out the client’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist elicits some change talk about the client’s desire for income, not feeling stuck, and ability to try a small step. However, the therapist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or reflect the client’s own change-relevant statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or dismissing it. The main limitation is that sustain talk is not consistently turned toward the client’s change goals, and the focus remains somewhat on barriers.
