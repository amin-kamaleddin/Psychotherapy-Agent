# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout. The warmth is somewhat task-focused and formulaic, limiting deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture the client's perspective at the content level but often miss the emotional texture of his experience. The empathic effort is adequate but not highly nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on the burden of mandated services and forms. However, it becomes overly task-focused and lacks a clear plan or momentum toward more engaged participation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and respects his preferences in a largely collaborative way. Collaboration is somewhat passive and does not fully co-create a meaningful broader plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through noncoercive language and respect for the client's desire to sign and leave. This support is mostly tied to the immediate administrative task rather than broader self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to elicit the client's preferences, but the evocation remains superficial and procedural. Repetitive questioning limits deeper exploration of values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and treats the client as the expert on his own experience. The partnership is mainly confined to administrative problem-solving rather than a shared agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Attempts to evoke change-related language are limited and mostly logistical. There is a missed opportunity to build on the client's statements about wanting the experience to feel less pointless.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely follows the client's strong sustain talk instead of gently softening or redirecting it. The session remains anchored in the status quo without using double-sided reflections or strategic pivots.
