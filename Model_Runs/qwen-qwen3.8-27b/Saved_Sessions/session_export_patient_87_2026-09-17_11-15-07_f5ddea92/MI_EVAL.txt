# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance with no pathologizing language or prescriptive statements. The psychiatrist pauses and sits with Marco's vulnerability (e.g., 'I want to sit with that') rather than rushing past it, demonstrating genuine unconditional positive regard throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, nuanced, and progressively deepening—capturing emotional undercurrents rather than just paraphrasing content. The discriminating question ('Does it feel like the week is lost, or like *you* are lost?') exemplifies complex reflection that opens new dimensions of Marco's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical arc from surface-level ambivalence to deeper values to a concrete behavioral strategy, all connected to the dietary/weight loss goal. Minor deduction for not explicitly tying the walking strategy back to the specific dietary behaviors Marco needs to modify.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration with consistent use of 'I wonder if' and 'I'm curious' language. The session direction shifts based on Marco's responses, and key themes (invisible, 7pm moment, shoes) emerged collaboratively rather than from a clinician-led agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No 'should,' 'need to,' or 'you must' language is used; the psychiatrist consistently positions solutions as Marco's own (e.g., 'That's not a strategy I gave you — that's you'). The session ends with Marco exploring the idea rather than being pressured to commit to a specific plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A series of strategically timed questions consistently elicit change talk from within Marco rather than providing external reasons for change. Change talk progresses from tentative ('I guess so') to strong value-based statements ('I'm tired of feeling invisible') to concrete behavioral plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Marco as the expert on his own life and never provides advice, prescriptions, or solutions. Every question is open and exploratory, and the session direction is shaped by Marco's responses rather than a predetermined agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of strategic questions to evoke Marco's values, abilities, and self-efficacy, with explicit amplification of client-generated insights (e.g., 'That's not a diet goal — that's *you*'). The 'guy in shoes' vs. 'trying person' contrast question masterfully evokes contrast change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never challenged, argued with, or reinforced—instead it is validated and gently redirected through curiosity about underlying patterns. The psychiatrist transforms fixed barriers into examinable processes Marco can influence, using the 'guy in shoes' metaphor to bypass all-or-nothing thinking.
