# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating the client’s goal of safer weekends rather than imposing abstinence. Warmth was strong but somewhat brief, with fewer explicit affirmations of the client’s broader worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client’s frustration, need for control, and concern about judgment, though reflections stayed mostly cognitive rather than deeply emotional. More exploration of underlying shame or fear would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on safer methamphetamine use and moved from broad concerns to concrete harm-reduction steps. The target behavior could have been named and consolidated earlier to strengthen momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used menu-style questions and allowed the client’s preferences to shape the plan, but some content was still clinician-generated rather than co-created. More initial client-led ideation would improve collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was given repeated choices and was not pressured to quit, including respect for not drafting a text in the room. Explicit statements that the client can choose any part or none of the plan would further strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited some client-generated ideas, but the psychiatrist offered practical options before fully exploring the client’s own reasons, values, and confidence. The session would benefit from deeper evocation of motivations and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and respected the client’s decisions, making the interaction feel shared rather than directive. The session remains partly coach-like because the clinician generated much of the initial content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirmed some practical change talk, such as the client’s plan to use test strips and bail if pushed. Systematic elicitation of desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment was still limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled the client’s resistance by accepting the 'safer weekends, not quitting' frame rather than arguing against it. More advanced two-sided reflections could further soften sustain talk.
