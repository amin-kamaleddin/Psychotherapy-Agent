# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates respect and positive regard, with a strong opening affirmation connecting Tariq's behavior to his character. The repeated generic 'I understand' responses prevent this from reaching the highest level of individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions, particularly regarding the phone loss and family connection. The repeated vague 'I understand' prompts and quick pivot from Tariq's fear about the future prevent consistent depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation has a reasonable flow but lacks a clear strategic arc, with abrupt transitions and the generic 'I understand' responses providing no momentum. The final 'one thing' intervention is a good directional move but feels therapist-driven rather than emerging naturally.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive framing like 'what if we started with one thing.' The repetitive generic responses and therapist-offered structure slightly undermine evidence that Tariq's ideas are genuinely shaping direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** No pressure, directives, or 'you should' language throughout; consistently invites Tariq to generate his own answers through open questions. Tariq is clearly positioned as the decision-maker in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently asks Tariq to identify his own motivations, fears, and resources rather than providing them. Questions like 'what would normal look like' and 'what have you built' effectively elicit self-generated insight.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a generally collaborative stance with inclusive language and allows Tariq to lead much of the conversation. Does not assume the expert role, though some responses feel more managing than partnering.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes desire, reasons, and ability talk, with a strong question about what Tariq has already built. Could have more consistently reinforced and expanded each piece of change talk as it emerges rather than moving on.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles blame narrative well with a double-sided reflection acknowledging legitimate frustration while noting Tariq's own accountability. The sustain talk about being 'punished' and comparing to other guys is not actively addressed or redirected toward agency.
