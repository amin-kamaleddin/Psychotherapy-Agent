# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Elise’s reasons without moralizing. Acceptance feels supportive but somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Elise’s ambivalence and social concerns. Empathy would be stronger with deeper exploration of identity-related fear and social exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and the specific social bonding concern. Direction is coherent, though it could more consistently tie steps to Elise’s values and change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise is invited to choose options and rehearse behavior, creating a collaborative process. The interaction remains expert-driven because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor affirms choice, uses low-stakes reversible steps, and offers options rather than commands. Frequent advising and scripting can shift the interaction toward directive problem-solving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurs through open questions and confidence scaling. Much of the session is driven by clinician-provided information and solutions rather than eliciting Elise’s intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and offers choices, supporting partnership. The interaction frequently shifts to an expert-driven model where the counselor dominates the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise produces some change talk and chooses a concrete experiment. The counselor often shifts to advising and script-building instead of consistently amplifying Elise’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, reducing defensiveness. The counselor could more effectively use double-sided reflections and pivot toward resolving ambivalence.
