# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts Hana’s ambivalence and anxiety without pressure. However, the acceptance is somewhat functional and formulaic, lacking fully expert-level warmth and affirmation of Hana’s worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and capture ambivalence, somatic experience, and emerging meaning. However, early responses miss new information, and deeper shame, self-doubt, and fear of judgment are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the exam-related target behavior and progressively narrows the goal toward a small practice step. However, the closing direction is incomplete because the plan is not summarized, readiness is not checked, and commitment is not consolidated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and follows Hana’s lead, resulting in a jointly created practice plan. However, collaboration remains mostly implicit, with limited explicit agenda-setting or negotiation of priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana’s choice, control, and freedom from pressure are consistently emphasized through options such as stopping, privacy, and self-selected cues. However, her ultimate freedom to decide and ownership of both trying and not trying are not made fully explicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Hana’s own motivations, strategies, and past competences rather than giving advice. However, evocation is mainly behavioral and does not deeply explore values, identity, confidence, or readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana largely leads the content and proposes most elements of the plan, supporting a collaborative partnership. However, some counselor-led framing remains, and her expertise over her own decisions is not explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana generates substantial change-oriented language and concrete low-pressure steps. However, the psychiatrist does not systematically develop change talk across desire, ability, reasons, need, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally redirected rather than challenged, moving from fear toward manageable action. However, double-sided reflections of ambivalence are not always explicit, and some self-doubt remains unaddressed.
