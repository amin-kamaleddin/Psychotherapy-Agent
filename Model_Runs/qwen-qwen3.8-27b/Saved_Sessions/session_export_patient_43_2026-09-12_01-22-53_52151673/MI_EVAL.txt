# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with statements such as “I hear you” and “no pressure to change.” Warmth was adequate but somewhat procedural when the session shifted to creating a script for the client’s wife.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate reflections of the client’s guarded stance and the pressure from others. Reflections were helpful but remained surface-level and did not fully explore deeper emotions or values related to ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session began focused on smoking and health concerns but drifted toward interpersonal conflict with the wife. The target behavior was not consistently maintained or guided toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options and incorporated client preferences, especially when selecting a response for the wife. Collaboration was limited because the psychiatrist supplied scripts rather than co-creating solutions with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized lack of pressure and respected the client’s refusal to experiment or rehearse. Autonomy support was strong, though slightly weakened by providing scripted lines and a specific alternative phrase.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Used some open questions but did not skillfully evoke change talk through rulers, values, or exploration of the cough. The client’s own reasons for change were not developed or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited client input and allowed choices to influence the session. Partnership became uneven when the psychiatrist adopted an advisory role by generating multiple scripts and framing the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made limited attempts to build on the client’s minimal change talk, such as references to the cough or possible future quitting. The psychiatrist shifted toward options and advice instead of deepening the client’s own arguments for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoided confrontation with the client’s sustain talk, which was appropriate, but did not effectively redirect it toward change. The response to the client’s request for a “line” risked reinforcing avoidance rather than exploring discrepancy.
