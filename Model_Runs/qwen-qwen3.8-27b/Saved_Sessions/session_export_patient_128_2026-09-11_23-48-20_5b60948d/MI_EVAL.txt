# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys acceptance and nonjudgment, normalizing Tyler’s nervousness and avoiding harsh labeling. It is not a 5 because the warmth is pragmatic rather than deeply affirming, with missed opportunities to validate his courage and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate empathy for Tyler’s practical concerns and desire to stay social without risk. However, the psychiatrist does not fully reflect underlying self-doubt, shame, or peer-pressure concerns before returning to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related risk and avoiding another citation. The direction is behavior-specific but remains narrow on logistical harm reduction rather than broader values-based change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, especially when Tyler modifies the reminder label and exit text. However, much of the session is counselor-led because the psychiatrist frequently presents structured options before exploring Tyler’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with repeated invitations for choice and acceptance of Tyler’s realistic limits. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still provides many strategies and drafts before fully eliciting Tyler’s own preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some evocation efforts by asking what Tyler wants to work on and which strategies feel doable. However, evocation remains limited because many solutions and reasons are supplied before deeper client-generated motivations are explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and offers choices, showing some partnership. The interaction becomes uneven when the psychiatrist frequently supplies lists and plans, reducing the sense that Tyler is the expert on his own options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some relevant change talk, such as not wanting another citation or expulsion, and asks for client input on strategies. However, there is limited use of targeted questions to amplify or deepen desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with or moralizing about Tyler’s sustain talk. However, sustain talk is only partially acknowledged and not consistently softened through double-sided reflections or discrepancy development.
