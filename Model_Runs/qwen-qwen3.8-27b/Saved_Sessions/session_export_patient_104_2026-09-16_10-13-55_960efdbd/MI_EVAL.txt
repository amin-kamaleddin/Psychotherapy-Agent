# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing toward fatigue, fear, and parental self-doubt. Acceptance is strong but occasionally feels more procedural than deeply emotionally individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and supportive, especially early in the session. However, empathy often stays content-focused and moves quickly into advice or planning without fully exploring the emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on sleep, reassurance, and a practical safety/soothing plan. Focus is clear and appropriate, with only minor drift into administrative details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician collaborates well on the practical one-page plan and invites client input on format, contacts, and wording. Collaboration is strongest in logistics and less present in motivational or conceptual direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, respects limits, and supports choice throughout the session. Using Nadine’s own calming line is a strong autonomy-supportive move.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the weakest area, with many closed or practical questions. The session relies more on providing information and scripts than drawing out Nadine’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partnership in inviting preferences and shaping the plan, but the interaction remains partly expert-driven. The client is not consistently positioned as the primary expert on her baby and household context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nadine produces some change-relevant language, but the clinician mostly asks practical, behavioral questions. The session provides more of a plan than it elicits deeper client motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reduces pressure and softens concerns without arguing or reinforcing sustain talk. However, there is less consistent use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots that transform ambivalence into stronger change motivation.
