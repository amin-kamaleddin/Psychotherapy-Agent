# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Ethan's choices. Warmth is generally strong but remains somewhat procedural and does not consistently normalize ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate double-sided reflections capture the tension between social fit and negative consequences. Empathy is inconsistent and misses deeper feelings around fear of ridicule, labeling, homework, and social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and moves toward a concrete low-risk plan for a future hangout. Focus is somewhat diluted by repeated safety information and plan summaries.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses joint language and allows Ethan to shape the plan, including the look-alike cup and cover story. Collaboration is strong but lessened by repeated advice-giving and lists of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ethan's choice is repeatedly respected, especially his refusal of scripts, check-ins, and extra safety planning. Some repeated safety instructions feel advisory unless framed more explicitly as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some initial change talk about avoiding trouble and not wanting to throw up. Evocation becomes limited later as the session shifts toward advice and safety instruction.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Ethan's input and adapts to his preferences, keeping the plan collaborative. It is not a 5 because the clinician still takes an advisory role in offering options and safety content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and affirms some change talk around lower-risk alternatives and wanting to avoid sickness. Change talk is not consistently deepened or expanded with scaling, summary, or values-based follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Ethan's sustain talk and uses some double-sided reflections. Softening is present but not consistently active enough to redirect sustain talk toward values or goals.
