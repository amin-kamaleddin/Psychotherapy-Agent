# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Elena’s mixed feelings and concerns. The safety screen is clinically appropriate but somewhat procedural, which slightly limits warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately acknowledges Elena’s worry, conflict, and fear, but reflections are often brief and reassurance-focused. Deeper reflections about the tension between protecting her daughter and avoiding unnecessary suffering would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on vaccine concerns, risks, and practical next steps for Elena’s daughter. Early clarification of the target behavior and more precise age-specific framing would improve focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative in inviting preferences, options, and role-play, and Elena’s responses shape the conversation. However, the psychiatrist still supplies much of the content, so a more fully collaborative approach would elicit her own questions and wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids pressure and offers multiple choices, but could more explicitly affirm Elena’s ownership of the decision. Providing scripts is useful, yet stronger autonomy language would make the stance more client-centered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some evocation in the opening question and role-play process, but the session relies heavily on psychoeducation and scripted practice. More evocative questions about values, importance, confidence, and desired outcomes would better elicit Elena’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance by asking for preferences, offering options, and inviting rehearsal. Greater partnership would involve more explicitly inviting Elena’s own beliefs, decision-making style, and priorities before providing detailed expert content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena expresses some change talk, such as wanting to protect her daughter and preparing to ask follow-up questions. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or reinforce this language with evocative follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with nonjudgmental reframes rather than argument or dismissal. However, the conversation remains heavily focused on risks and side effects, and there is limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic redirection toward change.
