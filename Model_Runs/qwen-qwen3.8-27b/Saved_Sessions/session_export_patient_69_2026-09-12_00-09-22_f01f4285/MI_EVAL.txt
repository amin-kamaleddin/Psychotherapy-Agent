# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of the client’s frustration without labeling him resistant. The stance is somewhat procedural, and repeated safety statements feel more like risk management than deep acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at a surface level, such as naming the day as exhausting and frustrating. However, the psychiatrist does not fully explore the client’s mandated experience, loss of choice, or helplessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with an appropriate focus on engagement and a possible small change. It quickly drifts into administrative e-signature logistics and repeated safety reminders, weakening therapeutic direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites the client’s preferences, showing intent to collaborate. Collaboration is uneven because the clinician continues directing the process after the client states he can handle the task independently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering options, allowing the client to stop, and stating respect for the client’s choices. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated instructions and monitoring after the client says he does not need guidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited to initial open questions and a brief prompt to consider one small change. The psychiatrist does not explore the client’s values, goals, or what might make the mandated process feel less burdensome.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session includes partnership through options and invitations for the client to choose. It becomes less collaborative when the psychiatrist provides detailed step-by-step instruction despite the client’s stated competence and desire to finish.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only minimal attempts to evoke change talk, such as suggesting one small, manageable change. The client’s brief statements about handling the task are not reflected or expanded into stronger intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce the client’s sustain talk but largely avoids it by shifting to logistics. Ambivalence between external requirements and internal resistance is not strategically reflected or explored.
