# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and appreciation for Derrick’s effort and realistic planning. The main limitation is that warmth sometimes feels more like praise for problem-solving than deeper validation of Derrick’s emotional ambivalence and social pressures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong perspective-taking, especially around Derrick’s competing values around his license, job, and social life. The empathy is mostly cognitive and practical, with less exploration of underlying feelings such as shame, fear, belonging, or independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on preventing a repeat DUI by addressing drinking, driving risk, and peer pressure. The direction is strong but becomes somewhat checklist-like, with repeated quick checks and structured options that limit more natural, client-led momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by letting Derrick shape the plan, including the cash backup, one-tap rideshare, exit line, and Marcus holding his keys. The interaction is still somewhat therapist-managed because the psychiatrist frequently initiates the next step and offers specific choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Derrick’s choice by normalizing a harm-reduction approach and respecting his refusal of reminders or scripts. Some phrasing still carries subtle directive pressure, such as asking whether he will commit to specific steps or offering step-by-step help.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Derrick’s own reasons for change, such as protecting his license, job, and avoiding jail. The evocation is mostly practical and behavior-focused, with limited exploration of deeper values, identity, or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance by asking Derrick what he wants to change and inviting him to select the most doable options. The session could feel more co-led by giving Derrick more control over the agenda and less reliance on therapist-generated prompts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits desire, need, ability, reason, and commitment language through rating scales and planning questions. The change talk is effective but mainly tactical and harm-avoidance-focused, rather than deeply connected to Derrick’s values or identity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Derrick’s sustain talk and reframes it as protecting what matters without quitting his life. There is less use of more strategic MI moves, such as double-sided reflections, to fully address the peer-pressure and “buzzkill” ambivalence.
