# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistently non-judgmental, warm, and respectful stance throughout the session. The explicit statement that the role was to listen, not judge, helped create a safe space despite the client's initial defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client's ambivalence between the relief of drug use and its relational costs. The score is slightly reduced because the client's exhaustion was not fully validated before moving toward problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the target behavior and its impact on the client's stated concern. The conversation moved logically from assessment to exploring ambivalence, planning, and anticipating barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the session as a joint exploration, using open-ended questions and inviting the client to define problems and solutions. The final plan was clearly co-created rather than imposed by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respected the client's choices, including his rejection of alarms and rehearsal. The plan was framed as a client-led experiment, preserving his sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited the client's own motivations, ideas, and solutions instead of providing direct advice. This supported client ownership of the change plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist operated as an equal collaborator rather than an expert, frequently inviting the client's input. The client was allowed to drive key decisions in the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Scaling questions and pros/cons questions helped the client articulate his own reasons for change. The session moved from ambivalence to specific, client-generated change talk and a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled skillfully by using a manageable 'experiment' frame rather than confronting denial directly. This reduced threat and allowed the client to engage with a small behavioral change.
