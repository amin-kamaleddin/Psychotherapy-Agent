# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental language and normalizes the patient's fear without disapproval. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and do not consistently reach deep, unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are present but often remain surface-level paraphrases rather than capturing deeper emotional meaning. Several opportunities to explore fear, secrecy, and identity concerns were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on disclosing diabetes to a friend, beginning with Sofia. It leans toward practical problem-solving and ends without strong consolidation or summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates collaborative opportunities through roleplay and choices, but the session structure is largely counselor-built. The client's ideas are accommodated without substantially shaping the overall direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms the patient's freedom to choose and explicitly confirms she can stop if things go badly. However, frequent provision of scripts and plans can subtly undermine client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the psychiatrist relies heavily on providing frameworks, scripts, and advice rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations. There is limited use of scaling, future-oriented questions, or reflective amplification.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction includes interactive roleplay and choices, supporting some collaboration. However, the psychiatrist often assumes the expert role and drives the session with ready-made solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expresses some change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms it at times. The counselor does not consistently deepen or expand this change talk with more skilled MI techniques.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges concerns without reinforcing sustain talk and offers practical ways to reduce perceived risk. However, MI-specific techniques such as double-sided reflections are limited, and final sustain talk remains unresolved.
