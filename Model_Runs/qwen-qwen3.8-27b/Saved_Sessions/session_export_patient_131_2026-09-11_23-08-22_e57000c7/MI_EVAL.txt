# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains respectful, affirming stance with no shaming and acknowledges pain about seeing children, normalizing incremental change. Feels partly procedural and safety-planning oriented rather than deeply sustaining, with limited reflection of identity as a good father.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately reflects loss and effort but misses several emotionally charged cues such as fear of not appearing serious to courts and discomfort about sounding like a broken thing. Reflections remain behavioral and logistical rather than consistently emotionally precise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on alcohol reduction with minimal drift, moving clearly from goal clarification to concrete action and follow-up. Focus is mainly logistical rather than exploring deeper values, meaning, and family identity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices, drafts sample texts for approval, and adapts the plan to client preferences with clear collaborative texture. Could be stronger if therapist more explicitly positioned client as equal expert and supplied fewer behavioral rules.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client is repeatedly invited to choose among options, days, contacts, and wording, with explicit non-coercion and low-friction accommodations for his concern about feeling controlled. Some remaining directive pressure appears in the daily check-in and structured plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks questions that elicit some client-generated content but much motivational content is counselor-supplied rather than drawn from client. Does not fully explore client's own reasons for change in terms of his children, Kirsty, or whānau identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client's contributions shape choices among options and wording, but therapist retains much of the agenda including the six-step plan and specific trigger list. Does not explicitly convey that client is the primary expert on his own values and preferred path.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Obtains useful concrete change statements through guided questions but more often supplies the SMART/trigger/accountability framework than evokes client's own values. Little explicit exploration of how reduced drinking supports his children, whānau, or court expectations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce status quo and offers Option A without requiring immediate moving, respecting client's dislike of sounding like he's on a leash. Introduces multiple external controls (removing alcohol, stashing cash, daily check-ins) that keep focus on constraints rather than resolving underlying ambivalence.
