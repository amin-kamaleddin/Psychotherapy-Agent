# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while accepting Samir’s ambivalence and limits. Acceptance could be strengthened by affirming Samir’s self-worth more explicitly beyond compliance and productivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Samir’s ambivalence, exhaustion, guilt, and practical constraints. The interviewer consistently takes his perspective and reframes his experience in a compassionate, meaningful way.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, clinically appropriate focus on a concrete, low-burden change around in-cab dust exposure. Direction is somewhat weakened by a repeated prompt and an unclear ending with no summary or next-step plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer builds on Samir’s ideas and avoids imposing a plan, making the session feel joint and non-authoritative. Collaboration could be stronger by using more open-ended invitations and reducing leading, structured questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Samir is consistently treated as the decision-maker, with repeated emphasis on his choice, ownership, and limits. The interviewer avoids pressure, prescriptions, and controlling language while respecting his ability to skip if needed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer skillfully elicits Samir’s own motivation, including his self-directed reminder, “Take care of the driver, not just the car.” Evocation could deepen further by exploring broader intrinsic values, confidence, and importance of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Samir’s experience and preferences substantially guide the conversation, and the interviewer seeks his internal wisdom rather than professional expertise. Partnership is limited by a fairly structured sequence and some leading questions toward a preselected path.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer effectively evokes useful change talk and helps Samir commit to a workable, self-directed plan. Cultivation is not quite perfect because it does not fully elicit the full range of DARN change talk or use scaling to deepen confidence and importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through acceptance, rolling with resistance, and redirecting toward a low-threshold action. Softening could be more advanced by using double-sided reflections or directly transforming sustain talk into change talk.
