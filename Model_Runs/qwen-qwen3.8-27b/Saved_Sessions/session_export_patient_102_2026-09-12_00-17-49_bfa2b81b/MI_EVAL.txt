# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shame or moralizing about Milan’s drinking. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and does not fully address deeper relational or identity concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, surface-level reflections of frustration and judgment. Empathy remains mainly cognitive and does not deeply explore ambivalence, shame, fear, or social identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session starts with drinking concerns but quickly shifts to school catch-up and remains there. Direction from the primary alcohol-use target is weak, limiting strategic MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaboration is strong on the practical school task, with Milan shaping the email, date, and focus. Collaboration is more procedural and does not extend fully to alcohol-use goals or values.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Milan’s choices, accepts his refusal of alcohol-focused help, and avoids coercion. The score is reduced because some recommendations remain fairly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative questions early on, but the session quickly moves into advice and problem-solving. Evocation of Milan’s own motivations, values, and confidence around drinking is insufficient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and co-constructs the school email, showing some partnership. The stance remains somewhat expert-driven, especially in framing early intervention options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some practical intentions about school but does not build meaningful change talk around alcohol use. Useful client statements are not explored as potential motivations for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Milan’s sustain talk is not met with confrontation, but it is also not skillfully softened or redirected. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore ambivalence and the costs or benefits of change.
