# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s effort, but acceptance is sometimes tied to using the inhaler. There is also limited deeper validation of the client’s frustration, shame, or feeling labeled as sick.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses double-sided reflections and shows some accurate perspective-taking, but the responses can feel formulaic and repetitive. Deeper emotional themes such as fear, shame, and burden are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and moves toward a simpler, client-defined goal. However, repeatedly asking for a “workable next step” after the client says he has none creates a stall and reduces strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to define acceptable contact and co-creates a simple, client-owned statement. Collaboration is somewhat uneven because the repeated focus on a “next step” feels partly clinician-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s freedom to choose and avoids lectures or pressure. Some questions still subtly presuppose future engagement, and placing approval on using the inhaler can slightly narrow the client’s sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes useful client language such as “handling it my way” and “calling isn’t a waste of my time.” Evocation remains limited by a task-oriented focus and less exploration of underlying values, confidence, and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms that the client knows his own life best and avoids a purely expert role. Partnership is less fluid when the psychiatrist repeatedly centers the inhaler and asks for a next step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk related to wanting to breathe easier and using the inhaler. Change talk is not consistently strengthened, summarized, or expanded through deeper reflections or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles refusal and sustain talk well by avoiding argument and reframing contact as a safety net rather than surveillance. Some sustain talk remains dominant and is not always redirected toward autonomy, safety, or forward-looking meaning.
