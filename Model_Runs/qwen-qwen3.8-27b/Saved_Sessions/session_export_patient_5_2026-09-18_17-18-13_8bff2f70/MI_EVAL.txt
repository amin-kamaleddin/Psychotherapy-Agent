# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming language throughout. Key phrases like 'You were absolutely not being rude' and 'That's not something to feel awkward about' demonstrate genuine validation without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and layered reflections that capture emotional undertones (frustration, anxiety, self-criticism) with precision. Particularly astute in reflecting the 'nagging' feeling held back due to not wanting to seem rude, and the underlying overthinking pattern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains good focus on the target behavior with logical progression from understanding medication to actionable next steps. Minor deduction for the final expansion into the broader 'spreadsheet' pattern, which slightly drifts from the specific medication safety goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is shaped by the client's input at every turn, with consistent checking and co-construction of meaning. Language like 'I'd love to work through this with you' and frequent invitations for input reinforce a genuinely equal power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's ownership and choice, using explicit autonomy language ('You're in the driver's seat here') and never assuming or pressuring. The framing of next steps as the client's decision rather than an imposition is exemplary.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws out the client's own motivations and insights, eliciting a powerful self-insight about the 'spreadsheet' pattern. Could be more systematic in evoking change talk, such as using commitment or scaling questions after the client articulates an improved plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within the client, explicitly naming her insights and following her lead on topic and direction. Never assumes preferences or needs—instead checks and invites input throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evokes several instances of change talk, including the client articulating a more specific plan and demonstrating self-awareness. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk, nor does she elicit explicit commitment language or explore confidence/ability in detail.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively handles sustain talk by validating without reinforcing the gap and gently reframing self-criticism (e.g., pushing back on 'careless'). Could be stronger by more explicitly exploring the emotional weight of the phenelzine-citalopram discovery.
