# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite, nonjudgmental, and supportive, with validation of stressors and no obvious disapproval. However, the acceptance felt generic and transactional rather than deeply individualized to the client's alcohol-risk referral.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathy was limited to brief factual reflections about busyness and needing a longer warm-up. There was little exploration of feelings, fears, identity, or ambivalence related to the presenting concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session missed the core clinical target by never addressing the alcohol-use screening or reason for referral. It drifted entirely into homework and time-management tasks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely counselor-directed, with the clinician generating the plan, rankings, and time blocks. The client contributed information but was not meaningfully engaged as a co-creator of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered some structured choices and stated the client did not have to commit to follow-up. However, autonomy was limited by a prescriptive plan and by not affirming the client's ownership of choices related to the target behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor asked mostly fact-finding questions rather than eliciting the client's own values, motivations, or reasons for change. There was no evocative work toward the actual presenting concern.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor maintained an expert/director stance, providing a ranked plan and specific timing recommendations. There were small collaborative gestures, but the client was not positioned as the expert on her own priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There was no meaningful effort to evoke change talk about alcohol use or the relevant clinical concern. Client statements focused on task compliance rather than desire, reasons, or ability to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not explicitly reinforced, largely because the alcohol topic was avoided. The counselor also did not demonstrate active skills for working with ambivalence or redirecting toward change.
