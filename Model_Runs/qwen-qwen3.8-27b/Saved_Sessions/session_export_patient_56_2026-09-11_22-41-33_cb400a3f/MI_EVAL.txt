# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, accepting that change is not required. Affirmations are positive but occasionally generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client’s settledness and concern about adding obligations. Empathy could be stronger by more fully exploring the meaning of “stability isn’t a performance” and the client’s preference for simple, authentic language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on values clarification, family happiness, and a small related behavior. Direction could have remained more strategic by emphasizing the meaning and future relevance of the value rather than mainly guiding a brief assignment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and allows the client to reject suggestions, making the final plan client-driven. Collaboration is somewhat limited by the counselor-led structure, worksheets, and pre-made choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client’s freedom to choose, optional steps, and no pressure to change. The client explicitly appreciates not being pushed to fix anything.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces her own statement and some meaningful self-insight. The session relies more on structured prompts than on open-ended questions that draw out motivation, values, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and respects her choice to keep the original gratitude line. The interaction remains somewhat clinician-directed due to the structured values check and counselor-generated options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates useful change talk, especially about naming stability and what feels right. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk with stronger evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument and emphasizing optional, low-pressure steps. A higher score would require more active reframing of sustain talk as a strength of maintenance or alignment.
