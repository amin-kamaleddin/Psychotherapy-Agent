# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and repeatedly validated Seamus’s frustration and boundaries. Acceptance was strong but occasionally undercut by repeated introductions of smoking follow-up.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was adequate reflective listening to Seamus’s annoyance with warnings and negative medication experience. Empathy remained largely surface-level and missed deeper feelings such as feeling controlled, exhausted, or reduced to “the smoking patient.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session had some focus but lacked a coherent strategic direction, drifting between smoking follow-up, dental self-care advice, logistics, and safety warnings. It honored the client’s priority but did not skillfully move toward a clear next step or change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited Seamus to choose among options, often respecting his refusals. Collaboration was somewhat uneven because the clinician repeatedly introduced new suggestions and practical services rather than staying fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was clearly supported through options, affirmation of Seamus’s choice, and respect for his decision to pause smoking discussion. It was slightly weakened by repeated offers of future smoking check-ins and unsolicited practical advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some client input early on, particularly around past quit attempts and what might be helpful now. Evocation was limited because the session shifted into advice and logistics without drawing out Seamus’s values, concerns, or motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and invited Seamus’s preferences. Partnership was not fully client-led because the psychiatrist repeatedly reinitiated topics and introduced additional options or advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was only limited effort to cultivate change talk, mainly through initial questions about smoking change. The psychiatrist did not effectively explore values, consequences, confidence, or small steps, and did not expand on any client interest in change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and did not escalate Seamus’s resistance about medication or smoking discussions. However, softening sustain talk was inconsistent, with little use of double-sided reflections, normalization of ambivalence, or linking dental goals to possible smoking-related benefits.
