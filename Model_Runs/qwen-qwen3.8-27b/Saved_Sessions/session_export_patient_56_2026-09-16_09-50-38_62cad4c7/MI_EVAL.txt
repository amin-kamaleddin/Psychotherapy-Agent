# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental stance while validating the client’s values and choices. The warmth remains somewhat task-oriented and does not fully express unconditional positive regard separate from performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate reflective listening to capture the client’s ambivalence, exhaustion, and desire to protect family time. Deeper emotional meanings, such as fear of failure or identity pressure, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on a concrete, value-linked goal: noticing and protecting evening energy. It becomes somewhat procedural and could more consistently reconnect the plan to the client’s core values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options, co-creating wording, and incorporating the client’s preferences into the plan. A more fully collaborative approach would invite the client to generate the check-in design before offering a script.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supports choice by asking preferences, reducing pressure, and allowing the client to customize the plan. Some directive language around the script and follow-up slightly limits autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful opening and preference questions, but the session focuses more on selecting a practical tool than drawing out deeper motivation. More evocative questions would strengthen the client’s confidence, values, and change reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client genuinely shapes the plan through choices about focus, wording, timing, reminder method, trial length, and follow-up. The counselor’s use of pre-built options keeps the interaction collaborative but somewhat structured.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses some change language about protecting family time, avoiding burnout, and reducing guilt. The counselor does not consistently elicit or amplify deeper change talk, such as benefits, confidence, or success criteria.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handles concerns about guilt, pressure, and checkbox thinking by reframing them gently and nonjudgmentally. The final worry that forgetting could become failure is not fully addressed or normalized.
