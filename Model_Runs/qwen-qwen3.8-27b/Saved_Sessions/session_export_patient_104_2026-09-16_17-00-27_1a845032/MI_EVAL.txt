# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, with warm affirmations that support Nadine’s exhaustion and fear. Acceptance is strong but sometimes tied to effort or performance rather than fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, complex, and clinically attuned, especially around 3 a.m. guilt, isolation, and fear of failing. Some deeper emotional content, such as shame or feeling invalidated by her mother, remains underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is coherent and relevant but lacks a consistent focus on a specific target behavior or change goal. The late move toward reducing nighttime phone checking is promising but not fully developed into a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively and lets Nadine’s concerns shape the conversation, including her requests for a short list and neutral check. However, the clinician still introduces some counselor-led structures before fully eliciting Nadine’s preferred format.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure, directives, and ultimatums, leaving decisions with Nadine through open questions. Explicit autonomy-supportive language about her ownership and freedom of choice is less frequent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nadine’s ideas and emerging motivations are elicited, but change talk is not consistently drawn out or deepened. Techniques such as scaling confidence, readiness, values, or discrepancy are limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nadine is treated as the expert on her experience, and the psychiatrist frequently invites her perspective. Partnership is strong but could be stronger by letting Nadine set the agenda before introducing specific tools.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is meaningful evocation of desire and need, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on Nadine’s change-oriented statements. More skillful development of readiness, confidence, ability, or commitment would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by avoiding debate and gently separating factual worry from emotional guilt. The psychiatrist could further soften sustain talk with more double-sided reflections or pivots toward change talk.
