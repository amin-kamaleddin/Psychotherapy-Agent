# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, affirming language and accepts the patient’s ambivalence without judgment. The tone is professional but somewhat task-focused, with less explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient’s concerns, triggers, and plan details. Reflections are mostly content-focused rather than exploring deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on smoking reduction, work-related triggers, and practical coping steps. There is no topic drift and a clear sense of progress toward a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributes meaningful choices, but the psychiatrist frequently initiates and frames the problem-solving process. The session is collaborative but remains somewhat expert-driven rather than fully co-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers repeated choices and accepts realistic limits, supporting the patient’s ownership. The structured suggestions and specific strategies still lean slightly toward advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks about confidence, obstacles, and what would make the plan easier, eliciting useful patient-generated ideas. However, the session relies more on therapist-supplied strategies than on evoking the patient’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and choice questions create some partnership, and the patient’s input shapes the plan. The clinician still provides a fairly extensive menu of specific techniques, making the interaction less fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some confidence and commitment language, but does not consistently amplify or build on the patient’s own change talk. More effort is needed to deepen motivation rather than focus mainly on planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles the patient’s doubts without arguing or dismissing and redirects toward small, doable steps. The response could be stronger by more explicitly pivoting sustain talk into change talk.
