# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Claudia’s ambivalence and choices. The tone is generally warm, though some procedural phrasing and repeated slip instructions can feel mildly corrective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, with accurate summaries of Claudia’s perspective, behavior, and mixed feelings. Empathy could be deeper by more fully reflecting emotional undercurrents such as tiredness, low mood, or feeling empty.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on reducing alcohol use and moves logically from pattern identification to ambivalence, goal setting, planning, confidence, and follow-up. Drift into the bath-related issue is quickly linked back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Claudia’s input, offers options, and integrates her ideas into the final plan. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still structures much of the process and provides many concrete options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice, self-direction, and respect for Claudia’s readiness are repeatedly supported. Autonomy support is slightly limited by more suggestive or prescriptive suggestions, such as the timer and the firm bathroom rule.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Claudia’s own ideas, confidence level, and practical plans through open questions and scaling. Evocation is mostly practical and confidence-focused rather than deeply value-based.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with Claudia actively choosing the pantry location, bath ritual, playlist, and check-in time. The psychiatrist still carries much of the agenda, making the session somewhat therapist-structured.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling successfully elicit concrete change talk, such as keeping less wine at home, pausing before a second glass, and using smaller bottles. Cultivation would be stronger with more value-driven amplification of Claudia’s personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Claudia’s sustain talk and instead reflects it while redirecting toward a more manageable plan. Use of more explicit double-sided reflections could further reduce defensiveness and resolve ambivalence.
