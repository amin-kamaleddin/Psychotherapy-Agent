# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the patient's concern about pressure. Acceptance is slightly weakened by a more administrative tone rather than warm, individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects both the practical and emotional themes in the patient's statements. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and occasionally formulaic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with appropriate attention to smoking reduction but quickly shifts to dental logistics. This limits the strategic focus on the MI target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively on task-level decisions, such as format, order, and contact method. The collaborative stance is strong but narrow, not fully extending to ambivalence around smoking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated, explicit statements of choice and control. The psychiatrist respects the patient's boundaries without pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to evoke motivation, such as what would make a small reduction doable. However, evocation is not sufficiently deep or sustained.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the patient as the authority on his own life and invites input throughout. Partnership is strong procedurally but less fully developed in relation to change motivation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a reasonable attempt to draw out the patient's own language about reducing smoking. The change talk is not followed up deeply enough in terms of importance, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the patient's resistance and does not amplify sustain talk. The main limitation is that sustain talk is more accepted or avoided than actively softened through reflection or autonomy-respecting redirection.
