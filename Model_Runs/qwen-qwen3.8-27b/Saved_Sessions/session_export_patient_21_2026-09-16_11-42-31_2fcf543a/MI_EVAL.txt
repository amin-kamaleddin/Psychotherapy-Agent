# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout the session. Acceptance was clear but somewhat procedural and task-focused, with less emphasis on deep warmth or unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided accurate content-focused reflections, such as summarizing the client's desire for an easy, meaningful morning routine. However, emotional cues such as vulnerability, anxiety about failure, and self-criticism were not consistently explored or deeply validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session had strong strategic direction, remaining consistently focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity through a small morning movement routine. The counselor guided the conversation logically from motivation and barriers to a concrete, doable plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor collaborated well, with the client actively choosing the stretches, if-then plan, sticky note, backup option, and check-in format. However, the counselor still functioned as a strategy generator by offering several options before fully eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by asking for preferences, respecting the client's boundary about partner accountability, and emphasizing low pressure. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client's freedom to pause or decline the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor used evocative prompts, including an open-ended question about what matters most and readiness and confidence rulers. Evocation was limited at times because the counselor frequently moved into offering options rather than drawing out more client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveyed a collaborative stance and allowed Camila's choices to shape the final plan. Partnership could be stronger by eliciting more of the client's own solutions before providing counselor-generated options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited change talk by exploring importance, readiness, confidence, and expected benefits. However, the counselor often shifted quickly into problem-solving and did not consistently amplify or expand the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged barriers and normalized slips without arguing or dismissing the client's concerns. More advanced MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections, were not consistently used to address ambivalence and fear of failure.
