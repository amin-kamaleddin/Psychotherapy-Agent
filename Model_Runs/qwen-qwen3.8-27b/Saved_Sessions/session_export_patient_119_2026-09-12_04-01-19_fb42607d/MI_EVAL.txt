# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout the session. The warmth is professional but somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper emotional attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture the client's ambivalence, practical constraints, and shifting confidence. Emotional undercurrents such as anxiety, guilt, and fear of judgment are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on medication adherence with clear, logical progression. Each step moves the client from assessment to a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist genuinely incorporates the client's ideas and builds the plan together. However, the counselor primarily generates the structure and options, limiting full client co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, explicit support for the client's right to choose and direct the conversation. The counselor avoids pressure, presents options, and normalizes the client's pace and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's own motivations, reasons, and solutions using open questions and scaling. Evocation could be deepened by exploring values, identity, and long-term vision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client meaningfully contributes to the plan and the counselor respects her choices. The structured, menu-based approach still positions the psychiatrist as the primary agenda setter.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling and targeted questions successfully elicit change talk and increase the client's confidence. The counselor could more powerfully amplify and reflect the client's own readiness and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and used to create realistic, low-pressure plans. More strategic double-sided reflections could help the client actively resolve ambivalence.
