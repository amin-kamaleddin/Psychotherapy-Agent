# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and genuine unconditional positive regard throughout, with no disapproval, labeling, or moralizing. Affirmations are specific and non-formulaic (e.g., 'You just did something really hard there, Lars'), and shame is met with gentle reframing rather than minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections consistently capture both the cognitive and emotional dimensions of Lars's experience, naming identity-level meaning beneath surface statements (e.g., the identity threat of asking for help with forms). There is deep attunement to emotional subtext, particularly around the father comparison, where the psychiatrist simply acknowledges the weight without probing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a coherent thread from boredom/peer pressure through career goals, practical barriers, and deeper identity issues, all connected to the target behavior of reducing recidivism risk. Transitions are organic and follow Lars's lead without straying into tangential territory or forcing a predetermined agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is explicitly framed as joint work from the opening ('we figure things out together'), and reflections are offered as invitations to confirm or correct rather than assessments. The power dynamic remains genuinely equal, evidenced by Lars treating the psychiatrist as a peer resource rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decision-making is consistently placed in Lars's hands through open questions and active reinforcement of his self-direction (e.g., 'you're not stuck,' 'That's a real, concrete next step'). No pressure is applied to commit to specific actions or timelines; autonomy support is active rather than merely non-directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every reason for change emerges from Lars's own mouth, elicited through careful reflections, open questions, and strategic silences. The evocation is so natural that Lars appears unaware he is being guided, which is the hallmark of skilled evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Lars as the expert on his own life, using questions that invite his perspective rather than prescribing solutions. Even when offering a perspective (the phone call idea), it is framed as a reflection of Lars's own thinking rather than an external suggestion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocative questions and amplifying reflections draw out Lars's own reasons for change without being heavy-handed, such as asking what 'anywhere good' looks like to him. The session culminates in Lars spontaneously articulating a concrete next step and a vivid future vision, indicating internalized motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by acknowledging the legitimate function of the lads and the 'buzz' before gently redirecting to underlying needs, and the fear of looking stupid is reframed to show Lars his concern and solution are compatible. The only minor area for growth is that the genuine social and emotional value of the friendships could have been acknowledged more explicitly before redirecting.
