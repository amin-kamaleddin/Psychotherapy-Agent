# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent warmth and a nonjudgmental stance, explicitly validating ambivalence with phrases like 'Those two things can sit together.' However, the warmth remains somewhat measured and professional rather than deeply affirming, lacking explicit affirmations of the client's worth as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking, capturing emotional subtext such as the weight of the word 'referred' and the 'slips' pattern. However, some reflections stay at the cognitive level rather than fully reaching the affective core, particularly around the fear of not being around for her kids.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from referral anxiety through identity concerns to a concrete, client-generated plan without drifting off-topic. The transition from identity discussions to practical planning could have been slightly more explicit in connecting the dots.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels like a joint exploration, with the clinician affirming and building on the client's own ideas rather than replacing them. Collaboration could be strengthened by more explicitly inviting the client's input on how to proceed at key transition points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of autonomy support: the clinician never prescribes or tells the client what to do, frames the plan as a low-pressure test, and consistently positions choices as hers to make. The explicit removal of expectations ('I'm not here to quiz you') and the question about what success would look like for her specifically reinforce the client as the authority on her own life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out the client's own motivations and values rather than providing external reasons for change, with all 'reasons' emerging from Selene herself. Could be improved by more explicitly amplifying and reflecting back change talk once it appears to build momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a collaborative, curious stance rather than an expert one, using open-ended questions and inviting the client's perspective at key junctures. Partnership could be strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language and acknowledgment of the client's expertise in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-calibrated questions effectively elicit multiple pieces of change talk, including the long-term motivation around her kids and the concrete pill-by-the-mug plan. The change talk could have been more explicitly reflected back and amplified to build greater momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without arguing or dismissing, using reframes like 'slips' and double-sided reflections to acknowledge both sides. Could be further improved with more strategic use of double-sided reflections and explicit pivots from sustain talk toward change talk.
