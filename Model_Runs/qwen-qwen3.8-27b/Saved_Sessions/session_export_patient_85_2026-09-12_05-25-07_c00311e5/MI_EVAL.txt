# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s frustration without blame or shaming. It would strengthen acceptance to explore the client’s self-criticism and discouragement more deeply before moving into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both behavior and emotion, including ambivalence about orange juice. Some reflections remain somewhat surface-level or label-based rather than exploring deeper meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing orange juice consumption and advancing toward a concrete behavior-change plan. Each turn moves the work forward in a practical, behavior-specific way.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as a partner rather than an authority, following the client’s lead while contributing helpful expertise. The interaction reflects strong teamwork and mutual participation in planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker through choice-based questions and non-directive language. There is no pressure, persuasion, or imposed plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites the client to generate his own ideas, plans, and strategies. Offering specific drink options slightly reduces the emphasis on client-generated motivation and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s perspective clearly shapes the session, and the psychiatrist builds on his contributions. Occasional directive suggestions, such as specific brand names, slightly reduce the client-as-expert emphasis.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively elicit concrete plans, confidence, and commitment language. The session could go deeper by eliciting the client’s own values and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s enjoyment of orange juice and possible cravings without moralizing. A more explicit double-sided reflection would further honor ambivalence while orienting toward change.
