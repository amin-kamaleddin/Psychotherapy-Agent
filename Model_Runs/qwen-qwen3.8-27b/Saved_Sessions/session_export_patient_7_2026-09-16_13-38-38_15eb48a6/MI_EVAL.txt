# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, but the warmth becomes formulaic with repeated 'I hear you.' It does not fully normalize the client's guardedness and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately address pressure, overwhelm, boundary concerns, and the need for silence. They remain somewhat surface-level and become repetitive, missing deeper meaning behind the client's guardedness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The initial direction toward reducing pressure is appropriate for a resistant client. After the client asks for silence, the session lacks a clear minimal goal or agreed-upon next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist uses collaborative language and attempts shared control, but the interaction remains therapist-led. The client's minimal engagement is not sufficiently used to shape the next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice language is frequent and often strong, but autonomy is undermined by continuing to speak after the client explicitly asks for silence. Boundary-honoring is present but inconsistent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions early attempt to draw out the client's perspective, but evocation becomes closed and stops after the client requests silence. There is little effort to elicit values, preferences, or small engagement talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist explicitly positions the client as the expert and invites client lead, which supports partnership. In practice, the therapist continues to narrate and lead rather than fully co-create direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation of change talk is rare and weak, with one useful open question followed by limiting closed options. The therapist misses opportunities to elicit engagement or participation talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing and does not reinforce resistance, but does not fully soften sustain talk. Continuing to narrate the quiet after the client says 'silence' can re-trigger resistance.
