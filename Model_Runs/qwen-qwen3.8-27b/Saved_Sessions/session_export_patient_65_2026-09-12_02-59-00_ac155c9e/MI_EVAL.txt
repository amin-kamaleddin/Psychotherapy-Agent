# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Julian’s frustrations. Acceptance feels consistent but somewhat task-focused, with little explicit affirmation of Julian’s worth or courage beyond the immediate problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Julian’s practical concerns and preferences. However, reflections stay mostly surface-level and do not fully explore the anxiety, authority stress, or emotional weight behind his statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused on a clear target: completing court-ordered service hours before the deadline. The counselor consistently guides the conversation toward concrete steps, though with less attention to motivation than to task completion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and offers choices, creating a collaborative structure. In practice, Julian mostly confirms counselor-generated options rather than contributing his own ideas or solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor respects Julian’s preferences and avoids pressure, especially around texting versus calling and leaving information blank. Autonomy support is limited because choices are mostly defined by the counselor rather than generated by Julian.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor provides most of the content, including placement options, scripts, and backup plans. There is little effort to elicit Julian’s own motivations, values, strengths, or ideas for completing the requirement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction includes some partnership language and shared decision-making. Overall, the counselor remains the primary expert and solution generator, so the relationship feels more directive than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Julian offers brief change talk, such as saying he would go if he knew where or planning to text tomorrow. The counselor reflects these statements neutrally but does not deepen or amplify them to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue with or reinforce Julian’s sustain talk, such as saying he does not want to change his life. However, the counselor also misses opportunities to gently reframe or explore that ambivalence using an MI-consistent approach.
