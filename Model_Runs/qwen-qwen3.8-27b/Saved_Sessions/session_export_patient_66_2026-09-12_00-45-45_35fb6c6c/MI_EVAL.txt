# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids pressure, normalizing the client’s frustration with the new policy. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming the client more fully, including his values, work role, and desire for control, rather than only the practicality of his plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture the client’s main tension between enjoying smoking and wanting to avoid conflict. However, empathy does not consistently deepen, as the session shifts into practical problem-solving without exploring underlying feelings, ambivalence, or self-image.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the workplace smoke-free issue, but the direction is more practical than MI-oriented. Instead of guiding the conversation toward readiness, importance, confidence, or possible change, the counselor quickly moves into logistics and problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, as the client chooses car breaks and rejects certain suggestions. However, the counselor introduces many options and repeatedly summarizes the plan, which makes the interaction feel more like guided advice than a true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is clearly supported through non-coercive language, respect for the client’s choice, and avoidance of quitting pressure. The main limitation is the frequent advice-giving and repeated recaps, which can create a subtle advisory tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not meaningfully evoke the client’s own motivation, values, confidence, or reasons for change. Questions remain practical rather than evocative, with little effort to draw out the client’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and adapts to the client’s preference for car breaks. However, much of the session is driven by the counselor’s suggestions and summaries, reducing the sense of genuine co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is very little effort to elicit or strengthen change talk. The psychiatrist does not explore the client’s own motivations, values, confidence, or possible benefits of change, and the client produces minimal change-related language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor does not soften or redirect sustain talk; instead, the session largely reinforces the client’s plan to continue smoking. Statements such as “I won’t push quitting” are autonomy-respecting, but in context they support maintaining the status quo rather than balancing sustain talk with change talk.
