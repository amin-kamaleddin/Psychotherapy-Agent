# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgment, and support while respecting the patient's boundaries around drinking and quitting. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural and formulaic, preventing a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's content, ambivalence, and preference for reduction over quitting. Empathy is generally good but remains mainly content-level, missing deeper emotional themes such as exhaustion, fear, and parenting responsibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing evening smoking and progresses coherently from problem identification to planning and follow-up. Repeated introduction of multiple strategy options slightly dilutes the momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites participation and builds the final plan around the patient's own ideas. Collaboration is evident, but the repeated use of structured options creates a less equal power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is frequently supported through permissive language, acceptance of a smaller goal, and offering follow-up as optional. However, repeated menus of strategies subtly imply a preferred course of action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some internal motivation using rulers and questions about confidence, wins, and barriers. Evocation is limited because the conversation shifts too quickly to external problem-solving before motivation is fully expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as the decision-maker and affirms her client-generated solutions. Partnership is strong but not full because the clinician remains the primary agenda-setter and frequently offers options before the patient explores them independently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked through importance/confidence rulers, small goals, and affirmation of the patient's son as a motivator. The main limitation is that change talk is not consistently deepened, amplified, or strategically reinforced before moving to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the patient's reluctance to quit and respects her boundary around alcohol. Softening sustain talk is limited because there is little use of double-sided reflections or values-based reframing to move sustain talk toward change.
