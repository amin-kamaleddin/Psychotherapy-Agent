# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance without arguing or moralizing the client's drinking. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic, with fewer explicit affirmations of the client's honesty or difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's ambivalence, needs, and shifting plans, including nuanced distinctions between being tired versus wired. Minor limitation: the final response does not fully reflect the client's self-consciousness about replacing beer with an app.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and narrows effectively to concrete evening decision points. However, it becomes quite problem-solving-focused and could better link small steps to the broader goal of reducing alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by building directly on the client's own ideas and following the client's leads without pushing a prescribed plan. The session consistently reflects a partnership between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's sense of choice, avoids controlling language, and follows the client's preferences rather than persuading them to accept a plan. The client is repeatedly positioned as the expert on his own life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes several client-generated ideas and practical steps, such as not getting in the car, capping the bottle, and using passive background audio. Evocation could be stronger by more consistently exploring deeper values, confidence, or broader motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveys that the client has the expertise and wisdom to guide change, using frequent client-directed questions and credit-giving language. The client's ideas substantially shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes concrete change talk around small, doable steps and helps the client expand practical alternatives. Development could be stronger by more consistently exploring desire, need, values, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging it without challenging or reinforcing it, and redirects it toward low-effort, client-consistent options. The redirection could be more strategic by using double-sided reflections to connect the client's competing concerns more actively to change.
