# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, supportive stance by normalizing Rafael’s struggles and using validating language. Acceptance is strong but remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate reflective listening that captures Rafael’s context and emotional difficulty. Empathy is strong but could be deeper in exploring shame, fear of failure, and the pain of repeated unmet expectations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on returning to exercise and moves from general barriers to specific, actionable steps. Direction is clear and practical, though stronger MI direction would connect the behavior more explicitly to Rafael’s values and summarize themes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a cooperative tone, and Rafael is invited to choose among options such as walking, reminders, and fallbacks. However, the psychiatrist supplies most of the plans and strategies, limiting fully co-constructed collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking Rafael to select reminders, fallbacks, and the level of follow-up. At the same time, directive language such as “try this exact plan” reduces the consistency of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session includes some evocative questions about barriers, past supports, and possible next steps. Evocation remains limited because the psychiatrist quickly shifts into concrete planning without deeply exploring Rafael’s values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and asks for Rafael’s preferences, allowing him to influence the plan. Partnership is present but uneven because the psychiatrist often takes the lead as the primary problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rafael offers some change talk, such as valuing being active and feeling able to try a small step. The psychiatrist acknowledges this briefly but does not consistently deepen it through scaling, values exploration, or amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Rafael’s concerns and uses normalizing, low-stakes language. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections or explicit redirection from sustain talk toward change talk.
