# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, validating Jordan's fatigue and stress without blaming him. The warmth is solid but somewhat task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects exhaustion, frustration, and practical constraints accurately and consistently. Deeper underlying feelings such as mistrust, shame, fear of another dead-end call, and ambivalence could be captured more precisely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting issue of work instability, payroll problems, and rent pressure. Occasionally, the practical problem-solving orientation slightly dilutes the MI focus on motivation and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by offering options and responding to Jordan's preferences, such as choosing 211, reading instead of writing, and using a text backup. More client-generated planning would make the collaboration even stronger.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and avoids coercion by offering multiple low-effort options and respecting Jordan's limits. However, the counselor still generates much of the plan, which can subtly reduce Jordan's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some good evocative questions early in the session about what feels pressing and what small change might help. The session often shifts too quickly into advice and practical steps before Jordan's own motivation is fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan's preferences and allows his choices to shape the session, creating a collaborative feel. More explicit power-sharing, such as asking what Jordan thinks would work, would strengthen the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan offers meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand it. More use of scaling, affirmations, or evocative follow-ups would help strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Jordan's sustain talk and often acknowledges it before redirecting toward action. More double-sided reflections and strategic pivots toward hope, values, or exceptions would better soften sustain talk.
