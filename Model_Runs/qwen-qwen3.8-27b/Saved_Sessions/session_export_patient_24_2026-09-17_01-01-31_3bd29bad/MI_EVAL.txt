# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and affirms Daniel’s effort and perspective. Acceptance is strong, though somewhat more focused on the task than on Daniel as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Daniel’s confusion, ambivalence, and self-protective stance. The empathy is solid but remains more cognitive than deeply emotionally attuned.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays connected to the referral concern, linking drinking to marathon training and recovery. It could become stronger by more explicitly tying the monitoring plan to decisions about reducing alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a simple self-monitoring plan and invites Daniel to define what is useful. The interaction is collaborative, though the psychiatrist still introduces some options and framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Daniel’s choice and expertise are consistently emphasized, with no pressure to cut down. The plan is framed as something Daniel can do on his own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite Daniel to reflect on mornings, sleep, and training. However, there is limited evocation of Daniel’s deeper values, confidence, or reasons for changing drinking itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Daniel as the expert on his body, routine, and limits. Power sharing is strong, though the introduction of the tracking framework slightly reduces the purely client-led quality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds some willingness around noticing mornings and monitoring, but does not strongly evoke desire, need, ability, or commitment to reduce alcohol use. Change talk stays mainly about tracking rather than the core drinking goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by validating that drinking may feel normal and helpful without arguing or confronting. The response could be more nuanced by using explicit double-sided reflections that hold both sides of Daniel’s ambivalence.
