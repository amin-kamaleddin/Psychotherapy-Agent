# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician was respectful, calm, and nonjudgmental, conveying positive regard for the client. However, the responses were somewhat functional and task-oriented rather than deeply affirming of the client's specific struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offered adequate but inconsistent perspective-taking regarding the client's stress. They missed opportunities to reflect the deeper emotional ambivalence about wanting to protect school performance versus feeling controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session initially focused on stress and alcohol use but drifted into academic performance, quiz anxiety, and general study plans. This diluted the primary focus on alcohol-related coping.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated a collaborative stance by shaping the final plan around the client's specific choices and preferences. The collaboration was slightly uneven because the clinician supplied many of the specific interventions rather than having the client generate them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician regularly supported choice and did not pressure the client into larger plans or counseling. However, slightly directive language like "commit to" could have better emphasized the client's explicit ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation was limited, with the session quickly shifting into advice and problem-solving. The clinician missed opportunities to use evocative questions to draw out the client's own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was partial partnership by offering some choices, but the clinician heavily relied on an expert role by providing specific techniques and structured plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician failed to consistently evoke, reflect, or amplify the client's change talk about wanting practical tools and not wanting to mess up things they care about.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician accepted and did not argue with the client's sustain talk about not being ready for large changes. However, they lacked MI-skilled techniques like double-sided reflections to actively soften the ambivalence.
