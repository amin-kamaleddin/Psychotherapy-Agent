# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed respect and validation, often acknowledging Samir's experience without blame. However, acceptance was somewhat procedural and occasionally undercut by repeated safety warnings that implied risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured surface-level distress and practical burdens, such as feeling overwhelmed by the medication routine. The psychiatrist missed opportunities to explore deeper meanings like autonomy, control, and distrust of medical care.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on medication burden and the possibility of skipping a dose. However, the focus often drifted toward risk management and compliance planning rather than clarifying Samir's own goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were initial collaborative openings, but the psychiatrist frequently continued offering options after Samir declined them. This made the interaction feel more counselor-directed than mutually led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly honored Samir's right to choose and stated respect for his decisions. Repeated advice, safety planning, and risk framing, however, created subtle pressure toward compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some early questions invited exploration, but the psychiatrist did not consistently draw out Samir's values, motivations, or confidence in change. The session shifted to information and practical options instead of eliciting change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist began by inviting Samir to set the focus, but the session became more expert-driven as practical solutions were introduced. Samir's declining preferences did not sufficiently shape the direction of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made a few evocative attempts, such as asking what a meaningful break would look like. Patient statements that could have been expanded into change talk were not reflected, amplified, or built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided agreeing that stopping medication was acceptable, but also did not effectively soften Samir's sustain talk. Statements favoring the status quo were acknowledged but not redirected through reflections or nonconfrontational exploration.
