# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the patient's concerns. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with limited deeper affirmation of the patient's individualized values and safety concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately tracks the patient's need for clear information and support. The empathy is primarily cognitive rather than fully emotional, especially around the patient's fear of being unsupported during the washout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the safe transition from phenelzine to citalopram without drifting to unrelated topics. Direction could be strengthened by more clearly guiding the patient toward a concrete, client-owned next step with her prescriber.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient to shape the conversation and collaboratively drafts a message to the prescriber. Collaboration is effective but remains somewhat procedural rather than co-creating broader priorities for the transition plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice throughout by offering options, avoiding pressure, and respecting the patient's decision-making. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming that the patient is the final decision-maker in her care.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides useful medical information and asks some open-ended questions, but the approach is more information-driven than evocative. More strategic use of the patient's own language would better elicit personal values, confidence, and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and invites the patient's input on wording and approach. A deeper partnership would involve more explicitly inviting the patient to define what a good plan looks like for her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some change talk, especially around safety and wanting a supported plan. The psychiatrist does not consistently ask deeper questions about the importance, confidence, or personal values driving the decision.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the patient's hesitation and acknowledges her concerns about the 14-day gap. However, responses tend to rely more on medical information than strategic reflection to help resolve ambivalence.
