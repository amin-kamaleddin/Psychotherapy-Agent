**Comments:** Co-constructs a practical plan around Marcus’s preferences, including phone reminders, a sticky note, and a receptionist handoff. Strong collaboration, but could be more equal if Marcus generated more of the content rather than choosing among clinician-proposed options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports choice consistently and respects Marcus’s stated limits, such as not being ready for fingersticks or role-play. Could more explicitly affirm his ownership of decisions and the absence of a single right way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks some initial questions about goals and small first steps, but the session relies more on offering scripts and options. More evocative questions about values, confidence, and past success would strengthen client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative questions and allows Marcus to shape many plan details. Could more explicitly honor Marcus as the expert on his own life and readiness before supplying structured options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change-oriented language, such as wanting to learn the meter and avoid doing things “the hard way.” Could more consistently amplify, reflect, or expand this change talk using values, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with resistance and accepts Marcus’s doubt about fingersticks and readiness. Could use more MI-style double-sided reflections and ambivalence work rather than mainly practical redirection.
