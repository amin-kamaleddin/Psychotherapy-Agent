# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Richard’s concerns about medication and side effects. The acceptance was strong but somewhat brief and functional rather than deeply emotionally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were several empathic responses to Richard’s central ambivalence about staying active while fearing muscle problems. Empathy became inconsistent when the psychiatrist shifted into informational or task-focused responses before fully exploring his anxiety and frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on cholesterol management, medication adherence, labs, follow-up, and warning signs. The direction was generally strong, though it became overly logistical and medical at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by asking for preferences, offering options, and inviting rehearsal, and Richard’s feedback shaped parts of the session. However, much of the content was clinician-supplied rather than co-created, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and encouraging Richard to use his own voice. Autonomy was limited at times by directive advising, scripts, checklists, and specific instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited initial motivations around retirement, staying active, and hiking with Richard’s wife. However, the counselor often supplied reasons, scripts, and solutions instead of drawing out Richard’s own values, confidence, and deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed partnership through open invitations, listening, and adjusting to Richard’s preferences, such as using his own words. The stance became more expert-driven when the psychiatrist provided detailed medical information, scripts, and checklists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Richard produced meaningful change talk about wanting to stay active, make the call, and plan for retirement, but the psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on it. The session moved quickly into logistics and supplied language rather than expanding the client’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist softened concerns by normalizing muscle worries and offering a low-dose trial as a manageable test step. Softening was limited at times because additional focus on risks, labs, and warning signs could keep attention on potential problems rather than motivation or self-efficacy.
