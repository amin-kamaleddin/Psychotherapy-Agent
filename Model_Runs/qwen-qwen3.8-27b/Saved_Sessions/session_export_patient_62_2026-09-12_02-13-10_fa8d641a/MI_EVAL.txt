# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating Jordan's pain, ambivalence, and resistance. Acceptance is strong but can feel formulaic and lacks more individualized affirmation of Jordan's worth beyond his struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate reflections that capture exhaustion, discouragement, and ambivalence about work and hope. Empathy is solid but does not consistently explore deeper painful themes such as shame, fear of failure, or loss of self-worth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Jordan's job-related stuckness and offers concrete, manageable next steps. Direction is clear, but it becomes overly task-focused before Jordan's ambivalence and motivation are fully explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks for preferences and offers options, creating a collaborative tone. However, the frequent provision of prompts, lists, and drafted suggestions shifts the interaction toward expert-driven problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly affirms choice, avoids pressure, and respects Jordan's request to pause. Autonomy support is undermined somewhat by continuing to present multiple task-based options after Jordan says he does not want another checklist.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses open questions and some reflections to invite Jordan's priorities and change-oriented language. Evocation is limited because it does not deeply explore Jordan's values, self-efficacy, or deeper reasons for change before introducing tasks.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and avoids fully directive language, but the session becomes unevenly collaborative. Jordan's sense of being assigned tasks indicates that the partnership did not consistently position him as the expert on his own direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor encourages Jordan to identify priorities and reflects some change-oriented statements, such as wanting something to go right. Change talk is not consistently amplified or developed into desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with sustain talk and validates Jordan's doubts without dismissal. The counselor does not consistently pivot hopelessness toward change, and some reflections may reinforce the belief that effort will not help.
