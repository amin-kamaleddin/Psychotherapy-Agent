# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, acceptance, and nonjudgment without moralizing about weight, eating, or failure. However, the acceptance was sometimes formulaic or managerial rather than deeply individualized to Sofia’s full emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was adequate but often reflected surface-level content before moving into problem-solving. The psychiatrist did not consistently explore deeper feelings such as exhaustion, chronic self-criticism, or fear of repeating past failures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and moved effectively from broad concerns to a specific, concrete micro-behavior plan. Some parts became overly procedural, but the overall direction remained clear and purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction was practically collaborative, with Sofia selecting and tailoring options to fit her life. However, the psychiatrist supplied most of the content and structure, making the collaboration less balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by asking Sofia to choose among options and respecting her preferences. Nevertheless, the session remained advice-driven, with detailed scripts and recommendations that could have reduced Sofia’s sense of self-generated decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence scale helped elicit some action-oriented change talk and increase Sofia’s confidence. However, the psychiatrist did not consistently draw out deeper personal values, hopes, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was meaningful collaboration in that Sofia’s preferences shaped the final plan. However, the psychiatrist maintained an expert problem-solving stance and did not fully position Sofia as the expert on her own life and patterns.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited practical change talk and helped Sofia articulate a specific plan to increase confidence. Deeper change talk related to desire, importance, values, or ability was not consistently developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk in a nonargumentative and validating way. However, concerns were often met with additional advice rather than reflective pivots that could transform ambivalence into change talk.
