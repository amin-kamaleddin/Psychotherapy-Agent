# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client's concerns. Acceptance is solid, though it sometimes blends into task-focused guidance rather than consistently demonstrating deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective statements accurately capture surface concerns and help keep the client engaged. Empathy is limited by quick shifts to advice and insufficient exploration of deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation, key triggers, and a concrete first step. There is some drift into procedural medication details, but the focus remains generally clear and behaviorally relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and adapts the plan based on his preferences. Collaboration is present but uneven, as the client often responds to the psychiatrist's suggestions rather than co-creating the plan from his own perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is offered options and the plan is narrowed to a smaller experiment. However, the prescriptive dosing, scripts, and step-by-step instructions reduce the client's opportunity to generate his own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies heavily on external information, medication details, and practical scripts. The client's internal motivation, values, and reasons for change are not consistently evoked or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes client choices and avoids a purely expert-driven stance. Partnership is limited by the strong expert role maintained around medication, dosing, and behavioral scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses some willingness and change talk, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it. Change talk is not consistently expanded, amplified, or consolidated using follow-up questions or DTARC strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Barriers are acknowledged and normalized without argument. Softening is limited because the session remains focused on obstacles and logistics rather than pivoting back to values, strengths, or reasons for change.
