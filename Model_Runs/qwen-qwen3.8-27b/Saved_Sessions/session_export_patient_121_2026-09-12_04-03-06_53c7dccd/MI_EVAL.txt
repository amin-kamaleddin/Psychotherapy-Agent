# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes the client's guardedness without shaming. The warmth is strong but sometimes formulaic, and the psychiatrist moves quickly into problem-solving before fully expressing unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of surface-level frustration, unfairness, and safety concerns. However, the empathy often misses deeper emotional layers such as hurt, fear of rejection, isolation, and exhaustion of being monitored or blamed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the client's presenting concern about peer conflict and class-based distress. Direction could be stronger by collaboratively selecting one clear target instead of cycling through several solution options before narrowing the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates the client's constraints, especially around hidden class-notebook notes, and asks for her input on doable options. Collaboration is weakened by repeatedly presenting expert-generated menus and scripts before the client has fully generated her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's choice and avoids pressure, including allowing her to skip options and framing the final plan as a trial. Minor pressure emerges when the psychiatrist continues offering options after the client has already expressed resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evocative questions about what would be different, what has helped, and what would show improvement. Evocation is inconsistent because the psychiatrist often shifts to therapist-generated solutions before fully drawing out the client's own values, motivations, and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts the plan based on the client's preferences, showing some collaboration. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently takes on an expert/problem-solver role by generating scripts, options, and safety plans despite the client's stated need for autonomy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk by asking open questions and highlighting the client's desire for less exhaustion and safer coping. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, expand, or deepen the client's own change language, and introduces solutions before fully evoking motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk and often validates concerns about being misunderstood or monitored. The skill is inconsistent because the conversation remains focused on reasons not to act, and the psychiatrist relies more on alternative plans than on MI-consistent strategies to soften resistance.
