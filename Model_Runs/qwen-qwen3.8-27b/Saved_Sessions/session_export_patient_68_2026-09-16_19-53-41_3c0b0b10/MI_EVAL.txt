# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids labeling Kevin, but the stance feels procedural rather than deeply warm. More explicit affirmations of effort and values would strengthen positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence between social connection and injury concern, but remains mostly cognitive. Deeper emotional reflections about embarrassment, fear, and stress would improve empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, ankle injury, and social evenings, but cycles through similar questions without consolidating progress. Summarizing and narrowing to a client-owned focus would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin’s ideas shape the discussion, though the interviewer’s repeated structured questions make it feel guided rather than fully co-directed. More client choice in agenda and process would enhance collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer avoids direct advice and respects Kevin’s readiness, but words like safer and experiment subtly imply harm reduction. Explicitly affirming Kevin’s full ownership, including not changing, would improve autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions encourage Kevin to identify options, but the interviewer does not consistently elicit or expand deeper values, fears, and confidence. More amplification of Kevin’s own change talk is needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Kevin as the expert and uses collaborative language, but the approach becomes formulaic. More explicit power-sharing and agenda negotiation would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin generates useful change talk, but the interviewer rarely amplifies it or explores confidence, desire, ability, and commitment. The session shifts too quickly to practical problem-solving rather than deepening motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer handles sustain talk without arguing and uses double-sided reflections, but sometimes keeps focus on problem language. More frequent pivots from sustain talk to values and safer options would improve this criterion.
