# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoids lecturing. However, the tone feels formulaic and the repeated scripts/checklists after the client asks to step back weaken genuine attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some basic reflections, but they remain superficial and logistical. The psychiatrist misses deeper reflections of Seamus’s frustration, defensiveness, and past negative medication experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear MI-consistent direction and becomes repetitive and procedural. The flow is overdirected rather than purposefully moving toward a collaboratively chosen goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is driven more by the clinician’s suggestions, scripts, and checklists than by shared decision-making. Seamus’s clear boundary to stop the prep work is not fully honored, making the interaction feel directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms choice and boundaries, but autonomy support is inconsistent because directive content continues after Seamus asks for less input. Stronger support would involve asking permission before offering information and following the client’s lead.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited evocative efforts, and the session quickly shifts into advice-giving. The psychiatrist does not deeply explore Seamus’s values, goals, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally invites input, but the stance remains expert-driven. Seamus’s pushback about managing things himself is not fully reflected in a more equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Attempts to evoke change talk are limited and not followed up meaningfully. The readiness ruler and client’s later opening about possibly stopping later are not developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue, but the scripts and checklists reinforce the status-quo and avoidance frame. There is little use of double-sided reflections or reframing to gently open space for change.
