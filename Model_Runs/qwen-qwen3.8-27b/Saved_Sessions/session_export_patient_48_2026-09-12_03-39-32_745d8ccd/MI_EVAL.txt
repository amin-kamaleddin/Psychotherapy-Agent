# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the patient’s constraints and preferences. A 5 would require slightly deeper positive regard around self-doubt and fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects ambivalence, time pressure, perfectionism, and relief when pressure is reduced. A 5 would require more emotionally nuanced reflections of fear of being judged or “ruining it.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on increasing physical activity and maintains clear momentum toward a specific, actionable step. The psychiatrist anchors the conversation to the behavior-change goal while responding to concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner, and the patient substantially shapes the step, backup, timing, and check-in format. The session feels genuinely co-created rather than prescriptive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers options, asks for preference, avoids pressure, and honors the patient’s stated boundaries. The language consistently reinforces that the patient is in charge of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers help the patient express willingness and a small sense of ownership. Evocation would strengthen if it drew out the patient’s own values, reasons, and hopes more consistently rather than relying on clinician-provided options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and lets the patient’s preferences shape the plan. A 5 would require more explicit statements that the patient is the expert on his own capacity and slightly less agenda-setting in how options are framed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session evokes meaningful change talk around willingness, confidence, and commitment, especially with the confidence ruler and doability questions. Stronger skill would reflect and amplify that change talk more consistently, especially around values and personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with barriers and effectively reframes perfectionism, fatigue, and fear of failure in a low-pressure way. A 5 would require more consistent strategic redirecting, such as double-sided reflections.
