# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment or labeling. The counselor directly counters self-criticism with warmth (e.g., 'You're not trying to be an idiot. You're not') and normalizes Tyler's experience repeatedly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate reflective listening that captures specific emotional and cognitive experience rather than surface-level paraphrases. Notable for picking up on subtle cues like the double 'I don't know' before a risky disclosure and articulating the exact experiential gap Tyler is feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question and reflection ties back to the core issue with clear strategic purpose. The session progresses logically from understanding the incident, to exploring the emotional undercurrent, to identifying the coping loop, to a tentative actionable first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is structured as a joint exploration where Tyler's words, metaphors, and ideas drive the content and direction. The counselor builds on Tyler's language ('the shortcut,' 'the track,' 'putting it on the shelf') rather than imposing clinical frameworks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tyler consistently directs the session, and the counselor never imposes a plan or uses directive language. When Tyler suggests 'a rule or something,' the counselor allows it to stand without validating or invalidating, preserving Tyler's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is elicited from within Tyler rather than provided externally. The counselor's questions are designed to draw out Tyler's own reasoning, leading to self-generated insights about the loop, the shortcut, and a first step toward structure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong exploratory language and shared responsibility ('you don't have to figure it out alone') maintain a collaborative stance. Could be elevated by more explicit acknowledgment of Tyler as the expert on his own experience and by inviting his input on session direction more frequently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively picks up and reflects Tyler's change talk through accurate summaries. Could be strengthened by more explicit amplification of change statements (e.g., 'You just said you could probably have a rule—what does that feel like?') or scaling questions to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates Tyler's concerns about social connection and identity without arguing or dismissing them. Could employ more strategic double-sided reflections or pivots to gently explore the gap between justifications ('it's just one') and Tyler's stated values about education and safety.
