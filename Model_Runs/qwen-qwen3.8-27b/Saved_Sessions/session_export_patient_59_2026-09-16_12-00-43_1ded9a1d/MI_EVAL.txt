# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids arguing or labeling while acknowledging the client’s frustration and lack of pressure. Warmth is present but somewhat task-focused, with fewer deeper affirmations of the client’s strengths or personhood.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s practical, compliance-focused perspective and impatience with the process. However, empathy remains mostly cognitive and factual, with limited exploration of underlying emotions such as fear, frustration, or peer/family dynamics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear focus on marijuana use and related life areas, but quickly becomes administrative due to the client’s resistance and practical concerns. The counselor keeps direction clear but does not consistently guide the conversation back toward the behavioral target or small risk-reducing steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works collaboratively by offering options, adapting to the client’s choices, and using language that supports joint problem-solving. Collaboration is strong but partly therapist-led because the counselor controls administrative next steps and available options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated emphasis on the client’s freedom to choose and respect for his refusal to involve his mother or make commitments. The counselor avoids pressure and consistently frames the client as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses open questions to evoke the client’s perspective and some commitment language, but evocation remains procedural and underdeveloped. There is insufficient follow-up on evocative moments to connect the client’s desire to finish the requirement with broader values or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and repeatedly invites the client’s preference in decisions. Collaboration is somewhat limited because the counselor still directs logistics and provides prestructured options rather than co-creating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor makes some efforts to evoke change talk by asking about desired change, concerns about staying the same, and affirming the follow-up commitment. These efforts are limited by a lack of deeper follow-up on the client’s values, goals, or safety-related reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s minimizing sustain talk and offers choices. However, the counselor does not use more effective MI-consistent strategies such as double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy to redirect the status-quo framing.
