# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Some affirmations feel generic, and the client’s final ambivalence is not fully acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and capture the client’s emotional experience. Empathy is sometimes followed quickly by advice or structured options, limiting deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior and ends with a realistic plan. It does not fully consolidate the client’s values or resolve the final uncertainty.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes meaningfully and shapes parts of the session. However, the therapist frequently uses structured options and tools, creating a trainer-like dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and avoids pressuring the client to commit. Some suggestions remain somewhat prescriptive, reducing full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help elicit the client’s own motivations. Evocation is diluted by frequent advice and technique suggestions rather than deeper follow-up.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites input and avoids an authoritarian stance. Repeated use of menus and structured options weakens the sense of true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is generated, but it is not consistently deepened or amplified. The therapist moves too quickly to concrete techniques and options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not argued with or dismissed. The therapist could use more double-sided reflections and better address the final sustain talk.
