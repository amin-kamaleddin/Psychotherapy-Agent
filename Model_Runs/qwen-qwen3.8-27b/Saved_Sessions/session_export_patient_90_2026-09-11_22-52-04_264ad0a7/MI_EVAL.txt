# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, validating tone and avoids shaming. Acceptance is slightly limited by persistent corrective medical messaging.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's concerns and stress. Empathy remains mostly surface-level and does not deeply explore underlying ambivalence or emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and moves toward concrete action steps. The direction is clinically clear but somewhat rigid and less collaboratively defined.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does much of the problem-solving and offers pre-made scripts. The patient remains largely a passive recipient rather than an active co-creator of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and supports the patient's control over process. Directive statements about medical necessity reduce MI-style autonomy support around the content of care.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides reasons and solutions instead of drawing out the patient's own motivations. Opportunities to elicit the patient's own change talk and resources were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A collaborative stance is initiated, but the psychiatrist quickly takes an expert-driven role. The patient contributes less to planning, making the power dynamic uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to evoke the patient's own reasons for change are rare and weak. The psychiatrist supplies medical rationale and solutions rather than amplifying the patient's own desired changes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with counterarguments, fixes, and problem-solving. This can increase defensiveness rather than help the patient explore and resolve ambivalence.
