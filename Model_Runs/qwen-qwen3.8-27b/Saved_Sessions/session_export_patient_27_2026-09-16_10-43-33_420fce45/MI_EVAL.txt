# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally shows respect, nonjudgment, and normalization of the client's ambivalence. However, warmth sometimes becomes evaluative rather than fully individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's worry, ambivalence, and fear of being labeled. However, they often stay surface-level and move quickly into practical suggestions before deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the client's diabetes disclosure and fear of stigma. It becomes somewhat mechanical at times, with repeated attention to crafting exact wording rather than deeper motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows the client to choose among options. However, collaboration is limited because the counselor frequently supplies scripts, wording, and practical solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly affirms the client's freedom to choose and respects her boundary to stop for the day. However, repeated scripted suggestions can subtly steer the client's choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used, but they are not deep or consistent enough to fully elicit the client's values or motivation. The counselor often shifts quickly into advice and problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some collaboration by inviting the client to choose and adjust options. However, the interaction remains somewhat expert-driven because the counselor provides most of the content and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses some change talk, such as wanting not to keep hiding it or not feeling stuck next time. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates hesitation and does not argue with the client's fears. However, the repeated focus on scripts and possible reactions keeps attention on obstacles rather than redirecting toward values or reasons for change.
