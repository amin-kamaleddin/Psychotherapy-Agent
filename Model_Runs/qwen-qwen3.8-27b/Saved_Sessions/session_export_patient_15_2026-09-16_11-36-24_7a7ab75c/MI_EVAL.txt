# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms the client’s strengths. However, acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not consistently validate the client’s ambivalence, minimization, or emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides some accurate reflections of the client’s plans and confidence. Yet empathy remains surface-level and misses deeper emotional meaning, especially around avoidance, low urgency, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on concrete blood pressure self-management, with organized attention to walking, salt reduction, start time, and follow-up. The main limitation is that it remains narrow and does not clearly address broader concerns such as alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the client to build a practical plan and incorporates the client’s preferences and contributions. The interaction is still fairly therapist-led and structured, limiting fully client-directed collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites client choice, honors the preference for simplicity, and avoids overt pressure. Autonomy support is stronger when the client leads, but suggestions and specific strategies can shift the interaction toward counselor-driven problem-solving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions and a confidence scale to draw out intention and readiness. However, evocation is often superficial and gives way to logistical planning rather than deeper exploration of values, concerns, or motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a collaborative stance by inviting the client’s input and treating preferences as important. The client’s choices shape the plan, but the psychiatrist still leads heavily and offers suggestions, reducing a fully client-led partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk related to action plans, confidence, and commitment. The client’s reasons for change, values, and desired outcomes are not consistently explored or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging the client and does not reinforce minimizing statements. However, there are limited opportunities to gently reflect the implications of downplaying risk or redirect sustain talk toward deeper consideration.
