# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, normalizes Kevin’s reaction, and affirms his values and effort. It falls short of a 5 because the acceptance is somewhat structured and does not fully address his shame, embarrassment, or self-worth apart from the behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses strong complex reflections that accurately capture Kevin’s ambivalence, social concerns, and conflict around safety versus stress relief. Empathy is limited by occasional interpretive framing and by not fully reflecting the emotional undercurrent, especially the final comment about forgetting to text.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on Friday drinking and moves coherently from ambivalence toward concrete action steps. Direction is strong but not fully completed because the session ends without a clear summary, reinforcement, or consolidation of the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a strength: Kevin’s own ideas largely shape the direction of the session, and the psychiatrist builds on them without imposing a solution. The counselor also uses Kevin’s language, such as “taking it slow” and “choosing later,” which strengthens the collaborative process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Kevin’s stated boundary and frames the plan as his own choice rather than prescribed change. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly affirming his freedom to choose not to act and by reducing questions that assume he will try.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Kevin’s own motivations, discrepancies, and practical ideas through open questions. The score is lower than 5 because the counselor sometimes supplies options or structure instead of fully eliciting Kevin’s values, confidence, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites Kevin’s input and treats him as the expert on his own life, allowing him to generate much of the plan. However, the stance is not fully partner-led at all times because the psychiatrist still introduces guiding categories and a noticeable structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin produces substantial change talk, including ideas to delay drinks, use a low-key cue, involve Dave, and create a safer Friday. The counselor could cultivate change talk more deeply by explicitly eliciting confidence, commitment, and values rather than occasionally steering toward preferred next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively acknowledges and reflects Kevin’s reasons for maintaining drinking, such as the buzz, friends, and stress relief. The session could soften sustain talk further by dwelling longer in those reasons before moving into problem-solving and planning.
