# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is polite, nonjudgmental, and reassuring, but the warmth remains functional and administrative. Genuine positive regard and individualized acceptance are limited, partly undermined by the inaccurate opening statement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately summarize the client’s logistical concerns about forms, time, and band. However, they miss the deeper anxiety, shame, or ambivalence associated with being flagged as high-risk for alcohol.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has clear logistical focus but is misdirected away from the relevant target behavior, alcohol use and its implications. The conversation remains focused on forms, signatures, and scheduling rather than motivational or clinical exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction appears collaborative through repeated options but remains helper-driven and directive. The client’s own perspective, values, and expertise are not meaningfully integrated into the process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s pacing and avoids pressure or persuasion. Autonomy support is present but limited to logistical choices, not extended to values, goals, or self-direction regarding alcohol use.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is no meaningful effort to draw out the client’s motivations, values, fears, or hopes related to alcohol or other relevant concerns. Missed opportunities, such as exploring what playing music and school mean to her, are not followed up.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses structured options that create an appearance of choice while maintaining control of the agenda. The dynamic is more administrative and service-oriented than a genuine partnership between equals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No questions or reflections are used to evoke desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. The client produces no change talk, and the psychiatrist does not attempt to elicit it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client minimizes the significance of the alcohol screening, and the psychiatrist does not reinforce that sustain talk. However, the response does not meaningfully reframe or explore the underlying ambivalence, focusing instead on reassurance and logistics.
