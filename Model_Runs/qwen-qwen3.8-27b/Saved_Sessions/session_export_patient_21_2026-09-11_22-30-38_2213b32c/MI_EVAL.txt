# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates the client’s goals and modifications without judgment or pressure. However, the affirmations remain somewhat procedural and generic rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows understanding of the client’s perspective and addresses practical concerns like the post-lunch slump. However, reflections are largely cognitive rather than deeply attuned to the client’s emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on increasing physical activity and follows a logical, structured progression. There is no topic drift, and every intervention ties back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is surface-level collaboration as the client contributes ideas like the after-coffee cue and label changes. However, the psychiatrist generates most of the plan, making the interaction feel more consultative than equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports client ownership by accepting modifications and emphasizing that the plan is the client’s to control. It could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client’s right to stop or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some questions and allows the client to contribute, but the session relies heavily on offering suggestions. There is limited exploration of the client’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and the client makes some adjustments, showing a collaborative tone. However, the overall dynamic is clinician-led, with the client largely accepting a pre-formed plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates several examples of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or deepen them. More strategic reflection, scaling, or exploration of motivation could strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk effectively by normalizing setbacks and reframing skipping as a starting point. The responses are supportive, though sustain talk is not used more actively to explore ambivalence.
