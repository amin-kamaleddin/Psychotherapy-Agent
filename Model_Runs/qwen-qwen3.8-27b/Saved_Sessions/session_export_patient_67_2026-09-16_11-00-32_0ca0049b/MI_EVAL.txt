# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed respect and nonjudgment, validating the patient's worry about being seen as a problem. Comments avoided shaming and normalized the difficulty of disclosing the driving incident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate and complex reflections of ambivalence, fear, and the desire to focus on health information. Statements such as 'you're torn' and 'that makes sense' captured the patient's mixed feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on reducing alcohol use and related safety. The conversation moved clearly from assessment to safety planning to a concrete appointment and drinking-reduction plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative moves, such as choosing doable steps and co-creating a note for the doctor. However, several turns were more expert-driven, with the psychiatrist recommending tests, limits, and a script before the patient generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly supported the patient's choice, emphasized that the patient could decide what to try, and avoided requiring promises. The patient selected Monday and Tuesday, weekend limits, rideshares, and the note-based disclosure approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some patient-generated insights emerged through scaling questions and questions about what feels most important. More consistent evocation of the patient's own reasons, values, and solutions would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist included partnership moves such as asking permission, offering options, and inviting the patient to choose. At times the role shifted toward advisor, especially when giving specific medical recommendations and a prepared script.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change talk through scaling, safety concerns, and discussion of health risks. Change talk could be strengthened by more systematically eliciting and amplifying the patient's own reasons and desires to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged, such as the patient not wanting to quit yet or avoid cold turkey. It was not consistently softened with double-sided reflections or other moves that reduce resistance.
