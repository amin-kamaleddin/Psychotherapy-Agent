# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Linda’s concerns without moralizing. Acceptance is strong but sometimes formulaic, using mildly evaluative language such as “right thing” rather than deeper individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective statements accurately capture Linda’s ambivalence between wanting relief and fearing medication, surveillance, and failure. The empathy is generally accurate but remains more cognitive than emotional, especially around her fear of being “watched.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma control, inhaler use, side-effect concerns, and a concrete two-week plan. The main drift is spending time on safety criteria and checklist formatting, which could otherwise be used to deepen motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts the plan to Linda’s preferences, such as simple tracking, a bedtime rating, and a short reminder phrase. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still organizes much of the structure and follow-up plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking permission, offering options, and allowing Linda to modify the plan. Autonomy support is slightly limited by mildly prescriptive or approving language, such as “you’re doing the right thing.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some motivation and planning by asking about importance, confidence, doability, start time, and cues. However, much of the session relies on psychoeducation and structure rather than drawing out Linda’s deeper values, reasons, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels collaborative rather than purely directive, with input sought on preferences, ease, timing, and reminders. Partnership would improve by more explicitly positioning Linda as the expert on her own routine and body.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and helps Linda identify a concrete first step, such as starting Monday and using the spacer. The main limitation is not consistently amplifying or linking Linda’s statements to values, self-efficacy, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles barriers well by reframing fears about dependence, side effects, tracking, and failure in a balanced way. The repeated emphasis on emergency signs may inadvertently keep the worst-case scenario salient.
