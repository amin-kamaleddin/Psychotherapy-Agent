# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and nonjudgment throughout, using warm and affirming language. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth is sometimes procedural and shifts into an educational/expert stance that slightly distances from Linda's emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, especially in the early session. The empathy is not consistently deep because the psychiatrist sometimes focuses more on content and planning than on feeling and misses opportunities to reflect more layered emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on the target behavior: asthma management, inhaler use, and medication adherence. The psychiatrist also guides the conversation toward concrete next steps, including a minimal one-week tracking plan, phone reminders, a calming phrase, a confidence rating, and a reward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally creates a collaborative interaction rather than a one-way advisory conversation. The collaboration is not fully 5 because the psychiatrist at times introduces detailed plans and clinical information before fully eliciting Linda's preferred level of structure or meaning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports Linda's sense of choice and avoids overt pressure. A 5 would require more consistent and explicit emphasis on Linda's ownership of the process, especially during moments when the psychiatrist provides suggestions or thresholds.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some efforts to evoke motivation from within Linda by asking what she wants to focus on, what worries her, and what small change might feel helpful. However, the session contains a substantial amount of information-giving and planning that is not primarily evocative, and the psychiatrist misses several opportunities to deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Linda's input and adjusts the plan based on her preferences, which supports partnership to some degree. However, the psychiatrist still frequently assumes the expert role, which can make the power balance feel uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are meaningful efforts to evoke change talk, including asking what Linda would like to change and what small change might feel helpful. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently develop or amplify this change talk, missing opportunities to reflect or build on her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk and often responds to it in a respectful, normalizing way. However, the softening is not consistently MI-consistent, as the psychiatrist sometimes responds more with direct information or reassurance than with MI strategies such as double-sided reflections.
