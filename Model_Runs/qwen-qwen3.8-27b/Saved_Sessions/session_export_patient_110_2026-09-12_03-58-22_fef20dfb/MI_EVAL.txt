# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and affirms the client's choices. However, acceptance feels somewhat procedural and does not deeply acknowledge the emotional weight of his situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects ambivalence and concern, but relies on repetitive reflections and misses opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as fear of heart disease, family history, and loss of independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on cholesterol management and the medication decision, progressing logically from ambivalence to a practical plan. Direction is strong, though information delivery sometimes comes at the expense of deeper emotional exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites input, but often shifts into an expert/informant role by providing detailed information, lists, and a completed plan. There is limited genuine co-construction with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports autonomy by asking for permission, offering options, and avoiding pressure toward medication. It could be stronger if the client's ownership of the decision were more explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses questions and confidence scaling to elicit client motivations, but frequently moves into information provision instead of staying client-led. Change talk is drawn out but not consistently deepened or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates structural partnership by offering choices and seeking input, but the session remains largely counselor-directed through predefined content and a structured pathway. True collaborative partnership is present but underdeveloped.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces meaningful change talk, such as wanting to try lifestyle changes first and prove he can do it on his own. The psychiatrist affirms these statements but rarely reflects, amplifies, or strategically builds on them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately validates the client's reluctance to start medication immediately and avoids arguing against his sustain talk. However, there is limited use of MI-specific techniques, such as double-sided reflections or strategic pivots, to soften it.
