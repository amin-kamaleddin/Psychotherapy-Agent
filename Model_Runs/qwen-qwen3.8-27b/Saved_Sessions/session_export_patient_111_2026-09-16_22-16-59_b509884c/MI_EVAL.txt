# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, nonjudgment, and validation of Richard’s perspective and boundaries. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicit affirmation of his strengths, values, or willingness to engage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Richard’s ambivalence, sense of pressure, and the functional role of smoking in managing daily stress. The counselor could deepen empathy by attending more to underlying emotions such as fear, humiliation, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays reasonably focused on the target behavior within the context of work breaks and daily routine. Direction could be sharper by more explicitly connecting the conversation to smoking reduction or cessation and a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the interaction as a partnership and consistently invites Richard’s input and leadership. The client’s ideas clearly shape the content and direction of the session without expert imposition.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through consistent emphasis on Richard’s choice, ownership, and self-direction. The closing plan is a clear example of autonomy-supportive practice, as it is entirely client-defined.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Richard’s own values, boundaries, and preferred way of coping, which supports client-centered exploration. However, evocation of internal change motivation and confidence to reduce or stop smoking remains limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positions Richard as the expert on his own life and avoids taking an instructive or agenda-driven stance. There is strong evidence of shared control and mutual respect throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-centered language and a small self-directed behavior experiment emerge, but explicit change talk about smoking cessation is not consistently cultivated. More strategic questioning could help Richard articulate his own reasons, abilities, or commitments for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and maintains a nonconfrontational stance. More skillful softening could include double-sided reflections or strategic reframing that gently opens the possibility for change.
