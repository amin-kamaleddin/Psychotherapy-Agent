# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the client’s mixed feelings and affirming her cautious planning. The affirmations are slightly generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s caution and desire for safety, but the empathy remains mostly cognitive and problem-focused. More emotional attunement to her surprise, anxiety, and small losses would be needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear target: the statin prescription, safety concerns, grapefruit interaction, labs, and follow-up plan. Minor drift occurs from repeated script offers and detailed information, but focus is generally strong.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates the client’s preferences and helps build a workable plan. However, the psychiatrist dominates much of the content and repeatedly offers the same support, making the process less equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s autonomy, including her decision to wait for labs before starting the medication. The extensive safety instructions and recommendations make the session feel somewhat advisory.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses several evoking questions that help the client articulate readiness and next steps. Much of the session, however, is didactic rather than focused on drawing out the client’s deeper values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites input, but the session becomes information-heavy and repeatedly offers scripts or role-play. This makes the interaction feel more expert-led than truly co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk and affirms the client’s readiness to try the medication. There are missed opportunities to amplify and strategically reflect the client’s own language about safety and health.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles the client’s hesitation about medication and preference for grapefruit juice without arguing or becoming directive. The responses could be more sophisticated by using double-sided reflections to soften ambivalence.
