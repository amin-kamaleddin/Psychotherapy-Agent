# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, with statements that normalize Timur’s hesitation and protect his dignity. Acceptance is strong, though it could be deeper by more explicitly affirming Timur as a person beyond his record, choices, and performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is good reflective listening and an accurate summary of Timur’s ambivalence, pride, and practical barriers. Empathy could be stronger by naming deeper emotional undercurrents such as shame, fear of humiliation, and identity loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior, linking employment, the probation officer, and the immediate call to Timur’s broader goal of stability and staying out of trouble. Direction could improve by periodically connecting practical steps back to values and self-respect.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Timur’s choices clearly shape the plan, especially the co-created script and follow-up format. Collaboration could be more exemplary by allowing Timur to generate more of the next steps and language before the psychiatrist offers options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, avoids coercion, and respects Timur’s boundaries around timing and follow-up. Autonomy support could be stronger by consistently returning to Timur’s own ideas and wording before introducing concrete plans or scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some effective evocative questions, especially around readiness and what a real chance would mean. However, the session shifts toward problem-solving and scripting too early, limiting opportunities to draw out Timur’s deeper values, confidence, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a partnership stance, but it is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently introduces structured options and scripts before fully eliciting Timur’s own plan. A stronger partnership approach would have asked more open questions first and made clearer that the expertise about Timur’s best next step resides in him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits useful change talk, including willingness to call the probation officer, take a trial shift, and use a proud, short script. The psychiatrist could strengthen this by more consistently amplifying and expanding change talk, linking it to Timur’s values, identity, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without argument or shame, and the psychiatrist normalizes difficulty while preserving Timur’s dignity. Softening could be enhanced by using more advanced MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to work directly with both sides of his ambivalence.
