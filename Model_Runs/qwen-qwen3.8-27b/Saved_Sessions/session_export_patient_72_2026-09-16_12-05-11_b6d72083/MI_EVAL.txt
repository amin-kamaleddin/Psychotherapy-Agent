# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed respect, support, and nonjudgmental affirmations without shaming the patient’s barriers. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicit normalization of past lapses, gym-pressure concerns, and self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated good reflective listening and accurate summaries of the patient’s goals and practical barriers. Empathy could be improved by exploring the emotional meaning behind the goals, such as frustration, self-doubt, and fear of turning the plan into homework.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear target behavior and moved logically from goals to a specific, actionable plan. Direction was strong, with no meaningful topic drift and a concrete final plan tied to the patient’s goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was collaborative, with the patient choosing the activity, schedule, backup plan, and simplified prompts. Full collaboration would involve more balanced idea generation before the clinician offers specific solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offered choice, respected the patient’s decisions, and supported a no-guilt missed-day plan. Autonomy support could be stronger with more explicit statements of patient ownership and freedom to change the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early evocation was effective through questions about meaning, past consistency, importance, and confidence. Evocation became shallower during planning, as the psychiatrist shifted toward logistical suggestions without fully drawing out deeper intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited patient input when selecting and refining options. Partnership could be enhanced by more explicit power-sharing and asking the patient to generate the next step before offering suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited change talk through open questions and scaling, drawing out the patient’s reasons for change. Change talk could be deepened by reflecting, amplifying, and summarizing the patient’s own motivations before moving into logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by avoiding argument and converting concerns into practical, low-pressure coping plans. Sustain talk could be softened further with double-sided reflections and deeper normalization of the patient’s hesitation.
