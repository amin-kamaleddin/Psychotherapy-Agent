# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while avoiding blame or shaming. Acceptance is strong but not complete because the final concern about the notes making her “hate herself more” is not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate reflections and acknowledges frustration, loneliness, and fear. Empathy remains limited by frequent focus on surface-level behaviors rather than deeper body shame, permanence, and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the client’s weight-loss frustration, self-esteem, and need for support. Direction is generally clear, though offering multiple topics and practical suggestions at once slightly reduces focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting the client to choose note-taking methods and the mom-message approach. Collaboration is strong but not advanced because the counselor supplies much of the structure, scripts, and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports choice, offers options, and explicitly allows the client to stop if the plan feels like pressure. Autonomy support is strong but reduced by the volume of counselor-generated scripts, tips, and structured suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some motivation by asking what matters, how important change is, and what would raise confidence. Evocation is limited by premature advice-giving and insufficient exploration of the client’s internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session reflects a collaborative stance, with the client’s preferences clearly shaping the plan and conversation. Partnership is strong but not perfect because the counselor frequently provides preformed options and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some change talk, including desire to feel less stuck, recognition of strength, and commitment to small actions. Change talk is not consistently amplified or deepened through client-generated values, strengths, or confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue against the client’s reasons for staying the same and softens some self-critical language. Softening is limited because sustain talk such as “I’m built this way” and the final worry about self-hatred are not fully reframed or balanced.
