# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental acceptance. Client concerns are affirmed without correction or disapproval, even when he raises social discomfort and urges.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing the client's shift toward being present with his son. Some reflections remain more summarizing than emotionally exploratory, especially around guilt, fear, and identity change.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol use in social settings and concrete behavioral targets. The conversation remains relevant, specific, and change-oriented throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites joint problem-solving and allows the client's ideas to shape the plan. Collaboration is strong, though the therapist still guides through a fairly structured question sequence and occasional menu-like choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is consistently highlighted, and the psychiatrist avoids imposing rules or specific solutions. The client is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker and author of his own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's own motivations, values, and confidence through open-ended questions. The approach is evocative, though it could more consistently amplify and expand the client's strongest change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert on his own life and change process. Expert language is avoided, and the client is invited to define goals, obstacles, and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits concrete change talk related to family presence, self-direction, and specific weekend plans. Some opportunities to deepen change talk, such as confidence or importance scaling, are not consistently used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcement and is used as material for the client's problem-solving. The handling is appropriate, though it could include more explicit MI softening strategies such as double-sided reflections or value-based reframing.
