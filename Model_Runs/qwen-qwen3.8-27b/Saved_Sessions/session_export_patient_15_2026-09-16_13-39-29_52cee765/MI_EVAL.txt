# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the patient’s perspective without pressure. The warmth is professional and supportive, though not deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track the patient’s concern about inconsistent readings and his need for certainty. The empathy is practical and clear, but does not deeply explore underlying emotions or fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is focused and coherent around verifying blood pressure measurement. However, it does not guide toward the main behavioral targets of hypertension management or alcohol reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the patient to construct a practical plan and script for the clinic visit. Collaboration is strong but remains limited to the narrow measurement task.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices and avoids imposing advice, supporting the patient’s sense of control. Autonomy is respected but not fully consolidated as a broader change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help the patient articulate practical preferences and reasoning. The evocation does not deeply draw out values, concerns, or motivation for broader health behavior change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient’s perspective and allows his ideas to shape the next steps. The stance is collaborative, though the psychiatrist still leads much of the session structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session mainly stays on measurement accuracy and does not meaningfully generate change talk about healthier behaviors. The patient expresses some willingness to act, but not strong motivation for target change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the patient’s minimizing statements, which is appropriate. However, the conversation is not effectively redirected toward broader change or deeper motivation.
