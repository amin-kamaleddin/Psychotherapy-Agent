# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and nonjudgment, affirming Kevin’s values and goals. It was not a 5 because the warmth remained somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflected Kevin’s stated experiences and preferences in a helpful, grounded way. However, the empathy remained largely cognitive and did not fully explore the underlying emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing drinking-related risk during nights out. The session stayed tightly aligned with the presenting concern and moved efficiently toward concrete steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor co-created a practical plan with Kevin, allowing his preferences and constraints to shape the final decisions. The process was collaborative, though the counselor still directed much of the sequencing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supported choice throughout by offering options, accepting rejections, and emphasizing that no step was mandatory. It was not a 5 because the approach still included a fairly directive list of suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited some useful change talk, including Kevin’s plan to skip shots and use a self-check. However, the evocation was not deep enough and shifted quickly toward problem-solving rather than exploring Kevin’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a collaborative stance by inviting Kevin’s input and building the plan around his preferences. The interaction was not fully equal because the counselor still structured much of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited several change-related ideas and affirmed them in practical terms. However, there was limited amplification of Kevin’s own reasons for change or deeper development of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handled sustain talk well by accepting Kevin’s reasons for maintaining his behavior and reframing them into smaller compatible changes. It was not a 5 because there was less use of advanced reflective techniques to pivot sustain talk into change motivation.
