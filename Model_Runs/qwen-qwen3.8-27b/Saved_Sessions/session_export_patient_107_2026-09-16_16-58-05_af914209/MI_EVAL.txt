# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language that normalizes the client’s ambivalence about coffee. Acceptance is strong but sometimes sounds formulaic and does not fully demonstrate deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and captures the client’s balancing of routine, function, and health concerns. Empathy is mainly cognitive and does not deeply explore underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on coffee use and moves from tracking to noticing cues to considering small changes. Direction could be stronger by consolidating the discussion into a clearer, more concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas, such as tracking, decaf, and noticing taste changes, shape the conversation. Collaboration is evident but could be more explicit in co-creating goals and deciding the next step together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice without pressure, emphasizing that the client remains in the driver’s seat. Autonomy is respected by framing change as something the client may choose rather than something being imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections encourage the client to articulate his own reasons and possible steps. Evocation could be deeper with more strategic use of summaries, scaling, or values-based exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites the client’s input throughout. Partnership is clear but would be stronger with more explicit language about exploring the issue together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, including the client’s emphasis on choice and avoiding dependency. The work would be advanced further by using stronger techniques such as scaling, summarizing, and amplifying change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or judgment, allowing the client to express the value of coffee. Softening could be stronger by more consistently using double-sided reflections to create discrepancy and move toward change.
