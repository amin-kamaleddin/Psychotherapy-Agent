# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and reassuring, affirming the client’s effort without blame. It could be warmer by explicitly validating the underlying ambivalence around her evening routine.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The responses acknowledge the client’s behavior and common triggers but remain mostly factual and plan-focused. Deeper reflections of her fear of failure, value of the bath ritual, and mixed feelings are needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing evening wine intake, triggers, the bath routine, slip prevention, and a concrete next-evening plan. There is little topic drift, and the focus on action is a clear strength.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes key plan elements, such as tea and reading, one glass in the bath, and putting the bottle away. However, the psychiatrist often introduces multiple specific techniques, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and respecting the client’s reluctance to use a script show some autonomy support, but the psychiatrist repeatedly proposes a detailed action sequence that feels directive. More emphasis on the client designing and owning modifications would strengthen it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurs through the readiness ruler, doability questions, and trigger exploration, but the session moves quickly into advice. Fewer questions about values, hopes, and self-efficacy limit depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and lets the client select elements, but much of the plan is therapist-generated. Greater partnership would invite the client to generate more of the strategy before adding specific techniques.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk, such as choosing tea and reading, limiting the bath drink, and agreeing to try the plan. It relies more on proposing solutions than asking questions that expand the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and normalizes difficulty, but does not deeply reflect or explore the value of the bath ritual and the role of wine. More double-sided reflections would better soften sustain talk.
