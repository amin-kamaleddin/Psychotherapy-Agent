# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates respect and nonjudgment, reframing potential negatives as strengths (e.g., 'that's not pride, that's ownership'). Could be stronger with more explicit normalization of the difficulty of re-entry and affirmation of client worthiness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures the duality of Lars's experience and distinguishes between types of barriers. Reflections tend to stay at the cognitive/behavioral level, missing deeper emotional undertones such as shame around literacy and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary session structure with a clear, progressive arc from goal identification to concrete action steps. Each step builds on the previous one with momentum, and the psychiatrist never allows the conversation to wander while maintaining a client-centered pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing with inclusive language and genuine joint decision-making at key points. Could be elevated by more explicit acknowledgment of mutual expertise and slightly less structuring by the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent and skillful reinforcement of client agency throughout, with the psychiatrist asking Lars to generate solutions rather than suggesting them. Autonomy support is woven naturally into every interaction without being preachy or directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation with open questions that draw motivation entirely from within Lars, and skillful reflections that deepen self-motivation using his own words. The psychiatrist never lectures, argues, or provides external reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Lars as the expert in his own life and invites his input before directing. Could be strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language and explicit negotiation of the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits substantial change talk and responds with amplifying reflections and strategic questions that deepen it. Could be stronger with more explicit consolidation and summarization of Lars's best change talk at key moments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively without dismissal or moralizing, integrated into the conversation with validating reflections. Could be stronger by using double-sided reflections to actively pivot sustain talk toward change rather than simply acknowledging it.
