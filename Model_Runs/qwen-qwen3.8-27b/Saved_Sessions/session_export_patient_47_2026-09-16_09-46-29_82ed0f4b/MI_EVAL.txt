# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and consistently honors the client's autonomy and mixed feelings. The score is not higher because some approving language subtly ties the client's value to choosing the proposed plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate, especially in summarizing the client's torn position and desire to keep independence. However, empathy remains fairly surface-level and does not deeply explore the emotional meaning behind the fall, son's worry, or identity-related defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol-related risk and developing a practical, time-limited plan. The focus is strong and clinically useful, though somewhat clinician-structured.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed and adjusted to the client's preferences, such as no son involvement and limited checking-in. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist frequently offers multiple options or menus rather than allowing the client to generate ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly frames the client as the decision-maker, asks permission, and respects her boundaries around privacy and independence. The score is not higher because some specific safety and behavioral suggestions remain mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated change talk, including the bottle-to-home experiment and 30-minute pause. Evocation is weakened by frequent questions that ask the client to choose among clinician-provided options rather than exploring her own values, reasons, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative surface, and the client's choices shape the final plan. Partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist takes a fairly directive role by offering long lists of strategies before inviting client selection.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully draws out willingness, confidence, and a concrete two-week plan. Change talk is not deeply developed because the client is often asked to choose from clinician suggestions rather than exploring her own deeper reasons or future self.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk and honors her desire for independence and privacy. The score is not higher because double-sided reflections or gentle exploration of the tension in statements like "I'm not an alcoholic" are missing.
