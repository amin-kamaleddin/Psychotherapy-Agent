# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client’s frustration and continued engagement. Some comments are slightly leading and could more explicitly affirm the client’s worth beyond progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor provides strong complex reflections of the client’s goals, routine, and shift from deprivation to sustainable change. The main limitation is that the emotional layers around past regain, pride, ambivalence, and fear are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing fruit juice consumption while keeping the work clinically appropriate and actionable. Even when the client broadens to walks, clothes, or feeling puffy, the psychiatrist anchors the conversation back to the juice routine and the client’s preferred path forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is highly collaborative, with the client’s ideas driving the session and the concrete plan. The psychiatrist builds on the client’s contributions—wife involvement, water pitcher, weekend allowance—without overriding them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through open, non-controlling questions and client-owned decision-making. The counselor avoids imposing rules or advice and repeatedly frames the plan as the client’s own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out the client’s own motivations, confidence, and coping rationale rather than supplying reasons to change. Reflections amplify the client’s language, such as confidence and a flexible rule, helping the client articulate a stronger internal commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as a partner by inviting the client to identify manageable steps, meaningful signs of progress, and practical home solutions. The rating is not a 5 because the counselor does not more consistently and explicitly name the client as the expert on his own life, household, and routine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and expands the client’s change talk across desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment. The client generates strong DARN-C statements, and the counselor reinforces useful phrases with brief amplifying reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and integrates the client’s love of juice into the plan in a supportive way. The main weakness is that the softening is not always explicitly strategic, and the final response is somewhat generic rather than a deliberate double-sided reflection or pivot to change reasons.
