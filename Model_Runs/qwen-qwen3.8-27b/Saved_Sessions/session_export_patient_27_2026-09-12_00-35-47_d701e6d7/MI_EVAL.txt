# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally warm, affirming, and nonjudgmental, which helped reduce shame and normalize Daniela’s fear of disclosure. The stance occasionally became more corrective and skill-coaching than fully accepting of her inner conflict.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed understanding through normalization and brief reflections of Daniela’s fear and nervousness. The major ambivalence between wanting safety and not wanting to be seen differently was not consistently reflected with deeper empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of disclosing diabetes to a trusted friend in a safe and practical way. The direction was clear, though somewhat narrow because it centered on scripting and delivery rather than deeper change exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered options, and Daniela contributed by choosing and modifying language. However, the session remained uneven because the psychiatrist supplied most of the substantive content rather than co-creating it with Daniela.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was strong support for choice and self-determination through optional language, offers, and permission to proceed at her own pace. The approach could be stronger by asking more open questions before supplying exact wording or behavioral instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few early evocative questions, but the session relied heavily on the psychiatrist providing scripts, techniques, and suggestions. This limited the development of Daniela’s own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited participation and offered choices, which supported a collaborative stance. The session became more counselor-led in content, with the psychiatrist shaping much of the direction rather than allowing Daniela to lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were moments of affirming Daniela’s agency and supporting her statements about wanting safety and trying a short message. The psychiatrist did not consistently deepen or draw out her own change language through more evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with Daniela’s concerns and helped keep her fear from becoming a block through normalization and practical next steps. However, the sustain talk was not actively softened with MI-consistent double-sided or paradoxical reflections.
