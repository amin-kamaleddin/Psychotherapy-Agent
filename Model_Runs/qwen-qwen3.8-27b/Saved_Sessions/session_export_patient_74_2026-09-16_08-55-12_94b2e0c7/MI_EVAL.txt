# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment, even when Linda states she is not quitting. The main limitation is that some affirmations feel generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Linda’s ambivalence, practical constraints, and shifting preferences. The reflections are strong but remain largely logistical rather than exploring the emotional weight of her concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains focus on smoking and its impact on her children. However, it quickly shifts toward practical avoidance strategies rather than consistently guiding toward readiness or values-based discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds collaboratively on Linda’s ideas, such as slippers by the light switch and using the car. The initial list of options, however, feels somewhat counselor-driven rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda’s choice is repeatedly honored, and there is no clear pressure to quit. The limitation is that the psychiatrist still invites her to “try” a plan, which may feel evaluative to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with useful open questions, but soon shifts to supplying options and problem-solving. There is limited effort to draw out Linda’s own values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone, and Linda’s suggestions shape the session. The psychiatrist does not, however, consistently position Linda as the expert on her own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Linda offers several meaningful change statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify them with MI-specific techniques. The focus moves quickly to practical logistics rather than expanding her reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Linda’s sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, the response largely accommodates maintaining the habit without using reflections or redirection to explore the tension between her values and behavior.
