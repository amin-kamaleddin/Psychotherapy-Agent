# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, especially in acknowledging family connection and Tariq’s effort. Acceptance is strong but feels somewhat procedural rather than fully affirming unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, client-centered reflections about frustration, unfairness, and the importance of family contact. The reflections could go deeper into underlying anxiety, vulnerability, and fear of being trapped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains some focus on release and family connection but narrows quickly into phone-call logistics. The broader target behavior of managing frustration and maintaining compliance under stress receives less sustained attention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Tariq’s lead and builds on his ideas about supervisor confirmation and written clearance. Collaboration is effective but remains partly counselor-led, with less explicit co-creation of the agenda or next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking Tariq what would help and how he would ask for clarity. Autonomy support could be strengthened with more explicit language about his ownership and independent decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions that invite Tariq to generate ideas and some change-oriented language. Evocation is practical but does not consistently deepen values, hopes, or internal motivation for broader behavior change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance and invites Tariq’s perspective throughout. Partnership is not fully equal because the psychiatrist still structures the agenda and steers toward release and the phone call.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and affirms some change talk, especially around staying calm and making a respectful request. The responses do not consistently expand or amplify Tariq’s strengths, ability, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct argument but remains heavily focused on the officer, fairness, and fear of being set up. There is limited use of double-sided reflections or pivots toward alternative perspectives and personal agency.
