# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicates nonjudgment and positive regard throughout, though the opening line slightly misattunes and some responses feel formulaic. Overall, the stance is respectful but not fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects themes of being pushed, judged, and wanting to be seen as responsible. However, the reflections remain surface-level and do not fully explore underlying shame, fear, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to alcohol use, peer pressure, and parental concern. However, it lacks a consistent focus on a specific behavioral target, such as reducing risky drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers options and avoids lecturing, supporting a collaborative stance. Still, the interaction feels somewhat directive at times, as reflected in the client's comment about “parroting stuff back.”

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirms client choice and respects refusal to make plans. Occasional suggestions may feel subtly steering, even when framed as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended and permission-based questions draw out some client material. However, the counselor does not consistently build on the client's own language to strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a partnership stance by offering choices and asking permission. Some suggested agendas may feel directive to a resistant adolescent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few attempts to elicit change-related statements, but several opportunities are missed. The counselor does not reflect, amplify, or build on the client's self-efficacy or change-relevant language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk. However, the client's framing that the issue is “not a big deal” remains dominant and is not consistently reframed.
