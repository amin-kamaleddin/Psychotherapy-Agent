# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Jamal’s stress, goals, and importance of eligibility. Acceptance is strong but remains tied more to performance and team value than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures ambivalence, fear of rejection, and need to belong. The counselor could go deeper into shame, identity, and the emotional meaning of being seen as “that guy.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use, saying no, and protecting eligibility. It becomes somewhat repetitive around micro-skills and leaves the concern about continued teasing unresolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and allows Jamal to choose lines and approaches. Collaboration could be stronger if Jamal generated more of the scripts and solutions himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses choice-based, nonpressuring language and avoids pushing abstinence or reporting teammates. The direct scripts, plans, and coach/AD suggestion make the approach somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-driven ideas are evoked, such as using a nonalcoholic drink. The counselor relies too heavily on generated options rather than asking questions that elicit Jamal’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jamal is frequently invited to participate and his ideas influence the plan. The counselor still holds much of the structure and content, limiting full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some change talk around importance, confidence, and small steps. More amplification of Jamal’s own reasons and values would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not fully addressed, especially fears of gossip, being labeled, and reporting teammates. Double-sided reflections could help pivot the conversation away from the status quo.
