# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts Marcus’s limits and ambivalence. Warmth is solid but sometimes procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects Marcus’s overwhelm, constraints, and practical concerns effectively. Misses opportunities to deepen the emotional weight of fear and exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation focused on asthma and narrows it to a concrete medication step. Direction becomes somewhat task-driven and list-heavy at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Incorporates Marcus’s routine, preferences, and communication style into the plan. Collaboration is strong, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently emphasizes choice and respects Marcus’s refusals without pressuring. Repeated follow-up prompts and implementation nudges create subtle pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses scaling and barrier questions to elicit some motivation and self-identified supports. The session is more problem-solving than evocative overall.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and offers choices rather than unilaterally directing the session. However, the psychiatrist often functions more as adviser than equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk through scaling and follow-up questions. Does not consistently reinforce, expand, or return to Marcus’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges dog-related, financial, and fatigue concerns without challenging or arguing. Mostly bypasses sustain talk rather than actively softening it.
