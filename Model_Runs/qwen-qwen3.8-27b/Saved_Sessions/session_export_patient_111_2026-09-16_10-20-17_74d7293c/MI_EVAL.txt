# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys nonjudgmental respect and validates the client’s ambivalence and choice. The opening is somewhat agenda-driven and the warmth remains more professional than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides strong, accurate reflections of the client’s ritual, pressure from his wife, and need for morning space. Some deeper feelings behind the client’s defensiveness are not explored in enough depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session identifies smoking early but repeatedly drifts toward related collateral issues. The direction toward the target smoking behavior is uneven and lacks a clear, consistent path.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and offers options, but often proposes interventions before fully co-creating them with the client. The collaboration feels more like offering acceptable options than a genuine exchange between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the client’s right to choose and avoids pressure or commands. The repeated introduction of counselor-generated alternatives remains subtly directive for a resistant client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks appropriate evocative questions about the client’s perspective and priorities. The exploration remains superficial and often shifts into problem-solving rather than deepening the client’s internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for the client’s preferences and offers choices, but still frequently generates the options and solutions. The sense of true power sharing is moderate rather than strong.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some attempts to elicit change-relevant language, but the session produces little genuine client change talk about smoking. The psychiatrist misses key opportunities to develop and expand on the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client but does not effectively soften or shift the strong sustain talk. The conversation remains heavily anchored in the client’s reasons for maintaining the status quo.
