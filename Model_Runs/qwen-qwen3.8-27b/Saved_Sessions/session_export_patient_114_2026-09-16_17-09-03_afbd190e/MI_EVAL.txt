# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids labeling the client as an alcoholic or out of control. Acceptance is strong but sometimes remains task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's ambivalence between safety, social life, and fear of being judged. Empathy is primarily cognitive and could explore emotional themes such as shame, embarrassment, or guilt more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a safety plan but narrows too quickly into logistics and label avoidance. It would benefit from more consistent attention to the broader target behavior of risky alcohol use and its consequences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the client to shape wording, cues, and plan details, making the conversation feel jointly constructed. Collaboration is strong but more agenda-setting and goal co-creation would strengthen it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the decision-maker, and the psychiatrist avoids coercive or directive language. Autonomy support could be stronger with explicit statements affirming the client's right to accept, modify, or discard the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite the client to generate practical solutions and self-management cues. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke deeper change talk, values, or confidence to change drinking behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's perspective and builds on the client's own ideas, reflecting a collaborative stance. The session is still somewhat interviewer-led, with the psychiatrist introducing specific plan components and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk about school, avoiding trouble, and maintaining control, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen it. More reflection, scaling, or exploration of the client's reasons for change would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses sustain talk around being labeled as weak, out of control, or a burden, but the psychiatrist does not consistently soften or redirect it toward change. Responses often preserve the client's current self-view without linking it to discrepancy or future goals.
