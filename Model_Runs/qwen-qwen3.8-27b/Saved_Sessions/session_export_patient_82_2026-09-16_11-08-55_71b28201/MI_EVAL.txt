# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers a warm, nonjudgmental, normalizing stance with several shame-reducing affirmations. The main limitation is occasional shifting toward instructive consequences, which slightly reduces unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Lukas’ ambivalence, social pressure, and somatic experience accurately and supportively. Some reflections become interpretive or conclusion-driven, and the deeper identity/shame conflict is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on early alcohol use, peer pressure, and Lukas’ important goals and identity. Strategic direction is weakened by premature problem-solving and failure to address Lukas’ final identity question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites preferences, but often introduces their own list of options and strategies. A more collaborative stance would wait longer for Lukas to generate his own ideas before offering behavioral options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly supports choice and honors Lukas’ request to avoid a lecture. Autonomy support is reduced when the counselor imposes direction or suggests specific next steps despite Lukas’ stated readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes evocative attempts by exploring the event, bodily sensations, and a positive inclusion experience. Evocation is limited because the counselor often provides interpretations or conclusions instead of helping Lukas discover his own meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative invitations and choice questions, but the psychiatrist frequently offers structured options even after Lukas asks for exploratory discussion. This makes the interaction feel more counselor-directed than truly partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates some change-talk opportunities by connecting drinking to goals and highlighting the diner example. However, they often supply reasons and conclusions instead of evoking Lukas’ own desires, ability, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing, including fitting in and not wanting to kill the vibe. Responses often redirect to consequences or strategies rather than using double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk.
