# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental, but the warmth is surface-level and the repeated recaps begin to feel procedural rather than affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Shows basic understanding of time pressure but misses the emotional content around stress, breathlessness, and work demands.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and consistent focus on the target behaviors and safety concerns, though the focus is more logistical than MI-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some joint planning occurs, but repeated recaps and summaries make the client feel managed rather than working as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respects the client's stated limits and offers options, yet the process remains fairly prescriptive and counselor-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicits the client's own motivation or change talk, leaving key concerns about breathlessness, fatigue, and control underexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices and asks for input, but the overall stance remains directive, with repeated instructive summaries that reduce collaborative balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions focus on practical choices rather than eliciting the client's personal reasons, values, confidence, or motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue with the client and accepts practical limits, but does not actively explore ambivalence or redirect sustain talk toward change.
