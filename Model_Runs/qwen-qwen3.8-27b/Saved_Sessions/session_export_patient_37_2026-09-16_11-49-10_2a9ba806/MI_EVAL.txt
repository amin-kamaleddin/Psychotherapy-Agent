# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming, but warmth is occasionally generic and mildly evaluative/coaching.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence and the need for concrete information, but they repeat supportive themes and miss deeper emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session remains tightly focused on the vaccine decision, moving coherently from concerns to a concrete plan and appointment preparation with little drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan and script are clearly co-constructed, adapting to the client’s needs for spacing, written questions, and rehearsal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong permission-seeking, choices, and flexibility support autonomy, but some directive coaching and lack of explicit reaffirmation of the right to refuse or continue waiting prevent a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates some change talk, but the clinician provides more factual education than deep evocation of the client’s own values, hopes, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and offers options, but still functions as the primary knowledge source and plan-builder rather than fully centering the parent as the decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked and reinforced, but the session relies more on information and planning than systematic elicitation of the client’s reasons, abilities, desires, and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hesitation is normalized and redirected without argument, though the focus on risks and side effects keeps the conversation partly anchored in negatives.
