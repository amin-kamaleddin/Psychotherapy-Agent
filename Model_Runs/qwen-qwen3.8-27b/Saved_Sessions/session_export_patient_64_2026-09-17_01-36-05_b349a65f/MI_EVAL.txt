# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Jordan’s ambivalence and expresses nonjudgmental positive regard, especially by acknowledging his caring motives. It is not a 5 because some reflections begin to interpret his internal shift rather than remaining purely accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The reflections accurately capture Jordan’s mixed feelings, values, and inner tension without premature resolution. The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates nuanced, client-centered understanding of his ambivalence and emerging readiness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on the flu vaccine decision and gradually moves from broad ambivalence to a concrete next step. The direction is clear, purposeful, and tied to Jordan’s own concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Jordan as an equal partner, inviting him to co-create language, identify priorities, and plan a doable next step. There is no authoritative lecturing or imposition of the clinician’s preferred solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jordan is repeatedly positioned as the expert on his own values, body, and timing, with strong protection of his right to remain undecided. The session consistently avoids pressure while still supporting a small, client-owned step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Jordan’s own motivations, values, and emerging confidence rather than supplying external reasons. Some reflections are slightly leading, which keeps the score just below the highest level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shares power by following Jordan’s lead, using his language, and letting his concerns shape the conversation. The session is clearly client-led rather than driven by a clinician agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan clearly moves from hesitancy to early commitment language, showing strong cultivation of desire, need, and self-efficacy. The score is not a 5 because a few questions subtly presume vaccination as the intended choice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled respectfully and is paired with the client’s values rather than argued against. It could be stronger by more consistently reframing concerns in a way that further reduces their weight while preserving autonomy.
