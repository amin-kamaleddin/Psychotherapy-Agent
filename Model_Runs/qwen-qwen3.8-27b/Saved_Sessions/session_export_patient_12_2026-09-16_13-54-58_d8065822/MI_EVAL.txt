# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental affirmation of Amira’s worth without moralizing her drug use or arguing with her feelings. It occasionally feels formulaic and centers the therapist’s availability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence and connects her request for less fixing to past abandonment. Some reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper shame, abandonment fear, or self-worth pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clear MI stance but little sustained connection to the target behavior of reducing drug use. Feelings and relational themes are explored without being tied to a concrete change target or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative tone and inclusive language invite her preferences and avoid lecturing. The therapist repeatedly returns to the same staying-without-fixing frame, so Amira’s contributions do not fully shape direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong affirmation of her choice, respect for her boundary not to believe in change yet, and pressure-free open questions. Could more explicitly return all change, use, and treatment decisions to Amira.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocative questions draw on her expertise about what would feel safer or more believable. The evocation stays focused on being heard and the therapeutic relationship rather than her own motivation or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine invitations position Amira as expert, such as asking what to explore and what would feel safer. The agenda nonetheless remains steered toward the therapist’s concern about staying/not leaving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only minimal, undeveloped invitations toward feelings, goals, or tiny steps, with no effective DARN-C questioning or amplification of her own change statements. The session does not meaningfully build motivation for reducing drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does not argue or dismiss her sustain talk but leaves futility, isolation, and burden repeatedly reflected without strategic pivots. The focus on staying stuck is maintained rather than softened toward hope, strengths, or small steps.
