# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment. He accepts the client without shame or labeling, even when the client minimizes alcohol use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence, social pressure, and performance concerns. Empathy is strong but occasionally generic and does not consistently explore deeper feelings such as shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on alcohol use and its relevance to soccer, scholarships, and performance. The conversation steadily moves toward a concrete, client-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint language and invites the client’s perspective throughout. The plan is co-constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly reinforces the client’s sense of choice and ownership. There is no pressure, persuasion, or imposition of external expectations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited from the client’s own values, goals, and ambitions rather than supplied by the psychiatrist. The client clearly articulates his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life and goals. The interaction demonstrates clear power sharing without an expert-driven stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, change-oriented questions effectively elicit desire, reasons, need, and ability. The psychiatrist skillfully amplifies the client’s own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and softens minimization without arguing or escalating. A score of 5 would require more explicit double-sided reflection and discrepancy work.
