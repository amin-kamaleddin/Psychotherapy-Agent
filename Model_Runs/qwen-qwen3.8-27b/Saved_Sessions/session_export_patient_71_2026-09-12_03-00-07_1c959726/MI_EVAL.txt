# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful tone throughout. Laura's self-doubt and ambivalence were met without pressure or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Laura's emotional conflict, hesitation, and fear of failure. The clinician responded to the meaning behind her statements rather than only the surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a specific, doable morning behavior. Each question moved the conversation toward a concrete, client-owned plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-created through Laura's choices and preferences rather than prescribed by the clinician. Laura controlled key decisions such as the action, alarm label, time, and backup language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supported choice and ownership. Options were offered, but Laura was repeatedly invited to decide what felt most doable and respectful of her values.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out Laura's own motivations, confidence, and ideas for change. However, the session occasionally became less evocative when the clinician offered specific scripts or concrete solutions before fully exploring Laura's own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction reflected an equal-partner stance. Laura's agenda, concerns, and judgments substantially shaped the direction and final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited and affirmed several change statements, including commitment to a tiny action and increased confidence. The score is limited by moments when problem-solving shifted focus away from deeper amplification of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well through validation and reframing rather than confrontation. The score is 4 because the clinician sometimes moved quickly into solutions, which reduced space for Laura to resolve her own resistance in her own words.
