# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing with the client's ambivalence or minimization. Minor assumptions and one leading statement prevent a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening, capturing the social, performance, and stress-related aspects of the client's ambivalence. One generic response to an emotionally vulnerable moment limits otherwise strong empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior of marijuana/vaping use in basketball, school, and social contexts. The conversation moves logically from broad health concerns to a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as a partner rather than an authority, building the plan directly from the client's ideas. The client's contributions substantially shape the session's direction and final strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client's choice and avoids imposing a quit goal or correcting her partial plan. The client is supported in developing a plan she feels she can live with.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client's own reasons and ideas for change, especially the locker delay and hand-check plan. Slightly deeper evocation of values, confidence, or commitment would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys partnership by inviting the client's perspective and acknowledging her as the expert on her own life. The session is somewhat structured, so power sharing could be made more explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client produces several forms of change talk, including desire, ability, and planning, largely evoked by the psychiatrist's questions. More systematic cultivation of all DARN-C dimensions would make this stronger.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by validating it without dismissing or arguing against it. More deliberate pivots back to the client's values and goals could further strengthen this skill.
