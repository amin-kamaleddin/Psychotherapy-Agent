# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and uses normalizing statements such as “It’s normal to feel mixed about it.” Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is reasonable through accurate summaries of Kevin’s drinking pattern and mixed feelings. It could be stronger by reflecting deeper emotions such as embarrassment, fear, shame, or social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting concern of alcohol-related injury and safer weekend drinking. It ends with a concrete, relevant plan, though it emphasizes practical safety more than broader motivation change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin contributes several plan elements, but the psychiatrist often provides structured option lists rather than fully co-creating the plan with him. More collaboration would involve inviting Kevin to generate ideas before offering suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers choices, and respects Kevin’s decision to check in later. Some directive guidance and detailed lists slightly reduce the client-led feel.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions elicit useful client content, but the interaction is often advice-based. More evocation would involve exploring Kevin’s personal values, concerns, and own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin’s input shapes parts of the plan, but the psychiatrist frequently leads by supplying multiple options. A stronger partnership stance would share power more explicitly by asking Kevin what might work for him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin expresses several change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify them. More deliberate reflection and expansion of Kevin’s own motivations would strengthen this skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Kevin’s sustain talk and acknowledges that he does not want to give up weekends. Advanced MI skills such as double-sided reflections could more effectively hold both safety and social enjoyment.
