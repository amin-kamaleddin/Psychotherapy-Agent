# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Anika's concerns without labeling or disapproval. The warmth is competent but somewhat procedural, and the quick move toward advice-giving slightly limits unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate reflective listening and understands Anika's ambivalence about fun, relaxation, basketball, and peer pressure. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore the emotional meaning of losing the team or the value of friendship.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on vaping use and its impact on basketball, moving coherently from presenting concern to a concrete behavior-specific plan. A 5 would require more consistent strategic guidance toward deeper change motivation rather than externally generated action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through inclusive language and role-play, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies the content of the plan. This makes the interaction feel more like choosing from a clinician-generated menu than solving the problem as equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly uses choice-focused language and respects Anika's preferred casual refusal style. However, the substantial amount of clinician-provided advice and structure can subtly reduce the sense that the plan emerged entirely from Anika's own motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to evoke Anika's readiness and capability through questions and the readiness ruler. However, evocation is somewhat superficial and heavily structured by clinician-provided strategies and scripts rather than Anika's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and collaborates on strategy selection, but the partnership is uneven. The clinician controls much of the sequence and supplies many solutions, making the session feel more expert-driven than truly co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes concrete change statements and reinforces Anika's confidence in stopping for a month. The session misses opportunities to deepen or amplify Anika's own language around values, identity, and deeper motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and does not argue or shame Anika for wanting to vape. However, sustain talk is not consistently pivoted back toward change, such as exploring underlying health, stress, or risk concerns.
