# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used nonjudgmental, respectful, and affirming language without blaming or dismissing the client. Acceptance could be strengthened by affirming Sofia’s emotional struggle and ambivalence more explicitly, rather than mostly praising her plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate, complex reflections that captured ambivalence, frustration, and relief. Empathy could be deepened by more fully addressing Sofia’s body-image concerns, loneliness, repeated diet failure, and shame-based self-talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior of increasing movement through small, sustainable lifestyle changes. Direction was clear, consistent, and consistently guided the client toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative, with the client contributing key ideas such as the five-minute goal, alarm, shoes-by-the-door cue, and tea reward. Collaboration could be stronger by letting the client generate more plan components rather than the psychiatrist offering multiple options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supported client choice through open questions, confidence scaling, and optional next steps. Autonomy support was slightly limited by several therapist-generated suggestions and plan components.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked the client’s own motivations, ideas, and confidence, especially through scaling questions. Evocation could be deepened by exploring values, meaning, identity, and the personal significance of change rather than mostly procedural logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporated the client’s input and allowed her ideas to shape the session. Partnership was weakened by frequent therapist-led suggestions and problem-solving, which reduced the emphasis on the client as the primary source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited and built on the client’s change talk, including ability, readiness, and confidence. Cultivation could be stronger by more explicitly amplifying the client’s language and connecting change to deeper values or identity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk skillfully by reframing barriers, reducing perfectionism, and normalizing missed days without arguing. Softening could be improved by more consistently redirecting from barriers toward values, hopes, and reasons for change.
