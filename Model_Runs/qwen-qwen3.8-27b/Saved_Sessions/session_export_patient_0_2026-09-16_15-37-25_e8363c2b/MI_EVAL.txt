# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental acceptance. They validate Adrian's perspective and ambivalence without disagreement or moral correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and effectively capture Adrian's tension between health, social connection, and authenticity. However, they could go deeper into underlying fear, shame, and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation remains consistently focused on substance use, infection-related pain, and social functioning. The session would be stronger with a clearer, collaboratively defined target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses Adrian's own language and builds on his ideas, making the interaction genuinely collaborative. Solutions are client-led rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through acceptance of Adrian's preferences and in-the-moment decision-making. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, coercion, or a rigid plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit Adrian's own reasons for change, such as avoiding sickness and feeling less 'wired.' Evocation could be deeper by exploring broader values and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong collaborative stance and invites Adrian to define the problem and solutions. Slightly more explicit agenda negotiation would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adrian spontaneously produces meaningful change talk about reducing use and staying functional. The psychiatrist could further amplify this by exploring importance, confidence, and values-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids argument and does not reinforce reasons to maintain the status quo. More strategic double-sided reflections would help gently tilt the conversation toward change.
