# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming, even when the client minimizes the need to change. Warmth is consistent but somewhat formulaic, with reliance on repeated phrases such as “I hear you.”

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s resentment, need for autonomy, and ambivalence about his wife’s concerns. Some responses remain surface-level and do not fully explore deeper emotional themes such as fear, identity, or relationship pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally connected to tobacco use, but the focus shifts among communication, routine, and being pressured. The psychiatrist does not consistently steer the conversation toward a clear, coherent MI goal around smoking cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and avoids an overly expert stance, but repeatedly offers structured options that the client experiences as controlling. This limits genuine co-creation and equal partnership in the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s right to choose and does not pressure him to quit. However, therapist-generated options and scripted language can feel mildly directive and reduce full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions are used, but they are often broad rather than specifically designed to elicit the client’s own motivations for change. The psychiatrist occasionally provides explanations or scripts instead of drawing out the client’s own values and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and avoids argument, but the session remains unevenly guided. The repeated use of menus and structured choices undermines a fully client-led partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are minimal efforts to elicit or amplify the client’s own reasons for change. Change talk is acknowledged but not strategically expanded through importance, need, ability, or confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk, which is appropriate, but does not effectively reframe or reduce it. Much of the client’s resistance is reflected rather than explored in a way that might open the door to ambivalence.
