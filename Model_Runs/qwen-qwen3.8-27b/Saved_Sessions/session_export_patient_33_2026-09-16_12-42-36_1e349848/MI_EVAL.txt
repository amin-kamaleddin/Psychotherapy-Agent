# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance while avoiding pressure toward abstinence or medication use. Acceptance was solid but somewhat task-focused and not consistently emotionally deep enough for a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used brief, accurate reflections of the patient’s fear, ambivalence, and practical concerns. However, empathy remained mostly surface-level and cognitive rather than exploring deeper emotions and meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear harm-reduction target with concrete safety steps. Direction was strong, though detailed procedural education occasionally shifted the focus away from pure MI direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporated patient preferences and allowed the patient’s choices to shape the plan. Collaboration was good, though the clinician still supplied much of the practical content rather than fully eliciting patient-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported the patient’s pace, choice, and non-coercion, including respecting his reluctance to use medications. It could be strengthened by more explicit reinforcement that the patient is in control of all decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some readiness, confidence, and intention to take safer steps. However, the session relied more on providing scripts and instructions than on evoking deeper internal motivation, values, and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited patient input and shared some decision-making. Nevertheless, the interaction remained more expert-driven and instructional than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful statements of readiness, ability, and intention around harm-reduction action. There were also missed opportunities to deepen and amplify the patient’s own change talk around values and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing the status quo. However, the clinician did not consistently use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk and connect it to harm-reduction values.
