# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and accepts Anika’s ambivalence without challenging her, but acceptance is not consistently warm or deeply affirming. The rapid shift into practical planning can make the interaction feel more task-oriented than genuinely accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflective listening and summarizes Anika’s ambivalence, but empathy remains largely cognitive rather than emotionally deep. There are missed opportunities to reflect underlying feelings such as fear, defensiveness, and social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on protecting basketball eligibility and performance while moving through importance, barriers, strategies, and confidence. However, the direction can become overly procedural and driven more by structured planning than by motivationally evoking Anika’s internal reasons.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration because Anika chooses parts of the plan, such as the refusal line and reminder method. However, the psychiatrist contributes many specific scripts and solutions, reducing the sense that Anika is the primary expert in the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports choice by asking for preferences and inviting Anika to select what works for her. Autonomy support is somewhat weakened by frequent provision of exact scripts and practical steps before fully eliciting Anika’s own preferred wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses helpful evocative questions about importance, confidence, and barriers, which leads to some change talk. However, evocation is interrupted by frequent advice-giving and practical coaching rather than deeper reflection of Anika’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts a collaborative stance by inviting Anika’s input and making the plan “together.” The interaction can feel more counselor-guided skill training than true power sharing because the psychiatrist repeatedly introduces specific solutions and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits change talk by asking about importance, barriers, alternatives, and confidence, leading Anika to articulate goals and plans. The evocation could be stronger if the counselor had expanded Anika’s values and self-efficacy before moving to practical scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Anika’s sustain talk and acknowledges the fun and relaxing aspects of vaping. However, the counselor does not consistently use MI-consistent strategies such as double-sided reflections or re-framing to fully soften sustain talk and resolve ambivalence.
