# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental language and normalizes Marcus’s concerns without blame. The warmth is effective but somewhat generic, limiting it from a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows understanding of Marcus’s anxiety, hesitation, and fear of failure. Empathic responses are brief and reassurance-focused rather than deeply reflective of his emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on making blood sugar self-monitoring manageable and moves through steps in a logical sequence. Some portions become more didactic and procedure-focused than collaboratively guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus’s preferences influence the plan, such as using a checklist, a short fridge note, and trying independently first. The interaction remains more clinician-guided problem-solving than fully collaborative MI dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Marcus’s pace, emphasizes choice, and avoids pressuring him to commit beyond what he is ready for. Autonomy support could be stronger by further emphasizing his own ownership of solutions and self-evaluation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful practical questions but does not strongly elicit Marcus’s personal values, motivations, or confidence. Missed opportunities to draw out his own reasons for change weaken this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative stance by inviting preferences and adapting to Marcus’s requests. However, the psychiatrist often remains in an expert-instruction role, making the partnership feel uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offers some spontaneous change talk, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it briefly. There is limited active effort to deepen, expand, or amplify his change-oriented statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Marcus’s sustain talk and uses normalization and reassurance to reduce defensiveness. More advanced MI strategies, such as reflective redirecting of ambivalence, are underused.
