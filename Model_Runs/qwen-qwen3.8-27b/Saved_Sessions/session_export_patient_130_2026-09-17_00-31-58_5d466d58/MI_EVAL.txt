# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming labels while validating Viktor's experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong reflective listening to capture ambivalence and the emotional catch-22, but does not always deepen into underlying fear, shame, or the function of drug use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session clinically relevant and connected to drug use, mood, and relationship impact, though it occasionally drifts into marital dynamics without explicitly anchoring to reducing use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Viktor's perspective and lets his ideas meaningfully shape the conversation, but the therapist still guides much of the agenda and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports Viktor as the decision-maker and avoids pressure or directive advice, with room to make autonomy more explicit during sustain talk.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Draws out Viktor's own insights about triggers and the car buffer, but does not consistently elicit deeper values, confidence, or personal motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly conveys that Viktor is the expert on his life and invites his input, yet the session remains largely therapist-guided rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions elicit change-related statements, but there is limited explicit amplification of importance, ability, need, or confidence for reducing drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without argument, but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or redirect Viktor's statements toward his own values and agency.
