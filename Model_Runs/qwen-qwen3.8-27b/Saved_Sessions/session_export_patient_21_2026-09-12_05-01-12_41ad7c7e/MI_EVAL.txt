# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes concerns, but validation can feel slightly formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but remain surface-level, missing deeper emotional themes such as past failure and relational vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear, actionable goal and progresses logically toward implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is co-constructed through options and client choice, but the counselor frequently supplies the main ideas and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids pressure, supports choice, and frames the plan as optional and self-directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses evocative techniques, but the session does not deeply explore the client's underlying values, motivations, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input, yet the consultant-to-client dynamic can feel expert-driven because the counselor provides much of the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, especially through the confidence ruler, but it is not consistently reflected, amplified, or explored more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without argument or invalidation, though double-sided reflections could be used more to hold ambivalence.
