# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental tone that affirms the client’s effort and self-management. Time constraints are accepted without frustration, pressure, or criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s medication worry, practical needs, and desire for a clear resolution. The empathy could be slightly deeper in acknowledging the emotional dimension of the concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the citalopram and phenelzine safety question. Each exchange moves toward a concrete, actionable plan without drifting to unrelated issues.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the client’s preferred direct and practical approach, helping shape the plan rather than imposing it. Collaboration is slightly reduced when the psychiatrist takes on the role of information provider.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker in charge of her choices and pace. The psychiatrist avoids pressure or persuasion, even when providing safety guidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes the client’s own plan, wording, and sense of competence. The evocation is practical and effective, though it does not deeply explore values or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly follows the client’s lead and invites her to direct the session. Partnership is strong but slightly less balanced when medication safety information is provided before exploring the client’s own understanding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out the client’s own direct wording and follow-up plan through skillful questions. Change talk is strong but remains primarily practical rather than deeply motivational.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s time pressure is framed as a reasonable boundary rather than a problem. The psychiatrist helps convert this constraint into a manageable, safety-focused plan.
