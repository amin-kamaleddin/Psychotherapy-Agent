# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while affirming the client's uncertainty. It is not a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat structured and lacks deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence, caution, and concrete cues. Deeper reflection of self-critical themes, such as fear of failure or perfectionism, would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on being more present at home, especially during breakfast. The psychiatrist skillfully narrows broad values into a concrete, client-owned action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is highly collaborative, with the therapist using open questions, reflections, and client-led formulation. The plan is co-constructed from the client's own ideas and priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated emphasis on choice, optionality, and non-coercion. The therapist never pressures the client to commit or imposes a solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits the client's internal motivations, values, and personalized cues rather than providing advice. This helps the client build a meaningful and self-owned change process.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveys that the client is the expert on her own life and values. The therapist invites the client's perspective without assuming the correct answer.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session effectively evokes desire, reason, need, and some ability talk through client-led values and small steps. It would be stronger with more explicit elicitation of confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist softens resistance to rules, checklists, and perfection without reinforcing sustain talk. More active use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing would further resolve ambivalence.
