# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Mateo's pain, frustration, and fear in a respectful, nonjudgmental way. Acceptance is strong but feels somewhat procedural because warmth is often paired with problem-solving and prescriptive instructions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several appropriate reflections and validations of Mateo's emotional experience. However, the reflections remain mostly surface-level and do not consistently capture his deeper ambivalence, fear, or sense of being stuck.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of increasing physical activity within rehab and pain management. It moves toward a concrete plan, such as the mailbox walk and follow-up with the doctor, though it becomes overly procedural at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo contributes meaningful ideas, such as the mailbox walk and voice memo, and the psychiatrist accepts them. However, the interaction remains advisor-driven, with the psychiatrist frequently directing plans, tracking rules, and safety thresholds rather than co-creating the session with Mateo.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice in some moments by asking what feels doable and acknowledging safety. Autonomy is undercut by repeated prescriptive advice, tracking expectations, and continued problem-solving even when Mateo expresses low energy and disinterest in counseling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few open questions but does not consistently evoke Mateo's own motivations, values, hopes, or sense of agency. The session relies more on providing clinical suggestions than drawing out Mateo's reasons for change or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a professional stance but often positions themselves as the expert giving instructions. Although there are a few collaborative questions, the session feels more like a clinician managing an activity experiment than a partner working alongside Mateo.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo offers spontaneous change talk, such as trying the mailbox walk and continuing rehab. The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce, expand, or build on these statements to strengthen his motivation or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, which is appropriate but can reinforce stopping or fear. The psychiatrist does not consistently use reflective pivots, double-sided reflections, or strategic affirmations to help Mateo consider reasons for change.
