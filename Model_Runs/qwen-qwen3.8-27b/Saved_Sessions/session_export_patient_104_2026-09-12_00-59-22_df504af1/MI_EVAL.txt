# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates Nadine’s exhaustion without blaming her. Acceptance is strong but occasionally feels general, and the repeated safety language makes the stance slightly managerial.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reflects fatigue and uncertainty adequately, but does not fully attend to deeper emotional cues such as fear of failing, feeling “at my limit,” or “drowning.” Empathic responses often remain brief before moving into advice or problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on infant sleep, exhaustion, and a small coping plan. Direction is uneven because of repeated safety language, tangential suggestions, and an advice-driven pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, as the clinician adapts to Nadine’s constraints and preferences. However, much of the content is clinician-supplied, and the session is not fully co-constructed with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently invites choice and respects Nadine’s limits, including her wish to try the plan on her own. This is a strength, though the amount of direct advice and safety instruction still makes the interaction somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited; after an initial open question, the clinician largely provides information, reassurance, and practical advice. There is little effort to draw out Nadine’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician solicits input and adjusts the plan based on Nadine’s preferences, which supports partnership. However, the clinician remains the primary source of information and drives much of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some change talk by asking for a small, doable goal and framing the plan as an experiment. Change talk is not consistently reflected, amplified, or linked to the client’s own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is softened by simplifying the plan and offering gentler alternatives rather than arguing or dismissing. More MI-specific techniques, such as double-sided reflections or ambivalence exploration, would strengthen this criterion.
