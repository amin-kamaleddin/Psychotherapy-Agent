# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Amina’s guardedness without pressure, judgment, or defensiveness. Acceptance could be deepened with warmer affirmation of her effort to attend while feeling shut down.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Amina’s needs for safety, control, and reduced pressure. Empathy could be more nuanced by exploring deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or mistrust rather than only surface-level demands.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays appropriately focused on immediate engagement, safety, and reducing pressure. There is less forward momentum after Amina requests fewer questions and less talking, with limited bridging toward future engagement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is conducted as a genuine collaboration rather than an expert-led interview. Amina’s minimal cues, such as “no questions” and “less talking,” actively shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by repeatedly emphasizing that Amina is in control and does not owe explanations. Boundaries are accepted without coercion or negotiation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open, low-pressure questions attempt to elicit Amina’s own preferences and frame. Evocation remains limited to session logistics and does not fully develop her minimal responses into deeper motivation or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that Amina is the expert on her own experience, pace, and needs. The power-sharing stance is clear and consistently maintained.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only limited attempts to draw out client-generated language toward engagement or change. Small positive cues such as “Mm-hmm,” “Fine,” and “Good” are not consistently reflected or amplified into change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing Amina’s resistance and partially reframes it as a legitimate need for autonomy. However, the clinician does not consistently use double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence or gently turn it toward possible movement.
