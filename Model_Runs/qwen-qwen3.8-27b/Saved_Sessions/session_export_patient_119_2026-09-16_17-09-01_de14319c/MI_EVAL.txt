# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Selene’s ideas and ambivalence without pressure. Score is not a 5 because the warmth is solid but somewhat clinical, with fewer explicit affirmations of her worth or difficulty beyond her suggestions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate complex reflections that capture both sides of Selene’s ambivalence and notices subtle emotional cues such as nighttime worry. It could be stronger by reflecting deeper feelings like fear of failure, shame, or identity-related meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on medication adherence and moves from broad difficulty to concrete low-pressure options with little drift. Direction could be more strategic by more clearly connecting the plan to values and consolidating a commitment statement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a collaborative interaction in which Selene’s ideas substantially shape the emerging plan. It could be strengthened by more explicit power sharing, co-construction language, and invitations for Selene to evaluate or veto options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, avoids persuasion or coercion, and frames the work around reducing pressure. The session strongly emphasizes Selene’s role in deciding what fits her life, which merits the highest score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Selene’s own goals, barriers, and solutions rather than giving external reasons for change. Evocation could be stronger by more explicitly exploring values, identity, and confidence in addition to practical problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Selene as the expert on her own life and avoids imposing an expert agenda. Partnership could be enhanced by more explicit statements that the final decision and responsibility belong to Selene.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Selene produces meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist occasionally reinforces it with affirming questions and reflections. The main gap is limited skillful expansion, consolidation, and deeper exploration of ability or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging, reframing, and avoiding argument or pathologizing. It could be stronger by more actively pivoting sustain talk toward values-based change talk.
