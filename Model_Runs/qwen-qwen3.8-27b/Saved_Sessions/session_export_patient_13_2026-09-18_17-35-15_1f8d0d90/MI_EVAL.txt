# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone throughout with multiple instances of normalization and unconditional positive regard. Occasionally reads as slightly formulaic and could go further in affirming the client's worth independent of her ability to act.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful empathic accuracy with precise, layered reflections that capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions of the client's experience. The clinician picks up on subtle language, validates the protective function of avoidance, and captures dual experiences with remarkable precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with a logical and strategic progression from exploring fear to identifying a small step to addressing relapse fear. The session maintains momentum toward a concrete outcome without feeling rushed or forced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of collaborative work with genuinely inquisitive questions that create space for the client to discover her own answers. The session structure emerges from the client's responses rather than a predetermined agenda, and the clinician incorporates her language and metaphors back into reflections.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports autonomy by validating the client's right to choose a small step, never pressing or minimizing difficulty. The framing of the final question as 'what would you want that version of you to hear?' rather than 'what are you going to do?' exemplifies this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently skillful evocation through questions that draw out the client's own motivations rather than providing external reasons. The clinician notices and amplifies the client's own change language, and the entire session is built on eliciting internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance throughout, positioning the client as the expert on her own life. However, the clinician takes some initiative in structuring the session, creating a slight gradient of guidance rather than full equality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through strategic questions targeting desire, ability, and commitment language. The client's progression from contemplation to a concrete, self-identified action step is remarkable and largely driven by the clinician's evocative skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well using double-sided reflections and validation while gently introducing alternative perspectives. Could be slightly more proactive in neutralizing the final sustain talk about future relapse into avoidance.
