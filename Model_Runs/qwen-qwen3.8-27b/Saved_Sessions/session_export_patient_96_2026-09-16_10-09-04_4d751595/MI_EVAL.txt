# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language such as “that makes sense” and “I respect your choice.” Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not deeply engage Marisol’s broader values or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Marisol’s view of the piercing as self-expression. Empathy is accurate but repetitive and does not deepen to explore identity, anxiety, or the tension between autonomy and health.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the lip piercing and its oral health implications. Direction is mostly practical and logistical rather than strategically guiding Marisol through an MI change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marisol is asked to choose among formats such as checklist, text, and reminders. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist largely determines the substance of the care plan rather than co-creating it with Marisol.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong through choice offers, “no pressure,” and respect for Marisol’s decisions. The score is reduced because the psychiatrist still gives prescriptive care recommendations instead of fully returning the plan to Marisol.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early questions invite some reflection, but the interview shifts quickly into advice, checklists, and reminders. It does not effectively draw out Marisol’s internal motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and says “you’re steering this,” which supports partnership. Partnership is uneven because much of the content and care plan is still controlled by the psychiatrist rather than shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk are limited and not strategically developed. The psychiatrist relies more on practical care advice than on amplifying or exploring Marisol’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marisol’s sustain talk is strong, but the psychiatrist often validates the status quo instead of consistently softening it. There is limited use of double-sided reflections or gentle pivots toward protecting oral health.
