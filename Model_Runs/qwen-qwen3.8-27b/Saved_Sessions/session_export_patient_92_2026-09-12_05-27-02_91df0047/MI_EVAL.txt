# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s confusion and concerns. The stance is strong but can feel slightly formulaic and shifts toward problem-solving at times.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture key ideas such as confusion about the “high-risk” label, enjoyment of the social buzz, and preference for authentic cues. However, they stay mostly content-level and do not fully explore underlying identity, pride, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use and moves coherently from risk information to possible small changes. Direction occasionally becomes too focused on selecting a concrete plan for an ambivalent client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and builds on the client’s own suggestions, such as buying only four drinks and setting an exit time. The interaction remains somewhat expert-guided due to repeated offers of new options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through permission-seeking, nonjudgment, and acknowledging the client is not ready to quit. Repeated willingness probes and plan suggestions can create mild pressure to engage.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions about noticed changes, possible small changes, confidence, and early cues. Evocation is present but limited by heavy reliance on advice, risk feedback, and behavioral options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are elements of partnership, including permission-based feedback and building on the client’s ideas. The session still feels somewhat expert-guided rather than fully equal and client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerges around liver concern, stopping at four drinks, leaving by 11, and using loud music as a cue. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, affirm, or expand the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and acknowledges sustain talk such as enjoying the social buzz and not wanting to quit. At times, the counselor continues toward planning despite the client’s expressed uncertainty.
