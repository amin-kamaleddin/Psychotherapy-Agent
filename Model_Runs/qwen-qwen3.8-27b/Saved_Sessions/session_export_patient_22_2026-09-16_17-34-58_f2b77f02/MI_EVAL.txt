# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and avoids criticizing Camila's drinking or ambivalence. The acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with fewer explicit affirmations of Camila's worth, resilience, or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Camila's push–pull experience and the social fear of being boring, invisible, or “the buzzkill” without alcohol. Empathy could be deeper by fully exploring the emotional meaning of statements such as “without it, I’m just... me.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing alcohol use, especially in social and pre-gaming contexts. Direction is good but slightly circular, and a concise summary would help consolidate the discussed steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds collaboratively on Camila's own strategies, such as one drink, an alcohol-free Wednesday, skipping pre-gaming, and using Sarah as a support. Collaboration is strong but could be more explicitly co-created rather than following a similar guided question pattern.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Suggestions are framed as experiments and choices, respecting Camila's right to decide rather than imposing abstinence. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit statements affirming that the decision is hers to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes client-generated cues, anchors, and confidence, such as the sip cue and reflecting on what success would mean. Evocation is procedural and effective but could more deeply explore Camila's values, hopes, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Camila as the expert on her own life, social world, and what feels doable. Partnership is strong, though the therapist still leads the pacing and sequence more than a fully power-shared conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Camila offers several concrete change ideas, including one drink, an alcohol-free Wednesday, skipping pre-gaming, and a non-alcoholic drink cue. The psychiatrist could better reinforce, amplify, and summarize this change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Camila's fears and reframes them as realistic tests rather than rules to pass or fail. Softening sustain talk could be improved by using more double-sided reflections to move worry toward change talk.
