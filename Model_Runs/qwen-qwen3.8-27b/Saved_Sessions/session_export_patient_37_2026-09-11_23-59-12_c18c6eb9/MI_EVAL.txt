# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing. Acceptance feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of uncertainty and pressure, but they remain surface-level. The client's fear, guilt, and emotional weight could have been reflected more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on the vaccine decision and concrete next steps. It is more task-oriented than focused on resolving ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and adapts the session to the client's choices. The strong provision of expert content slightly limits full co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is offered options and not pressured to decide immediately. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client as the final decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial concerns and preferences are elicited, but the session shifts quickly into information and scripts. There are missed opportunities to draw out values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and offers choices, which supports partnership. The expert-driven structure reduces the sense that the client is the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's protective motivation is supported and acknowledged. There are missed opportunities to amplify and expand change talk strategically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client's hesitation. More advanced techniques, such as double-sided reflections, could better redirect sustain talk.
