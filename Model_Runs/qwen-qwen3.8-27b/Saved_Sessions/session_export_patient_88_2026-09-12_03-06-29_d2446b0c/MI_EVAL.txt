# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or moralizing Marcus's drinking. The acceptance is strong but slightly formulaic, with room for warmer, more individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The reflections accurately capture Marcus's meaning for drinking, including confidence, social belonging, and lowered inhibitions. However, some deeper emotional undercurrents, such as embarrassment or belonging anxiety, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on drinking, social functioning, and related risks without unnecessary drift. A clearer collaboratively chosen target or change focus would make the direction more strategic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, asks permission, and invites Marcus to select what he wants from the conversation. There is still subtle clinical steering toward risk assessment and reconsideration, which Marcus notices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is repeatedly positioned as being in charge, and the psychiatrist avoids direct advice or pressure to cut down. Some invitations still carry a subtle push toward change, which sounds agenda-driven to Marcus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, evocative questions that draw out Marcus's own values around belonging, confidence, and enjoyment. Some change-relevant language emerges, but the psychiatrist does not consistently follow up or deepen that motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance, respects Marcus's role as expert, and follows his stated desire to be heard. A few questions still feel clinically goal-oriented and contribute to Marcus feeling the psychiatrist is 'fishing.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate attempts to elicit pros and cons, comparisons, and future preferences, which produce some ambivalence and mild change talk. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect back, amplify, or strategically deepen that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Marcus or directly reinforce the sustain talk. The session remains somewhat anchored in reasons for staying the same and lacks stronger double-sided reflections that would soften that sustain talk toward ambivalence.
