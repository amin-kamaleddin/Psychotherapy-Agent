# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the adolescent's frustration without shaming him. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and occasionally competes with directive advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflections of annoyance, tension, frustration, and the desire to be trusted. Empathy is present but often remains surface-level and quickly shifts to advice or options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on relevant concerns such as substance use, peer group, parental conflict, and safety. Direction is clear topically but lacks MI-specific momentum, often returning to the same options without progressing toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone with repeated invitations for the client to choose a direction. The collaboration becomes superficial when the psychiatrist keeps offering therapist-generated options instead of building on the client's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently uses choice-based language and honors the client's desire to be treated like an adult. Autonomy support is a clear strength, though unsolicited advice and scripts can undercut client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks mainly risk-assessment and preference questions rather than deeper questions about values, goals, or internal motivation. The client's own reasons for change are only limitedly evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative phrases and reduces direct confrontation by offering choices. Partnership is uneven because the session is often shaped by the clinician's scripts and agenda rather than the client's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers protective or change-oriented statements, such as never driving after use and knowing when to stop. The psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or builds on these statements, so change talk is not consistently cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to sustain talk with validation, but this can inadvertently reinforce the client's resistance and preference for the status quo. There are few effective double-sided reflections or explorations of the cost of maintaining current behavior.
