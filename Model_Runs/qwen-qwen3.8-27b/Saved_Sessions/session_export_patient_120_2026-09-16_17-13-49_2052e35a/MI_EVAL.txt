# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician communicates respect and validation without judgment, often summarizing Sofia's ambivalence without blaming her drinking. Acceptance is uneven and can feel procedural rather than deeply affirming, especially around her fear, shame, and identity concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses strong reflective listening to accurately track Sofia's conflict between protecting her baby and maintaining her social identity. Empathy is solid but sometimes more cognitive than emotional, not fully mirroring the intensity of her underlying fear or distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the target behavior of reducing or eliminating alcohol use during a socially triggering pregnancy context. The therapist purposefully moves from broad ambivalence to concrete, doable steps such as dinner, texting Elena, and using a normalizing phrase.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with Sofia's ideas—dinner instead of the bar, lemonade, texting Elena, and casual wording—shaping the session plan. Some therapist-led reframing can still feel polished or interpretive, slightly limiting a fully balanced, client-driven stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist consistently invites Sofia to identify her own solutions, wording, and next steps, avoiding directive advice. The main gap is not explicitly reinforcing her right to choose or countering her expressed sense of pressure and obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicits Sofia's own motivations by asking what would feel safe, connected, and doable rather than supplying reasons for change. Evocation is strong but stops short of consistently deepening values, ability, confidence, or commitment talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist generally adopts a partnership stance, allowing Sofia's concerns and ideas to guide the conversation and avoiding expert-driven solutions. The stance is not always fully balanced, as some interpretive frames still sound therapist-led rather than client-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist successfully draws out client-generated change talk and helps Sofia build a realistic, low-pressure experiment. A key weakness is that the strongest change talk, such as pride and protection, is not consistently amplified or consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about not losing friends or feeling weird is handled without argument, shame, or confrontation, often reframed as a solvable problem. The therapist could use more skillful double-sided reflections to further reduce the weight of Sofia's social fears while preserving her identity.
