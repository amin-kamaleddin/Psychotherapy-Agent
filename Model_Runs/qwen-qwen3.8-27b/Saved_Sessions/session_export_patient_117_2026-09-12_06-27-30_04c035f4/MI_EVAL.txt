# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, repeatedly normalizing the client’s experience and progress. Acceptance is somewhat task-oriented and less warm during moments of shame or self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflective listening and accurately reflects practical concerns, but empathy remains mostly cognitive rather than emotionally deep. There is limited reflection of the client’s affect, embarrassment, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains strong, consistent focus on the target behavior, specifically reducing lunchtime smoking. The psychiatrist keeps the conversation tied to journal review, triggers, a concrete goal, strategies, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and collaborates on plan details, but often provides structure, options, and technical solutions before fully inviting client-generated ideas. The interaction is more counselor-guided than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices and framing plans as optional, allowing the client to select replacement strategies and timing. However, frequent provision of specific plans slightly reduces the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling and readiness questions to elicit some motivation, helping increase confidence from 5 to 6. Evocation is present but superficial, with limited deep exploration of the client’s values, personal reasons, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows several choices, but often adopts an expert, directive problem-solving role. A stronger partnership stance would more explicitly position the client as the expert on his own life and routine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist cultivates some change talk through readiness and scaling questions, leading to increased confidence and commitment. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, deepened, or expanded beyond practical readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and normalizes concern about slipping in a non-punitive way. However, the session does not consistently use double-sided reflections or reflective pivots to resolve ambivalence effectively.
