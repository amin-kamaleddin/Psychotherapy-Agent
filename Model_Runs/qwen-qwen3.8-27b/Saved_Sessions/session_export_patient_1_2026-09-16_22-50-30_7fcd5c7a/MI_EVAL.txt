# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance while avoiding blame for Ahmed’s mini-cigar use. Some responses were somewhat generic and did not consistently deepen warmth or normalize ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Ahmed’s concerns, logic, and the importance of his evening routine. Empathy was occasionally more cognitive than emotional and did not fully explore underlying fear or discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session began with the relevant concern but drifted toward alternative explanations such as coffee, grinding, dust, and dental care. More consistent gentle guidance back to mini-cigar use as the target behavior would improve direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with the psychiatrist framing the work as joint problem-solving. Ahmed’s plan to “separate the variables” shaped the session structure and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized Ahmed’s expertise, choice, and ownership of the process without pressure or moralizing. Language such as “you know your body better than anyone” strongly supported autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions helped draw out Ahmed’s thinking, but they mainly focused on testing alternative causes rather than eliciting motivation for change. More targeted questions about values, readiness, and confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively shared power by inviting Ahmed to choose the focus, define fairness, and determine next steps. Ahmed’s contributions clearly shaped the direction of the interview.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some moments of conditional change talk, especially around fairness and needing clear evidence. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or expand Ahmed’s own reasons for reducing or stopping mini-cigars.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and acknowledged sustaining statements rather than challenging them. However, the focus on alternative explanations allowed the sustain talk to remain relatively strong.
