# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with repeated affirmations of the patient’s effort. Acceptance is slightly limited by a formulaic, procedural tone during safety planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections address practical content but often miss deeper emotional themes such as exhaustion, loneliness, and hopelessness. The final patient statement is not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on immediate safety and identifying safer coping steps. Repetition of safety prompts and coping options reduces momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and frames work as collaborative, but largely designs the safety plan. The patient’s contributions do not substantially shape the session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges patient choice and avoids forcing decisions, but repeatedly asks for agreement to the safety plan after the patient says she cannot promise. Stronger MI would more firmly leave the decision to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions draw out some patient speech and limited change talk, but do not deeply explore values, motivations, or reasons for change. The patient’s change talk remains thin and underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for patient preferences and offers choices, supporting a collaborative stance. However, the session quickly moves into a directive clinical agenda rather than following the patient’s priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the patient’s willingness to lock the drawer and try grounding, evoking some change talk. She does not deeply expand importance, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the patient’s sustain talk without arguing or challenging it, which is appropriate. She uses few MI-consistent techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to redirect toward change.
