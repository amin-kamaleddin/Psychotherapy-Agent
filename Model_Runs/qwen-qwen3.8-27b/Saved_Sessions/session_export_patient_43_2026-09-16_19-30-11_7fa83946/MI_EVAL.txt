# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating toward both smoking and change. The main gap is that some warmth is somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture ambivalence well, especially the tension between needing smoking for stress and wanting less pressure at home. Empathy could be stronger by exploring the emotional weight beneath Eric’s practical statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on a concrete, doable target: not smoking in the truck during the commute. The clinician effectively narrows the work from broad ambivalence to the first ten minutes and a specific coping cue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed from Eric’s own ideas, such as the radio, watching the road, and “two on ten.” Collaboration is strong, though the clinician still guides the agenda and flow more than a fully mutual partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently frames Eric as the expert and acknowledges that he is not deciding to quit. Some autonomy-framed questions still gently steer toward making a specific change doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively draws out Eric’s own practical strategies and change talk. Evocation would be stronger if it more systematically explored Eric’s values, meanings, and deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Eric’s contributions clearly shape the conversation and the resulting plan. The session could be more strongly partnered by visibly negotiating agenda and next focus with Eric.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Eric produces useful change talk, including “maybe I won’t smoke in the truck,” “I can handle that,” and “I’ll try.” The clinician could further amplify deeper change talk around values, need, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through balanced reflections without arguing or shaming. The clinician could more fully explore what smoking is protecting for Eric before moving toward a behavior step.
