# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent, nonjudgmental respect and validates Elise’s social value of smoking without arguing or mocking. Acceptance is strong but could be richer by more explicitly affirming Elise as a person, her courage, and the difficulty of separating smoking from friendships.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections often accurately capture the dual role of smoking as a social connector and private reset. Some deeper feelings—fatigue, fear of rejection, identity loss, and anxiety—are not fully reflected or explored, preventing a top score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and its social implications, with questions tied to remaining connected without the cigarette. Direction is good but lacks a clear summary or next-step focus that would create stronger forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer treats Elise as the expert on her social life and invites her to generate ideas rather than prescribing solutions. The collaboration is strong but still fairly therapist-led, with limited explicit co-direction or shared agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids controlling language and explicitly tries not to add pressure, supporting Elise’s sense of choice. More frequent, explicit affirmation that the decision is entirely hers would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite Elise to identify her own reasons for change and the meaning of being connected without smoking. Evocation is effective but could be deeper by following up on subtle change talk with questions about importance, confidence, and self-meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer maintains a collaborative stance and frequently asks for Elise’s perspective, reinforcing her role in the process. Partnership is good but not fully equal in feel, since the session is still largely structured by the psychiatrist’s reflections and questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change-related statements, such as feeling less tense or being able to listen. However, change talk is not consistently reinforced, expanded, or transformed into stronger motivation using targeted MI techniques.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Elise’s reasons for smoking and acknowledges the social benefits. More skillful double-sided reflections and strategic reframing would help hold sustain talk while gently tilting toward change.
