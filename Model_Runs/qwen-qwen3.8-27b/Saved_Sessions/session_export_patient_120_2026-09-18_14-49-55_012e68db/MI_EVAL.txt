# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session, never labeling or dismissing Sofia's struggles. However, the acceptance occasionally reads as somewhat clinical rather than deeply individualized, lacking explicit normalization of Sofia's experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptionally accurate and nuanced reflective listening, consistently going beyond paraphrasing to capture emotional depth, underlying meaning, and the weight of Sofia's ambivalence. Complex reflections such as naming the paradox of friends who both support and hinder, and capturing the deeper meaning behind seeking connection, are particularly striking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session progresses logically from emotional exploration to identifying barriers, finding a resource, and planning a concrete action with clear momentum toward behavior change. The psychiatrist prevents drift into unproductive rumination while maintaining a natural, non-pushy flow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is clearly a two-way process shaped by Sofia's responses, with the psychiatrist using open-ended language to frame the session as a joint exploration. However, occasional interpretive moves (e.g., 'I think that's a really big insight') subtly shift the dynamic from equal partners to the psychiatrist as interpreter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames all actions as Sofia's choices, using language like 'you could,' 'what would it feel like to,' and 'is there a way you might' without ever prescribing. Sofia's final commitment emerges entirely from her own sense of agency rather than external pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out change talk from within Sofia through strategic questions and positive reflections of her own language, leading to a concrete self-initiated plan. However, the evocation could be more systematic in eliciting multiple types of change talk (desire, ability, need, values).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, positioning Sofia as the expert on her own experience and allowing her responses to shape the session's direction. The partnership could be slightly strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging Sofia's expertise and reducing moments where the psychiatrist positions herself as the interpreter.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively cultivates change talk through strategic questions that draw out Sofia's own motivations, culminating in a concrete commitment to text Maria tonight. The evocation is present and effective but could be more systematic in explicitly eliciting multiple types of change talk (e.g., desire, need, values).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is skillfully acknowledged and held without being reinforced or met with adversarial dialogue, allowing the conversation to naturally pivot toward change. The approach could be slightly more proactive in exploring and softening the 'living it out' theme that may underlie Sofia's ambivalence.
