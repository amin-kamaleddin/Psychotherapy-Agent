# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor shows consistent, nonjudgmental respect and normalizes Ryan's ambivalence without blaming his drinking. It is not a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat task-oriented and could more deeply affirm Ryan as a person separate from his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Ryan's ambivalence, practical worries, and discomfort with being managed. The empathy is not a 5 because it often summarizes practical concerns rather than exploring deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or value conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use in social situations and protecting Ryan's record. It is not a 5 because the direction sometimes becomes overly procedural, shifting from MI exploration into detailed harm-reduction planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works jointly with Ryan, adapts to his preferences, and incorporates his own ideas into the plan. It is not a 5 because the counselor still frequently offers many options and initiates next steps, making the collaboration feel uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently emphasizes choice, respects Ryan's refusal of a formal check-in, and avoids commanding or shaming him. It is not a 5 because repeated suggestions of scripts and check-ins can subtly pressure Ryan to accept more structure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions early on elicit Ryan's own concerns and reasons for change. The score is limited because the session frequently shifts from evocation to practical solution-giving, with limited deeper discrepancy, confidence, or commitment work.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan's input is accepted and included, but the counselor often takes on the role of planner by offering multiple options and scripts. This weakens the partnership message that Ryan is the primary expert on his own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan produces meaningful change talk, and some questions elicit it. The counselor does not consistently develop or amplify Ryan's change talk, leaving it somewhat superficial and often interrupted by planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally validates Ryan's sustain talk instead of arguing or reinforcing it, and redirects to values such as protecting his record. It is not a 5 because repeated planning options can keep Ryan focused on what he does not want.
