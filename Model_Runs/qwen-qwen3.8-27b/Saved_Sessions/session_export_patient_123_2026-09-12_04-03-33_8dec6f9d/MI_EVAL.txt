# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes Sofia’s uncertainty, though the warmth is somewhat procedural and task-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture content and mixed feelings, but they remain mostly at the content level and miss deeper emotional experiences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session focuses on study planning rather than the alcohol-related screening referral, so it lacks clear direction toward the target behavior of concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on the plan and lets Sofia’s choices shape next steps, but the structure and options are still largely provided by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia’s choices, pace, and preferences are consistently respected, with minimal pressure, though ownership could be reinforced more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-generated solutions and confidence are elicited, but there is limited exploration of deeper values, ambivalence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia is invited to choose topics, classes, and plan details, but the psychiatrist still provides a fairly structured framework rather than fully co-leading the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits small commitments and confidence increases, but does not consistently amplify or reflect back broader change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and incorporated into practical alternatives, though double-sided reflections could further validate ambivalence.
