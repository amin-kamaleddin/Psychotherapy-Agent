# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but moves quickly into advice and offers limited affirmations of Amina’s values as a parent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at times, but they often do not fully track or validate Amina’s deeper fear, exhaustion, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on parenting routines and co-parenting conflict, though it becomes prematurely solution-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supplies concrete plans rather than co-creating interventions with Amina, creating an uneven power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are sometimes offered, yet repeated scripts, directives, and legalistic suggestions undermine Amina’s sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** After the opening, the psychiatrist relies on external advice instead of eliciting Amina’s own motivations, strengths, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina is treated as an expert in some moments, but the session remains largely driven by the psychiatrist’s suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina offers clear change talk, but the psychiatrist mostly fails to amplify, reflect, or explore it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk and ambivalence are met with direct reassurance or added plans rather than complex reflections and evocative questions.
