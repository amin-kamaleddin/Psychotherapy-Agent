# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Hana’s choices even when she is not ready to quit. The acceptance is solid but somewhat procedural, and could be strengthened with warmer validation of her ambivalence and stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but remain surface-level, such as noting Hana’s curiosity and weighing the meaning of occasional smoking. The therapist does not deeply explore emotional concerns like fear of hidden gum damage or the role of smoking in her unwind time.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the dental concern and its connection to smoking-related oral health. If smoking cessation is the primary goal, the direction drifts toward dental hygiene, so stronger strategic momentum toward the smoking-change goal would improve the score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist collaborates by offering options and building on Hana’s suggestions, such as alarm use, cue-pairing, and floss picks. However, the session remains partly expert-led because the therapist provides much of the information and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Hana’s sense of choice through options, confidence scaling, and acceptance of her not-ready stance. A stronger 5 would include more explicit statements affirming that all decisions about smoking are hers to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist successfully evokes motivation and confidence for the flossing plan, with Hana generating a concrete routine and higher self-efficacy. Evocation is limited for the core smoking-cessation issue, as her initial curiosity and internal reasons are not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative, with Hana’s input shaping the plan through her preferences and practical ideas. Full power-sharing would require more explicit deference to Hana’s priorities and positioning the therapist more clearly as a guide.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change talk and self-efficacy around flossing, especially when Hana links flossing to the smoking cue. The main limitation is the lack of consistent elicitation of change talk for reducing or quitting smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Hana’s sustain talk and responds acceptingly to her not-ready statements. However, there is limited active use of sustain talk to explore ambivalence, such as through double-sided reflections.
