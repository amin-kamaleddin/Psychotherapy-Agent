# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates warmth, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard throughout. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and not fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections and perspective-taking, but empathy remains largely surface-level. Does not fully explore emotional undercurrents such as guilt, identity concerns, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, steady focus on smoking cessation and evening triggers. Focus is practical rather than consistently MI-guided in deepening motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options and meaningfully adjusts based on Natalia’s preferences. Collaboration is present but still partly shaped by the clinician’s suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames suggestions as options and avoids coercion. Autonomy support is strong, though some suggestions remain somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective early use of scaling and open questions to elicit motivation. Evocation becomes inconsistent as the session shifts toward practical problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and collaborates on choices, supporting a partnership stance. However, the clinician often leads with lists and plans, limiting client-driven exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits meaningful change talk early, especially around her son as a motivator. Change talk is not consistently amplified or deepened later in the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or shaming. Softening is mostly neutral rather than actively exploring discrepancy or turning sustain talk toward change.
