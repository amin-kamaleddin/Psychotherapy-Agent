# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Dylan's emotional experience with warm, non-judgmental reflections. However, acceptance stays largely at the surface level of emotional validation without explicit unconditional positive regard or normalization of his defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with complex reflections that accurately capture Dylan's perspective and subtle emotional undercurrents. Some reflections are repetitive, and the psychiatrist could have explored the identity threat Dylan experiences more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on the conduct incident but remains diffuse, circling around Dylan's feelings without progressively building toward a clear target behavior. The final directional question about a normal weekend is promising but comes only at the very end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses a non-authoritarian stance with some collaborative language and invites Dylan's input. However, the psychiatrist does not co-create a plan or explicitly frame the session as a joint effort, leaving the power dynamic somewhat therapist-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Dylan's autonomy without imposing advice or directives, allowing him to define his own experience. Could be strengthened with more explicit ownership language (e.g., 'that's your call') which would be more powerful with a resistant client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some evocative questions throughout but the approach is inconsistent, often giving way to extended reflective validation. Missed key opportunities to evoke change talk at strategic moments, and the best evocative move comes too late in the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a generally collaborative tone with some inclusive language, but does not explicitly position Dylan as the expert on his own life or negotiate the agenda. The session feels more like one-way exploration with Dylan responding rather than a true two-way partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A significant weakness — the psychiatrist makes very limited attempts to evoke or amplify Dylan's change talk. When Dylan offers admissions like 'maybe I should've just not gone,' the psychiatrist reflects frustration rather than strategically building on the shift.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with an appropriate non-argumentative stance but largely validates it at face value rather than using double-sided reflections or discrepancy work. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to help Dylan hear the tension between his stated values and the behavior that jeopardized them.
