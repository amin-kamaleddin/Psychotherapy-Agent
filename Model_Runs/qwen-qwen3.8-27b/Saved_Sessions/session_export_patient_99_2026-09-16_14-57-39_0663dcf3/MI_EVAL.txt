# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client's concerns without moralizing. Affirmations are generally strong but remain somewhat general and could include more explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate and captures the client's desire to keep things low-key and direct. The clinician falls short of a 5 by not deeply exploring the underlying feelings behind minimization, embarrassment, or social identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays reasonably focused on alcohol use in relation to ankle recovery and progresses toward a practical plan. The focus becomes too narrow around one Wednesday and immediate symptoms, limiting broader discussion of motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows the client to shape key decisions, such as the ankle follow-up and one-Wednesday test. Collaboration is strong but not fully client-led because the clinician still introduces multiple structured options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms the client's choice, avoids lectures or pressure, and supports a low-effort plan. Some therapist-generated suggestions subtly steer the content, preventing a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes the client's own ideas, but mainly around practical, injury-based indicators. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, personal meaning, and readiness for change is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a non-expert, collaborative posture and lets the client's life experience guide the session. It is not a 5 because the clinician still introduces preset structures and options before fully opening the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective change talk is elicited, including willingness to test skipping one Wednesday and continuing if it helps. The session lacks consistent use of DARN-C questions, rulers, or amplification to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client's sustain talk, but does not consistently turn it toward change. More skilled softening would use double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to balance keeping things casual with protecting recovery.
