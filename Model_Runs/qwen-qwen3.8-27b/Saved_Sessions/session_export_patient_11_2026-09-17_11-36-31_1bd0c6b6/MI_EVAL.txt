# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and non-judgmental throughout, with strong reframing of the client's self-critical language (e.g., 'not a character flaw, a state shift'). Minor deduction for occasional affirmations that feel slightly therapist-driven rather than organically emerging from the moment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and sophisticated reflections that capture both the cognitive and emotional dimensions of the client's experience, with progressive depth across the session. Reflections are varied (simple, complex, double-sided) and precisely track the evolving emotional landscape.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (increasing physical activity) with strategic, progressive movement from broad motivation to concrete implementation. No topic drift; every question and reflection is directly tied to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative stance throughout, using language like 'I'm curious' and 'can I' to position self as a fellow explorer rather than authority. The resulting plan is entirely the client's creation, with the psychiatrist adapting questions and reflections to what the client brings forward.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is the structural foundation of the session—every question elicits the client's own choices rather than influencing them. The psychiatrist never uses directive language, and the client makes all decisions about frequency, timing, location, and method independently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within the client through varied, skillful questions that draw out and expand her internal motivations. No external reasons for change are provided at any point; all motivation is client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Substantively (not just superficially) collaborative, with explicit attribution of insight and agency to the client throughout. The psychiatrist consistently reinforces that expertise, motivation, and solutions reside within the client, never positioning self as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully cultivates change talk through strategic questions, explicit naming and reinforcing of the client's own change language, and building on emerging commitment talk. The session ends with the client in a state of increased, specific, self-generated change talk and concrete planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk appropriately without arguing or dismissing, using strategic reframes to reduce the perceived threat of failure. Limited by the relatively low amount of sustain talk in the session; a double-sided reflection on the client's final expressed doubt could have further deepened the softening.
