# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor consistently validated Isabella’s values, autonomy, and choices without judgment or blame. Warmth and a nonjudgmental stance were maintained even when discussing risk, slips, and resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Isabella’s practical concerns, ambivalence, and need for control. Emotional nuance was sometimes limited, especially around the deeper meaning of wine, identity, or self-concept.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on alcohol use during pregnancy. Each step moved logically from exploration toward a concrete, time-limited behavior plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed through questions, preferences, and shared decision-making. Client input clearly guided the follow-up format, content, and wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through explicit non-directive language and client-controlled planning choices. The counselor avoided pressure, monitoring language, and expert-driven directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited Isabella to generate her own motivation, plan, and confidence. Deeper follow-up questions could have further developed values, confidence, and social coping.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor clearly positioned Isabella as the expert on her own life and allowed her choices to shape the session. The final plan reflected her preferences, language, and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited useful change talk through open questions, scaling, and affirmations. Follow-up probes could have further expanded the client’s own reasons, ability, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not challenged and was often acknowledged. However, key resistances around tradition, identity, partner concerns, and social drinking could have been softened more skillfully.
