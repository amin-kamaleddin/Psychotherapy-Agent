# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental but remains somewhat generic and procedural. At times, “That sounds reasonable” feels prematurely accepting when the patient minimizes the alcohol concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is one useful early reflection of frustration, but most emotionally relevant statements about age, peers, and game nights are answered with information. The session stays more educational than empathic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stated focus is alcohol use, but the session frequently drifts to ankle care, urgent care, and administrative details. The target is not collaboratively narrowed or sustained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions and offers an A/B choice, but still supplies most of the structure and content. The patient’s concerns about game nights and social pressure are not used to co-create a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and respects the patient’s decision to use a local urgent care. However, the session remains quite directive with specific medical instructions and proposed limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly provides reasons for change rather than evoking the patient’s own motivations. There are a few open openings, but they are not followed by deeper exploration of values or priorities.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely expert-driven rather than equal-partner. The psychiatrist leads the agenda and supplies most of the content, limiting the patient’s role as an expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers some willingness and small change statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently notice, affirm, or expand them. The session moves quickly to advice instead of building motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing, but sustain talk about risk, tracking, and giving up game nights is not effectively softened. The alcohol ambivalence is left unresolved or met with more directive advice.
