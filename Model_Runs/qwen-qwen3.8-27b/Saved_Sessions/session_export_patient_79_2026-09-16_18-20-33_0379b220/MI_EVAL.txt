# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids judgment and validates Luis's need for control despite his resistance. Acceptance is strong but feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Luis's concern about surveillance, grading, and loss of autonomy. The therapist could explore deeper emotions and vary repetitive protective framing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and a low-pressure self-management cue. Direction would be stronger with a clearer final consolidation of the agreed plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan by inviting Luis to define the cue, follow-up language, and success. The interaction treats Luis as an equal partner rather than imposing a medical directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Luis's freedom to choose, including no compliance tracking and no follow-up call if he does not return. Autonomy is central, with little pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions help Luis generate his own smallest step, success definition, and preferred next-visit script. Evocation could be stronger by eliciting deeper values, confidence, and importance of staying well.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Luis's ideas directly shape the session, and the psychiatrist avoids acting as the expert imposing a plan. Partnership is clear through collaborative questions and mutual agreement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Luis offers useful change talk, such as a visual cue and a defined good result, but much of the speech remains autonomy or sustain talk. The therapist needs to affirm and amplify change talk more consistently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reframes resistance as information rather than failure and drops language like checklist and compliance. This reduces defensiveness, though it could more actively transform sustain talk into committed change talk.
