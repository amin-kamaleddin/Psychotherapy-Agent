# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and nonjudgmental, but the tone stayed relatively transactional. There was limited affirmation of the client's effort or self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interviewer offered surface-level reflections but did not deeply explore the client's stress, ambivalence, or underlying concerns. The session moved quickly into problem-solving before fully validating the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stayed focused on practical weight and blood pressure changes. The interviewer narrowed the discussion to a specific, manageable action despite some procedural drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's constraints shaped the final plan, but the interviewer provided most of the structure and content. The interaction was more clinician-guided than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interviewer supported choice by asking the client to select a doable option and allowing him to leave without extra commitments. However, frequent recommendations and medical caveats slightly reduced the client's sense of leading the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interviewer mainly provided reasons for change and specific behavioral suggestions. The client's own motivations and values were not deeply elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some invitation for client input, but the interviewer largely framed the options and recommendations. The client was not consistently positioned as the expert on his own change process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interviewer made limited attempts to elicit change language, mainly by asking which option was doable. The client's own reasons for change were not actively amplified or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer avoided arguing with the client's barriers and suggested minimal steps, but did not consistently redirect sustain talk toward change. The client's focus on time and work constraints remained largely unreframed.
