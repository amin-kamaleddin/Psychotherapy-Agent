# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with validating statements such as “I hear you” and “treat slips as information, not failure.” Warmth is somewhat procedural and lacks deeper individualized affirmations of the client’s strengths or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening reflection captures the client’s irritation and ambivalence about external pressure versus personal motivation. However, empathy becomes briefer and more task-focused as the session moves into practical problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of getting to school on time. The psychiatrist efficiently narrows the concern, identifies relevant barriers, and keeps interventions tied to that goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting the client to choose changes, adjusting to feedback, and avoiding an authoritarian stance. The approach could be stronger by asking more about the client’s own ideas before offering practical solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by letting the client select changes, respecting boundaries, and emphasizing no pressure. Repeated practical instructions and recaps can make the interaction feel somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help the client identify barriers and select small changes, supporting some change talk. The evocation remains practical and does not deeply explore the client’s values, confidence, or intrinsic motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and allows preferences to shape the plan, showing a collaborative stance. However, the repeated offering and recapping of practical options can subtly shift the interaction toward expert guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change statements related to sleep, school punctuality, and personal ownership. These statements are not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened to strengthen the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument, validating frustration, and reframing motivation away from simply stopping nagging. There are missed opportunities to use more active MI techniques with ambivalent moments.
