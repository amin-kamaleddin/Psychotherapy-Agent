# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and reduces shame around slips. Acceptance is supportive but somewhat procedural and does not fully validate the emotional weight of Aisha’s self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The responses accurately reflect Aisha’s practical constraints and ambivalence, but often stay on the surface of the task. More emotional reflection of shame, body image distress, and fear of failure would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on a clear target behavior: a short, realistic after-work stretch routine. The movement from motivation to a concrete plan is organized, coherent, and clinically focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Aisha’s preferences and co-creates many parts of the plan, such as the checklist, cue, and check-in. Collaboration is strong but uneven because the clinician supplies much of the concrete behavioral content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choice, permission, and low-pressure language around performance. Autonomy support is good, but the plan remains fairly prescriptive with many clinician-generated tactics.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial open questions and a readiness ruler elicit Aisha’s reasons for change, but the session quickly shifts into behavior planning. There are missed opportunities to deepen Aisha’s own values, confidence, and definition of success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha’s choices shape the plan, and the psychiatrist generally adopts a collaborative, respectful stance. Partnership is limited by the psychiatrist’s expert-driven provision of specific exercises and tactics rather than first eliciting Aisha’s own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha expresses meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms some of it. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or explore this change talk in a way that deepens motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively softens Aisha’s fears of failure and inconsistency by normalizing missed days and flexible effort. The responses are practical and reassuring, though more MI-specific reframing would strengthen the skill.
