# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and validation, but the stance is often directive and tied to the client’s progress. Unconditional positive regard and broader normalization of ambivalence are limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture some of the client’s surface concerns and stress. Deeper feelings such as shame, fear of being a neglectful mother, and loss of control are not sufficiently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and moves toward a manageable next step. It becomes somewhat task-oriented, reducing emphasis on exploring ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to choose among options and contribute to the plan. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, leaving the interaction more therapist-led than equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Choice is acknowledged at times, but specific scripts, thresholds, and plans can feel controlling. The client’s freedom to choose pace or a different path is not strongly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some initial open questions are helpful, but the session quickly shifts to closed, option-based questions. The client’s own values, motivations, and self-generated reasons for change are not consistently elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts a collaborative stance by asking the client to select among options. The overall approach remains more guided than truly partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some movement toward action, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or strengthen this change talk. Missed opportunities include confidence, importance, and values-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but often met with problem-solving rather than MI-consistent softening. The client’s competing motivations are not fully integrated through double-sided reflections or strategic reframing.
