# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and reduces defensiveness with validating statements such as “I’m not here to judge you” and “That makes sense.” The warmth is sometimes procedural or summary-like, and there are limited statements of individualized unconditional positive regard, preventing a full 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate and consistent reflective listening, capturing both sides of Anika’s ambivalence around vaping, basketball performance, and fear of losing her spot. Reflections are strong and clinically useful, with only a minor limitation in occasionally sounding structured rather than deeply probing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on the target behavior of vaping/marijuana use in relation to basketball, school standing, and weekly functioning. There is little topic drift, and the conversation moves clearly toward a concrete, actionable plan while remaining client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist functions as an equal partner rather than an authority, co-constructing the agenda and building plans from Anika’s own ideas. The final plan, texting Sarah to meet after dinner, emerges collaboratively and supports a genuinely equal therapeutic alliance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported through pressure-reducing statements, open-ended questions, and choice-affirming language. The psychiatrist avoids coercion, persuasion, and direct advice, repeatedly positioning Anika as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits motivation from within Anika by asking questions that draw out her own values, coping strategies, and practical steps. The score is limited because evocation is somewhat focused on practical action steps and does not consistently explore deeper identity, values, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Anika’s input at nearly every stage, and her answers directly shape the session’s direction and content. The interaction is highly collaborative and power-shared, with no assumption of an expert or prescriptive role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strengthens Anika’s change-focused statements about shooting better sober, wanting to win, and feeling confident she can manage. The score is not a 5 because there are fewer follow-up questions that deepen the meaning behind her change talk or confidence rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging the appeal of vaping without judgment and redirecting toward change planning. The softening is strong but not maximal because it relies more on neutral acknowledgment than on double-sided reflections that explicitly link sustain talk to Anika’s own values.
