# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Elias’s honesty, fear, and ambivalence. It remains somewhat formulaic and could more explicitly affirm his worth independent of recovery or normalize relapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening and captures Elias’s ambivalence, shame, fear, and desire to change. It could deepen further into the identity-level meanings behind statements such as fear of being “the drunk guy” or loss of self-trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on relapse prevention, maintaining recovery, and developing a concrete coping plan. There is no significant topic drift, and the conversation advances in a clear, change-focused, and client-paced way.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is built collaboratively from Elias’s own ideas rather than imposed by the psychiatrist. The collaboration could be stronger with more explicit equal-partner language, such as co-creating the agenda or choosing options together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Elias’s choice and ownership without direct advice or pressure. Elias is encouraged to decide the concrete details of his plan, reflecting strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely evokes Elias’s own values, fears, and solutions through open questions and minimal leading. It could be more powerful by asking follow-up questions that evoke deeper personal meaning and motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that Elias is the expert on his own life, and his ideas consistently shape the session. The partnership could be made more explicit through agenda negotiation or clearer acknowledgment of Elias as the primary decision-maker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates opportunities for Elias to express desire, ability, and commitment, and affirms this change talk. It could be stronger by using rulers, summarizing change talk, or strategically expanding on what success would mean to Elias.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Elias’s sustain talk without challenging, minimizing, or reinforcing it. It could be enhanced with more active double-sided reflections or strategic reframe questions to help resolve ambivalence.
