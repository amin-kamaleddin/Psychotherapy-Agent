# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful, and validating stance throughout. Acceptance is strong but can feel somewhat formulaic with repeated generic phrases.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture surface-level ambivalence and discomfort, but often miss the deeper social fears, isolation, and worry about burdening others.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on community participation and small behavioral steps, with occasional emphasis on logistics over motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Isabella’s input and incorporates her ideas, but still provides most of the structure and suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through choice language and options, though dense behavioral planning can make the plan feel more therapist-constructed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and small-step questions elicit some motivation, but the client’s deeper values, hopes, and reasons for change are not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A collaborative tone is present, but the psychiatrist often leads the agenda and generates most of the options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, but there are limited opportunities to expand or amplify the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and validates ambivalence, but does not consistently use double-sided reflections to move toward change.
