# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, using validating phrases such as “I hear you.” However, frequent directive problem-solving and expectations for change can reduce the sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing Jamal’s ambivalence between fitting in and protecting eligibility. The empathy could go deeper into emotions such as fear of rejection and belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on alcohol use, eligibility, and practical change planning. Direction is clear, though it becomes repetitive at times without always building a stronger path toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration through offering options and incorporating client ideas. However, the counselor often takes the lead by providing scripts, plans, and strategies rather than jointly constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported by asking for preferences and offering choices. Yet repeated prompts to make concrete decisions and choose a rule can feel pressuring given the client’s ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses effective evoking techniques such as scaling and follow-up questions. However, the counselor frequently shifts into advice before fully eliciting the client’s internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership is present through asking what the client wants and offering options. The interaction is often expert-driven, with the counselor supplying much of the plan rather than drawing on the client’s own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked through scaling and small steps. However, frequent advice-giving limits further development of the client’s own values, reasons, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges sustain talk without directly arguing against it. Softening is inconsistent because accepting goals such as “max 1” or “not getting caught” can reinforce the status quo.
