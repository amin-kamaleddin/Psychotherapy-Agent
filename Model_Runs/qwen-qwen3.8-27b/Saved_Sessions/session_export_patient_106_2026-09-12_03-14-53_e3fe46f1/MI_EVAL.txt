# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of the patient’s honesty and effort. The warmth is appropriate but somewhat procedural, with limited deeper affirmation of her exhaustion and identity as a protective parent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient’s ambivalence and dual needs to cope and protect her son. Empathy is often content-focused, with fewer reflections of underlying emotions such as guilt, fear, exhaustion, or worry about being a bad example.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking, stress, and the patient’s son, moving toward a concrete low-pressure plan. Some portions drift into harm-reduction logistics, which can feel more advisory than MI-process-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by building the plan around the patient’s stated limits and preferences. However, at times options are offered before fully eliciting the patient’s own ideas, which could make the partnership feel less fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with repeated permission-seeking, choice-offering, and acceptance that the patient may skip or adapt the experiment. A 5 would include even stronger explicit reinforcement that the patient fully owns both successes and setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks about importance, doability, and signs of success, which helps generate a patient-centered plan. Evocation is limited by an advice-driven approach and less exploration of deeper values, parenting identity, long-term concerns, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and frequently invites the patient’s input, with the patient substantially shaping the plan. At times the psychiatrist remains advisory by offering options and factual information, rather than more explicitly treating the patient as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some meaningful change talk, especially around protecting the son and trying a small experiment once. Change talk is not consistently deepened, amplified, or followed up with stronger scaling, importance, or confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the patient’s sustain talk and validates the smoking break as understandable. However, key statements such as needing the break, being healthy, and smoking outside are not consistently softened or explored for discrepancy.
