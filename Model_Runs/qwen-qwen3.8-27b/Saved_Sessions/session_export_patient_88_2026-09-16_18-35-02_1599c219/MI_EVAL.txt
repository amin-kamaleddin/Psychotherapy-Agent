# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and avoids labeling or challenging the client. Warmth could be deepened beyond accurate reflective summaries with more individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Marcus’s ambivalence, social pressure, and emotional concerns about fitting in. Some reflections remain more cognitive and summarizing than emotionally deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on alcohol use at parties and developing safer, autonomous strategies. There is no meaningful drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is largely co-constructed, with the client offering many concrete ideas. Some clinician introductions of terms subtly steer the framing of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Marcus’s right to choose and avoids directive or pressuring language. The client’s conditional plan is accepted without being pushed toward stronger commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions help Marcus generate his own change talk and practical options. The evocation could be strengthened by exploring deeper values, identity, and confidence more fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician clearly positions Marcus as the expert on his own life and social context. The session is strongly shaped by the client’s own contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits concrete client-generated change ideas and reinforces them using Marcus’s own words. Change talk could be more consistently amplified into stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing or moralizing when the client expresses reasons to continue drinking. Sustain talk could be more actively linked to ambivalence and values rather than left as a stable endpoint.
