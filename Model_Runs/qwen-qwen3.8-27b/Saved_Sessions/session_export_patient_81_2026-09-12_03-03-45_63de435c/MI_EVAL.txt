# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the patient's concerns without moralizing. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic, lacking deeper affirmation of strengths or normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture the patient's ambivalence, practical concerns, and preferences. However, reflections remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional themes such as identity, fear of exclusion, and discomfort with peer pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the patient's chosen target of practical money management and weekend spending. The focus is clear but narrow, with limited connection to broader values, career, or social identity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as a joint effort and actively invites patient input into the plan. Collaboration is strong but occasionally guided by the psychiatrist's proposed structures rather than fully emergent from the patient's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces the patient's freedom to choose, respects his limits, and avoids pressure or lectures. He offers options, accepts the scaled-back plan, and explicitly supports patient ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks appropriate questions to draw out the patient's views and practical preferences. However, evocation remains mostly superficial and does not consistently elicit intrinsic motivation, values, or deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by offering options and letting the patient's ideas shape the session. Partnership is strong but occasionally involves fairly specific suggestions rather than fully shared direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked around practical goals, avoiding rent requests, and seeing where money goes. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk by linking it to values, confidence, or importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist neutrally acknowledges the patient's sustain talk without reinforcing it. He does not actively soften, reframe, or explore the discrepancy between the patient's minimization and stated financial concerns.
