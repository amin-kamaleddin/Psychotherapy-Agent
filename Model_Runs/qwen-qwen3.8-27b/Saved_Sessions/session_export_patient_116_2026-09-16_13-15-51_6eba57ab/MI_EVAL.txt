# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance by asking permission and accepting refusal of scripts, forms, alarms, and check-ins without blame. Some repeated offers of options and a somewhat formulaic pattern prevent a full score of 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers adequate reflections of content such as side effects, skipping doses, feeling like a project, and importance of work/family. Empathy remains surface-level rather than deeply exploring the emotional meaning behind phrases like 'static.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on medication adherence, side effects, routines, and safety without wandering into unrelated topics. Could be stronger by narrowing to one primary barrier such as autonomy, side effects, or medical distrust.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively by inviting input, adjusting options based on client objections, and treating preferences as important. The therapist still generates many options, which can make the session feel somewhat therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and ownership by repeatedly emphasizing that the client is free to stop, decline, or decide on his own terms. Accepts boundaries around scripts, forms, check-ins, and alarms without pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some evocative attempts by asking what matters most, what gets in the way, and what would feel tolerable. Evocation is limited by frequent shifts into clinician-generated suggestions rather than deeper exploration of the client's own motivations and ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a partnership stance through permission, joint language, and responsiveness to client preferences. The client's expertise is not fully highlighted, and the therapist remains somewhat option-generating rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens space for change-relevant language by asking about values, intentions, and possible routines. The clinician does not consistently deepen or reinforce the client's change talk, often shifting into problem-solving suggestions instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids arguing and accepts resistance such as no pillbox, no alarm, no homework, and no check-ins. Sustain talk remains central and is not consistently redirected toward change using strategic double-sided reflections.
