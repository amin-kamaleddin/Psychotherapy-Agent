# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, validating tone and acknowledges Aizada’s frustration and fear. Acceptance remains tied to her biomedical requests rather than expressing deeper unconditional regard and normalizing ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are several reflections of Aizada’s emotional distress, but they stay largely surface-level. The psychiatrist quickly shifts to problem-solving instead of exploring deeper fear, identity loss, or feelings of being dismissed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has clear momentum but is directed entirely toward biomedical tasks such as letters, scans, and clinic options. It does not strategically guide the conversation toward ambivalence, the stress-symptom connection, or client-driven change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language initially, but the interaction becomes heavily counselor-driven. The psychiatrist provides solutions, drafts documents, and makes recommendations rather than co-creating the approach with Aizada.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Autonomy support is minimal because the psychiatrist takes over letters, clinic options, and next steps rather than supporting Aizada’s agency. Aizada’s final request for the counselor to make the decision shows the breakdown of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Evocation is largely absent, as the psychiatrist provides answers, plans, and options without eliciting Aizada’s own motivations or insights. The session relies on counselor-driven solutions rather than drawing out the client’s internal resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices early in the session, but quickly shifts to a directive, manager-like role. Aizada’s disengagement is evident when she asks the psychiatrist to book the first available option rather than continue choosing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Aizada offers clear change talk about stress making her symptoms worse, but the psychiatrist does not reflect, explore, or expand it. No meaningful change talk is evoked or strengthened throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces Aizada’s sustain talk by supporting the biomedical framing, testing demands, and GP dismissal. There is no use of double-sided reflection to gently broaden her perspective or soften the cancer fear.
