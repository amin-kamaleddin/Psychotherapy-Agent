# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s progress and effort. However, acceptance is somewhat generic and task-focused, with less acknowledgment of the client’s underlying vulnerability and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses are limited and often replaced by factual, problem-solving statements. The psychiatrist misses key opportunities to reflect the client’s fear of failure, self-doubt, and emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing smoking and narrows the work to a concrete Monday experiment. The main limitation is that the direction becomes overly plan-driven and mechanical at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes choices and shapes the final plan, indicating some collaboration. However, the psychiatrist often drives the conversation by generating many options and refinements rather than creating more space for client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects some client preferences and uses autonomy-supportive language. Autonomy is undercut by frequent prescriptive instructions and repeated restructuring of the client’s plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a helpful open start and some eliciting of the client’s intended experiment. Evocation remains limited because the psychiatrist does not consistently draw out deeper motivation, values, confidence, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows client choice, supporting a functional partnership. Still, the process feels more expert-led than truly collaborative, with the counselor frequently leading the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it with supportive affirmations. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or strategically use the client’s own change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental language and avoids arguing with the client’s concerns. Sustain talk is not consistently softened through reflections or pivots, and doubts are often met with additional behavioral strategies instead.
