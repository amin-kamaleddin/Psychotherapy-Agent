# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental but often ties acceptance to the client's cooperation and correct choices. More unconditional regard and normalization of ambivalence would strengthen this criterion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and captures practical and emotional concerns. Deeper emotional attunement to fear, overwhelm, and internal conflict would make the empathy more consistent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on medication adherence and moves toward concrete next steps. The focus on logistics sometimes reduces attention to the client's values and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and incorporates the client's ideas, such as linking medication to coffee and setting alarms. The dynamic becomes less collaborative when the clinician frequently generates options before asking for the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client's choices and avoids coercive language. Some advisory statements, such as calling pharmacist checking a smart move, subtly reduce the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some evoking questions and elicits client-driven reasons for change. The process is limited by frequent clinician advice and insufficient use of the client's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and lets the client shape much of the plan. The interaction becomes less equal when the clinician steps into an expert problem-solver role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as wanting to stay healthy for her family. These statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or used to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids argument and sometimes reflects the client's minimization without judgment. Double-sided reflections and deeper exploration of ambivalence would better soften sustain talk.
