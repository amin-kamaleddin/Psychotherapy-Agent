# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor was largely nonjudgmental and validating, normalizing Nadine’s exhaustion, guilt, and parenting concerns. A few reassuring phrases such as “you’re not being dramatic” risked briefly minimizing her emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor showed adequate perspective-taking early in the session, but reflections often stayed surface-level and shifted quickly into problem-solving. There were missed opportunities to deepen emotional attunement after statements such as not remembering when she last ate or fearing she is admitting she cannot cope.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained generally focused on the presenting concerns of infant sleep, parental exhaustion, and practical support. A clearer collaboratively defined change goal would have sharpened the direction further.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There was meaningful collaboration in drafting messages and selecting small, manageable strategies. The counselor still supplied much of the content, so the interaction was collaborative but not consistently client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently offered options and avoided pressuring Nadine to call the pediatrician or adopt specific routines. Stronger autonomy support would include more explicit affirmation that her decisions are hers and more space for her to generate next steps independently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited some client preferences and concrete next steps, such as sending the message and trying white noise or a timer. However, there was limited open-ended exploration of Nadine’s values, confidence, internal reasons for change, and existing strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and used Nadine’s choices in several parts of the session. Nevertheless, the counselor retained a fairly expert role by drafting messages and proposing concrete routines, limiting a fully partnership-driven agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evoked some change-related intention, especially around small actions like sending the message, using white noise, and trying a timer. There was less consistent deepening of change talk through questions about importance, confidence, values, or personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately normalized sustain talk and gently reframed feelings of guilt and weakness without arguing against them. More strategic use of double-sided reflections, affirmations, or active pivoting would have helped resolve the tension more effectively.
