# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and frequently affirms Elena, such as “you’re a good mom” and “small steps matter.” However, the acceptance is sometimes undercut by quickly moving into problem-solving and action plans.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Elena’s stress, exhaustion, and fear of quitting, but the reflections are often brief. Deeper exploration is limited because empathy is quickly followed by advice or planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation, stress, and protecting her son, which provides clear direction. The direction becomes directive when the psychiatrist repeatedly pushes plans or experiments despite Elena’s expressed hesitation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input by offering choices, but the session is mainly led by the clinician’s problem-solving agenda. Elena’s preferences are used to adjust plans rather than co-creating them with her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through options and language like “if you want to explore quitting” and “small steps matter.” Autonomy support is weakened by the repeated emphasis on trying a small step or experiment, which can feel like subtle pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encourages small action and acknowledges Elena’s willingness to try not smoking in the car. There is less effort to evoke Elena’s own values, motivations, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and shows some collaborative stance by asking “which would help?” and “which sounds doable?” The partnership is limited because Elena’s ideas do not substantially reshape the agenda or next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly asks logistical or action-oriented questions rather than evoking change talk. Elena’s small change statement is affirmed, but it is not followed by deeper exploration of what the step means to her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena’s sustain talk, such as needing smoking to cope and not being ready, is acknowledged but not deeply softened with MI strategies. The psychiatrist often responds with harm-reduction advice or smaller plans, which may reinforce the status quo.
