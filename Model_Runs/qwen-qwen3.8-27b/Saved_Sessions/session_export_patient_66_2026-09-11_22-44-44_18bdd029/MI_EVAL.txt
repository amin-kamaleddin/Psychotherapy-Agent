# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids arguing with the patient’s resistance to quit. The stance is supportive but can feel generic and task-focused rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is one clear empathic reflection about losing a routine and social concern, but the psychiatrist often misses deeper identity-based and emotional themes. Empathic accuracy becomes inconsistent as the patient’s concerns become more nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally relevant to smoking and workplace disruption, but it lacks a clear, strategic focus on a specific patient-centered goal. More agenda-setting and summarizing would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts some patient choices and incorporates his preferences into the final plan, but the session is largely expert-driven. There are too many unsolicited options and practical suggestions, which limits true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the patient’s autonomy by avoiding pressure to quit and respecting his limits. The score is limited by occasional directive language and repeated offering of concrete plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few open evocative questions early on, but then shifts heavily into giving information and advice. Rich patient statements about image and competence are not used to elicit deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist quickly adopts an expert, advisory role rather than consistently conveying that the patient is the expert on his own change. The patient’s input shapes the micro-plan, but not the overall direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not strongly evoke, amplify, or consolidate it. Reinforcements are brief and do not help the patient explore desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with the patient’s sustain talk, but does not actively soften or reframe it. More double-sided reflections would help hold both the patient’s attachment to smoking and his concern about appearing disruptive.
