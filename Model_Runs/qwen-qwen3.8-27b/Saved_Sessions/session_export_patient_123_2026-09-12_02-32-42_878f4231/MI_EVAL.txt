# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes the client’s discomfort without blame or pressure. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and could be more individualized and affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections track the client’s frustration and pressure to perform, but remain surface-level. The clinician misses deeper feelings of being watched, labeled, or ambivalent about being flagged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts away from the alcohol-related referral toward managing check-in questions. It lacks consistent guidance connecting the client’s discomfort to the underlying target concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers choices and invites input, but remains the main source of solutions and scripts. The broader agenda is not truly co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician respects the client’s choice to stop and avoids coercion or judgment. Mildly directive script-giving and limited autonomy support around the alcohol concern prevent a higher rating.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open questions begin the session but are not developed, and the clinician substitutes suggestions for eliciting client motivation. There is little exploration of values, concerns, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician shares practical decisions but does not fully acknowledge the client as the expert on her own life. Direction remains more service-oriented than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal elicitation of change talk about alcohol or the referral concern. The session focuses on scripted responses rather than the client’s own motivations or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and validates the client’s desire to stop, which prevents escalation. It does not strategically use reflections or curiosity to redirect dismissive talk toward the underlying issue.
