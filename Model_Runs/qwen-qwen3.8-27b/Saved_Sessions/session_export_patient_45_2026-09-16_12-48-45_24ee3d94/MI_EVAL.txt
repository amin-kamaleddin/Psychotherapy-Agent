# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance. Warmth is somewhat task-focused and could better address the client's emotional burden.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's perspective, exhaustion, and internal conflict. Empathy could go deeper into the fear, privacy concerns, and emotional meaning of lifelong medication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing beer intake after hard shifts and builds a concrete plan. It could be more efficient by reducing repeated readiness/confidence questions and rapid option offers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is clearly shaped by the client's preferences and chosen supports. Collaboration could become less structured by inviting more client-generated options first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices and avoids coercion. Autonomy could be strengthened by more directly reinforcing the client's ownership rather than relying on the doctor's validation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and questions help draw out motivation, but the psychiatrist quickly moves into problem-solving. More evocative questions could connect the behavior to the client's values, identity, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative and repeatedly invites the client to choose. The psychiatrist's structured options and drafts make the process somewhat therapist-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates a concrete plan and some reasons for change. The psychiatrist could better amplify and deepen the client's own change language and capability statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client's resistance. Sustain talk is acknowledged but not actively reframed or redirected toward the client's own agency and values.
