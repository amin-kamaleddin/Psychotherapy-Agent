# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating language throughout the session. Warmth is strong but somewhat indirect, and the final concern about being in trouble is not explicitly reaffirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers clear complex reflections of both content and underlying meaning. However, some reflections become slightly interpretive, and the client’s mild anxiety at the end is not deeply reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session remains broad and exploratory around after-school routines, friends, and social comparison. It does not maintain a clear link to the specific alcohol-related referral behavior or target risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the session as a joint exploration and adapts to the client’s stated interests. However, the counselor remains the primary agenda-setter by continuously supplying guiding questions and multiple-choice prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids directives, labels, and pressure while affirming the client’s expertise. A key opportunity is missed when the client asks whether she is doing something wrong, without an explicit reaffirmation of her autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to explore meanings around connection, ease, rest, and energy. This evocation remains general and does not clearly develop motivation for change relative to the referral concern.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s perspective and treats the client as an expert on her own after-school life. Partnership is limited because the client remains passive while the counselor continues to lead with question stems.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little explicit change talk, particularly regarding alcohol-related risk. The counselor does not effectively elicit or amplify the client’s desire, ability, need, or intention for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with the client’s minimizing statements and gently reframes some of the routine. However, the sustain talk is not consistently softened or strategically redirected toward change.
