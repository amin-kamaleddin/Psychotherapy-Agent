# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirming statements and honors the client's boundary about alcohol. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper in explicitly normalizing ambivalence and emotional difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects ambivalence, practical concerns, and the fear of losing progress. Deeper empathic responses would more specifically capture emotions such as fear, exhaustion, and parental worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on reducing evening smoking and addressing the cough, moving coherently toward a concrete plan. Initial questions were broad enough to risk distraction from the target behavior, so direction could be tighter from the start.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids lecturing or imposing a plan and instead builds the client's own ideas into a realistic strategy. The client contributes substantially to the plan, reflecting a genuine collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strongly supports autonomy by avoiding pressure to quit, respecting the client's boundary on alcohol, and inviting her to choose doable steps. The session reinforces the client's ownership and freedom to decide her own path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client's own reasons, values, and strategies for change rather than supplying reasons for change. Client-generated insights about health, family, and practical coping are consistently drawn out and expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction conveys that the client is the expert on her own life, with the psychiatrist acting as a guide rather than an authority. Collaboration and shared decision-making are evident throughout the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes the client's own reasons for change and helps her articulate a concrete plan. The client generates specific strategies and reframes possible relapse as acceptable progress.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively by avoiding confrontation and gently reframing the client's justifications. The clinician helps the client see reduction as meaningful rather than challenging or minimizing her reasons for maintaining the status quo.
