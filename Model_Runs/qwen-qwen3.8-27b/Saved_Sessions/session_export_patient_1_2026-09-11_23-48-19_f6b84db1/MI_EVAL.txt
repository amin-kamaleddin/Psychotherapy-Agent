# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician is generally respectful and nonjudgmental. However, repeated corrective medical information and red-flag warnings can feel patronizing and lecture-like.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are competent but remain fairly surface-level. They do not fully capture the client’s ambivalence, pride, desire for autonomy, or mild concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior, including cigars, alcohol, and oral/dental health. The limitation is that this focus does not consistently translate into strategic movement toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician attempts collaboration by asking the client to choose among options. However, the interaction is uneven because recommendations and cautions are often supplied before the client has fully shaped the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is repeatedly acknowledged, and options are offered rather than commands. This is undercut by repeated prescriptive advice and medical warnings, which can reduce the client’s sense of freedom.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is driven more by advice and risk information than by the client’s own motivations. The clinician misses opportunities to develop the client’s own importance, need, desire, or ability statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative language is present, such as inviting the client to choose realistic options. Overall, the stance remains more expert-driven than truly power-sharing, especially when the client indicates he wants to leave.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician makes limited attempts to evoke the client’s own reasons for change. Client statements that could be expanded into change talk are not strategically reflected or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustain talk is prominent, and the clinician acknowledges it without directly reinforcing it. However, the response does not effectively soften or reframe the sustain talk toward ambivalence or change.
