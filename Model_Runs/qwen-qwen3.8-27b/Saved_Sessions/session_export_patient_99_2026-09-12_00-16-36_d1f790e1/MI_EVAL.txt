# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful and nonjudgmental, validating frustration and normalizing why staff asked about alcohol. The tone was somewhat procedural and abrupt at times, which prevented a top rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Matteo’s annoyance and medical context but remained mostly surface-level. Deeper exploration of identity, social normality, or underlying worry would have strengthened empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use, medical relevance, and a practical tracking plan. It became somewhat task-oriented and advice-focused rather than strategically motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and adapted to Matteo’s preference for a low-effort tracking method. Much of the collaboration was uneven because the clinician supplied many scripts, checklists, and options rather than co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was frequently supported through open invitations, options, and acceptance of Matteo’s plan. Autonomy support was slightly weakened when the psychiatrist interpreted checklist answers and introduced specific recommendations before fully eliciting Matteo’s own conclusions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session emphasized information, checklists, and problem-solving more than drawing out Matteo’s own motivations, values, or confidence for change. There were few evocative questions to explore deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were partnership elements, such as asking what would be useful and agreeing to Matteo’s plan. However, the psychiatrist often took an expert problem-solving role, limiting a more equal collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk were limited, with missed opportunities to explore Matteo’s own reasons, values, or confidence around tracking and change. The psychiatrist moved quickly from acknowledgment to practical options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with sustain talk and offered some neutral reframes. However, the response was not highly skillful in softening or redirecting Matteo’s resistant or normative statements.
