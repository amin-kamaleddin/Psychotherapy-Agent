# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly creating a safe, non-judgmental space from the first statement. There is no moment of judgment or implicit pressure — even when Matteo says he's not ready to promise anything, the session simply holds space for that.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening deepens progressively from simple paraphrases to highly sophisticated complex reflections that capture underlying emotional meaning. The most powerful moment captures Matteo's loss of self as 'a part of yourself that got overwritten… a lonely way to put it,' accurately matching his increasing vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a natural arc from safety → exploration → costs → catalyst → evocation of desired self. The direction is strong and purposeful but feels more organic than architecturally planned, leaving a small gap from a perfect score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is entirely co-created in real time, with the psychiatrist following Matteo's lead fluidly and adapting to every new thread he introduces. Every intervention is a question or reflection that invites Matteo to go deeper, with no predetermined agenda or expert positioning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never pushes, negotiates, or implies that change is the only acceptable outcome — Matteo's autonomy is the organizing principle of the entire session. The final exchange, where Matteo says he's not ready to promise anything and the psychiatrist simply holds space, is a model of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation for change emerges entirely from within Matteo through a series of strategic questions that progressively deepen his own awareness. Matteo's final statement — wanting to figure out what Wednesday looks like if it's not the bar — is entirely his own language, elicited skillfully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions themselves as a learner rather than an expert, never lecturing, providing information, or suggesting strategies. The session direction is entirely driven by Matteo's contributions, with collaborative language like 'I'm curious' and 'What do you make of…'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Matteo's change talk through strategic questioning and gentle amplification, but could have more explicitly reinforced and expanded on each instance. A 5 would require more consistent, skillful amplification of every change talk statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged as real and valid without being reinforced as unassailable, and the psychiatrist effectively pivots toward change without arguing. Could be strengthened with more explicit double-sided reflections to more strategically honor both sides.
