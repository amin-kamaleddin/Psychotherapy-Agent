# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Tyler’s stated priorities without moralizing. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with less explicit affirmation of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Tyler’s concerns about control, fairness, and monitoring. However, the empathy often stays at the surface level and does not consistently name deeper emotions such as frustration, embarrassment, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally relevant to substance-related concerns, but the focus is broad and sometimes circular. It would benefit from narrowing toward a specific target behavior or decision.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and allows Tyler’s priorities to shape the conversation. Collaboration is strong, though the therapist still introduces some of the key framing and agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with clear respect for Tyler’s choice, fairness, and right to define what is acceptable. The psychiatrist avoids coercion, promises, and controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions, but they mainly focus on preferences, space, and parental pressure rather than deeper motivation related to substance use. More scaling, values-based, or future-oriented evocation would strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tyler is consistently positioned as the expert on his own life, and his priorities strongly guide the session. The therapist still introduces some agenda items and framing before fully letting the client define them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are attempts to invite Tyler’s own goals and language, but change talk related to substance use or risk reduction is limited. The therapist does not consistently elicit, summarize, or amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and reduces pressure, which helps prevent a power struggle. However, sustain talk is often acknowledged or neutralized rather than strategically redirected toward ambivalence or change.
