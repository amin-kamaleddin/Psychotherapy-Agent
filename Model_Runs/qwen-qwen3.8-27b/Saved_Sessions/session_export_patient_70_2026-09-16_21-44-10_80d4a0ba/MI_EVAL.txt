# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, normalizing difficulty and validating the client's dignity. The score is not a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat behavioral and task-oriented, lacking deeper explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the client's ambivalence and internal conflict. The main limitation is that reflections remain primarily cognitive and behavioral, with limited exploration of deeper emotional experiences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains an excellent focus on the target behavior and concerns directly tied to reducing recidivism and supporting reentry. The session stays centered on a concrete, high-risk situation and progressively narrows broader concerns into an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction strongly reflects two equal partners working together, with reflective, collaborative language and a co-created micro-plan. The client's ideas clearly shape the session direction without the psychiatrist dominating or imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's perception of choice and control by emphasizing ownership, avoiding coercion, and offering low-pressure options. This strong, consistent autonomy support is a key strength of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes motivation from within the client by asking questions that lead the client to articulate his own goals, strategies, and language. The score is 4 rather than 5 because evocation is mainly focused on practical next steps, with less explicit attention to eliciting deeper personal motivation, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within the client, guiding the session with questions that invite the client's own knowledge and judgment. This creates a strong power-sharing dynamic in which the client is treated as the authority on his own life and change process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes change talk by asking open, forward-looking questions that elicit statements of desire, ability, and planning. The score is 4 rather than 5 because it lacks a broader range of change-talk evocation techniques, such as importance/confidence scales or stronger amplification of DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk appropriately without arguing or challenging, acknowledging the pull and redirecting toward client-controlled strategies. The score is 4 rather than 5 because double-sided reflection is not used explicitly enough to hold both sides of ambivalence and gently turn it toward change.
