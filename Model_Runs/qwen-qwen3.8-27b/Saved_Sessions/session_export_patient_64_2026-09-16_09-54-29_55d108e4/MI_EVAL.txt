# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance and normalizes Jordan’s conflict without blame. Acceptance is consistent but somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Jordan’s ambivalence between low personal risk, convenience, and protecting his grandparents. Empathy is solid but remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore deeper emotions or self-perception issues.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior, the flu vaccine, and how to make it feasible. It maintains a clear trajectory from ambivalence to a concrete low-effort plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s preferences and adapts to his grocery-run idea and casual script preference. However, the session remains uneven because the clinician frequently supplies facts, scripts, checklists, and decision rules.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by emphasizing that Jordan can make the decision that fits his values and offers an exit option in the if-then plan. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by frequent advice-giving and the clinician-generated plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with evocative questions and later asks what would make the decision easier. However, the session quickly shifts to information-giving rather than consistently eliciting Jordan’s own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jordan’s input and incorporates his ideas into the plan, showing some partnership. Yet the session becomes quite directive when the clinician provides facts, scripts, checklists, and a specific decision rule.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan expresses protective motivation about his grandparents, and the psychiatrist reflects that value. Change talk is elicited but not consistently deepened, amplified, or elaborated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not aggressively challenge Jordan’s reasons against vaccination and validates concerns such as hassle and sore arm. However, some direct medical explanations function more like persuasion than softening sustain talk.
