# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and acknowledges the patient's perspective without pressuring. However, the warmth remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient's stated concerns and preferences. However, empathy remains surface-level and misses emotional undertones around defensiveness or identity related to mini-cigar use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with a change-focused invitation but quickly shifts to symptom management and dental follow-up. The psychiatrist does not maintain a consistent MI direction toward the smoking-related behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and co-creates a practical plan with the patient. However, the provider remains the primary source of medical information, limiting full collaboration on behavior change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the patient's sense of choice and respects their boundaries. The score is limited by the amount of direct medical advice provided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses an evocative opening question and scaling but does not develop it further. There is minimal effort to elicit the patient's intrinsic motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a partial collaborative stance by asking for preferences and co-creating a practical plan. However, the interaction becomes clinically directive and does not strongly position the patient as the expert on their own values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are rare and weak efforts to cultivate change talk, such as the initial open question. The psychiatrist does not explore or amplify the patient's emerging openness to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or reinforcing sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, the patient's minimization is not actively worked with using MI-consistent techniques such as double-sided reflections.
