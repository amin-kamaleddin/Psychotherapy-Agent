# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Marcus's frustration and need for privacy. However, the acceptance feels formulaic and remains tied to a problem-solving agenda, with the statement about not telling parents being a boundary concern if a parent is present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate but stay at a surface level, mostly addressing irritation and feeling judged. The counselor does not fully explore deeper themes such as autonomy, resentment of surveillance, or the emotional weight of being treated like a child.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with relevant questions about alcohol use, safety, and peer pressure but quickly drifts toward parent-facing scripts and communication management. This loses consistent focus on the target behavior and the client's own ambivalence about drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts collaboration by offering choices, inviting tweaks, and suggesting role-play. However, the content is largely counselor-generated, and Marcus's stated preference against scripts is not fully incorporated as the guiding agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive statements and an avoidance of direct commands, but repeated script options and prescriptive safety advice undermine a fully client-led stance. The counselor does not consistently reinforce that Marcus is the expert in his own life and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocative questions are present but limited, and the counselor does not effectively follow into values, ambivalence, or importance/confidence rulers. The session often shifts to advice and external reasons for change rather than eliciting client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative style by asking Marcus to select or adjust options, but the power balance still tilts toward the counselor's framework. Marcus's repeated rejection of scripts is acknowledged but does not lead to a meaningful shift in direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offers several valuable change talk statements about safe driving, refusal, and leaving parties, but the counselor does not actively amplify them. Missing opportunities to ask amplifying questions, scale confidence, or connect these behaviors to personal values weaken this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids direct argument but does not effectively soften or redirect sustain talk using double-sided reflections or doorway questions. Adding external concerns about legality, safety, and brain development can feel corrective rather than exploratory.
