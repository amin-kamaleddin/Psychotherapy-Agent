# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of the client’s ambivalence, especially around the dog and practical stressors. The confusing “RESPONSE 2/3” message slightly interrupts the warmth and consistency.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is accurate reflective listening and perspective-taking around breathing concerns, dog attachment, and fear of judgment. Some reflections are formulaic, and the process artifact interrupts emotional flow.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally remains focused on asthma self-management and practical barriers such as medication reminders and appointment access. A stronger closing summary could more explicitly connect the chosen steps back to the asthma goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas strongly shape the plan, and the psychiatrist builds on the client’s proposed steps. Some therapist-generated examples and the “RESPONSE 2/3” message prevent a fully clean collaborative dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support is shown by treating the client as the decision-maker and avoiding pressure about the dog or medication plan. The psychiatrist consistently frames the chosen steps as doable on the client’s terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively uses open-ended questions and client language to elicit motivations, barriers, and feasible steps. Adding confidence, importance, or expectation scaling questions would further deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a partner stance by supporting client-led choices and framing the work as joint. The confusing “RESPONSE 2/3” message is a significant process issue if it is an actual utterance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and affirmations help the client generate concrete change talk, including the inhaler reminder, timing, and phone call. The final commitment could be more explicitly consolidated and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about the dog, finances, and prior failed attempts is handled well without arguing or escalating resistance. More sophisticated double-sided reflections could further open space for ambivalence resolution.
