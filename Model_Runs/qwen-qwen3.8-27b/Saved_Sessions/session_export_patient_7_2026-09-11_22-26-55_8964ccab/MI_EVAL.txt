# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepted the client's statements without blame or labeling. The warmth was consistent but somewhat formulaic and repetitive, especially during closure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured the client's expressed tiredness and reluctance, but they tended to stay at a surface level. There was limited exploration of the underlying irritation, ambivalence, or sense of intrusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remained generally contained and safety-focused without becoming aimless. However, it did not clearly connect the client's tiredness to the referral target or establish a specific shared direction for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and used collaborative language, which supported a sense of shared decision-making. Collaboration was limited by the client's minimal engagement and by the counselor's structured, assessment-driven approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently respected the client's right to choose, including the option to end the session and refusing unwanted techniques. A minor limit was offering a specific sleep tip after the client said she did not need tips.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some open-ended questions and explored changes in sleep, appetite, concentration, and motivation. These efforts were reasonable but did not deeply evoke the client's internal values, reasons for change, or personal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited preferences and gave the client authority to decide whether to continue or end the session. The interaction, however, remained partly clinician-directed and did not fully center the client's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was limited effort to draw out the client's own language in favor of change. When the client expressed tiredness and reluctance, the counselor moved toward advice and closure rather than amplifying change-oriented statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with the client's resistance and responded with acceptance and choice. The response pattern became repetitive and did not consistently turn sustain talk toward a change direction.
