# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, validating language and avoids judgment about Jamal’s ambivalence or lateness. It is strong but not a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat procedural and not fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician reflects the morning difficulty, confidence rating, and charger barrier accurately. It falls short of 5 because it does not consistently explore deeper feelings such as loss of control, frustration, or autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on punctuality and concrete morning-routine steps, giving the conversation clear structure. The direction narrows too much around avoiding the mother’s nagging rather than maintaining a broader focus on school arrival and self-management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a plan and includes Jamal’s contributions, such as the watch backup and follow-up text. However, the clinician often leads by supplying multiple specific options before fully inviting the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal is invited to choose steps, rate confidence, and commit to actions, supporting some sense of ownership. Autonomy is undermined by frequent directive advice and rapid solution-giving that makes the plan feel more clinician-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and barrier questions effectively elicit confidence, planned actions, and obstacles. The evocation remains superficial because the psychiatrist moves quickly to advising rather than drawing out deeper, intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and allows Jamal to make some choices, showing a collaborative stance. However, repeated therapist-led questions and multiple supplied strategies make the interview feel less like an equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jamal produces several clear change statements, and the psychiatrist uses scaling and success questions to support them. The score is not 5 because the evocation is often interrupted by premature problem-solving and advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustain talk focuses on escaping his mother’s pressure, and the psychiatrist often validates that frame rather than redirecting it. It remains the weakest criterion because there is little reframing toward intrinsic values, autonomy, or competence.
