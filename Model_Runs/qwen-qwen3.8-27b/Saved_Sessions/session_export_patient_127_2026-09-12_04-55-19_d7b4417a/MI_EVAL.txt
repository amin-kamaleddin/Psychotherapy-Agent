# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, nonjudgmental regard, and affirmation of the client's progress. Acceptance is somewhat professional and could include warmer, more explicit validation of the client's worth beyond performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client's ambivalence, fear, and practical constraints accurately. Deeper emotional content, such as shame, identity threat, and fear of rejection, is not always fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the client's goal of staying straight, building routine, and taking a small employment step. The direction is coherent, though it sometimes moves too quickly into practical planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting input and helping the client choose realistic steps. The process is still somewhat clinician-led, with the psychiatrist frequently generating options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, avoids pressure, and respects the client's limits. Some suggestions are mildly directive and could better emphasize that the client fully owns the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some useful client motivation, including values around Fiona, mornings, and small coping steps. Evocation is not sustained deeply enough, as the session shifts into planning and script-writing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites collaboration and allows the client to shape the plan. The balance of power remains somewhat expert-guided because the psychiatrist proposes many options and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generates some change talk by asking about what helps the client stay abstinent and what small step feels doable. The client's own change language is not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges ambivalence without arguing or dismissing the client's concerns. However, there are missed opportunities to use more nuanced reflections that honor both sides and pivot sustain talk toward change.
