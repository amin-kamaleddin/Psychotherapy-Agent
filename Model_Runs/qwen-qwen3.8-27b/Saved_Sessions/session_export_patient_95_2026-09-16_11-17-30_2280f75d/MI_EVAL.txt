# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong unconditional positive regard and validation without judgment or moralizing. Repeated safety checking after the client asked for silence slightly reduced the feeling of full acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, often sophisticated reflections captured exhaustion, hopelessness, and ambivalent fear of death. Reflections sometimes repeated and did not fully deepen into the client’s metaphors, limiting fluid depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on acute suicide risk and safety, which is appropriate in context. It lacked a clearer MI-consistent progression from risk assessment toward a shared change-focused agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative offers and inclusive language were present. Uneven collaboration emerged from repeated safety management and unilateral emergency-contact statements.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed choice, offered options, apologized, and created a client-controlled check-in word. Recurrent safety statements and monitoring still felt controlling to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some strong evocative questions about what has kept the client from acting and what might help hold on. Evocation of values, coping, and reasons to live was not consistently developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited client preferences and attempted shared presence, but the psychiatrist often drove the conversation through safety questions. The client experienced parts of the session as being managed rather than co-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Noted and reflected the client’s ambivalent part that does not want death to be the end. Change talk was not consistently expanded through values, hopes, or small steps toward change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided direct argumentation and acknowledged hopeless statements, sometimes reframing them toward part of the client seeking relief without death. The conversation remained anchored in risk and means rather than consistently softening sustain talk.
