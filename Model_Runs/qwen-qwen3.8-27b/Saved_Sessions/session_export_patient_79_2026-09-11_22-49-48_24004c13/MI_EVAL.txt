# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and frequently validates Luis's frustration and fatigue. Acceptance is slightly weakened by task-focused directives that can feel more like management than genuine relational support.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Luis's exhaustion and frustration but often remains surface-level and repetitive. Deeper feelings about feeling disrespected, burdened, and controlled by the medical system are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on asthma management, inhaler confusion, and practical follow-through. The focus sometimes becomes narrow and repetitive around the same small tasks, limiting strategic progression.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites preferences, but often supplies specific scripts, options, and instructions. This can feel more like the clinician directing solutions than a true equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Luis's choice is acknowledged, and the psychiatrist asks which option feels easiest. However, repeated introductions of new tasks and scripts can feel pressuring when Luis is clearly resistant to taking on more responsibilities.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to draw out Luis's own motivations, values, or confidence for change. The psychiatrist often shifts to advice, information, and practical options instead of eliciting change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes some collaborative questions and agenda-setting choices. The session nonetheless drifts into an advisory, problem-solving role that reduces Luis's leadership in the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Luis offers spontaneous change talk, such as not wanting to return to the ER, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on it. Missed opportunities leave his motivation underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and generally validates Luis's sustain talk rather than escalating defensiveness. However, responses sometimes stay aligned with his externalized blame, limiting a balanced exploration of both burden and motivation to change.
