# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor shows respect and support through validating statements like 'That makes sense' and 'I hear you.' However, repeated safety checks and quick triage can make the client feel monitored rather than fully accepted as an autonomous adult.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor reflects surface-level overwhelm but misses core ambivalence, fear of failure, and frustration with being given homework or scripts. Repeated safety questions interrupt emotional tracking and deepen disconnection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some focus on the diabetes referral and one small task, but the session drifts into safety checks, generic distress management, and scripting. The target self-management behavior is never concretely explored or connected to a specific client-centered goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor offers collaborative language and options, but the work remains expert-driven rather than truly client-led. The client's repeated refusals of scripts, writing, and choices do not lead to a new jointly created direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive language appears, but the counselor repeatedly asks the client to choose options or adopt scripts despite the client saying choosing feels pressurizing. This undermines the client's sense of freedom and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks an opening question but then supplies strategies, checklists, and exact wording instead of eliciting the client's own values, concerns, and motivations. This is contrary to MI evocation and leaves the client with few self-generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor uses 'we' language and offers options, but the interaction remains directive and solution-focused. The client does not substantially shape the agenda, and repeated offerings of variations maintain an expert-led stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor makes no meaningful effort to evoke the client's desire, ability, need, or reasons for better diabetes management. Instead of amplifying emerging interest, the counselor offers coping options and scripts, and the client produces almost no change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges sustain talk without arguing, but does not effectively soften or redirect it toward client-centered change. The session repeatedly returns to action steps, options, and scripts, keeping focus on overwhelm and inability.
