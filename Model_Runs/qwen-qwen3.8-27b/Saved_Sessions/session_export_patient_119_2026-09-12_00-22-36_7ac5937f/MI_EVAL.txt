# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language, but the rapid shift to practical advice can feel task-focused. The client’s initial nervousness about being criticized is not fully honored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are surface-level reflections of the client’s ambivalence and practical barriers. Deeper emotional cues, such as fear of failure and exhaustion, are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on diabetes medication adherence and moves toward a practical plan. The main limitation is that the focus becomes more task-driven than MI-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts the plan based on client input, such as shifting from coffee to nighttime to the phone. However, the psychiatrist still supplies most options and drives the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some autonomy support through offering choices and avoiding judgment. Repeated prompts to start tonight or try a 7-day test can feel pressuring during moments of hesitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early questions invite some client input, but the psychiatrist quickly shifts to strategies and implementation advice. There is limited evocation of the client’s own values, importance, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently takes on an expert problem-solver role by generating multiple strategies and steering toward a concrete plan. This reduces the sense that the client holds primary expertise and authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently elicit, reflect, or expand on it. The conversation moves quickly to behavior planning rather than strengthening the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not met with arguing, and some normalization is present. However, there is limited active softening through double-sided reflections or exploring the function of resistance.
