# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental stance. However, acceptance feels somewhat procedural and not consistently deep or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately name key feelings such as frustration, urgency, and anger. Later reflections become more superficial and miss opportunities to fully capture Tariq’s emotional complexity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a clear and consistent focus on the immediate target concern of obtaining a phone call. The main limitation is that direction remains narrow and logistical rather than connecting to broader goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tariq’s preferences influence some decisions, and the psychiatrist occasionally invites input. However, the psychiatrist frequently takes over with options, scripts, and repeated instructions, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some autonomy support by inviting choices and acknowledging Tariq’s decisions. However, repeated scripts and directive instructions reduce the client’s sense of ownership and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist focuses more on logistics than on eliciting Tariq’s internal motivations, values, and reasons for change. Important opportunities to explore what the call and family connection mean were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input at times but largely adopts an expert problem-solver role. The interaction is more counselor-led than partnership-led because the psychiatrist drives the plan and wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited effort to build on Tariq’s mild change talk or proactive language. There is little use of scaling, importance, or confidence questions to strengthen change motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge or reinforce sustain talk directly and moves toward practical next steps. However, there is little active use of double-sided reflections to genuinely explore or redirect ambivalence.
