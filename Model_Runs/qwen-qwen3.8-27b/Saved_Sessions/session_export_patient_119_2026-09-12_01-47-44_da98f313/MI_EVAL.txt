# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and validates practical struggles, but the tone often shifts into advice rather than warm, individualized acceptance. There is limited sustained affirmation of Selene's ambivalence, self-doubt, and fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects practical burdens such as chaotic mornings and tired evenings. However, empathy remains mostly cognitive and logistical rather than deeply reflecting Selene's underlying anxiety, guilt, and conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The target behavior—medication adherence—is relatively clear, but the session becomes scattered across many practical options. It lacks a focused MI strategy, such as agenda setting, decisional balance, or a concise summary leading to a coherent change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Selene's ideas, and client-generated cues such as keys, toothbrush, and mirror shape the plan. Collaboration is uneven because the counselor often leads with lists of solutions before fully exploring Selene's own thinking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses open invitations, choices, and confidence ratings, which supports autonomy at a surface level. However, frequent advice, rapid option-giving, and directive dosing guidance create subtle pressure and weaken autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some useful questions are asked, including about barriers and confidence ratings. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Selene's own values, motivations, or self-efficacy, instead frequently moving into advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Selene's input and responds to her suggestions, creating moderate partnership. However, the counselor often assumes an expert problem-solver role by supplying many options before deeply exploring Selene's perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Selene offers meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist only briefly acknowledges it rather than deeply exploring its meaning, importance, or self-efficacy. The session repeatedly shifts into practical advice, so change talk is present but not consistently cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or shaming Selene's sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, minimization is often normalized rather than strategically softened using double-sided reflection, discrepancy work, or gentle pivoting toward change.
