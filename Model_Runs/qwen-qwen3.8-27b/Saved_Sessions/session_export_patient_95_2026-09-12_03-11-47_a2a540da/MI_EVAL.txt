# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and validating, but repeated safety monitoring makes the client feel managed rather than fully accepted. The tone is nonjudgmental, yet the interaction feels conditionally relational and risk-management driven.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures Maria’s ambivalence, pain, and the meaning of the pills and her sister. The psychiatrist consistently tracks emotional shifts with depth and perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the acute crisis, immediate pain, and safety without wandering. The direction is appropriate for a suicide-risk context, though it leans more clinical than MI-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and collaborative language, but the safety agenda often overrides the client’s stated needs. Collaboration feels uneven because the session is not fully co-created around Maria’s priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Verbal affirmations of autonomy are undermined by repetitive, near-identical risk checks that feel controlling. The client explicitly feels treated like a child and not in charge of her own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions effectively draw out Maria’s own reasons for staying alive and ambivalence. The psychiatrist avoids giving advice and allows the client to articulate her own values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but repeated safety questions create a power dynamic that weakens the therapeutic alliance. The client feels the clinician is driving the agenda rather than partnering with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes change talk by exploring what keeps Maria alive and her connection to her sister. However, this change talk is not consistently deepened or used to build confidence in change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or dismissal, but the psychiatrist often shifts back to safety interventions. MI techniques are not consistently used to soften the despair or reframe the “nothing works” narrative.
