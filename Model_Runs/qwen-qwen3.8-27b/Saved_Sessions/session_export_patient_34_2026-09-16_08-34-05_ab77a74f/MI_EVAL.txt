# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses validating statements such as “I hear you” and “that makes sense.” However, acceptance remains somewhat procedural and tied to legal/advocacy goals rather than fully affirming Dylan’s deeper values and emotions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Dylan’s perspective about fairness, housing, and reputation. The empathy is adequate but limited to cognitive/procedural concerns and misses deeper emotional undercurrents such as stress, embarrassment, and fear of being labeled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has clear practical direction toward producing a short statement or email. However, the focus is primarily procedural and legal rather than guiding Dylan toward an MI-relevant next step regarding values, responsibility, or behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well on the task by offering options and following Dylan’s choices in real time. It is less fully collaborative because the psychiatrist remains the expert generating the framework, drafting language, and guiding the process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Dylan is regularly offered choices and his decisions are respected, such as choosing a no-admission option and deciding not to add extra detail. Autonomy is strong, though the psychiatrist’s drafting and directive guidance slightly reduce the degree of Dylan leading the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There are a few initial evocative questions, but the session quickly becomes drafting- and advice-based rather than eliciting Dylan’s own motivation, values, or reasons for change. Missed moments of self-exploration are not followed up, leaving motivation largely external.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates some partnership by inviting Dylan to choose between options and follow his preferences. It remains limited because the psychiatrist continues to act as the expert advisor and does not strongly convey that Dylan is the expert on his own life and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are early attempts to invite Dylan to name values and desired outcomes, but these are not developed. Potential change talk is not reflected, amplified, or expanded, so the session misses opportunities to strengthen Dylan’s own language for responsibility or change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Rather than gently softening Dylan’s minimization and avoidance, the psychiatrist often validates and operationalizes that stance. The session reinforces sustain talk by helping reduce accountability without exploring the consequences or using double-sided reflections.
