# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and asked permission throughout, which reduced shame. However, the acceptance felt somewhat procedural and quickly shifted into education and advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate summary reflections to validate Milan’s perspective and safety. Empathy was mostly cognitive and did not deeply explore underlying emotions, social meaning, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained focused on alcohol use, risks, refusal skills, and role-play. However, the direction was more task-driven than collaboratively guided toward Milan’s personal change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Milan had some input through choosing topics and role-play options. The counselor still provided much of the structure, limiting Milan’s ability to generate his own plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and asked permission, supporting autonomy in several ways. However, directive suggestions such as a drink limit and scripts reduced full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asked some open questions but quickly moved into education and skill-building. There was limited evocation of Milan’s own values, confidence, or motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used permission and options to create a less authoritarian interaction. The expert-driven checklist and refusal lines reduced the co-created, client-centered nature of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was only limited effort to elicit or amplify Milan’s own change talk. The counselor missed opportunities to explore reasons, values, and what Milan might want to preserve.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Milan expressed strong sustain talk and minimization, but the psychiatrist did not effectively soften or redirect it. Advising a hard limit after Milan resisted one may have increased defensiveness.
