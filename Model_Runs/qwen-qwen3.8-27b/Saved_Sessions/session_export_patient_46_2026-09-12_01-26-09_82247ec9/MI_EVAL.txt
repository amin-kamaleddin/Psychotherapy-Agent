# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language but is not consistently warm or affirming. The repeated safety and corrective tone can make the patient feel corrected rather than fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are surface-level and miss several important emotional cues about social awkwardness, fear of exclusion, and shame. Some statements, such as referring to blackouts, also risk minimizing the patient's actual experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use but shifts too often into psychoeducation and harm-reduction advice. It does not consistently guide the conversation toward the patient's own ambivalence, values, and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative invitations, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies the agenda, advice, and plan. The interaction feels more clinician-directed than truly co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and begins with autonomy-supportive language, but the session becomes increasingly prescriptive. The repeated practical plan can feel controlling to a resistant adolescent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few open questions but often overrides them with advice and risk information. The patient's own change talk is not deeply drawn out or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership by asking for preferences and involving the patient in choices. However, the power balance remains uneven because the psychiatrist provides much of the content and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expresses meaningful change talk about hating hangovers and being willing to try small changes. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, reinforce, or expand this language into stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly agree with the patient's sustain talk, but the response is not fully MI-consistent. The reality-check approach may intensify resistance rather than helping the patient reframe the appeal of drinking.
