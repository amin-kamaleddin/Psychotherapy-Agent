# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and supportive, using statements that normalize the client’s struggles. However, acceptance remains somewhat generic and procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some perspective-taking is shown, especially in recognizing the client’s preference for low-friction, practical options. The therapist often moves too quickly from emotional content to practical planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a specific, sustainable morning practice tied to the client’s health values. The main limitation is occasional drift into procedural details and additional behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences meaningfully shape the final plan, but the therapist often leads by offering menus and options. More client-initiated idea generation would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects several client choices, including skipping the alarm and keeping weekends flexible. However, directive language and specific prescriptions make the client’s full ownership less explicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is a useful early question, but the session relies mostly on therapist-generated advice rather than eliciting the client’s own motivations. Strong change talk and values are not fully drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction shows partial partnership, as the client modifies components of the plan. The therapist frequently supplies options and structure, making the process more expert-driven than fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen it. The session shifts quickly into planning before the client’s reasons for change are fully explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist reduces pressure by introducing low-demand fallbacks and a no-guilt stance. However, responses are mostly practical rather than using MI-style reflection or double-sided reflection.
