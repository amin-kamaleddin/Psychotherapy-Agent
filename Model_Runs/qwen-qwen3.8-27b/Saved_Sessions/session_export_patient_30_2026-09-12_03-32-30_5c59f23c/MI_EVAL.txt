# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client’s concerns about medication and autonomy. The stance is solid but slightly procedural, with limited acknowledgment of deeper emotional weight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects ambivalence, preference for control, and discomfort with being monitored. Empathy remains mostly cognitive/behavioral and misses deeper emotional themes such as independence and identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cholesterol management, medication concerns, and concrete next steps. Direction is clear, though it leans more toward behavioral planning than motivationally linking actions to values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options, adjusting to preferences, and incorporating client choices into the plan. The partnership is effective, though the counselor still leads much of the session structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker, with clear statements that the psychiatrist will not push or override choices. Autonomy is strongly respected regarding medication, tracking, follow-up, and testing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful attempts to evoke motivation, such as asking about hopes, confidence, and indicators of success. However, the counselor frequently shifts into advice and problem-solving before deeper client motivation is developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the client by offering structured choices and adapting to what feels least burdensome. The approach is collaborative, though the client is not fully invited to set the agenda or lead the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk around wanting to feel normal, having more energy, and managing risk without medication. These statements are often summarized rather than amplified or strategically reflected.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client’s sustain talk about side effects, medication resistance, and not feeling ill. The sustain talk is accepted but not consistently reframed or redirected toward change.
