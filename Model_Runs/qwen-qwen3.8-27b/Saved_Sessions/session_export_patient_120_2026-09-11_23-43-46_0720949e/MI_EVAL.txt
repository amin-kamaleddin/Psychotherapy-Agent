# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers respect, validation, and normalizing statements, but frequently shifts into advice and problem-solving. This limits deeper acceptance of Sofia’s shame, guilt, and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some reflective and validating responses, but they often remain surface-level. The psychiatrist misses deeper emotional themes such as fear of losing her personality, social belonging, and past drinking shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on Sofia’s alcohol use during weekend social situations and moves toward concrete coping steps. Direction is strong, though the approach becomes more behavior-management than MI-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates Sofia’s choices, such as the code word “Blue,” preferred script, and use of her ring. However, collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist supplies many scripts, plans, and behavioral structures.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia is given choices and there is no overt pressure, which supports some autonomy. The frequent provision of scripts and structured plans can still make the session feel directive rather than fully client-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Sofia’s own reasons, values, fears, and confidence for change. Much of the session relies on counselor-generated scripts and advice rather than evocative questions and reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often takes on an expert role by providing scripts, strategies, and a structured plan. Although Sofia’s preferences are used, the session does not consistently convey that she is the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia offers some change talk and the psychiatrist affirms it while using role-play. However, there are few open-ended questions or reflections that actively expand her desires, abilities, and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia expresses strong sustain talk about friends, fun, and feeling “weird,” and the psychiatrist acknowledges some of this. The response is often practical planning rather than MI-style softening, double-sided reflections, or rolling with resistance.
