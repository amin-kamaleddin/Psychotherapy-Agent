# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without criticizing or invalidating Jamal. Acceptance could be warmer and more individualized by affirming Jamal’s intrinsic value and courage beyond plan compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used clear reflections that captured Jamal’s exhaustion and feeling of being pinned. Empathy could be deepened by exploring underlying themes such as autonomy, self-respect, and identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of getting to school on time with a logical progression from assessment to planning. Direction could be stronger by reducing multi-part questions and including a final value-based summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements, especially in script development where Jamal edited and selected language. Collaboration was limited by the psychiatrist generating many of the options and scripts rather than eliciting Jamal’s own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by asking Jamal to select and adapt plan components. Autonomy support was weakened by frequent therapist-generated options and instructive phrasing, with less emphasis on Jamal’s right to set his own pace and boundaries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used open questions and importance/confidence rulers to elicit some change talk and increase confidence. Evocation was limited by quickly moving to advice and scripts without fully developing Jamal’s own intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and permission, which supported a non-authoritarian stance. However, the session felt more therapist-led problem-solving than equal partnership because the psychiatrist supplied much of the plan content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited change talk by asking about importance, confidence, and preferred changes. Change talk was not consistently amplified or deepened, especially through follow-up questions on ruler scores or intrinsic meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk about maternal nagging without arguing or dismissing it. Sustain talk was not sufficiently redirected toward intrinsic motivation or a broader sense of agency.
