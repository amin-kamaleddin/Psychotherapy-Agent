# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental tone with clear validation helped preserve rapport. At times it was formulaic and task-focused, limiting deeper individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate reflections captured frustration and the desire for respect. The emotional complexity of her ambivalence and independence was not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed connected to alcohol use, safety, and a reduction goal. It often drifted into scripts, problem-solving, and relationship management, reducing strategic focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient’s preferences shaped the session and the psychiatrist adapted to her choices. Much of the content, however, was counselor-provided, creating an uneven collaborative dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support for choice was evident, including tone, plan adjustments, and no obligation to return. Repeated prescriptive scripts and safety steps made the approach somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few early questions, but the session was mostly driven by advice, scripts, and problem-solving. The psychiatrist did not consistently elicit her values, health concerns, or intrinsic motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some partnership through invitations to choose and adapt to her preferences. The psychiatrist often adopted an expert/coaching role, which limited true collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged and affirmed the patient’s change talk about cutting back and setting goals. There were few evocative follow-up questions to deepen or expand that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with minimization and used normalizing, nonjudgmental language. However, she did not strategically redirect sustain talk or use double-sided reflections to strengthen the change side.
