# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating throughout, with strong normalization of Erik's struggles. Warmth is somewhat structured and formulaic rather than deeply personal, preventing a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with sophisticated perspective-taking (e.g., 'thinking Erik vs. tired, in-the-car Erik'). Missed opportunity to deeply reflect Erik's emotionally significant statement about picking up his kid, and two instances of the generic prompt 'I understand. Can you tell me more about that?'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical arc from gym comparison through motivation to a concrete action plan. Transition between gym and diet topics feels abrupt, and the session lacks a clear closing summary or commitment step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing using Erik's own language and co-constructed summaries. The psychiatrist does most of the questioning and framing, with Erik primarily responding, keeping the power balance slightly uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model example of autonomy support — no prescribing, no 'should' language, and consistent framing of choices as Erik's own. Even when Erik raises skepticism, the psychiatrist validates rather than pushes back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik generates most key insights ('stay in my lane,' 'picking up my kid,' 'debt, payout, then guilt') through well-crafted evocative questions. Could probe deeper on values/identity and incorporate scaling questions for confidence talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Erik as the expert on his own life, using his language and crediting him with insights. Pacing is largely therapist-driven, and the agenda is not explicitly negotiated with Erik.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions effectively elicit Erik's own alternative solutions and commitment language. Does not explicitly evoke confidence talk or fully develop ability and need components.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk by validating rather than arguing, gently pivoting toward positive experience. Session ends without fully resolving Erik's final expressed fear of the plan falling apart.
