# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing tone without shaming. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and becomes mixed with rapid problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some surface-level feelings accurately but often moves quickly to advice and medical information. Deeper emotions, such as fear of losing control and pressure around the IUD, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains relevant to alcohol use and contraceptive reliability but is somewhat diffuse. It moves across many options without maintaining a clear, focused change target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor frequently leads the conversation with lists of options, advice, and medical information. This creates an expert-driven stance rather than a true collaborative partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally acknowledges choice and avoids direct pressure, but repeated recommended actions reduce the client’s sense of full ownership. Autonomy support is present but inconsistent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely driven by counselor-supplied reasons, options, and recommendations rather than eliciting the client’s own motivation. There is limited use of open, scaling, or exploratory questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative gestures are present, such as asking what the client would like to discuss and which options feel doable. However, the overall stance remains more expert-driven than equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client generates some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke or expand it. The counselor often shifts to advising and offering options instead of exploring the client’s values and motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without challenging the client, but rarely uses MI-consistent techniques such as double-sided reflections. Responses often add more options rather than redirecting ambivalence toward change.
