# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, with specific, genuine affirmations that honor the client's experience (e.g., reframing persistence as strategic, validating her fear without judgment). The psychiatrist never labels or minimizes, maintaining a consistently warm and respectful tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding empathy operating at multiple levels, with complex reflections that capture emotional texture and meaning rather than just content (e.g., the vivid folding chair reflection, holding two versions of self simultaneously). Each reflection builds on the last with increasing depth and specificity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, strategic progression from validating fear through exploring stress connections to reconnecting with identity, with each step building logically on the previous. The session lacks a brief summary or gentle exploration of next steps to consolidate the powerful work done.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative stance with a consistently equal power dynamic — the psychiatrist asks questions, the client provides content, and the psychiatrist reflects and deepens. Aizada's insights are treated as co-created knowledge rather than confirming a pre-existing hypothesis.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support with explicit permission statements ('You don't have to fix that yet,' 'I'm not asking you to pick up the brush tomorrow') that remove pressure to act. Zero coercion or implied obligation throughout; the client's agency is respected in every interaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation through open questions that allow the client to generate her own insights and language (e.g., 'What would it be like if they had somewhere else to go?'). The psychiatrist never suggests solutions but creates conditions for the client to discover her own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary partnership with the client consistently positioned as the one who holds the knowledge ('I want you to hear yourself'). The psychiatrist explicitly cedes authority and pace to the client while demonstrating genuine curiosity about her inner world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk through strategic questions that elicit the client's own language of capability and hope (e.g., 'I think she's in there'). However, the psychiatrist could have more explicitly elicited desire and commitment language, or circled back to amplify the client's rich statements about painting and being 'fully here.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk by validating the client's biomedical concerns without arguing or dismissing (e.g., 'You deserve to have your fear taken seriously'). The only slight improvement would be more explicit use of double-sided reflections to hold both the need for tests and the emerging insight alongside each other.
