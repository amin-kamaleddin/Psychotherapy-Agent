# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a nonjudgmental tone and used respectful, autonomy-supportive language. However, acceptance was somewhat formulaic and did not fully validate Lucía’s frustration with repeated advice or past failed quit attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered adequate but surface-level reflections and acknowledged stress, fatigue, and preferences. Empathy was limited because responses often remained generic and did not deeply explore the client’s ambivalence, irritation, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear, concrete target behavior: delaying the first cigarette by a small amount of time. The final plan was specific and relevant, though it could have been better connected to the client’s own values and reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influenced the final plan by choosing a 10-minute delay and a low-effort substitute. However, the counselor frequently led with options, steps, and repeated recaps, making the interaction feel more counselor-directed than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor often emphasized choice, reversibility, and lack of judgment. Autonomy support was weakened by repeated reminders and added instructions after the client indicated she was done and felt she was being over-explained.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some initial questions about stress, past quit attempts, and readiness, but the session quickly moved toward suggestions and action planning. The counselor did not consistently evoke the client’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and offered choices, which supported some collaborative intent. The partnership was not fully developed because the counselor often presented options, gave step-by-step directions, and controlled the pace of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered some change talk, but the counselor did not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on it. More strategic follow-up questions would have helped deepen the client’s own reasons, desires, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged the client’s reasons for continuing to smoke without arguing or lecturing. Sustain talk was not skillfully softened, as there was little use of double-sided reflections or redirection toward change.
