# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s ambivalence without moralizing. Acceptance is strong but occasionally remains procedural rather than explicitly affirming the client’s worth beyond her behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture the client’s fear, hope, and ambivalence. A notable empathy lapse occurs when a previous response is repeated after the client raises new social concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on tobacco use and narrows the work into a concrete, achievable ten-minute pause. Direction is clear, though the plan could have been summarized more explicitly at the end to strengthen momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by building the plan around the client’s own ideas, such as holding the pack and using the electric-bill thought. The repeated response to new client content slightly weakens the moment-to-moment collaborative balance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by inviting the client to identify what feels doable and by avoiding pressure or a fixed quit date. Autonomy support is solid but could be stronger by more explicitly affirming all choices, including not acting today.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client’s own motivations related to health, finances, guilt, and self-efficacy. Some questions are slightly leading, and powerful change talk is not always expanded fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the authority on her own life and lets her ideas shape the plan. Partnership is clear, but the clinician still leads the conversation heavily and does not fully negotiate the agenda or pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces change talk about feeling less impossible, gaining proof, and believing she can try again. Occasional advisory suggestions and missed amplification opportunities keep this below an excellent level.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with sustain talk and acknowledges some reasons for maintaining smoking. However, the social-sustain concern about friends and looking “weird” is missed, showing inconsistent strategic softening.
