# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone that conveys basic positive regard. Acceptance remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully affirm Aizada’s ambivalence beyond the biomedical concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and avoids minimizing her symptoms. However, it does not consistently explore deeper emotional meanings such as fear of dying, loss, loneliness, or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on gastrointestinal symptoms, testing, and obtaining a specialist referral. It is not consistently directed toward MI-relevant change processes such as coping, uncertainty tolerance, or emotional processing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration through offering options and inviting input. The interaction remains more expert-driven, with the clinician supplying scripts, checklists, and the medical framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports Aizada’s sense of choice and avoids pressuring her to accept a psychological explanation. Autonomy support is slightly limited by the prescriptive nature of the testing-focused plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the session quickly moves into advice, scripts, and checklists. There is limited exploration of Aizada’s own values, motivations, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and some client input, which supports partnership. However, Aizada mainly selects from clinician-generated options rather than bringing her own expertise and goals into the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited efforts to cultivate change talk, and client statements that could reflect change are not amplified or developed. The focus remains largely on practical problem-solving and testing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk but often reinforces it by providing extensive medical scripts and test checklists. Ambivalence is not sufficiently explored through double-sided reflections or redirection toward values and change.
