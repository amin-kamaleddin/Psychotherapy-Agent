# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing stance that reduces shame. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural and shifts toward advice-giving, limiting fully unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures Mei’s ambivalence and contextual concerns. Empathy is less consistent in depth, focusing more on practical worry than underlying emotions, and the final self-critical statement is not explicitly reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and logical focus on reducing alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability. Direction is slightly diluted by introducing many actions at once and ending without a clear final summary or next-step agreement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, builds on Mei’s ideas, and co-constructs a plan. However, collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist often supplies multiple external options and a detailed structure, shifting the balance toward clinician-led problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices and avoids direct coercion, supporting Mei’s capacity to decide. Autonomy support is weakened by the specificity of the psychiatrist’s suggestions, detailed if-then plan, and text rewrite, which can position the psychiatrist as the primary source of direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance and confidence questions elicit meaningful client-generated concerns and practical plans. Evocation remains moderate because the psychiatrist frequently provides answers instead of deeply exploring Mei’s values, past successes, or self-defined meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses choice language and builds on Mei’s contributions, creating a sense of shared decision-making. Partnership is uneven, as frequent lists of options make the interaction feel more expert-driven than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evokes change talk around avoiding family patterns and developing practical strategies. This is not consistently amplified because the psychiatrist supplies scripts and strategies rather than helping Mei generate and reinforce her own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doubts and sustain talk are normalized rather than challenged. However, repeated confidence checks and focus on risk leave sustain talk more anchored than strategically transformed into change talk.
