# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, validating ambivalence and practical barriers. Acceptance is slightly weakened by occasional directive language that can feel managerial.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and accurately captures the tension between needing a break and protecting her son. Deeper emotional meanings, such as exhaustion and fear of failing as a parent, are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of reducing in-home smoking and making breaks shorter. The plan is clear, concrete, and directly tied to the clinical concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Natalia is invited to choose and her preferences shape parts of the plan. However, the psychiatrist often acts as the solution generator, which reduces fully collaborative co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects some client decisions, such as rejecting gum and tea. Autonomy is undermined by repeated specific suggestions and compliance-focused prompts like making the step inside nonnegotiable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early use of the readiness ruler and questions about ambivalence are appropriate evocative moves. The session then shifts too quickly into planning, limiting further exploration of the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and supports the client’s choices, creating some collaborative momentum. The expert-driven problem-solving stance limits a stronger equal-partner dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Natalia produces meaningful change talk about not wanting to be the reason her son gets sick and wanting an efficient break. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or develop this change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally accepted rather than argued against, which avoids confrontation. The response is often problem-solving rather than using double-sided reflections to explore the tension between coping and valued goals.
