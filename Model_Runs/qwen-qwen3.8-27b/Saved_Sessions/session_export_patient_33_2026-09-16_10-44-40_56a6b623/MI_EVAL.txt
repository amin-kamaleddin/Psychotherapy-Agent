# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and affirming, often validating Diego’s decisions as practical or brave. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not fully embrace Diego’s ambivalence and self-image.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are adequate early on but remain mostly surface-level and task-focused. There are missed chances to deeper reflect fears of infection, shame, and losing function.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on harm-reduction, overdose prevention, naloxone, test strips, and wound care. However, the direction is more practical task completion than progressively clarifying Diego’s own change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego’s preferences shape the plan, including simple cards, discreet access, and privacy. However, the psychiatrist often supplies the content first and then asks Diego to approve it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission asks, choices, and respect for boundaries are frequent, especially around privacy and not forcing abstinence. Directive instruction at times reduces the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early open questions provide some evocation, but the session mostly shifts to psychoeducation and information-giving. Diego’s motivations are not consistently drawn out through open questions or reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some co-creation in choosing cards, scripts, and discreet resources, but the psychiatrist often drives the session with expertise and lists. Asking for city or ZIP after privacy concerns creates a slight expert-driven pressure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Diego offers strong change talk about staying alive, avoiding death, and keeping his arms usable, but the psychiatrist rarely amplifies or explores it. Fewer follow-up questions limit the development of client-driven motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Diego’s reluctance to quit and accepts harm reduction as a legitimate goal. However, there is little active double-sided reflection or strategic redirection of the sustain talk.
