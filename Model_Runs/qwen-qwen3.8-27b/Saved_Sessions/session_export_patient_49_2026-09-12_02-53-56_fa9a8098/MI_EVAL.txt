# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance toward Hana’s ambivalence and past difficulty. Acceptance could be deeper by more explicitly affirming Hana’s worth beyond the smoking behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Hana’s ambivalence, ritual, and practical concerns about breath and control. They could go deeper into the shame, fear of failure, and the unresolved Marcus/one-cigarette concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking and builds a clear, specific after-lunch replacement plan. Some rapid succession of questions made the flow slightly heavy for a minimally verbal client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed around Hana’s preferences for tea, timing, location, and coping phrase. The counselor still supplies options and tips before fully eliciting what Hana thinks might work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission-seeking, choice, and the option to smoke after five minutes strongly preserve Hana’s autonomy. It would improve with more explicit language that the experiment and outcome are fully her decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Hana’s motivations, concerns, confidence, and ideas. Evocation could be stronger by eliciting more of her own solutions before introducing specific coping strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana is invited to choose among options and help construct the plan, showing a collaborative stance. The counselor could more explicitly frame Hana as the expert and allow more client-led sequencing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions about worries, benefits, importance, and confidence lead Hana to express a small intention and increased confidence. Deeper DARN-C-style eliciting could further amplify her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and keeps a low-pressure stance, but does not consistently redirect status-quo reasons toward change. The final sustain talk about Marcus and smoking one cigarette is not effectively addressed.
