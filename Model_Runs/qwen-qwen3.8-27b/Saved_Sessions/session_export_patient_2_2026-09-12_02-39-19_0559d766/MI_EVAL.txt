# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client's boundary around alcohol and realistic goal. Warmth was generally supportive but sometimes formulaic as the session became more task-oriented.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured practical concerns, ambivalence, and confidence changes well. Some deeper emotional themes, such as fear for her son and caregiving stress, were not explored as fully as possible.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing evening smoking and moved efficiently from problem identification to planning. Questions and reflections repeatedly tied back to the target behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and adapted to the client's preference for a low-effort, short-term plan. Collaboration was strong but occasionally guided by the counselor's structured list and pre-shaped plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respected the client's choices, avoided pressuring her to quit, and supported her right to make a small, self-selected plan. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit statements of client ownership and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions, importance/confidence rulers, and barrier questions helped elicit motivation and increase confidence. Evocation was somewhat procedural and did not deeply explore personal reasons for change or scaling follow-ups.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choices shaped the session focus, target, strategy, and practical supports. The counselor remained collaborative overall, though parts of the planning were more counselor-driven than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expressed desire, ability, and reasons for change, and confidence increased during the session. The psychiatrist affirmed some change talk but missed opportunities to expand, amplify, and consolidate it before moving into advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not challenge the client's minimization or preference for reduction over quitting, which is appropriate. However, there was limited use of strategic redirection or double-sided reflection to gently soften sustain talk.
