# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental while affirming the patient's desire for accurate information. It stops short of a 5 because the tone is somewhat procedural and lacks deeper individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's practical concerns and need for safety and confidence. It does not reach a 5 because empathy remains mostly content-focused rather than exploring deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on clarifying citalopram use, side effects, and the phenelzine interaction for safe decision-making. It lacks a 5 because the shared MI target behavior is not consistently named or summarized.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint problem-solving language and invites the patient to shape the plan. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still controls the agenda and pacing, limiting fully visible co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressuring and invites the patient to generate choices and decisions. It would benefit from more explicit autonomy language, especially about the patient's right to decide whether to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively draw out the patient's own preferences, plans, and practical safety reasoning. It does not reach a 5 because evocation is more behavior-focused than values- or motivation-focused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as an active participant and invites input on priorities and organization. It does not reach a 5 because the shared agenda-setting and power-sharing language are not explicit enough.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are attempts to elicit change talk around safety, confidence, and next steps. It is limited because the psychiatrist does not consistently use DARN-C, scaling, or amplification of the patient's own motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns and uncertainty are handled well by reframing them as careful self-advocacy and directing attention to actionable steps. It stops short of a 5 because more advanced ambivalence techniques, such as double-sided reflections, are not used.
