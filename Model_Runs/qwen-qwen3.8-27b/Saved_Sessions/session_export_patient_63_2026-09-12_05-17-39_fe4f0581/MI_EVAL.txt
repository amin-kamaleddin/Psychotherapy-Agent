# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating without moralizing or shaming. Acceptance was occasionally formulaic and did not consistently deepen into stronger affirmation of Jordan’s strengths or dignity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Jordan’s ambivalence and practical concerns, but often stayed at a surface level. Deeper emotions such as shame, fear of being seen as “over it,” and powerlessness were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation remained focused on the presenting issue of shoplifting in the context of peer pressure, temptation, and job-related risk. The direction was tactic-focused rather than strategically focused on motivation, values, or a clear next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited Jordan to choose among options, but supplied most of the content and strategies. Jordan often responded to the psychiatrist’s suggestions rather than co-creating the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently acknowledged Jordan’s choice and invited input, supporting autonomy. However, repeated directive suggestions and scripted options created subtle pressure to select and implement a strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions helped elicit Jordan’s goals, triggers, and past resistance. After this initial exploration, the psychiatrist shifted toward providing scripts and tactics rather than continuing to evoke Jordan’s internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and expressed willingness to follow Jordan’s lead, but the session quickly became counselor-driven. Jordan’s perspective was acknowledged, yet the psychiatrist’s suggestions largely shaped the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few attempts to elicit change talk, especially around protecting Jordan’s job and keeping his record clean. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, reflect, or build on Jordan’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk but often responded with coping strategies rather than reflective pivots. Stronger MI responses would have gently shifted attention from temptation and avoidance toward Jordan’s values and reasons for change.
