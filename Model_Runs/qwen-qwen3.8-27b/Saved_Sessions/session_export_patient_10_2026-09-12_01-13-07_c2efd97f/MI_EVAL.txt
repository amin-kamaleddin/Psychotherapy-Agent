# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Amina’s frustration and capability. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic and could be deeper, especially around her role as a single mother and repeated disappointments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflections of frustration and practical strain, but they remain surface-level. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as disappointment, exhaustion, fear of burdening family, and conflicting needs.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target concern of securing employment while arranging reliable childcare. There is little drift, and the conversation consistently moves toward practical next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina contributes ideas that shape the plan, such as the Wednesday block and contacting the neighbor. However, the psychiatrist frequently leads by offering many practical options, making the interaction feel more consultative than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking Amina what feels doable and respecting her preference to send messages herself. The score is limited because the counselor still frequently prescribes specific steps, reducing Amina’s full ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open and evocative questions, but motivation is more supplied through practical suggestions than elicited from Amina. There are missed opportunities to explore her values, confidence, hopes, and reasons employment matters to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Amina’s input and builds on her ideas, showing a real but uneven partnership. The overall stance remains relatively expert-driven, with the counselor generating much of the practical agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina expresses several intentions and abilities, and the psychiatrist affirms these statements. However, change talk is not consistently deepened using MI-specific strategies such as exploring confidence, values, or what reliable childcare would allow her to do.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Amina’s barriers without arguing or dismissing them. Softening is mostly achieved through practical redirection rather than more advanced MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or decisional balance.
