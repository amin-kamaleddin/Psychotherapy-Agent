# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, using statements like “that makes sense” and “I hear you.” However, repeatedly pushing for a plan after the client resists commitment subtly makes acceptance feel conditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers useful but mostly surface-level reflections of Jonathan’s situation. Emotional themes such as shame, fear of failure, and fatigue are not explored deeply enough.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on diabetes self-management and blood glucose checking. The direction becomes overly task-focused and prescriptive, narrowing too quickly to logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration, with some input from Jonathan on preferred options. The counselor remains the primary generator of solutions, making the interaction uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and uses nonjudgmental language, but repeatedly encourages trying or committing despite Jonathan’s resistance. Stronger respect for his right to wait would improve autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some initial input about barriers and past successes. However, evocation becomes superficial and shifts toward behavioral planning rather than exploring Jonathan’s deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks a few partnership-oriented questions but quickly assumes an expert problem-solving role. Jonathan is not consistently positioned as the primary expert on his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor makes limited efforts to elicit change talk and does not develop or amplify it effectively. Jonathan’s change talk remains thin because the focus quickly shifts to compliance and logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and acknowledges Jonathan’s burden, especially around guilt. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened or redirected toward ambivalence, values, or possible benefits of change.
