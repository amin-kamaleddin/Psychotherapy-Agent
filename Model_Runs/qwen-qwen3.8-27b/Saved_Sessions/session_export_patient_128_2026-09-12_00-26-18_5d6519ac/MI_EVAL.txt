# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a generally respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers supportive statements, but the warmth is somewhat procedural. The session becomes practical and directive, and the client's ambivalence is not fully normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects surface distress and stress effectively but often stays at a cognitive level. He misses deeper ambivalence about peer belonging, reputation, and fear of the record.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear practical focus on conduct consequences, which connects to the alcohol behavior. However, the direction becomes administrative rather than MI-focused on drinking patterns, values, and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements such as options and role-play, but the psychiatrist supplies lists, scripts, and assumes the officer role. The interaction feels more like preparation than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks preferences and avoids forcing treatment, but also provides many directive instructions and scripts. These helpful steps can reduce the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist starts with open questions but quickly shifts to advice, steps, and scripts. He does not consistently draw out the client's values, discrepancies, or intrinsic motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices and follows the client's preference to address conduct, but the session is largely expert-driven. The psychiatrist directs steps and scripts, limiting genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client makes some change talk about caring for studies and responsibility, but the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or expand it. He supplies scripted language rather than eliciting the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not endorse the drinking, but he does not skillfully soften minimization such as 'just college' or 'lost track.' Redirection to change and double-sided reflections are limited.
