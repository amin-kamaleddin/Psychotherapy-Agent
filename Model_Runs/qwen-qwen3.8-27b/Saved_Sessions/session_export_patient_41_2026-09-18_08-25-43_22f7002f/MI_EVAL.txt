# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, with normalization and validation consistently present. Particularly strong in reframing what Elira sees as a character flaw ('laziness') as a protective strategy rather than a deficiency.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and consistent empathic reflections that go beyond surface-level paraphrase to capture the experiential and emotional core of Elira's experience. Skillfully reflects the sensory and automatic nature of avoidance, as well as the identity-level pain of feeling disconnected from her 'showing up' self.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the target behavior (avoidance/follow-through) with logical, progressive movement from identification through exploration to evocation. Could be slightly more strategic in connecting insights to a concrete next step or behavioral exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of collaborative interaction with genuine power balance—Elira's insights (momentum, the quiet inner voice, identity fusion with avoidance) drive the session direction. Consistent use of inclusive language and client-led pacing throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Elira's ownership and right to choose, never pressures or imposes a timeline. Explicitly affirms her right to be uncertain ('You're not just settling, you're evaluating') without rushing her toward commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Elira rather than providing external reasons for change, producing genuine client-driven insights. The final question externalizing and personifying her change motivation is particularly skillful.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within Elira, never providing unsolicited advice or telling her what to do. Language throughout is collaborative ('I'm curious,' 'I wonder'), and session direction is clearly shaped by Elira's responses.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk through strategic questions, resulting in meaningful client-generated insights (momentum, 'it's not nothing,' the quiet voice). Could have more explicitly amplified and developed individual pieces of change talk, particularly the momentum insight, throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully with double-sided reflections that honor the protective function of avoidance without reinforcing it. The identity-related sustain talk ('I'm used to being the one who's bad at things') could have been explored more fully as a double-sided reflection rather than primarily validated and pivoted.
