# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently models nonjudgmental respect, even when the client resists change. The clinician affirms the client’s perspective without blame, shaming, or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects the client’s values around weekends, friendship, and not wanting to stand out. However, the empathy remains more cognitive than emotionally deep, with limited exploration of underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on alcohol use and moves toward a concrete, low-effort experiment. Direction is clear, though it could be sharper by reducing option-listing and repeated summaries.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as an equal partner and uses the client’s input to shape the plan. The final intervention is co-constructed and based on the client’s preferred low-key approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Noncontrolling language is used consistently, and the client’s choice is explicitly respected. The plan remains optional and does not pressure the client to continue or report back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits client-generated ideas, such as ordering water with the first round. However, the client’s own motivations could have been evoked more deeply through additional reflective questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strong collaborative stance in which the client is the expert on his own life. The session follows the client’s lead and avoids imposing a preferred course of action.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician helps create and reinforce modest change talk around a small trial step. However, the change talk is not fully developed, with limited deeper elicitation of desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully normalizes resistance and avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk. The clinician redirects toward low-effort options while preserving the client’s values and autonomy.
