# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect and nonjudgment, normalizing ambivalence and the client’s decision not to quit. However, some emotional undertones, such as frustration about the session feeling like a chore, were not fully validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture the client’s surface-level conflict between enjoying smoking and worrying about her children. Deeper feelings, such as resentment, guilt, or concern about being a role model, are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on smoking and reducing children’s exposure. The direction often shifts toward practical behavior management rather than deeper MI-focused motivational work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative questions, but the interaction is largely clinician-led. The psychiatrist supplies most of the options rather than building primarily from the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician frequently affirms choice and frames the plan as an experiment rather than a requirement. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated advice and a strong emphasis on trying specific strategies tonight.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few open-ended questions are used, but there is limited follow-up on the client’s motivations or values. The session relies more on problem-solving than on evoking the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves more like an expert advisor than an equal partner. The client’s input does not strongly shape the session, which feels driven by the clinician’s suggested options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses some change-relevant ideas, but the psychiatrist does not consistently build on them. There is little strategic effort to elicit stronger desire, ability, or commitment for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the client’s reasons for smoking without arguing or reinforcing them negatively. However, sustain talk is not consistently redirected or explored in a way that opens space for change.
