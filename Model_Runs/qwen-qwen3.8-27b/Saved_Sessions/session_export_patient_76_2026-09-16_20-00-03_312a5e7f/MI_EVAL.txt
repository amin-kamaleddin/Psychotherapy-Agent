# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and affirmation of Liora’s experience and effort. Warmth is strong but somewhat structured, and could be deeper with more explicit individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening to capture ambivalence, somatic distress, and the shift from psychological noise to chest pain. Reflections are accurate but occasionally repetitive and do not always reach deeper feelings such as loneliness, shame, or hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the immediate target concern, narrowing from broader safety planning to a manageable next step. Direction is generally clear but sometimes circular and does not consolidate into a concrete, change-focused plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, uses “we/together” language, and adapts when Liora prioritizes being heard. It is not fully reciprocal because the therapist still leads the structure and offers options that frame the work as therapist-guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice and self-direction by avoiding advice-giving, pressure, and imposed safety plans. Autonomy support could be made even more explicit during high distress by affirming Liora’s right and capacity to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions to invite Liora to identify what feels safe, small, or doable. Evocation remains limited in depth, not strongly accessing Liora’s values, hopes, confidence, or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly locates expertise in Liora and invites her to choose among options rather than imposing an agenda. Partnership is strong but not fully co-created, as the therapist still controls much of the session structure and decision frames.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, such as Liora’s tiredness and desire not to keep cutting, through questions that activate change-relevant thinking. However, this change talk is not consistently deepened, amplified, or developed into stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and uses double-sided reflections to validate both the function of cutting and Liora’s ambivalence. Softening is present but not consistently effective, as sustain talk remains prominent and is not strategically reframed to create space for change.
