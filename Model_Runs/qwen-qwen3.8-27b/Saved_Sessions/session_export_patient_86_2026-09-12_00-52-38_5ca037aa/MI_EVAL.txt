# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and tolerant of resistance, using statements like “I hear you” and “you’re running this.” However, the warmth was practical and task-focused rather than deeply affirming of the patient’s long-standing smoking history and prior setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections were mostly surface-level and practical, with limited exploration of the patient’s ambivalence, fatigue, autonomy concerns, or emotional experience. There was little use of complex or double-sided reflection to help the patient feel understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking cessation and related oral health outcomes, quickly narrowing tangential topics. It ended with a concrete next step, the 3-day patch trial and Friday follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited some input and adapted to the patient’s preferences for brevity and simplicity. However, the interaction remained largely clinician-led, with limited co-created problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by avoiding pressure, offering a low-effort trial, and emphasizing the patient’s choice. Autonomy support could be strengthened by reducing directive advice and more explicitly validating the patient’s right to choose among options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some client-driven change by suggesting a small experiment and allowing the patient to decide. Evocation would be stronger with more open-ended questions about values, reasons for change, past experiences, and confidence in trying a small step.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the patient’s control and invited input, supporting a collaborative stance. Nevertheless, the session was largely directive, with many plans and recommendations rather than mutually co-created approaches.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful change talk was elicited, such as the patient agreeing to try the patch for a few days and possibly smoking less. These statements were not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened through follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not challenged and was met with practical redirection. More effective softening would involve acknowledging the pull of the status quo while gently exploring underlying concerns about autonomy, past failure, or confidence.
