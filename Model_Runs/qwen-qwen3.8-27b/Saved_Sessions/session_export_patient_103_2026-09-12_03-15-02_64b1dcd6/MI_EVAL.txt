# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally calm and validating with phrases like "I hear you," but is weakened by repeated condescending reframing (e.g., "a puppy," "fast control moves") even after the client objects to being managed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides some accurate reflections of the client's frustration and fear for his job, but stays mostly surface-level and repetitive, using reductive language like "vent" and "spiraling." The underlying meaning of invisibility and professional identity threat is never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Remains focused on the immediate appointment problem without wandering, but does not clearly establish or guide toward a behavioral/coping change target. The conversation repeatedly drifts into external problem-solving rather than MI-focused direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes some collaborative checking-in and preference questions, but the psychiatrist repeatedly positions itself as the problem-solver, offering drafts and intervention menus. The client's own expertise and stated preferences are not fully integrated into shaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently frames responses as choices and explicitly invites the client to select among options. Score reduced because the choices are often narrow/prescriptive and some phrasing ("forces faster service," "managed action") subtly undermines autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Shows limited evocative intent in questions but quickly follows up with concrete scripts and action suggestions rather than deeper exploration. The client's values, past coping, and personal reasons for wanting a different outcome are not drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language and asks for preferences, but becomes increasingly counselor-driven with repetitive menus of interventions. The client explicitly states the suggestions feel like being managed, yet the psychiatrist continues to offer "one quick, firm thing."

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Includes a couple of evocative questions but does not follow up to deepen, amplify, or summarize any change-oriented statements from the client. No clear development of desire, ability, need, or commitment language occurs.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mirrors the client's sustain talk without redirecting it toward ambivalence or client-controlled change (e.g., "You're right — this isn't about polishing words"). Frequently shifts into practical problem-solving instead of using double-sided reflections or gentle reframing.
