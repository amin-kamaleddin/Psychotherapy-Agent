# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with supportive reflections, though acceptance remains somewhat formulaic and does not always deeply validate Amina’s underlying fear, exclusion, or distress. Mild action-oriented language slightly tempers unconditional, pressure-free regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures Amina’s ambivalence, comfort, and ambivalent relationship with the phone. Empathy could be deeper by exploring emotional undertones such as shame, anxiety, fear of missing out, or autopilot distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on evening phone use, sleep, and class, progressively narrowing to concrete behavioral strategies. Some circularity around the 10 PM urge and lack of periodic summaries prevent consolidation of direction and broader goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is one of the strongest areas: Amina’s ideas drive the session, and the psychiatrist builds on her suggestions rather than imposing an external plan. The interaction feels like genuine co-creation rather than a clinician-led advice session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions and frames options as flexible experiments rather than commands, supporting Amina’s sense of choice. However, some wording implies a trial will occur, and the counselor does not consistently make explicit that Amina is fully in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits motivation and solutions from Amina rather than offering advice or reasons for change. Evocation remains somewhat narrow, focusing more on behavioral problem-solving than on values, discrepancy, or deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Amina as the expert on her own habits and invites her to shape the plan. Partnership is not fully power-shared because the counselor still drives the question sequence and occasionally offers narrow menus of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes client-generated change talk and reinforces Amina’s concrete plans, such as the 9 PM stop and five-minute DM timer. The main limitation is that the counselor does not consistently deepen change talk by exploring values, confidence, importance, or likely outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Amina’s sustain talk and uses some balanced reflections. However, the counselor does not consistently use double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or reframing to strategically soften doubts and barriers.
