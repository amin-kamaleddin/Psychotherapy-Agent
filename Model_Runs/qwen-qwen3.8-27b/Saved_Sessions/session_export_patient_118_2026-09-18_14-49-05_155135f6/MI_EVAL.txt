# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with clear affirmations of the patient's right to choose. The warmth is somewhat measured and procedural rather than deeply personal, lacking explicit statements of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the patient's frustration and emotional state. Misses an opportunity to reflect back the significant 'glaring flaw' self-image pain at the session's end.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear strategic trajectory and does not consolidate the patient's movement toward ambivalence about quitting. Ends abruptly without a summary or next steps, leaving the therapeutic work incomplete.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with genuine acknowledgment of the power dynamic and active seeking of the patient's input. Could be elevated by more consistently co-constructing the agenda rather than the psychiatrist holding most of the conversational initiative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout, consistently positioning the patient as the decision-maker without prescribing, advising, or pressuring. Explicitly respects the patient's timeline and reframes resistance without undermining choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some effort to evoke the patient's own motivations but is inconsistent and often superficial. Fails to use MI-specific techniques like scaling questions or importance rulers to deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with strong acknowledgment of the power imbalance and frequent invitations for the patient's perspective. Could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language and fully following the client's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient offers some change-relevant language but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or deepen these moments. Lacks systematic use of MI-specific techniques to further elicit and expand change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by affirming the patient's autonomy and validating concerns without minimizing them. Uses effective double-sided reflections to acknowledge concerns while opening a pathway forward.
