# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Marek’s ambivalence without challenging his identity concerns. Acceptance was strong but not exceptional because direct affirmation of the client’s worth/courage was limited and the late follow-up push was not immediately repaired.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Marek’s mixed feelings about social drinking, health concerns, and wanting to keep the experience casual. Empathy remained largely cognitive/practical rather than fully exploring deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or identity conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on Marek’s weekend drinking and its impact on Monday functioning, moving logically from meaning of the screening result to a concrete, low-pressure trial. The direction was well-sequenced and appropriate to his contemplative stage.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Marek as an equal partner by building on his own ideas and considering the social context of his drinking. Collaboration was strong, but the therapist still introduced key agenda items such as the trial, cue, and follow-up.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforced that Marek was the decision-maker and avoided pushing tracking or promises. The final check-in question was experienced as pressuring and weakened autonomy support without an immediate explicit repair.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully drew out Marek’s own practical change talk, internal cues, and sense of ability. Evocation was less complete because it did not fully explore deeper values, identity, or long-term personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and positioned Marek as the expert on his own life and preferences. Partnership was good but not fully power-sharing because the therapist still led the session structure and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited Marek’s own reasons, plans, and confidence through open questions and affirming reflections. Change talk was frequent and practical but not fully deepened into stronger values, identity, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided arguing with Marek’s resistance and validated both the value of social connection and emerging health concerns. The late push for follow-up intensified sustain talk around autonomy and being monitored, and was not fully softened.
