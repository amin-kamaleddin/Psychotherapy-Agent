# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout. Explicitly protects the client from self-criticism (e.g., pushing back on 'pathetic') without being heavy-handed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface paraphrasing to capture underlying beliefs and emotional meaning (e.g., 'like you have to struggle with it to prove you care'). Accurately names the client's paradox of caring while feeling stuck.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on medication adherence while following the client's lead in approach. Each turn moves the session purposefully from ambivalence toward commitment without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal power dynamic; all key decisions (coffee strategy, one-week timeframe, definition of success) emerge from the client's contributions. The psychiatrist facilitates co-construction rather than directing outcomes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never prescribes, imposes a timeline, or uses directive language like 'you should' or 'you need to.' The client's commitment emerges entirely from within, facilitated but not directed by the psychiatrist.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within the client through evocative questions and amplification of the client's own words. Change talk escalates naturally from general ('I do want to be healthy') to specific commitment ('take it with my coffee in the morning').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly attributes solutions to the client ('That's you, not me, figuring something out'). The psychiatrist serves as a mirror and evoker rather than an expert with answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully names, reinforces, and amplifies the client's change talk at key moments (e.g., 'that's you giving yourself permission to be human'). Creates conditions for change talk to escalate naturally across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses masterful double-sided reflections that honor the client's protective concerns while redirecting toward change (e.g., 'there's a part of you that's protective... but I also hear you're willing to test it'). Never dismisses, argues with, or minimizes concerns.
