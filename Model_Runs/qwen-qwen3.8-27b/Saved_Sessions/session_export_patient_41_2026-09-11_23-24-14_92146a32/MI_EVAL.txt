# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes the client's anxiety. It is strong but sometimes brief and procedural rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist reflects some frustration and fear, but often moves quickly to problem-solving. Deeper reflection of shame, self-judgment, or fear of being seen as lazy would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the avoidance behavior and the specific email task. The focus could be strengthened by more consistently linking the task to broader values, confidence, or self-efficacy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative phrases, but the therapist frequently takes on the organizer/problem-solver role. More open invitations to build the plan from the client's ideas would improve collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist repeatedly emphasizes choice, safety, and permission to stop. Some suggestions remain fairly directive, such as pasting into notes or using a timed send plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist asks some evocative questions, such as evidence and what she would tell a friend. The session relies more on therapist-provided solutions than on eliciting the client's internal motivation, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites preferences and offers choices, but often supplies the structure, scripts, and options. The client is more often selecting from therapist-generated options than driving the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates some change-related ideas, such as drafting or copying the email, but the therapist does not consistently amplify or expand this talk. More eliciting of values, confidence, or future self would strengthen the response.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing or minimizing and offers low-demand, permission-based next steps. It would be stronger if more active double-sided reflections were used to pivot hesitation into change talk.
