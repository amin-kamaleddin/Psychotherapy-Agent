# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers supportive statements, but acceptance often remains procedural and tied to the problem-solving plan. There is warmth, yet limited unconditional affirmation of Yasmin’s experience and worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some perspective-taking reflections show understanding of Yasmin’s frustration and ambivalence. However, the reflections are often brief or interpretive and miss emotionally rich moments, such as fear of losing control and the meaning of weight loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete behavioral target: reducing tracking burden through a structured daily check-in. The direction is clear and avoids aimless drift, though it becomes narrow and does not fully integrate broader concerns about body image, fear of failure, and meaning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Yasmin contributes meaningfully to the plan by choosing timing, fields, limits, and a compassionate phrase. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, including coping strategies, rescue rules, and scripts, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers several choices about focus, experiment details, timing, and limits, which supports Yasmin’s sense of control. Autonomy support is limited, however, by the counselor’s structured plan and frequent expert role in prescribing options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the psychiatrist frequently provides explanations, strategies, and scripts rather than eliciting Yasmin’s own motivations. Key opportunities to explore values, hopes, and the meaning of sustainable health are missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language such as “we can” and “co-create” is used, and Yasmin has input into plan details. Partnership is limited by the psychiatrist’s frequent expert stance in diagnosing, listing drivers, and prescribing interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Yasmin produces some change talk, such as willingness to try the experiment, but it is mostly client-initiated. The psychiatrist does not systematically evoke or amplify change talk related to values, importance, confidence, or past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not reinforce sustain talk and briefly normalizes ambivalence, but much of it is bypassed or moved into problem-solving. A major missed opportunity is the unaddressed fear that weight loss is the “whole point” and progress feels like a “consolation prize.”
