# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses validating and nonjudgmental statements, but the procedural tone and repeated directives reduce the sense of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are early empathic reflections, but the clinician does not consistently deepen responses to Kenji’s anxiety, self-doubt, and feeling overwhelmed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing alcohol use and reaches a concrete goal, though the direction is occasionally diluted by repeated safety warnings and task detail.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and input, but much of the planning is clinician-led and does not fully co-create a simpler alternative when Kenji expresses burden.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kenji is allowed to choose a goal and pace, but phrases such as “Pick one now” and “I’ll expect your check-in” create a more controlling structure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient is helped to identify a small change, but deeper motivations, values, and confidence are not sufficiently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction has collaborative structure, but the psychiatrist carries much of the momentum through detailed plans, scripts, and expectations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kenji offers change talk and receives positive affirmation, but the clinician does not consistently expand it into stronger reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk, but often responds by adding more structure instead of reflecting and gently exploring the ambivalence.
