# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Eric’s perspective on smoking as a coping method. Acceptance was strong but sometimes formulaic and not fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated accurate reflective listening, including recognizing ambivalence and the link between smoking and work stress. Empathy remained somewhat cognitive and did not fully explore Eric’s emotional experience of pressure, identity, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking and health concerns, but direction lacked strategic momentum. The psychiatrist repeatedly returned to offering options rather than following Eric’s precontemplation and exploring deeper values or ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some collaboration because the psychiatrist asked Eric to choose among options and respected his preferences. However, the psychiatrist still drove much of the agenda, and Eric’s contributions mainly narrowed or rejected clinician-generated choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported Eric’s autonomy by avoiding coercion, respecting his readiness level, and explicitly stating that Eric was in charge. The repeated offering of low-pressure options still subtly kept the clinician’s agenda visible.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evocative strategies, such as ruler questions and inquiry about past quit attempts. Follow-up was limited, so Eric’s change talk and self-generated reasons were not deeply developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Eric’s participation and respected his refusal to engage in tools or worksheets. Partnership was constrained because Eric was not fully positioned as the expert on his own life, work stress, or reasons for smoking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Eric offered some change talk, including health symptoms and a future intention to see his doctor if breathing worsened. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, expand, or evoke this change talk, instead shifting toward external options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Eric’s sustain talk and used some double-sided reflection. However, the session did not consistently soften sustain talk or use it as a doorway for further self-exploration.
