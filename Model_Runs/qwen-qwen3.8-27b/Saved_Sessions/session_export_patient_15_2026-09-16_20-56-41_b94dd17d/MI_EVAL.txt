# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and normalizes the client’s ambivalence about the blood pressure readings. The warmth is somewhat procedural and less deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses complex reflections to capture ambivalence and the balance between routine and health readings. It does not fully explore the emotional meaning or values connected to the beer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on hypertension and evening drinking while moving toward a concrete, testable plan. Momentum is good but slightly diluted by tracking mechanics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites the client to co-create options such as wine, a weekly check, and focusing on morning feeling. The psychiatrist still provides much of the structure and scaffolding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly emphasizes choice, ownership, and the right not to change, including explicit low-pressure language. Some prepackaged options and a testing frame slightly reduce full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses open-ended questions to draw out the client’s own ideas rather than lecturing about reasons to change. The evocation remains more practical than values- or confidence-based.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the client as the expert on his daily life and repeatedly invites his judgment. The psychiatrist still structures the session and offers specific options, so collaboration is not fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits meaningful action statements such as skipping the second weekend beer and using a weekly check. It does not consistently affirm, amplify, or deepen the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with the client’s doubts and normalizes the tension between routine and readings. It misses opportunities to redirect sustain talk through double-sided reflections or values questions.
