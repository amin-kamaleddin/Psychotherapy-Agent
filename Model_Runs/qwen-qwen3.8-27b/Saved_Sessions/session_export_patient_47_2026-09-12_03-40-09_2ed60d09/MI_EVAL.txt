# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing with the client. Acceptance is slightly limited by procedural language and fewer individualized affirmations of her courage and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening effectively captures ambivalence and some underlying concerns. However, empathy becomes less nuanced when missing deeper feelings about identity, fear of being labeled, and social irritation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and builds a clear, concrete plan with follow-up. Direction could be stronger with more summarization and deeper motivational exploration before planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Evelyn’s preferences and incorporates them into the plan, supporting shared decision-making. However, frequent clinician-generated options make the interaction somewhat expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices, asks permission, and respects Evelyn’s boundary about keeping Tuesday. Autonomy support is slightly limited by subtle steering through clinician-suggested options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions elicit change talk, but the session shifts quickly into strategies before deeper internal motivation emerges. Values, health concerns, and family-related motivations are not consistently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting Evelyn’s input and allowing her ideas to shape the plan. Partnership is reduced somewhat by the frequent introduction of counselor-generated options before eliciting client-generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits practical change statements and uses the confidence ruler effectively. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand Evelyn’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and reframes sustain talk in a non-punitive way while honoring Evelyn’s limits. Some opportunities are missed to more deeply reflect underlying tension, such as using wine to relax or social pressure to conform.
