# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, validating David's choices without judgment and never expressing disappointment. When David sets a firm boundary about not being lectured, the psychiatrist responds with respect rather than pushback.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates highly accurate reflective listening, consistently picking up on David's emotional undertones and specific words rather than offering generic reflections. Reflections are tailored to David's individual situation, personality, and expressed concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation follows a clear, logical arc from exploring goals to a concrete, actionable plan without losing focus on the target behavior. The only minor weakness is that the 'what would it NOT look like' question briefly expands scope before being quickly re-anchored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a model of collaborative interaction between equals, with the psychiatrist using inclusive language and co-constructing the plan with David rather than prescribing it. The final plan is framed as David's own achievement, not a clinician-imposed recommendation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is pervasive and consistent, with the psychiatrist repeatedly affirming David's right to make his own decisions on his own timeline. The session is entirely structured around David's choices, standards, and pace, with no use of guilt, fear, or urgency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation — all motivation is drawn from within David through well-crafted open questions, with the psychiatrist never providing a single external reason for change. The psychiatrist's role is purely to create space for David to articulate and own his own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions David as the expert on his own life, explicitly stating he knows his triggers better than anyone and that his values are the criteria, not the clinician's. Genuine choice is offered about how to proceed, and the psychiatrist never positions themselves as knowing best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes practical change talk by asking open questions that elicit David's own reasoning and affirming his self-knowledge. The evocation could be slightly stronger with more explicit elicitation of confidence or ability language, such as importance/confidence rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Perhaps the strongest element of the session — David's prominent sustain talk is never argued against, minimized, or reframed aggressively, but simply accepted as a given and incorporated into the working model. The psychiatrist introduces no health warnings or reasons to quit, avoiding any dynamic that would create resistance.
