# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect and nonjudgment, with few explicit signs of disapproval. Some mildly pressuring prompts, such as repeatedly asking what would raise confidence and proposing a signed agreement early, prevent a perfect acceptance score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture Isabella's ambivalence, autonomy concerns, and worry about her baby. Empathy could be stronger by reflecting deeper emotional meanings behind statements about excuses, judgment, and fear of being watched.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and moves toward a concrete, realistic behavioral goal. Some time spent on administrative details, such as format, language, and scripts, slightly dilutes the motivational focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by adjusting the plan to Isabella's preferences, including no swaps, informal Italian, and no follow-up reminders. A more fully collaborative approach could have invited Isabella to generate more of the plan structure first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a standout strength, with consistent reinforcement of Isabella's choice, ownership, and self-directed decision-making. The psychiatrist avoids imposing abstinence or compliance and frames the final plan as Isabella's own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The importance/confidence ruler and follow-up questions effectively elicit change talk and increase confidence. Evocation could be stronger if the psychiatrist had explored Isabella's deeper motivations and values before providing medical information and structured options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites input and co-creates practical details, showing respect for Isabella's expertise in her own life. However, the psychiatrist still drives much of the session by offering many structured options and specific ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Isabella generates meaningful change talk, including commitment to protect her baby and a concrete plan to put the wine away. The psychiatrist could do more to amplify and deepen this change talk through reflective elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and adapts well when Isabella rejects substitutes, a formal agreement, or reminders. Some earlier programmatic suggestions may have increased resistance briefly, though the psychiatrist corrects course effectively.
