# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates David’s concern about protecting his strength. Acceptance is consistent but not fully individualized or affirming of David’s honesty and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the tension between following medical advice and avoiding weakness, fatigue, or loss of control. Empathy is useful but often stays at the practical level rather than exploring deeper emotional or identity-related meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target issue and moves logically from concern to trial, monitoring, and follow-up. Direction could be stronger with clearer summaries and more strategic consolidation of the emerging plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David’s preferences clearly shape the conversation, including the one-week trial, monitoring plan, reminder, and short follow-up. Collaboration is solid but lacks more explicit language framing the work as equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes David’s choice, right to stop, and need for control. There is little to no pressure, coercion, or controlling language, making this a strong area of practice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses frequent questions to draw out David’s own preferences and criteria for action. Evocation is often practical rather than deeply motivational, with limited exploration of values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites David to define what matters and allows his answers to guide the session. Partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist still controls the question structure and does not fully emphasize David as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerges, such as openness to a tiny dose and recognition that checking in may make sense. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen these statements to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk carefully and does not argue with David’s side-effect concerns. However, the session does not sufficiently shift sustain talk into change talk or balance the medication risks with long-term health goals.
