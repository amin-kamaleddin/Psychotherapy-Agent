# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful tone with several validating moments, but acceptance remains at a surface level without deep, individualized positive regard for the patient as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate and shows reasonable perspective-taking, but is largely cognitive and fact-based rather than emotionally attuned to the patient's underlying vulnerability and self-deprecating tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session fundamentally lacks direction toward the target behavior (alcohol use); the entire conversation wanders through hobbies and sensory preferences without ever anchoring to a specific, actionable change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and engages in dialogue, but drives the conversation through leading questions and interpretive prompts, making the interaction resemble a structured interview more than genuine collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids prescriptive advice, respects the patient's agency, and frames suggestions as questions rather than directives. The minor suggestion to make it easier to get to the creek is appropriately framed as exploratory.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some effort is made to evoke the patient's own perspectives through open-ended questions, but the evocation is shallow and exploratory rather than targeting intrinsic motivation or eliciting reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally invites the patient's perspective, but frequently assumes the role of the curious expert who controls the pace, topic, and depth of exploration, keeping the power dynamic tilted toward the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to evoke change talk specifically related to the target behavior; the small step the patient identifies is not affirmed as a change-related commitment or explored for confidence and importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The patient does not express any sustain talk, so the domain is essentially inapplicable; no opportunity is present to demonstrate skill in managing ambivalence or resistance.
