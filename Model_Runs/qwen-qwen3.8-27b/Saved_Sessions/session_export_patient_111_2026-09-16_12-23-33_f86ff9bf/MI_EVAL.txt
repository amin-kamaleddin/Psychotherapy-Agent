# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses validating, nonjudgmental language and avoids arguing with the patient. The warmth is strong but can feel procedural because of repeated suggestions and check-in planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient’s points about reading, respect, and marital conflict. Deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or resentment are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session opens with clear focus on smoking and readiness, but quickly drifts toward reading routines, marital boundaries, and session logistics. There is little strategic guidance keeping the discussion centered on tobacco change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient’s preferences substantially shape the session, and the psychiatrist offers choices and adapts to his wishes. Occasional expert-driven suggestions prevent a fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirms the patient’s control, avoids pressure, and respects his desire to stay silent. Some proposed options and check-ins introduce mild direction that limits a top score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions evoke values such as respect, independence, and marital stability, but motivation for smoking change is not consistently or deeply elicited. The conversation often shifts to practical problem-solving instead.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the patient as an important source of knowledge and repeatedly invites input. Moments of expert-driven problem-solving, such as drafting a sentence or offering options, reduce the sense of fully collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness scaling and open questions elicit some change talk about not losing his wife and avoiding arguing. The psychiatrist does not consistently build on or sustain this change talk with deeper elicitation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally rolls with resistance rather than arguing, but repeatedly revisits the patient’s reluctance and lack of motivation. Double-sided reflections and strategic redirection toward change are limited.
