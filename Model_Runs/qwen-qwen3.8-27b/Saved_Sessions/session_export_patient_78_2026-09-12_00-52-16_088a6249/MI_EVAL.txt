# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental, with validating statements that avoid shaming. Acceptance is slightly procedural and occasionally interrupted by a quick move into advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are reasonable reflections of her tiredness and the function of smoking. Empathy is surface-level and misses deeper themes such as work stress, her daughter, anxiety, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays connected to smoking reduction and a small doable step. Direction is diluted by a broad opening, grounding exercise, and many unrelated coping options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is present because the client chooses a small strategy, especially the pen. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist supplies most of the options and frames the next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is well supported through choices, low-pressure language, and acceptance of limited commitment. The repeated offering of many options still leans somewhat toward counselor-led decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited; questions focus more on selecting a behavior than eliciting internal motivations. The client's own change-relevant statements are not expanded or built upon.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is evidence of partnership because client preferences shape the conversation. Partnership is incomplete because the psychiatrist often positions themselves as the source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only rare, weak invitations to explore the client's reasons for change. The client's change-relevant language is not reflected, amplified, or used to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids confrontation and acknowledges some sustain talk without judgment. Sustain talk is often bypassed by offering alternatives rather than explored to help resolve ambivalence.
