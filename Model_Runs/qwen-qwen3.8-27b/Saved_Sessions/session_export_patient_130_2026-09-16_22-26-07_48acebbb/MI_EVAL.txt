# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or labeling. Warmth is strong but somewhat procedural, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate reflections that validate Viktor's perspective and reduce defensiveness. Deeper emotional themes such as shame, fear, or loss of autonomy remain underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear micro-focus on homecoming and reducing tension. However, it drifts away from the primary target of reducing drug use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor co-constructs a plan and invites Viktor's input throughout. The counselor still provides structured choices and maintains significant process control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports Viktor's choice, ownership, and self-awareness. The final plan is framed as Viktor's own, not imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor evokes Viktor's own goals around calm, presence, and relationship quality. Less evocation is directed toward Viktor's motivation to reduce use itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor respects Viktor's expertise and helps him articulate his preferred approach. The session remains counselor-structured rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Viktor offers meaningful change talk about communication, calmness, and being present at home. The counselor does not consistently elicit change talk directly tied to reducing drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and reframes defensiveness into acceptable language. Some sustain talk about use is accepted rather than skillfully turned into discrepancy.
