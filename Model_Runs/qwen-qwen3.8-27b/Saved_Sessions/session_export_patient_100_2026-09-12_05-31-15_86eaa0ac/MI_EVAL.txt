# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and affirming while validating the student's class-only boundary. The warmth is strong but remains more professional and task-focused than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the student's mixed feelings, frustration, and practical morning constraints. The empathy is mainly cognitive and practical, with limited exploration of deeper emotional or monitoring-related distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, relevant focus on improving grades through a simple morning routine and stays logically organized. The connection to the broader alcohol-use concern or future well-being is more indirect than ideal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed rather than prescribed, with the student actively contributing ideas and choosing key details. The counselor invites the student's knowledge and preferences throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supports the student's right to choose and control by repeatedly emphasizing optionality and self-determination. There is no pressure to change alcohol use or adopt a larger treatment goal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively draws out the student's own ideas, confidence ratings, and practical implementation plans. Evocation is strong for task-specific change but does not deeply connect to broader values or future motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shares power and allows the student's choices to shape the session's direction and content. The student proposes the core plan elements and selects the final wording, location, and backup.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicits concrete, actionable change talk around school performance, the checklist, and confidence in following through. The change talk is limited mostly to class tasks and does not extend to broader alcohol-related values or goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids arguing with or reinforcing the student's sustain talk but does not consistently use skilled MI techniques to soften or redirect it. Ambivalence around drinking behavior is accepted but remains largely unexplored.
