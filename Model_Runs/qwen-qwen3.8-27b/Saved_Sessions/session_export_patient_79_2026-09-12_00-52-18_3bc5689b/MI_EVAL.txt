# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental and respectful stance while validating the client’s frustration without arguing. The stance was slightly procedural and briefly shifted toward risk management, limiting full warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician provided adequate reflections of exhaustion and feeling policed but often moved too quickly into planning. Deeper ambivalence, autonomy concerns, and system-related resentment were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on simplifying asthma medication management and moved toward a concrete change goal. Some drift into safety, venting options, and additional tasks slightly diluted momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited input and adjusted the script to match the client’s preference for using fewer inhalers. However, most of the content and problem-solving remained clinician-driven rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and non-choice was explicitly normalized, supporting some sense of client control. The number of recommendations and directives may have increased the client’s sense of additional obligations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open questions were used briefly, but the session quickly shifted into advice, scripts, and practical planning. There was limited effort to draw out the client’s own motivation, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician showed some partnership by asking for preferences and adapting suggestions. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist remained the primary problem-solver, scriptwriter, and source of practical guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client produced some change-relevant language, but the clinician mostly affirmed feasibility and moved into planning. There were few questions designed to elicit desire, ability, need, benefits, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician did not argue with the client’s sustain talk and acknowledged barriers such as distrust and overwhelm. However, the conversation stayed heavily focused on difficulties, and added tasks may have reinforced the sense of burden.
