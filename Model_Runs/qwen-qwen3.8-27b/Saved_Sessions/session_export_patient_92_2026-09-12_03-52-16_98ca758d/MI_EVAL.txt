# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids labeling the client’s drinking. Acceptance is sometimes formulaic and does not fully normalize the client’s ambivalence or social fears.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and summaries to track the client’s perspective. However, some reflections are slightly interpretive and do not deeply explore the emotional meaning behind the client’s fear of being the odd one out.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on alcohol use, health concerns, and possible small changes. However, the psychiatrist moves toward planning or choosing a step a bit too quickly for someone still in contemplation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a partner and invites the client’s input before moving forward. However, many of the session’s structures and options are still introduced by the clinician rather than fully co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, asking permission, and respecting the client’s reluctance to commit. Some later suggestions to choose an experiment or schedule a follow-up create mild pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently tries to draw motivation from within the client through values, importance, and confidence questions. However, the session sometimes moves too quickly toward clinician-generated ideas before fully expanding the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and follows the client’s lead in several moments. However, the session is still partly driven by the psychiatrist’s menus, rulers, and proposed experiments, so power sharing is not fully consistent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes frequent attempts to elicit change talk about protecting the liver, avoiding hospitalization, and trying a small experiment. These statements are present but not consistently amplified or deepened to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging the client’s reasons for drinking without arguing. However, key sustain-talk moments about social identity and being the odd one out could have been explored more strategically to help the client reconcile them with change.
