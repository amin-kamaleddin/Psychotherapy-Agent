# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, supportive, and frequently affirming. Minor overuse of formulaic praise and a brief push toward accountability slightly reduced the impact.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were adequate and captured some frustration and concern. However, the therapist often moved too quickly from emotion to planning and did not deeply reflect identity, vitality, or discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed strongly focused on the behavioral goal of returning to the gym. The conversation consistently progressed toward a concrete, actionable exercise plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist and client co-built a useful plan, with several client-generated ideas. However, the therapist provided much of the structure and strategies, making the partnership feel somewhat uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client choice and ownership were repeatedly supported, including respecting his preference not to owe anyone. Still, the therapist offered multiple options and rapid sequencing that could feel steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and question use helped evoke confidence and commitment. The evocation remained more procedural than motivational, with less exploration of underlying values or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s input meaningfully shaped the plan. The session was less fully collaborative at times because the therapist introduced several strategies before the client generated his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively brought out desire, ability, and commitment language, especially through confidence and commitment scaling. The main limitation was not consistently deepening or elaborating the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist generally avoided arguing with barriers and redirected toward manageable action. However, sustain talk was not consistently reframed, and the early accountability suggestion could have reinforced concerns about owing others.
