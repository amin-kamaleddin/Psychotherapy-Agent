# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client's pace and choices. The warmth is professional and supportive, though somewhat procedural rather than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician provides an accurate initial summary of the client's ambivalence. However, empathy remains largely surface-level, especially when the client mentions isolation and reluctance to disclose use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear practical focus on harm reduction and nose healing. This direction becomes somewhat narrow and medicalized, drifting away from exploring the client's deeper ambivalence about methamphetamine use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is present at the start, but the psychiatrist often supplies a menu of expert-backed solutions. This shifts the dynamic from equal partnership to the clinician doing much of the problem-solving for the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, with the clinician respecting the client's right to refuse and allowing him to set the pace. The psychiatrist avoids pressure and accepts the client's limited scope of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the weakest area, as the psychiatrist repeatedly prescribes solutions, tactics, and alarm times. Instead of eliciting the client's own reasons and strategies, the clinician leads the content of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a strong partnership stance, but it erodes as the psychiatrist becomes more directive. The client is not consistently positioned as the expert generating his own behavioral ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some initial change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely expands or reinforces it. The clinician quickly moves to practical medical solutions, limiting development of the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client's sustain talk and does not create resistance. However, the clinician bypasses these moments rather than using reflections to gently explore ambivalence.
