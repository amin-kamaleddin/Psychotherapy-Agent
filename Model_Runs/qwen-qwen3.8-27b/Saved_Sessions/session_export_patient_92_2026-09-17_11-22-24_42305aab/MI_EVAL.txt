# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, with strong validation of the patient's perspective (e.g., 'I respect that completely'). The tone remains professional and measured rather than deeply warm and personally invested, which keeps it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate complex reflections that capture the patient's internal framework and autonomy themes. Reflections sometimes focus on cognitive/positional content rather than the emotional undercurrent, which limits the score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with logical progression from understanding perspective to exploring thresholds to planning next steps. Pacing is well-calibrated to the contemplative stage without pushing too hard or losing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with language that frames the interaction as joint exploration and active incorporation of the patient's contributions. The psychiatrist still controls the question sequence and overall direction, with the patient shaping content but not always the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary and the strongest domain in the session. The psychiatrist explicitly rejects the role of external authority, validates the right to not change, names and affirms autonomy, and frames all suggestions as options while preserving choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws out the patient's own motivations and reasoning rather than providing reasons for change, with skillful evocative questions and amplification of change talk. Could have evoked more from the patient's positive values and experiences beyond drinking to create a broader contrast.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites the patient's perspective and positions himself as a learner rather than an expert, never assuming authority. The partnership is somewhat structured by the psychiatrist's agenda, guiding through a series of questions that serve therapeutic goals rather than being fully co-determined.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes several effective attempts to evoke change talk, producing meaningful statements from the patient (e.g., 'Facts I can work with'). However, the psychiatrist tends to move forward rather than deepening and expanding on the change talk that emerges, limiting the overall impact.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating without arguing or reinforcing (e.g., 'That's fair — your dad made it to seventy'). One area for improvement is more explicitly reflecting the underlying message when the patient deflects rather than simply moving forward.
