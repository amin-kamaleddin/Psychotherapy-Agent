# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s struggle, reducing shame. Acceptance is strong but occasionally shifts into performance-focused praise rather than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but mostly cognitive, capturing ambivalence and barriers without fully naming underlying emotions. More complex reflections of fatigue, fear, and frustration would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and narrows a vague goal into a concrete, manageable action. Direction is strong, though repeated problem-solving prevents deeper consolidation of the client’s change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites input and ultimately incorporates client preferences into the final plan. However, collaboration is uneven because the therapist frequently supplies multiple options before allowing the client to generate his own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through permission-asking, choices, low-pressure language, and emphasis on the client’s control. Some commitment questions could feel slightly pressuring given the client’s ambivalence and low confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist uses evocative questions such as past successes, confidence scaling, and barrier exploration. Evocation is limited by frequent shifts into advice-giving and insufficient exploration of the client’s values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is an effort to collaborate, including asking permission and inviting choices. The partnership becomes uneven when the therapist takes on an expert role by generating many specific solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist makes several attempts to evoke change talk and uses a confidence ruler. However, the client’s change statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or built upon before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not strategically softened or redirected. The therapist often responds with more problem-solving options rather than using double-sided reflections or evoking discrepancy.
