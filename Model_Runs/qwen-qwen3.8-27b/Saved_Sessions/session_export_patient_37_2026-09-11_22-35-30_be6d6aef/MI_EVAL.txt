# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Elena’s concerns without shaming her. However, acceptance remains somewhat task-oriented and moves quickly toward problem-solving rather than deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic empathy is shown for Elena’s anxiety and ambivalence, especially early in the session. However, the responses often remain surface-level and do not fully reflect the emotional weight of feeling judged or frightened.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target issue: vaccine hesitancy and next steps for communicating with the pediatrician. The direction is clear, but it is more task-oriented than consistently MI-strategic in exploring values and personal reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting input, co-constructing the message, and adjusting wording based on Elena’s feedback. Collaboration is strong, but the clinician remains the primary source of content, structure, and solution language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena is regularly offered choices and noncontrolling language, and her preferences are respected. Autonomy support is consistent, though the frequent provision of ready-made scripts and answers slightly limits maximal client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some of Elena’s concerns and emerging openness, but the psychiatrist relies heavily on direct education and script-building. There are fewer evocative questions to help Elena generate and elaborate her own internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena’s choices shape the session, and she is given options for how to proceed. However, the psychiatrist functions as the main source of information and direction, reflecting partial rather than fully developed partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena produces meaningful change talk, such as feeling less scared and planning to send the text. The psychiatrist acknowledges some of this but does not consistently deepen, reflect back, or strategically amplify her change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and generally redirects concerns toward action, but handles sustain talk in a didactic way at times. Labeling a concern as “a myth” is effective but less MI-consistent than softer double-sided reflections.
