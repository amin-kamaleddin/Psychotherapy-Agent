# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintained a warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful tone with frequent validation. However, the acceptance was somewhat formulaic and could benefit from more individualized, specific affirmations of Linda’s unique strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, accurately capturing Linda’s ambivalence and core conflicts. The main limitation was staying largely at the cognitive level without fully reflecting the deeper emotional weight of her fear, loss, and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, logical focus on tobacco use and progressed well from ambivalence to a concrete plan. However, the direction was somewhat passive and could have included stronger strategic moves to consolidate momentum and close the session effectively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session felt collaborative, with Linda participating in shaping choices and the plan. However, the psychiatrist generated most of the specific strategies, limiting the extent to which Linda was asked to draw on her own expertise and past experiences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported Linda’s autonomy by validating her pace, avoiding pressure, and framing attempts as optional experiments. It could have been even stronger by more explicitly affirming Linda’s ultimate ownership of her decision and outcomes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used effective open-ended questions to elicit Linda’s own reasons, ideas, and preferences. However, the evocation remained at a moderate depth and could have more powerfully explored Linda’s values, identity, and long-term vision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Linda as the expert on her own life and consistently invited her input. The partnership was slightly weakened by the psychiatrist generating much of the plan content rather than evoking more of Linda’s own strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited instances of change talk through open-ended questions, but often failed to reinforce, amplify, or deepen that language. There were missed opportunities to strategically build on Linda’s emerging motivation and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately acknowledged and normalized Linda’s sustain talk around social loss and belonging. However, the response largely sidestepped the underlying ambivalence and did not strategically use double-sided reflections to help Linda resolve her concerns.
