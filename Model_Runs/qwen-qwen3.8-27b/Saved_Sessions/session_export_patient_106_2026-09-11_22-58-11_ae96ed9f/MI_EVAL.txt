# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is nonjudgmental and validating, reassuring the patient that she is not a bad parent and that no lecture is coming. However, the warmth is not consistently deep, and the repeated practical interventions can make the session feel somewhat corrective rather than fully affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of the patient’s experience, including being overwhelmed and using smoking as a reset. However, the reflections often remain surface-level and quickly shift into problem-solving without deeper exploration of exhaustion, guilt, fear, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of smoking and moves toward a concrete step, especially the 30-minute rule. There is some drift into tracking, NRT, and coping strategies, but the overall clinical direction is maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on patient-generated ideas such as leaving the pack in the car and agreeing to the 30-minute rule. However, the psychiatrist provides many specific recommendations and frames the session around practical harm reduction, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through language such as “if you want” and “no lecture,” and the counselor acknowledges that the patient may continue to smoke. However, repeated prescriptive suggestions and problem-solving structure undermine the patient’s sense of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the weakest area, with limited effort to draw out the patient’s own values, motivations, or reasons for change. The counselor frequently provides options and solutions rather than using open questions to elicit intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient to choose a doable option and includes her input in the final plan. However, the counselor often behaves as the expert by presenting multiple interventions and practical steps rather than letting the patient’s agenda lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient produces change talk about reducing smoking and trying the 30-minute rule, and the counselor reinforces cutting down as progress. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen the patient’s change talk with skillful MI prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with the patient’s sustain talk about needing a break, the wine comparison, or concern that the child is healthy. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or connecting values to change.
