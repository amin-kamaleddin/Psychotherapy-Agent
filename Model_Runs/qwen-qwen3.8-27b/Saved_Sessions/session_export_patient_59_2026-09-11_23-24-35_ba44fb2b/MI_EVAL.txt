# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with a resistant adolescent and did not shame, moralize, or confront him. Score not 5 because the tone remained somewhat procedural, and repeated checklists after dismissal may weaken unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provided accurate surface-level reflections early, especially about being made to come and wanting to end the session. Empathy did not deepen into the emotional meaning of the client's irritation, resentment, sense of control, or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed mostly on the mandated task and legal closure rather than unrelated topics. However, direction became administrative rather than consistently MI-guided toward the target behavior and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input, asked for preferred outcomes, and offered optional help, which supported some partnership. Collaboration was limited because the plan was largely therapist-generated, with the client mostly accepting or rejecting the offered checklist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged the mandatory nature of the visit and accepted that the client would handle the probation officer himself. Autonomy support was weakened by repeated directive checklists, scripts, and offers to draft materials after the client said to stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Asked a few initial MI-style questions about what the client wanted to address and the desired outcome. Evocation remained superficial, with no meaningful follow-up, scaling, or exploration of values, confidence, risk, or readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some collaborative moves, such as asking what outcome the client wanted and offering choices. The interaction became increasingly therapist-directed, reducing the client’s role as an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered useful change language about completing the requirement, avoiding future trouble, and not coming back. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or strategically use this change talk to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided directly arguing with the client or agreeing that the incident was “no big deal.” However, there was limited skillful use of double-sided reflection, discrepancy, or gentle redirection to soften minimization.
