# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment. The client's concerns and agency are validated without pressure or criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client's emphasis on accuracy, safety, and work functioning. It could deepen further by exploring underlying emotions such as fear, frustration, or vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on citalopram, side effects, and the phenelzine interaction. It could benefit from stronger strategic movement toward a concrete decisional or behavioral endpoint.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's input clearly shapes the structure and content of the session. The psychiatrist invites the client to prioritize questions and define what a useful answer would include.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positions the client as the decision-maker and respects her pace. There is no coercion, pressure, or paternalism.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, client-centered questions effectively elicit the client's values, concerns, and readiness criteria. It could go deeper in evoking self-efficacy, identity, or more specific change motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites the client to organize and direct the conversation. It could be stronger by more explicitly framing the client as the expert on her own goals and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor highlights shifts in the client's stance and asks questions that invite elaboration of readiness and action criteria. It could more deliberately amplify specific DARN change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is normalized and reframed without confrontation, preserving the client's perspective while moving toward action. It could more consistently pivot from sustain talk to explicit evocation of change.
