# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Erik’s frustration with being labeled and monitored. Acceptance is strong but occasionally shifts toward advice or external validation, slightly reducing warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Erik’s ambivalence about beer, his desire for simplicity, and his preference for autonomy. Reflections are practical and accurate but miss opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as fear, embarrassment, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and connecting it to diabetes monitoring. However, the focus is somewhat narrow and could have more explicitly linked the alcohol plan to broader diabetes and lifestyle goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician collaborates well by offering options and allowing Erik to shape the plan. The interaction is slightly less client-led because the clinician frequently introduces concrete suggestions before fully eliciting Erik’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician supports choice by asking Erik what is doable and respecting his limits. Some mildly directive moments, such as recommending specific steps and telling him to inform his doctor, reduce the fully autonomous stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits practical action steps and some self-directed commitments. There is limited exploration of Erik’s values, reasons for change, confidence, or deeper internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician treats Erik as the expert on his own routine and adapts the plan to his constraints. The interaction becomes slightly advisor-like when the clinician offers specific behavioral strategies rather than waiting for Erik to generate them first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes useful action-oriented change talk, such as skipping the first beer and using a nonalcoholic substitute. Change talk remains thin and task-focused, with limited exploration of desire, need, ability, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges Erik’s sustain talk without arguing or amplifying it, and redirects it into a manageable experiment. The main limitation is inconsistent use of double-sided reflections or other MI-style techniques to pivot sustain talk toward change.
