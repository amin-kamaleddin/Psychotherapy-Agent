# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with positive regard for the client’s struggle. The psychiatrist avoids labeling, disapproval, or pressure while treating the client’s concerns as understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures ambivalence, fatigue, identity, and values. Some reflections become repetitive or lightly leading, which slightly reduces precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strategically focused on the target behavior: a small, doable boundary after work. Each question and reflection connects back to protecting self-respect and self-care.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas strongly shape the conversation, including the laptop cue, tea ritual, and bodily signals. Collaboration would be stronger with more explicit checking of the client’s preferred direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client’s freedom of choice and ownership are consistently supported without urging commitment. The psychiatrist frames actions as doable, honest, and personally meaningful rather than imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes the client’s own motivations instead of supplying reasons for change. The client articulates the meaning and value of change in their own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session clearly reflects power-sharing, with the client’s expertise driving the work. The psychiatrist asks exploratory questions rather than prescribing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out desire, reasons, ability, and commitment. The client generates strong change talk, which the psychiatrist reflects and amplifies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is gently reframed without being amplified or dwelled on. The handling would be more nuanced with an explicit double-sided reflection of the client’s self-doubt.
