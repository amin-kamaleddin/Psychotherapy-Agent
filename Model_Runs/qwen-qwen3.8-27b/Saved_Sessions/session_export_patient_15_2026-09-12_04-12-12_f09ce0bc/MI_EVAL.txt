# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offered frequent affirmations of the client's strengths and engagement. Warmth was generally strong, though occasionally slightly assumptive or formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections and summaries were used frequently and were accurate at a cognitive level. They remained surface-level and did not consistently explore underlying emotions, ambivalence, or deeper meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target issue of elevated blood pressure and related behaviors. Focus became somewhat technical over time, shifting toward monitoring mechanics rather than broader motivational direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician asked for preferences, offered options, and built a practical plan around the client's stated comfort level. The session was collaborative but remained somewhat provider-driven in structure and clinical direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly supported choice, avoided pressure, and respected the client's decision not to keep a detailed alcohol log. Stronger explicit language reinforcing the client's ownership and decision-making authority would have strengthened this further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician used some evocative questions to draw out practical intentions and problem-solving. Evocation remained largely task-focused and did not consistently elicit deeper personal motivation, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited client input and incorporated preferences into the plan, showing some responsiveness to the client's contributions. The interaction remained somewhat provider-driven and did not consistently emphasize the client as the primary expert on his own priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicited some practical commitment language and affirmed strengths related to monitoring and past behavior change. Change talk remained mostly low-level intention rather than deeper intrinsic motivation or personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing with or challenging the client's minimization of alcohol and overall need for change. However, the session did not effectively soften or pivot sustain talk toward change, leaving the status-quo orientation largely unexamined.
