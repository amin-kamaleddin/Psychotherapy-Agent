# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and offers frequent affirmations and normalization. The warmth is sometimes tied to the client's performance rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and content-focused, showing adequate understanding of the client's concerns. The counselor often misses deeper emotional undercurrents, such as fear of inauthenticity or frustration with the co-parent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target behavior related to bedtime and consistent communication with the son. The focus is occasionally diluted by multiple scripts and practical tips.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client actively chooses, rehearses, and modifies content, showing some collaboration. The psychiatrist frequently leads with ready-made scripts and tips, reducing the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client's choice by offering options, asking permission, and allowing her to adapt the scripts. The support could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing the client's ownership throughout the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some goals, past successes, and confidence statements. Evocation remains somewhat superficial as the session shifts quickly toward practical scripts and advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone, with the client invited to choose focus and approach. The interaction becomes more expert-driven when the psychiatrist provides multiple ready-made options rather than first exploring the client's own language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful change talk around intention, confidence, and small steps. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or expand this change talk before moving into practical problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized without being reinforced. The counselor could more actively use double-sided reflections or value-linked pivots to gently turn ambivalence toward change.
