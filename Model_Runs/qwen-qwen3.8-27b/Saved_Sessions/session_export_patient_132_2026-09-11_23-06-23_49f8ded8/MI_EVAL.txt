# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and support, effectively normalizing Yasmin's ambivalence and fears. However, the warmth was occasionally formulaic, and a subtly corrective statement kept it from a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made reasonable initial efforts to understand and validate the client's perspective, but reflections remained largely generic and surface-level. There were missed opportunities to deeply explore underlying emotions, such as the shame within the binge cycle.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear and focused trajectory on specific, actionable steps, which prevented aimless drift and created momentum. However, the focus became somewhat narrow on the psychiatrist's proposed plan rather than continually tying the behavior back to the client's broader values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was generally collaborative, using joint language and inviting the client to choose between alternatives. However, the collaboration was uneven because the psychiatrist supplied the bulk of the session's content and solutions, rather than letting Yasmin's ideas shape the overall direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported autonomy by frequently offering choices and normalizing various approaches to the session's goals. The main limitation was the occasional use of directive, controlling language (e.g., "non-negotiable," "quick rules") that slightly reduced the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist included some good evocative questions early on, such as exploring what "frustrated" looks and feels like. However, the rest of the session was heavily driven by the counselor providing rationale, macro targets, and solutions, which limited space for Yasmin to develop her own internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated some partnership by asking Yasmin to make specific choices about her plan and scale use. Nevertheless, the counselor frequently assumed an expert role by supplying detailed rules, exact portions, and scripts rather than fully handing authorship back to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicited some change talk regarding sustainability and routine, but the psychiatrist did not consistently use strategic questions or amplifications to expand on it. More effort was needed to strengthen and draw out the client's own language for change and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoided arguing with or dismissing the client's sustain talk, such as her desire for weight loss and fear of restriction. However, she failed to consistently reframe this talk or gently shift the focus toward autonomy and well-being when addressing her worries about snacks and the scale.
