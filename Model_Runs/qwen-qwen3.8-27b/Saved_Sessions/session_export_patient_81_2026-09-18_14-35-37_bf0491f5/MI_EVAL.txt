# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgmental stance throughout, with well-timed affirmations that never label or minimize the client's experience. Affirmations occasionally feel slightly generic or scripted rather than deeply individualized to the client's specific strengths and vulnerabilities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate dual reflections of ambivalence and insightful reframings (e.g., the café week as asking him to be someone else). Empathy sometimes stays at the cognitive level rather than fully penetrating the emotional depth behind key statements like feeling like 'furniture.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical arc maintaining focus on methamphetamine use while skillfully connecting to identity, belonging, and career context. A few moments where the discussion drifts slightly tangential (e.g., exploring acting as a solution) before being brought back to the immediate target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership throughout, with the client's metaphors and framing genuinely shaping the session's direction and content. The psychiatrist adopts and builds on the stomach metaphor and works within the client's own definition of the problem rather than imposing a clinical framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model example of autonomy support woven into every question and response. The psychiatrist never recommends specific actions, never uses prescriptive language, and consistently positions all decisions as entirely the client's to make.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of open questions and reflective prompts that successfully elicit a range of change talk including desire, need, ability, and intention. Could push deeper with follow-up evocation when strong change talk emerges (e.g., 'I don't need a Tuesday. I need a Friday') to maximize its impact.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistent collaborative, non-expert stance with the client's agenda established from the opening question. Could be elevated by more explicitly acknowledging the client's wisdom and agency at key moments and inviting him to redirect the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk through well-timed questions targeting desire, need, ability, and action. Could go further in reinforcing the client's capacity and past successes, particularly when he expresses confidence in his ability to manage the change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without confrontation, validating positions like 'I don't need a program' while not reinforcing the 'just ease off' framing as a complete solution. Could more actively and gently explore the cost of maintaining the status quo, perhaps through double-sided reflections at key moments.
