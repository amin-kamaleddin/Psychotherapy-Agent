# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and treats slips as data rather than failure. Acceptance is slightly more task-focused than deeply personal and could better normalize ambivalence and struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledge the patient’s practical conflicts, such as dental worry and stress relief, but stop at content level. The feelings behind fear, anxiety, and relief are not consistently explored or mirrored with emotional precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on smoking cessation and follows a practical structure for logging, coping, and follow-up. It could more explicitly connect the plan to the patient’s deeper values or reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient’s preferences actively shape the plan, such as cutting down, logging at 1:30, and using a toothpick instead of gum. The psychiatrist still supplies many strategies, so collaboration is strong but not fully equal in idea generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids cold-turkey pressure, which supports patient ownership. However, frequent prescriptive advice reduces the sense that the patient is independently choosing solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions evoke practical action talk, but there are missed opportunities to draw out deeper values, confidence, and personal reasons for change. Evocation remains more logistical than motivational.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributes ideas and helps set the plan, showing real partnership. The psychiatrist’s expert role in supplying many strategies makes the collaboration uneven and less client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes and affirms some action and commitment talk, such as logging, gum, and breathing. It does not consistently cultivate deeper desire, need, importance, or confidence talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes sustain talk, avoids argument, and reframes slips as data. It could more actively use double-sided reflections to move from sustain talk to change talk.
