# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids shaming or pressure. The acceptance is warm but remains somewhat general rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflects the client’s stated experience accurately but remains largely surface-level. There is limited exploration of underlying feelings, values, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session moves toward a concrete micro-task, but the connection to the alcohol-risk referral is weak. The target behavior is not clearly tied to the presenting concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and incorporates the client’s preferences into the plan. However, much of the intervention structure is still counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly emphasizes the client’s choice, ownership, and freedom to modify the plan. The client is repeatedly given low-pressure options and control over the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are asked, but the session does not strongly elicit the client’s internal motivation for change. The focus shifts quickly to a practical task rather than drawing out personal reasons or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to guide decisions and builds the plan around their preferences. The session is collaborative, though not fully client-led in content and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor reinforces basic willingness to try, but does not deepen the client’s desire, ability, need, or reasons for change. Change talk remains limited and low-level.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles the client’s reservations without arguing, minimizing, or adding pressure. Concerns are normalized, and low-effort alternatives help maintain willingness.
