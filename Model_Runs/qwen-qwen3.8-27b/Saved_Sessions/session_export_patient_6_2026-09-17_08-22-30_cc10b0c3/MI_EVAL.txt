# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, nonjudgment, and validation without shaming the client. Acceptance could be even stronger by fully normalizing the client's ambivalence about peer belonging and fun, not just the protective future-oriented side.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's goals, doubts, and post-decisional self-replay. Empathy could deepen by more explicitly reflecting shame, fear, loneliness, or the emotional cost of peer pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target alcohol-related risk and the client's soccer, scholarship, and future goals. The conversation has a clear, behavior-specific trajectory from concern to concrete coping plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as a collaborator by building on the client's ideas and co-creating a practical plan. Collaboration could improve by allowing the client more control over agenda-setting and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is regularly invited to choose, reject options, and own the plan. Autonomy support could be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment that change is the client's choice and the plan only works if it fits his life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client's own values, such as soccer, scholarships, and protecting his future. The session ends with strong client-generated change talk, including 'I saved my future.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and positions him as the expert on his own life. Partnership could be more explicit by reducing clinician-led structure and more directly asking what the client wants to focus on.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits and reinforces the client's reasons for protecting his soccer and scholarship opportunities. The client develops increasingly committed, self-authored change talk by the end of the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fitting in and not seeming like a 'try-hard' is acknowledged without arguing or dismissing. It could be softened more effectively by exploring the appeal of belonging more fully before redirecting toward the client's goals.
