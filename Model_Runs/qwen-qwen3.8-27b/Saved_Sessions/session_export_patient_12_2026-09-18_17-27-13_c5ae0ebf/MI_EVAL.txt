# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, never judging Amira's substance use or failed programs, and skillfully validates her coping strategies while gently reframing. The warmth occasionally feels slightly performative and doesn't quite reach the depth of fully normalizing her experience as a human response.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate complex reflections of ambivalence and insightful naming of implicit dynamics. However, sometimes jumps to cognitive reframing rather than staying fully in the emotional space.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and logical progression from acknowledging pain to exploring ambivalence to establishing safety, staying focused on Amira's readiness for change. The session does not land on a concrete next step or explicitly connect back to the referral concern of drug use reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present and the psychiatrist seeks Amira's input and respects her contributions. In practice, the dynamic remains therapist-directed with Amira largely passive, lacking explicit co-creation of the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support woven throughout the session; the psychiatrist never pressures for commitment, explicitly validates Amira's boundaries, and gives her full permission to 'just be stuck.' This is the strongest dimension and directly addresses her core fear of being controlled or forced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws out Amira's own motivations using evocative questions without providing external reasons for change or lecturing. The evocation stays at the level of 'what would make this feel safer' rather than reaching deeper toward values or long-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with appropriate language and avoids an expert-driven approach. The partnership is somewhat one-directional in structure, with the psychiatrist doing the guiding and framing while Amira remains largely receptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes well-constructed evocative questions that elicit some change talk from Amira, but does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or deepen these moments to strengthen her sense of agency. There is no explicit elicitation of ability, confidence, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk using double-sided reflections and validation rather than arguing or dismissing, while gently opening space for different perspectives. Consistently avoids reinforcing hopelessness as a fixed state and redirects agency back to Amira.
