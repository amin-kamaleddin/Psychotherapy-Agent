# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, nonjudgment, and validation of Lucía's frustration and autonomy. The stance is warm and supportive, though it could include more individualized emotional attunement to her broader experience of being dismissed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Lucía's ambivalence, irritation, and need for practical, respectful support. Empathy is strong but often cognitive/summary-like rather than fully exploring deeper emotions such as fear of failure or resentment of being controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on smoking cessation, moving effectively from exploration to a concrete one-week experiment. Pacing is strategic, purposeful, and well connected to Lucía's stated concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is cooperative, with multiple invitations for choice, shared decision-making, and respect for Lucía's preferences. Collaboration could be stronger by using fewer therapist-generated options and more silence or client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, repeatedly emphasizing Lucía's freedom to choose and adjusting quickly when she resists tracking. The plan is framed as a voluntary experiment rather than a prescription, with clear respect for her boundaries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions, including the ruler and exploring change connected to Lucía's daughter. However, the session moves too quickly into options, limiting opportunities to deepen intrinsic motivation, confidence, and personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lucía is treated as the expert on her own choices, and her contributions clearly shape the session. The psychiatrist remains somewhat directive in offering structured options, which slightly limits a fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited and acknowledged, especially around doing this for her daughter and trying in her own way. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify and deepen change talk using DARN-C questions before moving to practical solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by rolling with resistance and honoring Lucía's boundary against medical lectures. Responses are respectful and effective, though they could more actively transform sustain talk into motivation for change.
