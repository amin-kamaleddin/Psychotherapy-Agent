# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, creating a safe tone. It could be slightly more emotionally individualized, especially around the client’s fear of repeating family patterns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s ambivalence between safety and social connection. Some reflections could linger longer on the deeper emotional meaning before moving into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing risky drinking and improving contraceptive reliability. The conversation is purposeful, consistent, and action-oriented without significant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences influence the plan, but the psychiatrist often supplies the tactics and language rather than fully co-creating them. This is reflected in the client rejecting a therapist-generated script as too confrontational.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are good autonomy-supporting moves early on, such as asking what feels doable and inviting choice. The later parts become more directive with scripts, steps, and suggested rewards, which can reduce the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses rulers and commitment questions effectively, and the client’s confidence increases from 4 to 7. However, the therapist often shifts too quickly into advising and scripting rather than evoking the client’s own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to contribute and her ideas are used, but the psychiatrist frequently positions themselves as the source of practical strategies. This makes the interaction feel somewhat expert-driven rather than fully collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through planning questions, rulers, and commitment language. Stronger opportunities are missed when the therapist supplies options and scripts instead of amplifying the client’s own expressed reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well, with normalization and acknowledgment rather than argument. It could be processed more fully using double-sided reflections before pivoting to practical tactics.
