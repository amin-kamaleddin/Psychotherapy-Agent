# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed the client’s concerns without blame or disapproval. The warmth was somewhat clinical and could have included more individualized emotional support.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged the client’s caution, but often responded to strong fear or overwhelm with practical information instead of reflective listening. Several opportunities were missed to validate or deepen the emotional meaning of the client’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the safe medication transition and ended with a clear, actionable plan. The final responses were somewhat repetitive, which slightly reduced the efficiency of the concluding direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client concerns genuinely shaped the phone script and question list, showing real collaboration. The interaction remained provider-driven in format, with long lists and few checks to see what the client thought.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected the client’s choices and offered options rather than imposing them, including support for seeking clarification or a second opinion. The directive to call the prescriber immediately was safety-appropriate but slightly reduced the autonomy-supportive framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The approach was primarily informational, with only occasional invitations to explore the client’s own motivations. More open, value-based questions could have helped her articulate why this transition matters personally.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and co-created scripts, but largely maintained the expert role. The client was not consistently positioned as the expert on her own body, preferences, and risk tolerance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generated change talk and the psychiatrist affirmed it, but did not consistently ask questions to expand or deepen it. Affirmations were helpful but somewhat repetitive, and the client’s values were not systematically evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled respectfully by validating concerns and reframing them into agency, such as “safety over politeness.” More classic MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, could have further softened resistance.
