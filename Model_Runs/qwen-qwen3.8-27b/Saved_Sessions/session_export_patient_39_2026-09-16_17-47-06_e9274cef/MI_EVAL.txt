### Acceptance

**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance and affirms Elias’s physical limits and choices. Not a 5 because some moments could more explicitly affirm his worth independent of whether he succeeds.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflections of Elias’s ambivalence, body concerns, and fear of failure. Could deepen by exploring emotional undercurrents such as shame, discouragement, and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, logical focus on realistic physical activity and a concrete weekly plan. Less complete because the broader referral goal of medical adherence was not fully integrated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs a personalized plan based on Elias’s own conditions, cues, and safety needs. Collaboration could be stronger with more explicit joint decision-making and prioritization.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports Elias’s choice, ownership, and right to stop without pressure or direct advice. Excellent in helping him define the action on his own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Elias’s own reasons, values, and self-defined small step. Could be enhanced by connecting the action to broader identity, independence, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions Elias as the expert on his body and uses his ideas to shape the session. Could be stronger by adding more explicit agenda negotiation and priority-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions elicit concrete change talk such as “just stand up,” “one minute,” and “I’ll give it a shot.” Could be stronger by amplifying or summarizing the change talk to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent at reframing limitations, skepticism, and all-or-nothing thinking as safety and self-protection. Avoids argument and converts resistance into conditions for possible action.
