# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, validating stance while avoiding judgment. Some evaluative language (“wise,” “brave”) kept it from being fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate empathic reflections were used, especially around ambivalence. Emotional depth around breakup, loneliness, shame, and fear was not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, organized focus on reducing daily drinking and a 15-minute delay experiment. More procedural than motivational, with limited ties to broader goals or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan using client preferences and collaborative language. Counselor remained the primary source of intervention options, limiting full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on client choice and control throughout the session. Slightly directive early when offering a “small experiment” before fully eliciting client ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions were used, but the process was more suggestive than client-driven. Intrinsic motivation was not deeply elicited or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with frequent client choice and shared language. Counselor still led the structure and generated most behavioral options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was present but underdeveloped and not consistently amplified. Lacked deeper exploration of values, importance, or personal meaning behind change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not challenged or dismissed. Missed opportunities to use reflections or pivots to help resolve ambivalence toward change.
