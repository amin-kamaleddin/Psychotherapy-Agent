# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Alessia’s need for accurate safety information. Acceptance is strong but remains somewhat task-focused rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician provides accurate reflective listening that tracks Alessia’s concerns, values, and plan-building. Empathy could be deeper by explicitly exploring fear of harm, loss of control, or worry about sounding demanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the central safety question and avoids major topic drift. Direction is weakened by redundancy and a lack of clear consolidation into a collaborative plan or commitment statement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Alessia’s perspective and builds on her ideas, supporting her in formulating her own plan. Collaboration is uneven because the clinician still provides the key safety point and repeats a question after it has already been answered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Alessia’s ownership by asking how she would phrase the question, what would be helpful, and what would make it easiest to act. Autonomy support is strong but slightly weakened by mild urgency around acting today.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses evocative questions to draw out Alessia’s practical plans and safety concerns. Evocation remains largely logistical and does not deeply explore her underlying values, confidence, or motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Alessia as the expert on her goals and invites her to lead in planning. Partnership is strong, though the interaction remains somewhat clinician-guided when introducing key content and repeating a question.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk about Alessia’s plan to call the pharmacist and prepare her question. The clinician does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk, such as by exploring confidence or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Alessia’s caution and offers helpful reframes about taking safety seriously. Sustain talk is not fully softened because the session remains centered on uncertainty without more skillful pivot toward change.
