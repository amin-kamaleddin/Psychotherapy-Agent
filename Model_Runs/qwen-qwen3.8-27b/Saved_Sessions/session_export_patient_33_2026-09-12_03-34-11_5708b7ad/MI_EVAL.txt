# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and supports harm reduction without shaming. Acceptance is appropriate but somewhat procedural rather than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers basic reflections and captures key themes, but often responds to emotionally meaningful material with practical next steps. Empathy is present but inconsistent and not deeply nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on safer heroin use and concrete harm-reduction steps. The psychiatrist consistently steers the conversation toward actionable, target-specific plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to patient preferences, creating a generally cooperative interaction. However, the plan is often supplied by the clinician rather than consistently co-constructed with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers choices, and avoids pressuring the patient toward abstinence. Autonomy support is strong but could be more explicit about the patient’s freedom to choose each step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with helpful open questions to draw out goals and past strategies, but the session quickly becomes informational and logistical. Evocation is not sustained or deep enough.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and adapts to the patient’s stated preferences. The partnership is somewhat uneven because the clinician shifts into an expert-information mode at key points.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces the patient’s practical planning and asks what feels doable, which elicits some change talk. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing against continued use and validates the patient’s readiness for harm reduction. Softening is effective but more passive than actively reducing the power of sustain talk.
