# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides respectful, affirming statements such as “that’s overwhelming,” “good plan,” and “you’re doing great.” Warmth is supportive but somewhat procedural, without deeper individualized validation of her emotional load.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Includes a few reflective statements about stress and the rehearsal, but mostly moves quickly into scripts and problem-solving. Key emotional themes such as fear of sounding transactional, job loss, and feeling “stuck in my head” are underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** After the initial question, maintains clear focus on the most urgent target: securing Friday childcare. The session stays concrete and task-focused, though it connects less to broader goals such as stress reduction or substance use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela participates in choosing the neighbor, editing the message, and selecting a coworker text. However, the psychiatrist supplies most of the structure, options, scripts, and backup plan rather than co-generating the session from her priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks about urgency, rehearsal, and message choices while accepting Daniela’s edits without overriding her. Frequent directive coaching and prescriptive scripts still limit a stronger message of her full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on providing solutions, scripts, and recommendations rather than evoking Daniela’s own motivations, values, or confidence. There are limited evocative questions or scaling, and strong client change talk is not consistently reflected or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Daniela makes meaningful choices, but the psychiatrist remains the primary expert supplying scripts, options, and the plan. A stronger MI stance would have invited her to identify her own options and language more fully.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela offers several confident, change-oriented statements and is affirmed with comments such as “clear and solid” and “you’re doing great.” More targeted evocative questions would have helped deepen and expand her agency language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela’s barriers are acknowledged and normalized rather than challenged, including examples such as “offering dinner isn’t taking advantage.” The sustain talk is mostly redirected into problem-solving without fuller double-sided reflection or ambivalence work.
