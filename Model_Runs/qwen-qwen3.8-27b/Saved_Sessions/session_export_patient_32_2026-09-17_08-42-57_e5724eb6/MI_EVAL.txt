# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental, affirming language and normalizes ambivalence without moralizing about the DUI or drinking. Some affirmations are generic or over-affirming, which can feel therapist-driven rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, complex reflections of Derrick's ambivalence, social pain, and fear of isolation. Some reflections are interpretive or slightly leading, and the patient's 'psychoanalyzing' comment is not directly reflected or repaired.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on avoiding another DUI, managing drinking with friends, and protecting license and work. It could be stronger with clearer agenda-setting, a clearer summary of the target behavior, and a consolidated next step before ending.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Derrick to design his own plan and explore what would work in a realistic Friday night scenario. Repeated reflective summaries and layered therapist statements sometimes reduce the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Derrick's sense of choice, respects that he does not want to quit cold turkey, and avoids direct advice or pressure. Some statements assign meaning or commitment to Derrick, which can subtly steer him rather than leave choice fully open.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently draws out Derrick's own motivations, values, and strategies rather than giving reasons for change. Some questions are leading, and affirmations sometimes substitute for deeper evocation of readiness, confidence, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly frames the session as collaborative and allows Derrick's ideas to shape the conversation and plan. Occasional interpretive assertions or over-affirmation make the interaction slightly more therapist-driven than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change talk through open questions and amplifies Derrick's own desire, ability, reasons, and need. The score is not higher because the strongest change talk is not consistently deepened, consolidated, or developed with scaling/readiness questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue or moralize against Derrick's sustain talk and acknowledges the importance of friendships and peer pressure. He could be stronger by using double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic minimization, and by reducing affirmation of the 'one beer' boundary in a way that may inadvertently sustain drinking.
