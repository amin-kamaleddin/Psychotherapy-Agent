# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and genuine unconditional positive regard throughout, with explicit statements rejecting judgment and labeling. No moment of disapproval crept in, even when the client minimized or discussed blackouts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and nuanced perspective-taking, including textbook double-sided reflections that validate both minimization and underlying discomfort. Consistently mirrors the client's language and framing, demonstrating deep attunement to his internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a logical arc from rapport-building through consequences to pattern recognition. When the client broadens the topic, the psychiatrist acknowledges it and gently returns to the target behavior without losing momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session structured as a genuine joint exploration rather than an interview; the client's responses consistently shape direction. The psychiatrist actively seeks input before moving forward and incorporates new information rather than redirecting away from it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports the client's right to choose from the opening statement through the end, never creating obligation, guilt, or implying the client should stop drinking. The framing is always exploratory rather than directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently uses evocative questions to draw out the client's own motivations without providing external reasons for change. Could have gone further in connecting to deeper values and long-term goals to create stronger discrepancy between current behavior and stated identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom about change reside within the client, using language like 'I think you know yourself better than any of us do.' The psychiatrist never assumes an expert role, gives advice, or positions themselves as the authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions effectively elicit the client's own reasons for potential change, including physical consequences and the unsatisfying pattern of 'next time it's the same thing.' Could have more explicitly reinforced and amplified the change talk that emerged or explored the client's confidence in making change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk through accurate reflections and double-sided responses that honor the client's perspective without reinforcing minimization. Could have more explicitly explored the disconnect between the client's stated values (responsibility, being a 'decent kid') and his behavior as a discrepancy-based approach.
