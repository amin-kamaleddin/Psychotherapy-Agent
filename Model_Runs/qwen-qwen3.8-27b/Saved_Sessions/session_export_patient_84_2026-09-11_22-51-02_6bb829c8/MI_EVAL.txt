# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the patient's frustration. However, repeated structured choices and later safety suggestions slightly undercut warmth and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor uses accurate reflections to capture the patient's frustration and desire to be trusted. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and formulaic because reflections are quickly followed by menus of options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to the referral concern and related family/peer issues. However, the counselor shifts among multiple agendas, making the direction feel scattered and reducing momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration through invitations for input and willingness to listen. Nevertheless, the counselor remains the agenda-setter by repeatedly offering predefined options rather than building from the patient's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor affirms the patient's choice and avoids direct lecturing. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated multiple-choice prompts and safety suggestions that can feel subtly pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor makes limited evocative efforts, mostly through an initial open question and brief reflections. There is little strategic use of values, importance, confidence, or future-focused questions to elicit the patient's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor shows partnership by inviting preferences and agreeing to keep things simple. The repeated use of numbered choices makes the interaction feel more counselor-managed than genuinely co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers some mild change talk, but the counselor does not deliberately amplify or develop it. Predefined options and safety suggestions limit opportunities for the patient to generate his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor avoids arguing with the patient's sustain talk and acknowledges his perspective. However, the counselor does not effectively soften or redirect sustain talk using double-sided reflections, discrepancy, or values-based questioning.
