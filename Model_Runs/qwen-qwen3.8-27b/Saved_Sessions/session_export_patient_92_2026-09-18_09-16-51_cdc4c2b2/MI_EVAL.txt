# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently respectful, non-judgmental stance throughout, normalizing Marek's experience without pushing back when he is defensive. However, the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic rather than deeply personal, lacking moments of affirming Marek as a person beyond his drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with multiple complex reflections that accurately capture Marek's emotional and cognitive shifts, including a particularly powerful final reflection addressing multiple layers of ambivalence. The score is held at 4 because some reflections are slightly repetitive in structure and the fear of social exclusion is named but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, consistent focus on Marek's drinking with a logical progression from defensiveness to exploring his comparison framework to actionable thinking. A brief mid-session summary or agenda-check would have strengthened alignment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language, follows Marek's leads, and adjusts tone to his requests (e.g., 'keep it real with me'). However, the session remains somewhat therapist-guided without a moment of explicit co-creation, such as negotiating the agenda or constructing a next step together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — the psychiatrist consistently returns the decision to Marek, never pressures or imposes, and frames the conversation as protecting his freedom rather than limiting it. The closing question, which asks what change might look like for him without implying he must change, is a standout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits Marek's own motivations and language rather than providing external arguments, and skillfully highlights his emerging shifts. The evocation could go deeper by exploring values (what the Friday nights mean beyond the drinking) or using a confidence ruler to further develop change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the session as a collaborative exploration, offers choices for direction, and uses open, curious language throughout. The partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly positioning Marek as the expert on his own life and by co-creating a clear plan or next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions and reflections to draw out Marek's own shift from dismissal to curiosity, with a direct and effective naming of his healthy stance. The final question successfully elicits Marek's own idea of what change might look like (three instead of six).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never argues against Marek's reasons for maintaining the status quo, instead reflecting and validating his perspective while gently noting the competing change talk. The double-sided reflection on 'cross that bridge' alongside the floated idea of cutting back is a strong example of this skill.
