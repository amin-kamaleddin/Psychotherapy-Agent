# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment are evident, especially in validating the patient's fear and goal of weight loss. Acceptance is slightly limited by a procedural, problem-solving style that sometimes moves quickly past deeper emotional complexity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflective listening and captures the patient's ambivalence about tracking, control, and fear of weight gain. Empathy is strong but often cognitive, with limited deeper exploration of shame, grief, or cultural pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on moving toward a more sustainable, non-punishing approach to health, activity, and nutrition. The main limitation is narrowing toward scale and waist management rather than consistently tying the plan back to broader values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by incorporating the patient's preferred weigh-ins, waist measure, squat progress, and support person. Collaboration is strong but occurs within a clinician-provided structure rather than equal co-creation of meaning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through choice-based language, reversible experiment framing, and invitation to select or modify plan components. The score is not a 5 because the provided scripts, stop rules, and structured framework remain somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client-generated motivation through questions about feeling better, safer first steps, and preferred signals. Evocation is present but often interrupted by explanations, options, and problem-solving rather than deeper values-based elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient is invited to contribute and shapes key elements of the plan, including weigh-ins, tracking, and support contacts. Partnership is limited because the psychiatrist often takes an expert problem-solving role by providing structured frameworks and decision rules.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient produces useful change talk about strength gains, sustainability, and trying the experiment. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on this change talk to strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk respectfully but does not sufficiently reduce the focus on weight-monitoring concerns such as scale jumps, waist measurements, and stop rules. The session builds a detailed protocol around the very metrics that may maintain diet-related distress.
