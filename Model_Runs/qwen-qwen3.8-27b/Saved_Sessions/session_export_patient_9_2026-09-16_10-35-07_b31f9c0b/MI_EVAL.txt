# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout the session. While warm and supportive, acceptance does not consistently include deeper affirmations of Amina’s strengths or parenting intentions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects several important feelings, such as worry, fear of failure, and not wanting to be the “bad guy.” However, reflections often remain surface-level and the session quickly moves into advice or problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused and effectively narrows the issue from broad co-parenting conflict to screen time, sleep, and a manageable bedtime target. The direction is clear, though the method becomes somewhat advice-driven rather than fully MI-congruent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and responsiveness to Amina’s preferences, such as allowing her to start the rule at home. However, the psychiatrist frequently acts as the primary solution-giver rather than fully co-creating the plan with Amina.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids harsh pressure, which supports Amina’s autonomy to some degree. Yet frequent scripts, routines, and prescriptive steps can make the session feel directive and reduce Amina’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the most limited area, with several missed opportunities to draw out Amina’s values, confidence, and internal motivation. The psychiatrist often supplies reasons and plans instead of eliciting Amina’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist establishes some partnership by inviting Amina to choose a focus and offering options. Over time, however, the interaction becomes more expert-driven, with Amina selecting from prepackaged solutions rather than leading the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina expresses clear change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently cultivate or deepen it. The session shifts quickly into advice and behavioral planning, limiting opportunities to amplify Amina’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without judgment, and some of Amina’s doubts are normalized in a supportive way. However, responses often lean toward problem-solving rather than using MI techniques to reframe or work through ambivalence.
