# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing with Brandon. Warmth is generally present but could be more individually affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture some of Brandon’s frustration and stress, but remain surface-level. The clinician could more accurately convey the emotional weight of feeling monitored and judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete, low-effort behavior tied to drinking. It would be stronger if the action were more clearly linked to Brandon’s broader personal goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers choices, adapts to Brandon’s preferences, and lets his input shape the session. The approach could be less therapist-driven by exploring Brandon’s ideas before suggesting strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with repeated emphasis on choice, no pressure, and self-directed experimentation. Brandon’s rejections are accepted without pushing or controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some of Brandon’s own thoughts and useful change language. However, there is less consistent follow-up to draw out deeper motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Brandon’s input and allows him to shape the direction of the session. A stronger partnership would more explicitly position Brandon as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician affirms some change-relevant statements and uses open-ended questions. There is room to more systematically reflect and amplify Brandon’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with or moralizing against Brandon’s sustain talk. The session could use more MI-specific responses, such as double-sided reflections, to work with ambivalence.
