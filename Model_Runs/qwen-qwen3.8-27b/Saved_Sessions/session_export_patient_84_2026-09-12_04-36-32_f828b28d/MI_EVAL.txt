# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or lecturing. However, the acceptance feels somewhat procedural and does not deeply affirm Malik’s emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at a surface level, capturing frustration, skepticism, and the desire to stop the yelling. Deeper emotional and meaning-focused themes, such as fairness, autonomy, and isolation, are often missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session frequently shifts from cannabis use to conflict management, de-escalation, and avoiding detection. There is limited sustained focus on the target behavior and its consequences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Malik is given some choices, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the agenda, scripts, and problem-solving content. The interaction is uneven and remains largely counselor-led rather than truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are verbal acknowledgments of Malik’s choice, but the counselor quickly moves into directives, scripts, and structured options. This undermines genuine autonomy support by shifting decision-making away from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions are useful, but evocation is weak and quickly replaced by counselor-generated advice. The session does not effectively elicit Malik’s own reasons, values, or motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist states that Malik is in charge, but the session becomes directive and expert-driven. Collaboration is partial because Malik’s expertise about his own life is not consistently centered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few early openings that could elicit change talk, but the counselor does not effectively notice, reflect, or amplify Malik’s own motivations. Change talk remains minimal and underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently reinforces the status quo by validating avoidance, concealment, and conflict management rather than exploring consequences. This strengthens sustain talk instead of softening it.
