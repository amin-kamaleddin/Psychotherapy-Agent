# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes Amira’s distress without criticizing her drug use. However, some responses feel formulaic and do not fully separate her worth from her behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflections that capture Amira’s loneliness, regret, and ambivalence. Empathy could improve by staying longer in the emotional pain before moving toward problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on helplessness and support, but it does not clearly connect the discussion to the target behavior of reducing drug use. A clearer summary linking support, regret, and change goals would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and builds on Amira’s own idea of a low-pressure check-in. However, the agenda remains therapist-led, limiting fuller co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is strongly emphasized through statements such as “There’s no pressure here” and “You know yourself best.” Repeated prompts to identify a next step may subtly pressure a passive, hopeless client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits Amira’s own ideas and avoids giving advice or external reasons for change. The evocation is somewhat repetitive and does not consistently explore deeper values, hopes, or identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira is positioned as the expert and invited to design her own support plan. The interaction remains more guided than mutually constructed, especially in the rapid sequence of questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician generates some practical change talk around low-pressure support and affirms useful client language. However, the session does not consistently amplify deeper motivations, confidence, or commitment to reduce drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Amira’s sustaining statements and offers some normalizing reframes. More strategic moves are needed to weaken the all-or-nothing sustain talk and redirect toward agency or exceptions.
