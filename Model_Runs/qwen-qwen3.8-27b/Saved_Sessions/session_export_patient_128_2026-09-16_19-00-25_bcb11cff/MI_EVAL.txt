# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows nonjudgmental respect and positive regard, normalizing Tyler’s ambivalence and stigma concerns. The stance remains warm, validating, and supportive without labeling or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Tyler’s mixed motives and practical concerns. Empathy could deepen further by explicitly reflecting underlying emotions such as shame, fear, and stigma.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safer social drinking and moves logically toward a concrete plan. It could be stronger by periodically connecting the plan to broader goals, such as avoiding future conduct issues and protecting what matters to Tyler.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works collaboratively and co-constructs the plan with Tyler. A few moments show slight counselor-driven shaping of the plan before fully eliciting the client’s own wording and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice and ownership by repeatedly inviting Tyler to decide, prioritize, and define what would work. Language is suggestive rather than controlling, and controllable pieces are identified without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is largely drawn from Tyler through open questions about safety, confidence, and what would count as enough. The counselor could evoke more consistently by waiting for Tyler to generate more of the plan language before offering specific examples.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance and lets Tyler’s ideas shape the conversation. Some moments show slight counselor-led formulation of the plan, which reduces fully client-generated collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, forward-looking questions effectively elicit change talk about safer plans, avoiding police involvement, and maintaining friendship. The counselor could more actively amplify and expand Tyler’s change talk to deepen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by reframing concerns as practical planning rather than obstacles. Stronger double-sided reflections could further soften lingering stigma and fear of being seen as “broken” or “an alcoholic.”
