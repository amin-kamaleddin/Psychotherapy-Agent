# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and nonjudgment, especially when Marcus resists focus on drinking. Occasional normative framing about health and avoiding trouble makes the stance slightly less purely accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections about peer inclusion, social identity, and fear of being left out. Some reflections become repetitive and do not fully explore underlying shame, exclusion, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains relevant to alcohol use, peer pressure, and parental concerns. However, it lacks a clear target behavior or agenda, leaving the therapeutic direction somewhat diffuse.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and Marcus's own language to create a collaborative tone. The collaboration is weakened slightly by an implicit steering toward change and health-related goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly respects Marcus's autonomy, including his request not to focus on drinking. Mild value-based framing around staying healthy and out of trouble slightly limits full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions elicit Marcus's perspective but often invite description of the current social reality rather than deeper change motivation. The psychiatrist misses clear opportunities to build on Marcus's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is positioned as the expert on his own social life, and the conversation feels collaborative rather than instructive. The agenda is not fully co-created, with the therapist still subtly guiding toward change topics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offers limited change talk, such as not wanting trouble and knowing when he has had enough. The psychiatrist does not effectively amplify, reflect, or scale these statements to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges it in a non-confrontational way. However, the clinician accepts the status quo too readily and does not consistently pivot toward values, exceptions, or consequences.
