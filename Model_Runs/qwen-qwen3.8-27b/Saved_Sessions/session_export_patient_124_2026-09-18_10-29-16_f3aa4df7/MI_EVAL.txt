# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, never correcting or pushing back when Sofia minimizes her use. Every response validates Sofia's experience without conditional approval, including strong developmental honor with 'You're seventeen and you deserve your space.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening with sophisticated emotional attunement, including capturing the somatic experience of 'tight' and naming the unspoken isolation of feeling like 'everyone else has it figured out.' Reflections evolve with Sofia's emotional state and track subtle language shifts like 'maybe' versus 'definitely.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear and consistent focus on marijuana use in the context of anxiety with a logical progression from rapport through exploring ambivalence to eliciting change talk. The session ends somewhat open-endedly; a slightly more purposeful closing with a small collaborative action step or follow-up point would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine joint exploration rather than an assessment, with the psychiatrist consistently building on Sofia's contributions and allowing her ideas to shape the conversation. Language throughout implies shared work ('I'm curious about,' 'what would feel doable to you') without imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support from the opening ('this is your space') through explicit rejection of authority ('Not some rule I'm giving you') to validating Sofia's choice to 'keep doing what I'm doing for now' as legitimate. The psychiatrist never pressures, implies obligation, or uses coercive language at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation throughout with questions that draw out Sofia's own motivations and language rather than providing reasons for change, resulting in a genuine shift from 'stopping is not on the table' to 'maybe cut back a little.' The evocation could have been slightly more sustained in the final minutes to deepen Sofia's engagement with the idea of a small step.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within Sofia, explicitly attributing insights to her own agency ('You figured that out yourself. That's not nothing'). The psychiatrist never offers advice, presents risk information, or positions themselves as having knowledge Sofia lacks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully cultivates change talk through strategic questioning and reflection, leading Sofia to generate genuine change language about not wanting to be 'the one who can't handle anything.' The session could have pushed slightly further into confidence talk or exploring specific steps, though this was appropriately calibrated to Sofia's contemplation stage.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every instance of sustain talk is met with understanding and gently reframed through the client's own values without arguing, countering, or presenting evidence. The use of double-sided reflections (e.g., honoring both 'it's not that bad' and 'I don't want to be the one who can't handle stress on their own') is particularly effective.
