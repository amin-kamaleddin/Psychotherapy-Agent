# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment are conveyed, though some phrases feel formulaic. Crisis/safety language is prudent but sounds generic and less individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture Milan’s surface perspective, but some interpretations are inferred rather than stated. The counselor does not fully explore the emotional meaning behind his comments.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on alcohol use and perceived control. It occasionally circles risk and safety themes instead of selecting a clearer mutually agreed focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options rather than imposing advice. Repeated risk and safety topics make the interaction feel somewhat less like a fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports Milan’s freedom to choose and avoids pressure or ultimatums. Milan’s choice to table the issue clearly shapes the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks Milan to share his perspective, but the exploration remains superficial. Deeper work on values, future goals, or personal motivation is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Milan’s input. The session is still somewhat counselor-led because risk, safety, and goal-related topics are introduced frequently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative questions, but they are infrequent and not well developed. The counselor does not use rulers, values exploration, or reinforcement of emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Milan, which is positive. However, sustain talk is often met with neutral acceptance, and repeated risk language may increase defensiveness.
