# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveyed respect, validation, and a nonjudgmental stance. Some moments felt more logistical than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were adequate reflections of frustration, loneliness, anger, and concern. Deeper fears, ambivalence, and underlying meanings were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed on the practical issue of contacting his mother. It was not consistently guided toward a broader behavior-change or values-based goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options and responded to Tariq's feedback. The counseling remained noticeably expert-driven in problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and preferences were asked. Prescriptive scripts and strategies reduced the sense that Tariq owned the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were limited attempts to draw out Tariq's own motivations and values. The counselor often supplied reasons, options, and scripts instead of eliciting change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and adjusted to Tariq's preferences. The balance of expertise remained tilted toward the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq offered meaningful expressions of hope, responsibility, and family motivation. These were not consistently reflected, expanded, or amplified into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with Tariq, but sustain talk was often acknowledged without skillful redirection. There was limited use of ambivalence-focused or double-sided reflections.
