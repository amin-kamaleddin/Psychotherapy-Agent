# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s social pressure and experience. It is not a 5 because explicit unconditional positive regard and normalization are somewhat limited, and some reflections are mildly interpretive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate, nuanced, and emotionally attuned, capturing underlying values, body sensations, and ambivalence. These reflections consistently deepen the client’s self-exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on alcohol-related behavior, social pressure, and the meaning behind holding a drink. It would be stronger with a clearer link to a concrete change goal or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician builds on the client’s ideas in a joint, exploratory manner rather than giving an expert lecture. More explicit “we” language or a co-created next step would make collaboration more visible.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids pressure and advice, inviting the client to generate her own options and strategies. More explicit reinforcement of client ownership would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicits the client’s own motivations rather than imposing external reasons to change. More systematic use of importance/confidence scaling or commitment questions would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert, with open questions inviting her perspective and shaping the conversation. Limited explicit power-sharing or agenda-setting keeps this from a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician evokes the client’s own goals, values, and reasons for change through values-based questions and reflections. It could be stronger with scaling, commitment language, or amplification of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized rather than corrected or argued with. A more explicit double-sided reflection or strategic pivot would balance sustain talk more effectively.
