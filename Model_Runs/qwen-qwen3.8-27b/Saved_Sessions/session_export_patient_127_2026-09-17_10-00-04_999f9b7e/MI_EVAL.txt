# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and uses open questions, but does not consistently express unconditional positive regard. Acceptance is present but not always explicitly warm or affirming of the client’s worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client’s ambivalence around stability, respect, and income. Empathy is adequate but does not fully capture underlying shame, self-criticism, or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete, manageable step related to employment and relapse risk. Direction is clear but could be more explicitly tied to the broader goal of reducing recidivism.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works around the client’s ideas, such as the warehousing ad and weekend concerns. Collaboration is evident but could be more explicitly framed as a joint process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasizes choice and small, self-selected steps without pressuring the client. Autonomy support is strong but could be reinforced with more explicit language about the client’s freedom to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions draw out the client’s own ideas and concerns. Evocation is present but remains somewhat surface-level and does not deeply link the client’s words to values or motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert and avoids lecturing or prescribing a plan. Partnership is strong but not always explicit in language about shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist prompts the client to generate possible steps and change-oriented ideas. However, the client’s change talk is not strongly amplified or summarized to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s self-critical statements but does not consistently soften or reflect the underlying fear. Sustain talk remains underexplored and not fully MI-consistently redirected.
