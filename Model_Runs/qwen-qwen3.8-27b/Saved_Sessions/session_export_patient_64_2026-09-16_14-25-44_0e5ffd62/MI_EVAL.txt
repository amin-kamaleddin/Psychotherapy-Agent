# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, normalizing the client's ambivalence and honoring his desire for privacy and autonomy. Acceptance is strong but somewhat summary-like and could more deeply individualize the client's emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's two-sided perspective and concern about not being pushed. Empathy remains mostly content-level and does not fully explore or validate the emotional weight of pressure, guilt, or family responsibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior, the flu vaccine decision, and moves purposefully from ambivalence to a concrete plan. There is no drift into unrelated topics, and the direction supports MI-consistent progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and allows his preferences to shape the conversation and final plan. Collaboration is strong but could be enhanced with more explicit shared problem-solving language and co-construction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's sense of choice and avoids pressuring him to decide. He explicitly honors the client's request to keep things low-key and leaves the next steps entirely with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's own motivations, especially protecting his grandmother and choosing the decision on his own terms. Deeper exploration of importance, confidence, and values would further strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a collaborative, non-expert stance and treats the client as the primary decision-maker. The partnership could be stronger with more explicit recognition of the client's expertise in his own life and shared agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, change-oriented questions and reflects the client's emerging reasons, helping him articulate a self-generated plan. Change talk is effective but could be deepened by more explicit exploration of importance, confidence, and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client's sustain talk and uses a helpful double-sided reflection. However, he does not consistently soften or redirect the repeated claim that he does not personally need the vaccine.
