# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment toward Margaret's values, routine, and ambivalence. The tone validates her independence and lived experience without pressure to change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective statements accurately capture Margaret's perspective, ambivalence, and the meaning of the pub and wine. However, the clinician could go deeper into underlying emotions such as fear, embarrassment, and loss of safety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing alcohol use while staying responsive to Margaret's concerns. The conversation moves purposefully from presenting worries to practical, client-driven steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist engages as an equal partner and invites Margaret's perspective throughout the session. The final plan emerges from her own ideas and preferred style of change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Margaret's right to choose is repeatedly affirmed, and the clinician avoids controlling or directive language. Change is framed as something Margaret decides on her own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively elicit Margaret's own reasons, values, and emerging willingness to try small changes. The session could be strengthened by more systematic evocation of importance, confidence, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Margaret as the expert on her own life and routine. Power is shared, and the session is shaped by her input rather than by professional expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and reflections help Margaret voice possible changes, such as swapping drinks or trying one alcohol-free day. The session could use more MI-specific techniques to expand and deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without argument, especially when Margaret resists giving up wine entirely. Some moments could more clearly reconnect her concerns to her broader values and future goals.
