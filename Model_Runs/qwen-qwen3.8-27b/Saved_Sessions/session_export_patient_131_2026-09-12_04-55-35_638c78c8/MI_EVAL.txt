# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client’s strengths and progress. Some affirmations feel slightly formulaic rather than deeply attuned to the client’s emotional struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence, need for autonomy, and fear of being judged. Reflections could deepen further by staying longer with the emotional weight of shame, separation, and the desire to prove himself as a father.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on drinking, Stopping Violence, and reunification by narrowing broad concerns into practical steps. It would be stronger if each planning step were more explicitly connected to the broader goals of reducing alcohol use, preventing violence, and rebuilding family trust.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses the client’s own language and preferences to co-create scripts, boundaries, and exit lines. The frequent use of pre-formed options and menus slightly limits the sense that the client is the primary source of ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports the client’s sense of choice by offering options rather than directives and adapting when the client resists writing. Stronger autonomy support would more explicitly affirm that the client is ultimately in charge of whether and how he acts on any plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some motivation by asking about the smallest step and by inviting the client to choose language that feels authentic. More evocative follow-ups about values, confidence, and the meaning of being a “solid” father would deepen the client’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client’s input and builds the conversation around the client’s stated non-negotiables. The frequent provision of structured menus and phrases creates a slightly counselor-driven dynamic rather than fully client-led exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits concrete change talk, especially around staying with Stopping Violence and reducing drinking. These statements are not consistently amplified through reflections or questions about importance, ability, and need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids challenging the client’s fallback plans and does not reinforce reasons to remain in the status quo. There are missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections to help the client explore the tension between independence and accountability.
