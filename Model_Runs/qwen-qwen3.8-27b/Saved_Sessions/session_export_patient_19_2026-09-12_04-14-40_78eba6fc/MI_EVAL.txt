# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and supportive, validating the client's ambivalence and self-doubt. A 5 would require slightly deeper, more individualized acceptance of shame and social-identity concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the mixed pull of excitement, shame, debt, and hope, including the loss of the pokies buzz. Empathy could be deeper by fully exploring fear of social exclusion, identity loss, and the pain behind self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing gambling, managing debt, and coping with urges, linking steps to the referral concerns. It occasionally becomes overly procedural during app setup and coping options, narrowing the motivational focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choices shape the plan, such as the app, one-week trial, walking, music, and privacy boundaries. Frequent provision of practical options and instructions creates a subtly expert-driven tone.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes freedom, non-coercion, and client control, respecting refusal to surrender cards and the one-week experiment. This is close to exemplary autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist evokes some internal motivation by exploring values, goals, and concerns, such as Egypt, trust, and stability. More skillful evocation would continue drawing out the meaning, self-efficacy, and consequences of those goals rather than shifting to practical advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is invited to set agenda, preferences, and goals, and her expertise about her life substantially guides the work. For a 5, the therapist would rely more on client-generated planning and fewer counselor-offered menus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks values-oriented questions and affirms emerging change talk, but does not consistently develop, amplify, or summarize it. The move into practical problem-solving limits deeper cultivation of change motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing or minimizing sustain talk, responding with normalized, low-pressure reflections. More strategic MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections and linking sustain talk to values, would better soften ambivalence.
