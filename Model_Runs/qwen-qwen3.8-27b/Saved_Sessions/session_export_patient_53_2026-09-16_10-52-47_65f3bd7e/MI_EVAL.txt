# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, using normalizing statements to reduce shame. Acceptance was somewhat formulaic and could have more deeply validated underlying shame, fear of failure, or safety concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client's ambivalence, hesitation, and discomfort with pressure. The clinician often moved quickly from empathy into problem-solving rather than deepening the emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed generally connected to isolation and community engagement but narrowed to very micro-level options. There was no clear through-line to a specific, collaboratively defined target behavior or follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for the client's preferences and adapted when options felt pressuring. Collaboration was limited because many concrete options were clinician-generated and the final plan was not fully client-owned.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasized choice, no obligation, and no pressure while respecting the client's boundaries. Frequent prompts and new options subtly created an expectation to choose, preventing a higher rating.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asked appropriate evocative questions about meaningful activities, ease, and follow-through. Evocation was shallow because the clinician did not consistently deepen the values, hopes, or sense of ability behind the client's change-oriented statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed that the client is the expert and generally followed the client's lead rather than pressing. The interaction felt somewhat therapist-directed because the clinician repeatedly generated options and framed possible actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged around drawing, park walks, and possible cues, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen the client's own change-oriented language. The session remained dominated by doubt, with the small openings not converted into stronger desire, ability, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoided arguing with sustain talk but did not effectively pivot away from the client's repeated focus on past inaction and future uncertainty. The conversation remained centered on avoidance, with no skillful use of double-sided reflections, rulers, or future-oriented reframing.
