# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance and respected Anisa’s ambivalence throughout. Statements such as “I respect both sides” and “That doesn’t sound like an excuse” conveyed acceptance without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used accurate reflections that tracked Anisa’s conflict and emotions. Empathy could be stronger by more explicitly validating shame, fear, and the pain of hiding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking cessation and related behaviors, such as hiding smoking and creating realistic steps. The conversation moved consistently from ambivalence toward concrete, life-fitting actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician invited Anisa to co-create the plan and built directly on her ideas. The lighter-in-the-office barrier, pause, and home-honesty script were developed together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently emphasized Anisa’s choice, control, and expertise in her own life. No prescribed methods, quit dates, or obligatory framing were introduced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited Anisa’s own motivations and solutions rather than giving reasons to change. Evocation could be deeper by exploring her personal “why” and confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned Anisa as the authority on what was realistic and let her ideas shape the session. The interaction was genuinely guided by her values, constraints, and proposed steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively elicited and reinforced Anisa’s desire, need, and commitment to change. Change talk could be further amplified by explicitly strengthening her strongest statements about quitting and honesty.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician responded to ambivalence and sustain talk without argument, pressure, or dismissal. Softening could be improved by more explicitly normalizing the pull of the habit and the difficulty of quitting.
