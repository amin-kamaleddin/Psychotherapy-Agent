# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, including holding space for the deeply vulnerable statement 'I'm not a person people fight for' without fixing, minimizing, or rushing to repair. The psychiatrist validated Anisa's experience of being referred and normalized her reluctance to burden her daughter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Empathic responses were consistently accurate and went beyond surface-level reflection, naming unspoken meanings (e.g., identifying the value behind the 'gross' feeling) and reframing sustain talk as evidence of insight. The psychiatrist also accurately distilled Anisa's scattered expression into the clear summary: 'you don't need motivation, you need someone to know.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a remarkably tight, progressive focus from broad ambivalence to a specific actionable plan with minimal drift. Even when the emotionally charged statement about self-worth emerged, the psychiatrist honored it without detouring into a separate exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan component was generated by Anisa and reflected back as her own creation, with the psychiatrist acting purely as a facilitator. The session felt like two people thinking together rather than one person guiding another.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No coercion, urgency, or implied obligation was present throughout. The psychiatrist used conditional language, let Anisa sit with her own ambivalence, and framed all questions around Anisa's comfort and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A cascading series of open questions generated an entire action plan from the client without a single piece of external information or recommendation. Each question built on the previous answer, moving from abstract motivation to concrete action.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key question was phrased to invite Anisa's own knowledge, and the psychiatrist never provided expert information or recommendations. Language such as 'You built a plan with your own hands' explicitly attributed agency and expertise to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively evoked and affirmed through open questions and emphatic reflections, but the session did not explicitly elicit commitment language or confidence ratings. A 5 would require drawing out a small commitment statement or confidence rating before ending.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently validated while being gently redirected toward exception-finding, with the 'too small' concern reframed as evidence of strategic thinking rather than inadequacy. The psychiatrist never said 'But you can do it!' or 'Many people succeed!'—staying within Anisa's frame throughout.
