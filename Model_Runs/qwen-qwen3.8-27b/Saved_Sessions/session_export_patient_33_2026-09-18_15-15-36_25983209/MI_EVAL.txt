# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, non-judgmental tone throughout, normalizing Diego's experience and affirming his efforts without condescension. Occasionally reads slightly formulaic with repeated phrases like 'That's not nothing' without deepening emotional resonance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate double-sided reflections and emotional attunement (e.g., 'I can hear how much that gap hurts'). Occasionally stays at the content level rather than reaching the deepest emotional layer, such as when Diego says 'I'm not dying, I'm fine.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on harm reduction as the target behavior with logical progression from ambivalence to concrete actionable steps. Could benefit from tighter strategic pacing, perhaps with a brief collaborative summary before transitioning to the larger topic of being stuck.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as joint exploration, building on Diego's own ideas (his script, timing, values) rather than introducing external frameworks. Could be deeper by explicitly inviting Diego to rate his readiness or set the specific session goal rather than gently steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout—never pressures, advises, or imposes a timeline. Consistently reinforces Diego's ownership with language like 'what would make it feel like a safer moment' and validates his right to decide when and how to act.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Diego's own motivations and values through targeted questions without imposing external reasons for change. Could have pushed slightly further on confidence statements or explored what it would feel like to actually have the conversation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance with open questions that invite Diego to lead and position him as the expert on his own life. Slightly one-directional in that the psychiatrist does most of the reflective work while Diego remains primarily in a responsive role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through questions that elicit Diego's own values (e.g., 'What do you think those details say about what you're actually trying to do here?'). Could have more explicitly reinforced his readiness statement or amplified his specific commitment to the Tuesday coffee conversation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by holding both sides and normalizing the coexistence of wanting to be less stuck without wanting the specific change. Missed opportunity to more actively explore what the 'guilt' framing is protecting when Diego questions the point of harm reduction.
