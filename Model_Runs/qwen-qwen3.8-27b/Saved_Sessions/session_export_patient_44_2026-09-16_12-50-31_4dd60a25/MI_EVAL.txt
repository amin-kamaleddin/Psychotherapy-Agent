# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally respectful and affirming with nonjudgmental statements acknowledging Erik's progress and difficulty. Not a 5 because acceptance is somewhat procedural/praise-heavy and minimizes the emotional weight of Erik's discouragement before moving to planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate but inconsistent; captures practical goals but bypasses shame, self-criticism, and frustration. Quickly shifts to problem-solving without deeply reflecting Erik's emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains very clear, consistent focus on the specific target behavior (lunch prep and gym energy) throughout the session. Efficient progression from broad motivation to a concrete, measurable weekly plan with a strong directional ending.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with the text message and plan co-created based on Erik's preferences and wording choices. Not a 5 because the psychiatrist remains the primary organizer of content and offers many specific options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently seeks Erik's preferences, supports his boundaries on follow-up tone and frequency, and respects his self-determined limits. Not a 5 because multiple fairly specific coping suggestions are still offered before waiting for Erik to generate alternatives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Erik's own motivations and concrete plan elements through scaling and small-step questions. Not a 5 because suggestions (tea, backup sandwich, phone reminder) are supplied somewhat early before allowing Erik more space to self-generate ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers Erik repeated genuine choices on small steps, timing, backup options, and message wording. Not a 5 because the overall flow remains fairly clinician-led with numerous suggestions driving the session structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently and effectively evokes change talk, with Erik's confidence rising from 5 to 8 and generating concrete commitment statements. Not a 5 because change statements (reduced pain, not feeling wrecked) are only briefly acknowledged rather than amplified with fuller reflections or summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges barriers without arguing but repeatedly redirects to solutions too quickly, limiting exploration of Erik's reasons for maintaining the status quo. Would benefit from more double-sided reflections exploring the pull of sustain talk alongside his goals.
