# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with affirmations woven naturally into reflections. Never judges, labels, or minimizes the client's feelings, even when she says she 'shouldn't complain.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate complex reflections using and extending the client's own metaphors (door, catch-up). Precisely distinguishes between intact motivation and logistical barriers, and captures the paradox of what saved her also keeping her stuck.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with a logical progressive arc from identifying the barrier to arriving at a concrete first step. No topic drift; each exchange builds on the previous with increasing specificity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a strong collaborative stance through language like 'I'm curious' and 'Can we sit with both of those,' actively incorporating the client's language into reflections. However, collaboration remains more felt than declared, with no explicit agenda negotiation or overt team language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's perception of choice and ownership, never using directive language like 'you should' or 'you need to.' Respects her boundary when she frames the solution as staying in control, affirming rather than pushing further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key insights — the door metaphor, the 'alone' barrier, the 'I'll figure it out' pattern, and the smallest step solution — are generated by the client with the psychiatrist serving purely as a catalyst. Never provides reasons for change or offers solutions directly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the authority on her own life through open-ended questions and by attributing insights back to her. However, the partnership remains implicit rather than explicitly stated (e.g., no direct acknowledgment that she knows her situation best).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Makes consistent efforts to pick up and affirm change talk, including highlighting her statement that she doesn't want help and evoking the smallest step question. Misses an opportunity to amplify her early expressed confidence ('I still feel like I can do it') as a resource for further change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully with a textbook double-sided reflection that validates the risk while redirecting toward change. Could benefit from more creative pivoting (e.g., exploring worst-case vs. best-case scenarios) given the limited sustain talk present in the session.
