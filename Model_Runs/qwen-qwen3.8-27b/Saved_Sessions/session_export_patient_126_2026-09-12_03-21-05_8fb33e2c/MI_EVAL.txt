# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and avoids judgment, but the tone is procedural rather than warmly affirming. Validation of the client's vulnerability, values, or emotional experience is limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are mostly factual and surface-level, capturing behaviors rather than underlying feelings or ambivalence. The counselor does not deeply explore the personal meaning behind the client's minimization or reassurance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally on lab results, smoking history, and alcohol use, preventing complete drift. However, direction is inconsistent, shifting among lab explanations, risk reduction, and test-preparation advice rather than maintaining a clear change-focused target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration because the counselor offers options and asks for the client's preference. Nevertheless, the counselor remains the primary source of information and direction, so the client is not truly co-constructing the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally respects the client's choices and avoids coercive or pressuring language. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by the amount of directive guidance and medical information provided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks a few evocative questions but does not follow them up to elicit the client's own motivations, values, or confidence. The session relies more on information-giving and advice than on drawing out client-driven motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses collaborative language and offers multiple options, which supports some partnership. The interaction remains largely provider-led, with the counselor supplying most of the structure and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are minimal attempts to evoke change talk, and the counselor does not expand or affirm the client's statements in favor of change. Questions about concern are not followed by deeper exploration of desires, abilities, or needs.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges the client's minimization without arguing, but often reinforces sustain talk by framing the client's plan as sensible. There is limited use of double-sided reflections, ambivalence exploration, or strategic reframing toward change.
