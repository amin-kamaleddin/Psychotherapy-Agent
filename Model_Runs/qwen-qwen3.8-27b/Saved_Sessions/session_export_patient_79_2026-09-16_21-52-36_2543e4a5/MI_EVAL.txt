# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and validating even when Luis is defensive or dismissive. Statements such as “I hear you” and “I’m not going to argue for more tests or appointments” convey strong acceptance without argument or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, nuanced reflections that capture Luis’s feelings, metaphors, and ambivalence about control and asthma care. Reflections such as “a follow-up can feel like a leash” show strong perspective-taking and emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of asthma self-management and using the inhaler in a way Luis can accept. The conversation moves from general resistance to a concrete, client-owned action plan without losing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by using inclusive language and allowing Luis’s ideas to shape the direction of the session. The plan is co-constructed rather than prescribed, emphasizing shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirms Luis’s freedom to choose and avoids controlling or directive language. Options are framed as optional, personal decisions, reinforcing Luis’s sense of ownership over his care.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Most of the session is evocative, drawing motivation and solutions from Luis’s own values, preferences, and standards. The score is slightly reduced because the psychiatrist occasionally introduces a specific clinician-generated suggestion, such as the “Does this need help?” question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Luis as the expert on his own life and choices, using language such as “on your terms” and “your own rule.” Luis’s contributions substantially shape the session rather than the psychiatrist directing it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and strengthens Luis’s own change talk, including desire, reasons, ability, and commitment. Reflective amplification of statements like “I’m the one making the call” supports client-centered motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully avoids arguing with or reinforcing Luis’s resistance, often normalizing his need for autonomy. Some sustain talk could be softened further with more explicit double-sided reflections that also link autonomy to avoiding being caught off guard.
