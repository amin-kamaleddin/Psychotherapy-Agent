# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental and validating, though the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic. Positive regard is present but lacks deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Feelings are acknowledged, but empathy remains surface-level and quickly shifts to problem-solving. There is limited depth in reflecting Daniela’s fear of stigma and belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays on topic and maintains a clear focus on disclosure. However, the direction is more solution-focused than aligned with MI’s emphasis on ambivalence and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and invites input, but the interaction is largely advisor-driven. Daniela’s own expertise and perspective are underutilized in shaping the process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy language is present, but the pattern of repeatedly offering scripts undermines it. The psychiatrist could better honor Daniela’s reluctance by stepping back more.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session provides advice and scripts rather than evoking Daniela’s own motivations and values. Her internal reasons for change are largely bypassed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction has a cooperative tone, but the psychiatrist carries most of the cognitive load. The process feels more directive than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Daniela’s change talk is not meaningfully recognized or amplified. The psychiatrist does not use questions or reflections to strengthen her own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged, but not therapeutically explored. The psychiatrist tends to pivot to new options rather than working through ambivalence.
