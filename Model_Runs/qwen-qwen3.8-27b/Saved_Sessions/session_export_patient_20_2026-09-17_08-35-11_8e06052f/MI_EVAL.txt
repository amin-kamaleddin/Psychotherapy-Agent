# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts Brandon’s ambivalence without pressure. Warmth is supportive but somewhat efficient, with room for more explicit affirmation of the client’s effort or courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflections that capture the client’s ambivalence, emerging plan, and meaningful concerns. Some reflections are interpretive and could be deepened by exploring underlying feelings more client-centeredly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing after-work drinking and building a practical evening routine. The target behavior is clearly operationalized into concrete, client-centered steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan with the client and invites him to define details that only he can know. Explicit joint problem-solving language and checking before offering options would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by emphasizing the client’s choice, avoiding direct advice, and framing options as possibilities. Brandon is positioned as the owner of the decision and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit the client’s own motivations, ideas, and solutions. The counselor occasionally supplies specific options, which can reduce the degree of pure evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions the client as the expert and builds on his suggestions rather than imposing an agenda. The structured use of multiple options can subtly shift toward counselor-guided problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms and amplifies the client’s change-oriented statements and invites him to generate solutions. Change talk could be strengthened by more systematically developing the client’s desire, ability, reasons, and need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument and reflecting the client’s practical concerns without reinforcing resistance. Using more double-sided reflections would more actively help resolve ambivalence.
