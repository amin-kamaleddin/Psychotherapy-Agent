# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids pushing the client. Warmth is somewhat procedural and could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some perspective-taking, but it mainly reflects surface-level statements. Deeper emotional and identity-level concerns are not consistently addressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session develops a concrete plan by the end. However, the early discussion is broad and drifts between stain management and smoking reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician incorporates the client's ideas and builds a practical plan together. Collaboration is strong but not fully reciprocal throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with consistent emphasis on client choice and no pressure. The client is positioned as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through practical questions. Deeper motivations, values, and confidence are not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and supports a collaborative stance. The power balance could be more explicitly equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is generated, but it remains practical and appearance-focused. More advanced MI techniques could strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Resistance is handled well without argument or confrontation. The clinician mostly sidesteps sustain talk rather than exploring it further.
