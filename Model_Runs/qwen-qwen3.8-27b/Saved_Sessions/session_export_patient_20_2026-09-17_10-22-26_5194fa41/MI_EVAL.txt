# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepting of the client’s ambivalence. The psychiatrist validates both the interest in change and the possibility of still having a beer without guilt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s feelings, concerns, and emerging values. There is strong perspective-taking, especially around restlessness, social pressure, and wanting to feel more present.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on a clear, manageable alcohol-related experiment. Broader concerns such as sleep, work, and relationship impact are skillfully linked back to the small behavioral step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as a partner rather than an authority. The client’s ideas—soda, delaying the first drink, no schedule—clearly shape the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly given space to choose, define, and scale the experiment in a low-pressure way. There is no coercion, and the client’s freedom to fail or drink is explicitly preserved.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within the client rather than imposed through advice or external warnings. Questions effectively connect the small experiment to the client’s own values, clarity, and sense of control.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life and experience. Shared language and open-ended questions reinforce a collaborative, equal stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits multiple reasons for change, but the development of confidence and ability talk is not maximized. A stronger follow-up on self-efficacy or past successes would make this dimension more complete.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s reasons to maintain drinking. Statements validate the drink’s function while gently keeping the conversation open to smaller change.
