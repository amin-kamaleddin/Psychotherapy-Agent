# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, but acceptance is often tied to the practical plan rather than deeply affirming the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful reflections around fatigue and burden, but the counselor does not fully explore the client's ambivalence, frustration, or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and gives clear, practical recaps. However, direction is more behavior-management than MI process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's preferences are used to shape the plan, but the psychiatrist remains the primary source of information and plan design.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and frames the plan as an experiment, but continues to give directive instructions even when the client expresses low motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor invites some client input, but the session is driven more by advice and NRT information than by eliciting intrinsic motivation or the client's own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moments, but the interaction remains largely expert-driven and directive rather than a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few planning questions elicit willingness, but the session lacks open-ended questions that draw out the client's motivations, values, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and reduces all-or-nothing pressure, but mostly bypasses sustain talk with practical information rather than reflecting ambivalence or rolling with resistance.
