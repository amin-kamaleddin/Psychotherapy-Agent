# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects and validates Mateo’s experience without pressure. It falls short of 5 because the warmth is competent but somewhat procedural and occasionally infers Mateo’s intensity rather than offering fully individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate, complex reflections that capture Mateo’s ambivalence between pain, rest, and progress. Some reflections are slightly leading and do not fully address the deeper emotional weight of his fear, frustration, or discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of building a safe, manageable rehab plan. Each question moves logically toward concrete steps, feedback, and next-morning testing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo’s ideas clearly shape the plan, and the psychiatrist builds on his suggestions in a collaborative way. However, the clinician still supplies many frames and interpretations, so the collaboration is strong but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Mateo’s ownership by asking him to define his own standards, plan, and rest-day signal. Suggestions are offered as possibilities, preserving Mateo’s choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently elicits Mateo’s own motivations and solutions through open-ended questions. Some questions are mildly leading or interpretation-first, reducing the purity of the evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly positions Mateo as the expert on his own body, goals, and rehab experience. Mateo’s contributions substantially shape the session and the final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk by inviting Mateo to generate practical reasons, standards, and plans. It is not a 5 because the evocation is more practical than motivational and could more explicitly elicit desire, ability, need, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or coercion, helping keep the conversation safe. More strategic double-sided reflections and rolling-with-resistance would be needed to further diffuse limitations and strengthen Mateo’s sense of agency.
