# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, normalizing stance and avoided shaming. Acceptance was sometimes brief and task-focused rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some accurate reflections and validations, but important emotional and identity themes were not consistently explored. The counselor often shifted to advice-giving before deepening empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed connected to the client's presenting concerns and concrete next steps. However, focus was inconsistent and did not consistently clarify the target behavior related to alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's choices were incorporated, but the interaction was more directive than collaborative. The psychiatrist frequently provided preselected options rather than co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported the client's freedom to choose and validated her right to make her own decisions. Prescriptive options and micro-tasks occasionally limited the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful open questions were asked, but evocation was often superficial and followed by advice. Important client statements about identity, values, and self-worth were not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership language was used, but the session was largely expert-driven with structured scripts and tasks. The client's perspective and preferences were not consistently used to guide the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client generated several change-related statements, but the psychiatrist rarely expanded or reflected them. Opportunities to strengthen change talk through scaling, importance questions, or commitment language were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and offered alternatives when the client was hesitant. The approach would have been stronger with more strategic double-sided reflections and ambivalence exploration.
