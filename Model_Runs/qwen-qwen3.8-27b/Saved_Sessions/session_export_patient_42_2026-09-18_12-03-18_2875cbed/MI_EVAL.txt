# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, explicitly creating a non-judgmental space and following through on that promise throughout. When Elise pushes back, she immediately validates and apologizes for making it sound easy, with no moment of judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex, layered, and progressive, consistently going beyond surface content to capture emotional meaning and underlying concerns. Particularly powerful in connecting Elise's post-divorce isolation to her current fear of being 'enough, just standing there.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on smoking cessation with a well-structured progression from ambivalence exploration to deeper meaning to a small, self-designed step. Each section flows logically from the previous one without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session consistently feels like a genuine two-person exploration between equals, with the psychiatrist inviting co-creation of meaning and seeking Elise's perspective before offering her own. When a misstep occurs, she immediately corrects and follows Elise's lead, demonstrating a collaborative rather than authoritative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Elise's freedom to choose and her ownership of the change process, never imposing a timeline or specific outcome. The small step is entirely Elise's creation, with the psychiatrist's role limited to honoring and validating it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are sophisticated and layered, consistently eliciting motivation and meaning from within Elise rather than providing it. The evocation is progressive throughout, drawing out authentic change talk such as 'tired of not being tired' and 'I don't have to fix it.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys that expertise about Elise's life and change resides within Elise, using collaborative language and following her lead throughout. The statement 'Those are yours, not your doctor's' explicitly re-centers Elise as the expert on her own motivation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully and consistently evokes change talk without imposing it, highlighting Elise's own observations and externalizing ownership of motivation. Elise produces genuine, self-generated change talk throughout, including 'I don't have to fix it' and 'maybe I'm ready to stop using the cigarette to avoid the question.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk effectively, never arguing or dismissing, with the 'doorway' reframe elegantly acknowledging the cigarette's function without reinforcing it. However, some moments of sustain talk get extended airtime before being redirected, suggesting slightly more active pivoting could strengthen the session.
