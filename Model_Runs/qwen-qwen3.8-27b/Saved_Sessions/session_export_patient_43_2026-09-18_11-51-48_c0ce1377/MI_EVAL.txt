# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently nonjudgmental stance, validating Eric's ambivalence and pushing back without frustration (e.g., 'Not ready for that word yet isn't a failure'). However, the acceptance occasionally feels slightly performative and could be deepened by explicitly normalizing Eric's ambivalence or validating his identity beyond his smoking status.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate captures of Eric's core ambivalence ('that's a hard place to be') and normalizing reflections ('a very human way to protect yourself'). The psychiatrist could have gone deeper emotionally, particularly by reflecting the grief and fear embedded in the grandpa reference rather than staying at the cognitive level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, strategic progression from exploring ambivalence to identifying a concrete action step (calling the doctor on Monday), with questions progressively narrowing focus. The direction could be slightly more explicit in tying each step directly to smoking cessation rather than the more general 'health' framing used at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Eric as the expert on his own experience and uses inclusive, exploratory language throughout. However, the session follows a somewhat predictable MI arc, and for a higher rating there would need to be moments where Eric's input genuinely reshapes the session direction or where the agenda is explicitly co-negotiated mid-session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support woven into nearly every response — the psychiatrist consistently reinforces Eric's right to set boundaries, never pressures him to do more, and validates his chosen level of commitment. Statements like 'You don't have to do more than that right now' and 'Go take care of your Monday' demonstrate genuine internalization of MI philosophy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation throughout — the psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change, statistics, or lectures, instead consistently drawing motivation from Eric's own mouth. Questions like 'What would keep you on this side of the line?' and 'What would make this visit feel useful to you?' masterfully elicit Eric's own goals and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative, non-hierarchical stance is maintained with Eric given control over the agenda and his own language consistently used. The partnership could be elevated by more explicit co-creation moments beyond the initial open question, where Eric's contributions substantially reshape the session in unexpected ways.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Eric's own change talk, culminating in a concrete self-generated commitment ('I'll make the call. Monday. After my shift'). The cultivation could be more systematic in explicitly evoking all forms of change talk (ability, confidence, need, desire) rather than relying primarily on desire and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk deftly without argument or dismissal — acknowledging Eric's experience while not reinforcing rationalizations (e.g., 'You don't have to assign a cause right now'). In a couple of instances, double-sided reflections could have been more strategically oriented toward softening rather than simply validating the sustain talk.
