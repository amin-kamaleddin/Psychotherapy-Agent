### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating while avoiding stigmatizing language. Acceptance could deepen by more explicitly affirming Brandon’s values, internal conflict, and the difficulty of change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Brandon’s drinking pattern, morning consequences, ambivalence, and concern about judgment. Empathy could improve by more fully exploring emotional undertones such as distress, loss of control, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use, safety, and building a practical reduction plan. It moves effectively from exploration to a client-selected action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and lets Brandon shape the plan, including days, stop time, water pacing, and game-night strategies. The final plan is concrete, collaborative, and client-owned.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects Brandon’s right to choose, avoids pressure, and frames the plan as an experiment rather than a test. Client autonomy is strongly supported throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Brandon’s own reasons for change and uses scaling questions to encourage self-generated solutions. Evocation could be stronger by exploring values and motivation more deeply before presenting structured options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Brandon as the decision-maker and builds on his preferences and ideas. Partnership could be stronger by more explicitly positioning Brandon as the expert on his own life and sharing agenda-setting more openly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Brandon’s change talk is elicited through open questions, scaling, and problem-solving prompts. Change talk could be more fully developed by exploring desire, ability, need, commitment, and underlying values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and uses useful reflections such as keeping the beer ritual while reducing fallout. More active softening, such as balanced reflections and turning resistance into client-generated solutions, could make this more exemplary.
