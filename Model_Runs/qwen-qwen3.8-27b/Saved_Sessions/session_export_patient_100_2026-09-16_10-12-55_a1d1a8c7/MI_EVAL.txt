# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirming language that helps the client feel safe. Slight room for improvement by avoiding early assumptions and more explicitly normalizing adolescent ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong reflections that capture the client’s ambivalence between fitting in and protecting her future. Some emotional nuances, such as fear of judgment and self-disappointment, could be explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on the target behavior and moves logically from exploration to a specific, low-pressure plan. Opening questions and late-stage added supports could be streamlined for stronger closure and focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates excellent collaboration by co-creating the plan with the client’s choices and preferences substantially shaping the session. The interaction reflects a strong partnership between equals rather than an expert-driven approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice, avoids coercion, and respects the client’s boundaries. A few subtle directives, such as framing confidence improvement goals, could be made more explicitly autonomy-centered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes internal motivation by inviting the client’s own words and using importance/confidence scaling. Could deepen evocation by exploring values, identity, and future self more thoroughly before moving into planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites client input and allows preferences to guide the plan, showing a strong collaborative stance. The counselor still initiates much of the structure, so more explicit client-led agenda-setting would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently encourages the client to identify her own reasons, benefits, and small steps for change. Change talk could be deepened with follow-up questions about values, ability, and future self.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without argumentation and reduces pressure around social awkwardness. Some expressions of ambivalence and anticipated excuses could be reflected or reframed more explicitly.
