# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance, normalizing Sofia's stress and validating her "don't beat yourself up" approach. However, warmth remains somewhat brief and task-oriented rather than consistently deepening around her fear, shame, or social identity concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections adequately capture Sofia's ambivalence about marijuana's benefits and concerns, but remain more content-based than emotionally nuanced. Deeper feelings such as fear of being caught, shame, social identity worries, or anxiety about losing a coping tool are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains strongly focused on the target behavior of marijuana use and consistently narrows into a concrete, actionable plan. There is no meaningful drift into unrelated topics, and the focus stays on coping, readiness, triggers, and implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates Sofia's ideas into the plan, such as hiding cash, sending a meme, and using a 10-minute pause, showing meaningful collaboration. However, the process is still more guided problem-solving than equal-partner co-creation, as the therapist supplies many options and structures the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Sofia's input and avoids explicit commands, but frequently suggests specific experiments, tools, timers, scripts, and backup plans. Client choice is present, yet ownership of the process is not consistently emphasized, making some moments feel more leading than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and follow-ups evoke some motivation, but evocation is often interrupted by therapist-generated suggestions and stepwise problem-solving. Sofia's deeper values, wants, and self-identified reasons for change receive less sustained attention than her immediate coping concerns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and asks for Sofia's input, but frequently positions self as the one supplying structure, options, and scripts. This makes the interaction feel more therapist-led than truly shared, even though Sofia's contributions are incorporated into the final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes and affirms change talk through readiness scaling and plan-making, but much of the change language is therapist-directed rather than client-evoked. Sofia's own reasons, hopes, and self-efficacy statements are not consistently expanded or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Sofia's sustain talk and uses some double-sided reflection, but does not consistently soften it or strategically redirect it toward values. The session often moves quickly into planning, leaving the ambivalence partly unexplored.
