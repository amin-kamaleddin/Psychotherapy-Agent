# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental harm-reduction stance and does not shame Adrian for not wanting to quit. However, acceptance is somewhat formulaic and quickly shifts into practical problem-solving, with limited deeper affirmation of Adrian’s values or self-worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of Adrian’s practical and social concerns, such as wanting fun weekends without contaminants and not wanting to be judged. However, empathy remains surface-level and misses the emotional weight of shame, being seen as “not tough enough,” or feeling like the “weird one.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on the relevant target behavior, including methamphetamine-related risk, unknown contaminants, and recurrent infection. However, the direction becomes somewhat circular, repeatedly introducing the same options without consolidation and drifting into logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation feels more like a joint discussion than a lecture and incorporates Adrian’s preference for discreteness. However, the psychiatrist provides most of the content, such as options, scripts, and safety plans, making the balance too counselor-heavy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects Adrian’s right to choose, avoids pushing abstinence, and offers lower-effort alternatives when he resists. Some statements remain prescriptive, which slightly weakens autonomy support in an MI context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some initial concern and preference by asking what Adrian is worried about and which option feels doable. However, evocation is inconsistent and lacks deeper use of scaling, discrepancy, or follow-up to expand Adrian’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Adrian’s input and asks which options feel doable, showing a collaborative stance. However, the session becomes counselor-driven by repeatedly supplying menus, scripts, and safety plans rather than letting Adrian co-create the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some initial change talk, such as Adrian’s desire to avoid another infection and keep his own supplies. However, this change talk is not consistently deepened or amplified through scaling, follow-up, or strategic use of Adrian’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects and normalizes sustain talk without arguing against it or reinforcing Adrian’s reluctance to change. However, the counselor does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to transform that sustain talk toward change-compatible directions.
