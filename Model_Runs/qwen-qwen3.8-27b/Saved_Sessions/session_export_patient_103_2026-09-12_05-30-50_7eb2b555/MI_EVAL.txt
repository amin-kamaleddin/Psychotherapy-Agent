# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment or minimization. The response to anger is validating, warm, and non-controlling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Nabil's shifting emotions and core themes of disrespect, invisibility, and exhaustion. The psychiatrist conveys deep understanding of both the incident and its broader impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting issue but lacks a clear MI-strategic arc toward a change goal. Practical problem-solving moves sometimes pull attention away from exploration and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moves, but the psychiatrist repeatedly provides structured options that make the session feel practitioner-directed. This limits genuine shared ownership of the agenda and process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly affirms choice, respect, and Nabil's right to define the conversation. The repeated menu of options slightly weakens the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes feelings and experiences effectively but does not systematically draw out Nabil's values, hopes, strengths, or intrinsic reasons for change. Opportunities to elicit self-efficacy or personal meaning are missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds well to pushback and attempts to share control, but the recurring menu approach undermines the egalitarian partnership. The client's resistance indicates the collaboration is not fully balanced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is barely evoked because questions focus on the incident rather than motivation, goals, or desired differences. There is little effort to amplify Nabil's own reasons or readiness for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk and avoids arguing with the client. However, there is limited strategic use of MI skills to reframe or soften sustaining statements toward change.
