### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language that reduces shame and avoids arguing with Marek. Warmth is solid but could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Marek’s view that weekends, taste, and social ease matter while expressing surprise about the screening. Empathy could go deeper into emotional and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on weekend binge drinking and moves from risk information to a small experiment. There is some drift into logistics, but the target behavior remains clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marek to help choose a feasible plan, friend, and follow-up approach. The interaction is collaborative, though the counselor still provides a lot of structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated permission language and acceptance of Marek’s decision to frame the plan as an experiment. The counselor respects his right not to commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Marek’s own motivations, such as not wanting to get sick and staying clear-headed. Some risk information and suggestions reduce the focus on client-generated change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marek’s preferences clearly shape the session, including pacing drinks and choosing Tomas for a low-pressure night. The counselor remains somewhat guiding rather than fully co-equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions lead Marek to express meaningful change talk about not wanting to get sick and trying one spaced night. The counselor could more explicitly amplify and summarize Marek’s own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is reflected without direct confrontation, but the counselor does not consistently use double-sided reflections. Ambivalence between keeping the social vibe and protecting health is not fully resolved.
