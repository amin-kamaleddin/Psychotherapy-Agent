# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed the patient’s safety concern. However, acceptance was weakened by repeatedly offering additional help after the patient said she needed to leave.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the patient’s safety concern and plan to consult the GP. Empathy was limited by a mostly factual, medical response that did not fully acknowledge the tension between needing safety information and being pressed for time.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained focused on the medication safety issue and the next step with the GP. However, repeated restatements of the same warning and offer to draft a message made the direction less efficient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session included some collaborative opening and requests for input. Overall, the psychiatrist supplied most of the content and repeatedly introduced new optional tasks, limiting true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive language was used, and the final medication decision was appropriately left to the patient and prescriber. Repeated offers to draft a message after the patient stated her time constraint reduced the sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation was limited because the psychiatrist provided much of the reasoning and information instead of drawing out the patient’s own concerns, values, and motivations. Opportunities were missed to elicit the patient’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening invited the patient to identify what mattered most, but the conversation quickly became more expert-driven. The patient’s time constraint was not consistently used to shape a collaborative next step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offered meaningful change talk about avoiding an unsafe medication combination. The psychiatrist acknowledged these statements but did not actively explore, elaborate, or strengthen them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not reinforce the patient’s time-focused barriers and generally kept the focus on safety. However, the response did not strategically soften the constraint or use it to focus the session on a concise, client-owned plan.
