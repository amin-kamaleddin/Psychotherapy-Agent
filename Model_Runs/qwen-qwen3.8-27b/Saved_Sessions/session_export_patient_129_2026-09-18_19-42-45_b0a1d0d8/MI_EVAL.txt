# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist never labels, disapproves, or pushes back on minimization. Statements like 'I work for you, not your mom' and 'There's nothing wrong with that' create a consistently warm, nonjudgmental environment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and go well beyond surface content to capture underlying needs (e.g., 'this is really about belonging'). The detection of hedging language ('I guess') and real-time attunement to micro-shifts in stance demonstrates remarkable empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior while following Tyler's natural associations, with a logical progression from resistance to values exploration. The session lacks a concrete summary or bridge to next steps that would consolidate the gains made.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuinely collaborative dynamic by establishing a separate alliance from the parent and consistently following Tyler's lead. The psychiatrist incorporates Tyler's own language ('the crew,' 'the vibe,' 'the weird one') rather than imposing clinical framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports Tyler's autonomy through statements like 'I work for you, not your mom' and invitational questions that never prescribe. Every question is genuinely open, with no instances of 'you should' or 'you need to.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sophisticated evocative questions successfully bypass resistance and invite Tyler to access his own values (e.g., 'What do you think the Tyler who's not thinking that far ahead would want?'). The evocation could be pushed further to fully consolidate the emerging change talk before the session ends.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within Tyler, never lecturing or positioning as the authority. The entire session is structured around Tyler's own thinking, with questions that invite self-reflection rather than provide answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits key change talk, most notably 'I'd pick the crew,' which is a powerful values-based articulation. The psychiatrist could have more actively amplified and reflected back these change talk statements to consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles resistance deftly without arguing, lecturing, or counter-arguing, using double-sided reflections to validate while opening space for change. Could more explicitly employ rolling-with-resistance techniques during moments of pushback.
