# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and nonjudgmental acceptance throughout, with clear affirmations of Derrick's worth and effort. Slightly held back from a 5 because some affirmations bordered on generic praise rather than deeply individualized expressions of fundamental respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and meaning-level reflections that capture not just content but the emotional and existential weight of Derrick's statements (e.g., 'this it?' reframed as seeing his life on the same track). Nuanced attendance to underlying emotions including fear of identity loss and the weight of never having not done it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from rapport through ambivalence exploration to concrete commitment language, with each turn building on the last. Could benefit from slightly more explicit summarization of process milestones and a clearer articulation of the 'one week' goal as a defined endpoint.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session feels like two people thinking together rather than one person being interviewed, with genuine curiosity and each question a real invitation for Derrick to contribute. The psychiatrist builds directly on what Derrick offers rather than steering toward a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Derrick's ownership through language like 'this is your space' and 'not forever, not a lifetime promise, just one Friday.' No 'should' or 'need to' language, no imposed consequences, and Derrick's own framing ('seeing if the world ends') is preserved rather than overridden.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change talk is generated by Derrick in response to evocative questions; the psychiatrist never lectures, provides information about harms, or uses external motivators. Techniques like 'what would you tell yourself in that parking lot?' effectively elicit self-generated commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Derrick as the expert on his own life with exploratory questions and session direction shaped entirely by what he brings up. Exemplary use of questions that invite Derrick to generate his own answers rather than providing them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful elicitation and amplification of change talk, including a standout reflection that tracked Derrick's own shift from 'I'd feel weird' to 'maybe they'd even bring it up less.' The session ends with concrete commitment language generated entirely by Derrick.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well through acceptance and redirection into exploration without arguing or dismissing. Could be stronger with more consistent use of double-sided reflections to help Derrick explicitly hold both sides of his ambivalence.
