# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout, especially in normalizing Elise's ambivalence and low-key approach. It falls short of a 5 because acceptance is often tied to the doability of her choices rather than deeper affirmation of her worth, courage, or autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were accurate and supportive reflections, such as acknowledging the tension between health concerns and social connection. However, empathy often remained shallow and quickly shifted into problem-solving rather than staying with Elise's underlying feelings of restlessness, social fear, or discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well focused on the target behavior, particularly the first cigarette and social smoke break with Sarah. It is limited by an overemphasis on scripts and tactical rehearsal rather than broader motivational direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Elise to choose, rehearse, and refine wording, showing meaningful collaboration in the later part of the session. However, the clinician supplied most of the content and options, limiting true co-creation of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise was offered choices and was not directly pressured, which supports autonomy. However, the psychiatrist frequently provided prescriptive scripts and strategies, which made the process feel more guided than client-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evocative questions about importance, confidence, and concerns, drawing out some client-driven motivation. However, evocation was often superficial and quickly gave way to advice-giving without deeply exploring Elise's values or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked permission, invited choices, and included Elise in rehearsal, showing a collaborative stance. Still, the psychiatrist remained the primary architect of the intervention, with Elise mostly selecting from clinician-generated options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk around wanting to feel less winded, trying a delay, and managing the first week. However, these statements were not consistently amplified, deepened, or built upon to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoided arguing with Elise's sustain talk but remained overly focused on maintaining the social status quo. There was limited effort to roll with these concerns or explore alternative ways of belonging without smoking.
