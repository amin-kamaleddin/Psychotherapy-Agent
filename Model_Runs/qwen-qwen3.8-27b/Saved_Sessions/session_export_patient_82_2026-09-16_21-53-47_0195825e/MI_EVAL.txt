# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Lukas’s ambivalence. The warmth is consistent but somewhat formulaic and could more explicitly affirm his courage, self-awareness, or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflective listening to accurately track the conflict between peer belonging and staying focused. Empathy could be deepened by attending more to emotional undercurrents such as fear of rejection, shame, or feeling “seen through.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use in peer contexts and its impact on track performance, moving from ambivalence to practical strategies. It would be stronger if it ended with a concrete next step, commitment, or confidence rating.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Lukas’s ideas, such as the pizza idea and the “heading out early” line, rather than imposing solutions. However, the rapid sequence of leading questions and a repeated therapist message slightly reduce the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids giving advice and consistently treats Lukas as the decision-maker. Autonomy support could be more explicit in key moments by reinforcing that it is his call whether and how to use the chosen line.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits internal motivations without lecturing about alcohol risks or consequences. Evocation could be strengthened by drawing out deeper identity- and values-based change talk, such as what it means to show up at his best for the meet.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and invites Lukas to identify what matters most. The therapist still leads the structure fairly consistently and does not explicitly negotiate agenda or power sharing beyond the opening invitation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encourages Lukas to articulate his own values, goals, and strategies, generating clear change talk. The session would benefit from more systematic development of change talk using DARN-CAT and amplifying importance, confidence, or identity-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without challenging or shaming Lukas and connects it to broader values such as belonging and honesty. More active MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance, could further soften peer-conformity pressure.
