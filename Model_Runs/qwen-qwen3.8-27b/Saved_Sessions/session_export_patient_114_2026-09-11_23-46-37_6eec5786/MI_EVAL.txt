# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and acknowledges Ryan's concerns, but acceptance is uneven and often shifts toward practical problem management. There is limited language that affirms Ryan's worth independently of the incident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist recognizes some of Ryan's distress and validates his concern about being seen as a troublemaker. However, the reflections remain mostly surface-level and do not fully capture the shame, fear, or ambivalence behind Ryan's behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has some structure, but it frequently drifts toward statement drafting and administrative preparation rather than staying focused on alcohol use as the MI target. The core behavioral concern is addressed inconsistently.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, especially in revising the wording of the statement. However, the psychiatrist often supplies structure and content, limiting the sense of equal partnership in defining goals or next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan is not overtly pressured and is given some options, which supports a degree of choice. Autonomy is not consistently reinforced through explicit affirmations that Ryan is in charge of his own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited use of evocative questions and provides much of the language rather than eliciting Ryan's own motivations. There is minimal exploration of Ryan's personal reasons, values, or desired change regarding drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves Ryan in the process and responds to his concerns, showing some collaborative effort. However, the session remains largely guided by the psychiatrist's practical agenda rather than being co-created with Ryan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ryan offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently notice, reflect, or amplify it. The focus on wording and external consequences limits development of Ryan's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly reinforce Ryan's justifications, but participation in minimizing the role of alcohol may inadvertently sustain that minimization. More MI-consistent strategies, such as double-sided reflections, are limited.
