# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, validating tone that accepts ambivalence without judgment. The main limitation is that the warmth feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper affirmation or explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are strong complex reflections of Elise’s ambivalence and the emotional gap of losing a social cue. However, the empathy becomes repetitive and does not consistently deepen into underlying themes such as fear of exclusion or grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and its social function, but the direction is repetitive rather than strategic. The counselor circles the same obstacle instead of moving the conversation toward values, motivation, or a collaboratively chosen next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but Elise’s contributions do not clearly reshape the direction of the session. Repeated similar and verbatim questions make the interaction feel more task-driven and counselor-led than genuinely co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids pressure and leaves choice with the client. The main weakness is that repeated solution-focused questions subtly narrow autonomy by pushing toward a specific replacement cue.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Elise to reflect on what feels possible, but evocation remains limited and often draws out obstacles rather than motivation. There are few targeted attempts to elicit deeper desire, need, ability, or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Elise’s input and acknowledges her knowledge of her social context. However, repeated questions and a problem-solving focus reduce the sense of a balanced, power-sharing partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elise produces some change talk about not wanting to be sick or coughing, but the psychiatrist does not skillfully amplify or expand it. The session returns too quickly to problem-solving, leaving change talk faint and underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about smoking as social glue is acknowledged but not effectively softened. There is little evidence of rolling with resistance, double-sided reflections, or redirection toward values, strengths, or hope for change.
