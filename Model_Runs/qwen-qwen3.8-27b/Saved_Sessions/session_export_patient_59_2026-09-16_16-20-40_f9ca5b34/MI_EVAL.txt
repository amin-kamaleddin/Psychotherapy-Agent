# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated nonjudgmental respect and normalized the client’s concern about being judged. There was no evidence of moralizing, pressure, or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and captured the client’s tension between wanting fairness and wanting a low-pressure outcome. However, the empathy remained somewhat cognitive and logistical, with limited exploration of deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation generally stayed focused on the target behavior and related consequences. A stronger directional move would have more explicitly linked the client’s stated priorities to a concrete behavior-change goal around marijuana use or possession.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used the client’s language and allowed the client’s priorities to shape the agenda and plan. The interaction felt collaborative rather than expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasized that the client was in charge of decisions and was not being pushed to choose a particular option. The client’s ownership of the plan was consistently respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited practical, client-generated planning statements. However, there was limited exploration of deeper internal motivation, values, confidence, or stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly treated the client as the expert on his own life and returned decision-making authority throughout the session. Power-sharing was strong and consistent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change language was generated, particularly around doable steps such as being home by seven and not carrying weed. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was accepted respectfully without confrontation. However, the session remained centered on compliance and avoiding detection, with limited use of discrepancy development or double-sided reflections.
