# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout the session. Acceptance could be deeper and more emotionally specific when Farid expresses self-doubt or vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers reasonable reflective listening and some perspective-taking on Farid’s ambivalence. However, the reflections are often cognitive and surface-level rather than deeply exploring underlying emotions such as shame, discouragement, or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a very clear and consistent focus on the target behavior of increasing physical activity through after-work walks. Every major topic is tied to that goal, with little drift and steady momentum toward a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Farid contributes meaningful elements to the plan, showing some collaborative effort. However, the psychiatrist often supplies options and structures the process, making it feel more like guided problem-solving than a fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, respecting preferences, and avoiding direct commands. It could be stronger by more explicitly affirming that the decision and pace remain entirely Farid’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-driven motivation through open questions, confidence scaling, and commitment questions. However, the therapist often provides strategies and explanations, which limits deeper evocation of Farid’s own reasons, values, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Farid to make choices and contribute to the plan. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist still takes a strong expert problem-solver role by frequently offering specific options and repeatedly asking for commitment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes several attempts to elicit change talk, and Farid offers several statements of willingness and desire. These change-related statements are not consistently deepened, amplified, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and gently normalizes Farid’s concerns without blame. However, the conversation remains centered on barriers and excuses rather than consistently redirecting toward values, benefits, or change talk.
