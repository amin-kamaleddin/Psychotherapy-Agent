# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, normalizing Yasmin's need for control rather than labeling her. This creates a strong stance of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Yasmin's ambivalence, fear, and desire for sustainable change. The score is held at 4 because reflections are somewhat formulaic and do not fully explore deeper emotional or cultural layers of body dissatisfaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on moving away from punitive dieting and toward practical, sustainable markers and plans. Focus occasionally narrows to weight loss or scale goals, which slightly blurs the broader holistic-health target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Yasmin's ideas, co-constructs the plan, and avoids lecturing or dictating. The interaction reflects a strong equal partnership in developing safe, realistic strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Yasmin to define what would work, what is realistic, and what would make a plan safer. Decisions remain with Yasmin, and there are no prescriptive recommendations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reliably elicits motivation from within Yasmin through client-centered questions and reflections. Yasmin generates her own change talk, including desire for sustainability, non-scale markers, and backup plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Yasmin is treated as the expert on her own experience, and her language such as auditing, guardrail, and letting yourself go shapes the session. The psychiatrist supports and expands her ideas rather than imposing a fixed model.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and affirmations successfully evoke practical change ideas, including a Sunday check-in, monthly weighing, and a panic plan. The score is 4 because the session does not systematically deepen DARN-C change talk, especially confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, but weight, the scale, and fear of failure remain a central focus throughout. More consistent redirection toward non-scale values and self-trust would strengthen softening.
