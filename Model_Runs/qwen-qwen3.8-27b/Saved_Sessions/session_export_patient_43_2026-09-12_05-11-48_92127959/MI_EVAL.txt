# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive even when the patient minimized or resisted. Acceptance was strong but somewhat formulaic, with repeated phrases that limited deeper individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate and captured the patient’s main concerns about work, smoking breaks, and not wanting change. However, empathy was often surface-level and missed deeper feelings such as pride, fear of loss of control, or irritation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking, work capacity, and future concerns without wandering into unrelated topics. Direction could have been stronger by more consistently tying questions back to the patient’s own priorities and decision thresholds.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited the patient’s perspective rather than imposing a predetermined plan. Collaboration was uneven because the clinician still introduced several specific suggestions and experiments that the patient did not request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently supported through statements that the patient was in charge and no decision was being forced. Some repeated suggestions and offers could still feel subtly guiding for a resistant patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used evocative questions and elicited some important client-centered statements about wanting to keep working and lifting. These statements were not consistently reflected, expanded, or built into stronger change talk before moving to psychoeducation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided an expert-driven stance and respected the patient’s choice and pace. The partnership was occasionally weakened by repeated suggestions that made the clinician’s preferred strategies more visible.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were appropriate attempts to evoke change talk by linking smoking to work, energy, and future capacity. The patient’s change-relevant statements were not consistently amplified or developed into a stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided confrontation and did not argue with the patient’s minimization. However, sustain talk was often validated without being skillfully redirected using double-sided reflections or ambivalence-aware responses.
