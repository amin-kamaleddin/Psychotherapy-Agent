# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides consistent, warm, nonjudgmental language that validates Natalia's ambivalence and reduces shame. Slightly formulaic and does not fully deepen into explicit normalization of guilt or parenting stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects both protective motive and exhaustion-driven need, with strong perspective-taking on multiple statements. Could go deeper into the shame and fear behind statements like 'what if he smells it.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays on the smoking-as-reset theme but circles the same two-minute pause repeatedly without building toward a clear goal or concrete next step. Lacks a summary or collaborative move tied to Natalia's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses joint language and treats Natalia as expert on her own life, inviting her to shape the conversation. Slightly reduced by consecutive questions and one repeated question that make it feel more clinician-guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear noncoercive language ('even if the cigarette stays for now,' 'you don't have to solve it all today') that supports her sense of choice. Could be strengthened with more explicit reinforcement of her ownership and capacity to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine evocation questions elicit some small alternatives, but they remain superficial and leave sustain talk largely intact. Limited use of importance/confidence/commitment exploration or value-behavior discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Natalia's perspective frequently and lets her ideas repeatedly shape direction, especially around the balcony/car pause. Does not fully reach 5 because the psychiatrist still drives much of the sequence through structured questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits meaningful change talk ('maybe I could try not lighting up right away,' 'maybe that's enough for now') but does not consistently affirm, amplify, or expand these statements. Little importance/confidence/commitment language to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without argument but maintains the status quo rather than gently exploring discrepancy or using double-sided reflections. Does not strategically redirect toward the pull between short-term relief and long-term protection.
