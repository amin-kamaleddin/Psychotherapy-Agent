# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the client’s concerns about self-criticism. Acceptance is strong but occasionally feels procedural, especially when moving quickly through multiple questions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and clinically useful, capturing the client’s ambivalence and the gap between panic and actual outcomes. The main limitation is that reflections often stay cognitive and do not consistently deepen the emotional meaning of guilt, shame, or self-image.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target issue of self-respect, delayed responding, and guilt around choosing self-care. Direction is clear, but several multi-question turns can dilute focus and increase cognitive load.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s preferences shape the work, such as focusing on guilt first and accepting a mental tally instead of written tracking. Collaboration is strong, but the psychiatrist still frequently supplies options and structures before the client generates them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s sense of choice throughout, inviting preferences and avoiding pressure. There is no evidence of coercion, persuasion, or a hidden agenda; the client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client’s own strengths, values, and change language, including the choice of “I can wait.” Evocation could be strengthened by more explicitly connecting the client’s statements to self-respect and underlying values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s priorities and adapts when the client declines a formal plan. Partnership is effective but remains somewhat therapist-led because of frequent counselor-generated options and structured suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates opportunities for the client to generate meaningful change talk about choosing herself and gathering evidence. This could be strengthened by more explicitly eliciting and amplifying desire, ability, reasons, need, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by validating concerns and avoiding argument, such as accepting a mental tally and normalizing “therapy-speak.” The skill would improve with more double-sided reflections that transform sustain talk into change talk while preserving the client’s values.
