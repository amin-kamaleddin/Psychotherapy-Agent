# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard, never lecturing, labeling, or disapproving throughout. Even Matteo's sustain talk is validated as a legitimate goal rather than dismissed or contradicted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are sophisticated, precise, and progressively deepen understanding—capturing nuance like Matteo's ambivalence about 'intentionality' and his value of 'not losing his edge.' These are individualized, not formulaic, and accurately reflect both sustain talk and emerging change talk simultaneously.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a clear logical progression from establishing the drinking pattern through exploring thresholds, values, and self-monitoring. However, it lacks a brief closing summary to consolidate themes and does not explicitly tie back to the injury context that prompted the screening.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently builds on Matteo's responses and follows his lead rather than steering toward a predetermined agenda. Collaboration could be slightly strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language and a more clearly articulated joint decision at the close.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a defining feature—the clinician never says 'you should,' 'you need to,' or 'I recommend,' and consistently reinforces Matteo's ownership of his choices. Every reframe is offered as a question inviting the client's judgment rather than a directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently draws out Matteo's own motivations, thresholds, and values through open-ended questions rather than providing external reasons for change. Slightly more amplification of confidence and ability statements at key moments—particularly the final 'I'm already doing the thing' realization—would elevate this to the highest level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located with Matteo through language like 'what does that picture look like from your side?' and explicit acknowledgment that his lived experience is the authority on what works. The power dynamic is clearly inverted from a traditional clinical model, with the clinician functioning as a skilled inquirer who trusts the client's wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk is successfully evoked, including recognition of potential harm, desire to cut back, and a value-based reason ('not being that guy'). The clinician could push slightly further on amplifying the 'not losing your edge' framing and could more explicitly reinforce Matteo's shift in self-perception in the final exchange.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled with exceptional skill—never argued with, never minimized, but consistently acknowledged and used as a bridge toward change considerations. The most sophisticated example validates Matteo's goal of 'keeping it where it is' as a real goal rather than pushing against it, then gently explores what drift would look like.
