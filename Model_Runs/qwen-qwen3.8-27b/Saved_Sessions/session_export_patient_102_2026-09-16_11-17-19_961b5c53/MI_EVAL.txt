# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used nonjudgmental, validating language that respected the client’s defensiveness and autonomy. Approval was occasionally tied to desired behaviors, limiting unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client’s perspective, including fitting in versus not wanting to be labeled. Deeper emotional layers such as shame, embarrassment, and fear of exclusion were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use at social gatherings and related values, safety, and next steps. It occasionally shifted toward logistics rather than deepening the client’s reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and allowed the client to shape decisions, such as choosing water and declining follow-up. Some therapist-led suggestions made the interaction less fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently respected the client’s right to decide and avoided pressure or monitoring. Mild steering toward a specific water experiment prevented a perfect score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked appropriate questions to evoke the client’s own perspective and concerns. Evocation was not consistently developed, and the session moved too quickly into problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices and invited exploration rather than demanding compliance. Some mild directive framing around next-step tactics limited full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited some change talk, including safety planning and willingness to try water. These statements were not consistently amplified, summarized, or built into a stronger change narrative.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with the client’s minimization and redirected with validation. Strategic double-sided reflections to transform sustain talk into change talk were not consistently used.
