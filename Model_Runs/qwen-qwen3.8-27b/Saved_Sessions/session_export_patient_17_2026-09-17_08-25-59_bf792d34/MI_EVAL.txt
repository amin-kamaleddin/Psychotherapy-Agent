# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance throughout the conversation. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth does not fully normalize the long-standing nature of the struggle or the associated shame and stigma.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence and emotional experience. Empathy could be deeper by more explicitly validating the emotional cost of shame, disappointment, and repeated failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation, stress triggers, and practical micro-behaviors. It could be tightened by more explicitly stating the session target or collaboratively setting an agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor and client jointly explore practical ideas and build a workable plan. However, the counselor offers many structured options, which slightly reduces the extent to which the client’s own ideas drive the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent and consistent, with the counselor repeatedly framing the client as the decision-maker. Language such as “trial, not a rule” powerfully supports choice, ownership, and reduced pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit the client’s motivations, values, and problem-solving ideas. The score is not a 5 because some prompts are suggestive or include therapist-generated examples, which can guide rather than fully elicit client language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys a collaborative stance by inviting the client’s perspective and treating her as the expert on her life. It is not a 5 because the counselor still introduces several structured options and frames that slightly shape the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently elicits change talk through open questions about values, desired outcomes, and preferred alternatives. It could be stronger by more systematically evoking confidence, ability, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor does not counterargue or moralize when the client raises reasons for continuing smoking. Reframing sustain talk through validation and the shift from “rule” to “trial” effectively reduces resistance and preserves autonomy.
