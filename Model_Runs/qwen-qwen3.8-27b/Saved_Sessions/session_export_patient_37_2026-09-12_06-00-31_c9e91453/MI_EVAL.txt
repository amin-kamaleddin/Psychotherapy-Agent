# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s concerns without pressure. Warmth is solid but could be more individualized and explicitly affirming of the client’s care and competence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of ambivalence and safety concerns, especially at the start. However, later emotional statements about fear and guilt are acknowledged rather than deeply explored with more nuanced empathic reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the varicella vaccination decision and practical next steps. There is strong momentum from ambivalence to concrete preparation for the pediatrician visit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by following the client’s requests for information, a checklist, and questions. The interaction could be more client-led if the agenda and priorities were co-created more explicitly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s right to process and decide later is clearly respected, and the psychiatrist avoids pushing immediate vaccination. A few direct recommendations make the stance somewhat more directive than fully evocative.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially elicits key worries and values, but then shifts heavily into information provision. There are missed opportunities to deepen the client’s own motivation, confidence, and readiness through evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shares power by repeatedly asking which option would be most helpful and allowing the client to choose next steps. The clinician still takes an expert-educator role, which slightly limits fully client-led partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers clear movement toward acceptance, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or builds on this change talk. The session remains too educational to consistently draw out the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Concerns are acknowledged without ridicule or argument, and the psychiatrist redirects toward reliable information and practical steps. Some reassurance feels debate-like or fact-focused rather than using double-sided reflections to soften ambivalence.
