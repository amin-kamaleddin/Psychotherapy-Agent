# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used validating, normalizing language and avoided judgment. However, acceptance was somewhat procedural and included mild evaluative framing rather than stronger individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was an initial empathic reflection, but the session quickly shifted to factual information and problem-solving. The psychiatrist missed several opportunities to reflect Elena’s ambivalence, anxiety, and emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation stayed broadly focused on the referral concern and related reproductive health topics. However, the direction was more agenda-driven than MI-guided, with less consistent focus on the client’s own goals and change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative gestures were present, such as asking what the client wanted to focus on and offering options. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist remained the main information provider, and Elena’s repeated reluctance did not meaningfully reshape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently acknowledged choice and reduced pressure, which supported autonomy. However, this was undercut by repeated prescriptive suggestions and plan-building despite the client’s resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial open question was appropriate, but the psychiatrist did not consistently draw out Elena’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change. Most questions were closed or service-oriented rather than motivation-focused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made some partnership gestures by asking what the client wanted and offering choices. However, the session became expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying options and steps rather than treating Elena as the primary expert on her own decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena offered several statements that could have been developed as change talk, such as wanting to be careful and recognizing the need for testing. The psychiatrist did not consistently notice, amplify, or expand these statements, so change talk remained underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and used nonpunitive language when the client expressed reluctance or minimize the event. However, there was limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to gently connect avoidance to values and possible change.
