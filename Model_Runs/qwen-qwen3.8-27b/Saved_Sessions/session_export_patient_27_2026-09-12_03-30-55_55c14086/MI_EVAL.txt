# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used warm, nonjudgmental language and repeatedly normalized the client’s concerns. It fell short of a 5 because affirmations of the client’s worth and fear could have been more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor reflected the client’s thoughts and feelings with accuracy, including her worry about being seen as different. Some reflections remained summary-level and did not fully explore deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the target behavior of disclosing diabetes to peers. The counselor guided the work strategically from concerns to scripts, practice, and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client meaningfully shaped the session by choosing a short script, the best friend to consider, and texting as the practice format. Collaboration was structured because the counselor provided much of the practical content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supported autonomy by offering choices and avoiding pressure to commit immediately. It would be stronger with more explicit language emphasizing that the client owns the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor drew out some client motivation through questions about hopes, worries, and confidence. The session moved too quickly into advice and scripts, limiting deeper exploration of values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited client input and allowed her choices to guide the work. It would be stronger by more explicitly naming the client as the expert on her own relationships and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions produced useful change talk, such as the client considering texting a best friend. The counselor did not consistently amplify or deepen that change talk before shifting to practical options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor responded respectfully to fears of being seen as different, burdensome, or “the diabetes kid.” It would be stronger with more active MI-style responses that hold ambivalence while gently turning toward change.
