# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistent nonjudgmental and respectful stance despite the patient's resistance and anger. Minor limitations included formulaic language and occasional over-accommodation, which prevented a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate in content but became repetitive and template-like. The psychiatrist missed deeper emotional material, such as the patient's grief and ambivalence about his morning routine and his wife's fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session had a clear structural focus and progressed toward a concrete plan for the next morning. However, the target behavior of tobacco cessation was largely set aside after the initial exchange.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fostered a collaborative approach by inviting patient input and co-creating a response plan. The counselor still controlled much of the session structure and offered multiple options before fully accepting the patient's stated position.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's right to make his own choices was consistently affirmed without pressure to quit. Slightly persistent offering of scripts and strategies prevented a higher rating.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked the patient's own language and strategies for managing conflict with his wife. However, there was minimal evocation of the patient's own values, concerns, or ambivalence related to smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative, non-expert language and repeatedly invited the patient to choose next steps. The partnership remained somewhat superficial because the psychiatrist still directed the overall agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk about smoking were limited after the patient rejected the cessation framing. The session primarily generated willingness to manage conflict, not motivation to change tobacco use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk without arguing or dismissing the patient's reasons for maintaining the status quo. However, active MI techniques such as double-sided reflections were not used to hold ambivalence or gently redirect toward change.
