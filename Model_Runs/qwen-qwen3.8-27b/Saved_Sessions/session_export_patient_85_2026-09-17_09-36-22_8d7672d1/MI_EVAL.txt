# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard, nonjudgmental stance, and validation of the client’s experience and ambivalence. The counselor affirms the client’s efforts and self-awareness without judgment or conditional evaluation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and accurately captures the client’s mixed feelings about health, weight regain, and making the change manageable. The empathy is generally accurate but occasionally remains slightly surface-level regarding deeper emotions such as fear of failure or discomfort about asking his wife to change her habits.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing fruit juice intake as part of weight management. The psychiatrist strategically moves the conversation from ambivalence to identifying a small realistic step, planning home changes, discussing it with his wife, and defining measurable success criteria.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting the client’s input and allowing his perspective to shape the plan. However, the collaboration could be stronger with more explicit “we” language and more visible joint problem-solving, as at times the session feels slightly more guided than fully mutually constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports client autonomy by framing the change as a low-stakes, self-directed experiment. The counselor consistently avoids pressure and emphasizes the client’s freedom to choose, including the option to stop if he hates it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes motivation from within the client rather than supplying external reasons for change. The client generates most of the change talk, including practical strategies, self-regulation plans, and criteria for evaluating progress.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly conveys a partnership stance by positioning the client as the expert on his home, schedule, and realistic options. The session is co-constructed, with the client’s ideas shaping the direction and content of the work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively cultivates change talk by inviting the client to generate and expand his own reasons, plans, and commitments. The client produces strong change talk around a one-glass limit, reducing visible access to juice, replacing it with water or seltzer, and treating the change as an experiment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully acknowledges the client’s sustain talk without arguing, dismissing, or reinforcing the old behavior. Statements about quitting juice “feeling like torture” and loving the taste are validated while the focus is redirected toward a manageable, self-directed plan.
