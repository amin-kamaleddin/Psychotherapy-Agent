# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental, validating language and avoids moralizing about alcohol, violence, or ambivalence. Slight limitation is a somewhat formulaic warmth with less explicit individualized affirmation of strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate and nuanced reflections of the client’s perspective and emotions. Complex feelings around control, whānau support, and wanting to be present for children are captured well.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing alcohol, staying connected to treatment, and strengthening family connection. Each reflection and question is tied to specific, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as an equal partner rather than imposing a pre-set plan. The client’s ideas directly shape the session, including the test-run, short text, leaving the keys, and footy Sunday plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is repeatedly emphasized through language about choice, control, and the client directing his own process. There is no pressure, persuasion, or directive advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits motivation from within the client by exploring values, reasons, and confidence for change. Lecturing is avoided, and the client’s desire to be present for his children is amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative, non-expert stance is maintained and the client’s input substantially shapes the session. The partnership could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language or agenda negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, future-focused questions effectively evoke the client’s own reasons, ability, and confidence for change. The psychiatrist skillfully amplifies change talk without becoming prescriptive.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns about independence, being watched, and losing control are acknowledged without argument or minimization. The psychiatrist could more actively reframe sustain talk or resolve ambivalence toward change.
