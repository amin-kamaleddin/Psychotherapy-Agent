# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Mateo's frustration, guardedness, and ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but sometimes feels more task-oriented than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several accurate reflections and summaries of Mateo's perspective. Empathy remains somewhat shallow, missing deeper feelings of exhaustion, hopelessness, resentment, and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear, relevant goal: securing income through construction day labor to support staying out. The direction is strong, though it becomes overly focused on logistics at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo's choices influence the session, and his preference for in-person work is respected. Collaboration is weakened by the psychiatrist often taking the planner/problem-solver role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses autonomy-supportive language and accepts Mateo's limits and conditional plans. However, frequent scripts, checklists, and planning prompts can create subtle pressure for a mandated client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions evoke Mateo's goals, skills, and some change talk, such as wanting a normal life and trying a lumber yard. Evocation is limited by the quick shift into practical solutions rather than deeper exploration of motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Mateo's choices to shape the final plan. The partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist often supplies options, scripts, and next steps rather than fully co-creating with Mateo.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some desire, ability, and small commitment, including Mateo's goal of stability and plan to try a lumber yard. Change talk is present but not consistently deepened or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Mateo's sustain talk and acknowledges obligation and limits. However, stronger MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, are not consistently used to turn dominant sustain talk toward change.
