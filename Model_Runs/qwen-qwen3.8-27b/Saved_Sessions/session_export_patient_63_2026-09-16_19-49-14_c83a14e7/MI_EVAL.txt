# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and accepts Jordan’s ambivalence without judgment. Both the legal concerns and peer pressure/craving are validated, creating a nondefensive space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Jordan’s emotional experience, fatigue, embarrassment, and fear of losing peers. The counselor demonstrates strong perspective-taking beyond simple paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior, triggers, and concrete coping steps. Some repetitive phrasing occurs, and brief summaries would help consolidate progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds directly on Jordan’s own ideas and treats him as a co-explorer. Patient contributions consistently shape the next step in the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasizes choice, ownership, and possibility without pressuring Jordan to commit. Uncertainty and hesitation are allowed without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions effectively elicit Jordan’s own motivations, values, and possible resources. Change talk such as wanting out and avoiding court emerges from Jordan rather than from external persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a client-led stance and treats Jordan as the expert on his own life. The session could be stronger by more explicitly negotiating the agenda and inviting Jordan’s preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently builds on Jordan’s own change talk and planning language. It could be further advanced with more explicit DARN-C evocation, scaling questions, or exploration of commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk around peer pressure and craving is acknowledged without argument or defensiveness. The counselor could more consistently use double-sided reflections or cost-benefit framing after each sustain statement.
