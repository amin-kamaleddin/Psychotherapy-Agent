# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance. However, the warmth can feel slightly conceptual and lacks explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and effectively capture the client's ambivalence. However, they do not always explore the deeper emotional core, such as the shame or existential invalidation underlying the client's doubts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and narrows it to a concrete five-minute step. It occasionally becomes abstract when over-focusing on 'proof' and could benefit from scaling to clarify readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully co-constructs a low-pressure plan with the client, treating her as a co-designer. However, driving several consecutive questions reduces the sense of true equal partnership and client-led pacing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides exemplary autonomy support by consistently emphasizing the client's choice, respecting her boundaries, and avoiding pressure or directive advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses effective, client-centered questions to draw out the client's own ideas and solutions. However, the session stops short of fully developing the client's change talk or confidence levels.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative stance is maintained by repeatedly inviting the client's perspective and avoiding an expert-driven posture. The partnership is slightly weakened by moments where the therapist steers the process with back-to-back questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is successfully prompted to generate her own solutions and meaningful change talk. The psychiatrist could improve by more explicitly amplifying, summarizing, or rating the client's confidence in her plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging both sides without arguing or minimizing the client's feelings. The session could more explicitly normalize the pull of avoidance and pivot toward the client's core values.
