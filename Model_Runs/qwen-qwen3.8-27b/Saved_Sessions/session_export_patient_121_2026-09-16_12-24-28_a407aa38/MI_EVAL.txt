# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, validating, and affirming with positive regard throughout. Somewhat generic in tone and quickly transitions into problem-solving, limiting deeper emotional attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections that capture Sofia's concerns about safety, control, and being judged. Remains at a practical/surface level rather than exploring deeper shame, fear, or hurt behind key statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear strategic focus on reducing school overwhelm and creates a concrete small step. Becomes overly logistical with repeated menu questions about playlists, timings, and backup rooms, feeling more like behavior planning than MI.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative language and adaptation to Sofia's shifting preferences (scripts → library, texts → music-only). Predominantly menu-based interaction with numbered counselor-generated options limits truly equal collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invites choice, respects refusals, reduces pressure ("not a failure," "try again"), and accepts Sofia's decisions without pushback. Repeated counselor-supplied menus subtly position the psychiatrist as option-source rather than letting Sofia fully lead.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial open questions successfully elicit Sofia's goals and her own behavioral ideas (library, headphones, Mia). Evocation stays practical/behavioral rather than motivational, with few questions drawing out values, fears, confidence, or meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and Sofia's ideas shape the final plan. Uneven partnership as the psychiatrist repeatedly presents pre-made option menus ("Two simple options," "Quick, doable steps," "Which backup spot feels safest?"), making the session feel led rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia articulates some reasons, desires, and confidence, with brief affirmations ("Good choice," "smart choice"). Minimal DARN-C exploration; most responses to change talk shift into additional planning options rather than amplifying or exploring her reasons and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing, defending, or correcting Sofia's sustain talk (script rejection, counselor as "ammo," "not a failure"). Lacks active softening—no double-sided reflections or exploration of the underlying fear/ambivalence (e.g., protecting space vs. appearing weak or "sent somewhere").
