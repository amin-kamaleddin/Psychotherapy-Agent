# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids blaming Malik. Acceptance is solid but somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate and helps validate Malik’s surface-level concerns. However, empathy stays content-focused and does not fully explore deeper emotions or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session starts with a clear target around substance use but then follows Malik’s reframing toward his mother. Direction is present but becomes inconsistent relative to the original referral concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and adjusts the plan based on Malik’s preferences. Collaboration is strong, though the clinician still generates much of the content and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with consistent use of optional, choice-based language. It falls short of a 5 because the agenda and some solutions remain partly clinician-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Malik’s priorities, values, and strengths. Evocation is adequate but inconsistent and does not stay tightly connected to the cannabis-use change goal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids a sole-expert stance and invites Malik’s input throughout. Partnership is strong, though the clinician still leads much of the direction rather than fully co-creating it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited, mostly surface-level efforts to elicit change talk. The clinician does not sufficiently expand on brief change-related statements or develop stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and uses neutral reflections to prevent escalation. However, more skilled MI moves are needed to soften sustain talk and redirect toward change.
