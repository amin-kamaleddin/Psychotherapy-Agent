# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and validating tone. A 5 would require deeper, more individualized acknowledgment of the client’s emotional weight, self-doubt, and guilt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is present early when reflecting exhaustion and uncertainty, but it becomes less consistent as the session shifts toward problem-solving. More emotionally attuned follow-up to statements about fear, pressure, and guilt would strengthen this criterion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the client’s presenting concerns about baby development, reassurance, and rest. There is minor repetition around message timing, but overall direction remains clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts to the client’s fatigue and incorporates her preferences into the plan. However, much of the content is clinician-generated, limiting fully collaborative or co-created problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is consistently offered choices and her boundaries are respected without pressure. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing her ownership of decisions and change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions, but much of the session relies on information-giving rather than drawing out the client’s own motivations. The clinician does not fully follow up on or expand the client’s change-relevant statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaborative language and some client input, but the interaction is often clinician-led and expert-driven. A stronger partnership would involve more explicitly treating the client as the expert on her baby and her own needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some change talk, such as wanting to ask rather than worry. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on this change talk to increase client motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s sustain talk. However, there is limited active reframing or therapeutic use of that sustain talk to move toward change.
