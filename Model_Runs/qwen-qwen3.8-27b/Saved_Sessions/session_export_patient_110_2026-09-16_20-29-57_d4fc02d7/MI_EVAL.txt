# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard while validating Richard’s ambivalence. It falls short of 5 because the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not deepen individualized affirmations of Richard’s self-knowledge or courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Richard’s conflicting concerns about cholesterol, safety, and staying active with his wife. It is not a 5 because it does not fully capture the deeper emotional layer, such as the quiet dread or fear of “waiting for something to hurt.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior—choosing a medication plan and making a concrete call to the doctor—while repeatedly linking it to Richard’s values. It is limited by occasional repetition around the logistics of the call without fully consolidating broader motivation or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs practical steps such as writing questions, involving Richard’s wife, and using a calendar cue. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist occasionally introduces solutions and scripts rather than allowing Richard’s ideas to shape nearly all major decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking what feels manageable and avoiding pressure to take the medication or make the call. It falls short of 5 because suggested scripts and specific options slightly reduce explicit emphasis on Richard’s ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Richard’s own reasons for change, including staying active with his wife and preparing for retirement. It is not a 5 because the evocation is sometimes limited by problem-solving and does not consistently deepen or expand Richard’s own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites Richard’s perspective throughout the session. It is slightly less than 5 because the psychiatrist occasionally adopts a more guiding, advisory role, such as offering a specific phone script.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes meaningful change talk by helping Richard connect medication and activity to his relationship with his wife and future. It is not a 5 because the change talk is not consistently amplified, summarized, or deepened with confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Richard’s sustain talk without arguing or dismissing it, such as the idea that waiting feels safer. It falls short because it does not consistently use double-sided reflections or value-based redirection to actively soften ambivalence and resolve the minimization.
