# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and validated Lukas’s ambivalence without shaming or labeling. However, acceptance was occasionally undermined by rapid advice-giving, which made the stance feel more problem-solving than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were often accurate and helped clarify Lukas’s concerns about fatigue, grades, and peer pressure. Empathy remained somewhat surface-level, as the psychiatrist frequently moved from feelings into options or problem-solving before fully exploring the deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target issue of peer-related alcohol use and its potential impact on Lukas’s athletic and academic goals. However, direction was somewhat mechanical because the conversation repeatedly moved toward selecting a behavioral strategy rather than exploring values and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Lukas to choose among options and responded to his idea of confiding in one teammate. Collaboration was limited because the psychiatrist frequently supplied lists of strategies, making the interaction more therapist-driven than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy was supported by emphasizing Lukas’s choices, accepting that he did not want to promise, and offering low-pressure options. However, repeated prompts to pick a strategy and the suggestion to “set a personal limit” subtly pressured a contemplative client toward action planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked some opening questions but relied heavily on supplying reasons, tactics, and behavioral options. There was limited use of evocative MI questions to draw out Lukas’s own values, motivations, and preferred direction for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed some partnership by asking for Lukas’s preference and building on his idea of talking with one teammate. Partnership was uneven because the psychiatrist continued to generate and direct most of the solutions rather than consistently letting Lukas lead the exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Lukas offered spontaneous change talk about protecting his season, grades, and not wanting to reach for a can. The psychiatrist acknowledged these statements intermittently but did not consistently amplify or expand them into stronger self-motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist briefly acknowledged Lukas’s ambivalence and downplaying, but did not effectively soften or redirect sustain talk. Returning repeatedly to concrete coping options and a “one drink” limit risked normalizing harm reduction rather than exploring deeper change.
