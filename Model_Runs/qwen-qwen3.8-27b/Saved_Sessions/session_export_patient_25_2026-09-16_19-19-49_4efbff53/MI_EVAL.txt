# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirms the client’s strengths and progress. Acceptance could be stronger by adding unconditional positive regard that is not tied to performance or success.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence, social dilemma, and practical concerns with strong cognitive empathy. It is limited by less deep exploration of emotional undertones such as guilt, fear, or self-image concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing alcohol use in social gatherings, especially helping the client stop at two drinks. The conversation progresses clearly from intention to specific, actionable strategies without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas strongly shape the plan, showing a collaborative approach rather than advice-giving. However, the therapist still provides some structured prompts and suggestive options that slightly reduce co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports choice, avoids controlling language, and invites the client to select the most manageable step to try. The client clearly retains control over what he is willing to implement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the client’s own values, strengths, and routines rather than external persuasion or advice. The client generates rich change talk tied to family, self-care, and prior success with smoking cessation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert on his own context, preferences, and behaviors. The interaction is strongly balanced, with the client actively shaping the direction and content of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits and amplifies client-generated change talk through open questions and affirming reflections. The client’s own language becomes the main source of motivation and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about social pressure and honesty is acknowledged respectfully and reframed in autonomy-supportive ways. It could be stronger by more consistently defusing the client’s concerns rather than giving them sustained weight.
