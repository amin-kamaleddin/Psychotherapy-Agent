# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, creating a safe and accepting tone. Acceptance could be deeper with more explicit normalization and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic reflections were present but often surface-level and generic. The patient’s ambivalence, fear of judgment, and soccer-related concerns were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on risky alcohol use and protecting the patient’s soccer and scholarship goals. Direction was somewhat directive and practical rather than exploratory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributed input and shaped some decisions, such as choosing the “5 AM practice” line. The psychiatrist still controlled much of the agenda and supplied much of the content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by offering choices and respecting the patient’s pushback without pressure. The frequent scripts and practical advice still made the interaction somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was a good open-ended start, but the psychiatrist mostly supplied reasons, strategies, and scripts rather than eliciting the patient’s own motivation. The patient’s values and internal reasons for change were not systematically explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and accepted some patient preferences, showing partial partnership. The patient was not consistently positioned as the expert on his own life, friends, and risk tolerance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient expressed clear change talk, especially around soccer and scholarships, but the psychiatrist did not effectively reflect, amplify, or expand it. More DARN/CRAFR-style questions would have strengthened this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with the patient’s minimization and validated his concern about overreacting. However, sustain talk was not actively softened or redirected through double-sided reflections or values-based reframing.
