# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is nonjudgmental and avoids blame, but the warmth is somewhat formulaic and occasionally presumes client acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate double-sided reflections, but the focus is more on cognitive positions than underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear conduct focus, but it remains centered on penalty and process rather than moving the client toward change in underage drinking behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas shape the emerging agreement, but the counselor does not consistently follow the client’s repeated desire to close the matter.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports client choice and avoids pressure, but occasionally defines the client’s values or frames recommendations as protective options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses client language and values, but evocation remains superficial and does not deeply elicit internal motivation or long-term discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client to define fair next steps, but repeated similar questions can feel steering rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through agreement questions, but it is not amplified with importance, desire, ability, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with resistance, but some reflections can validate minimization and do not consistently pivot sustain talk toward change.
