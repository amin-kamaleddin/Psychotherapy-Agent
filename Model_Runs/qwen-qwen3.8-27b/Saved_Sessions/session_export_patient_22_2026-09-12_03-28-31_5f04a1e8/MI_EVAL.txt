# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance and accepts the patient's reduction-focused goal. Affirmations are consistent but somewhat formulaic, and could include deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's ambivalence and dual concerns, showing good perspective-taking. The empathic responses could go further by exploring emotional nuance, such as underlying social anxiety and fear of rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing weekday/social drinking and progresses logically from assessment to a concrete, actionable plan. Direction could be strengthened by more explicitly connecting the plan to broader values and summarizing progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by following the patient's preferred options and co-constructing the role-play. Collaboration could be deepened by more explicitly acknowledging the patient as a co-expert and sharing more of the therapeutic process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported through options, invitations, and respect for the patient's choice of reduction over abstinence. The psychiatrist could more explicitly affirm the patient's ownership and self-direction, especially during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits motivation from the patient's own values, goals, and language, using confidence ratings effectively. Evocation could be more varied and deeper, including values exploration and more detailed best-case scenarios.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance and consistently invites the patient's input. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging the patient's expertise and co-creating the agenda more transparently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Targeted questions, confidence ratings, and affirmations effectively evoke and reinforce the patient's change talk. The psychiatrist could vary evocation techniques and build more strongly on broader personal values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and the psychiatrist works within the patient's stated framework. Softening could be more active by using double-sided reflections to help resolve ambivalence.
