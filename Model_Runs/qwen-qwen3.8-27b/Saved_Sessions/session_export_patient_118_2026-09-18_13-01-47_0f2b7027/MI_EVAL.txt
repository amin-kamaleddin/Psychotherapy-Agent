# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warm, non-judgmental respect, using affirming language and never labeling the patient as resistant or non-compliant. The warmth is professional and genuine but stops short of explicitly normalizing the struggle or stating unconditional regard, which would elevate it to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy throughout, with complex reflections that capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions of the patient's experience. The psychiatrist skillfully identifies identity-level meaning, underlying resentment, and the weight of long-standing identity attachment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session shifts significantly toward dental treatment concerns in the final portion, diluting the MI focus on smoking cessation. The dentist/filling discussion, while patient-centered, lacks a clear strategic connection back to the target behavior or explicit negotiation of focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a collaborative dynamic by following the patient's lead, inviting input, and adapting to his shifting focus without imposing an agenda. However, the collaboration remains somewhat passive, lacking explicit co-creation of a plan or inviting the patient to help shape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with explicit statements like 'I'm not here to tell you what to do' and validation of the patient's need for self-determination. The psychiatrist never pressures, never implies the patient should quit, and fully accepts his stated boundaries without attempting to counter.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses evocative questions that draw out the patient's own motivations rather than providing information or advice. Deeper exploration of underlying values (health, family, independence) and more open-ended questions could elicit richer change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent use of language that positions the patient as the expert on his own life, with questions like 'what would that look like to you?' and 'I'm curious what that might look like to you.' The partnership could be strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language to further emphasize the patient's authority over decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully picks up and amplifies change talk as it emerges, framing the patient's statements as positive steps toward change. Could be stronger with more explicit amplification of change talk and more direct exploration of confidence and ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by reflecting identity-level meaning and working with the patient's own framing rather than arguing or pushing back. Limited use of active softening techniques such as double-sided reflections that acknowledge both sides while gently tilting toward change.
