# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental and validating, clearly communicating care for the client’s experience. The main limitation is a somewhat procedural, risk-management tone that can make the warmth feel less individually unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and captures key emotional themes such as exhaustion, pain, and not wanting to die alone. The main gap is returning quickly to safety questions before fully exploring the deeper meaning of the client’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on suicidal ideation and immediate safety, but it becomes somewhat circular. There is limited clear movement toward a single client-owned MI target or concrete step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and occasionally invites the client’s preferences, but the interaction is largely driven by safety protocol. The client’s resistant statements and need to simply “sit in it” are not consistently used to co-create the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices and later respects the client’s need for quiet, but repeated safety directives can feel controlling. Autonomy is acknowledged but not consistently reinforced, especially when the client resists feeling managed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes only limited attempts to draw out the client’s own reasons to stay alive. The session relies heavily on direct safety questions rather than evocative questions that would strengthen the client’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers options, showing some partnership, but the session remains mainly clinician-led. The client’s expertise and preferences do not substantially reshape the direction of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges some change talk, such as the client not wanting to die alone and being too tired to do it alone. However, there are missed opportunities to expand, deepen, and strengthen these statements into fuller change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates rather than argues with the client’s sustain talk, which is appropriate, but there is not enough strategic softening or pivoting toward ambivalence and change. The conversation remains largely focused on risk and safety management.
