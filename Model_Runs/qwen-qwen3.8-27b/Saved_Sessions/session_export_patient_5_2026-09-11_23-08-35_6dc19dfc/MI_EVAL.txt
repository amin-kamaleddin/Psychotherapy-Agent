# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, often validating the patient’s caution and safety-oriented behavior. However, acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and focused on compliance rather than fully acknowledging the patient’s broader ambivalence and emotional distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate reflective listening and support for the patient’s fear and burden. Empathy remains surface-level at times, missing deeper reflections on the push–pull between wanting safety, feeling stuck in waiting, and fear of giving up.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the medication transition, washout timing, side effects, and contacting the prescriber. Direction is strong but somewhat didactic, emphasizing safety information delivery over collaborative MI-style agenda-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers collaborative language and practical help, such as drafting a message, but the overall interaction remains expert-driven. The patient does not substantially lead the conversation or co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some respect for choice by offering optional help, but the session is highly directive with repeated safety instructions. Autonomy support would improve by more consistently framing decisions as the patient’s own and supporting self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with much of the session spent providing information rather than eliciting the patient’s own motivations, values, or readiness. There are several missed opportunities to draw out change talk, confidence, or personal priorities.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely clinician-led, with the psychiatrist serving as the primary source of information and guidance. The patient is not strongly positioned as an expert on her own experience, preferences, and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers some change talk around safety, doing it right, and contacting her prescriber, but the psychiatrist mainly responds with generic affirmations. There is limited evocation or amplification of the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient’s barriers and sustain talk, such as fear of being stuck, hassle, and wanting to act immediately, are often met with more safety warnings and prohibitions. More MI-consistent reflections, reframing, or exploration of ambivalence would better soften sustain talk.
