# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Julian’s concern about legal and paperwork uncertainty. It is solid but somewhat task-focused, without stronger explicit affirmation of Julian’s worth independent of compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Julian’s practical worries and emotional tone, such as feeling steady and avoiding embarrassment. It could go deeper by naming underlying anxiety, fear of judgment, or stress of being court-mandated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stays focused on the target behavior and builds a clear plan around where to go, proof, timing, and first step. The limitation is that direction remains logistical rather than connecting the behavior to Julian’s values or confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works with Julian to construct a concrete plan, including two questions and a selected day. It would be stronger if Julian more clearly set the agenda and the counselor avoided leading the options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently asks Julian what he would ask, do, or need, supporting choice and avoiding pressure. It becomes less explicit when the counselor introduces specific options before fully waiting for Julian to lead.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits practical intentions, such as asking the case worker and choosing a day. It does not deeply draw out Julian’s intrinsic reasons, confidence, values, or preferred outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Julian to name next steps and affirms his ideas, conveying a collaborative stance. The counselor still preloads preferred paths, so power sharing and agenda ownership are not fully realized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Julian generates small logistical change talk, and the psychiatrist reinforces these as strengths. Broader change talk about desire, need, ability, and confidence is not consistently evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Appropriately reframes Julian’s concern about extra paperwork without arguing or making the task feel heavier. It would benefit from more strategic reflections that connect the avoidant concern with capability and change.
