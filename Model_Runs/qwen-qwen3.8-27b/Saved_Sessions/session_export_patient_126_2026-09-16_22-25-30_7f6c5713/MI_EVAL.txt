# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance throughout the session. The stance is generally warm, but not fully unconditional, with moments that subtly tie acceptance to engagement or introduce a practitioner-led frame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening and captures the patient’s mixed feelings well. However, reflections often remain practical and do not deeply explore the emotional weight behind minimization, parenting concerns, or defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a coherent micro-focus on feeling steadier and trying a five-minute break. The main limitation is that the conversation is not consistently connected to the target health behaviors of smoking and alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the patient’s ideas and uses questions rather than direct advice or lectures. The interaction remains collaborative, but the agenda and framing are still largely therapist-guided rather than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and uses conditional, choice-based questions that support patient decision-making. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing that the patient has full choice and no obligation to adopt the proposed idea.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions and avoids giving reasons for change or providing advice. The evocation remains somewhat superficial and practical, without deeper exploration of values, importance, confidence, or intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative, patient-led stance and allows the patient’s ideas to shape the conversation. Full partnership is limited by moments of practitioner-led framing, such as stating that he is “glad you’re open to my lead.”

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some practical change talk around small steps and protecting the relationship with the patient’s child. The change talk is not consistently expanded using deeper MI techniques such as DARN-C, importance/confidence, or values-based amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles minimizing statements well by accepting them and rolling with resistance. The sustain talk is softened effectively, but not always expanded into deeper exploration of the patient’s ambivalence.
