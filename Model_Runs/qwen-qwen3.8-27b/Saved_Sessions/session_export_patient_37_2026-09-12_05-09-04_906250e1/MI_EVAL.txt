# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout, but the acceptance is somewhat professional rather than deeply individualized. There is a missed opportunity to explicitly normalize her fear of being judged by the pediatrician.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture Elena’s ambivalence and perspective, but empathy becomes less consistent when she expresses deeper fear, protectiveness, or shame. The psychiatrist does not fully track or reflect the emotional shift after she realizes natural chickenpox can be serious.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the vaccination decision and progressively narrows to practical next steps. However, the educational and procedural emphasis sometimes overtakes a fully client-led motivational process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena is invited to make choices and co-create some content, showing functional collaboration. The psychiatrist, however, remains the primary source of medical information, wording, and direction rather than a fully equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms Elena’s freedom to choose and offers multiple options without pressuring her to vaccinate. Autonomy is slightly undercut when external expert authority is foregrounded, such as stating vaccination is much safer overall.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended early questions elicit some concerns, ambivalence, and change talk. The session then shifts into information-giving and structured choices, limiting deeper and more consistent evocation of Elena’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Elena to shape parts of the session, but also takes on a clear expert role by supplying content and wording. This reduces the sense that Elena’s own wisdom and preferences lead the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on it. Elena’s own values, hopes, and readiness are not deeply developed in a skillful MI manner.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids attacking Elena’s concerns and keeps the conversation nonadversarial, but uses direct expert counterarguments and a bottom-line statement. More MI-consistent techniques, such as double-sided reflections, are not consistently used to soften sustain talk.
