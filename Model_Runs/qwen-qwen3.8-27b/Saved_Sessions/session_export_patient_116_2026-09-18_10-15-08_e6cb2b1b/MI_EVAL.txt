# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and non-judgment throughout; the psychiatrist never labels, criticizes, or shows disapproval of Samir's nonadherence. Acceptance could be stronger by explicitly normalizing Samir's resistance as an understandable response to medical authority rather than simply tolerating it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate, well-timed reflections (e.g., the pill-bottle moment, the 'honestly' shift). Some reflections stay at the surface level rather than capturing the full emotional intensity, such as the feeling of being reduced to 'a form being filled out.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session drifts in its early portion into rapport-building and lacks a clear sense of progression toward a specific goal. No summaries are used to consolidate themes or collaboratively identify a focus, making the session feel somewhat circular.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a genuine collaborative stance with inclusive, non-hierarchical language and actively incorporates Samir's feedback. However, the collaboration could be deeper through more explicit co-creation of the agenda or negotiating focus with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout; the psychiatrist consistently reinforces Samir's ownership of choices without any pressure, covert recommendations, or implied 'better' alternatives. This represents the gold standard of autonomy support, particularly given the client's resistance to medical authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open, exploratory questions are used but they elicit descriptions of Samir's current (sustain) process rather than motivations for change. There are missed opportunities to use values exploration, future-oriented questions, or even subtle probes for doubt that could open entry points for change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear partnership stance is maintained with explicit power-handing language ('I want to turn it over to you') and a non-hierarchical dynamic. The psychiatrist could more actively invite Samir to shape the direction of the conversation beyond just responding to questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Efforts to evoke change talk are limited and indirect; affirmations like 'You know yourself better than a schedule can' reinforce Samir's current approach rather than opening space for new perspectives. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to develop even minimal change language or probe for any sliver of doubt about the current system.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without challenging or arguing; the psychiatrist validates autonomy themes and pre-empts anticipated pushback. Could be strengthened with more double-sided reflections to create space for change talk to emerge alongside the sustain talk.
