# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently respected the client’s ambivalence and avoided arguing, correcting, or moralizing about smoking. Acceptance was clear and nonjudgmental, though it could have felt warmer and more personally individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used strong reflective listening and captured the client’s mixed feelings, need for autonomy, and value of the break. Some reflections were slightly leading or inferential, and the session could have explored deeper emotions more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on the target behavior and narrowed the goal to a concrete, manageable step: pausing for one break. Direction was strong overall, though the discussion became somewhat repetitive and would benefit from brief summaries to consolidate progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician worked with the client to build a plan rather than prescribing one, using the client’s own ideas as the foundation. The collaboration was strong, though the clinician still guided the process somewhat more than a fully equal partner might.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was excellent, with the clinician repeatedly framing any change as the client’s own choice, experiment, and terms. The client was able to generate a plan that preserved control, including the right to stop if it felt unhelpful.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out the client’s own motivations, values, and possible actions rather than providing external reasons for change. Evocation was skillful, though some questions were slightly leading and introduced specific monitoring language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician treated the client as the expert on his own life and used the client’s language throughout. Partnership was strong, though the clinician frequently introduced the session’s conceptual framework, which slightly reduced the balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician successfully elicited meaningful change talk and helped the client move toward a self-directed plan. The evocation of change talk was frequent and effective, though it could have been more systematic using scaling, confidence, or explicit DARN-C elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician handled resistance well by balancing the client’s reasons to maintain smoking with gentle invitations toward choice and possibility. The response was effective, though repeated use of terms like “quitting” and “change” may have kept the client partially anchored in resistance.
