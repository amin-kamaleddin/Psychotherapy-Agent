# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, normalizes Eric’s experience, and avoids labeling or criticizing his smoking. It is not a 5 because some affirmations sound procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Eric’s ambivalence, exhaustion, and feeling controlled by his wife’s repeated comments. Deeper feelings beneath his self-reliance and “I’m fine” are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear focus on smoking and cutting back, but drifts toward the wife’s nagging and general venting. It lacks a refocusing summary that returns to smoking or a small next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites Eric to choose how to proceed and follows his lead when he chooses venting. Collaboration is limited because the counselor still controls the agenda through repeated menu-style options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes Eric’s choice, says it is okay not to be ready, and avoids pushing advice or planning. It could more explicitly affirm Eric’s ownership of his body, decisions, and change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite Eric to express what smoking means and what change he wants. These openings are often not developed deeply enough, especially around the pause, health concern, and trust.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an authoritarian stance and asks for Eric’s input, creating a generally collaborative tone. It is not fully developed because Eric’s own values, goals, and priorities do not substantially shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few change-oriented statements, but the psychiatrist does not effectively reflect, amplify, or follow them. Eric’s limited change talk is left underdeveloped as the session shifts to venting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and accepts Eric’s “I’m fine” and “I’m strong.” It does not use double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to redirect sustain talk toward change.
