# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor shows basic respect and validation, but the session remains brief and largely directive. Unconditional positive regard is present but not strongly or consistently demonstrated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several empathic statements, but the counselor does not deeply explore the patient’s feelings or ambivalence. Reflections are helpful yet somewhat surface-level for the complexity of her stress and parenting concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking reduction and practical next steps. Direction is strong, with a concrete, actionable plan toward a patch trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and offers choices, but still drives the problem-solving process. The interaction feels partially collaborative rather than a true partnership between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient is repeatedly given choices and the opportunity to decide what feels doable. Autonomy is respected, though the counselor could have paused longer before suggesting interventions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor moves quickly to advice and planning without drawing out the patient’s own motivations for change. More time exploring her values, reasons for change, and readiness would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks the patient what she would like to focus on and offers options. However, the overall tone remains clinician-led rather than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient’s own change talk is not actively elicited or strengthened. The conversation shifts rapidly to options before the patient’s reasons for change are fully developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient’s resistance and concerns about stress, exhaustion, and needing a break are acknowledged but not deeply explored. Sustain talk is not consistently softened or used to build motivation.
