# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, non-judgmental tone throughout, validating even defensive pushback without correction. Warmth occasionally feels slightly patterned rather than deeply individualized to Elena's specific circumstances (e.g., single parenthood, guilt).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate, nuanced reflective listening that captures the emotional meaning behind behaviors (e.g., 'not just the smoke, but that little pocket of calm'). Skilled corrections show genuine understanding of the client's frame, moving well beyond surface-level paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Follows a clear strategic arc from exploring smoking's function to identifying an alternative to reinforcing client-generated action. One minor drift (the timing question) requires recovery, preventing a perfect score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely co-constructed, with Elena's ideas driving the direction throughout. The psychiatrist never lectures, advises, or takes over problem-solving, instead listening, reflecting, and gently guiding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly honors Elena's right to choose not to change ('if anything,' 'you don't have to be sure it'll work') and consistently affirms her ownership and self-paced progress. Never pressures, argues, or imposes a timeline, even when Elena minimizes her own progress.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never inserts external reasons for change — no health statistics, moral arguments, or advice. All motivation and solutions are entirely self-generated by Elena, which is the hallmark of masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within Elena, explicitly stating 'You're the one who said it — not me. I just listened.' Genuinely willing to be corrected and follow the client's lead rather than the clinical agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and reinforces Elena's own change-oriented language, bouncing it back in affirming ways. Could be stronger with more explicit DARN-CAT evocation or a confidence/commitment question to consolidate the identified step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively validates and gently redirects sustain talk without ever arguing or dismissing (e.g., 'You're right. Ten minutes in a parking lot isn't going to magically end a habit'). Could benefit from more explicit double-sided reflections to further balance the ambivalence.
