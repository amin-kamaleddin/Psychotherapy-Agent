# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing stance that reduces defensiveness. Acceptance could be strengthened by more direct affirmations of the client's worth, courage, and resilience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client's ambivalence, exhaustion, and emotional tone. The clinician could deepen empathy by exploring underlying feelings such as guilt, fear, and the burden of constant responsibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use, coping, and the second drink. It effectively moves from broad ambivalence to a concrete, manageable action step without drifting to other issues.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a plan using the client's language, ideas, and preferred coping strategies. Collaboration could be more fully client-led by allowing the client to generate more options before introducing structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure, advice, and shame while supporting the client's sense of choice. Autonomy support would be stronger with explicit language affirming that the client is ultimately in charge of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses open questions to elicit the client's own motivations, solutions, and plan rather than persuading or lecturing. Evocation could be deepened by more explicitly drawing out values, hopes, fears, and confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert and invites her judgment throughout the session. Partnership could be made more explicit through stronger power-sharing language such as acknowledging that the client knows her life best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits practical willingness and some action-oriented change language, such as trying music and reducing to one drink. There is room to better amplify values, confidence, and stronger personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, correction, or reinforcement. The clinician could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly hold both sides and then pivot toward the client's values.
