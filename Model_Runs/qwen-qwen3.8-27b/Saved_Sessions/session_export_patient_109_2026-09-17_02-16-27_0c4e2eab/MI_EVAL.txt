# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Some affirmations were slightly generalized, and the opening framing was mildly inferential before the patient fully stated the issue.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflections captured the patient's ambivalence, practical constraints, and emotional experience. A few reflections became slightly idealizing and did not consistently deepen underlying feelings such as shame or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the target behavior of re-engaging with physical activity. The conversation progressed logically from barriers to options, then to a specific, realistic action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited the patient to co-create the plan. However, the therapist still did much of the structuring and summarizing, making the collaboration slightly more guided than fully peer-like.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was strong and consistent, with repeated emphasis on the patient's choice and self-direction. The psychiatrist did not impose a plan and respected the patient's limited motivation and constraints.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out the patient's own motivations, ideas, and commitments through evocative questions. The evocation was somewhat narrow, with limited use of deeper values, confidence, or future-self language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned the patient as the source of useful judgment and used inclusive language. The therapist's frequent framing and organization subtly placed them in a more guiding role rather than a fully co-constructive one.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful action-oriented change talk, such as the patient generating specific, doable steps. There was less exploration of deeper change talk related to values, confidence, or future-self imagery.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing or minimizing, and the therapist redirected toward smaller, manageable steps. The therapist did not consistently use explicit double-sided reflections to reframe sustain talk into ambivalence resolution.
