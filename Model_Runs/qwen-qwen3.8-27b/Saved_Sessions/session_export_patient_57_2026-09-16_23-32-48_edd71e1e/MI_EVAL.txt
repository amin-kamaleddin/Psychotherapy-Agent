# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is warm, respectful, and avoids shame while validating the stress of being questioned. It falls short of 5 because support sometimes leans on therapist-applied praise rather than explicit unconditional regard independent of team behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the conflict between fitting in, avoiding weakness, and wanting to avoid drinking before practice. It is not 5 because some reflections remain cognitive, infer unstated motives, and do not fully explore fear of rejection or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use, team expectations, and practical strategies such as holding keys or skipping shots. It lacks a concise explicit behavioral target and a collaboratively defined next step, with some drift into identity and leadership themes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works as a partner, reflecting and building on the client’s ideas about limiting, driving, and involving a teammate. A fully collaborative 5 would show more client-controlled agenda-setting rather than the counselor structuring much of the framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces the client’s choice, ownership, and self-direction without commands, ultimatums, or pressure. The language around keys is especially strong in reframing the plan as a personally owned role rather than an external threat.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections help the client articulate his own values, risks, and possible strategies rather than accepting reasons from the counselor. It is not 5 because the counselor does not consistently deepen motivation with scaling, confidence, past success, or explicit commitment questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicates respect for the client’s knowledge of his peers and team while avoiding an expert stance. It falls short of 5 because the counselor still selects many topics and frames, with less evidence of negotiated agenda and client-led direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently evokes change language, and the client offers meaningful strategies such as skipping shots, staying in control, and protecting teammates. It is not 5 because the counselor does not consistently amplify, summarize, or use DARN-C language, scaling, and affirmations of past ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handles sustain talk without arguing or moralizing and offers smaller workable steps that reduce the overwhelming size of the problem. It falls short of 5 because there is less consistent use of double-sided reflections to pivot ambivalence toward stronger change talk.
