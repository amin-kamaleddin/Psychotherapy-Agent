# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and validation, but the warmth is brief and quickly shifts to advice-giving. Acceptance remains more clinical and task-oriented than sustained unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges that the breathing problems are scary and draining and that the client's frustration is understandable. However, the reflections are surface-level and are quickly followed by problem-solving rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and related lifestyle behaviors, which is appropriate for the target behavior. However, the direction is heavily medical and logistical, emphasizing practical next steps rather than values-based exploration or motivational direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for client input at times, and the client does contribute ideas such as using a patch rather than gum. However, the psychiatrist often redirects or overrides these suggestions with clinician-driven plans, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices and acknowledges the client's pace, which supports autonomy to a degree. However, the overall tone is directive, with frequent recommendations and plans that weaken consistent autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly provides reasons for change rather than eliciting the client's own motivations and values. Questions are often closed-ended or multiple-choice, limiting the client's opportunity to articulate change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts an expert-driven stance, offering detailed medical advice, drafting messages, and outlining step-by-step plans. The interaction resembles a medical consultation more than a collaborative partnership between equals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to draw out or amplify the client's own reasons for change. When the client states wanting to stop flare-ups without losing his livelihood, the psychiatrist does not reflect or explore this in a way that strengthens intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges harm reduction as valuable, but also states that quitting entirely gives the best lung benefit, which functions as a subtle counter-argument. The client's resistance and sustain talk are not consistently softened or redirected using MI-consistent strategies.
