# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with strong validating statements throughout, but occasionally reads as slightly scripted and does not fully normalize the struggle with past failed attempts in a way that conveys deeper unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Erik's ambivalence and emotional shifts. However, occasionally shifts into interpretation or self-correction rather than staying purely within the client's emotional world.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression from ambivalence to identifying a window of change, with each question building on the previous one. The session ends slightly abruptly without a clear collaborative closing that ties the thread together or identifies a specific first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing and effective repair when challenged, with good use of Erik's own language. However, occasionally imposes interpretations (e.g., 'It comes from you') rather than fully co-creating meaning with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-directive with explicit affirmations of Erik's autonomy and ownership throughout. Could be strengthened in the closing moments by more explicitly reinforcing that the choice is entirely his and he can explore at his own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully uses questions to draw out Erik's own motivations, successfully moving him from 'what's the point' to 'where would you say I start.' Could be stronger by more explicitly connecting change to Erik's deeper values, family, and identity rather than primarily behavioral aspects.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive language and avoids expert positioning, demonstrating power-sharing when overstepping. Slightly uneven in moments where reflections are more interpretive than exploratory, rather than inviting Erik to share his own understanding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk at key moments with strategic questions and affirmations. Does not fully develop the change talk that emerges, particularly the final readiness statement, which could have been leveraged more deeply to solidify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by holding both sides, reframing defeatist language, and exploring underneath resistance rather than arguing against it. Could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the legitimacy of resistance like 'I don't want it to be fixable' before gently exploring it.
