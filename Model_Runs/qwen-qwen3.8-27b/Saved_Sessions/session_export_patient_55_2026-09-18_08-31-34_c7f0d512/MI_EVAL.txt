# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warm, nonjudgmental presence throughout the session, validating Isabelle's honesty without labeling or minimizing. Her response to Isabelle's pushback about feeling 'in a box' is a strong example of genuine acceptance in action.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate reflections that capture the emotional texture of Isabelle's experience, such as the loneliness of holding silence while others play and the intrusion of music into rest. The reflections add meaning and depth rather than merely paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a coherent thematic arc from general overwhelm to the specific challenge of stopping, with smooth client-driven transitions. However, it lacks a clear closing summary or collaborative identification of a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of collaborative interaction where Isabelle's contributions directly shape the session in real time, such as the immediate pivot when she asks to be talked about her music. The psychiatrist consistently treats Isabelle as the expert on her own experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength of the session; the psychiatrist never imposes external standards and consistently frames choices as Isabelle's to make. Key phrases like 'What would it feel like to be the one who tells you?' powerfully support her capacity for self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of Isabelle's self-knowledge and internal motivation, drawing out her own recognition of the cycle and her existing self-awareness. Could go one step further in moments like 'I don't know how' to elicit more specific ability or reason language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions herself as a partner rather than an expert, using inclusive language and collaborative agenda-setting. The moment where Isabelle's feedback about feeling 'in a box' directly changes the psychiatrist's behavior in real time exemplifies advanced MI practice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change-relevant language and amplifies Isabelle's self-awareness, such as 'that self-awareness is something to trust.' Could be more systematic in drawing out specific DARN-CAT language, such as asking what a small version of stopping would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Isabelle's fear of falling behind and the perceived binary without endorsing it, using phrases like 'No third option in the frame' to subtly signal alternatives. Could have more actively deployed double-sided reflections to further soften the sustain talk.
