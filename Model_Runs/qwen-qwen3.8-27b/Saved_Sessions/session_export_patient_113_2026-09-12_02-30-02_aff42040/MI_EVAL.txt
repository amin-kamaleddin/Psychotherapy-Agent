# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and validates Rowan's ambivalence. Acceptance is strong but feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture several of Rowan's feelings, but often remain surface-level. The counselor misses deeper emotional nuances, such as fear, loss of control, or feeling policed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session starts with the target behavior but quickly shifts to work stress and practical problem-solving. It lacks consistent focus on the device-use goal and does not effectively reconnect the conversation to the referral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks for Rowan's preferences, supporting shared decision-making. However, the counselor occasionally positions himself as the solution provider, limiting fully collaborative planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through affirmations of choice, non-judgment, and respect for Rowan's decisions. Some suggestions remain subtly directive, preventing a perfect score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some open questions, but the counselor does not consistently draw out or build on Rowan's own motivations. Opportunities to evoke confidence and self-efficacy are missed in favor of practical advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses power-sharing language and invites Rowan's input, but also steps into an expert/helper role. Partnership is present but not fully realized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is only briefly acknowledged and not skillfully amplified or developed. The counselor misses opportunities to use Rowan's own statements to explore motivation and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing, but it is not effectively softened or redirected. The counselor does not use double-sided reflections or connect autonomy and values to the target behavior.
