# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintained a nonjudgmental, validating stance and respected Diego’s ambivalence. However, repeated safety directives and the repeated rehearsal prompt felt slightly parental and reduced warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately tracked Diego’s perspective and goals, but remained more cognitive than affective. Deeper feelings such as stigma, discomfort, and the burden of unsaid material were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a concrete harm-reduction goal, especially Narcan safety and communication with the housemate. The focus occasionally became repetitive and procedural rather than strategically advancing the conversation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some co-creation, including scripts and adaptation to Diego’s preferences for brevity and privacy. The psychiatrist supplied much of the content and structure, making the interaction feel more expert-led than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego made key choices about wording, timing, and follow-up, showing meaningful autonomy support. Repeated directive safety language and persistent rehearsal requests after Diego had already stated readiness felt somewhat controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist began with useful evocation by asking how heroin use fits with Diego’s current life goals. Much of the rest of the session shifted into advice and instruction rather than eliciting deeper values, confidence, or self-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and allowed Diego to shape parts of the plan. However, the session was often expert-driven, with multiple options and scripts offered before Diego had space to generate his own approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego expressed clear change talk, and the psychiatrist affirmed it appropriately. The change talk was acknowledged more than amplified, with little use of deeper questions, scaling, or summary reinforcement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not met with argument, shame, or judgment. The response was mostly neutral acceptance and redirection rather than active softening through double-sided reflection or normalized ambivalence.
