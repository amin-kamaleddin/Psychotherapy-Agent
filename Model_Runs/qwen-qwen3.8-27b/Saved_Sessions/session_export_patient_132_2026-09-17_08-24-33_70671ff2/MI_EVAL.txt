# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing stance toward Yasmin’s dieting struggles. It could strengthen to a 5 by adding more explicit, emotionally warm affirmation of her worth independent of the scale or her choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing frustration, ambivalence, and the shift from “willpower” to “fuel.” Deeper validation of shame, exhaustion, body-image pressure, and the emotional meaning of the scale would make the empathy more complete.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays reasonably focused on moving from unsustainable dieting toward workable habits and a healthier relationship with the scale. Periodic summaries could better reconnect the scale discussion to the broader goal of sustainable, non-diet health.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Yasmin’s emerging ideas, such as breakfast, strength training, and a two-column note. The therapist still leads the structure and sometimes summarizes or frames the plan before the client fully owns it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is clear autonomy support through open-ended questions and avoidance of pressure or directives. The psychiatrist could more explicitly emphasize Yasmin’s freedom to choose, modify, or abandon any proposed experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Yasmin’s own reasons for change, especially around eating breakfast and training for strength. Deeper exploration of values, importance, confidence, and internal motivation would make the evocation stronger.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Yasmin as the expert and avoids giving direct advice. Some therapist-authored summaries, such as naming “the experiment,” subtly take ownership of the formulation and could be softened.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk about breakfast, strength, and feeling “fueled” rather than starving. More deliberate amplification, reflection, and consolidation of the client’s change statements would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** This is the main area for growth because Yasmin’s sustain talk around the scale remains strong and is only gently held rather than strategically redirected. More double-sided reflections and values-based pivots away from the number as the verdict would improve this criterion.
