# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Daniela’s fear of stigma and ambivalence. Acceptance could be strengthened with warmer, more explicit affirmations of her courage or worth independent of disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and consistent, capturing both sides of Daniela’s ambivalence and her identity concerns. The counselor frequently uses Daniela’s own language and offers complex reflections of the meaning behind her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target: low-key disclosure to Sarah that preserves Daniela’s normalcy and control. The conversation moves purposefully from selecting a person to testing disclosure, boundaries, and trust signals without significant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is co-constructive, with Daniela contributing the person, language, and criteria for success. The psychiatrist works within Daniela’s frame rather than imposing a clinical or prescriptive agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoids controlling language and returns decision-making to Daniela. Daniela is supported in choosing the pace, amount, and limits of disclosure, including her right to step back if it becomes too heavy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Daniela’s own motivations and strategies rather than supplying reasons for change. It could be stronger by more deliberately eliciting and amplifying values, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor positions Daniela as the expert on her friendships, goals, and readiness for disclosure. The session is driven by Daniela’s contributions rather than by a prescribed disclosure plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites useful client-generated change talk and concrete next steps, such as testing disclosure with Sarah and using a boundary phrase. Change talk could be further developed by explicitly eliciting and summarizing DARN-C language, especially confidence and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniela’s concerns are acknowledged without argument and reframed as protective and understandable. The response could be stronger by using more double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to autonomy and values when fear is prominent.
