# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance while Richard expresses ambivalence about medication and concern about being a burden. The stance is warm and affirming, though somewhat formulaic and not deeply emotionally affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate content-level reflections of Richard’s goals, barriers, and practical plans. Emotional nuances such as fear of slowing his wife down and self-doubt are not consistently captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cholesterol management, walking, rechecking labs, and the medication decision. The direction is clear but sometimes becomes overly prescriptive with counselor-specific details before Richard fully articulates them.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as collaborative and Richard’s input shapes parts of the plan. The session remains more counselor-led than truly equal, especially when specific plans, scripts, and timing are introduced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through permission-like questions and acceptance of Richard’s chosen timing for scheduling the recheck. However, several counselor-generated specifics make the process feel more directed than client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard is effectively invited to express his own motivations, plans, confidence, and backup strategies. The evocative quality is reduced when the counselor supplies solutions, scripts, and plan details too early.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Richard’s choices and input, showing some partnership. The power balance is uneven because the counselor frequently structures the plan and introduces next steps on the client’s behalf.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence scaling effectively evoke Richard’s values, plans, and commitment to walking and checking in with his wife. The counselor does not consistently deepen the strongest change talk or stay fully evocative.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and redirects concerns about medication and fatigue into manageable action. The softening is effective but not consistently strategic or explicit with double-sided reflections.
