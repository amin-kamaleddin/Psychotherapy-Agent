# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard, normalizing Nathaniel’s ambivalence and protecting him from shame. The warmth is solid but sometimes feels somewhat clinical and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong reflective listening and accurately captures Nathaniel’s ambivalence, fear, and protective concerns. Some reflections are a bit generic or repetitive, and the emotional weight of the fear could have been explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of coffee use and guides the conversation toward a concrete, self-chosen experiment. At times the direction becomes slightly abstract, and the psychiatrist could more explicitly link Nathaniel’s values and health worries to the proposed step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language, incorporates Nathaniel’s ideas and metaphors, and avoids imposing a predetermined plan. The session would feel even more collaborative with more explicit co-creation and direct invitations to set or correct the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open, nonprescriptive questions and strongly supports Nathaniel’s need for control, including keeping a safety valve. The repeated focus on “next step” may subtly imply that change is expected, rather than explicitly affirming that the choice is entirely Nathaniel’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits Nathaniel’s own motivations, ideas, and solutions rather than supplying reasons for change. The evocation is often procedural and could be deeper by exploring Nathaniel’s values, health concerns, and discrepancy between current use and longer-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Nathaniel as the expert on his own experience, and Nathaniel’s ideas clearly shape the session. The partnership would be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment that Nathaniel ultimately decides whether any experiment is worthwhile.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes useful change talk, including Nathaniel’s own practical experiment, flexibility, and self-monitoring ideas. The skill is strong but could be enhanced by more systematically eliciting and affirming specific change talk themes such as desire, ability, need, commitment, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against or reinforcing Nathaniel’s sustain talk and generally responds nonjudgmentally. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently soften or redirect statements like “coffee is fuel” or “it keeps me together,” leaving the motivational force of sustain talk somewhat unaddressed.
