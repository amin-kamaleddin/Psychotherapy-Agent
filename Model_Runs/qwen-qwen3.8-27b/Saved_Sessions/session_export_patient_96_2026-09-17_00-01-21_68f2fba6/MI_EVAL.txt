# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor respects the client’s autonomy, avoids judgment, and repairs irritation by clarifying they are not trying to tell the client to remove the piercing. The tone is warm and respectful, though at times it feels more procedural than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses strong reflective listening to capture the client’s focus on autonomy, respect, and irritation. Some reflections become repetitive and do not fully explore the deeper emotional or identity meaning behind the client’s strong sustain talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally keeps the conversation focused on the lip piercing, oral health, and practical concerns. Focus occasionally drifts into meta-discussion about how the counselor should support the client rather than consistently guiding toward a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client to define what would feel respectful, supportive, and useful, shaping the session around the client’s preferences. It falls short of full collaboration because the counselor still leads parts of the agenda and positions themselves as the provider of backup information.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is one of the strongest parts of the session, with repeated affirmation that the client is in charge and that removal is not being demanded. Statements such as “I’m not here to tell you to remove it” and “I can be the backup you use only when you decide to use it” demonstrate excellent autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits the client’s values, priorities, and preferred style of support, which is meaningful. However, evocation is limited because it is not consistently used to draw out internal motivation for change or connect values to possible health-related actions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor clearly communicates that the client’s expertise and judgment reside with her, using phrases like “you’re the one making these calls” and “work from your lead.” The partnership is strong but not perfect because the counselor still frames parts of the agenda around long-term health and structured options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little explicit evocation of change talk, with minimal attention to reasons, desire, ability, need, or commitment related to managing or changing the piercing. Some future help-seeking language appears, but it is not consistently amplified or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s reasons for maintaining the piercing and redirects toward autonomy and practical support. However, the session remains heavily centered on sustain talk, and the counselor does not consistently use double-sided reflections or skillfully pivot toward change or health-related values.
