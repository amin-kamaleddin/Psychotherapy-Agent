# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses warm, nonjudgmental statements that validate Elena's concerns. The structured problem-solving tone occasionally makes the interaction feel less deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Elena's values, ambivalence, and team-related worries with good nuance. The counselor sometimes moves quickly from emotional exploration to scripts and logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior and the concrete Tuesday trial. It moves steadily from values clarification to an actionable plan with little drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena contributes meaningfully to decisions about timing, messaging, and risk level. The counselor's structured scripts and contingency plans can make the process feel somewhat counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites choices, emphasizes reversibility, and supports Elena's control over the plan. Providing many prewritten options can shift the client from generating solutions to selecting among them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions help Elena articulate her own motivations and increase her confidence from 6 to 8. The counselor's frequent provision of wording and structure reduces the purely evocative quality.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a partner by offering options and letting Elena choose key elements. The amount of expert content supplied can slightly limit the sense of the client as the primary source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently elicits and affirms change talk, including progress and values-based reasons for change. Some change language is reflected rather than fully amplified or extended.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is present but not dominant, and the counselor validates concerns without arguing or minimizing them. More explicit double-sided reflections would further transform worry into change language.
