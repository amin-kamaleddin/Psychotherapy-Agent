# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honors Marco's request for practical, non-lecture help. It falls short of a 5 because the acceptance remains somewhat task-oriented and does not deeply normalize his ambivalence or fatigue.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Marco's preference for practicality and his history in a safe, surface-level way. However, the reflections do not fully explore the deeper exhaustion, cynicism, or identity-related weight of his long-term smoking and health challenges.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays organized and moves clearly toward a concrete dental self-care plan. Its direction is limited, however, because the oral health goal is not used more strategically to explore possible smoking reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco's choices meaningfully shape the plan, such as selecting gum, flossing frequency, and storage location. The collaboration is uneven, though, because the psychiatrist supplies most of the technical details rather than fully co-constructing the plan from Marco's perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly supports Marco's choices, avoids pressure, and respects his boundaries about reminders and timelines. This is not a 5 because the volume of advice could subtly steer him, and ownership of setbacks is not consistently explored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated ideas about feasibility and how success would feel. The evocation remains fairly practical and does not draw out deeper values, self-image, or motivation related to broader health or smoking reduction.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist providing much of the content and technical guidance. Marco is not sufficiently treated as the primary expert on his own habits, preferences, and likely barriers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk around small dental self-care actions. However, the change talk is limited, practical, and not skillfully expanded to broader motivations, confidence, or smoking-related change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Marco's sustain talk, which is appropriate, but then accepts the status quo too quickly. There is little use of double-sided reflection, reframing, or gentle pivoting to explore the other side of his ambivalence.
