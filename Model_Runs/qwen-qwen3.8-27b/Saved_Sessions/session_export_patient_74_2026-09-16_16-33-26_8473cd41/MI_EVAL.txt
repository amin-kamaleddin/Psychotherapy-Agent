# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Linda’s experience without blaming her smoking. It could be slightly deeper by affirming her intrinsic worth beyond her parental role.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Linda’s ambivalence and uses her own language effectively. Some reflections remain cognitive and could probe the underlying emotional pain more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target concern and progresses logically toward practical strategies. It could be strengthened by periodically summarizing themes and linking strategies back to Linda’s values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is framed as joint exploration, with frequent invitations for Linda’s input and use of her ideas. More explicit acknowledgment that her contributions are shaping the session would deepen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Linda’s ownership of decisions without pressure, advice, or implied obligation to change. This is a clear strength, especially in honoring her right to continue her current behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit Linda’s values, concerns, and practical ideas rather than providing reasons for change. The evocation could be more strategically targeted toward change talk about smoking specifically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda is consistently positioned as the expert on her family and household needs. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicit co-creation of the agenda and sharing the psychiatrist’s perspective more openly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some values-based language but does not consistently cultivate change talk about reducing or quitting smoking. There are missed opportunities to explore discrepancy, desire, or ability to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without harsh rebuttal, but is given considerable space and sometimes validated in ways that could reinforce it. The psychiatrist could more consistently redirect from sustain talk toward values or discrepancy.
