# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental tone with strong validation of the patient's minimization and corrections. Occasional slight interpretive overreach (e.g., 'That sounds exhausting') subtly shifts from pure acceptance to gentle challenge, preventing a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and nuanced reflective listening throughout, capturing both emotional and cognitive layers of the patient's experience. Demonstrates sophisticated understanding of the underlying social anxiety, surfacing the 'empty hand' and 'ticket' insights that the patient only articulates after the reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and logical arc from rapport-building through exploration to self-generated strategy and trigger identification, with explorations always tied back to the target behavior. Could benefit from a brief collaborative summary or closing that reinforces what was accomplished and affirms forward direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration between equals, with the action plan and trigger identification emerging entirely from the patient. Language throughout communicates mutual respect ('I'm curious,' 'Does that land?'), making the session feel like two people thinking together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support with explicit statements of patient control and no prescriptive language anywhere in the session. All actions framed as the patient's choices, and his stated boundaries (e.g., not stopping going out) met with full acceptance and working within that frame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation with a remarkable progression from self-identified pattern to self-generated strategy to self-identified trigger, all elicited through strategic questioning. The clinician never provides external reasons, statistics, or expert knowledge—everything is drawn out from within the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within the patient, following his lead rather than imposing a predetermined agenda. The session direction is shaped substantially by the patient's contributions, with the clinician serving as a mirror and question-asker.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes a natural progression from minimal change talk ('I guess it's not like I'm some alcoholic') to concrete self-generated action plans ('wait for the third round'). The evocation is progressive, never forced, and culminates in the patient articulating a specific strategy and identifying his own trigger.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully through validation and a double-sided approach (acknowledging 'I function' while inviting exploration of the gap). Occasional reflections of sustain talk linger slightly (e.g., 'That sounds exhausting'), though overall the softening is effective and respectful.
