# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated nonjudgmental warmth and normalized missed doses as understandable and care-related. There was no labeling, blaming, or minimization of the client's concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate and clinically attuned reflections of ambivalence, control concerns, and double-dosing worry. Some reflections were summarizing rather than deeply exploring underlying anxiety, dread, or self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on medication adherence and moved logically from routines to anchors, standards, cues, and a one-week trial. The direction could be stronger by explicitly consolidating the agreed target behavior and next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well by inviting the client to propose and shape solutions, such as morning tea, pocket storage, and a jacket anchor. The session could reach a higher level by giving the client more explicit control over choosing which topics to explore.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasized choice, ownership, and a flexible “good enough” standard. Language avoided pressure, mandates, or controlling advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used open, client-centered questions to elicit the client's own values, preferences, and solutions. Change talk and practical ideas emerged organically rather than being supplied by the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the client as the expert on her own life and built the plan from her contributions. There was clear power sharing and client-directed influence throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reflected and expanded the client's desire, ability, and values-based reasons for change. The change talk was not fully maximized by deeper exploration of confidence, readiness, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, endorsing, or amplifying it. It could be further improved by more actively reframing minimization statements into future-oriented change language.
