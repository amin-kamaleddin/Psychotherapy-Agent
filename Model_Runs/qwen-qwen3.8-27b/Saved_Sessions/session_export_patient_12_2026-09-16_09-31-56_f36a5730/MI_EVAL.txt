# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, offering clear affirmations and avoiding blame for drug use. Some language is formulaic and repeated safety checks can feel slightly corrective rather than fully affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers reasonable reflections of shame, hopelessness, and numbness but often interrupts emotional content with questions and suggestions. Important statements about isolation and being unseen are not deeply reflected, limiting empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly focused on safety, isolation, and immediate coping while addressing substance use risk. Direction is inconsistent due to repeated shifts among many options and repeated safety checks, reducing momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira’s ideas shape the final plan, indicating partial collaboration. The therapist frequently generates multiple options and structures the conversation, which can make the client feel passive rather than equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and asks for preferences, supporting Amira’s autonomy. However, directive statements such as giving a specific phone placement and declaring “That’s your plan” weaken autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses readiness scaling and small-step questions to evoke Amira’s own intentions. Evocation remains limited because the therapist often offers solutions instead of drawing out values, fears, hopes, and past strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choice and allows Amira to influence the plan, showing genuine partnership. Frequent numbered options and the statement “That’s your plan for tonight” can make the interaction feel more clinician-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses readiness scaling and asks for small steps, evoking some change talk. However, change language is not consistently deepened, amplified, or connected to values and past capabilities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged without arguing, and the therapist occasionally redirects toward small actions. Double-sided reflections or strategic pivots are used too infrequently to resolve ambivalence.
