# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Linda’s concerns without arguing or criticizing. The main limitation is that the acceptance is somewhat procedural and could be more individualized and emotionally specific.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are accurate and helpful reflections of Linda’s ambivalence, including her desire for better breathing and fear of long-term medication effects. The limitation is that empathy is often brief and not used to fully deepen or explore the emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on asthma management, inhaler use, and a concrete next step with her clinician. The main limitation is that the direction sometimes shifts too quickly into medical problem-solving before the motivational process is fully explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and allows Linda’s preferences to influence the plan, especially her request for simplicity and a shorter script. However, the collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content rather than first eliciting Linda’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering options, inviting input, and accepting Linda’s preference for a simpler plan. The score is limited by some subtle directional recommendations and the amount of expert guidance provided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions help Linda articulate goals, but later questioning becomes more practical and closed rather than exploring her values, confidence, and motivations. The psychiatrist often shifts to advice and planning instead of continuing to elicit Linda’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally collaborative stance and invites Linda to participate in choosing next steps. The interaction is somewhat expert-guided because of the substantial medical content and prepared statements introduced by the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, such as wanting to sleep through the night and saying she can do the planned step. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently build on, amplify, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by validating concerns instead of arguing or reinforcing resistance. The limitation is that the response sometimes moves quickly into advice rather than using more advanced MI strategies such as double-sided reflections.
