# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, normalizing difficulty without shaming or arguing. It is not a 5 because the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and occasionally interpretive rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of ambivalence, identity conflict, and the push-pull between belonging and change. It falls short of a 5 because some reflections remain more thematic than deeply affective, especially around exhaustion, guilt, and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing shoplifting and related risk, moving coherently from ambivalence to peers, warning signs, and coping. It is not a 5 because the target behavior and next step are not explicitly summarized or narrowed collaboratively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation feels like a joint exploration, with the psychiatrist building on Jordan's own language and ideas. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist often sets the agenda and provides categories, limiting full power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports choice and avoids pressure, which is helpful in a court-mandated context. It is not a 5 because autonomy is not consistently and explicitly reinforced, and some suggestions subtly steer the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and Jordan's own concepts to draw out his ideas, concerns, and motivations. It is not a 5 because the evocation stays somewhat surface-level and does not deeply explore values, confidence, or past success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative, non-expert stance and repeatedly invites Jordan's perspective. It is not a 5 because the therapist still provides much of the structure and options, rather than fully sharing agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and lightly affirms Jordan's change talk, such as wanting to be “solid” and avoiding the store. It needs stronger elicitation and amplification of desire, ability, confidence, and values to consistently build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge Jordan's sustain talk and occasionally reframes it toward change. It needs more strategic double-sided reflections and clearer pivots from sustain talk toward values, confidence, or next steps.
