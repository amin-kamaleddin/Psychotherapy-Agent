# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Oliver’s ambivalence. However, the counselor sometimes shifts quickly into problem-solving and language coaching, reducing the depth of warmth in emotionally vulnerable moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are accurate reflections of Oliver’s ambivalence and values, especially around generosity and needing routine. Empathy is inconsistent, often summarizing the situation rather than fully exploring guilt, fear, shame, or bodily distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of asking the flatmate to leave by a specific date. Momentum is maintained well through script selection, role-play, and message drafting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the message and adjusts wording based on Oliver’s feedback, demonstrating a collaborative style. The interaction remains somewhat counselor-guided because the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure and content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver is offered choices and is not forced into scripts or role-play, which supports autonomy in a basic way. The counselor’s frequent provision of detailed plans, scripts, and polished language reduces the sense that Oliver is fully owning the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions about what matters to Oliver and what feels hardest, but often moves quickly into advice and scripts. There is not enough time to expand Oliver’s own change talk, values, past strengths, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver’s input influences the session, and the psychiatrist invites preferences around scripts, dates, and role-play. The counselor still takes a fairly expert-driven role by providing the main framework, reducing the client-led nature of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited and reinforced, especially when Oliver chooses a softer script and delivers the boundary line. However, the counselor often supplies the language, plan, and rationale, making change talk sound more like compliance than self-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Oliver’s sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized without debate or amplification. The counselor could strengthen this further by using more advanced double-sided reflections that link Oliver’s sustaining values to change.
