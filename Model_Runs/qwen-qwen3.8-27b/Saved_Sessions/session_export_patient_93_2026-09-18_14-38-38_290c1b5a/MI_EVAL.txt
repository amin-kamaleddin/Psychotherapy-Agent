# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout, using Margaret's own language without moralizing. Slightly measured rather than deeply effusive in warmth, preventing a full score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and layered reflections that progressively deepen, including an exceptional complex reflection of Margaret's emotional shift and identification of existential meaning behind 'my own legs.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression from rapport through discrepancy exploration to value-based change talk with consistent focus on alcohol reduction. Could benefit from slightly more explicit transitional markers between session phases.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance with open-ended questions, use of client language, and no lecturing or unsolicited information. Slight therapist-led pacing in topic selection prevents a full score.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional throughout — explicit non-directiveness, respect for boundaries, no prescriptions or recommendations, and active help distinguishing internal vs. external motivation. Margaret's decisions consistently accepted without pushback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful strategic questions that progressively elicit Margaret's own motivations and values without ever providing a reason for change. Every insight in the session emerges from Margaret herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative with invitations for Margaret's perspective and explicit rejection of the expert role. Could be elevated by more explicit acknowledgment of Margaret as the expert on her own life and more active co-creation of session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful elicitation and reinforcement of change talk, which grows progressively stronger from 'I guess I can cut down some' to a value-based commitment. Could have further amplified the later, stronger change talk more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validate-and-redirect approach to minimization, with a sophisticated turn-back of Margaret's sustain question. Could have more explicitly acknowledged the validity of her question before gently pivoting.
