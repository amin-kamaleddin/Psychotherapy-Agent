# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts Luis’s boundaries without arguing. Lacks enough individualized, unconditional positive regard to fully counter a mildly clinically corrective tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects practical frustration with complexity and the desire for autonomy. Reflections remain somewhat surface-level and miss deeper emotions, such as fear of control or distrust of the medical system.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps tight focus on the target behavior: rescue inhaler in the truck cup holder, one-word dash reminder, one-week trial, and emergency backup. The session is specific, behavior-focused, and aligned with the client’s stated preference for simplicity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs a simple plan by offering options and accepting Luis’s selections. Collaboration is solid but not fully equal at all times because the psychiatrist still introduces medical suggestions and documentation prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly emphasizes client choice, ownership, and the right to reject texts, follow-up, and extra supports. Safety information is framed as a backup rather than a directive, preserving autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits client-generated action and confidence ratings without lecturing or imposing a medical program. Evocation is practical but could be deeper by connecting the plan to personal meaning, values, or desired outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites input and adapts to Luis’s choices, creating a clearly collaborative plan. The interaction is still somewhat counselor-managed, with repeated clinical options and charting prompts rather than more consistent client-led agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits concrete change talk, such as keeping the rescue inhaler in the truck and trying it for a week. The evocation remains operational rather than exploring intrinsic benefits, values, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Softens resistance by avoiding argument and accepting Luis’s rejection of texts, follow-up, and “programs.” It does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategically pivot sustain talk into client-owned change language.
