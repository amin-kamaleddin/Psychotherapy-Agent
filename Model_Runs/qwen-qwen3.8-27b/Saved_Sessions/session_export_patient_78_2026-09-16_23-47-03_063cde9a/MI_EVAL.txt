# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard by validating frustration and treating ambivalence as reasonable. It is not a perfect 5 because the warmth is sometimes polished and task-focused rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures the patient’s need for control, stability, and not feeling lectured. However, the empathy is more cognitive than deeply emotional, with less exploration of underlying frustration, resentment, or autonomy loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear focus on reducing or avoiding smoking in high-risk situations and moves the conversation toward a concrete behavioral experiment. It falls short of a 5 because the opening question is somewhat closed and multiple-choice, and the counselor occasionally narrows the conversation more than necessary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor behaves as an equal partner rather than an authority figure, using inclusive language and inviting the patient’s input. The resulting plan emerges from the patient rather than from the counselor, reflecting a highly collaborative interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supports autonomy by avoiding pressure, not insisting on quitting, and framing change as a self-directed experiment. It is not a 5 because the counselor sometimes steers toward a “smaller step” or asks slightly leading questions, though the approach remains respectful.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently elicits motivation from within the patient rather than providing external reasons to quit. Open, evocative questions help the patient connect change to her own goals of control, stability, and caring for her daughter.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor clearly conveys that expertise about the patient’s life and change resides with the patient, not the clinician. The session is not clinician-led; the patient’s ideas shape the direction of the interview.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evokes and reinforces the patient’s own change talk through open questions and affirmations. The patient generates many concrete ideas, such as cutting back, avoiding smoking at work, and catching early warning signs.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor handles the patient’s resistance effectively without arguing, persuading, or challenging. Sustain talk is reframed as reasonable and turned into a smaller, self-directed experiment.
