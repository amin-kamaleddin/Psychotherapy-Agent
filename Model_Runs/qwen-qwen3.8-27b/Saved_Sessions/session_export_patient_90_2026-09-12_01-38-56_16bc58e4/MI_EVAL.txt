# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, especially by affirming Marcus’s concern for the dog and his need for care. Acceptance is slightly limited by a procedural, task-oriented tone that can feel more managing than fully emotionally present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides several accurate reflections of Marcus’s exhaustion and ambivalence, showing adequate understanding. However, empathy often remains surface-level and misses deeper feelings of fear, shame, helplessness, and the meaning of the dog.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on asthma management and urgent medication access. Direction is uneven, though, as the session frequently shifts among many topics, which can increase overwhelm and dilute the core MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear collaborative moments, particularly in drafting and revising the message to the doctor. The overall interaction remains more advisor-led, with many medical and environmental recommendations delivered rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids outright coercion, which supports some client autonomy. However, frequent directive instructions about breathing, calling the doctor, and sending the message can undercut client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly provides medical information and action steps rather than drawing out Marcus’s own motivations and values. There are few open-ended, evocative questions to expand the client’s change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, creating moments of partnership. The stance often shifts to an expert-driven approach, limiting the consistency of a true equal-partner interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offers several useful statements of readiness and hope, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or connect them to values. Responses often move quickly into logistics and medical advice instead of deepening motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often avoids arguing with Marcus’s resistance and acknowledges the legitimacy of keeping the dog. Sustain talk is not consistently softened, as suggestions like pet fostering and additional dander-reduction tasks can strengthen resistance and overwhelm.
