# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect and nonjudgment while normalizing David’s concerns. The stance is warm but remains somewhat professional and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures David’s ambivalence, practical constraints, and concern about muscle weakness. However, the reflections sometimes repeat and do not fully explore the deeper emotional meaning behind his fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target issue and follows a coherent path from concerns to practical criteria and next steps. The focus becomes somewhat repetitive around getting a clear answer and could move more decisively toward a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with David to define acceptable risk and useful next steps rather than simply giving advice. Collaboration is strong, though the clinician still leads the structure and holds the medical-information role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and supports David’s right to choose, including the option to defer the decision. It would be even stronger if the freedom to decide against the medication were affirmed more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit David’s views and preferences, but the exploration remains somewhat superficial. There is limited attention to his internal values, long-term goals, and deeper personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites David’s perspective and asks permission before checking the chart, supporting a collaborative stance. However, the partnership is not fully framed as shared expertise and the clinician still guides the agenda fairly strongly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, such as David being open to a short trial if the risk is low. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, affirm, or deepen this change talk in a way that would strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates sustain talk without arguing, which is appropriate. However, the responses mostly mirror David’s concerns rather than actively soften them through double-sided reflection or strategic redirection.
