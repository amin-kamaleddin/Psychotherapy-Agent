# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently non-judgmental, respectful tone, validating Malik's goals and perspective without moralizing or labeling. Warmth is genuine but somewhat restrained, falling short of the deep unconditional positive regard that would characterize a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, including catching subtle emotional shifts in Malik's language. Empathy occasionally stays at the cognitive level rather than fully exploring the emotional depth beneath his minimization (e.g., the fear underlying his 'heart going fast').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session focuses heavily on the mother-son trust dynamic and Malik's social life, with the specific target behavior (cannabis use) surfacing only near the end. While developmentally appropriate for a precontemplation-stage adolescent, the connection to the referral behavior is not consistently maintained throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses strong collaborative language and follows Malik's leads rather than a pre-planned agenda. However, the behavioral dynamic remains asymmetric—Malik gives brief responses while the psychiatrist does most of the steering, limiting evidence of true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary throughout—never pressures, imposes, or implies guilt; consistently positions Malik as the decision-maker and expert on his own life. This is the strongest skill displayed in the session and would be difficult to improve upon.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Malik's own values, motivations, and language without providing any external reasons for change. Could be strengthened by more explicitly amplifying and consolidating the powerful change talk that emerges (e.g., 'the pretending').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as collaborative and non-hierarchical with explicit partnership language and shared power. The dynamic remains somewhat therapist-driven, with Malik largely passive, falling short of a fully reciprocal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk appropriate for a precontemplation-stage client, eliciting a breakthrough moment where Malik identifies 'the pretending' as the core issue. Misses opportunities to explicitly reinforce, summarize, and amplify the change talk that emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with notable skill by validating and redirecting rather than challenging, using skillful pivots to move Malik past resistance. Does not directly address the peer-identification sustain talk ('they don't think it's a problem') or use double-sided reflections.
