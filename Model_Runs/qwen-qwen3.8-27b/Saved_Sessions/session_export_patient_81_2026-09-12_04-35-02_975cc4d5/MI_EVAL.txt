# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and responds to defensiveness with normalization and affirmation. Acceptance is generally strong but remains somewhat transactional rather than fully expressing deep unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence between practical consequences and the importance of the party scene. However, the empathy is more cognitive than fully emotionally attuned to identity, belonging, and fear of exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear focus on the weekend use pattern and its impact on work, rent, and auditions. Direction is appropriate, though it sometimes becomes more logistical than MI strategically motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and involves the client in selecting options for a plan. However, frequent therapist-generated options can make the interaction feel less fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks what feels doable and avoids imposing a plan, but does not consistently reinforce that change is entirely the client’s choice. Some commitment-focused questions may feel subtly pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful practical change steps and uses a scaling question near the end. Deeper client-generated motivations, values, and identity concerns are not consistently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids an expert-driven stance, supporting collaborative alliance. Partnership is slightly weakened when the therapist frequently offers structured choices that guide the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits concrete commitments such as leaving the script visible, setting an alarm, and placing the coat by the door. However, change talk is not consistently amplified or expanded to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates sustain talk such as needing the party scene to feel like himself. However, responses often shift to problem-solving rather than using double-sided reflections to link sustain talk with change.
