# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental support. The clinician validates Elise’s struggle without pressure, criticism, or frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Elise’s ambivalence accurately and helps her feel understood. However, some reflections remain surface-level and could explore the emotional weight more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on smoking cessation and the social ritual that maintains smoking. There is brief drift into work stress, and the broader cessation goal is not always explicitly connected to the small experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative in spirit, and Elise helps shape the final plan. However, the psychiatrist’s structured list of strategies and if-then plan makes the interaction less fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirms Elise’s right to choose and repeatedly reduces pressure. Ideas are offered with permission and framed as non-coercive options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful values-based questions to draw out client motivation. However, the clinician moves relatively quickly to suggestions without fully exploring Elise’s reasons, hopes, confidence, or future self.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and often positions Elise as the expert on her experience. The session is partly guided by clinician-introduced suggestions, which slightly reduces the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-owned change language is elicited through questions and affirmations. The development is inconsistent and does not systematically explore reasons, ability, desire, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without argument and redirects toward underlying connection. The approach is effective, though it misses opportunities for deeper reframing or double-sided reflection.
