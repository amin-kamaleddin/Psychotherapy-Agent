# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance by validating Marcus’s overwhelm and small steps. Acceptance could be deeper in moments involving self-worth and fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured concerns about forgetting, unclear targets, and fear of failure. The empathic depth could be stronger by naming underlying shame or fear of being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear and consistent focus on improving diabetes self-management, especially blood glucose checking. There was little topic drift, and the conversation moved logically toward a concrete plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked as a genuine partner by inviting Marcus to choose and tweak the routine, wording, grounding step, and follow-up format. Marcus’s preferences clearly shaped the final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marcus was consistently supported in making his own choices, including the timing, wording, and level of commitment. The psychiatrist avoided pressure and explicitly left follow-up optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation was evoked, but the psychiatrist often moved quickly into advice, psychoeducation, and plan-building. More space was needed for Marcus to articulate his own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance by asking for input and treating Marcus as the expert on his own routine. The session was still somewhat led by clinician-generated options and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforced Marcus’s willingness to try a small, doable step. However, change talk was not consistently amplified or deepened before shifting into practical planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled gently by normalizing difficulty and reframing missed days as opportunities to reset. Some resistance was accepted but not consistently redirected toward hope or change.
