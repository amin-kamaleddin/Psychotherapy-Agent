# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and used affirming language to reduce defensiveness. The warmth was practical and supportive, but it did not consistently validate the client's deeper values, fears, or identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided accurate reflections of frustration and fear, but often moved quickly into problem-solving. Deeper emotional themes such as isolation, distrust, and fear of being a burden were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior, moving logically from stability to warning signs, coping steps, and support planning. There was slight drift into logistics, but the overall direction remained clear and change-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and used collaborative language, but the counselor remained the main architect of the plan. More open-ended follow-ups would have strengthened the sense of shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offered choices and respected the client's decisions about tracking, coping, and contacting support. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing ownership and lack of pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some client-generated ideas and goals, especially around tracking anger and building stability. However, the session shifted quickly into directive advice before fully developing the client's own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client choices and avoided a purely expert-driven stance, but the session remained fairly counselor-led. The client's ideas were used, yet the psychiatrist still directed much of the structure and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful change talk around concrete steps such as tracking anger, walking, breathing, and planning. The client's values and goals were present but not consistently amplified into stronger, internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responded to sustain talk without arguing and acknowledged past failed attempts. However, the responses were mostly neutral, and there were missed opportunities to strategically redirect ambivalence toward change.
