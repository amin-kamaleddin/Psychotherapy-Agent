### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, which is important in a court-mandated context. Acceptance is slightly limited by focus on practical outcomes rather than deeper emotional acknowledgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good initial reflections and recognition of ambivalence around guilt and peer pressure. Deeper emotions such as shame, fear of being “the odd one out,” and need for belonging are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on shoplifting and moves the session toward a concrete, behavior-based plan. Could be strengthened by connecting the plan more to Jordan’s values and longer-term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is developed through back-and-forth, with Jordan contributing key elements. The psychiatrist still provides much of the structure and menu of options, limiting full partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Jordan’s preferences and respects his boundaries, especially around rehearsal and texting. Some language subtly evaluates choices, reducing fully explicit autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses scaling and readiness questions effectively to elicit motivation. Evocation is interrupted by problem-solving and advice, with missed opportunities to explore deeper values and internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Jordan’s input and allows him to choose among options. The psychiatrist frequently supplies solutions and scripts, making the interaction more expert-led than fully partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses evocative questions and scaling to help Jordan articulate motivation and move from a 5 to a 7. Change talk is often tied to external outcomes and could be amplified more consistently.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges Jordan’s concerns about peer risk without arguing or endorsing the status quo. Sustain talk is redirected to small steps but not fully processed with advanced MI techniques.
