# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or harshly challenging Daniel’s minimization. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm or explicitly normalizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening to capture Daniel’s ambivalence and preference for low-effort change. Empathy is generally good but stays somewhat surface-level, not fully exploring deeper fears, ritual attachment, or self-defeating statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays highly focused on alcohol use in relation to marathon training, sleep, recovery, and performance. There is clear momentum from exploration to a concrete if-then plan and scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed, with Daniel’s ideas shaping the fridge rule, check-in format, and timing. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist sometimes leads problem-solving by presenting multiple options before fully exploring Daniel’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites Daniel’s input, offers low-pressure choices, and allows him to adjust the plan. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming ownership and freedom to choose, especially after his self-defeating language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some commitment and confidence through scaling and planning questions. However, evocation is limited because the session moves quickly to practical options without deeply drawing out Daniel’s intrinsic motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel’s contributions clearly influence the plan, showing real collaboration and some power sharing. The psychiatrist often appears expert-driven, especially when presenting many options and strategies, which can reduce the sense of Daniel as the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session evokes willingness, confidence, and concrete planning language. Change talk is mostly practical and behavioral rather than deeply motivational or value-based, so deeper reasons for change are underexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or amplifying Daniel’s sustain talk and uses some double-sided reflections. The main weakness is failing to consistently soften unresolved doubts, weekend ritual attachment, and fear of failure.
