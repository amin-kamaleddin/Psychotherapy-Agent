# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while avoiding blame or pressure. Some affirmations feel slightly generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s fear, overwhelm, and conflicting feelings, especially around being a burden and being dismissed. However, the session moves too quickly toward problem-solving, limiting deeper exploration of emotional themes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior of accessing medical care for physical symptoms and maintains a clear path from distress to a concrete next step. A stronger MI direction could have included more strategic summaries of ambivalence and consolidation of a self-chosen commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively by revising the message based on the client’s preferences and wording changes. The interaction is somewhat structured around clinician-generated templates rather than fully client-led drafting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects the client’s choices, including pausing, copying the message, and not using the nurse to send it. The provision of scripts and fallback options occasionally reduces emphasis on the client generating her own solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some useful evocative questions early in the session to elicit fears and preferred support. The session then shifts too quickly toward scripts and reassurance, limiting further exploration of the client’s own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by frequently inviting input and respecting the client’s preferences. The session remains somewhat clinician-guided because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, including scripts and templates.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers several pieces of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or expand this language. There are fewer questions about importance, confidence, values, or future outcomes that would strengthen client-generated change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client and appropriately validates avoidance and overwhelm. However, the session does not consistently redirect sustain talk toward change or use deeper reflections to explore ambivalence.
