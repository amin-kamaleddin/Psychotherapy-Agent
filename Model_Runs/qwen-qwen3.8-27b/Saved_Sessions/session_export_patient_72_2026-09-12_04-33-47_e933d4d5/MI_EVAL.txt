# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance with frequent affirmations. The warmth is effective but somewhat formulaic, and the premature 'confidence 9' summary slightly reduces attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and helpful, especially in capturing frustration and clarity about wanting to feel stronger and less stiff. Empathy remains mostly surface-level and does not fully explore deeper values or ambivalence around gym pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of initiating a flexible, sustainable exercise routine. Pacing is efficient and the plan is progressively refined without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas substantially shape the plan, including timing, phone strategy, prep steps, and script wording. The interaction is collaborative but still somewhat counselor-guided because the psychiatrist introduces many options and directs rehearsal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by inviting the client to select and adapt plans, scripts, and reminders. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client’s freedom to choose and no-obligation stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses confidence scaling and open questions to elicit the client’s own plans and confidence. More evocative questioning could focus on internal reasons, values, and what the plan means to the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to contribute and the plan is built from her ideas. The interaction remains fairly counselor-led, with many concrete suggestions offered before the client fully chooses.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling effectively evoke strong change talk, including concrete commitments and high confidence. Change talk could be amplified by reflecting and exploring what feeling stronger means to the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to barriers with pragmatic, low-pressure support and does not reinforce reasons not to change. More double-sided reflections could be used to address ambivalence about gym pressure and distractions.
