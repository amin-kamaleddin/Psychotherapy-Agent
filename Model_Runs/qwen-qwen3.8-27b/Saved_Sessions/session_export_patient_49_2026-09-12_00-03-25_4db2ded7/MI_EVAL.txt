# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, calm, and nonjudgmental, normalizing Hana’s ambivalence and smoking behavior. The acceptance was solid, though somewhat procedural and less individually affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected some of Hana’s concerns accurately but mostly at a surface level. Deeper empathic reflections of her social, emotional, and fear-of-failure themes were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking cessation and moved toward a concrete, behavior-focused plan. Direction was good, though it shifted quickly from exploration to practical intervention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was collaborative and reflected Hana’s preferences, including spearmint gum and a small experiment. Some collaboration remained task-based and clinician-initiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by offering choices, respecting Hana’s selected option, and reducing pressure around the experiment. It would have been stronger with more explicit reminders that Hana was in charge of her own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evocative questions, such as exploring pros/cons and inviting a small experiment. However, the session included a lot of practical advice and problem-solving, which limited deeper evocation of Hana’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some partnership, as Hana was invited to choose options and helped shape the final plan. However, the psychiatrist still carried much of the agenda and offered many solutions rather than consistently treating Hana as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana produced change talk about trying spearmint gum, tracking cravings, and possibly expanding later. The psychiatrist did not consistently reinforce, amplify, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalized Hana’s reasons for smoking but also spent time listing and rating their benefits. There were few strategic softening reflections to reduce the power of sustain talk or reframe it toward change.
