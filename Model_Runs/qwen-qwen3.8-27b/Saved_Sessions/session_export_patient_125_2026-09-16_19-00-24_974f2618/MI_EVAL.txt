# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses nonjudgmental language and normalizes Tariq’s mixed feelings. It does not reach a 5 because the warmth remains more procedural than deeply affirming of Tariq’s personal worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate and helpful reflections that capture Tariq’s shift toward agency. It falls short of a 5 because it often summarizes the cognitive content without fully exploring the emotional depth behind his frustration and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a strong area, with clear, consistent focus on the target behavior and steady movement from emotion toward a concrete plan. The session stays oriented toward manageable, behaviorally relevant goals without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Tariq’s input and frames the work as a joint problem-solving process. It is not yet a 5 because the counselor-led question-and-summary pattern limits the sense of explicit, equal-partner co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy with open questions and by acknowledging what is outside Tariq’s control. It does not reach a 5 because it does not more explicitly affirm Tariq’s right to choose, including the choice not to commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Tariq’s own change talk and practical intentions. It stops short of a 5 because it does not deepen the evocation of core values, confidence, or commitment in a more motivational way.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and allows Tariq’s responses to shape the session. It is not a 5 because it lacks clearer, explicit language that makes the equality of roles unmistakable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently draws out practical change talk and reinforces Tariq’s own language in favor of change. It does not reach a 5 because it does not more skillfully elicit confidence, importance, or deeper self-motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk reasonably well by acknowledging frustration without arguing or becoming defensive. It is not a 5 because it sometimes reflects the blame language back too directly instead of more consistently using double-sided reflections.
