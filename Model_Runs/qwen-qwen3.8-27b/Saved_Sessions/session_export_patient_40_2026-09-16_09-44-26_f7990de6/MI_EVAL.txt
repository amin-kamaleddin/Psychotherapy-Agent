# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes ambivalence without blaming the relapse. Acceptance is strong but could be more individualized in acknowledging shame and emotional pain.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are present and show understanding of Elias’s practical and relational concerns. However, the reflections are often cognitive and do not consistently explore deeper emotions such as shame, fear, or discomfort saying the script aloud.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains strongly focused on relapse prevention and concrete steps such as choosing a support option, setting a favorite, and rehearsing a script. Direction is a strength, though it becomes somewhat procedure-focused and underconnects to broader values or progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias contributes useful ideas, including the support line, backup brother, script, and text message. The psychiatrist also supplies much of the structure and wording, which can make the process feel more guided than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, asks permission, and accepts uncertainty about follow-through. There are mild directive moments, but overall the counselor supports Elias’s right to choose his own plan and pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias generates meaningful change talk about calling for support rather than using. Evocation is present but relatively surface-level and becomes quickly procedural, with less depth around values, confidence, and past success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias is invited to choose options and contribute to the plan, but the psychiatrist remains the primary generator of structure, scripts, and steps. A stronger partnership stance would emphasize Elias as the expert and share more power in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits change talk through questions about supports, doable steps, and commitment. Change talk is reinforced, though it could be deepened further through values-based exploration and confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and responds respectfully to hesitation and uncertainty. However, some sustain talk, such as “I can handle just one,” is not fully explored or softened with double-sided reflections.
