# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and affirming throughout, with no judgment or minimization. Picks up on client's specific language ('lighter,' 'data collection,' 'troubleshooting') and reflects it back with genuine appreciation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface-level paraphrasing into genuine emotional and conceptual depth, capturing identity shifts, somatic experience, and metacognitive insights. Consistently accurate in capturing both cognitive and emotional dimensions without over-interpreting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target domain with a logical progression from reflection through planning to commitment. Each step builds on the previous one with no topic drift, maintaining momentum without feeling rushed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is structured as a genuine dialogue rather than a therapist-led interview, with the client's ideas shaping direction at every point. The psychiatrist follows the client's lead (e.g., adopting 'data collection' framing, exploring running) rather than steering toward a pre-planned agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's ownership and freedom to choose, never using directive language like 'should' or 'must.' Strongly affirms the client's right to stop or modify the practice without expressing disappointment or pushing for stronger commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Virtually every significant insight, plan, and commitment originated from the client, elicited by well-timed open questions and accurate reflections. The psychiatrist never provides reasons for change or fills silences with their own content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom about change reside within the client, frequently asking her opinion rather than offering their own. Explicitly affirms the client's self-generated solutions, recognizing that the plan came from her, not the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each question is strategically designed to draw out the client's own motivations, eliciting rich and varied change talk including desire, ability, reasons, and commitment language. Builds on each piece of change talk naturally without it feeling forced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles the limited sustain talk appropriately by exploring it with curiosity rather than dismissing or minimizing. Scored 4 due to limited opportunity to demonstrate mastery given the client's low ambivalence and preparation/action stage.
