# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently nonjudgmental stance, validating Milan's minimization without pushback and normalizing his feelings throughout. Slightly deeper explicit affirmation of Milan as a person beyond his behavior would elevate this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking are evident, with accurate reflections of ambivalence and subtle emotional cues like embarrassment and the word 'careful.' Some reflections could go slightly deeper into emotional undercurrents, such as the shame underlying the party experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a clear, logical progression from safety-building through values exploration to Milan's own articulation of change, without drifting off topic. A stronger sense of forward momentum toward concrete next steps or a shared goal would push this to a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is a genuine joint exploration, with the psychiatrist following Milan's leads (his mom, the word 'careful') rather than steering to a pre-planned agenda. More explicit collaborative framing (e.g., 'Let's figure this out together') would make the reciprocity more visible.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is woven into nearly every interaction, with the psychiatrist explicitly removing pressure multiple times ('You don't have to figure anything out tonight') while still gently exploring change. Milan is consistently positioned as the decision-maker in his own life without any implied pressure or ultimatums.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws out Milan's own motivations through well-timed questions, never providing reasons for change or giving advice. The session culminates in Milan spontaneously articulating his own change talk ('if I just drank less'), demonstrating that evocation was the primary mechanism driving the entire session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses invitational language ('I'm curious,' 'I'm interested') and positions Milan as the expert on his own life without taking on an authoritative role. More explicit acknowledgment of Milan's wisdom and invitations to co-create next steps would make the partnership more visibly reciprocal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-timed questions successfully draw out Milan's own reasons for considering change, from his fear about his mom to his desire to 'wake up and be fine.' The change talk could be further solidified with additional affirmations or summarizing of Milan's statements about wanting to drink less.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates sustain talk without arguing or dismissing it ('I'm not going to pretend a beer at a party is a crisis'), holding both sides of the ambivalence without reinforcing the sustain side. More strategic double-sided reflections after strong sustain talk could help Milan see the cost of maintaining the status quo.
