# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, especially through statements like “It’s your choice” and “I’m here to listen.” Acceptance is strong, but some reflections sound procedural, and the psychiatrist could more explicitly affirm Maya’s strengths and self-direction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally good, tracking Maya’s ambivalence, worries about volleyball, privacy, and belonging. However, the psychiatrist sometimes labels the experience clinically and misses deeper emotional meaning around social exclusion, identity, and feeling “official.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on drinking and its effects on school, volleyball, privacy, and future opportunities. Some time is spent on logistics and confidentiality, which is important but dilutes momentum toward concrete change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Maya’s input and co-creates a low-key follow-up plan, supporting a partner stance. Collaboration is not fully client-led, however, because the psychiatrist still controls much of the sequence and offers therapist-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a major strength, with consistent support for Maya’s freedom to choose and avoid pressure. The psychiatrist respects Maya’s need for privacy, her statement that she is “not ready for a plan,” and her preference to figure things out on her own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions, such as the readiness ruler and “What would need to change?” to elicit Maya’s own motivations. These moments are not consistently deepened, and there is limited exploration of confidence, ability, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist establishes a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance by asking for Maya’s perspective and inviting her input. The session remains moderately counselor-directed, though, and does not fully follow Maya’s agenda in real time.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk around volleyball, privacy, and future plans. This change talk is not consistently reinforced, amplified, or developed into stronger client motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing Maya’s sustain talk, such as “I’m not ready for a plan.” However, stronger softening is limited, as the psychiatrist does not consistently reframe, explore, or gently reduce the weight of sustain talk.
