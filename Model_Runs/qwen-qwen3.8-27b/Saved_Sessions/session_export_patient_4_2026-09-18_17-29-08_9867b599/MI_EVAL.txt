# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist never labels, minimizes, or judges, and consistently honors Aizada's pushback with simple validation (e.g., 'That's fair'). Warmth is genuine and specific rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking, including excellent double-sided reflections. However, the empathic work occasionally stays at the cognitive level rather than fully inhabiting the emotional experience (e.g., not explicitly reflecting the shame in 'I don't want to be that person').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from validation through exploration to a concrete action plan. The session lacks an explicit collaborative closing that consolidates achievements and names next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session structure emerges entirely from the client's responses rather than a predetermined agenda, and the power dynamic is genuinely equal. The psychiatrist explicitly defers to the client's formulation ('You've put it better than I could') and treats her pushback as valuable information.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support woven naturally into every interaction; the psychiatrist never prescribes, advises, or pressures. Language consistently positions Aizada as the decision-maker, and explicit permissions (e.g., 'You don't have to solve that before Saturday') reinforce her agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation — every insight in the session (the park walk, morning timing, medication strategy, 'both damage and stress' formulation) is the client's own. The psychiatrist's role is purely to create conditions for these insights to emerge, which is textbook MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the interaction as a partnership where the client holds the expertise about her own experience. She never positions herself as the one who knows the answer and the session is genuinely shaped by the client's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits change talk through open questions and strategic reflections, drawing out concrete change language (walking to shops, part-time work, park walk). However, the change talk is not always deeply amplified or developed further once it appears, and confidence/importance scaling is absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently validates and honors sustain talk (insistence on physical cause, distrust of GP, fear of cancer) before gently pivoting without arguing or dismissing. The overall pattern is effective, though the final exchange on busyness vs. drowning out was left unresolved.
