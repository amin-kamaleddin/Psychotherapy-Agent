# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, using affirming language that avoids blaming Nabil for his anger. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper by more explicitly affirming Nabil’s worth independent of his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening by accurately capturing the meaning behind Nabil’s anger, including his concerns about time, family, and being overlooked. However, some reflections are repeated, brief, or interpretive, and deeper feelings such as fear, shame, or powerlessness are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist keeps the session focused on appointment delays, feeling ignored, and the need for reliable communication in medical settings. Direction could be stronger by guiding Nabil toward a more specific behavioral plan or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance, inclusive language, and client-centered questions, allowing Nabil’s concerns to shape the session. Collaboration could improve by more explicitly co-constructing the plan with Nabil and checking whether the summary fits his view.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by asking Nabil to identify his own needs rather than giving direct advice. Higher autonomy support would include more explicit reinforcement of Nabil’s freedom to choose and less framing that implies a preferred emotional response.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions to draw out Nabil’s standards and preferences, but often embeds them in reflective interpretations. Evocation could be stronger by expanding Nabil’s own change language and exploring the meaning, confidence, and expected impact of his stated goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and frequently invites Nabil’s input, allowing his concerns to guide the conversation. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly deferring to Nabil’s expertise and reducing therapist-driven summaries and leading reflections.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated change language, such as Nabil’s requests for a clear time and direct update. However, these statements are not consistently deepened, amplified, or expanded into stronger desire, ability, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly avoids arguing with Nabil and makes some attempts to reframe anger in a workable way. However, certain statements may reinforce the payoff of anger or the belief that volume is necessary, rather than gently shifting focus toward change.
