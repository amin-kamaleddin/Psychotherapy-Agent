# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids pushing back when Isabelle says she is not trying to quit. Warmth is professional and behavior-focused rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate, practical reflections of Isabelle’s perspective and goals. It does not fully explore deeper underlying emotions such as stress, fear, or the meaning of music.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on alcohol use, stress, saxophone, and academic safety. The focus is somewhat diffuse and lacks a single concrete behavioral target or crisp summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Isabelle’s view and builds on her language about saxophone, schedule, and personal rules. The partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist still provides much of the structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong and consistent, with repeated emphasis on Isabelle’s ownership, simple rules, and self-direction. The psychiatrist avoids advice, pressure, or imposing a formal plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often evokes Isabelle’s own values and motivations, such as protecting her sax and honesty. At times, the psychiatrist interprets for Isabelle and does not fully amplify confidence or commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and lets Isabelle’s contributions shape the session. The partnership is strong but not fully co-created, as the psychiatrist still leads much of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change-relevant language about protecting saxophone, grades, and honesty. The evocation is mainly harm-reduction focused and does not strongly amplify confidence, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Isabelle’s sustain talk and reflects it without judgment. Some focus on monitoring and drifting temporarily keeps attention on risk before being adapted to Isabelle’s preference for simplicity.
