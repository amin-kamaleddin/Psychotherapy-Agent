# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard without shaming Lucía’s ambivalence. Acceptance was strong, though some affirmations felt slightly formulaic and could be more individually attuned for a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflective listening to capture key experiences, such as stress and concern for her daughter. Empathy was good but not fully deep, missing some opportunity to reflect underlying emotions more specifically.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, practical focus on smoking cessation and concrete steps like the kitchen rule. Direction became slightly overfocused on planning and restatement, which somewhat reduced client-led momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Lucía to choose what was doable and adapted to her stated limits. Collaboration was reasonable, though the therapist sometimes led with multiple suggestions and summaries, making the conversation less equally co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly honored Lucía’s choices, respected her boundary against tracking, and avoided coercion. Autonomy support could be stronger by pausing more and reducing repeated offers of additional ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made appropriate early evocative questions about what mattered and readiness. Evocation was limited in depth, with fewer follow-up questions to explore values, confidence, or deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveyed a collaborative, non-expert stance and invited Lucía to identify doable options. Partnership was good but shifted at times into a more advisory mode, especially when offering a list of strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk and responded supportively to the kitchen rule. Change talk was not consistently deepened or amplified with stronger reflections or follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and acknowledged the realistic reasons Lucía gives for smoking. Softening was moderate because the responses did not consistently validate the legitimate function of smoking while opening space for change.
