# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and affirming, with a supportive, nonjudgmental stance throughout. Acceptance was strong but occasionally tied more to task completion and performance than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made accurate reflections of Oliver’s ambivalence and boundary needs, tracking both relational and practical concerns. Deeper emotional pain, such as guilt and fear of damaging the friendship, was reflected only partially and could have been validated more consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior: asserting the boundary and setting a move-out date. Each step moved toward concrete action, with minimal drift and strong practical direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely collaborative, with the psychiatrist offering options while Oliver selected, modified, and tested the approach. However, the psychiatrist still supplied a lot of content and structure, limiting full client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Oliver was repeatedly invited to choose dates, scripts, timing, and wording, which supported his sense of agency. Autonomy support was good, but the volume of psychiatrist-provided scripts and planning reduced the space for Oliver to generate his own solution first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some client-generated statements and began with a useful evocative question. Still, much of the session relied on psychiatrist-provided scripts and plans rather than drawing out Oliver’s own values, reasons for change, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was clear collaboration, with the psychiatrist inviting input and incorporating Oliver’s preferences. The session nonetheless leaned toward an expert/coaching role by supplying multiple scripts and specific solutions, which limited stronger power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was present and acknowledged, especially around firmness on the 30th and willingness to follow through. The psychiatrist did not consistently deepen or amplify that change talk, and supplying language reduced opportunities for more client-generated commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by acknowledging fears without reinforcing them and redirecting toward action. The main limitation was not fully reflecting or softening Oliver’s final self-doubt about wavering on Thursday.
