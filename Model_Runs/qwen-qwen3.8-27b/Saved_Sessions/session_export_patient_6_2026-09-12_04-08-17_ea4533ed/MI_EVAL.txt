# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming, but some corrective language may frame the behavior as wrongdoing rather than fully affirming the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures the client’s ambivalence around soccer, sneaking out, and nearly being caught. However, the response is somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore underlying shame, fear, or loss of trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has some direction around alcohol use, soccer, and potential consequences, but the focus drifts toward managing detection, avoiding the police, and preparing a conversation with the mother.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, but frequently supplies lists of options, tips, and scripts, making the interaction more advisor-led than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the client’s sense of choice and avoids forcing a decision. However, some prescriptive scripts and concrete plans can feel somewhat imposed before the client has fully generated his own commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and rulers to assess importance and confidence, but often shifts from evocation to advice-giving before fully drawing out the client’s own motivation and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaborative format and some client-led choice, but the psychiatrist frequently assumes the expert role by supplying options, scripts, and practical tips.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk about protecting soccer and scholarships, but does not consistently amplify or deepen it into stronger, more specific motivation and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client’s minimization, but often moves to practical options without using double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to transform sustain talk into change.
