# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoids escalating Nabil’s anger. Validation is effective but more functional than deeply affirming of Nabil as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture frustration, urgency, and the need for accountability. The counselor misses deeper feelings such as powerlessness, job fear, and repeated disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the presenting issue and moves efficiently toward a concrete next step. However, the direction becomes more administrative problem-solving than MI-oriented change work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nabil’s preferences shape parts of the plan, especially the email format and specific accountability details. The psychiatrist still supplies much of the structure, making the interaction feel more service-focused than equal-partner collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers options and asks before providing materials. Autonomy support weakens when the counselor takes over the content and wording of the solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few questions elicit Nabil’s preferred direction and acceptable outcomes. The session is mainly driven by the counselor providing options and scripts rather than evoking Nabil’s own motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts to Nabil’s choices. The interaction shifts into an expert-driven stance when the counselor supplies the complaint process, wording, and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nabil produces some agency language around accountability, a named contact, and a clear plan. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand these statements into stronger change motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue with Nabil’s external-blame stance, but also does not effectively soften or redirect it. The session remains centered on holding the clinic accountable rather than creating dual awareness about client-owned change.
