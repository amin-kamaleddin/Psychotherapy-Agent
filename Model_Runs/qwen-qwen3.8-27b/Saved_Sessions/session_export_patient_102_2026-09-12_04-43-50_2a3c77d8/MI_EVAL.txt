# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized the teen’s experience without shaming or moralizing. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming the teen’s worth more explicitly and reducing corrective tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used accurate, nonjudgmental reflections of frustration and feeling judged. Limitations were lack of deeper exploration into underlying emotions and somewhat formulaic responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor kept the session generally within the topic but often drifted toward practicing responses to criticism. There was not enough consistent direction toward a specific goal of safer alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor engaged the teen in joint exploration and invited him to choose responses, creating a collaborative feel. This was limited by offering structured scripted options rather than relying more on the client’s own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor used consistent invitational language and allowed the teen to choose his own approach without direct pressure. Autonomy support could improve by more explicitly affirming that the decision is entirely the teen’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions mostly focused on process and preference rather than eliciting the teen’s own motivation for change. The counselor missed opportunities to build on statements like knowing his limits and wanting to be trusted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and avoided a purely directive stance, but often stepped into an expert-advice role. Partnership was present but could be stronger by recognizing the client as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some attempts to elicit change-relevant material, but the counselor did not effectively build on the teen’s change talk. The session focused more on managing criticism than strengthening motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor made some effort to redirect toward safety and consequences without blaming the teen. However, he did not consistently use MI strategies to explore ambivalence or reduce the dominance of sustain talk.
