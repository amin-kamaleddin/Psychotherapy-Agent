# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental and normalizing stance, though warmth sometimes became tied to task performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate but mostly content-focused, missing deeper emotions such as shame and frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a specific target behavior and narrowed the plan step by step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elira participated in shaping the plan, but the psychiatrist supplied most of the structure and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elira was generally given low-pressure choices, though autonomy was sometimes channeled through therapist-generated options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was limited exploration of Elira’s values, motivations, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone was collaborative, but the psychiatrist often took an expert role in generating strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged, but it was not consistently reflected, expanded, or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but not strategically redirected toward change or hope.
