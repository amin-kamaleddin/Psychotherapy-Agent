# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Nabil's frustration. Some language shifts responsibility toward the client's behavior, which can feel minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of Nabil's dilemma, but the empathy remains more cognitive than emotional. Deeper feelings of humiliation, powerlessness, and fear are not fully tracked.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the appointment delay and its impact on Nabil's work. However, the focus becomes repetitive and remains more strategy-focused than strategically forward-moving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Nabil's input and responds to his need for a specific time. The interaction is collaborative in parts, but the psychiatrist maintains the main framework rather than fully co-creating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive statements, but the repeated emphasis on finding a "doable" or "realistic" way to ask can feel pressuring. The psychiatrist does not fully affirm Nabil's right to choose whether or how to act.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions elicit some client-generated motivation, especially around needing a specific time. The evocation remains superficial and behavior-focused rather than exploring deeper values, confidence, or internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and allows Nabil to shape part of the focus. However, the conversation remains more guided than truly collaborative, especially around request-scripting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nabil expresses clear change talk, such as needing a specific time and planning his workday. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk through values-based reflection or evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about the broken system dominates the session. The psychiatrist does not strategically soften or redirect this sustain talk using double-sided reflections, autonomy, or exceptions.
