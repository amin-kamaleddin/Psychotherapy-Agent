# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental stance, with clear affirmation of Daniel’s strengths and validation of ambivalence. It falls short of a 5 because some affirmations feel slightly evaluative or tied to progress rather than fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate and complex reflections that capture Daniel’s competing values and concerns. Empathy could be deeper by sitting more fully with emotions such as embarrassment, fear of rejection, loneliness, or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on managing alcohol use in social settings and progresses from awareness to a practical plan. It is not a 5 because the counselor does not explicitly negotiate the agenda or consistently link reflections to a specific drinking-related goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor acts as an equal partner and co-constructs the conversation with Daniel. Daniel’s own ideas clearly shape the direction and final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces Daniel’s freedom of choice and his role as the author of his own behavior. Language such as “choice rather than autopilot” and leaving room to have a beer supports autonomy without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor successfully elicits Daniel’s own values, strategies, and change ideas rather than providing advice. It falls short of a 5 because the counselor does not fully develop confidence, commitment, or additional change talk when Daniel remains uncertain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor clearly demonstrates partnership by inviting Daniel’s internal knowledge and following his leads. The session is shaped by Daniel’s experiences, values, and ideas rather than the counselor’s agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor evokes substantial and concrete change talk, moving Daniel toward specific strategies such as a non-alcoholic beer, reset cues, and texting his wife. It is not a 5 because the counselor does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen the change talk, especially near the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handles sustain talk gently and collaboratively, avoiding argument or judgment. It falls short of a 5 because the strongest ending sustain talk about uncertainty could be more strategically softened or re-anchored to Daniel’s values.
