# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without moralizing Derrick’s drinking or DUI. The tone is generally affirming, though it could be strengthened by more explicit normalization of Derrick’s shame, fear, or grief.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening and captures Derrick’s conflict and emotional themes well. Deeper empathy could be developed by tracking underlying emotions such as shame, fear, anger, or identity-related conflict more consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the referral concern, with clear attention to avoiding repeat DUI risk, protecting Derrick’s license, and developing a practical weekend plan. Direction could be strengthened by using summaries or agenda checks to keep the session motivationally focused rather than purely solution-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with Derrick contributing several ideas that are incorporated into the plan. However, the counselor frequently supplies structure, options, and scripts, which can make the session feel more counselor-guided than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Derrick’s autonomy by offering options and respecting his rejections of suggestions such as a 30-day trial or exit-code text. More consistent emphasis on Derrick’s ownership of the plan would reduce the advisory tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk through questions about goals, confidence, barriers, and preferred strategies. Evocation remains moderate because it is often interrupted by frequent tactical suggestions rather than deeper exploration of Derrick’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Derrick’s preferences shape parts of the plan, but the counselor often takes an advisory role by generating lists of options, scripts, and tactics. A stronger partnership stance would involve more explicit invitations for Derrick to lead and position him as the expert on his own situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk and reinforces some of Derrick’s practical statements. The skill could be improved by more consistently reflecting, amplifying, and deepening Derrick’s own reasons for change rather than moving quickly into problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Derrick’s sustain talk and validates his concerns about peer pressure and not making a scene. However, there are fewer strategic double-sided reflections or reframes that would help him hold the conflict and choose change more actively.
