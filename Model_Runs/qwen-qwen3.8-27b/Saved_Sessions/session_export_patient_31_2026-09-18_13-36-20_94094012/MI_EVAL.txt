# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary unconditional positive regard throughout, with explicit validation of David's resistance (e.g., 'I'm not here to talk you into something you don't want'). The psychiatrist never labels, shames, or dismisses, maintaining warm and individualized acceptance even when the client voices strong sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, nuanced perspective-taking that captures the functional meaning behind behavior (e.g., the cigarette as a 'pause from logistics mode') rather than just surface content. The psychiatrist tracks David's emotional shifts in real time and responds to underlying meaning with no missed opportunities.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear strategic arc from exploring ambivalence through to narrowing practical conditions, with each question building on the last. A minor deviation occurs when the psychiatrist partially answers David's pharmacological question informationally before recovering into the evocative frame.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine collaboration where David's contributions (the driveway moment, the in-house boundary, the walking-around-block idea) shape the direction at every turn. The psychiatrist explicitly names the difference from the pharmacist and commits to being a co-creator rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support executed consistently, with explicit statements removing pressure ('You don't owe me a yes today') and validating 'I'll think about it' as a legitimate autonomous decision. The psychiatrist never issues directives or creates implicit obligation at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws motivation from within David rather than supplying external reasons, using masterful evocation questions that stay within the client's frame. One moment of providing research information ('the research is mixed') briefly shifts the dynamic, and David's emerging confidence language could have been more aggressively amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions David as the expert on his own experience, using open invitations rather than leading questions and avoiding the expert role entirely. Even when providing information, it is framed as a personal preference rather than authoritative evidence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk, connecting David's existing boundary (not smoking in the house) to his emerging openness to change. Could push further by explicitly developing David's confidence and ability language (DARN-CAT) to elevate this to a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Perhaps the strongest skill in the session — each instance of sustain talk is handled with validation, reflection without amplification, and a pivot to the client's own counter-arguments. No adversarial dynamic is ever created, and implicit double-sided reflections hold both sides without reinforcement of the sustain position.
