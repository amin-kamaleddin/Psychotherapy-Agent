# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating, with strong reframing of shame (e.g., 'that's not a character flaw, that's a really hard place to be'). The tone is respectful throughout, though the emotional warmth occasionally feels slightly clinical rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with insightful reflections that capture underlying meaning (e.g., 'You're not just cutting up plastic, you're cutting up a part of how you've been navigating the world'). Reflections are skillful but occasionally feel slightly too polished, and moments of grief (e.g., the lost time in Cairo) could have been explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with a strong session arc from ambivalence to tentative commitment. The final segment exploring social connections drifts slightly from the immediate behavioral target and could have been more explicitly tied back to gambling-specific triggers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a genuine joint exploration, with the psychiatrist consistently building on Anya's language and metaphors rather than imposing clinical frameworks. The power dynamic is fully equal, with no visible agenda beyond following Anya's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Anya's autonomy and right to choose, using invitational language ('what do you think,' 'I'm curious') without ever prescribing or using directive words like 'should' or 'need to.' The psychiatrist removes pressure by validating the 'maybe version' as sufficient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits change talk from within Anya rather than providing reasons for change, drawing out her own motivations (Egypt, the 'Cairo version of me') without ever stating them directly. Could be strengthened with more varied techniques such as scaling questions or importance/ruling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary throughout — the psychiatrist explicitly rejects the expert/authority role, consistently asks 'what do you think' and 'what does that look like for you,' and uses inclusive language ('we,' 'us'). The session is framed as a collaboration between equals from start to finish.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully catches and amplifies Anya's change talk, reframing it into empowering language (e.g., linking the 'Cairo version of you' to the action of cutting the cards). Relies heavily on reflection rather than active elicitation techniques such as scaling or confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by acknowledging doubt and fear without letting it dominate, and by exploring underlying needs rather than arguing. Could be strengthened with more active use of double-sided reflections (e.g., 'On one hand, the cards give you freedom, and on the other, they're what's keeping you from Cairo').
