# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard with specific, individualized warmth throughout. Never judges Marcus for his reluctance, attachment to his dog, or fear of spiraling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and nuanced reflective listening that captures ambivalence with precision and identifies deeper emotional meaning (e.g., body betrayal, loss of control). Reflections are layered and progressive rather than repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on asthma management and skillfully narrows scope to a manageable sub-goal (tracking inhaler use). Score held at 4 because the session doesn't fully close with a consolidated summary or specific agreement on next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative interaction where Marcus's ideas shape the session direction throughout. Uses inclusive language and consistently builds on Marcus's own contributions rather than imposing a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports Marcus's autonomy with phrases like 'this is your space' and 'you get to decide what it's for.' Never uses directive language; explicitly grants permission to set his own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Marcus rather than providing reasons for change, asking questions that draw out his own values and desires. The session is largely Marcus generating content, with the psychiatrist facilitating rather than directing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Marcus, explicitly rejecting the expert role. Never imposes a treatment plan and frames all questions as invitations rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk at key moments (e.g., 'That's not a small sentence. That's you telling me you're ready to try'). Could push further into DARN-C language, particularly eliciting confidence/ability statements or exploring values connected to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating without dismissing (e.g., 'That's a real fear, not an excuse') and gently reframing toward past competence. Could use more double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence rather than primarily acknowledging it.
