# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with strong validation of the patient’s fear. The opening felt somewhat procedural, and a few statements shifted toward correction before fully sitting with the experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows awareness of the patient’s fear, shame, and ambivalence. However, empathy often remained surface-level and quickly moved into problem-solving rather than deeper complex reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, relevant focus on the certification exam and avoidance. The session is practical, though the direction is more symptom-management and planning than MI-consistent motivational focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers choices, and adjusts the plan based on patient preferences. Collaboration is present but remains largely procedural rather than fully co-creating the treatment direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice, avoids pressure, and accepts the patient’s limited plan. There is still a slight push to add optional structure, which can subtly reduce autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician primarily provides advice, scripts, and coping strategies. There is limited use of evocative questions to elicit the patient’s own values, motivations, reasons for change, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and adapts to patient preferences. The role remains largely expert-driven, with the clinician selecting interventions and shaping the treatment agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers some change talk, but the clinician does not consistently expand, amplify, or reinforce it. Little attention is given to exploring deeper reasons for change, confidence, or past success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with the patient’s sustain talk and uses normalizing reframing. Sustain talk is acknowledged but not consistently managed by pivoting toward change or reducing the pull of the status quo.
