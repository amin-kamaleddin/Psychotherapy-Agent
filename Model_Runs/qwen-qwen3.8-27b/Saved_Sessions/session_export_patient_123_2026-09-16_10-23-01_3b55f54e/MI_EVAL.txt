# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, especially when acknowledging strengths and efforts. Acceptance was limited by somewhat surface-level affirmations and limited reflection of deeper emotional experiences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathic reflections were accurate early on but often stayed focused on practical content. Opportunities to explore underlying feelings around stress, isolation, and ambivalence were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted away from the stated referral concern and never addressed alcohol use or related risk. Although focused on study habits, the conversation was substantially off-target for the clinical purpose.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked collaboratively with the client’s preferences and adapted the plan accordingly. Collaboration was limited by the initial counselor-driven introduction of study strategies before eliciting the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s choices were consistently invited, respected, and incorporated into the plan. Autonomy support was reduced by repeated specific suggestions and steering toward trying the plan tonight.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative moments occurred, including open questions, confidence scaling, and client-generated ideas. Evocation remained shallow and off-target because the counselor relied heavily on advice and problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was evident invitation of client input and responsiveness to her preferences. Partnership was uneven because the counselor frequently took an expert role and guided toward a predefined plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered some change talk around trying the study-break plan. These statements were not consistently reflected, amplified, or expanded, and no change talk was elicited regarding the referral concern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argumentation and responded to with acceptance. Softening was limited because the counselor mostly used problem-solving rather than MI-consistent reflections or ambivalence work.
