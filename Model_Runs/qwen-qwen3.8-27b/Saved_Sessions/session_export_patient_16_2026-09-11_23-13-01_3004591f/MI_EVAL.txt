# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client’s concerns without moralizing. Acceptance is competent but somewhat functional rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflections that acknowledge stress and the importance of basketball. Empathy remains surface-level and does not fully capture the client’s emotional complexity or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on vaping use and basketball eligibility without significant wandering. Direction is more problem-solving than MI-guided, with less emphasis on the client’s internal motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and client jointly select approaches, adjust tone, and rehearse responses. Collaboration is present but uneven because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and acknowledges the client’s freedom to decide. Some directive scripting and recommended strategies reduce the sense that the client is leading the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some clarifying questions but mainly provides advice, scripts, and structured options. Deeper evocative questions about values, importance, confidence, and change reasons are limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and collaborates on scripts, and the client’s preferences influence the plan. However, the psychiatrist still carries much of the direction and expertise, making the partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk about playing better without vaping and planning to skip use. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify these statements with follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk and minimization persist, and the psychiatrist often normalizes minimizing rather than gently redirecting it. The focus on making use sound less serious limits movement toward change.
