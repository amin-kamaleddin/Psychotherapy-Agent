# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was consistently shown without judgment, labeling, or moralizing. The patient’s autonomy, values, and plan were validated throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate, nuanced, and attuned to both content and underlying concerns. The patient’s ambivalence was tracked without misattunement, preserving her self-concept.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and progressed logically from concerns to options to a specific plan. The clinician consistently tied questions back to the target behavior and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was strongly collaborative, with the clinician inviting input, offering options, and co-creating the plan. The patient’s preferences directly shaped the final approach, including avoiding bitters and texting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized the patient’s freedom to choose and avoided pressure or directives. Suggested strategies were framed as optional experiments rather than requirements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician used open questions, reflections, and scaling to draw out the patient’s own motivation and plan. Evocation could be deeper by further exploring personal values, hopes, and meaning of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was consistently treated as the decision-maker and expert on her own life. The clinician offered options rather than directives and let the patient direct the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively elicited and reinforced, especially around baby-centered values and a concrete plan. Deeper exploration of the patient’s own reasons and identity-based motivations could strengthen change talk further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally reflected without argument or reinforcement, and the patient was not labeled as an alcoholic. More explicit double-sided reflections could better expand the change side while honoring tradition and autonomy.
