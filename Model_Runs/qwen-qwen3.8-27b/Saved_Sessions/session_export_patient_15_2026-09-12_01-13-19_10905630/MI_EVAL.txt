# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was polite and nonjudgmental, but the warmth remained fairly surface-level and the interaction stayed task-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses were limited to general agreement rather than reflecting emotion, ambivalence, or the patient's underlying meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on blood pressure monitoring, but it remained task-bound and did not move toward broader behavior-change exploration, including alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaboration was mainly limited to logistical details, while the clinical goal and plan remained provider-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered some choices regarding the log format and did not pressure heavily, but still assigned a specific monitoring task and repeated instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relied on external guidance and recommendations rather than consistently eliciting the patient's own values, concerns, and motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for preferences on formatting, but the overall stance was expert-driven rather than genuinely collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked a few open invitations but did not deeply explore the patient's own reasons, benefits, or readiness for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient's minimization and hesitation were acknowledged, but the psychiatrist did not effectively soften or redirect sustain talk toward change.
