# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently expressed unconditional positive regard, warmth, and respect throughout the session. Responses were affirming, validating, and non-judgmental, creating a safe environment for action planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the client's perspective accurately and validated her practical goals and barriers. However, empathy was more focused on logistical details than deeper emotional nuances, such as frustration about lost routine or anxiety around gym sales pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused and strategically organized around the client's target behavior. The psychiatrist moved efficiently from barriers to concrete planning, role-play, and action steps without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was highly collaborative, with the psychiatrist offering options while allowing the client to choose, adapt, and co-create the plan. The gym script was especially strong because the client generated the core message and the psychiatrist helped refine it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported the client's autonomy by emphasizing her right to choose and ownership of decisions. Language avoided pressure, and the client was repeatedly reminded that she could proceed without committing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out the client's own reasons, confidence, and problem-solving ideas through open-ended questions. The score is slightly limited because the psychiatrist also offered practical menus and scripts, which helped but reduced the emphasis on pure evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated a strong partnership stance by inviting the client's input and leaving decisions to the client. The role remained guiding and collaborative rather than directive or expert-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited commitment language, confidence, and specific action plans. There is room to further deepen change talk by exploring the client's core values and broader motivations more thoroughly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged and addressed barriers without arguing or dismissing the client's concerns. However, the approach relied more on practical problem-solving and scripts than classic MI techniques such as double-sided reflections.
