# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes ambivalence. Acceptance could feel warmer and more individualized toward Thomas’s specific struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are helpful but remain relatively surface-level. The psychiatrist misses important emotional and social themes, such as Thomas’s concern about appearing fake or making a big deal.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of reducing alcohol use in social settings. The focus becomes tactical too quickly, with more scripts than deeper motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Thomas is invited to choose options, and his preferences shape the plan. However, much of the content is counselor-generated, making collaboration feel uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses optional language and avoids coercion or pressure. There is still some prescriptive encouragement to try the plan, which slightly limits autonomy emphasis.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative techniques, such as the readiness ruler and small-step prompts. Evocation remains superficial and does not fully draw out Thomas’s values, confidence, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shares control by asking for Thomas’s preferences and choices. The interaction still feels more like an expert guiding a plan than a truly collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, including concerns about cost, family, and willingness to try a small step. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to expand, amplify, or reflect this change talk more effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Thomas but often accepts or validates his minimizing stance. More effective double-sided reflections or strategic redirection would be needed to soften sustain talk.
