# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes Hana's ambivalence without judgment, labeling, or pressure. The tone remains respectful and supportive even when Hana expresses uncertainty, annoyance, or discomfort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and nuanced, capturing both the value of the morning pause and Hana's concern that alternatives may feel performative or incomplete. The final reflection about the cigarette as permission to rest adds depth beyond simple paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the target behavior, the morning cigarette and its function as a pause, while gradually narrowing toward a concrete, testable action. The pacing is patient-centered and avoids significant topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative and shaped by Hana's ideas, such as the coffee pause, holding the mug, scrolling the phone, and allowed quiet. It could be strengthened by adding more explicit agenda-setting and reducing slightly clinician-led prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Hana's sense of choice by avoiding directive advice, persuasion, or implied obligation. The language consistently preserves Hana's freedom to decide and experiment on her own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Hana's own motivations and alternatives without supplying reasons to change, following her metaphors around pause, rest, and permission. Deeper evocation could more explicitly connect the small experiment to broader values such as dental health, long-term well-being, or autonomy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Hana as the expert on her own mornings, choices, and preferred solutions. The session moves forward based on Hana's contributions rather than an imposed plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes change-oriented language, leading Hana to identify a possible experiment to try this week. This could be strengthened by explicitly summarizing and amplifying Hana's change talk and asking more directly about confidence or readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging and normalizing Hana's reasons for maintaining the status quo without reinforcing them. The session could improve by more actively minimizing or reframing the sustain talk rather than dwelling somewhat on the difficulty of alternatives.
