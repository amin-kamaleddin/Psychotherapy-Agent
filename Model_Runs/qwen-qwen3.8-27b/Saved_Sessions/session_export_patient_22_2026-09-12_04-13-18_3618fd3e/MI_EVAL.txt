# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the patient's concerns. The warmth is helpful but could be more explicit in affirming the vulnerability of discussing social anxiety and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects the core ambivalence about drinking less while preserving social connection. Reflections are solid but remain somewhat surface-level and could explore deeper emotional concerns such as fear of awkwardness and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and moves toward a concrete experiment for Thursday. Introducing many options at once can slightly clutter the session, but the main goal remains clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's ideas influence the plan, including the mocktail, one-beer limit, and reward. The interaction often feels like guided problem-solving rather than equal co-creation because of the frequent use of clinician-generated strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission is sought, choices are offered, and the patient is allowed to modify the plan. Autonomy support would be stronger if the plan were more rooted in the patient's own solutions rather than the clinician's options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used, and useful information is elicited. Evocation is limited because concrete strategies are offered before fully exploring the patient's values, fears, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and allows the patient to select among options. However, frequent structured lists create a more expert-driven dynamic rather than fully collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient offers meaningful change statements, especially around confidence and avoiding weekday hangovers. The clinician does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk, missing opportunities to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged without argument, and the statement about viewing lapses as information is effective. More strategic use of double-sided reflections could better acknowledge social-drinking pressures while connecting them to the patient's goals.
