# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely nonjudgmental with unconditional positive regard throughout (e.g., "You're not weak," "doubts are normal"). Warmth remains somewhat clinical and task-oriented rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Contains decent reflections of the belonging tension and blank panic, but reflections are often brief, repetitive, and quickly followed by practical advice rather than deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus on shoplifting, urges, and peer pressure without wandering. However, the arc is behavioral/problem-solving rather than MI-guided, frequently shifting among many tactics without strategic movement toward values or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan co-creates some elements (refusal line, texting cousin, gum/band choice), but the psychiatrist generates most interventions and structures the conversation, leaving the power balance uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and occasionally asks Jordan to choose, but the repeated delivery of checklists, scripts, and prescribed steps can feel controlling. Autonomy support is present but uneven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little evocation of Jordan's intrinsic motivation, values, or reasons for change; session mainly provides coping tools and behavioral strategies rather than open questions drawing out his own motives.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session is largely expert-driven with numerous concrete strategies (urge-surfing, exit plans, refusal lines, checklists). Jordan's own wisdom and preferred direction are not consistently positioned as the primary source of the solution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan's change talk ("I know it's bad," "I have to stop") is rarely reflected or amplified. Few open MI-style questions to evoke deeper motivation; the question about doable plan parts is a modest commitment evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about friends, adrenaline, and fear of seeming weak is not effectively softened or redirected. Psychiatrist tends to agree and offer more urge-management tools rather than using double-sided reflections or reframing.
