# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, never reacting with judgment or disappointment when the patient minimizes or pushes back. All statements are received with warmth and validation regardless of the patient's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate complex reflections that capture the patient's dual experience, subtle shifts in thinking, and the emotional weight of identity and future concerns. Goes well beyond surface-level paraphrasing to name the deeper meaning in the patient's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior (alcohol use) while connecting it to the patient's specific goals, with a logical progression from ambivalence to concrete self-directed planning. The session builds momentum without ever wandering or losing strategic focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's ideas and responses substantially shape the session's direction, with the psychiatrist building on the patient's own words and metaphors in real time. The agenda is set collaboratively and the psychiatrist never overrides the patient's lead or imposes a predetermined path.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms that the patient is the expert on his own life and that all choices are his to make, never pressuring or implying a 'correct' answer. The plan is explicitly framed as self-directed and value-based rather than externally imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation clearly emerges from within the patient through strategic questions that draw out his own values, coping strategies, and commitments rather than through external persuasion or information-giving. The psychiatrist consistently elicits change talk and self-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly attributes insights and wisdom to the patient throughout ('That's your own thinking, not mine'), never positioning himself as the expert or prescriber. The entire session is structured around drawing out the patient's own knowledge and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully identifies, names, and amplifies every instance of change talk, helping the patient hear his own motivation more clearly. Change talk naturally escalates from initial ambivalence to concrete self-directed plans with clear momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively acknowledges and holds sustain talk without arguing or dismissing, consistently finding a way to validate while gently redirecting toward change. The handling of the 'everyone's doing stuff' statement could have been slightly more strategic, as it was not explicitly explored before moving to the friendship question.
