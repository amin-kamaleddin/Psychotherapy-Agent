# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, with supportive statements such as “I hear you” and “Totally fair.” However, acceptance remains brief and task-focused, with limited deeper normalization of Anika’s ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The summary accurately captures Anika’s central conflict between relaxing with friends and risking her spot on the team. Empathy remains surface-level and could more deeply explore feelings about belonging, identity, and social discomfort.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, focused trajectory from concern to goal-setting, script selection, rehearsal, and backup planning. The main limitation is moving into practical problem-solving somewhat quickly before fully exploring ambivalence and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anika participates by choosing scripts and refining language, which supports collaboration. However, the psychiatrist provides much of the plan, scripts, and structure, making the process more advisor-driven than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking Anika to select options and respects her desire to keep things low-key. Autonomy support is mixed because the plan is largely counselor-generated rather than elicited from Anika’s own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial open question elicits some of Anika’s goals, but the session quickly shifts into advice, scripting, and rehearsal. There is limited exploration of her values, confidence, stressors, and intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anika is invited to choose scripts and practice, showing some collaborative partnership. The session still feels fairly expert-driven because the psychiatrist supplies a multi-step plan and menu of options rather than waiting for more client-generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anika offers meaningful change talk about not using during season and feeling ready to try a plan. The psychiatrist affirms this but does not consistently ask evocative questions to deepen her reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging Anika’s sustain talk and redirects toward practical coping, which prevents debate. The response would be stronger if it used double-sided reflections to help Anika hold both the social benefits and the risks.
