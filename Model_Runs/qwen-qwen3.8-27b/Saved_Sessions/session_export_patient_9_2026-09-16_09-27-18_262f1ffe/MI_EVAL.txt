# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, often normalizing Amina’s goals and feelings. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming her worth beyond her actions and coping plans.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers clear reflections that capture Amina’s concerns, fear, and desire for stability. Deeper empathy would more fully explore the underlying emotional pain, ambivalence, and grief in the co-parenting conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on improving co-parenting communication and reducing conflict for the son. The direction becomes somewhat narrow and problem-solving-focused rather than exploring Amina’s emotional concerns more fully.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements, as Amina contributes choices such as the message timing, wording preference, and calming activity. The psychiatrist, however, supplies much of the plan, scripts, and structure, limiting Amina’s role in generating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some choices but also provides very specific scripts, timing, and follow-up instructions, which can reduce Amina’s ownership. Stronger autonomy support would include explicit reminders that Amina decides what to do and could modify or reject the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and values, especially regarding stability and protecting the son. More evocation would involve drawing out Amina’s own reasons, confidence, past strengths, and internal motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Amina’s input and responds to her preferences, but often takes on an expert problem-solving role. A more partnership-based approach would emphasize Amina as the expert in her own family dynamics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina expresses meaningful change statements about wanting stability and a calmer co-parenting relationship. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen these statements through questions about desire, ability, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Amina’s concerns about being perceived as cold, defensive, or chasing. The sustain talk is often redirected into practical options rather than explored through balanced reflections or ambivalence work.
