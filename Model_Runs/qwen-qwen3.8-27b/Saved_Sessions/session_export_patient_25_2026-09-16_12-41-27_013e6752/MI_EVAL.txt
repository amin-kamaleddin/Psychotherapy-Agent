# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and normalization. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not fully address the client’s ambivalence or fear of being perceived negatively in front of his child.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some accurate perspective-taking, especially early in the session. Reflections remain surface-level and do not deeply capture the client’s shame, social fear, or identity conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and progressively narrows to concrete actions. The plan could be more explicitly connected to the client’s deeper motivations and confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by inviting choices and summarizing a plan that includes client preferences. However, the therapist supplies most of the content, making the partnership uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supports choice and allows the client to simplify the plan. Introducing many optional strategies and backups can subtly imply pressure or suggest the plan is incomplete.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and rulers elicit useful information and some change talk. Evocation is not consistently used to deepen the client’s own motivation before moving into advice-giving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s input is used to shape the plan, showing some partnership. The session is often driven by the psychiatrist generating options, limiting the client’s role as the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance and confidence rulers effectively elicit change talk. The psychiatrist does not consistently expand or deepen the client’s values, abilities, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and not reinforced, but not actively softened with double-sided reflections or ambivalence-resolution language. The therapist often converts ambivalence into practical problem-solving.
