# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Tyler's experience throughout the session. Repeated safety checks and the conditional promise to tell his mother he was fine may have slightly reduced the sense of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used reflective listening consistently and accurately captured several of Tyler's surface-level concerns. Empathy was largely cognitive and missed deeper emotional themes such as shame, fear of being labeled, or loss of autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintained a general focus on the presenting issue, safety, and the conflict with Tyler's mother. However, the focus shifted toward managing the mother's reaction and ending the session rather than clarifying Tyler's ambivalence about substance use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaved collaboratively by offering options, inviting Tyler's input, and allowing him to shape the response he would use with his mother. Collaboration could be stronger because the therapist remained the primary source of the proposed script and safety agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently emphasized Tyler's choices, respected his refusals, and transparently discussed confidentiality limits. The repeated safety questions introduced some mild pressure and could be framed in a more autonomy-supportive way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made several evocative attempts by asking about Tyler's experience, values, and what would be most useful. These questions were not deeply followed up, and Tyler's statements about being done were not used to explore confidence, values, or prevention goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a generally collaborative stance, invited Tyler's input, and adapted when he chose options or rejected the safety plan. The partnership was slightly weakened when the psychiatrist took on a more expert-driven role with scripts and safety checks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made brief attempts to connect substance use to Tyler's goals and future, but did not effectively deepen change talk. Clear client statements such as "I'm done" and "one-time thing" were not reflected, amplified, or expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with or reinforce Tyler's sustain talk, but there was little active softening or strategic redirection. Some reflections may have unintentionally amplified the desire to make the issue go away and focus on his mother's reaction.
