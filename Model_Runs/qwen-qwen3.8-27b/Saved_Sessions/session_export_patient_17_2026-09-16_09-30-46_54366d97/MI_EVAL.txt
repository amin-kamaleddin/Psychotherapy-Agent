# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the client's ambivalence and affirming her plan. Acceptance is slightly limited because the warmth can feel somewhat procedural and plan-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of exhaustion, guilt, fear of failure, and the importance of the client's daughter. Empathy is slightly limited because stronger emotional depth would be needed around shame, stigma, and the meaning of quitting for the client's identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on smoking cessation and moves purposefully from ambivalence to a concrete, organized plan. Direction is strong, though the later portion becomes somewhat task-focused and plan-heavy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's preferences, and the client's choices shape the plan, including NRT, coping steps, and support. Collaboration is strong but not fully equal because the psychiatrist frequently leads with practical options, scripts, and instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports choice, avoids commanding language, and frames the patch as a trial rather than an obligation. Autonomy support is slightly limited because ready-made scripts and practical instructions may reduce opportunities for the client to generate her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client motivation early by asking about priorities, readiness, and what would help. Evocation becomes less consistent as the psychiatrist shifts toward advice, practical steps, and detailed instructions rather than deeper elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys collaboration by offering choices and inviting input, but also frequently assumes an expert or problem-solving role. Partnership is present but not fully established as equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and affirms the client's motivation and plan. Development of change talk is inconsistent because the psychiatrist often moves quickly to practical recommendations instead of amplifying the client's own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges barriers without judgment and uses low-pressure framing to reduce resistance. Softening is limited by less frequent use of double-sided reflections or reframing that would help the client move from sustain talk back toward change talk.
