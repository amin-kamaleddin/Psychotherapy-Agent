# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with positive regard and no blame or labeling. The warmth was strong but somewhat structured and could have been more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured ambivalence around safety, independence, and identity effectively. Important emotional themes such as helplessness, burden, and uncertainty about the fall were underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the target behavior without drifting into unrelated topics. The progression from values to a concrete self-monitoring plan was coherent and well-anchored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist built on the client’s ideas and incorporated her contributions into the plan. The interaction was somewhat guided by a sequential structure, and the counselor occasionally summarized before the client had fully elaborated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a major strength, with consistent emphasis on the client’s choice and personal meaning. Controlling, moralizing, or pressure-based language was avoided.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked the client’s own values, concerns, and motivations. At times, the counselor interpreted or assigned the client’s reason for change rather than leaving more space for client-driven meaning-making.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was consistently treated as the expert on her own life and change process. The session was strongly client-led, with the psychiatrist following the client’s direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did a strong job eliciting and reinforcing desire, need, and plan language. Change talk could have been further developed through explicit confidence, ability, or commitment scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well using balanced reflections and non-argumentative language, especially around family pressure. The core doubt about whether alcohol caused the fall could have been reflected more explicitly.
