# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating language while honoring the client's pace and concerns. Some statements remain more procedural than emotionally personal, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and include adequate summaries of the client's concerns and ambivalence. They often remain factual and miss deeper emotional or identity-related themes such as grief, self-blame, or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on tobacco cessation and progresses logically toward a concrete, manageable plan. It could be strengthened by more MI-specific summaries and explicit links between the chosen action and the client's stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's preferences and incorporates her choices into the plan. Collaboration is limited by the counselor's pre-structured menu of options rather than more client-driven co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly affirms choice, avoids pressure, and supports a gradual, client-paced approach. Some language and the detailed final checklist occasionally sound prescriptive or authority-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurs, especially around readiness and financial motivators. The session shifts quickly to options and planning, missing opportunities to deepen the client's own motivations, self-efficacy, and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and presents multiple options within a counselor-led framework. A stronger partnership would have allowed the client to generate more of the plan before options were introduced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, particularly around money and the feasibility of a small start. There are missed opportunities to more actively evoke ability, confidence, and values-based reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without judgment or argument. The psychiatrist does not fully explore or work with the ambivalence behind the client's identification with smoking and her social routine.
