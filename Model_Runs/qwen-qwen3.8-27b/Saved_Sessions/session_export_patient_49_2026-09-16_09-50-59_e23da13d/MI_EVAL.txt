# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes ambivalence and imperfect progress. Acceptance could become more personalized by explicitly affirming Hana’s insight, courage, and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures Hana’s ambivalence, practical constraints, and concern about failure. Empathy would be stronger if the clinician explored the emotional and social meaning of smoking, especially its connection to coffee and friends.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on smoking reduction and Hana’s dental concern. The broad concern is successfully narrowed into a specific, actionable plan tied to her daily routine.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Hana to participate in decision-making and co-create a low-effort plan. Collaboration could improve by inviting Hana to generate options before the clinician presents structured suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently emphasizes choice, permission, and client-led decision-making. Some prescriptive language, such as “quitting is best” and the exact reminder text, slightly reduces autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks useful questions about readiness, concerns, and doable steps. Evocation would be stronger if the clinician drew out Hana’s own values, confidence, and reasons for change rather than providing external health information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana is positioned as the decision-maker and actively selects key parts of the plan. More explicit power-sharing, such as asking Hana what she already knows or wants to try first, would strengthen the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some change talk through readiness questions and choices, but often supplies reasons for change instead of amplifying Hana’s own. More affirming and expanding Hana’s change statements would improve this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with Hana’s reasons for continuing smoking and offers alternatives that preserve parts of the ritual. More advanced techniques, such as double-sided reflections, would better address her ambivalence.
