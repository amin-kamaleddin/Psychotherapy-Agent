# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard without judgment, even when the patient is resistant or dismissive. Validates her right to her own experience and affirms her expertise on her own life throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, particularly in reflecting frustration at being dismissed by the medical system. Could go deeper in sitting with the emotional weight of feeling her body's signals are being invalidated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior (inhaler use/asthma management) while flexibly following the patient's lead. Slightly abrupt transition to the tracking question, but overall directional focus is strong and the session ends with a clear actionable insight.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance, consistently framing the conversation as joint exploration and following the patient's pace and lead. Could be stronger with more evidence of the session's direction being genuinely co-constructed in real-time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with consistent reinforcement of the patient's ownership, agency, and right to sit in an in-between space. Every intervention reinforces her control without a single moment of perceived pressure or imposed timeline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully uses evocative questions that lead the patient to generate her own insights and change talk, culminating in a masterful reframing of her final statement as clear directional change. Could have more consistently used scaling or other techniques to draw out values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and positions the patient as the expert on her own life, respecting her time constraints and following her lead. Could be stronger with more evidence of her ideas substantially shaping the session's direction in real-time.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully cultivates change talk, particularly in the final exchanges where the patient generates her own insight about wanting to know when to use the inhaler. The explicit amplification of her spontaneous change talk is a strong, well-timed intervention.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating concerns without amplifying them and explicitly disowning the 'pushing' role. Could have included a slightly more active pivot when the patient said she just needs the psychiatrist to stop making it a bigger deal.
