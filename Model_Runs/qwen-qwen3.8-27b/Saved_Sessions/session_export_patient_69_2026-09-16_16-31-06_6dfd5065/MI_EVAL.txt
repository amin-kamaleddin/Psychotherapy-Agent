# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a nonjudgmental, low-pressure stance and respects the client’s boundaries. It is slightly short of a 5 because explicit affirmation of the client’s strengths and worth is limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client’s fatigue, suspicion, and sense of mandated check-ins as paperwork. The reflections are helpful but remain somewhat surface-level, especially around deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear, coherent focus on narrowing to a concrete, client-selected task. The connection to the broader goal of sustained engagement is not made explicit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor behaves as a partner by asking permission, inviting client input, and organizing the session around the client’s preferences. The counselor’s expertise is used to serve the client’s agenda rather than direct it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly emphasizes client choice, control, and freedom from coercion. The client is repeatedly told he is in the driver’s seat and is not required to perform, justify, or commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits practical client-owned content such as job interest, auto repair, and preference for a list. Deeper values, self-efficacy, and personal reasons for change are not fully evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treats the client as the expert on his own experience and preferences. The client’s choices substantially shape the session rather than the counselor imposing an agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor moves the client from dismissiveness to some change-related language about jobs and auto repair. Change talk is not frequently amplified, reflected, or deepened with stronger MI strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with the client’s resistance and offers low-pressure, validating alternatives. The counselor occasionally remains in a practical, status-quo mode rather than consistently redirecting toward change.
