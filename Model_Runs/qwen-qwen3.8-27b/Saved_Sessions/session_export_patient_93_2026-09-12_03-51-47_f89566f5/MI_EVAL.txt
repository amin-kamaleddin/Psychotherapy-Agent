# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client’s choices. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and could be warmer and more individualized when the client minimizes or pushes back.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, routine, tiredness, and the fall. Deeper emotional meanings, such as retirement, loneliness, fear, and identity, are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, effects, and a practical plan with a clear follow-up. Repetitive summarization at the end slightly weakens strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well and lets the client choose realistic options. However, the therapist supplies many tactics and repeatedly frames the plan, limiting fully client-led solution generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is strongly supported, and there is no pressure to quit or make a larger commitment. Repeated menus of options and closing prompts could feel mildly therapist-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and benefit/downside questions elicit some motivation and change language. The session moves quickly to practical planning, missing opportunities to deepen internal motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats the client as the expert and shares decision-making. Power sharing is reduced by repeated therapist-supplied options and plan summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces useful change talk, such as capping drinks and reporting back. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or connect this change talk to values or self-ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing or minimizing the client. More advanced softening strategies, such as double-sided reflections and resolving the tension between her routine and health concerns, are not consistently used.
