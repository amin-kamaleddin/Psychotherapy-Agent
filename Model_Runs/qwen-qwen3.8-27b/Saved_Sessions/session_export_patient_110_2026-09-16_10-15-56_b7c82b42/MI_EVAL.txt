# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the patient's concerns about retirement, side effects, and testing. The tone remains supportive, though it can feel task-focused rather than deeply affirming the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses adequate reflective listening, especially around ambivalence about medication and lifestyle changes. Misses deeper emotional themes such as fear of memory changes, overexertion, and the importance of staying active with family.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, organized focus on cholesterol treatment through medication and lifestyle changes. Progresses logically from concerns to labs, activity, diet, and follow-up without losing the main goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporates the patient's ideas, such as short evening walks, swapping red meat for fish, and using a written list. Still, the psychiatrist drives much of the content, structure, and clinical advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices, options, and noncoercive language while building a practical plan. Could strengthen ownership by more explicitly affirming the patient's freedom to decide what feels right.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initially asks good evocative questions about what feels important, doable, and ready. Later shifts toward information-giving and concrete planning rather than deepening the patient's own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows collaborative moments by asking what would be comfortable and whether the plan feels manageable. The overall interaction remains more expert-led than fully shared in deciding direction and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some initial change talk around medication willingness, walking, and staying active with his wife. Does not consistently reflect, amplify, or follow up on this change talk with deeper MI-style questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and acknowledges concerns about side effects and fear of feeling "zombie." The focus on risks and monitoring keeps the conversation close to sustain talk without reframing it into stronger change talk.
