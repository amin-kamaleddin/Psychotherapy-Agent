# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with phrases like 'without judgment' and 'I hear you.' However, the acceptance becomes repetitive and lacks deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrates solid reflective listening and accurately captures the client's ambivalence and emotional texture. The main limitation is repetition, with less consistent movement into deeper feelings such as shame, fear, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally remains focused on gambling, debt, and related social situations, but the conversation becomes circular. It lacks stronger summarizing, scaling, or agenda-setting to create momentum toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and frames the work as a joint exploration. However, the clinician continues to drive the process and repeats similar questions, which limits full co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks what feels possible, safe, or doable for the client, without pressure or advice-giving. The support is strong, though it could be more explicit in affirming the client's ownership of decisions during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit some change-oriented ideas, such as leaving the cards at home or being 'Anya, not the gambler.' However, the clinician does not consistently deepen these statements into stronger internal motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys that the client knows her life best and invites her to lead. The limitation is that the interaction remains more verbally collaborative than fully enacted, with the clinician selecting and repeating questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes practical change talk, such as setting limits, leaving early, and staying for the conversation. However, there is insufficient reinforcement, summarizing, or amplification of these statements to build stronger momentum and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client's reasons for continuing to gamble without arguing or dismissing them. However, the sustain talk is not consistently softened or redirected toward change using double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic summaries.
