# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with clear support for Daniel’s smoking cessation. However, the positive regard remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes a reasonable effort to understand Daniel’s perspective and patterns. Reflections are mostly factual and do not deeply address the emotional weight of social pressure or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use in social settings and practical coping strategies. At times, it becomes more tactical and skill-based than MI-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel participates in role-play and contributes ideas, showing some collaboration. The psychiatrist still leads much of the content by supplying scripts and strategies rather than co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites Daniel to select options. However, rapid advice-giving and scripted responses can subtly pressure him to adopt recommended behaviors.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some effort to evoke Daniel’s motivations through open questions. The session shifts quickly into advice and planning without fully exploring his values or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel is invited to choose options and rehearse responses, supporting partnership. The psychiatrist often takes an expert role and at times speaks as if supplying Daniel’s exact line, which weakens ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel offers useful change talk, especially around his smoking cessation and willingness to try limits. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or expand this change talk with MI-style elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not argued with or reinforced, which is appropriate. The response is more behavioral and problem-solving than MI-based, with limited use of double-sided reflections.
