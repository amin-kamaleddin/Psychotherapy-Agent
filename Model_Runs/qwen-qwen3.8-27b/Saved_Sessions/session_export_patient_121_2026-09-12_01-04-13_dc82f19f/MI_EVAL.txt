# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a generally nonjudgmental stance and validates frustration, but the acceptance is often generic and paired with advice, lists, and safety questions. There is limited evidence of deeper, individualized positive regard for Sofia’s hurt, isolation, or repeated school conflicts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is present but shallow, often focusing on surface behavior rather than the underlying emotions of hurt, unfairness, exhaustion, or loss of connection. Missed opportunities include reflecting how painful it is that a former friend avoids her and how isolating or exhausting the ongoing blame feels.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly within the referral concern, but the focus is inconsistent and lacks a coherent agenda. The conversation shifts among scripts, messaging, staying home, grounding, safety checks, and ending without consistently guiding Sofia toward a specific change-related goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is not strongly collaborative because the counselor repeatedly supplies scripts, coping steps, and options rather than building a shared agenda with Sofia. There is limited evidence of joint decision-making, mutual expertise, or using Sofia’s strengths and preferences as the main drivers of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly emphasizes choice and non-coercion by inviting input, offering options, and not pressuring Sofia to go to school, apologize, or follow a specific plan. This is one of the stronger areas, though the repeated suggestions of scripts, lists, and reminders can feel subtly steering rather than fully client-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited because the main pattern is offering advice and scripts rather than eliciting motivation from within Sofia. There are few evocative questions about values, goals, importance, confidence, or past successes, so her own reasons for change are not consistently drawn out or strengthened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and offers choices, but the session remains largely counselor-driven through repeated scripts, coping steps, and safety language. There is little evidence that Sofia is treated as the primary expert on her own goals, relationships, and motivation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia expresses occasional change-oriented language, but the counselor does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or expand it into deeper change talk. Instead of using importance or confidence questions, the counselor moves toward offering scripts and options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia frequently expresses sustain talk and avoidance, but the counselor does not effectively soften or redirect this language. Statements normalizing avoidance are not paired with exploration of the costs of staying stuck, underlying values, or possible steps toward change.
