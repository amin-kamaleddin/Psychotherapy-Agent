# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warmth, respect, and a nonjudgmental stance throughout the session, validating Amina's self-protective language without being preachy. Could be slightly stronger with more explicit normalization of the struggle and a warmer affective tone in moments of self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout skill in the session — reflections are highly accurate, nuanced, and build progressively in depth, consistently tracking feeling-level content rather than just surface-level. The reflection naming the unspoken loneliness of wondering if efforts are even noticed is a particularly powerful empathic move.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and logical progression from barrier identification to deeper self-understanding, staying consistently connected to the employment goal without wandering. Lacks a directional anchor or concrete next-step focus at the close, where a brief summary tying insights to one small actionable step would strengthen the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session exemplifies high-level collaboration — the client's input substantially redirects the session, and the psychiatrist immediately acknowledges errors and rebuilds from Amina's corrected frame. The two-parts internal dialogue exploration was entirely Amina-driven, with the psychiatrist following and reflecting rather than leading.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The approach is inherently autonomy-supportive, consistently asking Amina's opinions and crediting her own insights without imposing a plan or using directive language. Could be strengthened with more explicit affirmations of her freedom of choice at key decision points.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Every major insight in the session comes from Amina herself, demonstrating strong evocation through strategic questions designed to elicit rather than inform. The psychiatrist could more actively amplify change talk and include a direct evocation of commitment language to push toward action.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance throughout, positioning Amina as the expert on her own life and explicitly crediting her insights. Could be slightly strengthened by more explicit negotiation of direction and occasional peer-level sharing to fully equalize the relationship.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes change talk through strategic questions that elicit Amina's own language (e.g., 'sharper,' 'receipts') and reflects it back effectively. The session lacks structured elicitation of DARN-C categories and a direct confidence-scaling or commitment question that would push toward explicit change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by reframing it as a conditional response rather than a fixed trait, reducing its power without invalidating it. A 5 would include more explicit pivots or double-sided reflections that actively create internal counter-argument against the sustain talk.
