# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and nonjudgment with supportive language. Warmth is generally present but could be deeper around the client's fear, shame, and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures the main content of the client's concerns. Important underlying feelings such as fear, shame, and self-doubt are not consistently reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains generally focused on reducing drinking and improving contraceptive reliability. Introducing many options at once can dilute strategic momentum toward a single change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites selection, but the work often resembles presenting options rather than co-creating them. More consistent use of the client's own priorities would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's pace and choices are consistently respected, and she is asked to select among options. Some counselor-generated suggestions and scripts are somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful early questions elicit the client's reasons and confidence. The session shifts too quickly into information and practical options rather than continuing to evoke the client's own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some invitation to choice and permission-seeking, but the interaction often feels expert-led. More explicit recognition that the client is the expert in her own life would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and asks relevant questions. The client's own reasons and values are not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally not challenged, and the client's concerns are validated. There are fewer strategic double-sided reflections or reframes that gently move ambivalence toward change.
