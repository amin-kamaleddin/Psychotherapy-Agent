# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and non-judgmental, with genuine affirmations that validate Elias's struggles. One slightly performative line ('I love that you put it that way') marginally undermines the otherwise authentic warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with a masterful synthesis of Elias's ambivalence (homework vs. kitchen tension). Missed an opportunity to explicitly reflect the emotional weight of his fear of deceiving his children, which was arguably the most painful element.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, progressive focus with well-calibrated pacing, moving logically from ambivalence to a workable framework. The ending lacks a tighter collaborative consolidation into a concrete, actionable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration throughout—Elias drives the content, sets the pace, and arrives at his own conclusions. The psychiatrist's contributions always serve to deepen Elias's own thinking rather than substitute for it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with zero directive language; the psychiatrist consistently returns ownership to Elias. Particularly strong in honoring his ambivalence and right to his own pace ('For now is doing a lot of work in that sentence').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation with all insights emerging from Elias himself—no external reasons for change offered. Missed a final evocative move to consolidate his commitment language ('I'll keep showing up') into a concrete, self-owned plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Elias as the authority on his own experience through open, non-directive questions. Two moments where the psychiatrist's own metaphors ('the prosecutor was you too,' 'your hands found something to hold') slightly intrude on the pure partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Elias's own evidence for change and personal definitions of motivation through strategic questions. Ends without a deliberate move to consolidate change talk into a confidence or commitment exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully with double-sided reflections and gentle pivots that validate without amplifying. Could have more elegantly turned one or two sustain talk moments into change talk vehicles by exploring the source of the doubt.
