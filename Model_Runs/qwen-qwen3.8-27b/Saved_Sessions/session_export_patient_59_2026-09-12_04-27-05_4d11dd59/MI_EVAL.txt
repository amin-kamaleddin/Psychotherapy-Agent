# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, asks permission before documenting, and avoids moralizing or arguing. It does not reach 5 because the warmth feels somewhat procedural and lacks deeper affirmation or normalization of Jamal's stress and court-referred experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Jamal's frustration, impatience, and desire for control. However, the empathy remains somewhat superficial, with limited exploration of deeper emotions, values, or complex ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a narrow, consistent focus on completing the administrative process without drifting aimlessly. However, the direction is not consistently tied to the target behavior of marijuana use/possession or to a behaviorally relevant change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist engages in joint problem-solving, offers choices, and adapts to Jamal's preference for quiet. It falls short of 5 because the clinician still frames the options and retains much of the agenda-setting and problem-solving structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirms Jamal's freedom to choose, respects his request for quiet, and avoids pressuring him to accept a program or rehearse statements. It does not reach 5 because the repeated offering of options, scripts, and a breathing exercise can feel mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few opening open questions, but the session quickly shifts to clinician-provided options and administrative guidance. The psychiatrist does not effectively draw out Jamal's internal motivation, values, concerns, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and respects Jamal's preferences, demonstrating some power sharing. However, the stance remains somewhat consultant-like, with the clinician providing menus and scripts rather than positioning Jamal as the expert on change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes only rare and weak attempts to cultivate change talk, using mild evocative questions at the start. Jamal's statements about being able to handle the process quickly are not reflected, amplified, or expanded into client-generated reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with Jamal's minimization and sustain talk, acknowledging statements such as 'it was just a small bag.' However, the clinician does not use double-sided reflections, gentle reframing, or discrepancy to move the conversation toward change.
