# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone. However, the warmth was somewhat functional and tied to the client's progress rather than fully expressing unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate, consistent, and deeply attuned to the client's ambivalence. The counselor effectively captured the tension between protecting academic goals and maintaining social connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of drinking with friends and the specific goal of developing a refusal strategy. The counselor effectively guided the conversation from problem identification to strategy selection and role-play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially framed the session as a joint exploration but frequently shifted into an expert-driven role. By generating lists of options and scripts, the counselor assumed much of the cognitive work instead of exploring with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently affirmed the client's freedom to choose and respected her decisions. However, providing a specific menu of pre-written scripts subtly steered the client's autonomy rather than allowing her to discover solutions entirely from within.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor made an initial effort to draw out the client's past successes and ideas, but largely abandoned evocation in favor of providing ready-made scripts. Telling the client what to say limited the development of her own internal change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** While a collaborative stance was established at the start, the partnership quickly shifted to a more directive, expert-driven dynamic. The counselor generated ideas rather than maintaining a true partnership in which both parties contributed to solution generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client was able to generate some initial change talk and strategies, such as the school-night rule. However, the counselor limited further evocation of change talk by providing a menu of pre-written refusal lines instead of drawing out the client's own unique expressions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged the client's sustain talk regarding fear of being left out or perceived as boring. However, the counselor bypassed this ambivalence by quickly pivoting to problem-solving and behavioral scripts rather than using advanced reflections.
