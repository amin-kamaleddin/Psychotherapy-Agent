# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, acknowledging Jason's frustration. However, warmth remains procedural and does not fully normalize ambivalence or express deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor makes reasonable efforts to reflect Jason's concerns and urgency. These reflections remain surface-level and do not deeply explore emotional undertones such as embarrassment, fear, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear and consistent focus on the conduct incident, desired warning, and practical resolution. The main weakness is that direction becomes highly administrative rather than focused on behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor and Jason collaborate on the note wording, date, and format. However, collaboration is limited to the administrative deliverable rather than co-creating the direction of change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers multiple choice points and respects Jason's preferences regarding the statement, delivery, date, and follow-up. The main limitation is that autonomy support is mostly procedural rather than reinforcing ownership over future behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks some appropriate open questions to draw out Jason's perspective and concerns. Later, the session shifts to logistical questions and does not consistently explore deeper motivations, values, or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative language is present, but the session is largely driven by the counselor's agenda and administrative steps. Jason is not strongly positioned as the expert on his own values, goals, and direction for change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial questions open the door to change talk, but the counselor quickly provides prewritten scripts rather than developing Jason's own language. Jason's change talk remains minimal and is not skillfully expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with Jason's minimization and blame statements, which is appropriate. However, the counselor does not meaningfully soften or redirect sustain talk using double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy exploration.
