# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and acknowledges Lars’s conflict without shaming. However, the acceptance remains functional rather than warm, with limited deeper affirmation of Lars as a person and the emotional weight of his goals.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows moderate empathic understanding, especially around Lars’s ambivalence about car work, mates, and feeling weak. Empathy is often brief and followed quickly by problem-solving, leaving emotionally rich themes such as prison fear and shame insufficiently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the high-risk Friday situation, alcohol use, peer pressure, and practical behavior change. It could be strengthened by more consistently linking the immediate plan to Lars’s broader values, relationships, and re-entry goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and Lars jointly refine the plan, with Lars selecting options and contributing the sober drink, tools, and reframing the timer as his own rule. Collaboration is strong but partly limited because many options and tactics are clinician-generated rather than emerging primarily from Lars.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lars is repeatedly offered choices, his preferences are respected, and his concerns about control are accommodated. Autonomy support is somewhat reduced by the clinician’s frequent suggestion of specific tactics and repeated focus on raising confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative techniques are used well, especially the confidence ruler and the question that leads Lars to identify not drinking before leaving as a key step. However, the session often relies too much on clinician-generated strategies rather than evoking Lars’s own internal resources and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is genuine partnership in inviting Lars’s input and adapting to his boundaries, such as not having Donna manage him. The balance is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently supplies many pre-made options and drives the planning content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk, including Lars’s increasing confidence and insight that staying sober before the hangout would improve the plan. This change talk is not consistently deepened, amplified, or connected to Lars’s larger reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reject Lars’s sustain talk and acknowledges the pull of loyalty and concern about appearing weak. Stronger double-sided reflections and value-based exploration would have helped soften sustain talk more effectively.
