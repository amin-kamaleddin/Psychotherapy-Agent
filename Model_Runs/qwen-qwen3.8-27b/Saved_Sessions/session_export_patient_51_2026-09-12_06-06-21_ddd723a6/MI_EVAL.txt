# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, validating, and normalizing language early on. However, repeatedly failing to honor the client's clear need to leave weakens acceptance and nonjudgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some perspective-taking about the client's busy schedule and stress. The empathy remains surface-level and repetitive, without deeper reflection of the client's underlying concerns, urgency, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays consistently focused on the medication safety issue without drifting into unrelated topics. It loses effectiveness by becoming repetitive and circular rather than progressing toward a client-owned next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening is collaborative, with the client invited to set the focus and participate in decisions. Collaboration becomes less genuine when the client's repeated time boundary is not integrated into the flow of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy early by stating the client is in charge and avoids direct medication directives. Repeatedly re-offering a message after the client has declined undermines genuine autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist primarily provides safety information rather than eliciting the client's own motivations, values, or concerns. Missed opportunities to reflect, amplify, or expand the client's existing change talk limit evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with clear collaborative intent and shared agenda control. The dynamic becomes less partner-like after the client repeatedly asks to leave, as the psychiatrist continues pushing the same option and safety recap.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers usable statements of concern, safety, and planning, but these are not actively developed. The psychiatrist does not use evocative questions or reflections to strengthen the client's own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or invalidate the client's desire to leave. However, the same safety information and offer are repeatedly returned to instead of skillfully reflecting and softening the client's tension.
