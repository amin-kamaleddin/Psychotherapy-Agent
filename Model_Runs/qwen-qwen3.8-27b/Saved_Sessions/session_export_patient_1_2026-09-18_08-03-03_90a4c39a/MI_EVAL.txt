# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful; validates the patient's explanations and self-definitions without confrontation. Falls short of a 5 due to a lack of explicit warm affirmations of the patient's worth or normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with accurate captures of the patient's rationalizations and defensiveness. Reflections remain at the cognitive/behavioral level, missing the emotional underpinnings such as the need for autonomy or the meaning behind the weekend ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear progressive narrowing of focus on the target behavior with good momentum and logical flow. The session lacks a consolidation or landing point, ending mid-conversation without a brief summary or collaborative next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites the patient's input and avoids imposing an external framework, allowing the patient's ideas to shape direction. The interaction remains somewhat structured by the psychiatrist's question sequence, limiting genuine back-and-forth dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong and consistent autonomy support with clear language like 'that's completely your call' and 'you can stop whenever you want.' Falls short of a 5 because the patient's ownership could have been more explicitly affirmed at key moments of resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions are designed to draw out the patient's own thinking rather than providing external reasons for change. The evocation remains superficial and misses opportunities to explore deeper values, identity, or the patient's own stated beliefs about being in control.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient is consistently positioned as an equal partner with language like 'I'd love to hear your take on that.' The partnership is somewhat one-directional in practice, with limited evidence of true mutual exchange or the psychiatrist sharing their own perspective transparently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine efforts to evoke change talk, including effective lines like 'That's not nothing' and 'There's something in that that feels doable.' The change talk that emerges remains very thin and superficial, with missed opportunities to explore values, use scaling questions, or deepen the patient's own capacity statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids reinforcing the patient's sustain talk, acknowledging it neutrally and gently redirecting without argument or confrontation. Techniques remain relatively basic; more strategic use of double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence-naming would elevate the skill.
