# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard while affirming Margaret's values and ambivalence.
A score of 5 is not given because the warmth remains somewhat concise and professional, with less deep normalization of her struggle and self-worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, capturing the central ambivalence and meaning of the routine.
Empathy could be deeper with more explicit feeling-level reflections about fear, embarrassment, and worry about independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use in a way that supports safety and preserves independence.
The conversation progresses logically from ambivalence to a concrete, targeted first-night plan without meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully collaborative, using inclusive and co-constructive language throughout.
The final plan emerges directly from Margaret's own suggestions rather than being handed to her by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with explicit honoring of Margaret's choice and a strong avoidance of pressure or control.
The plan is consistently framed as self-directed, body-based, and owned by the client rather than an external command.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes motivation from within Margaret rather than providing external reasons for change.
Margaret generates her own motivations, plans, and language, demonstrating strong client-driven evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a strong partnership stance by positioning Margaret as the expert on her own life.
The session is not expert-driven, and Margaret's contributions directly shape the direction and final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session does a strong job of eliciting and reinforcing change talk, turning it into concrete, clinically effective plans.
It falls short of a 5 because confidence and ability talk could be developed more explicitly, such as through confidence ratings or past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully without arguing, correcting, or challenging Margaret's reservations.
It is not a 5 because the counselor could more consistently use explicit double-sided reflections that both acknowledge sustain talk and clearly pivot toward change.
