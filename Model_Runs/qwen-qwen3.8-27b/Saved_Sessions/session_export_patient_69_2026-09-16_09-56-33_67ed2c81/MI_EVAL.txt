# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Kieran’s frustration and need for privacy. However, acceptance is somewhat procedural and does not fully normalize the emotional burden behind his resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Kieran’s practical concerns, boredom, desire for speed, and need for privacy. Empathy remains surface-level and misses deeper emotions such as resentment, loss of control, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the referral concern and initially offers a concrete target. After Kieran chooses the checklist, direction becomes task completion rather than guiding toward a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers options, invites input, and adapts to Kieran’s stated preferences. Collaboration is practical and respectful, but it relies on a pre-made checklist rather than co-creating goals or next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports choice, respects boundaries, and avoids pressure while leaving help available. Autonomy support is strong but mostly procedural rather than explicitly affirming ownership of the larger change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial questions begin to evoke goals and engagement barriers, but evocation stops once the checklist is introduced. The clinician does not draw out Kieran’s values, motivations, confidence, or agency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a collaborative stance and lets Kieran’s preferences shape the session. Partnership is strong logistically but does not explicitly convey that Kieran is the expert on his own life and readiness for change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to evoke change talk, mainly through an opening question and a future goal on the checklist. The clinician does not consistently draw out Kieran’s reasons, desires, abilities, or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, they do not effectively soften or redirect it using double-sided reflections, reframing, or linking concerns to possible change.
