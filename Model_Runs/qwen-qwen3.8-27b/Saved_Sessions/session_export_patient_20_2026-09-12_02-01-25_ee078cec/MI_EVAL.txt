# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect, acceptance, and nonjudgment, including validating effort and allowing for slips. Acceptance is somewhat functional rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate reflective listening but often remains factual rather than exploring underlying emotion, shame, or fear. Reflections are accurate but brief and quickly followed by advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on reducing bedtime shots and improving mornings, with relevant questions and limited topic drift. It becomes procedural early before fully clarifying values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, invites preferences, and adapts to the client's choices, such as the shower and shorter breathing. Collaboration is somewhat option-driven because the clinician supplies many strategies rather than fully eliciting client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites direction, asks permission, normalizes slipping, and reduces pressure. However, many clinician-generated options subtly create a guided or evaluated feel.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks readiness and confidence questions but repeatedly introduces specific behavioral options before eliciting the client's own motivations and solutions. Change talk is present but not consistently developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows the client's preferences to shape the plan, but supplies a large number of concrete options and behavioral prescriptions. The dynamic is somewhat directive rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk about desired mornings and avoiding missed meetings, but often moves quickly into problem-solving. It does not consistently deepen, amplify, or return to the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts the client's stated benefits, avoids arguing against drinking, and reduces defensiveness with normalizing statements. It does not consistently use double-sided reflections or pivot sustain talk into change language.
