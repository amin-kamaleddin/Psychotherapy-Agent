# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Rowan with respect and without blame, especially when he expresses resistance. Acceptance is slightly limited by a more task-focused tone rather than deeper person-centered validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections and shows understanding of Rowan's concerns about hassle, clutter, and not wanting lectures. The main gap is less deep exploration of underlying emotions such as shame, fatigue, or defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior of charging and using the device. The conversation moves logically from ambivalence to a clear, concrete plan without drifting off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rowan's choices clearly shape the session, and the psychiatrist adapts to his preferences in real time. The final plan is co-created rather than imposed, reflecting strong collaborative engagement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames decisions as Rowan's to make and respects his boundaries, including his refusal of a morning check-in. This strongly supports his sense of ownership and reduces pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful questions but often moves quickly into advisory problem-solving and specific options. There is less time spent drawing out Rowan's own intrinsic motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rowan's input and lets him choose among options, which supports a collaborative stance. However, the psychiatrist still generates much of the structure and could more explicitly affirm Rowan as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits movement from resistance to commitment talk, such as 'I'll plug it in' and 'I'll try.' The main limitation is that the change talk remains largely behavioral rather than expanded through Rowan's values or deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Rowan's sustain talk and responds by validating concern and redirecting toward small, autonomous choices. The main gap is limited use of more advanced reflections to actively transform resistance into change talk.
