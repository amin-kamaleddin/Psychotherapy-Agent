# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and reassuring. However, warmth sometimes feels procedural rather than deeply affirming of the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects surface concerns such as fear of the numbers and forgetting. Depth is limited because responses often move quickly toward practical solutions rather than exploring emotion or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of remembering and understanding blood glucose checks. Direction is strong, though achieved through practical planning rather than motivational direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts to the client's preferences, such as written tools and a shorter trial period. Collaboration is moderate because the psychiatrist still supplies most of the ideas, scripts, and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and nonpunitive reframes, such as restarting after a missed day. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated prescriptive steps and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few initial questions but quickly shifts into advice and task planning. There is limited effort to elicit the client's own values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's input influences the plan, but the psychiatrist remains the primary provider of options. The session feels more like expert guidance than true MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client makes several change-related statements, but they are not consistently evoked, amplified, or built upon. The focus shifts too quickly toward scripts, checklists, and practical steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reduces threat by normalizing forgetfulness and judgment. Stronger MI skills, such as double-sided reflections, would help redirect ambivalence more effectively.
