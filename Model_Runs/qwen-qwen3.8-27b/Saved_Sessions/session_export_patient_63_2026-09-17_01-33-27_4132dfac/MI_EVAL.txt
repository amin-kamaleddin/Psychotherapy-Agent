# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard while normalizing Jordan’s ambivalence. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat structured and could more explicitly affirm Jordan’s courage and personal values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, strong reflections of Jordan’s conflict between peer belonging and legal risk. Deeper empathy would better capture the fear, shame, and guilt behind statements like “I don’t want to fail again.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on reducing shoplifting and legal risk while moving logically from ambivalence to triggers and a concrete plan. Occasional drift into boredom, anger, and isolation could be more consistently tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and allows Jordan’s ideas to shape much of the conversation. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still leads much of the structure through consecutive questions rather than fully negotiating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Jordan’s agency by following his pace, asking open questions, and avoiding direct advice or lecturing. Stronger autonomy support would more explicitly affirm Jordan’s freedom to choose despite the court context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Jordan’s own motivations and ideas, including his desire for authentic choice and a “broke for a few weeks” plan. The evocation remains surface-level and does not consistently deepen into values, identity, future self, or confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally collaborative stance and treats Jordan as the expert on his own experience. A stronger partnership would include more explicit shared decision-making about the session focus and next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes several important change statements from Jordan. It could be stronger by more systematically using MI techniques such as importance/confidence scaling, values-based reflections, and amplifying Jordan’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Jordan’s desire to belong and does not argue with or reinforce his sustain talk. However, sustain talk remains central for much of the session and is not consistently redirected using double-sided reflections toward change.
