# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintained a warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance throughout the session. Statements like “I’m here to listen without judgment” and “treat it as data, not failure” demonstrated strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections and showed good understanding of Daniel’s ambivalence and pragmatic style. However, some deeper emotions behind his skepticism and fear of being wrong were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use, sleep, recovery, and marathon training goals. The conversation moved smoothly toward a concrete, behaviorally relevant plan with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used an equal-partner stance by offering options and allowing Daniel’s preferences to shape the plan. Collaboration could be stronger if Daniel were more explicitly invited to co-create the agenda and confirm priorities before each step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasized Daniel’s freedom to choose, avoid pushing a fixed plan, and framed slips as data rather than failure. This strongly supported Daniel’s ownership and sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evoking questions and confidence rulers, but often shifted quickly into information-giving and planning. More exploration of Daniel’s values, hopes, and personal reasons for change would have strengthened evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input, asked permission, and allowed Daniel to choose a low-effort option that fit his lifestyle. A more explicitly shared decision-making process and more frequent check-ins on Daniel’s priorities would have strengthened partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel offered some change talk, but the psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand on it. More follow-up questions about his goals and personal motivation would have better cultivated change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoided arguing with Daniel’s sustain talk and reframed the plan as low-effort. However, the session did not consistently use discrepancy, double-sided reflections, or values exploration to soften Daniel’s preference to maintain the status quo.
