# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance while acknowledging Jason’s concerns. It falls slightly short of a 5 because the warmth is functional and the session quickly shifts into problem-solving around managing consequences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor provides accurate reflective summaries and perspective-taking, showing understanding of Jason’s frustration and goals. However, the empathy remains mostly cognitive and does not fully explore underlying shame, fear, or identity conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has clear momentum toward a practical exit plan with specific steps. However, the focus becomes narrow, emphasizing avoiding detection and minimizing consequences rather than addressing broader alcohol-related risk and long-term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites Jason’s input and builds the final plan from his ideas, showing a collaborative stance. This is somewhat weakened by the early provision of a prescriptive list of practical tricks before fully eliciting Jason’s own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Jason’s autonomy by emphasizing his control over focus and decisions, and by avoiding pressure to stop drinking. The directive practical advice, including concealment-related suggestions, limits the strength of this autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes practical planning, confidence, and specific behavioral ideas from Jason. However, there are missed opportunities to evoke deeper values, internal motivations, and discrepancy-related change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys partnership by inviting Jason’s input and allowing his ideas to shape the plan. This is weakened when the counselor provides a list of suggestions that shifts the interaction toward expert-driven advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jason expresses meaningful change talk, including concerns about his record, degree, and not wanting to be reckless. The counselor does not consistently amplify, reflect, or deepen these statements to strengthen motivation for broader behavioral change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with Jason’s sustain talk and generally rolls with it. However, the counselor does not actively soften this talk and inadvertently reinforces it by focusing on avoiding consequences rather than addressing alcohol-related risk.
