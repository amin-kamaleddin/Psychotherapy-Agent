# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, with affirmations that normalize Elira’s struggle. It could reach a 5 with more explicit unconditional positive regard and less premature positive interpretation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing ambivalence, anxiety, and the protective function of avoidance. It could be deeper by explicitly validating shame, fear of feeling “dumb,” and self-distrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of following through after practice. The conversation is well paced and progressively narrows the goal into a concrete, manageable action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elira’s ideas strongly shape the plan, and the psychiatrist builds on her suggestions without overriding them. The collaboration could be more fully negotiated with more explicit power-sharing and preference checks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes choice, safety, and the ability to stop, reducing pressure to commit. It would be stronger with more explicit statements that the choice is completely Elira’s and less implicit assumption of readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits client-generated strategies and resources rather than prescribing solutions. It would be more MI-ideal by also evoking Elira’s values, confidence, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and frequently returns decision-making to the client. The session remains somewhat therapist-led in pacing and structure, limiting fully negotiated partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encourages action planning and some client-generated change talk, but the focus remains more on logistics than motivation. It would benefit from directly eliciting and amplifying desire, ability, reasons, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles avoidance and self-distrust well by reflecting, reframing, and redirecting toward smaller steps rather than arguing. It could be improved with more consistent double-sided reflections and explicit normalization of avoidance as protective.
