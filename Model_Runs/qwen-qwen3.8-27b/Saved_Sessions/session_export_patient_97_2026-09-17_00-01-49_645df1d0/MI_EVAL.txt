# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, with no shaming or moralistic pressure. Warmth is strong but occasionally feels structured or formulaic rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and accurately captures Mateo’s ambivalence and emotional state. Deeper exploration of fear, shame, or the emotional weight of his physical tension would make the empathy more complete.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, strategic focus on the target behavior and moves logically from broad ambivalence to a specific high-risk time. The psychiatrist guides toward change without unnecessary drift or rigidity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the work as joint problem-solving, with Mateo’s choices directly shaping the session and plan. The interaction reflects two partners rather than a clinician-led agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Mateo’s sense of choice and avoids controlling or coercive language. The final plan is initiated and owned by Mateo, with his right to choose size and timing clearly honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to elicit Mateo’s own motivations and practical solutions. Some moments are slightly leading or rely on clinician-generated meaning rather than fully evoking Mateo’s deeper personal reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly conveys that Mateo is the expert on his own life and choices. The session is shaped by Mateo selecting the focus, identifying risks, and proposing the plan, demonstrating strong power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and reinforces Mateo’s change talk around safety, calling his sister, and setting up a concrete plan. The score is held back by occasional use of clinician values language and limited explicit focus on confidence or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without reinforcing it and reframes the conversation toward autonomy and practicality. Deeper processing of Mateo’s fear of failure or ambivalence about whether the plan will work would make this stronger.
