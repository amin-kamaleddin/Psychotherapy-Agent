# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgment, and validation throughout the session. However, the warmth remains somewhat procedural and occasionally aligns with the client’s pessimism without fully affirming their worth and agency.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing exhaustion, frustration, and feeling knocked back. The clinician does not fully reflect deeper shame or identity-level meaning, such as the self-criticism embedded in the client’s comment about 211.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session keeps a general focus on financial stress, work, and feeling stuck, with some concrete redirection attempts. Momentum becomes inconsistent after the client disengages, shifting toward quiet support rather than strategic movement toward a change target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and lets the client’s choices shape the session, including breathing, quiet, and a check-in. Collaboration is not a 5 because the clinician still holds most agenda-setting power through therapist-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly told that choices are theirs, and refusals are respected without pressure. Autonomy support is slightly limited by the repeated introduction of structured, clinician-offered interventions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early evocative questions are present, but the clinician shifts toward external options rather than exploring values, strengths, or internal reasons for change. The client’s emerging motivation remains brief and underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A non-expert stance is established, and the client clearly influences the session through choices such as breathing, quiet, and a delayed check-in. The partnership is not a 5 because the clinician remains the primary source of structure and intervention ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few early attempts to evoke change language, but the psychiatrist misses repeated client statements such as wanting to get back on their feet and not feel stuck. These opportunities are not reflected, amplified, or connected to stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with the client’s helplessness, but rarely uses double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or externalization to balance it. Some responses, such as agreeing that breathing will not fix the bank balance, risk reinforcing the belief that nothing changes.
