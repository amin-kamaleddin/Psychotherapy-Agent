# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids blaming Lucía for smoking. Acceptance is slightly less individualized and can feel procedural rather than deeply affirming of her whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Lucía’s experience, especially the meaning of the smoke break as permission to pause. At times the counselor summarizes content rather than fully exploring the deeper emotions beneath Lucía’s words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly connected to smoking but often drifts toward stress, breaks, and coping rather than smoking cessation. Clearer strategic focus would connect each reflection to the change goal of quitting or reducing smoking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Lucía’s input and uses collaborative language, but also frequently controls structure through options, lists, and psychoeducation. This limits the sense of two equal partners co-constructing the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct pressure to quit and respects Lucía’s refusal to try alternatives. Some moments are still directive, especially when coping strategies or options are offered before fully establishing Lucía’s ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor uses some open questions, but the session is more explanatory than evocative. Lucía’s own values, concerns, and motivations for change are not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and attention to Lucía’s perspective. However, the psychiatrist often sets the agenda and provides structured options, which can reduce the sense of shared direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not consistently elicit Lucía’s own language in favor of change. There are missed opportunities to deepen change talk around importance, confidence, values, and future outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and rolls with resistance, but remains heavily focused on the benefits of smoking. Few double-sided reflections are used to honor the appeal of smoking while opening space for change.
