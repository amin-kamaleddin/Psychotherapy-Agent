### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful and nonjudgmental, honoring Ahmed’s time and minimizing his cigar use without arguing. It could be stronger with more individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but mostly surface-level and factual. The clinician misses deeper feelings such as frustration, concern about dismissal, or the personal meaning of bleeding gums.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays organized and focused on the dental exam. However, it lacks clear MI direction toward cigar use and oral-health change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician collaborates through choices, permission-seeking, and responsiveness to Ahmed’s preferences. It would improve by building more directly on Ahmed’s own agenda rather than clinician-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently asks permission, respects refusals, and creates a low-pressure environment. It could be strengthened by explicitly affirming Ahmed’s ownership of any decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly factual or logistical, with little effort to draw out Ahmed’s motivations, values, or confidence in changing. Strong cues such as gum bleeding and willingness to floss were not expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ahmed is invited to choose the focus and his preferences are respected, showing meaningful partnership. The clinician still retains considerable structural control through repeated options and check-ins.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only minimal invitations toward change, and very little client language supporting change is evoked or amplified. The small readiness cue about flossing is missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk

**Comments:** The clinician avoids confrontation and does not reinforce sustain talk. However, the responses mostly avoid the issue rather than using double-sided reflections or gentle reframing.
