# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a polite, non-judgmental tone but frequently shifts into directive problem-solving, which can subtly reduce unconditional positive regard. The eagerness to 'fix' hesitancy with plans can make the interaction feel slightly controlling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is present but somewhat superficial, relying more on factual validation than emotional depth. The psychiatrist often acknowledges the behavior while missing the underlying emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains strongly focused on concrete, actionable goals related to medication and walking. The psychiatrist effectively prevents the conversation from drifting and maintains clear momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative effort, but the psychiatrist assumes the primary role of idea-generator and problem-solver. The patient largely selects from clinician-provided options rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient's freedom to choose but limits autonomy through highly prescriptive schedules and scripts. The patient is guided toward following a pre-designed path rather than discovering their own approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions are used, but the psychiatrist quickly shifts from exploring motivation to providing solutions. Deeper evocation of intrinsic motivation and values is inconsistent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts a collaborative stance by inviting input, but the power dynamic becomes uneven. The patient often acts as an approver rather than a true partner in planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms initial motivators but misses opportunities to amplify later spontaneous change talk. Instead, the conversation quickly pivots to structured planning, limiting further development of change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist struggles to soften sustain talk and responds to medication concerns with medical education and reassurance. More MI-consistent techniques, such as double-sided reflections, are not used effectively to redirect toward change talk.
