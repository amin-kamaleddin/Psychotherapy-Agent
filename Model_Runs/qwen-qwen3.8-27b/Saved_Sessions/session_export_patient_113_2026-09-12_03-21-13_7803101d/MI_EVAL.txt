# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful toward Rowan, but somewhat formulaic with repeated phrases like "that makes sense." Lacks individualized positive regard that would affirm Rowan's agency, values, and personal strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures Rowan's feelings of pressure, hassle, fatigue, and resentment. Does not deepen to explore the emotional meaning behind the resistance, such as autonomy threat or ambivalence about needing help.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal and inconsistent focus on the device; quickly drifts into process-level questions about silence versus reflection. No clear strategic movement toward values, consequences, or a minimal first step around the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers collaborative options and uses "together," but repeated menus of choices feel burdensome to a client who says he doesn't want more work. Lacks true co-constructed problem-solving when Rowan defers back to the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, consistent affirmation of Rowan's right to choose with no pressure or coercive language. Mostly process-focused around how to talk or sit, rather than reinforcing autonomy around the actual device decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reasonable open questions but does not consistently pursue or expand on Rowan's minimal, ambivalent responses. No use of rulers, no exploration of values, and brief openings like "I guess" are not drawn upon further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and offers choices but becomes mechanical with repeated menus that may feel like additional work. Missed opportunity to reestablish genuine partnership when Rowan said "you're supposed to know what to do, not me."

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only rare, weak efforts with a single question about what would need to change. Rowan's ambiguous openings ("I guess," "I'll talk if I feel like it") are not reflected as small signs of openness or used to evoke further change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing but provides mostly one-sided validation of the sustain talk without balancing it with curiosity about the other side. No double-sided reflections or strategic pivots toward the ambivalent/change side.
