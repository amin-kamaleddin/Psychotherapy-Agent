# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates unconditional positive regard by normalizing Oliver’s ambivalence, guilt, and conflict-avoidant style without judgment or labeling. Oliver’s character and past generosity are affirmed in a respectful, non-pressuring way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Oliver’s ambivalence, practical concerns, and emotional tone, showing clear perspective-taking. However, the final strong anxiety and fear of damaging the friendship are not fully reflected or validated before moving toward next steps.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior of asserting a boundary with the flatmate. Each reflection and question builds momentum toward a concrete, actionable plan without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Oliver as a partner rather than directing the session, inviting Oliver to co-create the timing, message, tone, and follow-up plan. The client’s contributions substantially shape the intervention and final strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently leaves decision-making with Oliver and avoids pressure, obligation, or prescriptive language. Oliver’s plan is framed as his own choice, reinforcing agency and self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Oliver’s own motivations, preferences, and solutions through open questions and reflections. Some of the evocation remains therapist-directed through repeated preferred phrases, which slightly limits fully client-led change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Oliver as the authority on his own values, tone, and preferred way of communicating rather than taking on an expert role. The emerging plan is largely client-generated, reflecting a strong collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits change talk by helping Oliver identify what is possible, what would help, and what the message would feel like in his own voice. The session moves from ambivalence to a concrete plan, though scaling or confidence-focused questions could deepen the evocation further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist gently refrains from arguing against Oliver’s sustain talk and instead softens the perceived threat of setting a boundary. Some emotional costs and relational fears could be processed more fully before moving toward problem-solving.
