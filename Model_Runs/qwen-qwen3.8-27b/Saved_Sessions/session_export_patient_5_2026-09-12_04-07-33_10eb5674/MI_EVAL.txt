# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Alessia’s concerns throughout. The warmth can feel somewhat professional or formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflections of ambivalence, but empathic responding is often surface-level. The psychiatrist frequently shifts to information rather than exploring deeper fear, uncertainty, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains relevant to medication concerns, but the focus is sometimes diffused. Introducing many options and scripts reduces clear direction toward a single change target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers to tailor questions, showing some collaboration. However, much of the session remains clinician-led, with information and advice offered before fully exploring the client’s preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses invitational language, avoids pressure, and explicitly preserves Alessia’s choice. Some safety guidance is delivered in a prescriptive way, but autonomy is generally well supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with open-ended questions and some evocation of priorities. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently draw out values, confidence, or internal reasons for change, often responding with information instead.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and shares some control, but the interaction remains largely expert-driven. Detailed medication guidance and scripts place the psychiatrist in a more authoritative role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Alessia generates some change talk around safety, planning, and therapy, but the psychiatrist does not consistently elicit, reflect, or amplify it. The evocative questions are limited and not followed by deeper exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and validates Alessia’s concerns, preventing confrontation. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections, strategic summaries, or pivots to soften sustain talk.
