# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental and respectful stance throughout. Warmth is solid but somewhat formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections capture the patient’s ambivalence. However, the empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore the emotional and social meaning behind the drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Wednesday drinking and moves toward a concrete harm-reduction plan. Direction is clear, though it shifts to implementation before fully consolidating the patient’s priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient’s ideas are used to shape the plan, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies a ready-made menu of strategies. A more balanced collaboration would have waited longer for the patient to generate options first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options and asking permission. Autonomy is sometimes undercut by the directive tone of the tactical suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions help the patient articulate a plan and raise confidence. However, the evocation of deeper reasons, values, and motivation for change is not consistently strong.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient’s perspective is invited and incorporated into the plan. The psychiatrist’s frequent advice-giving creates an uneven counselor–patient balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expresses useful change talk, such as not feeling groggy and planning to eat first. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or expand this change talk before moving into tactics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met without arguing or reinforcing it. The psychiatrist could have used more double-sided reflections and strategic pivots to gently move sustain talk toward change.
