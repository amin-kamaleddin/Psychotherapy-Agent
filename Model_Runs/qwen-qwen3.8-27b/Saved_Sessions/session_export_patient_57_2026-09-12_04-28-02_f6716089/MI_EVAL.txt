# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with several affirmations and avoids arguing about Jamal’s alcohol use. Acceptance is sometimes formulaic and misses a chance to explicitly personalize the positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician provides accurate two-sided reflections of Jamal’s ambivalence about peer pressure, team value, and the need to avoid looking like the buzzkill. Deeper emotional reflections on embarrassment, shame, or fear of social threat would have strengthened the empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing Jamal’s alcohol-related behavior and developing a practical low-drama exit strategy. There is little topic drift and the focus narrows effectively toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal’s ideas are incorporated and some rejected options are respected, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies lists of possible solutions. A more balanced collaborative stance would have elicited and explored Jamal’s own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers many choice-focused questions and respects Jamal’s judgments about what feels forced or unrealistic. Some prompts are mildly pressuring, such as asking which tiny step he will actually try before the next meet-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and follow-up prompts help Jamal articulate conditions under which he would be more willing to change. The evocation is weakened by repeated movement into problem-solving and missed opportunities to explore values, identity, or deeper meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jamal’s input and allows some of his ideas to shape the plan, but often shifts into an expert problem-solving role. More consistently client-led exploration would have strengthened the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are effective attempts to elicit and affirm change talk, especially around the confidence ruler and Jamal’s willingness to consider a jacket cue. The clinician sometimes narrows Jamal’s motivation into behavioral options rather than expanding his own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Jamal’s sustain talk well without challenging or minimizing reasons to maintain the status quo. More active use of the sustain talk to explore costs of staying with the pattern would have made this more advanced.
