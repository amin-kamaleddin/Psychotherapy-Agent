# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, effectively normalizing setbacks. Slightly limited by praise that was too closely tied to performance rather than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provided accurate but somewhat shallow reflections of surface emotions like pride and frustration. Missed opportunities to use complex reflections to explore deeper emotional ambivalence regarding the client's attachment to the juice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent and efficient focus on the target behavior of juice reduction. Skillfully guided the session from general weight regain to a highly specific and concrete action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acted as a collaborative partner and built well on the client's own ideas and contributions. Was not perfect because the psychiatrist often took the lead by supplying exact phrasing and tips rather than waiting for the client to generate more solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively supported the client's sense of choice using strong scaling and open-ended questions. Autonomy support was slightly weakened by the provision of specific scripts and fallback options that subtly steered the client's choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used effective scaling and action-focused prompts to evoke confidence and planning. Evocation was limited by the tendency to quickly provide explanations and strategies instead of asking deeper questions to maximize the client's internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance and allowed the client to shape the plan. Partnership was uneven because the psychiatrist frequently stepped into an expert/problem-solving role by offering specific strategies and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully prompted strong, action-oriented change talk and commitment from the client. Could have more intentionally amplified the client's reasons for change by asking deeper questions about their values, health, and future impact.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged and validated the sustain talk without challenging or dismissing the client's attachment to the juice. Missed the chance to use a double-sided reflection to further soften and explore the underlying ambivalence.
