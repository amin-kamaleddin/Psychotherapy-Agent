# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while normalizing Erik’s concerns about shift work and lifestyle change. Some affirmations feel formulaic and task-focused, limiting deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening to capture Erik’s practical concerns and ambivalence. Empathy remains mostly cognitive and behavioral, missing deeper exploration of emotional meaning such as defensiveness, fear, or loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol reduction within diabetes management and a practical one-week plan. The conversation occasionally shifts toward detailed problem-solving rather than sustained motivational movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik contributes preferences and modifies parts of the plan, showing meaningful collaboration. However, the psychiatrist leads with multiple proposed solutions, leaving Erik largely in the role of selecting or adjusting clinician-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice through noncoercive language, multiple options, and normalization of slips. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly emphasizing Erik as the decision-maker for both the goal and the method.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical information about priorities, confidence, barriers, and fallbacks. Evocation remains largely task-oriented and does not deeply explore Erik’s intrinsic values, hopes, fears, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative tone and invites input at several points. The session remains heavily guided by the psychiatrist’s agenda and solution set rather than being fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some questions that allow Erik to express willingness and create a small plan. There is limited effort to amplify, reflect back, or deepen Erik’s own change-related language and motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Erik’s sustain talk and generally reflects and normalizes it. However, there is inconsistent use of strategic redirection or double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence and move toward change.
