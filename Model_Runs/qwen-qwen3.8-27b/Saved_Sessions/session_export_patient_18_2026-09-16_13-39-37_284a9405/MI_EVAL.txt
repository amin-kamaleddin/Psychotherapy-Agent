# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and reduced the patient's fear of being labeled or reported. Warmth was consistent but could be more individualized by affirming the patient's values or worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the patient's concerns about honesty, reputation, and autonomy. The reflections were clear but could explore the emotional undercurrent of her worries more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use and progressed logically from the referral to self-monitoring. Direction would be stronger with a clearer framing and final consolidation of the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked as a partner, allowing the patient's concerns to guide the conversation and inviting her perspective. More explicit language about figuring things out together would further strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was a major strength, with repeated emphasis on choice, no pressure, and no advice-giving. The psychiatrist supported the patient's emerging plan while respecting her right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions helped the patient generate her own definition of the line and a practical self-check plan. Evocation could go deeper by exploring values, confidence, or future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invited the patient's perspective and positioned her as the expert on her own experience. The power-sharing could be made more explicit by naming her expertise and choice in the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient produced useful change talk, including markers for concern and a concrete plan to preserve choice. The psychiatrist could more actively amplify and summarize this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responded to sustain talk without arguing, moralizing, or reinforcing a 'drinker' label. A double-sided reflection could further validate and soften the patient's resistance.
