# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses affirming language, though acceptance can feel somewhat scripted. It would be stronger if the counselor more explicitly normalized the client’s anxiety about release and separation from family.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately tracks the client’s feelings and concerns. However, reflections sometimes summarize the situation rather than fully exploring deeper emotional vulnerability around release.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on managing the phone suspension in a way that protects release and restores family contact. It would be stronger with clearer summary transitions and an explicit collaborative statement of the target behavior and intended outcome.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative, and the client’s ideas meaningfully shape the plan. To reach a higher score, the counselor could more visibly share power and explicitly negotiate the agenda rather than lightly steering toward a preferred direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice by inviting the client to generate options and avoids direct advice or coercion. It could be strengthened with more explicit affirmation of the client’s freedom to choose and fewer leading questions that steer toward a particular wording or outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently draws out the client’s own motivations, such as wanting to speak with his father and protect his release. It could be stronger if the counselor more deeply evokes values, identity, confidence, and the intrinsic importance of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client’s perspective and builds on the client’s ideas, especially around the written note and wording. It would be stronger if the counselor more consistently made explicit that the client is the expert on his own experience and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently evokes change-oriented language and helps the client generate safer, more respectful ways to make the request. It could be stronger by more explicitly reflecting, amplifying, and reinforcing the client’s own change talk and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids reinforcing blame toward staff and redirects the conversation toward safer, constructive action. However, the session gives considerable space to the client’s perceptions of unfairness, and the use of double-sided reflections or explicit softening strategies is limited.
