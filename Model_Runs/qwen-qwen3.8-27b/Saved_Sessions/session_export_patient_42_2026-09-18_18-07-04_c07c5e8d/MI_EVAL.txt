# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with warm, non-judgmental language throughout. Occasionally borders on slightly performative in its phrasing, and could be deepened with more individualized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic attunement with complex reflections that consistently capture emotional and identity-level meaning behind the patient's words. Never misses an emotional cue and tracks shifts precisely from social concern to identity fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior while strategically deepening through connected psychological territory (social bonding → ritual → identity → fear of being 'new'). Could benefit from a slightly more explicit connection back to the change goal or actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the conversation as a joint exploration with open questions and appropriate 'we' language. Could be elevated by more explicitly acknowledging the patient's expertise in her own experience and pausing to check in about direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with explicit non-pressuring language, no advice-giving, no pushing toward a quit date, and reinforcement of the patient's own agency. Among the strongest autonomy support observed in a session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled at drawing out the patient's own motivations and insights through well-timed questions that lead to self-discovery. Could be more consistent with scaling questions or deeper evocative techniques to push articulation of readiness further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with language that invites the patient's perspective and follows her lead as she deepens. Could be strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language and ceding of agenda to the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited but not consistently reinforced, amplified, or strategically deepened. Missed opportunities to affirm the patient's own movement toward change or use scaling questions to evoke more change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully with double-sided reflections that validate the concern while gently highlighting the change direction. Could more explicitly acknowledge the legitimate value of social connections before moving to deeper psychological levels.
