# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance. The client’s self-critical labels and ambivalence are accepted without moral judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and tracks both the client’s cognitive and emotional experience. The counselor captures complex feelings such as fear of failure, comparison, and the desire for authentic motivation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing relapse risk and building a safer, more structured identity. The conversation moves logically from ambivalence to specific, actionable low-risk plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively seeks the client’s input and builds the session around the client’s own ideas. The final plan is clearly co-created through shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is strongly emphasized through options, confidence checks, and statements such as “You’re in charge of where we go.” The counselor respects the client’s pace and avoids pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively draws out the client’s own reasons for change using targeted questions and confidence scales. Evocation could be strengthened by deeper exploration of core values and identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is framed as a collaboration from the start, with the client positioned as the expert on his own life. The psychiatrist uses inclusive, guiding language rather than an expert-driven stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits and reinforces the client’s motivations, strengths, and emerging plans. Change talk could be further amplified by more explicitly connecting actions to deeper personal values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk about old friends, being “lazy,” and fear of work is reflected neutrally without argument or dismissal. The counselor gently redirects these concerns toward capability, safety, and agency.
