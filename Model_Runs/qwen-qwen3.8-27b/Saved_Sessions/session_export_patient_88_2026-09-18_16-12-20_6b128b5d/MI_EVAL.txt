# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental stance with phrases like 'I'm not going to put you in a box' and 'I believe you,' particularly effective given Marcus's defensiveness. Could be elevated to a 5 with more individualized warmth, such as explicitly acknowledging Marcus's courage or self-awareness moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate empathic reflections that capture both content and emotion, including a complex double-sided reflection that perfectly holds Marcus's ambivalence. The reflection 'the drinks have become woven into the fabric of those hangouts' accurately mirrors his lived experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use at parties with a logical progression from safety to values to problem-solving, with no wandering. Could be strengthened by periodically summarizing emerging themes and collaboratively identifying a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is fully collaborative from start to finish, with Marcus's contributions directly shaping the conversation's trajectory. The power dynamic is genuinely balanced, with no moments of prescribing or taking over.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent autonomy support throughout, including 'that's completely your call' and validating Marcus's right to continue drinking. The framing of 'choosing' versus 'going along' reinforces that the decision is entirely his.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited entirely from within Marcus through targeted questions that help him discover his own values, never through external reasoning. The progression from dismissiveness to 'it sounds easier than I thought' is driven almost entirely by Marcus's own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional collaborative stance from the opening ('from yours, not from your doctor's perspective') through consistent returns to Marcus's expertise in his own life. The psychiatrist never assumes the expert role and positions himself as a curious collaborator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk through questions that help Marcus identify his own values, with effective amplification of his insights. Could be strengthened with explicit confidence/ability scaling questions (e.g., 'on a scale of 1-10, how confident are you that you could say nah?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through validation and redirection rather than argument, with a strong double-sided reflection holding both sides of Marcus's ambivalence. The sustain talk around 'if nobody was drinking it'd feel weird' could have been more actively softened by exploring what 'weird' would actually feel like.
