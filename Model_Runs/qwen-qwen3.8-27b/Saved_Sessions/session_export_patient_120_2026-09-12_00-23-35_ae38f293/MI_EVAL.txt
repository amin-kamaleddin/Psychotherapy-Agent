# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the client's fear. Acceptance is limited by frequent shifts into medical advice and problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several reflective statements about the client's ambivalence and sense of loss. However, empathy is inconsistent, and the client's identity concerns are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and weekend social risks. The direction is strong but remains more behavioral and advice-oriented than client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts more like an expert provider than a collaborative partner. The client's own values and insights are not strongly integrated into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some choices are offered, and the client's one-sober-night plan is accepted. Autonomy is undermined by repeated scripts and prescriptive behavior plans despite the client's discomfort.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides many reasons for change and practical strategies. There is limited effort to elicit or amplify the client's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but the session remains largely clinician-directed. The counselor's preferred strategies often take priority over the client's expressed preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the client's move toward trying one sober night. More evocative follow-up would strengthen the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's fear of losing friends is acknowledged, but sustain talk is often met with medical facts and limit-setting advice. This may inadvertently reinforce the idea that one drink might be acceptable.
