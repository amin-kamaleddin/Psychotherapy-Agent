# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and repeatedly normalizes Rafael’s struggle. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth is somewhat procedural and occasionally shifts into advice-giving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist understands practical barriers such as time and exhaustion, but emotional empathy is limited. Important feelings like fear of failure and shame are not fully reflected before moving into planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on returning to physical activity and narrows the goal into a concrete, manageable 7-day plan. Direction is a clear strength, though it could be made even more client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts the plan when Rafael says it is too much. Collaboration becomes especially strong after Rafael expresses his limits, but early suggestions were more advisor-like.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers multiple choices, respects Rafael’s limits, and uses low-pressure language such as “no shame” and “treat it as information.” Early directive statements slightly reduce the sense that the plan is fully Rafael’s own from the start.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest criterion. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Rafael’s own motivations, values, confidence, or reasons for change, and does not fully expand his change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Rafael’s preferences and adapts the plan accordingly, showing some collaboration. However, the initial expert-driven approach and detailed suggestions weaken the partnership stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some choice-based questions and affirms Rafael’s change-oriented statements. However, there is limited effort to amplify change talk, explore past successes, or build Rafael’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Rafael’s sustain talk and normalizes his concerns. The “1-minute rule” is especially effective, though double-sided reflections could have been used more to help resolve ambivalence.
