# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard with culturally attuned de-stigmatization from the opening. Never judges the violence, drinking, or ambivalence; models self-awareness and respect throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook double-sided reflections that validate ambivalence without collapsing it. Deeply attuned to the emotional weight of moments like 'the flinch,' with progressive deepening across the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong directional arc from ambivalence to resolution to commitment, with well-timed introduction of housing and the 'year from now' question. Could benefit from a brief collaborative summary tying all threads (alcohol, violence program, housing) together before closing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely joint exploration where the client's ideas shape every turn of the conversation. Inclusive language, responsive reflections, and modeling of humility signal a relationship between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful use of restraint — validates the client's chosen timeline ('that's not hesitation, that's pacing') and explicitly affirms his ownership of decisions. Refuses to impose external validation, trusting the client's self-knowledge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Progressive evocation from eliciting ambivalence to deepening change talk to eliciting spontaneous commitment language. The client's most powerful change motivation ('the flinch') emerges entirely from the evocative process.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently enacts 'working alongside' framing — never takes over, never provides unsolicited advice. Reflects back the client's own insights rather than imposing external judgment, including the closing refusal to confirm where the client 'is.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-designed open questions elicit substantial organic change talk and the psychiatrist strategically amplifies it. Could push further at peak moments by exploring confidence or ability dimensions more systematically (e.g., scaling questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk fully before using the client's own words to reframe (the 'already being watched' insight). Could be slightly more active in redirecting specific sustain talk moments, such as the Tristan comparison.
