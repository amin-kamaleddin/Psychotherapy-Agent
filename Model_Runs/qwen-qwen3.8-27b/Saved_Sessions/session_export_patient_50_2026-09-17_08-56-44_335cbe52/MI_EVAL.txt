# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, validating language and avoids judgment while acknowledging Hana’s struggle. Acceptance is slightly reduced because some warmth is tied to the desired agenda or infers feelings rather than reflecting Hana’s own words.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing Hana’s ambivalence, exhaustion, and the protective function of her anxiety. Empathy is limited by occasional inferential or leading reflections and limited exploration of deeper shame and embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the certification exam and test anxiety, with minimal drift. Direction is strong as the conversation progresses from general exam fear to a concrete, client-generated 10-minute practice plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Hana as an equal partner, with her ideas shaping the session and the interaction feeling mutually owned. Hana’s examples, concerns, and proposed strategies substantially drive the work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana is consistently invited to choose and define her own path, and the psychiatrist avoids coercive language. Autonomy support is not maximal because freedom to choose is not explicitly affirmed repeatedly and some counselor-supplied frames influence what counts as progress.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Hana’s own motivations, past successes, and behavioral ideas rather than supplying reasons for change. Evocation is limited by occasional counselor-supplied labels and the lack of strategic summaries or scaling to consolidate motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely avoids an expert stance and invites Hana to lead with her own experience and ideas. Partnership is strong but not fully client-led because the psychiatrist introduces key conceptual frames such as “next step,” “practice,” and “tiny cue.”

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana’s change talk is frequently evoked and reinforced, including desire, ability, and reason for change. The score is limited by the lack of MI-specific consolidation tools such as DARN/C summaries or confidence/commitment scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and reflects Hana’s sustain talk without arguing, minimizing, or challenging it. Softening is strong but could be more efficient by more consistently pivoting from fear to values, competence, and past success.
