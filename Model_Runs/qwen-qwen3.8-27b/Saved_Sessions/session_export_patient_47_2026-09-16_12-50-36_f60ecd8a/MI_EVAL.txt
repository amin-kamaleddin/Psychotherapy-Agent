# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and offers validating statements throughout the session. However, some language sounds slightly formulaic and focused on the plan rather than deeply affirming the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture Evelyn's ambivalence, social value of drinking, and practical concerns. The reflections are helpful but often remain at a behavioral/cognitive level and do not consistently deepen into underlying feelings or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of reducing alcohol use in the pub setting. The psychiatrist moves logically from assessment to ambivalence, scaling, concrete planning, and follow-up without losing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is largely co-constructed, with Evelyn choosing the one-glass goal, soda strategy, exit time, text to her son, and check-in time. The psychiatrist remains collaborative but occasionally positions herself as the primary source of practical solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Evelyn's choice, avoids pressure, and respects her preference to avoid strict rules. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by therapist-generated strategies and suggestions that are more advisory than client-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses useful MI questions about importance, confidence, barriers, and small steps to draw out Evelyn's own motivations and ideas. However, the session also includes several therapist-supplied strategies, which reduces the overall evocative focus.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a collaborative posture, and Evelyn's preferences clearly shape the plan. The psychiatrist also takes an advisory role by supplying specific coping strategies and alternative text options, which weakens the fully client-led partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that elicit some change talk and invites Evelyn to identify realistic steps. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen the change talk before moving into planning and practical suggestions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing Evelyn's sustain talk and acknowledges her reasons for keeping drinking. MI-specific softening techniques are limited, and the session moves quickly to planning without fully exploring the reasons for maintaining the status quo.
