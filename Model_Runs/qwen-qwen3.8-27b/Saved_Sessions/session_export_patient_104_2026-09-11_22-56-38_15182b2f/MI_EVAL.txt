# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly normalizes Nadine's experience, which reduces shame. The acceptance feels somewhat generic and procedural, and it does not fully capture deeper emotions such as fear, grief, or feeling burdensome.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflections of exhaustion and overwhelm, but the empathy is often factual or normalizing rather than emotionally exploratory. Responses frequently shift toward advice and reassurance instead of fully mirroring Nadine's underlying fear, guilt, or sadness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on exhaustion, infant care, immediate support, and safety, without wandering into unrelated topics. However, the direction is more informational and task-completion oriented than MI-focused, emphasizing practical steps over the client's own change agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration through questions, optional supports, and adjusting to Nadine's request for a short text. The interaction remains uneven, with the counselor frequently leading with plans, scripts, and instructions rather than co-creating priorities with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, such as the option to send a text, and does not pressure Nadine to complete every suggestion. The approach is still fairly directive, with detailed instructions and prescribed behaviors that reduce the sense of Nadine owning the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor mostly provides explanations, practical steps, and ready-made solutions rather than eliciting motivation from Nadine. There is limited exploration of her values, priorities, confidence, or personal reasons for making a change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for some input and adapts to Nadine's need for a shorter message, showing partial responsiveness. The overall stance remains expert-driven, with the counselor positioning themselves as the primary source of solutions rather than as an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nadine offers meaningful change talk, such as interest in a plan and willingness to ask about a bottle, but the counselor mostly responds with advice rather than evoking or strengthening this language. There are few moments that amplify her motivation, confidence, or commitment to a specific small step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps normalize rest and reduce guilt by reframing rest as part of caregiving. However, sustain talk and ambivalence are not consistently redirected toward change, and additional instructions can increase the sense of overwhelm rather than gently resolve conflict.
