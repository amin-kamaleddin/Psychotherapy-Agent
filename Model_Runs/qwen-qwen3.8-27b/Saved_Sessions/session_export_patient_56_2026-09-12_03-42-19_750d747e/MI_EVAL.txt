# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgmental support. Validation is strong even when the client expresses fear, guilt, or exhaustion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and captures the client’s ambivalence well. However, some reflections remain cognitive and plan-focused rather than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on aligning work behavior with the client’s core value of family. The discussion moves clearly from values to a concrete, measurable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor and client co-create the plan, with the client choosing key elements. The collaboration could be stronger if the partnership was framed more explicitly as equal and client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positions the client as the decision-maker and avoids pressure or coercion. The client is invited to choose, define success, and decide readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively draw out the client’s values, barriers, and possible solutions. The evocation could be stronger if it more consistently deepened internal motivation rather than moving toward planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses many client-centered invitations and allows the client’s preferences to shape the session. Offering a list of options early gives the interaction a slightly advisory tone.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some commitment and confidence statements, but does not consistently deepen or amplify broader change talk. More emphasis on desire, need, and reasons for change would strengthen the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor validates the client’s career concerns without arguing or pressuring. It could be more effective if using double-sided reflections to connect the concern back to the client’s values.
