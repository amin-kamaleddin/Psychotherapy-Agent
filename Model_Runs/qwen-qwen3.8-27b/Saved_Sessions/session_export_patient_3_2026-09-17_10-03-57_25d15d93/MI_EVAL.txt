# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while normalizing the patient’s ambivalence and self-monitoring concerns. Acceptance is strong but could deepen by affirming the patient’s worth beyond productivity and validating the emotional weight of body image distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening effectively captures the patient’s mixed feelings about wanting change and fearing failure. Some reflections introduce the psychiatrist’s interpretation before the patient has fully expressed the underlying shame, pain, and fatigue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on resuming movement and improves the conversation from broad motivation to concrete micro-steps. Direction could be stronger by closing with a brief summary that links the small step back to the patient’s broader values and body-image goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient’s ideas heavily shape the plan, including the five-minute step, porch option, breath check, and standing cue. The psychiatrist occasionally introduces organizing phrases that become central, reducing the patient’s full ownership of the plan’s language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the patient’s sense of choice, repeatedly framing actions as choices rather than rules. Pressure is avoided, and even small steps such as standing on the porch are treated as meaningful and valid.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions frequently draw out the patient’s own motivation, bodily cues, and preferred actions rather than providing reasons for change. A few counselor suggestions may prime the patient’s responses, and more purely evoking language would strengthen the patient’s own solution development.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that the patient is the expert on her own patterns and invites joint problem-solving. The guiding role is generally appropriate, but the psychiatrist’s structured formulations sometimes steer the conversation more than a fully partnership-oriented stance would.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates space for the patient to voice desire, preference, and ability, and reflects some of that change language back. Change talk could be deepened across more dimensions, such as importance, confidence, need, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or dismissal, and double-sided reflections help reduce defensiveness. The session could improve by more consistently integrating the two sides of ambivalence and gently resolving lingering worries about fatigue and stopping.
