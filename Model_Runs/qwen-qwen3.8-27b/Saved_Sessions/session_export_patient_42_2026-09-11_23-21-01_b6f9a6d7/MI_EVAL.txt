# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Elise’s ambivalence, fear, and social concerns. However, the warmth is somewhat formulaic and can feel procedural when quickly shifting into options and planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the central ambivalence between social connection and health concerns accurately. The response misses deeper emotional meaning related to identity, fear of exclusion, loyalty, and the role of “being useful.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking in social contexts and moves toward a practical, manageable step. Direction is strong but largely therapist-led rather than co-created from Elise’s own priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration, especially when the psychiatrist incorporates Elise’s preferences and ideas into the plan. However, much of the session remains counselor-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying the structure, options, and coping strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids direct coercion, including respecting Elise’s reluctance to role-play. The repeated detailed plans can subtly shift the experience toward “here is what you should try” rather than client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses useful open and logistical questions that elicit practical information. Evocation remains mostly superficial and does not consistently draw out deeper motivations, values, or meanings around quitting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often assumes the expert role by repeatedly providing options, scripts, and a structured plan. Elise is invited to choose, but the session does not strongly convey that she is the primary expert on her own life and social dynamics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elise offers change talk related to health and quitting, but the psychiatrist does not consistently notice, reflect, amplify, or develop it. There are missed opportunities to evoke confidence, commitment, and deeper motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or dismiss Elise’s sustain talk and attempts to preserve the positive social function of smoking. However, the response lacks advanced MI techniques to strategically soften sustain talk or reduce its centrality in the session.
