# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, using positive regard and validation for the client’s ambivalence and emotional load. Affirmations are strong but can be more individualized and explicitly normalize being stuck in a high-stress situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is effective, especially in capturing the client’s ambivalence about needing relief while fearing more stress. Deeper exploration of underlying shame, self-judgment, and fear of disappointing his wife would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, stress, sleep, and relational tension without wandering into unrelated topics. The target drinking change could be sharpened more consistently, such as clarifying a specific behavior change like delaying or reducing the first evening drink.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client’s own ideas and avoids lecturing or imposing solutions, creating a working exploratory alliance. More explicit co-creation of goals, agenda, or next steps would make the partnership feel less therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly affirms client expertise and avoids pressuring the client to stop drinking or abandon his pace. Some questions subtly steer toward reduction, so more explicit affirmation of the client’s freedom to choose no change would improve autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits the client’s own change-relevant values, such as being present, feeling less foggy, and improving sleep. It would be stronger if evocation more deeply used values, discrepancy, confidence/commitment scaling, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys collaboration and repeatedly invites the client’s perspective, positioning the client as the expert. The counselor still drives much of the question sequence, so explicit negotiation of goals and a shared plan would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly evokes and reinforces useful change talk around less blur, being present, reducing tension, and better sleep. It would be stronger with more systematic amplification of change talk, including ability, reasons, need, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without endorsement, reducing defensiveness around the function of the beer for stress relief. More consistent pivoting from sustain talk to discrepancy and values-based change would make this area more advanced.
