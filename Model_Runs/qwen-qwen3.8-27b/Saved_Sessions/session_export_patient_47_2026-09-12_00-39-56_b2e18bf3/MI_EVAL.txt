# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect and nonjudgment while validating Evelyn’s reasonable concerns. Warmth is professional but somewhat task-focused, so it falls short of fully individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture some surface emotions but are often shallow and quickly followed by information or advice. Deeper emotional themes such as embarrassment, irritation, and identity threat are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on alcohol use, safety, the fall, and next steps. There is some drift into guideline details and medical logistics, but the overall direction remains coherent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and co-constructs some parts of the plan, but often leads with structured advice and information. The dynamic remains more expert-driven than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evelyn’s right to decide is consistently respected, with permission-seeking and choice-offering throughout. The final plan is slightly prescriptive, which slightly reduces the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation is drawn out, especially around the fall and not wanting to worry her son. The psychiatrist relies more on external information and guidelines than on consistently eliciting Evelyn’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear collaborative efforts, including asking permission and inviting choices. However, the psychiatrist repeatedly positions themselves as the information provider and problem-solver, creating an uneven partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evelyn offers some change talk, particularly about trying a small water swap. The psychiatrist acknowledges this but does not systematically deepen, amplify, or consolidate her motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges Evelyn’s perspective without judgment. The softening is often passive, and responses frequently remain anchored in external limits and information rather than strategic MI techniques.
