# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with phrases like 'There's no right or wrong thing to feel about any of it.' Acceptance is strong but could be deepened with more explicit unconditional positive regard during moments of prominent sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing the emotional significance of the lost ritual. Occasionally stays at the cognitive level rather than fully accessing the emotional undercurrent, such as the grief of loss or identity questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from exploration to solution-building and no significant topic drift. Could benefit from slightly more deliberate guidance toward deeper values or longer-term readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is consistently framed as a joint exploration, with the client's responses shaping direction at every turn. The 'relocating' metaphor that emerges is a product of genuine collaboration, exemplifying strong MI partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports the client's autonomy through affirmations of ownership and choice. Autonomy support is not just present—it is the structural backbone of the session, with no pressure or implied obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the client's own motivations and solutions through evocative questions without providing advice. Could be strengthened by more deliberately evoking deeper change talk around values, identity, or contingency planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides with the client, using inclusive language and never prescribing or directing. The plan is entirely the client's—the psychiatrist facilitates but does not drive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk, picking up on the client's initiative language and reinforcing agency. Could be strengthened by more explicitly exploring the client's confidence or ability regarding the chosen solution.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately acknowledges and validates sustain talk but does not actively soften or pivot it toward change. Misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or gentle reframing that could hold the ambivalence more deliberately.
