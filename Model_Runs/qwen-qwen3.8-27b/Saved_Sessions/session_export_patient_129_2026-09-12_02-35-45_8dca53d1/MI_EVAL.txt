# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, repeatedly affirming Tyler’s choice to leave or not disclose. The stance is supportive but can feel slightly scripted or evaluative, so it is strong but not maximal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several accurate reflections of Tyler’s irritation, defensiveness, and desire for autonomy. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not consistently explore deeper feelings such as shame, fear, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a reasonable focus on the presenting issue but shifts heavily toward managing the mother conflict. Direction is maintained occasionally, but there is no consistent movement toward a specific behavior-change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, which reduces authoritarianism. However, the agenda and structure remain largely clinician-driven, with limited true co-creation of goals or solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** This is a strong area, with repeated emphasis on Tyler’s right to choose, disclose, and leave. The support is less focused on ownership of the substance-use decision itself, and advice offerings can subtly weaken the autonomy message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions about the reason for visit, preferences, and possible downsides. The evocation is present but shallow, with limited follow-up on values, goals, self-efficacy, or emerging change-relevant statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session conveys some partnership by inviting Tyler’s preferences and avoiding an authoritarian stance. However, the counselor frequently offers structured options and advice, making the interaction only partially collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is one clear attempt to elicit change-relevant language about likes and downsides. The psychiatrist does not effectively build on Tyler’s statements into reasons, abilities, desires, or needs for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk dominates the session, and the psychiatrist mostly validates it without redirecting it. There are few MI-consistent efforts to soften, amplify, or reframe the client’s reasons for maintaining the status quo.
