# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without moralizing or challenging the client’s minimization. It is strong but could be more deeply individualized by adding clearer unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of the client’s stress, confusion, and soccer-related concerns. Empathy could be stronger by exploring the deeper emotional meaning behind fear of consequences and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session largely stays focused on alcohol-related risk as it connects to the client’s soccer and scholarship goals. A more explicit closing summary would strengthen the final direction back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s preferences shape the plan, including focusing on soccer and keeping the approach low-key. The session could become more fully collaborative by using fewer preset options and more open invitations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes that the client is in charge and can choose whether to use plans or check-ins. The ending could better honor the client’s signal that he wants to stop without adding one more task.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client’s own reasons for change related to college soccer, health, and performance. Deeper questions about values, identity, and confidence would further amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s perspective and treats the client as an expert about his goals and preferences. The repeated use of multiple-choice options makes the session feel somewhat therapist-driven rather than fully co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits and reinforces meaningful client change talk about protecting his soccer future and avoiding alcohol. It could be stronger by using more evocative follow-up questions to deepen desire, ability, reasons, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s minimization and redirects the conversation toward values and goals. More skillful double-sided reflections or gentle pivots could better address the persistent “not a big deal” stance.
