# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental and validating stance toward Nabil’s frustration and time loss. The stance is slightly weakened by formulaic responses and drifting into advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects surface-level frustration and practical impact fairly accurately. Deeper emotions such as shame, fear, powerlessness, and feeling scripted are not consistently addressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on appointment delays and protecting work time. The approach becomes repetitive and narrow, with limited forward movement toward broader goals or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some use of preferences and client wording, which supports collaboration. The interaction remains mostly driven by the psychiatrist’s scripted options rather than Nabil’s own priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and does not force compliance. However, repeated scripts and practical tasks can reduce Nabil’s sense of ownership and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few initial evocative questions, but the psychiatrist mostly supplies scripts and reasons for change. Nabil’s own motivations, values, strengths, and confidence are not effectively drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, such as asking which option feels truer. The session still feels expert-driven, and Nabil experiences the scripts as being handed to him rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to elicit Nabil’s own reasons for change. When change talk appears, it is not skillfully amplified or connected to his values and goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Nabil’s sustain talk but often agrees with it and then adds more scripts or tasks. There is little effective use of reflection or redirection to open space for change.
