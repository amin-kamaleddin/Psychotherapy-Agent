# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with supportive phrases and a generally warm frame. Acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and procedural rather than deeply individualized, with limited explicit normalization of Richard’s ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening and captures the surface content of Richard’s concerns. Empathy remains mostly cognitive and does not fully address the emotional weight of his ambivalence or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cholesterol management and moves clearly from concerns to a concrete six-week trial plan. There is minimal drift and a logical, purposeful trajectory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is present because Richard selects options and contributes to the final plan. The psychiatrist generates most of the content, making the interaction more expert-led than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, avoids direct pressure, and affirms Richard’s decisions. Autonomy support is weakened by repeatedly adding structure and tracking options after Richard has declined them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions elicit Richard’s worries and motivation, but the psychiatrist often provides medical information and solutions instead of drawing out deeper values and internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and asks for preferences, supporting a collaborative stance. The partnership is limited because the psychiatrist frequently assumes the expert role and leads the content of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard produces meaningful change talk tied to hiking with his wife and avoiding feeling sick. The psychiatrist does not consistently highlight, amplify, or build on this change talk, tending instead toward planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce Richard’s sustain talk, such as minimizing the problem or resisting extra structure. However, it is not actively softened with double-sided reflections or gentle MI-consistent engagement with his ambivalence.
