# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, avoiding blame for reduced activity. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and repetitive, with missed opportunities to explicitly affirm Farid’s honesty and self-compassion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate and complex reflections of Farid’s ambivalence, especially around wanting proof versus avoiding a test. Empathy often remains cognitive rather than fully reflecting the emotional weight of shame, fear, and exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of a small post-work walk and related fatigue. Direction becomes circular around whether the step is real or enough, without a clear summary or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds directly on Farid’s ideas, including the walk, shoes, two-minute version, and number as a memory aid. Rapid questions and repeated formulations can make the session feel somewhat therapist-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Farid’s autonomy by avoiding pressure, large commitments, and direct advice. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit emphasis on Farid owning both the choice and the outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some realistic behavioral steps, such as a short walk or two-minute reset. Evocation is limited by a focus on logistics rather than Farid’s deeper values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Farid as the expert on his body, schedule, and habits. Partnership is slightly weakened by repeated formulations and suggestions that can feel directed rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk, including walking after dinner and a short reset. There is limited use of DARN-C, and the session does not consistently deepen or amplify Farid’s motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Farid’s sustain talk rather than arguing against it, using reframes like no finish line and memory aid. The session remains too locked in concerns about whether the step is real or enough, without a clearer pivot toward change.
