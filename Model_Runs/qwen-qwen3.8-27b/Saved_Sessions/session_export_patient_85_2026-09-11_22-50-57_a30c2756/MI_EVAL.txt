# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and validation, including praise for Marco's effort and kindness toward possible slips. It fell short of full acceptance because the approach was somewhat formulaic and driven by practical checklists.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed adequate reflective listening and acknowledged Marco's frustration and fear. Deeper empathy was limited because reflections often stayed surface-level and shifted quickly into problem-solving rather than exploring the meaning of the juice, ambivalence, or prior weight cycling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing orange juice intake with clear goals, substitution strategies, environmental changes, and follow-up. The direction was occasionally overly directive because it relied on clinician-provided plans and repeated advice rather than purely client-led evocation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporated Marco's input by involving him in choosing actions, addressing his wife, removing cartons, and scheduling follow-up. Collaboration was not fully developed because the psychiatrist frequently offered expert-driven scripts, checklists, and behavioral plans before fully eliciting Marco's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and respected Marco's decisions to reject a script, choose a call, and set a check-in time. Autonomy support was weakened by frequent directive suggestions and detailed plans that positioned the psychiatrist as the primary problem-solver.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions were used, such as asking what Marco wanted from the session, what felt doable, and how confident he was. The session would benefit from more consistent elicitation of Marco's values, internal motivations, and self-generated reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involved Marco in shaping parts of the plan and avoided imposing decisions without his input. Partnership was uneven because the clinician often led with structured advice, scripts, and craving strategies rather than allowing Marco's ideas to drive the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco produced strong change talk, and the psychiatrist used confidence ratings and some prompts that supported motivation. The psychiatrist did not consistently deepen or expand this change talk through more exploratory questions about meaning, values, or self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with Marco and briefly acknowledged ambivalence about giving up something he enjoys. Sustain talk was not effectively softened because responses to concerns about taste, water, cravings, and failure shifted mainly into practical substitutions rather than reflections, pivots, or dual-focus responses.
