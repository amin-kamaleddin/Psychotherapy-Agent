# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Margaret's proposed rule and ambivalence. The acceptance is strong but could include deeper, more individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Margaret's conflict between safety and the social meaning of the pub. The empathic work could more fully explore deeper feelings such as fear, grief, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing risky alcohol use while preserving Margaret's social role. The conversation progresses logically from concerns to a specific, client-owned behavioral plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan with Margaret rather than imposing one, letting her preferences guide the cue, reminder, and self-monitoring. This reflects a genuinely collaborative, equal-partner approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently reinforced through language emphasizing Margaret's choice, control, and private note. There is no pressure, moralizing, or implied obligation to meet a specific drinking standard.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Margaret's own solutions, reminders, and self-monitoring language through open questions. Some questions are slightly suggestive, but they still effectively evoke client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Margaret as the expert and organizes the session around her priorities, values, and judgments. This is a strong and consistent expression of partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits and reinforces change language, including practical steps and commitment to try the plan. Using importance/confidence scaling or values-based summarization would strengthen this further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by normalizing Margaret's stance and avoiding pressure or moral correction. More strategic double-sided reflections or reframing of slips would push this criterion to a 5.
