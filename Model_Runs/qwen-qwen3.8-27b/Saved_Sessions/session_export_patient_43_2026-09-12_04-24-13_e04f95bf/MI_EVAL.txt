# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepted Eric’s right to choose his own behavior. The warmth was solid but sometimes formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used good reflective listening to accurately capture Eric’s ambivalence, practical concerns, and outside pressure. Empathy was strong but mostly content-level, with fewer nuanced reflections of underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed reasonably focused on smoking-related behavior and a concrete target of not smoking inside the house. There was some drift into logistics and communication with Eric’s wife, though it remained broadly relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was collaborative and used inclusive language, with Eric’s preferred target shaping the session. However, the psychiatrist supplied much of the practical planning, making it feel somewhat clinician-guided rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supported choice, avoided coercion, and respected Eric’s boundaries. Autonomy support was strong but occasionally limited by offering multiple concrete suggestions before Eric explicitly requested them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evocative questions and the confidence scale to elicit a small commitment. Evocation was limited by a shift toward practical suggestions before fully exploring Eric’s deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaved as a partner, asked permission, and invited Eric’s preferences. A perfect score would have involved more consistent power-sharing and less clinician-generated planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change talk around a manageable step, such as not smoking in the house. Change talk was not consistently amplified or connected to Eric’s values, benefits, and hopes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted Eric’s sustain talk without argument or confrontation. The sustain talk was handled safely but not consistently softened with double-sided reflections or gentle reframing.
