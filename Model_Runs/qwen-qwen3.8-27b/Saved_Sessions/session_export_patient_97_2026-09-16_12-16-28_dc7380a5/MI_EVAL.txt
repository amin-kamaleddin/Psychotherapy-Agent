# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming or labeling. Some acceptance is tied to plan completion and performance, making it less fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are present and sometimes accurate, but often remain surface-level. Important emotional moments such as fear, self-doubt, and feeling like he is checking a box are underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a strong, consistent focus on reducing reoffending risk by replacing the Tuesday bar with a walk. The plan develops logically with little irrelevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaboration on the reminder text and some plan elements, but the psychiatrist often provides structure, scripts, and summaries. Mateo sometimes accepts suggestions rather than generating his own options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and flexible alternatives, supporting Mateo's sense of control. There is some pressure to complete steps immediately, which slightly limits autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for small changes, support, and confidence ratings, but frequently supplies the change rationale and plan content. More open-ended evocation would better elicit Mateo's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves Mateo in choices and co-creates the reminder text. However, the counselor often acts as planner, scriptwriter, and summarizer, reducing the sense that Mateo is the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked through questions about small steps, support, and confidence. The approach is limited by closed or multiple-choice questions and by the psychiatrist adding the client's reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or confrontation, but it is often redirected quickly into action planning. More exploratory double-sided reflections would better soften ambivalence.
