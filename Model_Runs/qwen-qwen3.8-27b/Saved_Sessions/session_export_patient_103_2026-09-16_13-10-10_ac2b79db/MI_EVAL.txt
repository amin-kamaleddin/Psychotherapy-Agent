# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and validating throughout, never labeling or challenging Nabil's perspective. Somewhat formulaic and repetitive; does not individually affirm his broader strengths or identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capturing his need for information, respect, and control of work time. Lacks deeper exploration of emotional undercurrents (humiliation, fear of looking unprofessional) and pivots too quickly to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on the assertive-statement and escalation plan with practical narrowing of options. Somewhat repetitive and task-focused; does not consistently summarize progress or link back to his broader goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Final plan is genuinely shaped by Nabil's choices (line wording, phone number, no index card). Not fully equal—counselor still supplies most structural options and drives session format.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers choices and asks for preferences regularly; respects his rejections of breathing, cards, and complaint scripts. Still subtly pushes structured interventions (scripts, rehearsal, checklists), creating mild pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence ruler elicits some ability and readiness talk (score moves 0→6). Mostly counselor-guided problem-solving rather than evoking his deeper values, desires, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites his input and adapts to his stated preferences for the plan. Frequently assumes the expert/problem-solver role with menus of options, making the overall direction largely counselor-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brings out ability/confidence language via repeated ruler questions. Does not elicit broader desire, need, or values-based change talk (what protecting his time means to him, what he wants to preserve).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk ("they need to change how they work") without judgment or argument. Responds to it with practical alternatives rather than using double-sided reflections to gently pivot toward his own agency.
