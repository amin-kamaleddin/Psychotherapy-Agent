# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, but the tone often feels brisk and problem-solving-oriented rather than deeply warm or relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clarifying questions show effort to understand the situation, but reflective listening and exploration of Oliver’s ambivalence, guilt, and fear are limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and actionable next steps, though the style is somewhat directive and less MI-aligned.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely therapist-led, with the psychiatrist providing scripts and structure rather than co-constructing a plan with Oliver.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are offered, but prescribed scripts, timelines, and escalation steps may reduce Oliver’s sense of being in charge of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies mainly on external scripts and advice rather than drawing out Oliver’s own values, reasons for change, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few collaborative prompts, but the psychiatrist quickly assumes an expert/scriptwriter role and limits Oliver’s active direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Oliver offers some change talk, but the counselor mostly supplies language and plans instead of consistently evoking and strengthening Oliver’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce reasons to stay the same and acknowledges fairness, but uses more neutral redirection and escalating language than skillful softening.
