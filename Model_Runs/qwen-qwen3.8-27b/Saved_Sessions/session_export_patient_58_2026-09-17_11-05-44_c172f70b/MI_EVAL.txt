# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, explicitly disarming the power dynamic and validating Jamal's experiences. However, the warmth remains somewhat professional rather than deeply personal or emotionally resonant, and there is no explicit normalization of the struggle with being criticized by authority figures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Jamal's internal experience, including the chain of consequences and the frustration of feeling perpetually monitored. Reflections are accurate but sometimes somewhat summarizing rather than deeply emotional, missing opportunities to explore the loneliness of late-night phone time or the pain of being 'that person.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior while appropriately broadening the lens to explore upstream factors, progressing logically from surface-level complaint to deeper understanding. The pacing is appropriate given the young client's brief responses, though the session could have moved slightly faster toward a concrete action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the conversation as a joint exploration using inclusive language and builds on Jamal's contributions rather than redirecting them. The collaboration could be made more visible through more frequent explicit power-sharing language such as checking understanding with 'Does that feel right to you?'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Jamal's autonomy and ownership, never imposing solutions or giving directives. The question separating intrinsic motivation from external pressure ('What would not being that person actually feel like in your own life, separate from them?') is particularly strong and well-integrated into the therapeutic flow.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently elicits motivation from within Jamal rather than providing external reasons for change, successfully drawing out desire, need, and reasoning statements. However, the evocation could be more systematic in targeting all types of change talk, particularly confidence statements and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance using inclusive language and explicitly frames the session as exploration rather than prescription, consistently inviting Jamal's input. The partnership could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language and by more explicitly negotiating the agenda with Jamal rather than subtly guiding direction through question sequencing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes multiple change talk statements including desire, reasons, and need, and builds on them by reflecting and amplifying. The approach could be more systematic in targeting specific types of change talk (DARN-C), particularly confidence and commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully by reframing constructively and validating underlying needs without arguing against screen time or reinforcing the problem. The softening is present and effective but not consistently explicit, with limited use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge the pull of the status quo.
