# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warm, nonjudgmental positive regard while normalizing Anisa’s difficulty and shame. Statements such as “tired doesn’t mean broken” and “a slip doesn’t make you the bad mother” directly counter her self-critical stance without labeling or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Anisa’s ambivalence, guilt, and fear of becoming irritable. A few emotionally vivid statements about shame and smoking could have been reflected with slightly deeper intensity or imagery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on smoking cessation and efficiently narrows from broad ambivalence to a concrete, realistic target: keeping the car smoke-free. The counselor guides the work purposefully toward an actionable one-week plan without becoming overly directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with inclusive language such as “if we could” and “can I ask.” The final plan is co-constructed from Anisa’s own ideas, with the counselor reflecting, organizing, and gently shaping her contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice and ownership by leaving decisions with Anisa, such as asking which step feels most doable and what she is ready to try. The plan is framed as her own choice and manageable, rather than an imposed task or promise of perfection.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Anisa’s own supportive self-talk, values, and sense of possibility, especially around the smoke-free car plan. The score is slightly lower because deeper motivations and confidence could have been elicited more systematically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Anisa’s perspective multiple times and her ideas substantially shape the session. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicitly acknowledging Anisa as the expert on her own life and by more visible co-creation of the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes meaningful change talk, particularly around the car boundary and “taking my life back a little bit.” The score is lower because the counselor could more explicitly elicit and expand all forms of change talk, such as desire, ability, need, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by reframing it into lower-pressure options and “permission to be human” rather than failure. The score could be higher with more consistent use of double-sided reflections to pivot more explicitly from sustain talk toward change talk.
