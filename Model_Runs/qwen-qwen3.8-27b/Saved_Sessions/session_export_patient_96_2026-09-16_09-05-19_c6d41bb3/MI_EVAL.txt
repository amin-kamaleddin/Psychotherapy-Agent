# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirmed the client’s right to choose. Acceptance could be deeper by adding more individualized warmth beyond respecting the decision.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were accurate reflections of the client’s autonomy, confidence, and preference to avoid a lecture. Empathy remained somewhat surface-level, especially around the “chip enamel” concern and the meaning behind “edgy.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed largely focused on the piercing, oral health risks, and reducing contact with the stud. The target behavior could have been named more explicitly earlier to keep the session more strategically guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and incorporated the client’s ideas, such as lip balm, pockets, and keeping the phone away. The interaction was still fairly therapist-led, with a structured checklist shaping the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported the client’s independence and avoided pressuring her to remove the piercing. Some of the repeated reminders and prescriptive tips slightly shifted the tone toward advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used useful evocative moves, including a confidence rating and a small if-then plan. However, much of the session was information-giving rather than drawing out the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and positioned her as an active participant. Partnership could be stronger if the client’s ideas more substantially shaped the overall agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited useful change-oriented statements and affirmed the client’s confidence. More systematic development of the client’s own motivations would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and used a helpful double-sided reflection about wanting the edgy look without problems. Sustain talk could have been more consistently redirected toward smaller, autonomy-consistent changes.
