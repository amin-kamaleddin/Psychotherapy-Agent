# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of the client’s refusal to discuss quitting, avoiding shame or lecture. The acceptance is somewhat procedural and formulaic rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but surface-level, capturing the client’s dental needs and preferences without exploring feelings, fear, or ambivalence. Missed opportunities include irritation at repeated advice, avoidance, and health worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear practical focus on dental pain and treatment, keeping it from becoming a general smoking lecture. However, it does not build a shared MI agenda linking dental care to smoking cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites input, adapts to the client’s preferences, and offers optional help, creating a largely collaborative interaction. Repeated one-way information and recaps can make the interaction feel more instructive than equal-partner problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong and consistent, with repeated affirmations that the client is in control and free to choose his own timeline. The counselor avoids pressure, coercion, or imposed directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most questions are factual or logistical rather than evoking motivation, values, confidence, or change talk. The initial scaling question is not followed through once the client declines to discuss quitting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally acts as a collaborator, asks what a successful visit would look like, and offers choices rather than directives. Some expert-to-patient dynamic appears through repeated information lists and summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are very few attempts to evoke or develop the client’s own language in favor of change, especially regarding smoking cessation. Existing self-directed statements are not amplified or built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing, lecturing, or reinforcing reasons against change. It mostly sidesteps sustain talk rather than using MI techniques to explore ambivalence or link smoking to dental outcomes.
