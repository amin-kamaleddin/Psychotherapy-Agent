# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and responsive throughout. Respects Aisha's boundaries immediately (e.g., 'That's your call, and I'm not going to push') without defensiveness or pushback.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections that capture underlying needs (e.g., 'the only moments you get to yourself'). Misses deeper emotional layers in vulnerable moments, such as the fear/shame embedded in the sick-child night.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on evening smoking reduction with excellent progressive pacing from awareness to contingency planning. Slightly diffuse in the opening segment before the target behavior emerges.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-person process where Aisha's ideas shape the session (e.g., the 'take three out of the pack' plan is entirely her idea). The psychiatrist never overrides Aisha's direction or introduces uninvited agenda items.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a structural feature of the session, woven into virtually every interaction. Zero coercion; the psychiatrist consistently communicates that Aisha is in charge of her own choices and pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits Aisha's own strategies, standards, and commitment language rather than providing reasons for change. Could go deeper in exploring values, identity, and explicit self-efficacy assessment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Aisha as the expert on her own experience, never assuming the prescriptive role. The closing question ('What would you say to me on a night like that?') is a particularly strong equality move.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent and skillful elicitation of change talk through open questions and active affirmation. Could more explicitly explore Aisha's values, confidence level, and deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without argumentation, validating comparisons while gently opening space for self-examination. Could use double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence more fully rather than resolving it quickly.
