# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental validation and respects the client’s pressures and humor. Acceptance is generally strong but sometimes feels task-focused rather than deeply affirming independent self-worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor tracks some external stressors and difficulty, but often responds to emotional content with scripts or problem-solving rather than deeper reflection. Key feelings of shame, fear, and social ambivalence are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays steadily focused on reducing gambling exposure and financial risk through concrete steps. Barriers are addressed while maintaining a clear focus on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client chooses among options, but the counselor often generates scripts and plans rather than fully co-creating them. The interaction is collaborative on the surface but unevenly client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids coercion, but frequently provides detailed plans before eliciting the client’s own solutions. More explicit affirmation that any step is optional would strengthen autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and doable-step questions elicit some change talk, but values, motivations, and meanings are not deeply explored. The counselor relies more on advice and scripts than on drawing out the client’s own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences shape parts of the plan, but the counselor often assumes an expert role by offering complete scripts and steps. Greater power-sharing and client-generated language would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes confidence and commitment language, such as choosing the card and sending the sister text. It does not consistently deepen or expand the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes ambivalence and avoids arguing with sustain talk, but often meets it with problem-solving rather than reflective redirection. More double-sided reflections would better soften resistance.
