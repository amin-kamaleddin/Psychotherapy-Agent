# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician communicates respect, nonjudgment, and support, with statements that reduce shame and normalize ambivalence. The warmth is occasionally more formulaic and tied to the client’s achievement identity rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician makes frequent reflective attempts that often capture the client’s content accurately. However, reflections are often paraphrastic rather than emotionally probing, and key feelings such as fear of being the only sober person are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use in the context of peer pressure, school, and sports, moving toward a concrete next step. Direction is strong but is sometimes driven more by the clinician’s planning agenda than by a fully client-led MI process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites the client’s input and adapts when the client offers his own line, “I’m wiped.” However, the interaction often feels expert-led because the clinician supplies many scripts, options, and practical supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician supports autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, and accepting a smaller client-chosen step. Repeated encouragement of role-playing and specific planning can feel pressuring after the client expresses discomfort with scripting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some internal motivation through values, goals, readiness, and confidence questions. Evocation is limited by the frequent shift into practical solutions and scripts before deeper exploration of the client’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites the client’s perspective and asks permission before gathering details, supporting a collaborative stance. The partnership is uneven because the frequent provision of scripts and options makes the interaction more advisory than truly co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician makes effective efforts to evoke change talk by exploring what matters to the client, goal conflicts, readiness, and confidence. However, the client’s emerging change talk is not consistently deepened, summarized, or amplified before the conversation moves to practical planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk and generally responds with acceptance. The session could be stronger if the clinician more consistently used double-sided reflections or strategic redirection to help the client resolve ambivalence and shift toward change.
