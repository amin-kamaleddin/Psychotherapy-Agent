# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance with strong opening addressing fear of judgment and explicit affirmations of the client's insight. Occasionally feels slightly scripted in phrasing, and lacks an explicit statement of belief in the client's capacity for change independent of behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong complex reflections capturing emotional nuance, particularly with the 'grey' metaphor and the social weight of friendships. Reflections occasionally remain at the cognitive level rather than exploring deeper emotional layers, and the client's humor as a defense mechanism goes unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior with a clear logical progression from broad concerns to specific patterns to value-based motivation. No topic drift, irrelevant detours, or premature advice at any point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance using the client's own words and metaphors to create shared language, consistently positioning the therapist as a fellow explorer. Could be elevated by more explicitly acknowledging the client's self-knowledge as the primary source of insight and co-creating a joint closing or next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports autonomy from the outset; all strategies originate from the client with no pressure, advice, or evaluation of choices. Questions such as 'What do you think that opens up for you?' keep ownership firmly with the client throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional evocation with virtually all change talk and strategies originating from the client herself. Every question is designed to elicit the client's own thinking, and no external reasons for change or suggested strategies are ever provided.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-hierarchical stance with genuinely open questions that invite the client's perspective. Could be slightly strengthened by softening interpretive statements with explicit invitations for correction and by inviting the client to set or adjust the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and builds on change talk with strong evocative questions that keep momentum toward change and link values to motivation. Could have been more deliberate in amplifying change talk when it appears, such as exploring the client's confidence level in identified strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully without challenging or amplifying minimization, effectively reflecting loss and loneliness without arguing. The sustain talk around 'I deserve a night out' is acknowledged but not further explored with a double-sided reflection.
