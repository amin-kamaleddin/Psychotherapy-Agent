# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, care, and nonjudgment while normalizing distress without moralizing drug use. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper by explicitly validating regret and self-blame as common or by naming that pain does not make Amira bad.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session shows accurate complex reflections of Amira’s ambivalence, fatigue, isolation, and self-blame. It could go further by exploring deeper layers such as shame, fear, grief, or the safety meaning behind hoping someone notices.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation remains generally focused on reducing use tonight and moving toward concrete behaviors such as putting the phone away or making it through the evening. Direction could be stronger by explicitly naming the target behavior and collaboratively defining a brief, specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Amira’s ideas to shape the session. The collaboration is respectful but still somewhat counselor-led, as the psychiatrist supplies many frames and options rather than fully co-creating the agenda or next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently emphasizes Amira’s pace, choice, and self-direction while avoiding direct pressure. Autonomy support would be stronger with fewer counselor-supplied menus and more explicit emphasis that any choice remains Amira’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated coping ideas and uses open questions to draw out small steps. Evocation is limited because it does not deeply elicit Amira’s values, motivations, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a non-authoritarian stance and uses language that supports shared decision-making. The session could feel more fully partnered by reducing the counselor’s role in structuring options and more explicitly sharing power in the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes and validates practical change talk such as not using today, putting the phone away, and making it through the evening. Change talk is not consistently deepened, amplified, or connected to importance, confidence, values, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully rather than reinforced or argued with. The session could soften it more effectively by using double-sided reflections, reframes, or consistent redirection toward change instead of repeatedly returning to loneliness and lack of support.
