# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Sofia’s fear and guilt. Acceptance was somewhat procedural and did not fully address the deeper identity conflict around pregnancy and her social role.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed basic empathy but used brief, supportive reflections followed quickly by problem-solving. There were missed opportunities to more deeply reflect Sofia’s ambivalence, fear of being judged, and conflict between protecting the baby and maintaining her social identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior, reducing or stopping alcohol use around Friday social situations. Direction was clear and practical, though somewhat narrow and prescriptive rather than linking behavior to deeper values and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was partial collaboration, with Sofia contributing ideas such as the mocktail line and sister backup plan. However, the psychiatrist remained more of an expert guiding the process than a true equal partner in co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices, avoided coercive language, and validated Sofia’s right to use an exit plan. Autonomy support was slightly weakened by the frequent use of directive, list-based interventions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided useful practical information but did not consistently elicit Sofia’s own motivations, values, or confidence for change. Few open MI-style questions were used to draw out and expand change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned herself as the expert offering scripts and strategies rather than a partner helping Sofia find her own wisdom. More genuine partnership would involve inviting Sofia to lead problem-solving and identify her own strengths and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia offered meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on these statements. The session moved quickly into planning without deeply exploring Sofia’s own reasons, confidence, or desired identity as a mother.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing against sustain talk and redirected it toward practical strategies. However, MI-specific softening techniques such as double-sided reflections and exploring the values behind sustain talk were used less consistently.
