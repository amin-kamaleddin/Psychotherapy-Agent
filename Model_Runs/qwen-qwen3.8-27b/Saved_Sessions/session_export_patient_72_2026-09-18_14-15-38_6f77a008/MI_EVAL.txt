# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone throughout, validating Leila's embarrassment and normalizing the gap between knowing and doing. Occasionally reads more clinical/strategic than deeply personal, and normalization of struggle could be more frequent and explicit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional empathic accuracy by picking up subtle emotional nuances (e.g., 'less like myself,' 'the silence') and reflecting them with genuine emotional weight. Reflections are complex, combining content and feeling, and consistently access the emotional undercurrent beneath practical planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on re-establishing an exercise routine with logical progression from problem identification through concrete action planning. Direction is purposeful but somewhat easy to maintain given Leila's high motivation and organization, limiting opportunities to demonstrate redirection through resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A masterclass in collaboration — the psychiatrist never leads with their own agenda or expertise, and every question is designed to draw out Leila's own thinking. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with Leila's contributions (YMCA drop-in, walk to the market, telling Sarah) clearly shaping every aspect of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy by never prescribing or directing — all plans originate from Leila herself, reinforced with language like 'You designed that yourself.' Could be slightly stronger in moments of doubt (e.g., 'I don't know if I'm ready') with more explicit affirmation of her right to choose her own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional evocation throughout — questions like 'what would that look like to you?' consistently elicit Leila's own solutions rather than providing them. The final summary ('you keep solving the problem before I even get to ask the right question') is a powerful evocative statement reinforcing her agency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom about change reside within Leila, positioning themselves as a reflective listener and questioner rather than an expert giving advice. A textbook example of MI partnership, with Leila's own insights driving the session forward at every stage.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently evokes and amplifies change talk through open questions, reflective amplification, and summaries that highlight the shift from 'stuck' to active. Misses an opportunity to explicitly reflect Leila's powerful identity change talk ('That sounds like me') and to elicit more confidence/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk (fatigue, couch, gym sales pressure) skillfully by reframing barriers as honest assessments and helping Leila find solutions that work with rather than against them. The opportunity to demonstrate more sophisticated softening (e.g., double-sided reflections) is limited by the client's high motivation and preparation stage.
