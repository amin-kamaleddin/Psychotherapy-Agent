# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgmental regard, and acceptance of Laura's ambivalence and lapses. Statements about stress eating, skipped breakfast, and tiredness are reflected without alarm, moralizing, or failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong, nuanced reflections of Laura's ambivalence, self-monitoring, and desire for a gentler internal voice. However, the deeper emotional cost of self-criticism, shame, and exhaustion is not always named as explicitly as it could be.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on making health more accessible on low days. The conversation stays tightly linked to after-work stress eating, skipped breakfast, and a concrete, value-congruent behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the conversation using Laura's language, metaphors, and ideas rather than delivering advice. The resulting plan is clearly shaped by Laura's own contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by emphasizing low stakes, privacy, support rather than pressure, and absence of quotas. Laura is allowed to define what feels like care for her own body.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes Laura's own values, preferences, and capacities rather than supplying reasons to change. Most of the important content, including the word 'still' and the after-work step, comes from Laura.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Laura as the expert on her own values, body, and preferences. The session is driven by questions that invite Laura's judgment rather than imposing the psychiatrist's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes client-generated change talk through open, evocative questions that lead to practical, value-congruent ideas. The main limitation is that the change talk is not consistently amplified, deepened, consolidated, or summarized.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging it without challenging Laura or reinforcing the status quo. However, the psychiatrist sometimes stays in the tension rather than using a more active double-sided reflection to pivot toward change.
