# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids labeling the client as lazy or failing. Warmth is consistent but could be more individualized and explicitly affirm the client's effort and worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's ambivalence, fatigue, guilt, and fear of the walk becoming another obligation. Deeper exploration of shame and self-criticism would strengthen the score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session initially focuses on increasing physical activity but drifts toward sitting in the car, guilt, and the meaning of rest. More consistent refocusing on a concrete exercise step is needed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as an expert and contributes the walk, 10 minutes, first minute, and parking lot ideas. Reliance on clinician-generated options slightly limits fully client-directed collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes the client's choice and avoids pressure to commit to exercise. More explicit normalization of choosing not to act and fewer suggested options would strengthen autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended prompts elicit several client-generated options, such as a short walk or the first minute. These ideas are not fully developed into stronger change talk, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is generally collaborative, with the client's ideas shaping the discussion. Frequent clinician suggestions shift some ownership from the client toward the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk, including smaller steps and walking to the parking lot. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify these statements or connect them to ability, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fatigue, guilt, and avoidance is handled without argument and is gently reframed. More decisive pivots from guilt toward values or a concrete next step would improve the score.
