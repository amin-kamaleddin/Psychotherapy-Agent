# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently nonjudgmental and validating stance throughout the session. Statements convey respect and positive regard without moralizing or imposing approval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and often captures the client’s ambivalence and self-protection accurately. The score is limited by a repeated question and some reflections that stay more at the content level than the deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, coherent focus on aligning the client’s week with self-respect and balance. The conversation steadily narrows from a broad goal to a concrete, values-based behavioral plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, with the psychiatrist building on the client’s language and treating her as the expert on her own life. Collaboration is slightly less fluid due to repeated questions and occasional counselor-suggested wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the client’s sense of choice by using open questions and respecting the decision to keep the boundary private for now. Some questions subtly suggest a preferred course, which reduces the level of fully autonomous support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client’s own motivations, values, and solutions rather than advising directly. Evocation is strong but not maximal because the session could have more explicitly explored importance, confidence, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s perspective and treats her as the expert on her work culture and values. The session remains partly counselor-led in structure, and some suggested phrases make the partnership less fully co-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client produces meaningful change talk and generates concrete steps for a smaller boundary. The psychiatrist supports this with open, client-directed questions but does not consistently deepen the change talk through summaries, amplifying reflections, or explicit commitment/confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk related to guilt and fear of being seen as flaky is responded to by reframing the behavior as self-respect and low-stakes practice. The score is not a 5 because later questions briefly prolong focus on coworkers’ perceptions and the session lacks a stronger closing pivot back to the client’s values or change language.
