# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, affirming language and accepts Margaret’s ambivalence about continuing to drink at the pub. It could be stronger by more explicitly normalizing her struggle with falling, family concern, and aging-related change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections track Margaret’s concerns about independence, safety, and social connection. Empathy could deepen by exploring stronger emotional undercurrents, such as embarrassment, frustration, or fear of losing independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol consumption in a way that supports steadiness and safety. The conversation moves clearly from the fall-related concern to a concrete Tuesday-evening plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works jointly with Margaret to build a workable plan using her own ideas, such as Tuesday, Janet, ginger beer, and leaving if needed. The interaction feels like shared problem-solving rather than clinician-driven direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids commands, persuasion, and advice-giving while repeatedly reinforcing Margaret’s ownership. The plan is framed as her own choice, including the flexibility not to promise every week.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Margaret’s own motivations and strategies, such as using Janet and reframing the urge as habit or boredom. Evocation could improve by exploring deeper values, confidence, and discrepancy more fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Margaret as the expert on her own life and preferences. The plan is built from her suggestions, with clear recognition of her contribution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions and reflections effectively evoke concrete change talk, including a realistic plan to reduce drinking on Tuesday. The counselor could more powerfully amplify confidence and summarize Margaret’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by accepting Margaret’s reasons for keeping the pub, not promising weekly change, and resisting the need for control. These concerns are integrated into a compatible, self-directed plan.
