# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance, though it becomes somewhat formulaic at times. The continued delivery of a safety script after the client expressed not wanting it slightly undermines the acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening by accurately capturing the client's desire for privacy and avoidance of labeling. The main limitation is a failure to consistently explore the deeper hurt, isolation, or shame behind the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and action-oriented focus on the target behavior of peer-related conflict and school safety. However, the focus becomes quite narrow on exit strategies without fully connecting the plan back to broader goals like school engagement or repairing relationships.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves collaboratively by framing the work as shared problem-solving and incorporating the client's specific ideas into the plan. The interaction is generally equal, though the psychiatrist does much of the organizing and structuring rather than fully co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client's autonomy by offering options, respecting boundaries, and agreeing not to check in. The slight limitation is that the psychiatrist repeatedly offers additional planning and scripts after the client has expressed a clear desire to stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully uses early questions to elicit the client's goals and confidence levels. However, the evocation becomes less consistent as the session shifts toward the psychiatrist suggesting practical options rather than drawing out deeper internal motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance by inviting the client's input and respecting their perspective throughout the session. The partnership is strong but could be improved with more explicit power-sharing language and negotiation of the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes clear efforts to evoke change talk, resulting in the client agreeing to a specific plan. The evocation remains shallow as it focuses primarily on practical steps rather than exploring the client's personal reasons or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing or directly contradicting the client's sustain talk. However, repeated statements that the problem is 'on them' reinforce externalization and miss the opportunity to explore the client's agency and empowerment.
