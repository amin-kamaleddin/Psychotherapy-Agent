# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating the patient’s mixed motives and imperfections. There is no disapproval or correction, even when the patient raises self-doubt or past lapses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening to capture ambivalence, privacy concerns, and fear of failure. Deeper, sustained emotional attunement to the patient’s family history and relapse fears would strengthen this further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moves clearly from broad ambivalence to a concrete, testable plan. The alcohol-use target could be more explicitly linked back into the final plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates rather than prescribes, helping the patient refine her own ideas. The final plan is patient-generated and fits the patient’s real-world context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the patient’s freedom and choice, respecting her decision not to use a friend check-in. The patient is consistently positioned as the expert on what would work for her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits the patient’s own reasons, preferences, and confidence rather than supplying advice. The patient generates a clear plan and her own criteria for noticing success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys genuine partnership by following the patient’s choices and inviting her input at each step. The interaction is collaborative rather than directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out meaningful change talk and reinforces the patient’s confidence. Deeper elicitation of desire, ability, reasons, and commitment would make this more complete.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully without argument or minimization. More deliberate use of externalizing or double-sided responses could further soften the patient’s doubts.
