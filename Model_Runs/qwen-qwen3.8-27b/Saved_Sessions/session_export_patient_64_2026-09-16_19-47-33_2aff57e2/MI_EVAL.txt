# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizes ambivalence. Acceptance could be warmer with more explicit positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track the client’s concerns across the session. Empathy could deepen further into values and emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the vaccination decision. The psychiatrist guides movement from ambivalence to a concrete, client-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and follows the client’s lead. The client’s ideas directly shape the session and final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client decision-making is consistently emphasized without pressure or persuasion. Autonomy support could be stronger with more explicit affirmation of the right to decline or change course.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively elicit the client’s own motivations and practical criteria for change. Evocation could be stronger by drawing out deeper values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the decision-maker and the session follows the client’s contributions. The psychiatrist still drives the agenda somewhat, limiting fully shared control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and affirmations help the client express desire and ability to act. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify and consolidate change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not met with argument but reframed into practical constraints. More double-sided reflections could further explore the client’s ambivalence.
