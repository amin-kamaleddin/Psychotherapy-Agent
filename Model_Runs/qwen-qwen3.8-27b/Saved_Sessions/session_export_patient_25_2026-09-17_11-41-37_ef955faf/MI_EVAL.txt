# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with warm validation of Daniel's ambivalence and self-doubt without condescension. Notably affirmed Daniel's competence with 'you're undervaluing it because it's your answer, not a program someone gave you.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening with progressive deepening, precisely capturing Daniel's ambivalence between 'it's probably fine' and 'make sure I'm not slipping.' The reflection 'that little word does a lot of heavy lifting' demonstrated remarkable attunement to emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear logical arc from identifying the concern, through mechanism (autopilot vs. decision), to a concrete client-generated action plan. The smoking bridge was used strategically once without drifting off the alcohol target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue where Daniel's ideas shaped every turn, with inclusive co-constructive language throughout ('what do you think?', 'you've found the gap'). The final plan — phone buzz, glass on table — was entirely Daniel's creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit autonomy support throughout, framing change as proactive self-directed choice rather than deficit correction. The final question asked Daniel to predict his own behavior rather than telling him what he should do.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never once provided external reasons for change; all motivation was self-generated by Daniel through carefully crafted evocative questions. Daniel's own words ('the second one where I'm not really choosing it anymore') carried the motivational weight.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Daniel as the expert on his own life, with every intervention structured as a question inviting his own solutions. The psychiatrist never prescribed, advised, or used authority language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions consistently elicited rich change language from Daniel, who articulated his own fears, principles, and commitment. Amplified and connected change talk back to prior success (smoking pause) to reinforce momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk effectively with reframing ('you're trying to keep something working') and normalization of social discomfort. Docked one point for not more actively softening Daniel's 'I don't care enough in the moment' with a double-sided reflection or pivot to his existing evidence.
