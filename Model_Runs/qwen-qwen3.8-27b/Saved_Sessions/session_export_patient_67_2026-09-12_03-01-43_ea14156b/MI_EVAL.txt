# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing stance throughout. Acceptance is strong, though it is somewhat procedural and could include more individualized affirmation of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and captures key themes such as surprise, ambivalence, and willingness to try tracking. Empathy could deepen by reflecting the emotional discomfort or self-doubt behind several of the client’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on alcohol use without meaningful topic drift. Direction is excellent as it steadily moves toward a concrete, behavior-focused tracking plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance and incorporates the client’s ideas into the plan. However, much of the structure and content is still counselor-generated rather than client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported through choice-focused questions and respectful acceptance of the client’s ambivalence. The psychiatrist could more explicitly emphasize the client’s right to modify or abandon the plan without penalty.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions, pros/cons, and rulers to draw out motivation. Evocation is limited because the session moves into practical planning before fully expanding the client’s own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is invited to set priorities, choose options, and determine plan details. Partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist still generates many options and plan components, making the interaction less fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk, and the psychiatrist uses some evocative tools such as the importance and confidence rulers. More follow-up reflections and open questions are needed to consistently expand the client’s own change reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing, moralizing, or pressuring the client and holds both sides of the ambivalence. However, sustain talk and low-confidence statements are not consistently transformed into change-focused language.
