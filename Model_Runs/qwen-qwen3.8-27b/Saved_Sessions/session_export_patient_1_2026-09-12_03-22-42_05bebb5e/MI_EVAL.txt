# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive without shaming or pressuring the patient. Acceptance could be stronger by affirming the patient as a person, not only accepting his choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reflects the patient’s statements accurately, especially practical and logistical concerns. Empathy remains surface-level and does not fully capture the patient’s underlying frustration, defensiveness, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session initially addresses tobacco use but quickly follows the patient’s redirection into occupational dust and symptom relief. The target tobacco-change agenda is not strategically reintroduced or maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-creates a practical symptom-management plan through options and patient choice. Collaboration is strong but remains bounded by the clinician’s agenda rather than fully addressing tobacco change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently offers choices, avoids pressure, and respects the patient’s decisions. Autonomy support could be more explicit by connecting patient ownership directly to tobacco-use change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions mainly elicit practical plans for hydration, lozenges, and symptom management. The clinician does not evoke the patient’s own motivations, values, or reasons for reducing cigar use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient clearly helps shape the plan, and the clinician invites input throughout. Partnership is collaborative but remains more clinician-guided rather than fully empowering the patient as the expert on his own change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some confidence and action talk, but only about self-care steps such as gargling and hydration. Change talk is not effectively cultivated for reducing or quitting mini cigars.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing but does not effectively reframe or soften the patient’s repeated minimization of cigar use. The patient’s sustain talk about cigars is largely left as the dominant frame.
