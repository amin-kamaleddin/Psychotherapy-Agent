# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Ethan's experience and validating his concerns. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural, and the therapist occasionally moves too quickly toward problem-solving language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses complex reflections to track Ethan's social and emotional conflicts accurately. Empathy could be stronger by exploring deeper feelings such as embarrassment, fear of rejection, or the cost of managing peer perception.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on alcohol use and related consequences. The target behavior is not defined sharply enough, making the direction less behaviorally specific.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Ethan's ideas rather than imposing a plan, creating a strong collaborative tone. The session remains somewhat therapist-led, especially in the repeated use of small-step structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist avoids directive language and invites Ethan's preferences, supporting his role as the decision-maker. Autonomy support is limited by the absence of explicit statements that Ethan is free to choose not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist evokes practical problem-solving and client-generated change ideas. Deeper intrinsic motivation, values, confidence, and commitment are not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and invites Ethan's perspective. Partnership is strengthened by client ownership language but limited by the therapist's ongoing control of structure and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions elicit several client-centered change ideas, and ownership is reinforced effectively. Change talk remains mostly practical, with limited development of deeper DARN-C language such as importance, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk by using double-sided reflections rather than arguing or correcting. Sustain talk remains prominent, and the therapist could more consistently connect social benefits to values or increase discrepancy.
