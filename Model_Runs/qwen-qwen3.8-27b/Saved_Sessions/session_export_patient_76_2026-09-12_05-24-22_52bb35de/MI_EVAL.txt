# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Engaged and nonjudgmental, but the response becomes procedural through urgent safety framing and repeated crisis-line reminders. This risk-management stance dilutes unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately reflects exhaustion, desire for quiet, safety, and the function of self-harm. It could go deeper by validating helplessness and hopelessness before shifting to safety logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the immediate self-harm concern. The direction is appropriate but narrow, limiting broader MI exploration of values or alternatives.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and asks what feels doable, but the psychiatrist still drives the agenda and safety-planning steps. Collaboration is present but not fully patient-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids commands and offers choices, which supports some autonomy. Repeated crisis-line prompts and structured risk-management choices may undermine the patient's sense of being in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly problem-solving and safety-oriented rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations. There is limited effort to evoke personal values, hopes, or reasons for safer change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is counselor-led and organized around safety protocols rather than shared exploration. The patient is passive and not fully positioned as the expert on her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays focused on immediate coping and safety rather than eliciting reasons for change. It misses opportunities to invite desires, values, or goals for choosing safer alternatives.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validates the function of self-harm without dismissing it, which is appropriate. However, it does not skillfully reframe the belief that cutting is the only thing that helps.
