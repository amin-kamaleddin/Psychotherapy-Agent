# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and respects the client's preference to reduce rather than abstain. Acceptance could be warmer and more explicitly affirming, with stronger validation of the client's difficulty and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures the client's ambivalence between staying connected and maintaining control. Empathy could deepen by more clearly reflecting underlying emotions such as fear, loneliness, shame, or anxiety about being perceived as boring.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing weekday drinking, especially around Wednesday, and developing a practical exit plan. The conversation moves purposefully toward concrete behaviors with little irrelevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively by building on the client's ideas and inviting her to evaluate options. Could become more fully co-constructed by checking in about priorities and allowing the client more control over the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports autonomy by respecting the client's boundary against quitting completely and helping her generate her own options. Could strengthen this further by more explicitly affirming the client's right to choose and to decide at her own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses open questions and helps the client generate practical ideas, but the evocation remains more behavioral than motivational. Could better elicit values, confidence, importance, and the personal meaning of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys a collaborative stance by inviting the client's perspective and positioning her as the expert on her own communication style. Partnership could be strengthened by more explicit agenda negotiation and shared power over the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Encourages some change talk by asking the client to generate solutions and articulate preferences. Could more systematically elicit desire, ability, need, and commitment language, and use scaling or value-based questions to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without arguing, using double-sided reflections that keep concerns within a change-compatible frame. Could more actively reframe sustain talk toward the client's values and change goals rather than remaining in the concern for several turns.
