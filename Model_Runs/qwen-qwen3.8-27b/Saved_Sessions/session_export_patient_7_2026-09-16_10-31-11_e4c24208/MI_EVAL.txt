# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, avoids argument, and validates Amina's boundary not to answer. The acceptance feels somewhat scripted and slightly misattunes at one point by praising her courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Amina's privacy concerns, discomfort with probing, and need for space. They remain mostly literal and repetitive, with less deeper perspective-taking of possible distrust, judgment, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on engagement, safety, and home-life concerns. It lacks a clear, consistent movement toward a specific client-identified target or next step after Amina becomes resistant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, asks for preferences, and ultimately follows Amina's direction to sit quietly. Collaboration is reduced when the counselor continues to introduce multiple options after Amina clearly asks for minimal interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amina is repeatedly told she controls whether, when, and how to engage, which is especially appropriate for a resistant adolescent. The autonomy message is slightly weakened by repeated choices and safety information after she asks the therapist to stop asking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is only a brief early attempt to elicit change talk through a question about what she wants to change and a small step. After Amina resists, the psychiatrist makes little effort to indirectly evoke her values, preferences, or motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and eventually follows Amina's direction, showing some partnership. However, frequent therapist-initiated options and safety statements make the session less fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes one brief, early attempt to elicit change talk. After Amina's resistance, there are no effective reflections, amplifications, or indirect invitations to explore change language or small steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles resistance reasonably well by not arguing or invalidating Amina. Repeatedly offering options and safety statements after she says "just sit there" can sustain her guarded stance without strategically redirecting the session.
