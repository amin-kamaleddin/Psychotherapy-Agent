# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming. Some emotional validation remains more functional than deeply empathic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Kevin’s ambivalence and concerns about autonomy. Deeper emotions such as embarrassment, fear, and social pressure are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol-related injury risk in social settings. The direction is practical but can feel therapist-led and repetitive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative questions and invitations to choose options. The psychiatrist still supplies much of the content and plan, limiting true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and frames the plan as an experiment. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by the volume of specific strategies and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and scaling to elicit motivation. Change talk is often interrupted by advice, psychoeducation, and solution-building.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins collaboratively but becomes more therapist-led. Kevin is often choosing from the psychiatrist’s options rather than leading the direction of change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful attempts to evoke reasons for change and confidence. Change talk is present but not consistently expanded or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Kevin’s reasons to keep drinking and acknowledges the value of fun. Sustain talk is mostly redirected rather than actively softened through double-sided reflection.
