# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, normalizing Lars's struggles without condescension. Key phrases like 'That's not a character flaw' and 'There's no pressure here' demonstrate strong acceptance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and perspective-taking are evident, including accurately capturing ambivalence and the emotional weight of the gap between aspiration and reality. A notable miss occurs near the end when Lars expresses fear of being 'broken,' which would have required deeper empathic engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session progresses logically from boredom to the car job goal, practical barriers, support systems, and the Friday pattern, each connecting to the central re-entry objective. Direction could be tighter in the final transition to the Friday/pub pattern before the session ends.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels like a genuine partnership, with language like 'I'm curious' and 'what does that sound like to you?' creating a joint exploration dynamic. Slightly reduced by moments where the psychiatrist introduces external ideas (e.g., the re-entry worker) rather than waiting for Lars to generate them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support is demonstrated throughout, with consistent framing of options rather than directives and explicit reinforcement of Lars's right to make his own decisions. The psychiatrist never tells Lars what to do and validates his boundary about Donna as a fair and appropriate limit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits Lars's own motivations through open questions, allowing him to articulate his own reasons for change rather than providing them. A score of 5 would require more explicit evocation of commitment and confidence language, such as scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance, positioning Lars as the expert on his own life and avoiding directive language. Occasional moments of slight guidance, such as introducing the re-entry plan, prevent a perfect score despite the overall strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is effectively drawn out through strategic open questions and amplified by reflecting it back with emphasis, such as 'You just designed your own support system in about two sentences.' More explicit evocation of DARN-CAT language or deliberate amplification of specific change talk would elevate this to a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled reasonably but could be more skillful, particularly Lars's fear of being 'broken' which would benefit from a double-sided reflection. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance techniques as frequently as a higher score warrants.
