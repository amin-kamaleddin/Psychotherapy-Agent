# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, with the psychiatrist never labeling or disapproving while consistently affirming Marcus's worth separate from his behavior. Validates his self-concept ('You're not that guy') while simultaneously creating space for ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, nuanced reflections that capture both content and emotional subtext, progressively deepening throughout the session. Examples include reflecting the dissonance of his surprise at not being judged and the social pressure of not wanting to be the one who says no.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use with a logical progression from validation through exploring peer dynamics to eliciting his own solutions. The session could benefit from a brief consolidating summary to tighten the strategic arc before cutting off.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing with inclusive language and joint exploration of the session's purpose. However, the overall structure still feels somewhat therapist-guided rather than fully co-created, with Marcus's ideas not visibly reshaping the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model-level autonomy support throughout, with explicit non-directive stances ('I'm here to talk, not to fix') and consistent framing of Marcus as the agent of any potential change. Never uses persuasive language or pushes toward a specific outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation that consistently draws out Marcus's own perspectives without providing external reasons for change. Could be elevated by eliciting more values-based and need-based change talk (e.g., 'What matters to you about how you show up to your friends?').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a strong collaborative stance with genuine power-sharing, including honoring Marcus's stated boundary ('no lectures, no judgment'). Could be elevated by more explicit acknowledgment of Marcus's expertise in his own life and co-creation of the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of evocation to draw out change talk, with successful moments of affirming self-awareness as a strength. Could do more to explicitly elicit and develop multiple types of change talk (desire, ability, need, values) rather than relying primarily on affirming his insight.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully without dismissing or arguing, using the 'right side of the line' framing to acknowledge his position while creating space for ambivalence. Could be more strategic with double-sided reflections that honor the 'for' side while gently introducing the 'against' side.
