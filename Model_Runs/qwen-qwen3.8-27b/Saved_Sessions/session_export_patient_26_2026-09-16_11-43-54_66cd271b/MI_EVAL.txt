# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming with explicit acknowledgment of Daniela's burden and effort. Slightly limited by occasionally generic or task-focused language rather than deeply individualized responses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate reflections around stress and logistical burden, including a solid composite reflection on her worry about dropping something. Misses deeper emotional layers such as shame about needing help, anxiety about freezing, and fear of burdening others; also does not directly validate her boundary about wanting messages written.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, organized focus on reliable childcare and smoking reduction with little aimless drift. Direction is driven more by the psychiatrist's practical suggestions than by Daniela's own goals and motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and allows Daniela to choose among scripts and plans, with her input shaping wording and focus. However, the psychiatrist produces most of the practical content and decision framework, keeping Daniela in a selecting rather than constructing role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly asks permission, offers alternatives, and uses noncoercive language like 'no pressure' and 'we'll do it when you feel ready.' Score limited by numerous prescriptive scripts and coping instructions, including continued script offers after Daniela indicates messages written are 'a bit much.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some evocative questions about urgency, doability, and preference that elicit brief change talk. Frequently shifts into advising and script-writing rather than drawing out Daniela's own values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frames work as collaborative with questions like 'which feels more doable?' and invites Daniela's input on content. However, the psychiatrist supplies the neighbor text, Head Start script, backup plans, and coping steps, making the session largely clinician-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generates some change talk around wanting a reliable night, Head Start, the 10-minute plan, and not smoking around her son. Missed opportunities to deepen with questions about importance, confidence, or values before moving into problem-solving and scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accepts Daniela's uncertainty and barriers nonjudgmentally with autonomy-supportive language like 'totally fine if it doesn't work.' Sustain talk is often bypassed with quick backup plans rather than strategically explored, and 'missing class would spike stress and make smoking worse' adds pressure rather than gently weighing perspectives.
