# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance with neutral language such as “that makes sense” and “I hear you.” Acceptance is consistent but somewhat formulaic and could more explicitly affirm Viktor as a person or normalize his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Viktor’s perspective accurately, including his sense of control, need for low-key autonomy, and discomfort with being treated as out of control. The reflection is often content-focused and misses deeper feelings such as frustration with his wife, fear of control, or resentment about being pushed into counseling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on Viktor’s drug use, its impact on his marriage, and his evening routine, moving from resistance to a specific behavior plan. However, the initial therapist turn is crowded with multiple questions, and the later session shifts heavily into procedural action planning rather than broader motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed around Viktor’s choices, including texting his wife, leaving with Dave, and keeping his keys in his pocket. Collaboration is strong but not fully equal, because the psychiatrist still structures the conversation, proposes options, and repeatedly drives the agenda with scaling questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with repeated emphasis on Viktor’s choice and respect for his rejections of logs, alarms, rehearsals, and check-ins. The main limitation is that frequent requests to rate confidence and commit to plan details can create subtle pressure to produce a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative techniques, such as scaling questions and eliciting Viktor’s own plan, but the session remains more suggestion-driven than deeply evocative. He often offers specific options instead of helping Viktor generate his own reasons for change or explore values more deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Viktor’s input, asks what feels doable, and accepts his rejections without pressure. The partnership is somewhat limited because the therapist still drives much of the structure, proposes experiments, and offers menus of options rather than fully centering Viktor’s agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical change talk and small commitments, including being home by 11, texting his wife, leaving with Dave, and checking the time. He does not fully amplify or deepen this change talk by helping Viktor articulate why the change matters to him or how it connects to his values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Viktor’s sustain talk and acknowledges his belief that his use is under control. However, he does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategically redirect sustain talk toward resolving the discrepancy between Viktor’s self-perception and the relational strain he acknowledges.
