# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized the client’s time constraints. Warmth was somewhat functional, and the final “RESPONSE 2” line disrupted rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening that accurately captured the client’s ambivalence and practical needs. Empathy was more cognitive and logistical than emotional, with less exploration of underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on a specific, small health behavior and moved the conversation toward a concrete plan. Minor redundancy and the final nontherapeutic line reduced the overall consistency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interview felt like equal problem-solving, with the client’s ideas and constraints driving the session. The clinician built on the client’s plan rather than imposing recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported the client’s sense of choice and control through low-effort options and an explicit stop rule. Language avoided pressure, blame, or implicit coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited the client’s own reasons, ideas, and solutions through open questions. The client generated the main plan, while the clinician reflected and expanded on it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the client as the expert on his own schedule and life, with the session following the client’s lead. The statement “That’s your solution, not mine” strongly reinforced partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent questions helped the client produce concrete change statements and a practical two-week plan. The session would benefit from more explicit scaling, commitment language, or summarizing reinforcement of the client’s reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client’s concerns about time and adding another task were never challenged or debated. They were effectively used as design constraints for a realistic, low-effort change.
