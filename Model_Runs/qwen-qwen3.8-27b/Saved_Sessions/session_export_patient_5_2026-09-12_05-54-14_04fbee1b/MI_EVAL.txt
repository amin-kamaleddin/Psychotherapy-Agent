# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental validation without labeling or disapproval. Some affirmations felt formulaic, but the overall stance was strongly accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected Alessia’s need for accuracy, stability, and caution, but the reflections remained relatively surface-level. Deeper emotional themes, such as fear of losing control and ambivalence about relying on support, were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the medication transition, washout planning, and anxiety management during the gap. Direction was clear and practical, though some information was provided before collaboratively prioritizing Alessia’s preferred focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-created a prescriber message and coping plan using Alessia’s medications, dose, duration, and preferences. Collaboration was strong but remained somewhat clinician-led because the psychiatrist supplied much of the substantive content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices, avoided pressure, and appropriately deferred final decisions to Alessia and her prescriber. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly emphasizing that the final decision belongs to Alessia.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and affirmed proactive behavior, but evocation was limited by heavy reliance on information and problem-solving. More evocative questions about values, confidence, importance, and readiness would have strengthened change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Alessia’s input and allowed her details to shape the message and coping plan. Partnership was evident but not fully equal, as the psychiatrist remained the primary information provider and problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alessia produced several change-related statements, and the psychiatrist affirmed some of them appropriately. Cultivation was present but not consistently skillful, with fewer evocative questions and some reliance on a ready-made message.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Alessia’s concerns respectfully and redirected toward safe, concrete next steps. Softening was mostly neutral acknowledgment rather than strategic MI redirection using double-sided reflections.
