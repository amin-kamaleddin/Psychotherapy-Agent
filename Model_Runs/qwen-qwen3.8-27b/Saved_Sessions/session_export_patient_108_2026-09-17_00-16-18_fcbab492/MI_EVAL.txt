# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and avoids judgment when Oliver expresses guilt, fear, or self-criticism. Responses reframe difficult self-statements in a warm, strengths-based way, supporting Oliver’s sense of worth and clarity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Oliver’s ambivalence, fear, and vulnerability. A few interpretations are slightly inferential or move ahead of Oliver’s stated experience, which slightly limits the depth of empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on the target behavior of setting a clear boundary with the flatmate and following through. Each interaction advances toward concrete, clinically useful goals such as wording, timing, and a self-monitoring strategy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist operates as an equal partner rather than a directive advisor, inviting joint exploration and co-creating language with Oliver. Oliver’s own ideas shape the session, especially in developing the boundary script and coping plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Oliver is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, with choices framed as options rather than recommendations. The psychiatrist avoids pressure or directive advice, reinforcing Oliver’s freedom to select his own approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out Oliver’s own motivation, reasons for change, and self-generated solutions. Oliver produces the key ideas himself, and the psychiatrist reflects and amplifies them effectively.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions Oliver as the authority on his situation and needs, rather than supplying the solution. The session is collaborative, client-led, and free of an expert-dominant stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Oliver’s change talk is actively reinforced and expanded throughout, including desire, ability, reason, and commitment statements. The psychiatrist strategically deepens this language, leading to concrete plans for assertive action.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by normalizing guilt, fear of being cold, and concern about caring too much. However, at times the psychiatrist moves quickly into reframing and solution-building before fully exploring the ambivalence.
