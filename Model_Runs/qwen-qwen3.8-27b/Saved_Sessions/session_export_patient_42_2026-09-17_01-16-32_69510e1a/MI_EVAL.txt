# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and normalizing, without judging or arguing about smoking. Some statements still sound subtly evaluative, and deeper feelings of being a “leftover” could be normalized more strongly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the social and emotional meaning of smoking, including fear of awkwardness and need to belong. The final powerful image of feeling like a “guest in my own break” was not directly reflected or processed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking during social breaks and gradually moves toward a concrete, low-risk experiment. The direction becomes more practical than motivational and could better connect the plan to her values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client’s ideas, such as coffee, stepping outside, holding a mug, and using a phone. The session remains therapist-led in agenda and structure rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy with open questions and low-pressure, optional next steps. Some questions subtly steer toward trying an alternative without explicitly affirming that choosing not to try is also okay.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client-generated ideas but does not consistently draw out deeper values, importance, confidence, or change talk. Classic MI evocation techniques are limited, so internal motivation is not fully strengthened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Elise as the expert on her social world and builds on her ideas. There is less explicit power-sharing, such as negotiating the agenda or checking how she wants to proceed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise offers some change talk, including wanting to be healthier and trying “maybe one time.” The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk, and confidence/ability/commitment are underexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by rolling with reasons for smoking instead of arguing. The final sustain talk about feeling like a “leftover” remains unaddressed, limiting full transformation into values-based change talk.
