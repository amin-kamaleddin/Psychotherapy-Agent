# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and genuine unconditional positive regard throughout the session, with specific individualized affirmations referencing Daniel's life context. The closing line 'no pressure on the outcome either way' is a masterful demonstration of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, nuanced reflective listening that captures both content and emotional meaning, with reflections that build on each other and synthesize multiple emotional threads. Goes beyond surface-level paraphrase to capture the significance of Daniel's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on target behaviors with a logical progression from smoking success to alcohol exploration to concrete commitment. Minor room for improvement in strategic pacing — the smoking success could have been connected earlier as a template, and the topic transition was slightly abrupt.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook MI collaboration — the conversation is structured as joint exploration rather than clinician-led assessment. Daniel's ideas consistently shape the session direction, with all key decisions (two-drink limit, test case, follow-up) originating from him.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports Daniel's autonomy at multiple points, including 'I'm not trying to push you' and 'that's your motivation, not a rule imposed on you.' The entire session is structured so Daniel makes every decision, with no directive language used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates a clear arc of evoked change talk, from vague curiosity to specific commitment to self-efficacy. The psychiatrist's own content contribution is minimal and always in service of drawing out Daniel's internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom about change reside within Daniel, explicitly rejecting the expert role with 'you don't need fixing, you need a sounding board.' Never offers unsolicited advice or positions self as having superior knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Daniel's change talk evolves organically from vague curiosity to specific commitment language through the psychiatrist's use of open questions, amplifying reflections, and affirmations. Every piece of change talk comes from Daniel himself, which is the hallmark of effective evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with exceptional skill by acknowledging its validity rather than arguing against it, then gently redirecting through exploratory questions. The sustain talk naturally dissolves as Daniel's own change talk emerges — the ideal MI outcome.
