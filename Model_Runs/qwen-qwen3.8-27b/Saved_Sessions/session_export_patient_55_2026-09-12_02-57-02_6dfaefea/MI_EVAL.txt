# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts client refusals without pressure. Warmth remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses accurate surface-level reflections but does not consistently explore underlying emotions or meaning. Misses deeper attunement to strain, overwhelm, or tension around being seen as a problem.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session drifts away from the alcohol-risk referral and among many sleep, stress, and coping topics. Lacks a clear MI-focused target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client input shapes some decisions, but the counselor remains the primary generator of interventions. The interaction feels counselor-led rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice and accepts refusals without coercion. Repeated offers of tiny options remain subtly directive, preventing a higher score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are asked, but the counselor quickly provides explanations and options instead of eliciting client motivations or values. The dominant style is explanatory and option-driven.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites client input and respects choices, but the counselor frequently generates solutions. The partnership feels uneven rather than truly reciprocal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is rarely elicited and not effectively amplified when offered. The session shifts to problem-solving rather than exploring desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk. However, it lacks double-sided reflections or strategic reframing, and some responses inadvertently validate the status quo.
