# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, but the acceptance often feels procedural and task-focused rather than deeply warm or individualized. Positive regard is present, though it does not consistently normalize broader ambivalence, shame, or fear of being seen as out of control.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and useful, especially in summarizing triggers and basic concerns. However, the empathy remains largely cognitive and does not consistently capture the emotional complexity around shame, defensiveness, autonomy, or fear of judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on the target behavior of reducing alcohol-related risk while maintaining social participation. The progression from referral concern to triggers, consequences, strategies, scripts, and rehearsal is clear and well-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is meaningful collaboration, particularly around the check-in friend idea and script development, and the client’s input does shape the plan. However, the clinician remains the primary organizer, introducing the goal, strategies, scripts, and rehearsal structure rather than fully co-constructing the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician regularly supports choice by offering options, asking which strategy feels most doable, and avoiding coercive or pressuring language. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming the client’s ownership of the process and slowing down before supplying a detailed plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some early questions elicit useful information and modest change talk, but the clinician quickly shifts into problem-solving, scripting, and rehearsal. There is limited exploration of the client’s personal values, discrepancy, confidence, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and does not completely dominate the session, asking questions and offering the client choices. However, the interaction remains uneven, with the clinician quickly proposing the goal, triggers, and multiple strategies before deeply exploring the client’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges from the client and is used to guide planning, but the clinician does not consistently cultivate, amplify, or deepen it. The session lacks greater use of importance or confidence rulers, amplifying reflections, and questions designed to strengthen the client’s own motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with sustain talk and does not reinforce drinking as simply social fun, but the response is often practical rather than MI-skillful. Sustain talk is met with scripts and problem-solving more than double-sided reflections or careful exploration of ambivalence.
