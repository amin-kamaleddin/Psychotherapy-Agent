### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and validation of Daniela’s ambivalence and caution. Acceptance could be stronger by expressing more nonconditional regard for her as a person, not just approving her strategies.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and often capture both sides of her ambivalence, such as wanting normal friendships and managing diabetes. Empathy could deepen the emotional meaning around identity, shame, and stigma, and the repeated therapist line is a possible rupture.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of low-risk diabetes disclosure to peers. It moves clearly from general ambivalence to specific, behavior-relevant disclosure and exit steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is built from Daniela’s language and ideas, such as “ew, low,” “juice box,” and “footnote, not a main topic.” Collaboration would be stronger with more explicit negotiation about which topic to focus on next.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reduces pressure and asks for Daniela’s own version of safe wording and timing. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly affirming that not trying this week is also a valid choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician evokes Daniela’s own motivations, plans, and problem-solving rather than supplying reasons for change. Evocation could be deeper by systematically exploring values, confidence, ability, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniela is treated as the expert on her friendships, social comfort, and what feels safe. Partnership could improve with more explicit checks on whether she wants to move into concrete planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client-centered questions effectively bring out change talk, including tiredness of hiding, possible timing, and confidence in a low-key approach. Change talk could be developed further by connecting it to values and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes Daniela’s fears without arguing or dismissing them, turning concerns into material to explore. A stronger approach would include more double-sided reflections that hold both sides of her ambivalence.
