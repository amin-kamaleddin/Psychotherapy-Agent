# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates consistent, warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Isabella's experience without judgment or guilt. Every response affirms her perspective and normalizes her cultural identity while honoring her ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex, accurate, and emotionally attuned, capturing not just content but the underlying emotional weight and shifts in Isabella's thinking. The psychiatrist tracks subtle transitions (e.g., defensive to reflective) with masterful complex reflections like 'You went from a podium to a cupboard.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, purposeful arc from ambivalence exploration through to a concrete, actionable plan (handout, kitchen table, garage) tied directly to the target behavior. Pacing is excellent, building momentum without feeling rushed or forced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Isabella as an equal partner, consistently using language like 'I'm curious,' 'What do you think,' and 'What would it mean to you' rather than directive statements. Session direction is shaped entirely by Isabella's contributions, with no imposed agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exceptional, with the psychiatrist never pressuring Isabella to commit or implying abstinence is the only acceptable outcome. When Isabella says 'I can always change my mind later,' the psychiatrist validates this rather than correcting it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out Isabella's own values, concerns, and motivations through open questions rather than providing external information or arguments. Virtually all change talk emerges from Isabella herself, demonstrating the MI principle that motivation must come from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly conveys that expertise about change resides within Isabella ('That's your expertise, not mine') and consistently follows her lead rather than directing the conversation. The power dynamic is genuinely egalitarian, with the session clearly co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully catches and amplifies shifts in Isabella's language in real-time and elicits substantial DARN-C change talk. Could be strengthened by eliciting explicit confidence ratings and using summarization to consolidate change talk at key inflection points.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively with double-sided reflections that honor Isabella's cultural identity while gently pivoting toward her own process, and the psychiatrist never argues or dismisses. The score is limited because the sustain talk in this session is relatively mild, and more explicit techniques (e.g., reflective disagreement, 'You see it one way, I see it another') could have been deployed.
