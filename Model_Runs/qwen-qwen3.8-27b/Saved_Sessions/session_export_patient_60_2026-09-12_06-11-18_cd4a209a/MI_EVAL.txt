# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and validates Jason’s perspective without moralizing. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with fewer explicit affirmations of Jason’s worth or complexity beyond the immediate task.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and repeatedly captures Jason’s cognitive perspective, goals, and concerns. However, empathy remains mainly surface-level and does not fully explore underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the alcohol-related conduct violation and preparation for the meeting. Direction is structured and relevant, though it emphasizes managing the conduct meeting more than broader behavior-change discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Jason’s input and incorporates his preferences into the session. However, collaboration is uneven because Jason often edits therapist-generated scripts rather than co-creating the language and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jason is given choices, is not forced to apologize, and is supported in finding language that feels honest. Autonomy support is generally strong, but the amount of therapist-generated scripting can subtly reduce Jason’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area, with limited eliciting of Jason’s internal motivations, values, discrepancy, and self-efficacy. The psychiatrist frequently supplies reasons and language instead of drawing out Jason’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a partially collaborative stance, with Jason’s preferences influencing the session. However, the psychiatrist often takes on an expert strategist role by providing examples, scripts, and role-play framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited around concrete steps such as education, avoidance, and moderation. However, the psychiatrist often supplies the change language rather than systematically expanding Jason’s own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Jason’s minimization or sustain talk and redirects toward concrete steps. However, the use of double-sided reflections, discrepancy-based reflections, and active softening strategies is limited.
