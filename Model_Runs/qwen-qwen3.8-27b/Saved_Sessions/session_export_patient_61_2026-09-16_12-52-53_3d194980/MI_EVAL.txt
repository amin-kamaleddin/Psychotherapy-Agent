# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and acknowledges Jonathan’s burden without invalidation. However, the warmth can feel somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflections of overwhelm and ambivalence, but the responses quickly move to logistics. The deeper emotional experience of feeling controlled, monitored, or like he is “just following orders” is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on diabetes self-management and moves logically from barriers to a concrete action plan. Multiple bundled questions, however, can overwhelm the client and slightly blur the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, creating a cooperative tone. Still, the interaction is more structurally collaborative than truly co-created, with the psychiatrist largely controlling the agenda and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Preference-based questions and confidence scaling support choice, but autonomy is weakened by drafting the message, stating the “agreed plan,” and prescribing a backup protocol. The client’s ownership is not consistently reaffirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question is genuinely evocative, but much of the session shifts to logistical planning. Jonathan’s own reasons, values, and change talk are not fully drawn out or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission and invites choices, which are positive partnership signals. However, the overall dynamic remains clinician-led, with Jonathan selecting from prebuilt options rather than co-creating the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms realistic steps and evokes some change language, but the emphasis is on practical planning. Change talk is not strongly reflected back, amplified, or linked to the client’s deeper motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Resistance is acknowledged without direct argument, which is appropriate. However, sustain talk is more tolerated than strategically softened, with limited use of double-sided reflections or reframes.
