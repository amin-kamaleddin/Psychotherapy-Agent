# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with explicit validation of Seamus's frustration and right to simply talk. Could be strengthened with more affirmations of Seamus as a whole person beyond his smoking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture the emotional and experiential core rather than merely paraphrasing, with particularly powerful reframes like 'the cigarette is just riding along.' The final summary reflection masterfully validates Seamus's effort while reinforcing his autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, progressive arc moving logically from the dental concern through pattern exploration to a single manageable starting point. Could be tightened with a brief summary near the end that explicitly reinforces the session's trajectory and creates stronger forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's shape is structurally determined by Seamus's responses, not a predetermined protocol, with appropriate use of 'we' language. The psychiatrist acts as a skilled listener and questioner rather than a director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with explicit pressure removal throughout ('I'm not asking you to commit to anything') and no directive language. Seamus's right to merely explore is validated without any attempt to convert.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A masterclass in drawing motivation from within — the psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change, instead using questions that elicit Seamus's own values, experiences, and planning. The session ends with Seamus generating his own plan elements entirely self-directed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Seamus as the expert on his own life, with explicit statements distinguishing his contributions from the psychiatrist's role. The power dynamic is consistently equal or slightly tilted toward the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits multiple types of change talk (desire, identity, ability, plan) through open questions that invite rather than direct. Could benefit from more explicit amplification and deepening of key statements, particularly the identity-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every sustain talk statement is met with pure reflection and validation without any attempt to argue, minimize, or correct. The conversation naturally moves forward without the psychiatrist needing to 'win' any argument.
