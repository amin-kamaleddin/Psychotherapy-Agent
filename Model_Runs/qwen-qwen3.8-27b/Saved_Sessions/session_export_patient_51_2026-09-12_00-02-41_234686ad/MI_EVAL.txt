# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, calm, and nonjudgmental, but the acceptance feels procedural rather than individually validating. Repeated safety warnings after the patient repeatedly leaves can feel intrusive despite good intent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is little actual empathic reflection of the patient’s concern, urgency, or difficulty making a safe decision quickly. The response remains mostly factual and medical rather than perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear clinical focus on the medication interaction and safety risk. However, it is not consistently MI-directed toward facilitating a change-focused next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening invites the patient’s concern and affirms the plan to consult the GP, but the interaction quickly becomes one-way information delivery. There is limited joint problem-solving or co-creation of a brief plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some autonomy-compatible language, such as encouraging consultation with the prescriber and pharmacist. Repeated prescriptive safety instructions after the patient is leaving reduce the sense of patient ownership and choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist primarily provides clinical reasons and guidance rather than evoking the patient’s own motivation, values, or confidence. There is little or no effort to draw out internal reasons for safe medication management.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly expert-driven, with the psychiatrist delivering warnings and guidance while the patient receives information. There is little evidence of power-sharing, co-created agenda, or treating the patient as an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only weak attempts to acknowledge or strengthen change talk, such as calling the plan to discuss the washout “solid.” The psychiatrist does not systematically evoke the patient’s reasons, values, confidence, or preferred next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce the patient’s time pressure as a barrier, but also does not reflectively handle or soften it. The session does not meaningfully redirect the urgency toward internal motivation for safe medication decisions.
