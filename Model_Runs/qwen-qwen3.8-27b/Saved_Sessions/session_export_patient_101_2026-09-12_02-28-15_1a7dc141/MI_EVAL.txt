# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without shaming. Acceptance could be stronger with more explicit, individualized affirmation of the patient's worth and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening accurately captures concern, confidence, and family history. Deeper emotional reflections about fear, embarrassment, and shame are missing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and contraceptive reliability and moves toward practical next steps. It drifts into administrative details and lacks stronger MI summaries to consolidate motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and lets patient choices shape the plan. The collaboration is somewhat clinician-led because much is provided as options and scripts rather than elicited from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient is given choices and accepted in prioritizing the implant first. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement that the patient is the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions invite the patient to choose and generate some motivations. The session shifts too quickly into psychoeducation and scripting rather than deeply evoking values, strengths, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a partner by offering options and inviting role-play. Power sharing is limited by the extensive lists and scripted language that make the interaction more expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that elicit useful change talk about feasibility and the next step. This change talk is not consistently reflected, amplified, or linked to values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doubts and overwhelm are acknowledged without arguing or dismissing them. Active softening is limited because the psychiatrist rarely uses double-sided reflections or reframes sustain talk into change direction.
