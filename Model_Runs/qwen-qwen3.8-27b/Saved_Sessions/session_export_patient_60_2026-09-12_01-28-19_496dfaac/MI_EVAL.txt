# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepts Jason's refusals. Warmth is somewhat generic and occasionally agrees with the framing of the situation as external unfairness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Simple reflections capture frustration but remain surface-level. Deeper reflections about Jason's age identity, values, sense of fairness, and responsibility are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays focused on procedural outcomes rather than the target behavior and its meaning. There is little use of summarization or values-based focus to guide change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative features appear, but the interaction is largely counselor-driven through repeated lists of options. Jason's own values and goals are not used to build a shared agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Jason's choice and respects his refusals. Autonomy support is somewhat weakened by repeated directive scripts and procedural plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open-ended questions are followed by a shift to advice and procedural problem-solving. Jason's own motivations, values, and reasons for change are not consistently evoked or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions and offers options, but the session is largely expert-driven. Jason's expertise and perspective are not central to setting the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is a limited initial attempt to explore change, but the conversation quickly becomes procedural. Jason's autonomy and ownership language is not reflected, amplified, or built on as change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk is repeatedly validated and amplified rather than softened. The psychiatrist reinforces a non-admission stance and focuses on avoiding consequences instead of exploring responsibility and values.
