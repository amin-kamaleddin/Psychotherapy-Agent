# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and supports Samuel’s choices without criticism. Acceptance is strong, though it remains somewhat professional and procedural rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several accurate reflections of Samuel’s concerns and practical realities. However, the empathy is more cognitive and plan-focused, with missed opportunities to explore the emotional weight of his ambivalence and stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing smoking, particularly managing work-related urges and building a concrete plan. Questions are purposeful, behavior-specific, and maintain clear momentum toward follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as joint exploration and frequently invites Samuel’s input in shaping the plan. It is not a 5 because the clinician often offers structured options before eliciting Samuel’s own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Samuel’s freedom to choose is strongly and explicitly supported throughout the session. The psychiatrist does not push rejected strategies and respects Samuel’s self-assessment, including uncertainty and limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Samuel’s own reasons, strategies, and confidence through open questions and scaling. It is not a 5 because several therapist-generated options are introduced before fully eliciting Samuel’s own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session treats Samuel as the expert on his own life and uses his preferences to guide the plan. Partnership is strong, though it can feel somewhat guided because the psychiatrist frequently provides structured menus before building from Samuel’s own suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling help Samuel articulate desire, reasons, ability, and concrete steps toward reducing smoking. Change talk is effectively reinforced, though the psychiatrist sometimes moves too quickly into problem-solving without deepening the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by acknowledging Samuel’s concerns without arguing, minimizing, or pressuring him. The response effectively softens resistance but does not consistently use more advanced techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to transform sustain talk into change talk.
