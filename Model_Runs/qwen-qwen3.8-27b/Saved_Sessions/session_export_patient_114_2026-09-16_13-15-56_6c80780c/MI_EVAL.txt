# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and validating, normalizing Ryan's anxiety and acknowledging his values. However, the acceptance is somewhat task-oriented and could include deeper unconditional positive regard during moments of fear or shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing ambivalence and emotional concerns. Some empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame, embarrassment, or fear of losing social identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the alcohol-related incident, risk reduction, and conduct process. The focus occasionally shifts into procedural problem-solving, which can reduce the motivational depth of the interview.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, supporting some collaboration. However, much of the plan, email, and problem-solving is expert-driven rather than fully co-created with Ryan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers options, and allows Ryan to make decisions, strongly supporting autonomy. The frequent provision of detailed drafts and plans may slightly reduce Ryan's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions are used to elicit Ryan's perspective and initial change ideas. However, the session moves quickly into advice and planning, limiting deeper evocation of values, ability, reasons, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins collaboratively and invites Ryan's preferences. Over time, the interaction becomes more expert-driven as the psychiatrist generates most of the solutions and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms Ryan's stated change goals and realistic choices. There is limited use of deeper MI techniques such as DARN-C, importance/confidence rulers, or elaboration to strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Ryan's sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, he often accepts it and moves directly into problem-solving rather than using reflections to soften sustain talk or explore discrepancy.
