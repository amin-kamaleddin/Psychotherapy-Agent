# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment, labeling, or pressure. Resistance was met respectfully and without punitive responses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures the client's burdens and concerns. Some reflections added therapist framing instead of staying fully in the client's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on charging and using the medical device. The conversation moved clearly from resistance toward practical workarounds.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician adopted a collaborative stance and treated the client as the expert on their routines. The client's ideas strongly shaped the content, though the clinician still guided the inquiry.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's pace and choice were respected, with explicit support for autonomy. A few therapist-led reframes and structured questions slightly reduced the feeling of full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician effectively drew out the client's own practical solutions and thinking. More evocation of values, confidence, and commitment would strengthen the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invited the client's expertise and avoided making decisions for the client. Some leading questions kept the collaboration slightly clinician-guided rather than fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician elicited useful client-generated change ideas around routines and device placement. The main gap was not fully deepening change talk through confidence, importance, or benefits.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well without argument, using double-sided reflections. The session could have pivoted more consistently to the client's change-friendly language.
