# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, validating Ryan’s struggle without blaming him. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicitly and consistently affirming Ryan’s ambivalence, fear, and self-doubt as fully acceptable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening and accurately captures Ryan’s internal tension between safety and social belonging. Empathy could be deepened by more consistently reflecting the emotional weight beneath Ryan’s statements, such as shame, vulnerability, and fear of being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on safer drinking in social situations and follows a logical progression from ambivalence to small changes. Direction could be improved by more explicitly framing the target behavior early and consolidating the explored ideas into a clearer next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a strongly collaborative stance throughout, inviting Ryan’s expertise and building directly on his ideas. The conversation is genuinely co-created, with Ryan’s suggestions actively shaping the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by asking Ryan to generate his own plans and avoiding pressure to commit to specific actions. This could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Ryan’s freedom to choose whether, when, and how to try any strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently asks evocative questions that help Ryan connect safer drinking to his own values, goals, and confidence. Evocation could be further developed by more systematically deepening Ryan’s desire, ability, need, reason, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveys that Ryan is the expert on his own life and social world. The session is clearly client-led, with shared decision-making and a balanced, non-directive stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits multiple change statements, including Ryan’s desire to protect his degree, maintain friendships, and create safer social options. These change statements could be more fully consolidated and amplified to strengthen Ryan’s sense of personal motivation and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk in an MI-consistent way by reflecting the function of drinking rather than arguing against it. Final sustain talk and self-doubt remain prominent and could be more actively linked to Ryan’s values, strength, and agency.
