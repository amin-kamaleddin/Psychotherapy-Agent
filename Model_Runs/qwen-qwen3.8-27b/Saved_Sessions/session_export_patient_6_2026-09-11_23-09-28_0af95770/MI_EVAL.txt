# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers several affirming statements. However, the warmth is mostly procedural and does not consistently communicate deeper acceptance of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are brief acknowledgments, but the client’s more complex emotions are not accurately or deeply reflected. Empathic responses remain surface-level and do not fully capture his fear, embarrassment, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use, refusal skills, exit planning, and safety. The main limitation is that direction is more skills-based than change-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates some of the client’s ideas and invites him to choose and practice scripts. However, the counselor still takes an expert role by generating most of the strategies and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids arguing, which supports some autonomy. The overall style remains quite directive, with prescribed scripts and safety instructions limiting stronger autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks an initial importance question but quickly shifts to advice and skill-building. The client’s own values and reasons for change, such as soccer and college, are not deeply evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and invitation to choose options. The power balance remains uneven because the counselor supplies most of the content and the client mostly selects from therapist-generated choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes a few initial attempts to elicit change talk, but does not consistently deepen or amplify it. Important client statements about not drinking again are not followed up or strengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s minimization, which is appropriate, but does not effectively soften or redirect it. The session remains focused on scripts and planning rather than resolving ambivalence.
