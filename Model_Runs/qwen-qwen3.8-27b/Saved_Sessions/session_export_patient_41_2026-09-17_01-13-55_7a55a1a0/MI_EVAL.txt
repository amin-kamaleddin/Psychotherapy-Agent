# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses validating, nonjudgmental language and counters self-critical statements without labeling the client as weak or irresponsible. Acceptance is strong but could be warmer and more individualized to reach a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence, fear of failure, and conflict between knowing and pressure. Empathy is solid, though it could dig deeper into shame and related underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on anxiety-driven avoidance and the specific target of taking a small action step. Direction is clear, but it would be stronger with a more explicit final plan or confirmed next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s ideas clearly shape the conversation, and the psychiatrist builds on suggestions such as the two-minute goal and treating leaving as a pause. Collaboration is good, though the psychiatrist still guides much of the structure and introduces part-based framing without checking its fit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports the client’s sense of control by emphasizing choice, permission to stop, and a survivable small step. Autonomy support is a strength, but some directive suggestions slightly narrow the client’s freedom to define the step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions that help the client generate ideas such as self-compassion, breathing, and showing up. Evocation is strong but could go deeper into values, confidence, and future-oriented motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites the client’s perspective and allows her contributions to influence the session. Partnership is present, though it could be enhanced by more explicit co-creation of the agenda and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects and affirms meaningful change talk, including the client’s idea that standing for two minutes counts as progress. Change talk is well supported, but it could be expanded more consistently to strengthen confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles avoidance and fear of failure without arguing, reframing leaving as a pause rather than quitting. Sustain talk is softened effectively, though the final concern about needing to “pass” could have been redirected more skillfully.
