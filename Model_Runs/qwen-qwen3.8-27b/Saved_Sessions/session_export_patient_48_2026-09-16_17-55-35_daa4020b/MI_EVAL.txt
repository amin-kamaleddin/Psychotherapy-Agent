# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and normalization of Farid’s fatigue. It falls short of a 5 because individualized affirmations of Farid’s strengths, effort, or awareness are limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture Farid’s ambivalence, fatigue, and practical burdens. The performance could be stronger by more deeply reflecting underlying emotions such as shame, self-doubt, or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on a small, manageable physical activity goal while addressing barriers like fatigue and self-doubt. It would be stronger with more explicit summaries and progression toward readiness or a specific change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports a collaborative stance by building around Farid’s ideas and choices rather than imposing solutions. However, frequent use of therapist-generated options makes the conversation slightly more therapist-directed than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and directive advice while respecting Farid’s preference for low-pressure, private strategies. It could be stronger by more explicitly reinforcing Farid’s ownership and right to set his own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively draw out Farid’s own ideas, such as short walks, private cues, and using music. Evocation would improve by more consistently eliciting values, confidence, importance, and readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Farid as the expert by asking what feels realistic and what would feel most like him. Partnership would be stronger with more explicit agenda co-creation and power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Farid generates several practical change ideas, including short walks, parking farther away, and using music. The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or deepen this change talk with affirmations, reflections, or values-based emphasis.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustaining talk well by acknowledging concerns without arguing or becoming corrective. It would benefit from more strategic language that explicitly reduces the power of Farid’s self-doubt and limitations.
