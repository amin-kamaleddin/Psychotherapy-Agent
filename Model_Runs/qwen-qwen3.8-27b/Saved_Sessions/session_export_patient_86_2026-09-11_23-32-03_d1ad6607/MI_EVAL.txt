# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgment, and no pressure while avoiding lectures. The warmth is somewhat generic and procedural, especially with repeated safety wording and recaps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes an adequate effort to understand the client’s perspective, including annoyance and preference for simplicity. Reflections remain mostly surface-level and do not fully explore the emotional weight of the client’s history.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on practical dental health and smoking-adjacent behavior that matches the client’s stated preference. There is some drift due to repeated safety statements and recaps, but the overall direction is clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and incorporates choices such as the gum cue. The expert-helper stance and structured advice prevent the interaction from feeling fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, avoids coercion, and accepts the client’s rejection of additional demands. The amount of advice and repeated safety script makes the tone slightly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are asked, but they are shallow and inconsistent. The psychiatrist does not use scaling, values-based, or importance/confidence questions to develop deeper motivation from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client meaningfully influences the plan by choosing to keep gum by the lighter. The detailed clinician-led recommendations and repeated recaps reduce the sense of full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-oriented change language and helps the client make a small practical commitment. However, change talk is not consistently amplified through importance, confidence, or values-based exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation with the client’s sustain talk and accepts the client’s position. The response mostly sidesteps sustain talk rather than actively softening it with ambivalence exploration or double-sided reflections.
