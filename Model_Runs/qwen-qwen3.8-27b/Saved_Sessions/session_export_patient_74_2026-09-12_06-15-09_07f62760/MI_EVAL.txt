# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with several affirming and normalizing statements. The main limitation is that the warmth remains somewhat task-focused rather than consistently conveying unconditional positive regard independent of change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening to capture Linda’s ambivalence, emotional discomfort, and fear of failure. A stronger empathic response would have stayed longer with the pain of her previous quit attempt before moving into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior, particularly the after-dinner cigarette, and uses rulers and triggers to maintain momentum. The psychiatrist could more deliberately guide the conversation from tonight’s small experiment toward a broader understanding of change over time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed, as Linda chooses the toothpick, tea, and timer and keeps the plan private. The limitation is that the psychiatrist still supplies many specific strategies, making the collaboration feel more like guided problem-solving than fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites Linda’s choice, respects her limits, and frames a slip as information rather than failure. However, the repeated introduction of specific behavioral options can feel somewhat suggestive and could better normalize choosing no immediate experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Linda’s own reasons, preferences, and confidence, especially around her children. The evocation is weakened by a large number of clinician-generated behavioral suggestions instead of deeper exploration of Linda’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input at multiple points and allows Linda’s ideas to shape the final plan. The interaction is not fully client-led because the psychiatrist still positions themselves as the strategist providing the menu of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda produces some change talk around being a role model and trying the toothpick, and her confidence increases slightly. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen this change talk, and DARN-C language is touched on but not fully developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respectfully acknowledges the genuine function and value of Linda’s after-dinner smoke without challenging her. There are missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections to redirect sustain talk toward discrepancy, values, or change.
