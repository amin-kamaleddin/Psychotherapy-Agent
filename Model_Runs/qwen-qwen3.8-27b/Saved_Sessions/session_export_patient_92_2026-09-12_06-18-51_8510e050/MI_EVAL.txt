# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  respectful, nonjudgmental tone with normalizing language throughout. Slightly task-oriented and clinical rather than deeply warm or individually affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  captures Marek's key perspectives, ritual values, and ambivalence with accurate reflections. Stays mostly at surface/practical level without deeper emotional exploration of fear of exclusion or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:**  Tight focus on weekend alcohol use with efficient progression from concern to concrete plan and coping strategies. No significant drift; one of the strongest areas.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  Final plan is co-constructed using Marek's own language and practical concerns. Many strategies are therapist-generated, limiting a fully equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  Regular invitations for choice, respects rejections, and normalizes slip risk. Some subtle pressure to lock in and raise confidence; lacks explicit "you are ultimately in charge" framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Some open questions and confidence scaling produce client language, but the session is more problem-solving than deeply evocative. Many solutions supplied before fully exploring Marek's internal motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Collaborative surface with repeated invitations to choose among options. Remains expert-driven—the psychiatrist supplies most specific strategies and largely constructs the framework Marek selects from.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Elicits some change talk via confidence ratings and choice questions, but relies heavily on therapist-generated strategies. Limited amplification or elaboration of Marek's own reasons, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  Sustain talk acknowledged and normalized without challenging or amplifying. Could benefit from more strategic double-sided reflections to fully transform sustain into change talk.
