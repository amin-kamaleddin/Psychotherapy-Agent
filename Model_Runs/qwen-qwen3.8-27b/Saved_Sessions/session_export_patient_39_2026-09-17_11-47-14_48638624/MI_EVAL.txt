# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, warm unconditional positive regard throughout, never labeling or expressing disappointment. The opening validation of showing up and the phrase 'I'm not saying the belief is wrong' exemplify genuine acceptance of Elias's perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offers multiple sophisticated reflections that capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions, such as 'I notice you didn't say what if I can't do anything' and the reflection on the push-through belief's deeper emotional meaning. Consistently goes beyond surface-level paraphrasing to honor underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, strategic focus on the target behavior (walking with wife) with logical progression from acknowledgment through ambivalence to a concrete action plan. Every question and reflection connects back to the goal with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every step forward is co-constructed with Elias generating the content (ten minutes, not timing it, shoes by the door) while the psychiatrist provides structure and strategic questions. Uses inclusive, co-creative language throughout and never unilaterally declares a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Elias's ownership and freedom of choice, explicitly validating that his legs might not move ('or they don't') and preserving his self-generated commitment language ('trying'). Never uses 'you should' or implies inaction would be a failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change talk is self-generated by Elias through skilled open questions that draw out his own values, beliefs, and motivations. The psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change or offers expert advice; motivation emerges entirely from within Elias.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly establishes the partnership frame ('work alongside you, not to fix you') and consistently attributes expertise to Elias ('you already know your body better than that worry wants you to'). The power dynamic remains clearly egalitarian throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits desire, ability, and commitment talk through strategic questions without ever directly asking 'why should you change?' Elias articulates his own goal, solution, values, and concrete action step entirely in his own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively softens sustain talk in most instances by reframing and exploring meaning rather than dismissing or arguing. Could benefit from more explicit double-sided reflections in a couple of moments to more actively hold both sides of the ambivalence.
