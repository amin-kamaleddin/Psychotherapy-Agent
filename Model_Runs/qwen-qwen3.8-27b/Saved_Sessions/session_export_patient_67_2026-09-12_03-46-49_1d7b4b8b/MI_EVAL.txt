# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance and normalizes the patient’s awkwardness. It falls short of exceptional because some phrases are slightly evaluative and the deeper shame is not fully acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and captures the patient’s ambivalence and social pressure. Some reflections remain surface-level rather than fully exploring the emotional weight of fear and embarrassment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and building a concrete next step. It is somewhat agenda-driven, moving quickly into suggestions and rehearsal before fully letting the patient set the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist co-creates the plan and invites the patient’s preferences, which shapes the intervention. It is less than ideal because the therapist supplies scripts and strategies before fully eliciting the patient’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, low-pressure invitations, and small steps that support the patient’s sense of control. Ownership could be strengthened with more explicit autonomy-supportive statements rather than structured suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses importance and confidence questions to evoke some motivation and personal preferences. The exploration of deeper values and reasons for change is limited, and advice-giving begins too early.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the patient meaningfully shaping the plan. Some therapist-led suggestions and evaluative language subtly position the counselor as the expert rather than fully sharing power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk through open questions and options. The therapist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or summarize the patient’s change statements and provides scripts early.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and redirects toward manageable actions. The patient’s social fears and ambivalence are not fully explored or softened with more balanced reflections.
