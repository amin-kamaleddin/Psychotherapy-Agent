# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys nonjudgmental respect and honors Liora's boundaries without labeling her. The main limitation is that some responses subtly reframe her despair in a more hopeful tone than she expresses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections often capture Liora's practical perspective and the functional role of self-harm. However, they do not consistently mirror the deeper shame, exhaustion, fear of disappointment, and hopelessness she voices.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of self-harm used to manage overwhelming stress and emotional noise. The focus remains somewhat abstract, and there is no clear consolidation of a concrete target or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Liora to define safety, boundaries, and possible options. The interaction remains relatively therapist-directed, and Liora's own ideas, such as holding ice, are not fully taken up as a collaborative thread.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Liora's autonomy by avoiding pressure, respecting boundaries, and not giving direct advice. Some statements subtly interpret ambivalence as change motivation, which slightly limits full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions about what would make an alternative feel possible and reflects underlying needs for relief without exhaustion. These efforts remain shallow and do not consistently elicit or deepen Liora's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly positions Liora as the expert on her own experience and invites her perspective on safety and starting points. The session is collaborative but not fully client-led, as the psychiatrist continues to shape the agenda with structured questions and interpretations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable attempts to invite Liora to consider low-effort alternatives and possibilities. When Liora mentions holding ice, the psychiatrist does not follow up to develop or amplify this emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing against Liora's sustain talk and validates the function of self-harm. However, the session remains heavily centered on why cutting is effective, and there are limited skillful double-sided reflections to gently widen attention toward change.
