# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a consistent, nonjudgmental stance by respecting Jason’s choices and avoiding labels. This acceptance keeps the conversation open despite minimizing and defensive statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Jason’s practical concerns about fairness, rules, and protecting his degree. However, the empathy is more cognitive than emotional, with less explicit reflection of underlying fear, shame, or social identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to the conduct issue and practical next steps but becomes overly administrative. It lacks clearer strategic movement toward broader change, a summary, and a concrete closing plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor behaves as an equal partner by inviting Jason’s input and building on his ideas. The session is co-created rather than driven by the counselor’s agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently emphasizes Jason’s right to choose and avoids pressure, commands, or persuasion. Jason is positioned as responsible for deciding how to handle the situation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit some client-generated ideas, but the evocation remains focused on practical rule clarification. There is limited exploration of deeper values, future goals, or confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor treats Jason as the expert on his own social life and choices. Questions invite him to shape the content and practical solution rather than receiving instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some willingness to accept a warning and complete a short module, but does not consistently amplify or reinforce change talk. More strategic emphasis on importance, ability, values, or confidence would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without argument or confrontation, and the counselor often redirects toward practical responsibility. More advanced double-sided reflections would help bridge sustain talk more clearly into change talk.
