# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows unconditional positive regard without judgment, labeling, or moralizing. The client’s frustration, ambivalence, and preference for a non-pressured approach are acknowledged respectfully throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate, emotionally attuned reflections that capture the client’s concerns, burdens, resistance, and emerging hope. The reflections track subtle shifts in feeling and motivation rather than restating surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking reduction and progressively narrows to a specific, manageable target: managing work-related smoking and the 2 PM slump. Each question remains tied to the client’s chosen behavior and measurement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the client rather than for them, using joint language and building directly from the client’s ideas. Constraints such as no logbook, no scorecard, and no daily checkups are incorporated into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker, with repeated affirmations of her right to choose and try the process her way. The counselor avoids pressure, grading, and external control while honoring the client’s boundaries.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes the client’s own reasoning, feasibility judgments, and small evidence of capability. Evocation could be strengthened by drawing out deeper values, future goals, or personal meaning related to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treats the client as the expert on her own life and the designer of change. The client’s contributions shape the direction, scope, and success criteria of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively affirms and amplifies the client’s own language in favor of change, including past success and the emerging self-defined plan. Change talk could be deepened further by linking the small step to broader confidence, values, or stronger commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through reflective, non-arguing responses that reduce resistance and redirect toward a smaller, self-directed experiment. The final statement of sustain talk is not explicitly softened or closed in the transcript, limiting the score.
